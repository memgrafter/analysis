# Limits for Learning with Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12213
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12213
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 17
- Key outcome: This paper provides a theoretical explanation for empirical failures of large language models (LLMs) to capture fundamental semantic properties like entailment and consistency.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates fundamental limitations in large language models' ability to learn semantic concepts essential for natural language understanding. Through theoretical analysis, the authors prove that LLMs cannot learn universal quantification, a concept critical for understanding entailment and consistency in formal semantics. The paper establishes that LLMs are restricted to learning concepts at the first level of the Borel Hierarchy, which severely limits their capacity to capture linguistic meaning. This theoretical framework explains empirical failures observed in BERT, RoBERTa, GPT3.5, and ChatGPT when handling universal quantification tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors employ mathematical analysis of Borel Hierarchy complexity to establish theoretical bounds on what LLMs can learn. They analyze auto-regressive probability models used in LLM training and prove that these models can only effectively learn sets at the first level (∆0₁) of the Borel Hierarchy. The paper connects this mathematical limitation to semantic concepts like universal quantification, which require higher complexity levels (Π0₁). Empirical validation involves testing multiple LLMs on universal quantification tasks, measuring their ability to consistently reason about statements involving "every" across varying domain sizes.

## Key Results
- LLMs cannot learn universal quantification due to their inability to grasp concepts beyond the first level of the Borel Hierarchy
- Semantic entailment and consistency, as defined in formal semantics, require universal quantification over infinite models that LLMs cannot learn
- Auto-regressive probability distributions in LLMs are non-degenerate, preventing effective learning of word order and sequence structure

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs cannot learn universal quantification because they cannot learn concepts beyond the first level of the Borel Hierarchy
- Mechanism: The Borel Hierarchy organizes mathematical concepts by complexity, with each level requiring more sophisticated learning capabilities. LLMs trained via auto-regressive models can only effectively learn sets at the first level (∆0₁), which are equivalent to finite combinations of basic open sets. Universal quantification requires understanding infinite domains and their logical combinations, placing it at a higher complexity level (Π0₁) that requires learning countable intersections of simpler sets.
- Core assumption: The mathematical structure of the Borel Hierarchy accurately captures the complexity of semantic concepts that language models need to learn
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that LLMs are unable to learn concepts beyond the first level of the Borel Hierarchy, which imposes severe limits on the ability of LMs, both large and small, to capture many aspects of linguistic meaning"
  - [section] "More generally, we show that LLMs are incapable of learning concepts beyond the first level of the Borel Hierarchy, which imposes severe limits on the ability of LMs, both large and small, to capture many aspects of linguistic meaning"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on linguistic competence and vocabulary tests but don't directly address Borel Hierarchy limitations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LLMs cannot effectively learn semantic entailment because entailment requires universal quantification over infinite models
- Mechanism: Semantic entailment (Γ |= φ) is defined as truth preservation across all possible models. This requires understanding that if a statement is true in every model where Γ is true, then φ must also be true. Universal quantification is the logical operator that captures this "for all models" requirement. Since LLMs cannot learn universal quantification, they cannot grasp the fundamental mechanism underlying semantic entailment.
- Core assumption: The definition of semantic entailment in formal semantics (based on universal quantification over models) is the correct framework for understanding linguistic meaning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we provide a theoretical foundation for these empirical findings by proving that LLMs cannot learn certain fundamental semantic properties including semantic entailment and consistency as they are defined in formal semantics"
  - [section] "Crucial to |= is the use of universal quantification over all possible structures—an infinite space of possible circumstances of evaluation or set of possibilities"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - papers on DL-Lite ontologies and linguistic minimal pairs suggest LLMs struggle with abstract semantic relationships

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LLMs cannot learn word order because their probability distributions over strings are non-degenerate and don't preserve sequence information
- Mechanism: LLMs generate probabilities for strings using auto-regressive models where each word's probability depends on previous context. However, non-degenerate distributions mean that as sequences get longer, the probability mass spreads thinly across many possible continuations. This prevents the model from learning that certain sequences are fundamentally different from their permutations. Without capturing word order, LLMs cannot distinguish between "every object is blue" and permutations where negation signs are incorrectly paired with predicates.
- Core assumption: Non-degenerate probability distributions in auto-regressive models inherently lose information about sequence structure that is critical for learning word order
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our theoretical argument suggests that we should expect certain empirical failures from LLMs, and in Section 5, we provide evidence that our predictions are borne out"
  - [section] "If M does not effectively capture word order, it will not reason soundly in propositional logic" and "LLMs unable to grasp semantic consequence defined in terms of universal quantification"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - papers on linguistic blind spots and spatial understanding hint at LLMs' struggles with structured information but don't directly address word order

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Borel Hierarchy
  - Why needed here: The Borel Hierarchy provides the mathematical framework for understanding why LLMs can only learn certain types of semantic concepts but not others. It establishes a strict ordering of set complexity that maps directly to the complexity of linguistic concepts like universal quantification.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain why the set of strings satisfying "every object is blue" is a Π0₁ set rather than a simpler ∆0₁ set?

- Concept: Universal Quantification
  - Why needed here: Universal quantification is the logical operator that underlies semantic entailment and consistency. Understanding why LLMs cannot learn this concept is central to the paper's argument about their limitations in capturing linguistic meaning.
  - Quick check question: Why does the inability to learn universal quantification over infinite domains prevent LLMs from understanding the meaning of "every"?

- Concept: Auto-regressive Probability Models
  - Why needed here: LLMs use auto-regressive models to generate probabilities over strings, and this mechanism is central to understanding why they have bounded learnability. The non-degenerate nature of these distributions creates fundamental limitations.
  - Quick check question: How does the auto-regressive generation process create non-degenerate probability distributions that prevent learning of higher-level Borel sets?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input text -> Embedding layers -> Transformer layers -> Context processing -> Auto-regressive probability estimation -> Output probability distribution over vocabulary
- Critical path: Training data → Transformer layers → Context embedding → Auto-regressive probability estimation → String probability distribution → Hypothesis learning
- Design tradeoffs: The auto-regressive approach enables efficient training on massive datasets but creates non-degenerate distributions that lose sequence information. Alternative approaches like denoising autoencoders might preserve more structure but are less scalable.
- Failure signatures: Inconsistent reasoning with quantifiers (treating "every" as applying to finite subsets), failure to respect word order in logical operators, inability to generalize learned concepts to longer sequences, and hallucinations when reasoning about universal statements.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test universal quantification: Provide strings with "every" applied to varying domain sizes and measure consistency of truth judgments
  2. Test word order sensitivity: Present permuted versions of the same logical statement and measure whether the model distinguishes them
  3. Test entailment reasoning: Present premise-conclusion pairs and measure whether the model correctly identifies valid entailments

## Limitations
- The theoretical framework assumes formal semantic definitions of entailment and consistency are the correct framework for understanding linguistic meaning
- Empirical validation focuses primarily on simple universal statements rather than the full complexity of entailment and consistency reasoning
- The mechanism linking non-degenerate probability distributions to loss of word order information requires more rigorous examination

## Confidence
- Claim: LLMs cannot learn universal quantification due to Borel Hierarchy constraints → High confidence
- Claim: Empirical evidence supports theoretical predictions → Medium confidence
- Claim: Non-degenerate distributions prevent word order learning → Low confidence
