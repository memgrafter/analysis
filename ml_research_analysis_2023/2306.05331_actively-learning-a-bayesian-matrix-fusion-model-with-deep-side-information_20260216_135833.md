# Actively learning a Bayesian matrix fusion model with deep side information

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05331
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05331
- Reference count: 3
- One-line primary result: Active learning achieves significant efficiency gains over passive baseline methods for Bayesian matrix factorization with deep side information, particularly with uncertainty sampling and batch size 8.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of efficiently aligning high-dimensional deep neural network representations with human behavioral annotations. The authors propose an active learning approach for adaptively sampling experimental stimuli to efficiently learn a Bayesian matrix factorization model with deep side information. Experiments on the One Million Impressions dataset demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves significant efficiency gains over passive baseline methods, with uncertainty sampling showing superior performance in reducing predictive RMSE while maintaining stability.

## Method Summary
The method combines Bayesian Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (BPMF) with uncertainty-based active sampling strategies to learn a model that maps deep features to human behavioral responses. The approach extracts deep features from pre-trained networks (StyleGAN2 for images, Sentence-BERT for traits), reduces their dimensionality through MLP layers, and integrates them into BPMF using MCMC sampling. Active learning strategies (uncertainty sampling and k-Center Greedy) are used to select the most informative samples for annotation, with batch sizes of 5, 8, and 10 tested.

## Key Results
- Uncertainty sampling with batch size 8 shows superior performance in reducing predictive RMSE while maintaining stability
- Reducing deep feature dimensionality by over 60% is tolerable for small training sets
- Shorter MCMC simulation chains combined with active learning can outperform passive learning with longer chains
- The proposed active learning approach achieves significant efficiency gains over passive baseline methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Uncertainty sampling in Bayesian matrix factorization improves efficiency by targeting data points with highest predictive uncertainty
- Mechanism: The method computes posterior predictive uncertainty for each unqueried data point using MCMC samples, then selects the batch with maximum combined uncertainty. This concentrates learning on information-rich regions of the response space.
- Core assumption: Higher predictive uncertainty correlates with higher information gain for model parameters
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The uncertainty sampling strategy, particularly with a batch size of 8, shows superior performance in reducing predictive RMSE while maintaining stability."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: k-Center Greedy sampling balances exploration and exploitation by maximizing coverage of the feature space
- Mechanism: The algorithm selects data points that minimize the maximum distance to the nearest center, ensuring diverse representation across the deep feature space rather than clustering in high-uncertainty regions
- Core assumption: Diverse coverage of the feature space leads to better generalization than pure uncertainty sampling
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.2.2] "k-Center Greedy sampling is a pool-based active sampling strategy... seeking k data points that maximize the mutual information between them and the remaining unselected data pool."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining deep side information with Bayesian matrix factorization enables multimodal alignment of machine and human representations
- Mechanism: Deep features from pre-trained networks serve as informative priors that capture complex perceptual relationships, which the BPMF model learns to map to human behavioral responses
- Core assumption: High-dimensional deep features contain sufficient information to predict human judgments when properly aligned through Bayesian inference
- Evidence anchors: [section 2.2] "Most human perceptual inference tasks involve combining information from multiple modalities... machine-generated deep features over conventional human-interpretable attributes."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing → Deep feature extraction (StyleGAN2/Sentence-BERT) → BPMF model with MCMC → Active sampling module (uncertainty/k-Center) → Prediction output
- Critical path: Feature extraction → BPMF inference → Uncertainty estimation → Sample selection → Model update
- Design tradeoffs: Higher-dimensional features improve representational power but increase computational cost; larger batch sizes speed up data collection but may reduce sampling efficiency
- Failure signatures: High RMSE with low uncertainty (model is confident but wrong), slow convergence (poor active sampling strategy), memory overflow (excessive MCMC samples or feature dimensions)

  1. Implement basic BPMF with passive sampling to establish baseline RMSE

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed active learning approach scale to datasets with more complex structures, such as 3D tensors, compared to the 2D response matrices used in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention the potential for generalizing their approach to datasets with more complex structures and diverse features, such as 3D tensors, but do not explore this in their experiments
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a specific application of first impressions with 2D response matrices, leaving the scalability to more complex structures unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of the active learning approach on datasets with varying dimensionalities (2D, 3D, etc.) would provide insights into its scalability and generalizability

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of incorporating additional modalities, such as participants' features, on the performance of the deep Bayesian matrix factorization model with active learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest the possibility of including a third information type, such as participants' features, alongside image and trait attributes, but do not investigate this in their study
- Why unresolved: The current study only considers bimodal side information (image and trait features), leaving the potential benefits or challenges of adding more modalities unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments incorporating additional modalities and comparing their performance with the bimodal case would shed light on the impact of multi-modal integration

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of active learning strategy (e.g., uncertainty sampling vs. k-Center Greedy) affect the performance of the deep Bayesian matrix factorization model in different data regimes, such as when the training set is large or small?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compare the performance of uncertainty sampling and k-Center Greedy strategies, but the study focuses on a specific dataset and batch size
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the performance of different active learning strategies across varying data regimes or batch sizes, leaving the optimal strategy for different scenarios unclear
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the dataset size, batch size, and active learning strategy would provide insights into the optimal choice for different data regimes

## Limitations

- Primary validation on a single dataset (One Million Impressions) with machine-generated faces limits generalizability to other domains
- Computational efficiency gains demonstrated through simulation but real-world annotation costs not fully characterized
- Uncertainty sampling shows superior performance but theoretical justification for information gain correlation is limited
