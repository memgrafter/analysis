# BoardgameQA: A Dataset for Natural Language Reasoning with Contradictory Information

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07934
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07934
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces BoardgameQA dataset for evaluating defeasible reasoning with conflicting information in language models

## Executive Summary
BoardgameQA is a novel dataset designed to evaluate natural language reasoning capabilities in scenarios involving conflicting information. The dataset presents defeasible reasoning problems where language models must resolve contradictions based on source preferences and implicit background knowledge. Through experiments with various language models and prompting strategies, the research demonstrates significant performance gaps, particularly in few-shot settings, highlighting the challenges of conflict resolution in pretrained models.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces BoardgameQA as a dataset containing 341 examples of defeasible reasoning problems centered around board game rules. The dataset is designed to test language models' ability to handle conflicting information by requiring them to identify relevant rules, resolve contradictions, and apply implicit background knowledge. The evaluation methodology involves testing various language model architectures under different conditions, including zero-shot, few-shot, and finetuned settings, with careful analysis of model performance across different reasoning complexity levels.

## Key Results
- Language models show significant performance gaps in handling conflicting information, particularly in few-shot settings
- Finetuning improves performance but remains limited in conflict resolution capabilities
- Models struggle with implicit background knowledge requirements and distractor handling

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dataset works by creating realistic conflict scenarios that mirror real-world reasoning challenges. By presenting multiple rules with varying source preferences and requiring implicit knowledge, it forces models to demonstrate true reasoning capabilities rather than simple pattern matching.

## Limitations
- Limited dataset size (341 examples) may not capture full complexity of real-world scenarios
- Focus on board game domain may limit generalizability to other reasoning contexts
- Evaluation methodology may not fully explore the impact of different prompting strategies
