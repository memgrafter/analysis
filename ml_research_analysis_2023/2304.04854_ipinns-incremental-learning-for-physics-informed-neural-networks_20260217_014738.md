# iPINNs: Incremental learning for Physics-informed neural networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04854
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04854
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: iPINNs achieve lower prediction error than regular PINNs for sequential learning of multiple PDEs without additional parameters

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces iPINNs, an incremental learning approach for Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) that enables sequential learning of multiple partial differential equations (PDEs) using a single neural network. The method works by creating task-specific subnetworks for each PDE through iterative pruning, allowing these subnetworks to overlap and share knowledge. iPINNs significantly improve performance on challenging equations within families, with relative errors as low as 0.246% compared to 37.4% for regular PINNs on a difficult convection equation.

## Method Summary
iPINNs implement sequential learning by first training a full network on the simplest PDE, then iteratively pruning and retraining for each subsequent, more complex PDE. The pruning process creates task-specific subnetworks that share overlapping parameters with previously learned subnetworks. This approach maintains knowledge from earlier tasks while adapting to new ones, and the sparse architecture naturally regularizes each subnetwork to prevent overfitting. The method is applicable to both learning families of equations and combinations of physical processes.

## Key Results
- iPINNs achieve significantly lower prediction error than regular PINNs on challenging convection equations (0.246% vs 37.4% relative error)
- The method successfully learns reaction-diffusion equations that regular PINNs cannot solve
- Task-specific subnetworks share approximately 20% of free connections while maintaining performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Iterative pruning with knowledge overlap enables sequential learning of multiple PDEs without catastrophic forgetting
- Mechanism: iPINNs start with a full network trained on Task 1, then iteratively prune and retrain for Task 2, allowing subnetworks to share connections. This creates a single network with overlapping task-specific subnetworks that retain knowledge from previous tasks while adapting to new ones
- Core assumption: Previous task subnetworks provide good initialization for new tasks if PDEs share similarities
- Evidence anchors: The abstract states iPINNs learn multiple PDEs by creating subnetworks that overlap with previously learned ones, and section analysis confirms this creates effective initialization when PDEs share similarities
- Break condition: If PDEs are not sufficiently similar, the overlap won't provide useful initialization and performance will degrade

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Learning simpler PDEs first provides better initialization for more complex PDEs
- Mechanism: By starting with simpler equations and incrementally adding complexity, the network develops representations that transfer effectively to harder problems. This is particularly effective when complex PDEs are combinations of simpler ones
- Core assumption: Simpler PDEs share representational structure with more complex ones
- Evidence anchors: The abstract demonstrates that previous subnetworks are good initialization when PDEs share similarities, and section analysis shows lower prediction error for sequential learning compared to regular PINNs
- Break condition: If the sequence order is reversed (learning complex before simple), the initialization may be poor and convergence difficult

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Task-specific subnetworks with overlapping parameters achieve better generalization than dense networks trained on all tasks simultaneously
- Mechanism: The sparse architecture naturally regularizes each subnetwork by limiting its capacity to overfit, while the shared parameters enable knowledge transfer between tasks
- Core assumption: Sparse representations are sufficient for each task while enabling effective knowledge sharing
- Evidence anchors: Section analysis shows each subnetwork is less parameterized than the original network and therefore does not tend to overfit, and the percentage of parameters occupied by all tasks is very similar for different orderings
- Break condition: If tasks are too dissimilar, the shared parameters may create interference rather than beneficial transfer

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Single neural network with K overlapping subnetworks (N1, N2, ..., NK), where each subnetwork is task-specific but shares parameters with others
- Critical path: 1) Train full network on Task 1, 2) Prune and retrain for Task 2, 3) Repeat for subsequent tasks, 4) Maintain all subnetworks during training
- Design tradeoffs: More overlapping parameters improve transfer but reduce sparsity; less overlap preserves task independence but may reduce transfer efficiency
- Failure signatures: Poor transfer between tasks (errors increase for earlier tasks), slow convergence on new tasks, or inability to learn complex PDEs that were learnable independently
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement 1-D convection equation learning for β = 1, 10, 20 sequentially and compare with regular PINNs
  2. Test reaction equation learning for ρ = 1, 2, 3 sequentially with different pruning parameters
  3. Attempt to learn reaction-diffusion equation after learning reaction and diffusion components separately, testing both orderings

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of iPINNs scale with the number of tasks and their complexity in high-dimensional PDE problems?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates iPINNs on 1-D PDEs; however, real-world problems often involve higher dimensions and increased complexity
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on 1-D cases and does not explore higher-dimensional or more complex PDE scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments on 2-D and 3-D PDEs with varying numbers of tasks and complexity levels, comparing iPINNs to regular PINNs and other incremental learning methods

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal pruning strategy for creating task-specific subnetworks in iPINNs to balance performance and computational efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using an iterative pruning algorithm but does not explore different pruning strategies or their impact on performance
- Why unresolved: The choice of pruning strategy can significantly affect the balance between model performance and computational efficiency, which is not thoroughly investigated
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of various pruning strategies (e.g., magnitude-based, gradient-based, or structured pruning) on iPINNs' performance and computational efficiency

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the order of learning tasks affect the overall performance of iPINNs, especially when tasks have varying degrees of similarity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions learning a combination of equations (e.g., reaction-diffusion) in different orders but does not explore the impact of task ordering on performance
- Why unresolved: The order in which tasks are learned could influence the knowledge transfer and overall performance, particularly when tasks have different levels of similarity
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the order of tasks with different degrees of similarity and measuring the impact on performance metrics like accuracy and backward/forward transfer

## Limitations

- Performance on higher-dimensional PDEs remains untested, limiting real-world applicability
- Pruning mechanism details are not fully specified, creating uncertainty about reproducibility
- Claims about knowledge transfer through overlapping parameters need validation on more diverse PDE families

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of iterative pruning with knowledge overlap is well-supported by the results on both equation families tested
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about better initialization from simpler to complex PDEs are supported but limited to the specific cases tested
- **Medium confidence**: Generalization benefits from sparse architecture are demonstrated but could benefit from comparison against other regularization methods
