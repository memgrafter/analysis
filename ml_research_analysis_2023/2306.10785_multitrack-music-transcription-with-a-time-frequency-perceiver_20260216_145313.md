# Multitrack Music Transcription with a Time-Frequency Perceiver

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10785
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10785
- Reference count: 0
- This paper proposes a scalable model for multitrack music transcription by extending the Perceiver architecture with an additional Transformer layer to capture temporal coherence.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Perceiver TF, a novel architecture for multitrack music transcription that extends the Perceiver model with hierarchical expansion and temporal Transformers. The method efficiently processes high-resolution time-frequency representations for multiple instruments through spectral cross-attention and temporal coherence modeling. By incorporating random-mixing augmentation and multi-task learning across instrument classes and vocals, the model achieves state-of-the-art performance on several public datasets, particularly excelling at discriminating between instrument sources and transcribing less common instruments.

## Method Summary
Perceiver TF augments the Perceiver architecture by introducing a hierarchical expansion with an additional Transformer layer to model temporal coherence across latent arrays. The model processes log-magnitude spectrograms through a convolutional module for local feature aggregation, then employs a Perceiver TF module containing spectral cross-attention to project spectral features into latent arrays, latent Transformers for self-attention on these arrays, and temporal Transformers for modeling coherence across time steps. The output module uses bidirectional GRUs to generate piano-roll activations. The model is trained with a multi-task loss across 12 instrument classes and vocals, enhanced by random-mixing augmentation that exposes the model to diverse instrument combinations during training.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on multi-instrument transcription with higher onset F1 scores across Slakh2100 dataset
- Achieves improved vocal transcription performance on MIR-ST500 compared to existing methods
- Demonstrates superior performance for less common instruments through effective discrimination enabled by random-mixing augmentation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The additional Transformer layer in Perceiver TF models temporal coherence across latent arrays from different time steps, enabling better modeling of temporal dependencies in multitrack music transcription.
- Mechanism: The Temporal Transformer module applies standard self-attention across sequences of latent arrays [θh0(k), θh1(k), ..., θh(T-1)(k)] for each of the K parallel Transformers, with trainable positional embeddings added during initialization to inform time positions.
- Core assumption: Temporal coherence between latent arrays at different time steps is crucial for accurate transcription and can be effectively captured through self-attention mechanisms.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Perceiver TF augments the Perceiver architecture by introducing a hierarchical expansion with an additional Transformer layer to model temporal coherence."
  - [section 3.2]: "The temporal Transformer module is placed to enable the communication between any pairs of Θh_t of different time steps... We add a trainable positional embedding to each Θ0_t during the initialization."
- Break condition: If the temporal relationships between frames are not significant for the transcription task, or if the positional embeddings do not effectively encode temporal information, the additional computational complexity of the temporal Transformer may not provide benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The spectral cross-attention module efficiently projects high-resolution spectral input into a compressed latent bottleneck, enabling scalable processing of time-frequency representations.
- Mechanism: The SCA module maps input spectral representation St into Key and Value matrices, while a latent array serves as Query matrix, performing QKV attention to project spectral features into latent arrays for each time step.
- Core assumption: Spectral features at each time step can be effectively compressed into a smaller latent representation without losing critical pitch and timbral information needed for transcription.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Perceiver TF augments the Perceiver architecture by introducing a hierarchical expansion with an additional Transformer layer to model temporal coherence."
  - [section 3.2]: "The spectral cross-attention (SCA) module operates directly on an input spectral representation St and projects it into the Key (K) and Value (V) matrices... This Θh_t plays an important role of carrying the spectral information from the first Perceiver TF block throughout the entire stack of blocks."
- Break condition: If the compression ratio is too high, critical spectral details may be lost. If the latent dimension K is not appropriately sized relative to F, the model may fail to capture important spectral patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Random-mixing augmentation significantly improves instrument discrimination by exposing the model to diverse instrument combinations during training.
- Mechanism: During training, instrument stems from different samples are randomly mixed together with specified probabilities (p=25% for multi-track, q=50% for vocal-mixture), forcing the model to learn instrument-specific features rather than relying on contextual cues from fixed instrument combinations.
- Core assumption: The model's ability to discriminate between instruments improves when trained on varied combinations of instruments rather than fixed, realistic mixes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "To address instrument discrimination, we adopt the random-mixing augmentation technique learned from music source separation (MSS)... This multi-task design along with the random-mixing technique allows more flexibility to train with enormous amount of augmented training samples."
  - [section 4.2]: "Each training sample is excerpted from a random moment of its original song... For a training sample si from a multi-track dataset, we denote its instrumentation template as μi ⊆ Ω... Then, for each instrument ωj in μi, it has a p% chance to be replaced by a ωj in μu, where i ≠ u."
- Break condition: If the mixing introduces unrealistic instrument combinations that never occur in real music, the model may learn to recognize these artificial patterns rather than genuine instrument characteristics.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Spectrogram representation and its time-frequency structure
  - Why needed here: The model operates directly on log-magnitude spectrograms (S ∈ ℝT×F×C), so understanding how spectral content relates to musical notes and instruments is essential for interpreting model behavior and debugging.
  - Quick check question: How does changing the window size and hop length affect the time-frequency resolution of the spectrogram, and what implications does this have for the model's ability to capture note onsets and pitches?

- Concept: Transformer self-attention mechanisms and their complexity
  - Why needed here: The paper relies heavily on Transformer modules with different attention patterns (cross-attention, self-attention, temporal attention), each with different computational complexities that affect scalability and training efficiency.
  - Quick check question: What is the computational complexity of the spectral cross-attention compared to the temporal Transformer, and how does this difference enable the model to handle more instruments simultaneously?

- Concept: Multi-task learning and loss computation
  - Why needed here: The model is trained with a multi-task loss function that computes binary cross-entropy for both onset and frame activations across J instruments, requiring understanding of how partial labels and masked outputs are handled.
  - Quick check question: How does the loss function handle cases where some instruments are not present in a training sample, and what is the impact of this design choice on model performance for rare instruments?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Audio waveform -> Spectrogram -> Convolutional Module -> Perceiver TF (SCA → Latent Transformer → Temporal Transformer) -> Output Module (GRU) -> Transcription prediction

- Critical path: Spectrogram → Convolutional Module → Perceiver TF (SCA → Latent Transformer → Temporal Transformer) → Output Module → Transcription prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Scalability vs. accuracy: Perceiver TF trades some direct spectral modeling (O(F²) in SpecTNT) for computational efficiency (O(FK) where K≪F), enabling larger instrument sets
  - Model complexity vs. data efficiency: The multi-task approach with random-mixing augmentation requires careful data management but improves generalization across instruments
  - Fixed vs. adaptive architecture: The hierarchical expansion with fixed latent dimensions provides stable training but may limit adaptability to very different instrument characteristics

- Failure signatures:
  - Instrument confusion (e.g., piano detecting string notes): Likely indicates insufficient discrimination in latent space or inadequate random-mixing augmentation
  - Poor performance on rare instruments: May indicate class imbalance issues or insufficient exposure during random-mixing
  - Slow inference on complex pieces: Could indicate temporal Transformer bottleneck or inefficient output module processing
  - Overfitting to training set: May suggest insufficient augmentation or model capacity that's too large for available data

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Remove the temporal Transformer to verify its contribution to performance, measuring the degradation in onset F1 scores
  2. Random-mixing sensitivity: Train with different mixing probabilities (p, q) to find optimal values for instrument discrimination improvement
  3. Latent dimension scaling: Vary the latent array dimension K to quantify the tradeoff between computational efficiency and transcription accuracy across different instrument sets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the discussion and limitations, several areas for future research are implied, particularly regarding the generalizability of Perceiver TF to other audio-related tasks and the exploration of different numbers of instruments in the model.

## Limitations
- The computational complexity analysis comparing Perceiver TF to SpecTNT focuses on asymptotic behavior but doesn't provide empirical runtime comparisons across different instrument set sizes
- The multi-task learning framework's effectiveness for rare instruments is demonstrated through overall F1 scores, but individual instrument performance metrics and class imbalance analysis are not provided
- The claims about improved instrument discrimination through random-mixing augmentation lack detailed ablation studies showing the technique's isolated impact on performance

## Confidence
High confidence: The architectural description of Perceiver TF, including the spectral cross-attention and temporal Transformer modules, is well-specified with clear mathematical formulations. The implementation details for the convolutional module and output GRU layers are explicitly stated.

Medium confidence: The random-mixing augmentation technique's implementation details and its impact on instrument discrimination require further clarification. The loss function formulation for handling multi-task learning across instruments with partial labels needs more detailed specification.

Low confidence: The comparative analysis against state-of-the-art methods doesn't include statistical significance testing of performance differences. The paper claims computational efficiency but lacks empirical evidence comparing training/inference times across different model architectures.
