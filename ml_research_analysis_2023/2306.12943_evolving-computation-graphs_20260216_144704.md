# Evolving Computation Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12943
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12943
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: ECG improves GNN performance on heterophilic datasets, with gains up to 10.84% for GCN

## Executive Summary
Evolving Computation Graphs (ECG) is a method that enhances graph neural networks on heterophilic datasets by constructing a complementary graph that connects nodes with similar embeddings from weak classifiers. The method runs GNN layers in parallel over both the original graph and the ECG graph, then combines their outputs. Experiments on 5 heterophilic datasets show ECG improves GNN performance in 19 out of 20 cases, with particularly strong gains for GCNs (up to 10.84%). The ECG construction is crucial as it provides more homophilic connections than the original graph.

## Method Summary
ECG builds a complementary graph by using weak classifiers (MLP or BGRL) to generate node embeddings, computing cosine similarities between these embeddings, and connecting nodes through k-nearest neighbor edges. During training, two GNN layers run in parallel - one on the original graph and one on the ECG graph. Their outputs are combined via concatenation and a learned weight matrix. The weak classifiers are trained separately without graph structure or labels, making them "weak" in terms of graph-specific inductive biases.

## Key Results
- ECG improves GNN performance in 19 out of 20 experiments across 5 heterophilic datasets
- GCNs show the largest improvements, with gains up to 10.84% absolute accuracy
- The ECG graph construction is crucial, providing more homophilic connections than the original graph
- Both MLP and BGRL weak classifiers are effective for ECG graph construction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Parallel message passing over both original and ECG graphs enables simultaneous exploitation of homophilic and heterophilic edges. The model performs two separate GNN propagations per layer, combining outputs via learned weights. Core assumption: homophily improves GNN performance. Evidence: [abstract] ECG runs layers in parallel over original and ECG graphs. Break condition: If weak classifier produces poor embeddings, ECG graph may introduce noise.

### Mechanism 2
Weak classifiers (MLP or BGRL) generate embeddings that identify nodes likely to share labels without overfitting to graph structure. MLP uses features alone; BGRL uses neighborhood structure without labels. These embeddings compute cosine similarities for ECG graph construction. Core assumption: weak classifiers can generate label-relevant representations without graph structure. Evidence: [section 2] Weak classifiers not exposed to graph structure biases. Break condition: If features lack meaningful patterns, embeddings become uninformative.

### Mechanism 3
ECG graph construction creates more homophilic edges than original graph, improving overall graph homophily statistics. k-nearest neighbor selection based on weak classifier embeddings preferentially connects similar nodes. Core assumption: nodes with similar embeddings are more likely to share labels. Evidence: [abstract] ECG provides more homophilic connections than original graph. Break condition: If original graph already has high homophily, ECG may not help.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input graph (X, A) → Weak classifier (MLP or BGRL) → Node embeddings → Cosine similarity matrix → k-NN selection → ECG graph (V, E_ECG) → Parallel GNN propagation (original + ECG) → Combined representations → Output predictions

- **Critical path**: Weak classifier embeddings computation → ECG graph construction → Parallel message passing through both graphs

- **Design tradeoffs**: MLP vs BGRL affects whether ECG graph emphasizes feature-based or structure-based homophily; k-NN parameter k balances graph density and homophily; DropEdge probability trades off noise reduction against information preservation

- **Failure signatures**: Performance degradation when weak classifier embeddings poorly correlate with labels; minimal improvement when original graph already has high homophily; overfitting when k is too large

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does ECG perform on heterophilic datasets with different types of weak classifiers beyond MLP and BGRL? The paper mentions that "the nature of the weak classifier employed in ECG is flexible" and that other self-supervised embedding methods could be explored.

- **Open Question 2**: How does ECG compare to specialized heterophily GNN architectures when combined with them? The paper states that "while this method enhances the performance of standard graph neural networks, it can also be applied to specialized architectures specifically designed to improve performance on heterophilic data."

- **Open Question 3**: How does the performance of ECG vary with different values of k (number of nearest neighbors) for different datasets and GNN architectures? The paper only reports the best-performing values of k but doesn't explore performance across a range of k values.

## Limitations

- Performance gains rely heavily on weak classifiers generating meaningful embeddings, but evaluation doesn't systematically analyze when embeddings fail
- ResNet architecture for MLP weak classifiers lacks complete specification
- Computational overhead of maintaining two parallel GNN streams is not discussed

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of parallel message passing over original and ECG graphs
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of weak classifiers (MLP/BGRL) for ECG graph construction
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that ECG graphs consistently provide more homophilic edges
