# Trainable Projected Gradient Method for Robust Fine-tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10720
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10720
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TPGM significantly improves out-of-distribution robustness while matching or surpassing in-distribution performance, showing 22% and 10% relative OOD improvement on sketch counterparts of DomainNet-Real and ImageNet datasets respectively

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes TPGM (Trainable Projected Gradient Method), a novel approach for fine-tuning pre-trained models that automatically learns per-layer projection constraints. The method formulates fine-tuning as a bi-level constrained optimization problem, using projected gradient methods with trainable projection radii to enforce constraints between the fine-tuned model and pre-trained model. TPGM demonstrates significant improvements in out-of-distribution robustness while maintaining strong in-distribution performance across multiple large-scale datasets and architectures.

## Method Summary
TPGM formulates fine-tuning as a bi-level constrained optimization problem where the inner level optimizes model weights on training data and the outer level optimizes projection radii (constraints) on validation data. The method maintains a set of projection radii γ for each layer, representing the maximum allowed distance between the pre-trained model θ₀ and the current fine-tuned model θₜ. During fine-tuning, it projects the model weights back into these constraints whenever they exceed the allowed distance, using either L2 or MARS projection operators. The projection radii are updated through the outer minimization loop using a separate validation set, allowing the algorithm to learn optimal per-layer constraints that balance fitting the training data and preserving generalization capability.

## Key Results
- TPGM outperforms existing fine-tuning methods in OOD performance while matching the best in-distribution (ID) performance
- Shows 22% and 10% relative OOD improvement compared to vanilla fine-tuning on sketch counterparts of DomainNet-Real and ImageNet datasets respectively
- Learned projection radii reveal that lower layers require stronger regularization while higher layers need more flexibility, aligning with common beliefs about feature learning in neural networks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: TPGM improves out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness by automatically learning per-layer distance constraints between the fine-tuned model and the pre-trained model.
- **Mechanism**: TPGM maintains a set of projection radii γ for each layer, representing the maximum allowed distance between the pre-trained model θ₀ and the current fine-tuned model θₜ. During fine-tuning, it projects the model weights back into these constraints whenever they exceed the allowed distance. The projection radii are updated through a bi-level optimization process that optimizes the radii on a validation set while keeping the model weights fixed, allowing the method to learn optimal per-layer constraints.
- **Core assumption**: Different layers in a neural network require different amounts of regularization during fine-tuning, with lower layers needing stronger constraints to preserve general features and higher layers requiring more flexibility to adapt to the target task.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "TPGM outperforms existing fine-tuning methods in OOD performance while matching the best in-distribution (ID) performance. For example, when fine-tuned on DomainNet-Real and ImageNet, compared to vanilla fine-tuning, TPGM shows 22% and 10% relative OOD improvement respectively on their sketch counterparts."
  - [section] "The learned projection radii reveal that lower layers require stronger regularization while higher layers need more flexibility, aligning with the common belief that lower layers learn more general features."
- **Break condition**: If the assumption that lower layers learn more general features while higher layers specialize is incorrect, or if the pre-trained model doesn't have this property, the per-layer constraint learning would not provide the expected benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The bi-level optimization formulation is key to learning different constraints for each layer.
- **Mechanism**: The bi-level optimization framework separates the model weight update (inner problem) from the constraint learning (outer problem). The inner problem minimizes the training loss with respect to model weights θₜ, while the outer problem optimizes the projection radii γ on a validation set. This separation allows the algorithm to learn optimal constraints that balance fitting the training data and preserving generalization capability without contaminating the validation set with gradient updates to the model weights.
- **Core assumption**: Optimizing projection radii on a dataset separate from the training data (validation set) is essential for learning different constraints for each layer that generalize well.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "We theoretically show that the bi-level optimization design is the key for TPGM to learn different constraints for each layer in Sec. 3.4."
  - [section] "Furthermore, as prior works have found that lower layers tend to learn more general features while higher layers specialize to a specific dataset, ϵ is likely to be smaller for the lower layers and larger for higher layers because pre-trained models likely have learned very good low-level general features. This offers one explanation of why TPGM automatically learns different constraints for each layer."
- **Break condition**: If the validation set is too small or not representative of the true data distribution, the learned constraints may not generalize well. Also, if the outer optimization doesn't properly balance the trade-off between fitting and generalization, it may lead to underfitting or overfitting.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: TPGM automatically adjusts regularization strength based on the size of the fine-tuning dataset.
- **Mechanism**: When fine-tuning with less data, TPGM learns smaller projection radii, which project the fine-tuned model closer to the pre-trained model. This prevents overfitting to the small dataset by relying more on the pre-trained model's features. The algorithm automatically detects when stronger regularization is needed based on the validation performance.
- **Core assumption**: With fewer training samples, the risk of overfitting increases, requiring stronger regularization to preserve generalization.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "TPGM also needs to adjust to different regularization strengths according to the size of the training set. TPGM can avoid overfitting to a small fine-tuning dataset through the outer minimization loop of the projection parameters on validation data."
  - [section] "We observe that 1) lower layers have smaller constraints and higher layers have larger constraints, meaning more freedom to grow, and 2) with only 10% training data, the learned constraints are much smaller than those trained with 100%."
- **Break condition**: If the validation set is not representative of the training data distribution, the algorithm might learn inappropriate constraint strengths. Also, if the projection update frequency is not properly tuned, it might lead to either too conservative or too aggressive regularization.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Bi-level optimization**
  - Why needed here: The paper formulates fine-tuning as a bi-level constrained optimization problem where the inner level optimizes model weights on training data, and the outer level optimizes the projection radii (constraints) on validation data. This formulation allows learning optimal per-layer constraints that balance fitting and generalization.
  - Quick check question: In a bi-level optimization problem, which variables are optimized in the inner level and which in the outer level?

- **Concept: Projected gradient method**
  - Why needed here: TPGM uses projected gradient descent to enforce the learned constraints during fine-tuning. After each gradient update, if the model weights exceed the allowed distance from the pre-trained model (defined by the projection radii), they are projected back onto the constraint set.
  - Quick check question: What is the purpose of the projection step in projected gradient descent, and how does it differ from regular gradient descent?

- **Concept: Matrix norms and distance metrics**
  - Why needed here: The paper uses matrix norms (like L2 norm and MARS norm) to measure the distance between the fine-tuned model and the pre-trained model. These norms define the constraint sets that the projected gradient method enforces.
  - Quick check question: Why might different matrix norms (L2 vs MARS) be used to measure the distance between model weights, and how could this choice affect the fine-tuning results?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Main model -> Projection radii γ -> Projection operator -> Model optimizer -> Projection optimizer -> Training loop
- **Critical path**: The critical path is the sequence of operations that must complete for each training step: (1) compute gradients and update model weights, (2) periodically update projection radii using validation data, (3) apply projection to enforce constraints. The frequency of projection updates (controlled by fproj) is a key hyperparameter that affects both performance and computational cost.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - L2 projection vs MARS projection: L2 projection uses Euclidean distance and has a simple closed-form solution, while MARS projection uses a different norm that may better capture the structure of weight matrices. The choice affects both performance and computational efficiency.
  - Frequency of projection updates: More frequent updates (smaller fproj) allow faster adaptation of constraints but increase computational cost. Less frequent updates reduce overhead but may lead to suboptimal constraints.
  - Validation set usage: Using a separate validation set for constraint learning prevents contamination but requires additional data. The size and quality of this validation set directly impact the learned constraints.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Underfitting: If projection radii are too small or updated too aggressively, the model may not fit the training data well, resulting in lower training accuracy and potentially lower validation accuracy.
  - Overfitting: If projection radii are too large or not updated enough, the model may overfit to the training data, showing high training accuracy but poor validation and OOD performance.
  - Poor OOD performance: If the learned constraints don't properly preserve the generalization capability of the pre-trained model, OOD performance may suffer even if ID performance is good.
  - Computational overhead: Frequent projection updates can significantly increase training time, especially for large models with many layers.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline comparison**: Implement TPGM with L2 projection on a simple dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10) using a small ResNet architecture. Compare against vanilla fine-tuning and L2-SP to verify that TPGM improves OOD robustness while maintaining ID performance.
  2. **Projection type comparison**: Run the same experiment using both L2 and MARS projections to determine which works better for the chosen architecture and dataset. This helps understand the impact of the projection operator choice.
  3. **Frequency sensitivity**: Vary the projection update frequency (fproj) from 1 (update every step) to T-1 (update only at the end) to find the optimal balance between performance and computational cost for the target architecture.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does TPGM's per-layer constraint learning compare to adaptive methods like Layer-wise Adaptive Rate Scaling (LARS) in terms of final model performance and training stability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that TPGM automatically learns per-layer constraints while existing methods use manual heuristics or expensive hyperparameter searches, and that TPGM outperforms existing approaches with little hyperparameter tuning cost.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates TPGM's effectiveness, it doesn't directly compare against adaptive optimization methods that also adjust per-layer learning rates/constraints.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing TPGM against LARS and similar adaptive methods on the same datasets and architectures, measuring both OOD robustness and training stability metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the learned projection radii from TPGM be transferred across different downstream tasks to reduce the need for task-specific fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that TPGM learns interpretable patterns about which layers need more/less regularization, and that lower layers generally require stronger constraints while higher layers need more flexibility, which aligns with common beliefs about feature learning in neural networks.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates TPGM's effectiveness within individual task fine-tuning scenarios, not whether the learned constraints have transferable properties across tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing whether projection radii learned on one task can be effectively transferred and reused for fine-tuning on different but related downstream tasks, and how this compares to learning from scratch.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does TPGM's performance scale with model size, particularly for extremely large models like GPT-3 or PaLM where fine-tuning is computationally prohibitive?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that searching hyperparameters becomes increasingly infeasible for larger datasets and neural networks, with a ViT-base having 154 trainable parameter groups leading to more than 10^45 combinations even with binary choices.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates TPGM on ResNet and ViT architectures, it doesn't test on the extremely large models where the computational benefits of automatic constraint learning would be most valuable.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments scaling TPGM to models with hundreds of layers or billions of parameters, measuring both performance and computational overhead compared to alternative fine-tuning approaches.

## Limitations
- Computational overhead from frequent projection updates is not thoroughly quantified, making it unclear how TPGM scales to very large models
- Results are primarily demonstrated on ResNet and ViT architectures, with limited exploration of how well the approach generalizes to other model families
- The theoretical justification for why learned projection radii align with the belief that lower layers learn more general features is based on observed patterns rather than deep theoretical understanding

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The bi-level optimization framework and its role in learning per-layer constraints is well-established and theoretically justified.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that TPGM improves OOD robustness while maintaining ID performance is supported by experiments, but the magnitude of improvements may vary across different datasets and architectures not covered in the paper.
- **Low confidence**: The assertion that the learned projection radii align with the belief that lower layers learn more general features is based on observed patterns but lacks deeper theoretical justification for why this alignment occurs.

## Next Checks
1. **Architecture generalization test**: Apply TPGM to non-standard architectures like ConvNext or Swin Transformers on diverse datasets (e.g., medical imaging, speech) to validate if the per-layer constraint learning mechanism generalizes beyond ResNet and ViT.

2. **Computational overhead benchmark**: Implement TPGM with different projection update frequencies and measure wall-clock training time compared to vanilla fine-tuning and L2-SP across multiple GPU configurations to quantify the practical computational cost.

3. **Constraint sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the initialization of projection radii and the projection update frequency to determine how sensitive TPGM's performance is to these hyperparameters, particularly for small fine-tuning datasets where regularization is most critical.
