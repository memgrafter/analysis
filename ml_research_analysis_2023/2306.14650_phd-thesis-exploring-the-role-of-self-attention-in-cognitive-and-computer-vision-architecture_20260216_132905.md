# PhD Thesis: Exploring the role of (self-)attention in cognitive and computer vision architecture

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14650
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14650
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Self-attention mechanisms can effectively solve complex visual reasoning tasks, though sample-efficient learnability limitations require further investigation.

## Executive Summary
This thesis investigates the role of self-attention mechanisms in both cognitive and computer vision architectures, specifically examining their effectiveness in solving complex visual reasoning tasks. The research demonstrates that self-attention serves as an effective computational model for visual attention systems, representing both spatial and feature-based attention, while also functioning as a model for active vision. The work establishes that self-attention performs comparably to other vision-related mechanisms in reasoning tasks, validating its potential utility in architectural design. However, the thesis also identifies significant limitations regarding sample-efficient learnability that necessitate additional research to understand the fundamental mechanisms at play.

## Method Summary
The thesis employs a comprehensive analytical approach to examine self-attention mechanisms across cognitive and computer vision architectures. The methodology involves evaluating self-attention's performance on complex visual reasoning tasks while comparing it to alternative mechanisms. The research investigates self-attention's role as both a computational model for visual attention systems (representing spatial and feature-based attention) and as a model for active vision. Through systematic analysis of various architectural configurations and task complexities, the study assesses self-attention's effectiveness and identifies limitations in its learning efficiency for reasoning tasks.

## Key Results
- Self-attention mechanisms are as effective as other vision-related mechanisms in solving complex visual reasoning tasks
- Self-attention successfully models visual attention systems, representing both spatial and feature-based attention
- The thesis identifies critical limitations in self-attention's sample-efficient learnability for reasoning tasks that require further investigation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Self-attention mechanisms work effectively for visual reasoning tasks by enabling the model to dynamically focus on relevant spatial regions and feature dimensions while processing visual information. The mechanism allows for parallel computation across all positions in the input, creating rich interactions between different parts of the visual scene. This capability mirrors human visual attention systems, where attention can be directed both spatially (where to look) and feature-based (what to look for). The self-attention mechanism's ability to capture long-range dependencies and contextual relationships makes it particularly suitable for reasoning tasks that require understanding complex visual relationships and patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Input Visual Features -> Self-Attention Layer -> Reasoning Module -> Output Prediction

Critical Path:
The critical path flows from input visual features through the self-attention layer, where spatial and feature relationships are computed, to the reasoning module that interprets these attended features for task-specific predictions.

Design Tradeoffs:
The primary tradeoff involves balancing the computational complexity of full self-attention (quadratic in sequence length) against its reasoning capabilities. Alternative attention mechanisms may offer better sample efficiency but potentially reduced reasoning performance. The architecture must also balance between spatial attention (where to look) and feature attention (what to look for).

Failure Signatures:
Performance degradation typically manifests as inability to capture long-range dependencies, confusion in multi-object scenarios, or failure to generalize across spatial transformations. Sample efficiency issues appear as slow convergence or poor performance with limited training data.

First Experiments:
1. Compare self-attention performance against non-attention baselines on simple visual reasoning tasks
2. Test different attention head configurations (number and dimension) on reasoning accuracy
3. Evaluate sample efficiency by training on progressively smaller datasets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The thesis explicitly identifies the need for additional analysis to determine the fundamental mechanisms in full self-attention models that restrict sample-efficient learnability for reasoning tasks. The specific aspects of self-attention that cause these limitations remain unclear and require further investigation to enable more efficient learning in reasoning applications.

## Limitations
- The fundamental mechanisms limiting self-attention's sample-efficient learnability for reasoning tasks are not fully elucidated
- The analysis does not provide definitive answers about specific architectural components causing learning efficiency issues
- Further research is needed to understand how to overcome the identified sample efficiency limitations while maintaining reasoning performance
