# Stuttering Detection Using Speaker Representations and Self-supervised Contextual Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00689
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00689
- Reference count: 5
- Proposed method achieves relative improvements of 12.08%, 28.71%, and 37.9% in UAR over baseline classifiers

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of stuttering detection by leveraging self-supervised contextual embeddings extracted from pre-trained deep learning models. The authors propose a novel approach that combines speaker embeddings from ECAPA-TDNN with Wav2Vec2.0 contextual embeddings for stuttering detection. They benchmark several traditional classifiers, including K-nearest neighbor (KNN), Gaussian naive Bayes, and neural networks, on the SEP-28k dataset. The proposed method demonstrates significant improvements over baseline methods, achieving a relative improvement of 12.08%, 28.71%, and 37.9% in terms of unweighted average recall (UAR). Additionally, the authors show that combining the two embeddings and concatenating multiple layers of Wav2Vec2.0 can further enhance the UAR by up to 2.60% and 6.32%, respectively.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a stuttering detection system that utilizes speaker embeddings from ECAPA-TDNN and self-supervised contextual embeddings from Wav2Vec2.0. They extract these embeddings from the SEP-28k dataset, which consists of 23,573 annotated speech segments from 385 podcasts. The extracted embeddings are then used to train and evaluate several traditional classifiers, including KNN, Gaussian naive Bayes, and neural networks. The authors also explore the combination of ECAPA-TDNN and Wav2Vec2.0 embeddings through score and embedding fusion, as well as the concatenation of multiple layers of Wav2Vec2.0. Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) is applied for dimensionality reduction before classification. The performance of the proposed method is evaluated using UAR and accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- Proposed method achieves relative improvements of 12.08%, 28.71%, and 37.9% in UAR over baseline classifiers.
- Combining ECAPA-TDNN and Wav2Vec2.0 embeddings via score and embedding fusion further improves UAR by up to 2.60%.
- Concatenating multiple layers of Wav2Vec2.0 enhances UAR by up to 6.32%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method works by leveraging the rich representations learned by pre-trained deep learning models, such as ECAPA-TDNN and Wav2Vec2.0, for stuttering detection. ECAPA-TDNN captures speaker-specific characteristics, while Wav2Vec2.0 provides contextual information about the speech signal. By combining these embeddings, the system can effectively capture both speaker-related and speech-related features that are crucial for stuttering detection. The use of self-supervised learning allows the models to learn meaningful representations without the need for extensive labeled stuttering data.

## Foundational Learning
- Self-supervised learning: Learning representations from unlabeled data by solving pretext tasks. Needed to leverage large amounts of unlabeled speech data for pre-training. Quick check: Ensure the pretext task is relevant to the downstream stuttering detection task.
- Speaker embeddings: Compact representations that capture speaker-specific characteristics. Needed to distinguish between different speakers and account for speaker variability in stuttering detection. Quick check: Verify the quality of speaker embeddings using speaker verification tasks.
- Contextual embeddings: Embeddings that capture the context and relationships between different parts of the speech signal. Needed to model the temporal dynamics and patterns associated with stuttering events. Quick check: Analyze the embeddings' ability to capture stuttering-related patterns using visualization techniques.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: SEP-28k dataset -> ECAPA-TDNN and Wav2Vec2.0 embeddings -> LDA dimensionality reduction -> Classifier (KNN, Gaussian naive Bayes, or neural network) -> Stuttering detection output
- Critical path: The most critical components are the pre-trained ECAPA-TDNN and Wav2Vec2.0 models, as they provide the rich embeddings that drive the stuttering detection performance. The choice of classifier and the fusion strategy also play important roles in the overall system performance.
- Design tradeoffs: The use of pre-trained models allows for leveraging large amounts of unlabeled data but may introduce domain mismatch between the pre-training and downstream tasks. The choice of classifier involves a tradeoff between model complexity and interpretability. Fusion strategies need to balance the contributions of different embeddings effectively.
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on minority classes (repetitions, blocks, interjections) may indicate class imbalance issues. Overfitting due to limited dataset size can be identified by monitoring validation loss. Performance degradation in cross-lingual setups may suggest the need for domain adaptation techniques.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What is the impact of using stuttering labeled datasets for pre-training the pretext task in self-supervised learning frameworks for stuttering detection? The authors suggest exploring the use of stuttering labeled datasets for pre-training to mitigate the audio domain mismatch between pre-text and downstream tasks.
- How does the proposed system perform in cross-lingual setups, where the model is trained on one stuttering language and tested on another? The authors acknowledge the possibility of failure in cross-lingual setups and suggest analyzing performance in such scenarios.
- What are the effects of speaker-adaptive training and domain adaptation techniques on stuttering detection systems? The authors mention the potential of these techniques to enhance stuttering detection systems by learning meta-data invariant features.

## Limitations
- The specific train, validation, and test set splits for the SEP-28k dataset are not provided, which may affect the reproducibility of the results.
- The exact hyperparameters and configurations used for the ECAPA-TDNN and Wav2Vec2.0 models are not specified, making it challenging to replicate the study precisely.
- The generalizability of the results to other stuttering detection datasets and real-world scenarios is uncertain due to the limited evaluation on a single dataset.

## Confidence

- Low confidence: The generalizability of the results to other stuttering detection datasets and real-world scenarios.
