# Bridging the Gap between Decision and Logits in Decision-based Knowledge Distillation for Pre-trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08909
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08909
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: Proposed method achieves significant improvements over strong baselines in NLU and MRC tasks, with comparable results to standard KD under certain settings

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of decision-based knowledge distillation for pre-trained language models where only teacher decisions (top-1 labels) are accessible. The authors propose a novel method that bridges the gap between teacher decisions and logits by estimating logits from decision distributions using test-time data augmentation and non-centered orthant probability estimation. The approach involves solving a root-finding problem to recover logit information, enabling effective knowledge transfer even when full teacher logits are unavailable. Extensive experiments on multiple NLU and MRC tasks demonstrate significant improvements over strong baselines.

## Method Summary
The proposed method estimates logits from teacher decision distributions through a combination of test-time data augmentation and non-centered orthant probability estimation. For each training sample, multiple augmented versions are generated using EDA (Easy Data Augmentation), and the teacher model provides decisions for each. These decisions are used to estimate the empirical decision distribution P(Y|x), which is then combined with a theoretical estimation using non-centered orthant probability with assumed Gaussian logits distribution. The root-finding problem is solved to obtain estimated logits, which are then used to train the student model with KL divergence loss between the softmax probabilities of estimated and student logits.

## Key Results
- Significant improvements over strong baselines in both NLU and MRC tasks
- Achieved comparable results to standard KD under certain settings
- Demonstrated effectiveness across diverse datasets including RACE, DREAM, SST-2, CoLA, MRPC, QQP, RTE, MNLI, and QNLI

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by bridging the information gap between discrete teacher decisions and continuous logits. By generating multiple augmented samples and observing their collective decisions, the method can estimate the underlying decision distribution. This distribution, combined with the assumption of Gaussian logits, allows for the recovery of logit information through non-centered orthant probability estimation. The root-finding approach effectively inverts the softmax function to recover the original logits scale, preserving the rich information that would otherwise be lost when only decisions are available.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Decision-based Knowledge Distillation**: A framework where only teacher decisions (top-1 predictions) are accessible rather than full logits
   - Why needed: Many practical scenarios only provide access to teacher predictions due to privacy or computational constraints
   - Quick check: Verify that teacher predictions are available but not full logits

2. **Test-time Data Augmentation**: Generating multiple variations of input samples during inference/training
   - Why needed: Increases the sample size to better estimate decision distributions
   - Quick check: Confirm augmentation techniques (synonym replacement, random insertion, etc.) are applied consistently

3. **Non-centered Orthant Probability**: Mathematical technique for estimating probabilities in multivariate normal distributions
   - Why needed: Enables theoretical estimation of logits from decision distributions under Gaussian assumptions
   - Quick check: Validate the recursive algorithm implementation from Miwa et al. (2003)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Augmentation -> Decision Distribution Estimation -> Orthant Probability Calculation -> Root-Finding -> Student Training

**Critical Path**: EDA augmentation → Teacher decision collection → Empirical distribution estimation → Theoretical logit estimation → KL divergence training

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- More augmented samples (larger N) improve estimation accuracy but increase computational cost
- Tighter σ assumptions improve precision but may reduce robustness to model uncertainty
- The method trades computational efficiency for the ability to work with only teacher decisions

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Performance plateaus similar to hard label training indicate poor logits estimation
- Excessive variance across runs suggests instability in the root-finding or orthant probability calculations

**3 First Experiments**:
1. Validate augmentation effectiveness by comparing decision distributions with and without augmentation
2. Test orthant probability calculation accuracy on synthetic Gaussian data with known parameters
3. Perform ablation on N (number of augmented samples) to find optimal trade-off between accuracy and efficiency
