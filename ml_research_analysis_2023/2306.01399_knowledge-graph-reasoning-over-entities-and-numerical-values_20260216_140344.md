# Knowledge Graph Reasoning over Entities and Numerical Values

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01399
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01399
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: NRN consistently outperforms baseline methods on numerical complex query answering, achieving up to 2.5% improvement in Hit@1 and 2.4% in MRR.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the task of numerical complex query answering (Numerical CQA) to extend knowledge graph reasoning to include numerical attributes and computations. The key challenge is that existing methods cannot handle numerical comparisons and computations within complex logical queries. To address this, the authors propose the Number Reasoning Network (NRN), which encodes entities and numerical values separately using two distinct phases: entity encoding and numerical encoding. NRN introduces new projections (attribute, reverse attribute, and numerical) to handle numerical reasoning, achieving consistent improvements over baseline methods on three benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
NRN extends knowledge graph reasoning by introducing a two-phase encoding process: entity encoding (using established methods like GQE, Q2B, or Q2P) and numerical encoding (using parameterized distributions). The framework uses three new projection types - attribute projection maps entities to numerical values, reverse attribute projection maps numerical values back to entities, and numerical projection handles computations and comparisons between numerical values. The model is trained by alternating optimization of entity and numerical loss functions, with numerical values encoded as parameterized distributions (e.g., Gaussian) rather than fixed vectors.

## Key Results
- NRN achieves up to 2.5% improvement in Hit@1 compared to baseline methods
- Consistent improvements across all metrics (Hit@1, Hit@3, Hit@10, MRR) on three benchmark datasets
- Demonstrates effectiveness in handling complex queries involving numerical attributes and computations
- Shows NRN can answer queries requiring both relational and numerical reasoning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** NRN achieves better performance by encoding numerical attributes using parameterized distributions instead of treating them as entities.
- **Mechanism:** By using a two-phase encoding process where numerical values are encoded into distributions (e.g., Gaussian) rather than fixed vectors, NRN captures the continuous nature of numerical attributes and their semantic relationships (e.g., greater-than, less-than).
- **Core assumption:** Numerical values have distributional properties that can be captured by parameterized distributions, and these distributions can be learned effectively for reasoning tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "NRN employs a parameterized density function to encode the distribution of numerical values" [section 3.2.4]: "we employ static numerical encoding methods to project the numerical values from the real space ℝ to the d-dimensional real space ℝᵈ... we further assume the intermediate numerical answers are drawn from a multi-variant probabilistic distribution"
- **Break condition:** If numerical attributes don't have meaningful distributions, or if the distribution parameters cannot be learned effectively from the available data, this mechanism would fail.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** NRN's alternating entity and numerical encoding phases allow for more accurate reasoning by switching contexts appropriately.
- **Mechanism:** The computational graph alternates between entity encoding (using established methods like GQE, Q2B, or Q2P) and numerical encoding (using parameterized distributions), allowing each phase to specialize in its domain and avoid conflating entity and numerical reasoning.
- **Core assumption:** Separating entity and numerical reasoning into distinct phases leads to better performance than joint encoding approaches.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1]: "In NRN, there are two encoding phases, which are respectively used for encoding entities and numerical values, following the computation graph" [section 5.4]: Experimental results show NRN consistently outperforms baseline methods that treat numerical values as entities
- **Break condition:** If the computational graph becomes too complex with many alternations, the overhead of switching phases might outweigh the benefits.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** NRN's attribute, reverse attribute, and numerical projections enable reasoning over numerical attributes that baseline methods cannot handle.
- **Mechanism:** New projection operations are introduced to handle numerical reasoning: attribute projection maps entities to their numerical attribute values, reverse attribute projection maps numerical values back to entities, and numerical projection handles computations and comparisons between numerical values.
- **Core assumption:** These new projection types are necessary and sufficient to capture all relevant numerical reasoning patterns in knowledge graphs.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1]: "To compute the computational graph involving numerical attributes, we need to define three types of new projections" [abstract]: "NRN incorporates new projections such as attribute, reverse attribute, and numerical projections to handle numerical reasoning" [section 5.4]: Experimental results show NRN performs better on numerical complex queries
- **Break condition:** If numerical reasoning requires operations beyond what these projections can express, or if the projections are not parameterized effectively, this mechanism would fail.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input Layer (KG with entities, relations, numerical attributes) → Parser (converts queries to computational graphs) → Entity Encoder (GQE/Q2B/Q2P) / Numerical Encoder (parameterized distributions) alternation → Attribute/Reverse Attribute/Numerical Projections → Distribution Layer → Output Layer (ranked predictions)
- **Critical path:** Query → Parser → Entity Encoder/Numerical Encoder alternation → Projections → Distribution combination → Output ranking
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Deterministic vs. probabilistic numerical encoding (DICE vs. Sinusoidal vs. learned distributions)
  - Fixed vs. learned distribution parameters
  - Joint vs. alternating encoding phases
  - Computational overhead of multiple encoding phases vs. accuracy gains
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor performance on numerical queries indicates issues with numerical encoding or projections
  - Slow training/inference suggests inefficiencies in alternating phases
  - Generalization failures indicate problems with distribution learning or query parsing
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Replace NRN's numerical encoder with baseline entity treatment to confirm performance drop
  2. Test different numerical encodings (DICE vs. Sinusoidal) to validate encoding choice
  3. Vary the number of projection operations to find optimal balance between expressiveness and complexity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does NRN handle queries with mixed numerical types (e.g., comparing dates with years, or lengths with areas) that require unit conversion or dimensionality reduction?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that NRN uses parameterized distributions to encode numerical values and that value types are considered during training, but does not address how mixed-unit numerical comparisons are handled.
- Why unresolved: The paper only discusses numerical projections like GreaterThan, SmallerThan, etc., without specifying how different units or dimensions are reconciled during reasoning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing NRN's performance on queries with mixed numerical types, or a detailed description of how unit conversion or dimensionality reduction is implemented.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the computational complexity of NRN compared to baseline methods, and how does it scale with the size of the knowledge graph and the number of numerical attributes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that NRN is slower than baseline methods in terms of training and inference time, but does not provide a detailed complexity analysis or scalability discussion.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides empirical timing results for specific datasets without analyzing the theoretical complexity or discussing how NRN would perform on larger graphs with more numerical attributes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A complexity analysis of NRN's operations (e.g., attribute projection, reverse attribute projection, numerical projection) and empirical results on larger knowledge graphs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is NRN to noisy or missing numerical data in knowledge graphs, and what strategies could be employed to improve its performance in such scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on the performance of NRN on clean benchmark datasets but does not address its behavior when numerical data is noisy or missing.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not discuss how NRN handles uncertainty in numerical attributes, which is a common issue in real-world knowledge graphs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating NRN's performance on knowledge graphs with injected noise or missing numerical values, or a discussion of potential strategies for handling such scenarios (e.g., robust encoding methods, uncertainty quantification).

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can NRN be extended to handle more complex numerical operations, such as arithmetic operations (e.g., addition, subtraction) or logical operations (e.g., AND, OR) on numerical values within queries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that NRN can handle numerical comparisons (e.g., GreaterThan, SmallerThan) and computations (e.g., TimesByTwo) but does not explore more complex numerical operations.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates NRN's ability to handle basic numerical comparisons and simple computations, leaving open the question of its applicability to more complex numerical reasoning tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing NRN's performance on queries involving more complex numerical operations, or a discussion of how NRN could be extended to support such operations.

## Limitations

- The paper claims 2.5% Hit@1 improvement over baselines but lacks ablation studies to show individual contributions of entity vs numerical encoding phases.
- Numerical encoding uses parameterized distributions without sufficient justification for why this approach is superior to simpler alternatives.
- Evaluation focuses on three datasets (FB15k, DB15k, YAGO15k) which may not capture full complexity of real-world numerical reasoning tasks.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core contribution of extending KG reasoning to numerical attributes is novel and well-motivated, given the clear gap in existing methods.
- **Medium confidence**: The proposed NRN architecture and its two-phase encoding approach is technically sound, though empirical validation is limited to three datasets.
- **Medium confidence**: The experimental results showing consistent improvements across metrics are promising but lack detailed ablation studies to isolate the impact of specific design choices.
