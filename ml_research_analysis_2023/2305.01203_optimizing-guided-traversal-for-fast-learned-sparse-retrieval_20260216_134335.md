# Optimizing Guided Traversal for Fast Learned Sparse Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01203
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01203
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Two-level parameterized guidance scheme using BM25+learned weights significantly improves retrieval relevance and efficiency for learned sparse retrieval

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the latency issue in learned sparse retrieval by proposing a two-level parameterized guidance scheme (2GTI) that combines BM25 and learned term weights for dynamic index pruning. The method maintains separate global and local pruning queues with different thresholds to prevent overly aggressive pruning that could drop relevant documents. Evaluations show that 2GTI significantly improves both retrieval relevance and efficiency compared to previous methods, particularly for SPLADE++ when retrieval depth is small or BM25 index is poorly aligned with learned sparse index.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a two-level parameterized guidance scheme that uses a linear combination of BM25 and learned term weights to guide pruning decisions during MaxScore-based traversal. The method maintains three separate queues for global pruning, local pruning, and final ranking, with different thresholds (α, β, γ) controlling each level. When BM25 and learned representations are poorly aligned, the paper introduces scaled alignment filling to reduce sparsity mismatch. The approach is evaluated on MS MARCO passage/document collections and BEIR datasets using various learned sparse representations including SPLADE++, uniCOIL, and DeepImpact.

## Key Results
- 2GTI significantly improves MRR@10 for SPLADE++ when k is small, maintaining relevance while reducing latency
- Scaled alignment filling reduces MRT from 328ms to 31.1ms compared to zero-filling while maintaining MRR@10
- 2GTI outperforms threshold under-estimation methods for controlling BM25 influence, achieving better latency-relevance tradeoffs
- The method can be applied to block-based algorithms like VBMW, achieving competitive performance with MaxScore

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Two-level pruning with BM25+learned weight hybrid scoring allows more aggressive pruning without sacrificing relevance
- Mechanism: Maintains separate queues for global and local pruning scores with different thresholds, creating a safety buffer that preserves documents that might be incorrectly skipped by single-level pruning
- Core assumption: The gap between pruning thresholds (α≠β or β≠γ) creates a safety buffer that preserves relevant documents
- Break condition: If α=β=γ, the two-level distinction disappears and method behaves like standard GTI with no additional pruning safety

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Weighted alignment filling reduces the sparsity mismatch between BM25 and learned sparse representations, improving pruning accuracy
- Mechanism: Uses scaled alignment where missing BM25 weights are estimated using the ratio of mean values of non-zero weights in both models, rather than zero-filling
- Core assumption: Zero weights in BM25 for tokens present in learned representations are too abrupt and cause inaccurate pruning decisions
- Break condition: If BM25 and learned representations are already well-aligned (as with DeepImpact), additional alignment filling provides minimal benefit

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Two-level guidance preserves relevance better than threshold under-estimation for controlling BM25 influence
- Mechanism: Adjusts the β parameter to reduce BM25's influence at the local level while maintaining α=1 at the global level, rather than loosening pruning thresholds multiplicatively
- Core assumption: Gradual reduction of BM25 influence through parameter tuning is more effective than aggressive threshold loosening
- Break condition: If the query set has very short queries where aggressive pruning is less risky, threshold under-estimation might provide sufficient relevance preservation with faster execution

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Query preprocessing -> tokenization and weight computation
  - Index alignment module -> BM25 and learned weight merging with filling strategies
  - Two-level traversal engine -> global pruning (term partitioning) and local pruning (block-wise refinement)
  - Queue management -> three separate queues for different scoring strategies
  - Result aggregation -> final top-k selection

- Critical path:
  1. Query tokenization and weight computation
  2. Index alignment and weight filling
  3. Global pruning with term partitioning
  4. Local pruning with block-wise refinement
  5. Queue updates and threshold management
  6. Final top-k extraction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Parameter configuration (α, β, γ) vs. simplicity of single-level pruning
  - Alignment filling accuracy vs. index size and construction time
  - Two-level control vs. single threshold adjustment for BM25 influence
  - Block-based vs. document-at-a-time traversal for different query lengths

- Failure signatures:
  - Relevance degradation: indicates overly aggressive pruning settings or poor weight alignment
  - Excessive latency: suggests parameters are too conservative or alignment is too complex
  - Memory issues: indicates queue sizes or index structures are too large for available memory
  - Inconsistent performance: suggests parameter sensitivity to query characteristics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run baseline MaxScore vs. 2GTI with different (α, β, γ) configurations on MS MARCO Dev set to observe relevance-latency tradeoffs
  2. Compare zero-filling vs. scaled alignment on a dataset where BM25 and learned representations are poorly aligned
  3. Test 2GTI vs. threshold under-estimation for BM25 influence control on short queries to validate the two-level advantage

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the two-level parameterized guidance scheme (2GTI) compare to other hybrid retrieval methods that combine learned sparse representations with BM25 in terms of relevance and efficiency across different query types?
- Basis in paper: The paper compares 2GTI to GTI and the original MaxScore method, showing improvements in both relevance and efficiency. It also discusses the effectiveness of 2GTI for different query lengths and stop word presence.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on specific learned sparse representations (SPLADE++ and uniCOIL) and doesn't extensively compare 2GTI to other hybrid methods like those combining dense and sparse representations or different learned sparse models.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive comparison of 2GTI against various hybrid retrieval methods across diverse datasets and query types, including dense-sparse hybrids and other learned sparse models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal configuration of the parameters α, β, and γ in the 2GTI scheme for maximizing relevance while minimizing latency across different datasets and query characteristics?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the impact of α and β on global and local pruning, suggesting that α should be close to 1 and β should vary between 0.3 and 1. However, it also notes that the optimal setting can vary depending on the dataset and query characteristics.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides general guidelines but doesn't offer a definitive method for determining the optimal parameter configuration for any given scenario.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study that explores the parameter space of α, β, and γ across various datasets and query types, potentially leading to a rule-based or machine learning approach for parameter selection.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of 2GTI change when applied to other block-based traversal algorithms beyond VBMW, such as Block-Max MaxScore (BMM) or Block-Max WAND (BMW)?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that 2GTI can be used for VBMW and provides some evaluation results. It also states that Block-Max MaxScore is slower than MaxScore under several compression schemes, suggesting potential differences in performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates 2GTI with VBMW and doesn't explore its application to other block-based algorithms or provide a comprehensive comparison of its performance across different traversal methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: An evaluation of 2GTI applied to various block-based traversal algorithms, including BMM and BMW, across different datasets and query types, comparing its performance in terms of relevance, efficiency, and memory usage.

## Limitations

- The method's effectiveness depends heavily on proper parameter tuning (α, β, γ), which may require dataset-specific optimization
- Performance gains are most pronounced when BM25 and learned representations are poorly aligned, suggesting limited benefits for well-aligned representations like DeepImpact
- Computational overhead of maintaining three separate queues and complex weight alignment filling may impact scalability for very large collections

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of two-level pruning (global and local) and its basic effectiveness in preventing overly aggressive pruning
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific weight alignment strategies (zero-filling vs. scaled filling) and their relative effectiveness
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of parameter settings (α, β, γ) across different datasets and query distributions
