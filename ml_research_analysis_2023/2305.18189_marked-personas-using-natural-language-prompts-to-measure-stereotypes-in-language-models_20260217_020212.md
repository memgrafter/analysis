# Marked Personas: Using Natural Language Prompts to Measure Stereotypes in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18189
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18189
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Marked Personas identifies significant stereotype differences between LLM-generated personas for intersectional demographic groups compared to human-written descriptions

## Executive Summary
Marked Personas is a novel prompt-based method for measuring stereotypes in language models without requiring lexicons or data labeling. The approach generates natural language descriptions of individuals from intersectional demographic groups and statistically compares them to unmarked default groups. Using the Fightin' Words method, the framework identifies words that distinguish marked groups from unmarked ones, revealing patterns of othering, exoticizing, and essentializing marginalized populations. The method demonstrates that GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 generate significantly more stereotypes than human-written personas, with intersectional analysis uncovering specific tropes like tropicalism and hypersexualization of minoritized women.

## Method Summary
The Marked Personas framework generates personas for 15 gender-by-race/ethnic groups (Asian, Black, Latine, Middle-Eastern, White x man, woman, nonbinary) using 6 different prompts with GPT-4 and GPT-3.5. Personas are created via OpenAI API with temperature=1 and varying length parameters. The Fightin' Words statistical method identifies words that significantly distinguish marked groups from unmarked default groups (z-score > 1.96). These "marked words" are analyzed for stereotype patterns and compared against human-written personas from Kambhatla et al. (2022). The approach also measures sentiment using VADER and validates results through SVM classification and Jensen-Shannon Divergence comparisons.

## Key Results
- GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 generate more stereotypes than human-written personas when describing intersectional demographic groups
- Marked words distinguishing groups reflect systematic patterns of othering, exoticizing, and essentializing marginalized populations
- Intersectional analysis reveals specific tropes like tropicalism and hypersexualization dominating portrayals of minoritized women

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging statistical differences in language patterns between marked demographic groups and unmarked default groups. By generating comparable persona descriptions across demographics and using the Fightin' Words approach to identify statistically significant distinguishing terms, the framework reveals implicit biases in language models without requiring pre-labeled stereotype data or lexicons. The comparison to human baselines establishes whether model outputs reflect societal stereotypes or introduce new biases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Fightin' Words statistical method**: Needed to identify words that significantly distinguish marked groups from unmarked ones; quick check is whether z-scores exceed 1.96 threshold
- **Intersectional demographic analysis**: Required to understand how multiple identity categories compound stereotype patterns; quick check is ensuring all 15 gender-by-race groups are properly represented
- **Natural language generation parameters**: Temperature=1 ensures diverse outputs while max_tokens/length controls output size; quick check is verifying all 2700 personas are generated
- **VADER sentiment analysis**: Provides quick sentiment scoring for short persona texts; quick check is ensuring proper preprocessing (lowercasing, punctuation removal)
- **Baseline comparison methodology**: Human-written personas serve as reference point; quick check is confirming proper anonymization and text normalization
- **OpenAI API version awareness**: Different model versions may yield different results; quick check is documenting exact API version used

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
OpenAI API -> Persona Generation -> Text Preprocessing -> Fightin' Words Analysis -> Marked Words Identification -> Sentiment Analysis -> Human Baseline Comparison

**Critical Path:**
Persona generation → Fightin' Words statistical analysis → Marked words identification → Intersectional trope analysis

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Using proprietary models limits transparency of bias mitigation vs. ensuring standardized generation
- Statistical significance (z>1.96) vs. practical significance in stereotype detection
- Broad demographic coverage (15 groups) vs. depth of analysis per group
- Automated analysis vs. need for human validation of stereotype patterns

**Failure Signatures:**
- Non-coherent personas suggest prompt or model capability issues
- No significant marked words may indicate improper unmarked group specification or preprocessing errors
- Sentiment analysis failure suggests text normalization problems

**First Experiments:**
1. Generate 2700 personas (90 per group × 15 groups × 2 models) using specified prompts and parameters
2. Apply Fightin' Words method to identify distinguishing words with z-score > 1.96 threshold
3. Compare stereotype lexicon word rates between generated and human-written personas

## Limitations
- Proprietary OpenAI models prevent understanding of built-in bias mitigation techniques
- Analysis limited to 15 gender-by-race/ethnic groups and 6 specific prompts
- VADER sentiment analysis may miss nuanced sentiment expressions in persona descriptions
- Focus on English-language models and Western demographic categories limits generalizability
