# Understanding Incremental Learning of Gradient Descent: A Fine-grained Analysis of Matrix Sensing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11500
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper provides a fine-grained analysis of the dynamics of Gradient Descent (GD) for the matrix sensing problem, showing that GD with small initialization follows an incremental learning procedure, sequentially learning solutions with increasing ranks until it recovers the ground truth matrix.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the incremental learning behavior of Gradient Descent in the matrix sensing problem, where the goal is to recover a low-rank ground-truth matrix from linear measurements. The key insight is that GD with small initialization exhibits a low-rank bias, starting with rank-1 solutions and progressively increasing rank until reaching the ground truth rank. This behavior resembles greedy low-rank learning heuristics and provides a theoretical foundation for understanding implicit regularization in gradient-based optimization.

The analysis applies to both over-parameterized and under-parameterized regimes, showing that when the parameterization rank is less than the ground truth rank, GD converges to the best rank-s solution. The results rely on the Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) of the measurement operator and small initialization assumptions, providing convergence guarantees and characterizing the entire learning process beyond just the initial rank-1 phase.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes Gradient Descent for matrix sensing by studying the evolution of a low-rank factorization Ut where the ground truth matrix Z* has rank r*. Starting with a small random initialization U₀ = αŪ, GD updates follow Ut+1 = Ut - μ∇f(Ut). The analysis decomposes Ut into parallel and orthogonal components relative to the ground truth, showing that the parallel component grows while the orthogonal component shrinks under appropriate conditions. The incremental learning procedure is characterized through a combination of RIP-based analysis and careful tracking of the singular value decomposition components throughout the optimization process.

## Key Results
- GD with small initialization follows an incremental learning procedure, sequentially learning solutions with increasing ranks
- The low-rank bias of GD favors simpler solutions first, initially behaving like power iteration
- In the under-parameterized regime, GD converges to the best rank-s solution that minimizes the sensing loss
- The analysis applies to both over-parameterized and under-parameterized regimes, providing characterizations for the whole learning process

## Limitations

- The precise threshold for "small enough" initialization remains ambiguous in practice, creating a gap between theoretical assumptions and numerical experiments.
- The analysis heavily relies on the Restricted Isometry Property with parameter δ<1/10, which may be overly conservative for practical scenarios.
- The ground truth matrix's degeneracy parameter κ affects convergence bounds but is not explicitly characterized or estimated in experiments.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The incremental learning mechanism and low-rank bias are well-supported by both theoretical analysis and established literature on implicit regularization in matrix factorization.
- Medium Confidence: The convergence guarantees in the under-parameterized regime rely on assumptions about local geometry that haven't been extensively validated across diverse problem instances.
- Low Confidence: The practical implications of initialization scale and RIP condition thresholds for real-world applications remain uncertain without broader empirical validation.
