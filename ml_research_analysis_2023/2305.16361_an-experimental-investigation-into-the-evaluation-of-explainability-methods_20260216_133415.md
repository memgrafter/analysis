# An Experimental Investigation into the Evaluation of Explainability Methods

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16361
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16361
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: No faithfulness metric consistently ranks dummy methods poorly, highlighting limitations in current evaluation approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive experimental investigation comparing 14 evaluation metrics across 9 state-of-the-art XAI methods and 3 dummy methods using ImageNet and CIFAR10 datasets. The study systematically examines correlations between metrics, the impact of baseline hyperparameters on faithfulness evaluations, and the reliability of metrics using dummy methods. Key findings reveal significant correlations suggesting metric redundancy, dramatic baseline sensitivity affecting faithfulness metric rankings, and fundamental limitations in how current metrics evaluate explanation quality. The results call for more robust evaluation frameworks and highlight the need for multiple baselines when assessing faithfulness.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 9 state-of-the-art XAI methods (Integrated Gradient, SmoothGrad, Guided Backpropagation, Grad-CAM, Score-CAM, Layer-CAM, Poly-CAM, RISE, CAMERAS) plus 3 dummy methods (random, Sobel, Gaussian) on ImageNet and CIFAR10 datasets using ResNet-50 and VGG-16 models. Researchers applied 14 evaluation metrics grouped into faithfulness (4 metrics), robustness (3 metrics), complexity (3 metrics), and randomization (4 metrics) families. The experimental design systematically varied baseline hyperparameters (black, white, random, uniform) for faithfulness metrics and computed pairwise Kendall's τb correlations between all metric scores to assess redundancy. Dummy methods served as an ablation study to test metric reliability in distinguishing meaningful from meaningless explanations.

## Key Results
- Sparseness and Complexity metrics show extremely high correlation (τ > 0.97) across both architectures and datasets, suggesting redundancy
- Max-Sensitivity and Avg-Sensitivity metrics are significantly correlated on ImageNet, indicating potential overlap in what they measure
- Faithfulness metric results vary dramatically depending on baseline choice, with concordance dropping below 0.2 between some baseline pairs
- No faithfulness metric consistently ranks dummy methods as poor explanations, revealing fundamental limitations in current evaluation approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Faithfulness metrics are sensitive to baseline hyperparameter choice, which can dramatically alter evaluation outcomes.
- **Mechanism**: Faithfulness metrics simulate feature removal by replacing pixel values with a baseline (black, white, random, uniform). The choice of baseline changes the reference point for "absence of feature," which directly affects the correlation between pixel perturbation and model output changes.
- **Core assumption**: The effect of pixel removal on model predictions is baseline-dependent and this dependency is large enough to change rankings between XAI methods.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "The choice of baseline hyperparameter significantly impacts faithfulness metric results, indicating the need for multiple baselines in experiments."
  - [section]: "we use four baselines: black, white, random and uniform values... We studied the influence of this baseline hyperparameter on the scores generated by faithfulness metrics."
- **Break condition**: If faithfulness metrics are designed to be baseline-invariant (e.g., by normalizing across baselines), or if the impact of baseline choice is negligible for a given model-dataset pair.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Some evaluation metrics are redundant because they measure highly correlated properties of XAI methods.
- **Mechanism**: Metrics from the same family (e.g., complexity metrics) or even across families (e.g., Max-Sensitivity and Avg-Sensitivity) can produce scores that are strongly correlated, implying they capture overlapping aspects of method quality.
- **Core assumption**: Correlation in metric scores implies redundancy in the information they provide about XAI method quality.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Significant correlations between Sparseness and Complexity metrics, and between Max-Sensitivity and Avg-Sensitivity metrics, suggesting potential redundancy."
  - [section]: "We studied pairwise correlation of metrics through Kendall's τb... For complexity metrics... Sparseness and Complexity metrics are significantly correlated with a Kendall's τ correlation coefficient of 0.97 (ResNet-50) and 1.0 (VGG-16) on ImageNet."
- **Break condition**: If high correlation is due to shared implementation details rather than conceptual overlap, or if the metrics capture complementary aspects despite numerical correlation.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Reliability of evaluation metrics can be assessed using dummy methods (e.g., random saliency maps) that should rank poorly for meaningful explanations.
- **Mechanism**: By evaluating dummy methods alongside real XAI methods, one can check if metrics consistently rank non-explanatory methods (like random maps) as low quality. If a metric fails to do so, it indicates a flaw in that metric's ability to detect uninformative explanations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Reliability analysis reveals that no faithfulness metric consistently ranks dummy methods poorly, highlighting the limitations of these metrics."
  - [section]: "Three dummy saliency maps were added... These three methods can be seen as an 'ablation study' on metrics... Indeed, they allow the comparison of rankings produced for a metric according to the expected ranking of dummy methods."
- **Break condition**: If dummy methods are not truly uninformative (e.g., Sobel edge maps might correlate with object boundaries), or if the ranking is not meaningful due to ties or scale issues.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Kendall's τb correlation coefficient
  - Why needed here: Used to measure rank correlation between metric scores to detect redundancy.
  - Quick check question: If two methods have ranks (1,2,3) and (3,2,1), what is the Kendall's τb value? (Answer: -1, perfect negative correlation.)

- **Concept**: Baseline hyperparameter in XAI evaluation
  - Why needed here: Central to understanding how faithfulness metrics simulate feature absence and why results vary.
  - Quick check question: What is the effect of using a white baseline vs a black baseline when evaluating a saliency method that highlights dark regions? (Answer: White baseline makes dark-highlighted pixels appear more "absent," potentially inflating faithfulness scores.)

- **Concept**: Dummy/ablative methods in evaluation
  - Why needed here: Used to sanity-check whether metrics can distinguish real explanations from non-explanatory maps.
  - Quick check question: Why would a Sobel edge detector be a poor XAI method for image classification? (Answer: It highlights edges based on gradients, not on relevance to class prediction.)

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  Image preprocessing -> Model inference -> XAI method application -> Metric evaluation -> Correlation analysis -> Baseline sensitivity analysis -> Reliability assessment

- **Critical path**:
  1. Load and preprocess images.
  2. Run model inference to get predictions.
  3. Generate saliency maps using XAI methods.
  4. Apply each metric to each saliency map.
  5. Aggregate scores per method.
  6. Compute pairwise metric correlations.
  7. Analyze baseline sensitivity for faithfulness metrics.
  8. Rank dummy methods and assess reliability.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Correlation metric: Kendall's τb chosen for robustness to ties and non-linear relationships, but slower than Pearson.
  - Baseline choice: Using multiple baselines increases robustness but also computational cost.
  - Dummy methods: Sobel and Gaussian may not be fully uninformative, but are standard in ablation studies.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High variance in metric scores across baselines suggests instability.
  - Dummy methods ranking high indicates metric flaws.
  - Near-perfect correlation between different metric families suggests implementation error or true redundancy.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run faithfulness metrics with black baseline only and compare rankings to multi-baseline results.
  2. Compute Kendall's τb between all pairs of metrics and identify correlations >0.9.
  3. Evaluate dummy methods with all metrics and check if they consistently rank lowest.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Which evaluation metrics show the strongest potential for redundancy and could be removed without losing significant information about XAI method performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies Sparseness and Complexity metrics as highly correlated (Kendall's τ > 0.9) on all datasets, and Max-Sensitivity and Avg-Sensitivity as strongly correlated on ImageNet. These pairs could be considered redundant.
- Why unresolved: While correlations are identified, the paper doesn't definitively establish whether these metrics provide unique information or if one could be dropped from evaluation suites. The threshold of 0.9 correlation doesn't necessarily mean complete redundancy.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic ablation study removing one metric from each correlated pair and measuring the impact on method rankings and conclusions would determine if the metrics are truly redundant.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do evaluation metric rankings change when applied to vision transformer architectures versus convolutional neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on CNN architectures (ResNet-50 and VGG-16) and notes that saliency maps for new architectures like ViT are "not entirely interchangeable with respect to CNNs." This suggests potential architecture-dependent differences in metric performance.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluates metrics on CNN models, leaving open whether the observed correlations, baseline sensitivities, and reliability issues persist across different neural network architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the entire experimental setup using vision transformer models on the same datasets would reveal if metric behaviors are architecture-dependent.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal baseline hyperparameter configuration for faithfulness metrics to ensure reliable and comparable evaluations across different XAI methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that faithfulness metrics show high variability in concordance depending on baseline values (black, white, random, uniform), with correlations sometimes dropping below 0.2 between different baselines.
- Why unresolved: While the paper recommends using multiple baselines, it doesn't identify an optimal baseline strategy or determine which baselines produce the most reliable comparisons between methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive analysis comparing method rankings and conclusion stability across all baseline combinations, potentially identifying a minimal set of baselines that capture the full range of metric behavior.

## Limitations

- The study only evaluates CNN architectures, limiting generalizability to newer architectures like vision transformers
- Dummy methods (Sobel, Gaussian) may not be truly uninformative as they can capture real image structure
- High correlation between metrics doesn't necessarily prove conceptual redundancy, only numerical similarity

## Confidence

- Baseline sensitivity findings: High confidence - systematic variation of baselines showed dramatic score changes
- Metric redundancy findings: Medium confidence - high correlation suggests overlap but doesn't prove conceptual redundancy
- Reliability assessment using dummy methods: Low confidence - dummy methods may not be truly uninformative

## Next Checks

1. Test baseline sensitivity on additional model architectures (e.g., Vision Transformers) to verify generalizability
2. Implement a more stringent dummy method (e.g., completely random noise) to better isolate metric reliability
3. Conduct ablation studies removing correlated metric pairs to assess impact on method rankings
