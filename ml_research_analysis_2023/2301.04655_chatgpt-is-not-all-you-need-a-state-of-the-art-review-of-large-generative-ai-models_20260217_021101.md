# ChatGPT is not all you need. A State of the Art Review of large Generative AI models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04655
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04655
- Authors: 6 organizations
- Reference count: 35
- One-line primary result: Comprehensive review of large generative AI models published 2022-2023 across text-to-image, text-to-video, and other modalities

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive review of large generative AI models published during 2022-2023, categorizing them by input-output formats and highlighting major developments across multiple domains. The review emphasizes the role of transformer architectures, diffusion models, and large-scale training datasets in enabling breakthroughs in tasks ranging from text-to-image synthesis to scientific text generation. The authors identify six organizations as dominant players in the field while discussing both the capabilities and limitations of these models, including computational demands, bias issues, and ethical concerns.

## Method Summary
This literature review synthesizes findings from 35 referenced papers published between 2022-2023, analyzing large generative AI models across multiple modalities. The review follows a systematic approach of categorizing models by their input-output formats (text-to-image, text-to-video, text-to-3D, etc.) and examining their architectural innovations, training methodologies, and practical applications. The authors leverage GitHub repositories for model APIs and examples of generated content to validate claims, though specific API access credentials and exact training configurations remain unspecified.

## Key Results
- Dominance of six major organizations in developing large generative AI models
- Emergence of text-to-3D synthesis with DreamFusion and Magic3D models
- Breakthroughs in audio generation (AudioLM) and scientific text generation (Galactica)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Large generative models succeed because they learn high-dimensional semantic mappings via transformer-based architectures trained on massive corpora
- Mechanism: The model ingests diverse multimodal data, projects it into a high-dimensional latent space, and uses attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies, enabling generation across formats
- Core assumption: Sufficient data diversity and model scale are necessary and sufficient for learning robust generative capabilities
- Evidence anchors:
  - Models are trained on enormous datasets (e.g., Wikipedia, GitHub, Google images) and deep neural networks/transformers to model high-dimensional probability distributions
  - Dreamfusion uses a pretrained 2D diffusion model and a differentiable generator to map text prompts to 3D models
- Break condition: Insufficient data diversity or computational constraints that prevent learning high-dimensional semantic representations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Generative models generate novel content by mapping inputs through learned latent spaces using conditional probability distributions
- Mechanism: The model uses conditional generative models (e.g., diffusion, VAE) that learn the joint distribution of input-output pairs and sample from this distribution to generate novel content
- Core assumption: The learned conditional distribution is smooth and well-behaved enough to allow meaningful sampling
- Evidence anchors:
  - Generative AI can generate novel content rather than just analyzing existing data, using unsupervised or semi-supervised learning
  - Stable Diffusion works in latent space using a latent diffusion model for faster image generation and modification
- Break condition: The learned distribution is too noisy or fragmented, leading to incoherent or repetitive outputs

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Large language models encode text effectively for multimodal generation tasks, enabling cross-domain mappings
- Mechanism: Pretrained language models (e.g., BERT, GPT) learn rich text representations that can be conditioned on or decoded to generate other modalities like images or audio
- Core assumption: Text representations learned by large language models are sufficiently general to serve as conditioning for multimodal generation
- Evidence anchors:
  - Imagen uses large language models pre-trained on text-only corpora to encode text for image synthesis, with larger models improving fidelity
  - Imagen uses a pretrained text encoder (like BERT) to map text to word embeddings, which are then transformed into images by conditional diffusion models
- Break condition: The text representations are too domain-specific or lack the necessary semantic richness for effective cross-modal generation

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input processing (encoders) -> Core model (transformer/diffusion) -> Output generation (decoders) -> Conditioning (text embeddings)

- Critical path: Data preprocessing and encoding → Model forward pass and attention computation → Sampling from learned distributions → Post-processing and output formatting

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. computational efficiency
  - Data diversity vs. domain specificity
  - Generation quality vs. inference speed

- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse or repetitive outputs
  - Hallucinations or factually incorrect generations
  - Inconsistent or incoherent multimodal mappings

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise impact of generative AI on creative industries, and how can we quantify job displacement vs. enhancement?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that generative AI models are "revolutionizing several industries like the art industry" and discusses implications for job positions being "transformed."
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions potential impacts but lacks empirical data or quantitative analysis of actual job market effects
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies comparing employment rates and job descriptions in creative industries before and after widespread generative AI adoption

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective methods for mitigating bias in generative AI models without significantly reducing their performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that generative AI models "face bias from the data which needs to be controlled" and mentions Galactica's attempt to address this through a "layer of no bias"
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies bias as a limitation but doesn't provide concrete solutions or evaluate their effectiveness
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different bias mitigation techniques applied to various generative AI models, measuring both bias reduction and performance impact

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we develop more efficient training methods for generative AI models that require less computational power and time?
- Basis in paper: The paper highlights that "a lot of time and computation capacity is necessary in order to run them" and mentions this as a key limitation
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies computational demands as a challenge but doesn't explore potential solutions or emerging techniques
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking studies comparing traditional and novel training approaches across multiple generative AI model architectures and datasets

## Limitations
- Claims about model capabilities and limitations should be viewed with medium confidence due to rapidly evolving research
- Exact computational requirements and environmental impacts are often estimated rather than precisely measured
- Claims about bias mitigation strategies are largely theoretical with limited empirical evidence

## Confidence
- High: Claims about model categorization and input-output format capabilities
- Medium: Claims about architectural innovations and training methodologies
- Low: Claims about real-world deployment impacts and ethical considerations
