# Understanding and Predicting Human Label Variation in Natural Language Inference through Explanation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12443
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12443
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: New dataset LIVE NLI captures within-label variation through ecologically valid explanations, and incorporating explanations slightly improves GPT-3's ability to predict NLI label distributions

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of human label variation (annotation disagreement) in Natural Language Inference (NLI) tasks by introducing the LIVE NLI dataset. The dataset contains 122 English NLI items, each with at least 10 annotations that include both labels and free-text explanations with highlights. The authors demonstrate that explanations can capture within-label variation where annotators arrive at the same label for different reasons, which is not captured by labels alone. Using chain-of-thought prompting with GPT-3, they show that incorporating explanations slightly improves the model's ability to predict NLI label distributions compared to prompting without explanations, though significant room for improvement remains.

## Method Summary
The authors created the LIVE NLI dataset using the Surge AI platform, collecting NLI items with multiple annotations where each annotator provides highlights and free-text explanations for their chosen label(s). They then used chain-of-thought prompting with GPT-3, comparing two approaches: Predict-then-Explain (prompt with premise-hypothesis pairs to predict labels, then generate explanations) and Explain-then-Predict (prompt with explanations to predict labels). The study evaluated GPT-3's performance in predicting label distributions and generating explanations using metrics like KL-divergence, Jensen-Shannon distance, and Total Variation Distance, while analyzing the quality and diversity of explanations through human evaluation.

## Key Results
- LIVE NLI dataset contains 122 NLI items with at least 10 annotations each, capturing ecologically valid explanations
- Explanations reveal within-label variation where the same highlights can explain different labels
- Incorporating explanations in chain-of-thought prompting slightly improves GPT-3's ability to predict NLI label distributions
- Entropy in label distributions plateaus after 5 explanations, suggesting diminishing returns from additional annotations
- Free-text explanations are crucial for understanding the relationships between highlighted words and labels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LIVE NLI's ecologically valid explanations improve GPT-3's ability to predict label distributions.
- Mechanism: Chain-of-thought prompting with explanations provides additional supervision signals beyond premise-hypothesis pairs alone.
- Core assumption: GPT-3 can learn from free-text explanations to better capture the reasoning behind label variation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "incorporating explanations in the prompt slightly improves the ability of GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020) to predict NLI label distributions"
  - [section]: "Explain-then-Predict slightly outperforms the setups with the same amount of training data"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors don't provide direct evidence for GPT-3's performance improvement with explanations.
- Break condition: If GPT-3 fails to generate plausible explanations or if explanations are too noisy/diverse to be useful supervision.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Explanations reveal within-label variation that labels alone cannot capture.
- Mechanism: Free-text explanations allow annotators to express different reasons for choosing the same label, which labels cannot represent.
- Core assumption: Annotators provide diverse explanations even when they agree on labels, revealing different reasoning paths.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "16 (out of 122) items exhibit such within-label variation... the same highlight can be used to explain different labels"
  - [abstract]: "explanations can capture within-label variation, where annotators can arrive at the same label for different reasons"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors focus on explanation taxonomies but don't directly address within-label variation capture.
- Break condition: If annotators consistently provide identical explanations for the same label or if explanations don't reveal meaningful reasoning differences.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Diverse reasoning types in explanations lead to higher entropy in label distributions.
- Mechanism: When explanations contain diverse reasoning (lexical, probabilistic, QUD), the resulting label distributions have higher entropy, capturing more uncertainty.
- Core assumption: More diverse explanations correlate with more varied label distributions and higher uncertainty.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "entropy increases with the number of explanations... but importantly, the entropy plateaus after 5 explanations for all labels"
  - [abstract]: "explanations for the label(s) of their choice... ensures ecological validity"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors don't provide evidence about entropy-diversity relationships.
- Break condition: If explanations don't show meaningful diversity or if entropy doesn't correlate with reasoning diversity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: NLI items -> Annotation collection (Surge AI) -> Explanation annotation pipeline -> GPT-3 prompting framework -> Evaluation metrics

- Critical path: 1. Collect NLI items with multiple annotations including explanations 2. Preprocess explanations and format prompts for GPT-3 3. Run in-context learning experiments with different prompting strategies 4. Evaluate predicted distributions against ground truth

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Explanation quality vs. annotation cost (more annotations = more diverse explanations but higher cost)
  - Prompt length vs. information richness (more explanations = better supervision but may exceed token limits)
  - Model confidence vs. distribution entropy (confident predictions may miss variation)

- Failure signatures:
  - Low inter-annotator agreement on highlights despite high label agreement
  - GPT-3 generating implausible or inaccurate explanations
  - KL-divergence performance no better than uniform baseline

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare Explain-then-Predict vs Predict-then-Explain performance on small test set
  2. Test effect of varying number of explanations per item (1, 3, 5, 10) on prediction quality
  3. Evaluate GPT-3's ability to generate explanations for held-out items

## Limitations
- Small dataset size (122 items) may limit generalizability and statistical power
- Use of paid crowdworkers through Surge AI may not reflect broader annotation contexts
- GPT-3's tendency to generate plausible but potentially inaccurate explanations makes evaluation challenging
- Study focuses exclusively on English NLI, limiting applicability to other languages or domains

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The existence of within-label variation captured by explanations
- Medium Confidence: The slight improvement in GPT-3's label distribution prediction when using explanations
- Low Confidence: The generalizability of findings to other NLP tasks or languages
