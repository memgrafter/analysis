# Are More Layers Beneficial to Graph Transformers?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00579
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00579
- Reference count: 35
- Key outcome: DeepGraph achieves 0.072 ± 0.004 on ZINC with 48 layers, outperforming previous models

## Executive Summary
DeepGraph addresses the fundamental limitation that graph transformers degrade in performance as depth increases beyond 12 layers. The core issue identified is the vanishing capacity of global attention to focus on substructures, which restricts the model's ability to learn expressive features. By introducing substructure tokens with local attention mechanisms, DeepGraph successfully overcomes this depth limitation, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple graph benchmarks including ZINC, PCQM4M-LSC, CLUSTER, and PATTERN datasets.

## Method Summary
DeepGraph combines global attention between nodes with local attention on substructure tokens to address the attention capacity decay problem in deep graph transformers. The method samples substructures from graphs, encodes them into tokens using permutation-based encoding, and applies local attention to substructure tokens and their corresponding nodes while preserving global attention between all nodes. The model uses greedy sampling algorithms to balance substructure coverage and types, with substructures including 2-hop neighbors, random walk neighbors, and geometric patterns.

## Key Results
- Achieves 0.072 ± 0.004 on ZINC dataset with 48 layers
- Outperforms previous models on CLUSTER (77.912 ± 0.138) and PATTERN (90.657 ± 0.062) classification tasks
- Successfully scales to 48 layers without performance degradation
- Demonstrates effectiveness across graph-level regression, node classification, and molecular property prediction tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deepening graph transformers without substructure-based local attention leads to decaying attention capacity, causing performance degradation.
- Mechanism: As layers increase, the self-attention mechanism's ability to focus on substructures diminishes exponentially, limiting the model's capacity to learn expressive features. This is due to the upper bound of attention capacity decreasing with each layer, making it harder for deep models to autonomously learn effective attention patterns.
- Core assumption: Attention capacity is crucial for learning substructure features, and its decay is the primary bottleneck for deep graph transformers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our further analysis reveals the reason is that deep graph transformers are limited by the vanishing capacity of global attention, restricting the graph transformer from focusing on the critical substructure and obtaining expressive features."
  - [section]: "Theorem 1 (The stacked attention modules decrease the capacity of attention) ... attention capacity of layer l is upper bounded by ... αi < 1 with probability 1"
- Break condition: If the model can maintain high attention capacity to substructures as depth increases, or if the decay rate is significantly slower than predicted.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Introducing substructure tokens with local attention alleviates the attention capacity decay problem in deep graph transformers.
- Mechanism: By explicitly introducing substructure tokens and applying local attention to nodes related to those substructures, the model can focus on substructure features more effectively. This approach not only encourages the model to focus on substructure features but also enlarges the attention capacity theoretically and empirically.
- Core assumption: Local attention to substructures can compensate for the global attention's decreasing capacity and enhance the model's ability to learn expressive features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our model enhances the ability of the global attention to focus on substructures and promotes the expressiveness of the representations, addressing the limitation of self-attention as the graph transformer deepens."
  - [section]: "Theorem 3 ... substructure based local attention not only introduces the substructure based attention to encourage the model to focus on substructure features but also enlarges the attention capacity of deep layers, which promotes the representation capacity of the model when it grows deep."
- Break condition: If the local attention mechanism does not significantly improve attention capacity or if the model cannot effectively learn substructure features with this approach.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of global and local attention, facilitated by substructure tokens, provides a better global-local encoding balance, leading to improved performance in deep graph transformers.
- Mechanism: The model tokenizes the graph into node level and substructure level, applying local attention to the substructure token and corresponding nodes while preserving global attention between nodes. This provides a better solution for explicitly introducing attention to substructures in the model while combining global attention.
- Core assumption: A balanced global-local attention mechanism can leverage the strengths of both approaches, leading to improved feature learning and representation expressiveness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our model enhances the ability of the global attention to focus on substructures and promotes the expressiveness of the representations, addressing the limitation of self-attention as the graph transformer deepens."
  - [section]: "Our method first samples substructures of the graph, then encodes substructures into tokens, and finally enforces local attention on substructures."
- Break condition: If the global-local attention balance is not optimal or if the model cannot effectively leverage both global and local information.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input features → Substructure sampling → Substructure encoding → Local attention on substructures → Global attention between nodes → Output predictions

- Critical path:
  1. Substructure sampling and encoding
  2. Local attention on substructure tokens and corresponding nodes
  3. Global attention between nodes
  4. Combining local and global information for final predictions

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Balancing the number of substructure tokens to avoid excessive computational cost while ensuring sufficient coverage of the graph
  - Choosing the appropriate substructure types (e.g., neighbors, geometric substructures) to capture relevant features
  - Determining the optimal depth of the model, considering the trade-off between representation power and attention capacity decay

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation with increasing depth, indicating attention capacity decay
  - Inability to effectively learn substructure features, suggesting issues with the local attention mechanism or substructure encoding
  - Overfitting or underfitting, depending on the model complexity and the size of the training data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise relationship between substructure sampling strategies and attention capacity preservation across depth?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions greedy sampling algorithms and balancing substructure types, but doesn't systematically evaluate how different sampling strategies affect attention capacity decay with depth.
- Why unresolved: The authors only test one sampling strategy (greedy with coverage balancing) without comparing alternatives or analyzing how sampling parameters influence attention capacity preservation in deep layers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies varying sampling strategies (random, stratified, coverage-based), sampling ratios, and substructure type distributions while measuring attention capacity at different depths would clarify the optimal sampling approach for preserving capacity.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does substructure token encoding method (permutation-based vs alternatives) impact the effectiveness of local attention in deep transformers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses permutation-based encoding with DFS ordering but only compares it to randomly initialized embeddings in ablation studies.
- Why unresolved: The authors don't compare permutation-based encoding against other encoding methods (e.g., GNN-based, spectral encoding) or analyze how encoding quality affects the benefits of local attention in deep layers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using different substructure encoding methods (GNN, spectral, learned) while measuring both performance and attention capacity would reveal whether permutation-based encoding is optimal or if other methods could provide greater benefits.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical limit of depth improvement achievable through substructure-based local attention?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows performance improvements up to 48 layers but doesn't explore whether this trend continues or establish theoretical bounds on depth improvement.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates depth benefits beyond current limits, it doesn't analyze whether there are fundamental constraints on how much local attention can compensate for attention capacity decay in extremely deep networks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Scaling experiments to hundreds of layers combined with theoretical analysis of attention capacity bounds would establish whether substructure-based methods can enable truly deep transformers or if there are inherent limitations.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis relies on assumptions about feature independence and uniform distributions that may not hold in practice
- Substructure sampling introduces hyperparameters that significantly impact performance but are not fully explored
- Optimal substructure types and combinations may be domain-specific and not generalize across all graph types
