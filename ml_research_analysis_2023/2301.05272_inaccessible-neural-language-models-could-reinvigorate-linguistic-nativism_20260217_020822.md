# Inaccessible Neural Language Models Could Reinvigorate Linguistic Nativism

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05272
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05272
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: Closed-source LLMs may bias researchers toward nativist frameworks by limiting access to empiricist approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how the inaccessibility of large language models (LLMs) could reshape linguistic research by limiting exposure to empiricist approaches. The authors argue that when LLM code remains closed-source, new researchers may only have access to rule-based, nativist methodologies, potentially creating a systematic bias in how linguistic theories develop. The paper highlights concerns about reproducibility and educational impacts, suggesting that open-sourcing LLM code could promote a more balanced research space between empiricist and nativist approaches.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a theoretical analysis of the relationship between LLM accessibility and research methodology biases. Rather than conducting empirical studies, the authors develop arguments based on the current state of NLP research infrastructure, examining how closed-source models create barriers to understanding empiricist approaches to language modeling. The analysis focuses on the downstream effects of limited access on researcher training, experimental reproducibility, and the theoretical framing of linguistic research.

## Key Results
- Lack of open-source LLM code may create nativist bias in new researchers
- Closed models limit reproducibility of linguistic experiments
- Open-sourcing LLM code could help balance empiricist and nativist research approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper argues that when researchers cannot access the underlying code of neural language models, they lose the ability to empirically test and understand how these models learn language patterns. This forces reliance on rule-based, nativist explanations that assume innate linguistic structures. Without the ability to experiment with and modify these models, researchers cannot develop empiricist counterarguments that language emerges from exposure and pattern recognition.

## Limitations
