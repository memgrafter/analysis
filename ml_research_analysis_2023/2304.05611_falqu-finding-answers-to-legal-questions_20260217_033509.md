# FALQU: Finding Answers to Legal Questions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05611
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05611
- Reference count: 9
- Key outcome: Introduces FALQU dataset with 9,880 legal questions and 34,145 answers from Law Stack Exchange, showing BM25 with Yake keywords achieves MRR@1000 of 0.414 and P@1 of 0.323

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FALQU, a new test collection for legal information retrieval built from Law Stack Exchange, containing 9,880 questions and 34,145 answers. Unlike prior legal IR datasets based on bar exams, FALQU includes diverse, real-world questions from different jurisdictions. The collection provides questions in XML format with title and body, and answers in TREC format, along with relevance judgments for one accepted answer per question. The authors evaluate baseline retrieval models including TF-IDF, BM25, and two Sentence-BERT variants, with and without fine-tuning. BM25 with Yake-extracted keywords achieved the highest effectiveness with MRR@1000 of 0.414 and P@1 of 0.323. The study highlights the potential of community Q&A data for legal IR and provides a publicly available benchmark for future research.

## Method Summary
The FALQU dataset was constructed by extracting legal questions and answers from the Law Stack Exchange dump, preprocessing to remove irrelevant posts, and splitting into training (8,892 questions) and test (988 questions) sets. Retrieval models were implemented using TF-IDF, BM25 with Yake-extracted keywords, and two Sentence-BERT variants (distilroberta and all-MiniLM-L12-v2) with fine-tuning. Evaluation used standard IR metrics MRR@1000 and Precision@1, with each question having exactly one relevant answer (the accepted answer marked by the asker).

## Key Results
- BM25 with Yake-extracted keywords achieved the highest effectiveness: MRR@1000 of 0.414 and P@1 of 0.323
- Traditional IR models outperformed neural approaches on this dataset
- Dataset contains 9,880 questions and 34,145 answers from diverse legal domains
- Accepted answers provide clear binary relevance judgments for evaluation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of different keyword extraction algorithms on retrieval effectiveness in FALQU?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using Yake for keyword extraction and shows it improves BM25 performance, but doesn't explore alternative keyword extraction methods.
- Why unresolved: Only Yake was evaluated as a keyword extraction method. The paper doesn't compare Yake with other keyword extraction algorithms or determine if the improvement is specific to Yake or generalizable to other methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using different keyword extraction algorithms (e.g., TextRank, RAKE, or TF-IDF-based methods) alongside BM25 to measure their impact on retrieval metrics like MRR@1000 and P@1.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How well do neural retrieval models generalize to other legal domains beyond those represented in FALQU?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates BERT-based models specifically on FALQU data and finds they underperform compared to traditional IR models, but doesn't test cross-domain generalization.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses only on FALQU's specific legal domains without testing whether models trained on this dataset transfer to other legal domains like case law retrieval or statutory interpretation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Cross-domain experiments where models trained on FALQU are tested on other legal IR datasets (e.g., COLIEE or legal case collections) and vice versa, measuring performance drops or transfer capabilities.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the effect of using different answer selection strategies (beyond the accepted answer) on retrieval performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "there is only one answer in the QREL for each question" and that answers not in QREL are considered non-relevant, but doesn't explore alternative relevance definitions.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation assumes the accepted answer is the only relevant one, but community answers often contain multiple relevant responses that could improve retrieval if considered.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments creating multiple relevance judgments per question (e.g., top-voted answers, expert annotations) and measuring how different answer selection strategies affect retrieval metrics.

## Limitations

- The study relies on the assumption that accepted answers on Law Stack Exchange are reliable indicators of relevance, though the quality and accuracy of these answers are not independently verified.
- The reported superiority of BM25+Yake over neural approaches may be influenced by the specific characteristics of legal Q&A pairs, which tend to be more keyword-focused rather than requiring deep semantic understanding.
- The dataset's representativeness of general legal information needs is uncertain, given the specific nature of Law Stack Exchange's user community and question topics.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The dataset construction methodology is well-documented and reproducible, with clear explanations of data sources and processing steps. The experimental setup with multiple baseline models is appropriate for establishing initial benchmarks.

**Medium confidence**: The conclusion that traditional IR models outperform neural approaches is based on limited evaluation metrics and may not generalize across all legal domains or question types. The relevance of accepted answers is assumed but not empirically validated.

**Low confidence**: The dataset's representativeness of general legal information needs is uncertain, given the specific nature of Law Stack Exchange's user community and question topics.
