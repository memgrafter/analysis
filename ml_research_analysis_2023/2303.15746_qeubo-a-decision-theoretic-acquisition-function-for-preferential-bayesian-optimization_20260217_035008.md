# qEUBO: A Decision-Theoretic Acquisition Function for Preferential Bayesian Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15746
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15746
- Authors: Not specified

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces qEUBO, a novel acquisition function for preferential Bayesian optimization (PBO) based on maximizing the expected utility of the best option. qEUBO generalizes the EUBO acquisition function to PBO and is shown to be one-step Bayes optimal, equivalent to the knowledge gradient when responses are noise-free, and enjoying an additive constant approximation guarantee under noisy responses. Extensive experiments demonstrate that qEUBO outperforms state-of-the-art acquisition functions across various settings, including settings with more than two alternatives per query. The authors also prove that qEUBO's Bayesian simple regret converges to zero at a rate o(1/n), while simple regret under qEI can fail to converge to zero.

## Method Summary
qEUBO is a decision-theoretic acquisition function for preferential Bayesian optimization that maximizes the expected utility of the best option. It generalizes the EUBO acquisition function to PBO and is designed to select the most informative queries for preference learning. The key idea is to evaluate the expected improvement in utility from choosing a particular query set, considering the posterior distribution over preferences. The authors show that qEUBO is one-step Bayes optimal and equivalent to the knowledge gradient acquisition function when responses are noise-free. They also prove that qEUBO enjoys an additive constant approximation guarantee to the one-step Bayes-optimal policy when responses are noisy.

## Key Results
- qEUBO is one-step Bayes optimal and equivalent to the knowledge gradient acquisition function when responses are noise-free
- qEUBO enjoys an additive constant approximation guarantee to the one-step Bayes-optimal policy when responses are noisy
- Extensive experiments demonstrate that qEUBO outperforms state-of-the-art acquisition functions for PBO across various settings, including settings with more than two alternatives per query
- qEUBO's Bayesian simple regret converges to zero at a rate o(1/n) as the number of queries grows, while simple regret under qEI can fail to converge to zero

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
qEUBO works by maximizing the expected utility of the best option, which is a decision-theoretic approach to selecting the most informative queries for preference learning. This is achieved by evaluating the expected improvement in utility from choosing a particular query set, considering the posterior distribution over preferences. The authors show that this approach is one-step Bayes optimal, meaning it makes the best possible decision given the current information. The equivalence to the knowledge gradient acquisition function in the noise-free case provides additional theoretical justification for the approach.

## Foundational Learning
- **Preferential Bayesian Optimization (PBO)**: A framework for optimizing functions where the objective is to find the best option based on preference judgments rather than absolute evaluations. Needed to understand the problem setting and the motivation for qEUBO.
- **Expected Improvement (EI) and Expected Utility of the Best Option (EUBO)**: Two common acquisition functions for Bayesian optimization. EI maximizes the expected improvement in the objective function, while EUBO maximizes the expected utility of the best option. Understanding these concepts is crucial for grasping the generalization from EUBO to qEUBO.
- **Knowledge Gradient**: An acquisition function that measures the expected improvement in the value of information from a query. The equivalence of qEUBO to the knowledge gradient in the noise-free case provides theoretical justification for the approach.
- **One-step Bayes Optimality**: A property of an acquisition function that makes the best possible decision given the current information. qEUBO is shown to be one-step Bayes optimal, which is a strong theoretical guarantee of its performance.
- **Additive Constant Approximation**: A measure of how close an approximation is to the optimal solution. qEUBO is shown to enjoy an additive constant approximation guarantee to the one-step Bayes-optimal policy when responses are noisy, which is a strong theoretical guarantee of its performance.
- **Bayesian Simple Regret**: A measure of the expected difference between the optimal value and the value of the chosen option. qEUBO's Bayesian simple regret is shown to converge to zero at a rate o(1/n), which is a strong theoretical guarantee of its performance.

Critical Path:

2. Compute the posterior distribution over preferences given the observed data
3. Evaluate the expected utility of the best option for each possible query set
4. Select the query set that maximizes the expected utility of the best option

Design Tradeoffs:
- The choice of utility function can significantly impact the performance of qEUBO, as it determines the preference structure that is being optimized
- The computational cost of evaluating the expected utility of the best option for each possible query set can be high, especially for large query sets
- The accuracy of the posterior distribution over preferences depends on the quality of the observed data and the choice of prior

Failure Signatures:
- If the utility function is not well-aligned with the true preference structure, qEUBO may perform poorly
- If the posterior distribution over preferences is not accurate, qEUBO may make suboptimal query selections
- If the computational cost of evaluating the expected utility of the best option is too high, qEUBO may be impractical to use

## Limitations
- The theoretical guarantees (additive constant approximation and convergence rates) are established under specific assumptions about noise models and query structures
- The experimental validation primarily focuses on synthetic benchmarks and a limited set of real-world applications
- The scalability of qEUBO to high-dimensional problems and its sensitivity to hyperparameters like the utility function choice remain underexplored
- The claim that qEUBO outperforms state-of-the-art methods is supported by experimental results, but the comparison is limited to a specific set of acquisition functions
- The paper does not address potential trade-offs in computational cost between qEUBO and other methods, which could be relevant in practice
- The analysis of query efficiency with more than two alternatives, while promising, lacks a rigorous theoretical foundation for why this approach is superior in all cases

## Confidence
- Theoretical guarantees (High): The proofs for Bayes optimality and convergence rates are mathematically rigorous
- Empirical performance claims (Medium): Experiments are thorough but limited in scope
- Query efficiency with multiple alternatives (Medium): Empirical results are strong, but theoretical justification is incomplete
