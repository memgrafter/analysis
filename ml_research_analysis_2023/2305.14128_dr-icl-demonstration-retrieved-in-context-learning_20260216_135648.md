# Dr.ICL: Demonstration-Retrieved In-context Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.14128
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14128
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Key outcome**: Retrieval-based ICL with BM25 outperforms random demonstrations and extends to instruction-finetuned LLMs and Chain-of-Thought prompting

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Dr.ICL, a demonstration-retrieved in-context learning approach that uses retrieval to find relevant examples for prompting language models. The method shows that even simple word-overlap similarity measures like BM25 outperform randomly selected demonstrations, and extends retrieval-based ICL to instruction-finetuned LLMs and Chain-of-Thought prompting. The study demonstrates that retrieving demonstrations from training data at test time yields better results than using no demonstrations or random demonstrations, and proposes training a task-specific demonstration retriever that outperforms off-the-shelf retrievers.

## Method Summary
The method involves retrieving demonstrations from training data using similarity measures like BM25 or GTR, and formatting them with queries for LLM inference. A task-specific demonstration retriever is trained using contrastive loss with language model feedback to learn which demonstrations lead to accurate predictions. The approach is evaluated on five datasets using PaLM and Flan-PaLM models, with and without Chain-of-Thought prompting for reasoning tasks.

## Key Results
- BM25 retrieval outperforms random demonstrations across all tested datasets
- Task-specific demonstration retrievers outperform off-the-shelf retrievers in almost all cases
- One-shot ICL shows more noticeable improvement with task-specific retrievers compared to few-shot
- Retrieval-based ICL improves performance on instruction-finetuned LLMs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Word-overlap similarity measures like BM25 capture task-relevant surface patterns that help LLMs infer task structure from demonstrations. These measures prioritize demonstrations containing overlapping terms with the query, signaling relevance to the task at hand. This works when task success depends on demonstrations sharing lexical overlap with queries rather than purely semantic similarity.

### Mechanism 2
Retrieving demonstrations from training data at test time improves performance even for instruction-finetuned LLMs because training data contains canonical demonstrations that capture task structure. Retrieval supplements the model's existing knowledge by providing task-specific examples that align with the query, even when the model has already seen the training data.

### Mechanism 3
Training a task-specific demonstration retriever outperforms off-the-shelf retrievers because these models learn to prioritize demonstrations that improve model prediction accuracy rather than just semantic similarity or keyword overlap. They capture both query-specific knowledge and general task structure, optimizing for demonstration quality rather than retrieval relevance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Retriever (BM25/GTR/task-specific) -> LLM (PaLM/Flan-PaLM) -> Training data -> CoT (optional)
- **Critical path**: 1) Prepare retrieval corpus from training data, 2) Select retriever, 3) Retrieve demonstrations for each query, 4) Format demonstrations with query, 5) Generate answer using LLM, 6) (Optional) Apply CoT for reasoning tasks
- **Design tradeoffs**: BM25 vs GTR (speed vs semantic capture), one-shot vs few-shot (efficiency vs demonstration quantity), task-specific vs off-the-shelf retrievers (performance vs training time)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor retrieval quality (no improvement over random), overfitting (good on training data, poor on test), label leakage (retrieved demonstrations contain correct answers)
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Compare BM25 vs random demonstrations on classification task with PaLM, 2) Test Flan-PaLM with/without retrieved demonstrations on NLI task, 3) Train task-specific retriever on reasoning task and compare to GTR

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of BM25 compare to GTR when retrieving demonstrations for instruction-finetuned LLMs on tasks outside the five studied datasets? The paper shows both outperform random demonstrations, but evidence is limited to five specific datasets.

### Open Question 2
What are the limitations of using the probability of the gold answer as a score for demonstration quality in the demonstration retriever training process? The paper uses pLM(yq | d, xq) but doesn't discuss potential biases or limitations of this scoring method.

### Open Question 3
How does the demonstration retriever's performance scale with the size of the training corpus? The paper mentions creating separate retrieval corpora for each task but doesn't discuss corpus size impact on retriever effectiveness.

## Limitations

- Limited dataset diversity with evaluation only on five specific datasets
- Potential label leakage risk when retrieving demonstrations from training data containing ground truth answers
- Effectiveness depends on training data quality and representativeness of test distribution

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: BM25 outperforms random demonstrations (strong empirical support across multiple datasets and models)
- **Medium Confidence**: Task-specific retrievers outperform off-the-shelf retrievers (supported but improvement magnitude varies)
- **Low Confidence**: Retrieval benefits instruction-finetuned LLMs (may be confounded by other factors)

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Dataset Generalization Test**: Evaluate retrieval-based ICL on a held-out dataset from a different domain (e.g., biomedical or legal text) to assess generalizability to novel domains requiring different reasoning types.

2. **Retrieval Quality Analysis**: Conduct detailed analysis measuring semantic similarity between retrieved demonstrations and queries, presence of answer leakage, and overlap ratio between retrieved demonstrations and ground truth answers.

3. **Ablation Study on Retriever Components**: Perform ablation study removing key components of task-specific retriever training (e.g., contrastive loss, language model feedback) to isolate which aspects contribute most to performance improvements.
