# On the existence of solutions to adversarial training in multiclass classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00075
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00075
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proves the existence of Borel measurable robust classifiers in three models of adversarial training for multiclass classification.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies three models of adversarial training in multiclass classification: distributional-perturbing (DRO), closed-ball, and open-ball models. The authors prove the existence of Borel measurable robust classifiers in each model and establish connections between them through optimal transport theory and total variation regularization. A key contribution is proving the existence of Borel measurable solutions to the agnostic adversarial training problem in the binary classification setting, improving upon previous literature where robust classifiers were only known to exist within the enlarged universal sigma-algebra of the feature space.

## Method Summary
The paper uses optimal transport theory and total variation regularization to study adversarial training. The approach involves approximating the lower semi-continuous cost function with a sequence of bounded Lipschitz costs, solving dual problems for each approximation, and taking weak* limits to obtain Borel measurable solutions. The framework connects adversarial training to generalized barycenter problems and multimarginal optimal transport, enabling the use of computational optimal transport tools for solving these problems.

## Key Results
- Existence of Borel measurable solutions for the distributional-perturbing (DRO) model
- Equivalence of solutions between the DRO model and the closed-ball model
- Connection between the closed-ball model and the open-ball model for all but countably many values of the perturbation parameter epsilon
- Existence of Borel measurable solutions to the agnostic adversarial training problem in binary classification

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - DRO model solver (Theorem 2.5) → Closed-ball model solver (Theorem 2.9) → Open-ball model solver (Theorem 2.10)
  - Approximation engine (Lemma 3.7) → Dual problem solver (Theorem 3.4) → Robust classifier constructor (Proposition 3.10)
  - Measurability checker (Proposition 4.1) → Solution equivalence verifier (Theorem 2.9)

- Critical path:
  1. Verify cost function satisfies Assumption 2.3
  2. Construct approximation sequence {cn} and solve dual problems
  3. Take weak* limit to get solution g* for DRO problem
  4. Construct Borel measurable classifier f* from g*
  5. Verify f* solves both DRO and closed-ball models
  6. Check if ε is a continuity point for the open-ball model

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using DRO model provides well-definedness but requires approximation machinery
  - Working with universal sigma-algebra would simplify some proofs but creates measurability issues
  - The countable exceptions in Theorem 2.10 are a necessary consequence of the structure of the open-ball model

- Failure signatures:
  - If Assumption 2.3 fails, the approximation argument breaks down
  - If the feature space doesn't have precompact bounded subsets, the dual problem may not have solutions
  - At exceptional ε values, the closed-ball and open-ball models may have different solutions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the approximation sequence {cn} from Lemma 3.7 converges to c for a simple cost function like c(x,x') = |x-x'| on R
  2. Test the construction of f* from g* on a finite empirical measure to verify Borel measurability
  3. Check the equivalence between DRO and closed-ball models for a simple binary classification problem with synthetic data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What properties must a parametric family of classifiers satisfy to approximate optimal robust classifiers in adversarial training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention this as an important practical question, noting that while they prove existence of optimal robust classifiers, extending results to parametric families like neural networks remains an open challenge.
- Why unresolved: The paper establishes theoretical existence results but doesn't analyze how well parametric families can approximate these optimal solutions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis showing specific conditions on parametric families that guarantee approximation of optimal robust classifiers to arbitrary precision, potentially with quantitative bounds on the number of parameters needed.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can we characterize the regularity of optimal robust classifiers and quantify the smoothness of class boundaries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note this as an open geometric/theoretical question, suggesting that since optimal classifiers need not be unique, one could ask when it's possible to find at least one optimal classifier with specific regularity properties.
- Why unresolved: While the paper proves existence of optimal classifiers, it doesn't provide characterization of their geometric or regularity properties.
- What evidence would resolve it: A mathematical characterization of the regularity (e.g., Sobolev smoothness, bounded variation) of optimal robust classifiers, along with proofs of when such regular optimal solutions exist.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the framework be extended to non-0-1 loss functions like cross-entropy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors identify this as a crucial direction for practical implementation, noting that while their analysis assumes 0-1 loss, practitioners prefer strongly convex losses.
- Why unresolved: The theoretical results rely heavily on the structure of the 0-1 loss, and extending to other loss functions would require new analytical techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: A generalized framework that establishes existence and characterization results for adversarial training with arbitrary convex loss functions, potentially using similar optimal transport connections.

## Limitations
- The results rely on strict technical conditions (Assumption 2.3) on the cost function that may not hold for all practical adversarial training scenarios
- The theoretical focus is on existence rather than construction or computation of robust classifiers
- The countable exceptions in Theorem 2.10 mean the closed-ball and open-ball models may have different solutions at specific perturbation parameter values
