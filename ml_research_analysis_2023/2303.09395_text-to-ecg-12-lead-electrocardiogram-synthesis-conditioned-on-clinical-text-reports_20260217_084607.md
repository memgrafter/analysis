# Text-to-ECG: 12-Lead Electrocardiogram Synthesis conditioned on Clinical Text Reports

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09395
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09395
- Reference count: 0
- This work introduces Auto-TTE, a text-to-ECG generative model that synthesizes 12-lead ECG signals from clinical text reports

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Auto-TTE, a novel text-to-ECG generation framework that synthesizes 12-lead electrocardiogram signals from clinical text reports. The system employs a VQ-VAE for discrete signal encoding, an autoregressive Transformer for sequence modeling conditioned on text and patient embeddings, and a HiFi-GAN decoder for high-fidelity waveform reconstruction. The method is evaluated on two datasets (PTB-XL and Sejong) and shows improvements over diffusion and Tacotron-based baselines across multiple metrics including AUROC, CLIP score, and FID.

## Method Summary
Auto-TTE uses a VQ-VAE to discretize raw ECG signals into a learned codebook representation. An autoregressive Transformer model is trained to predict the discretized signal sequence conditioned on tokenized clinical text and patient-specific embeddings. The generated discrete codes are then converted back to continuous waveforms using a HiFi-GAN decoder. This architecture enables generation of realistic 12-lead ECG signals that align with clinical text descriptions while maintaining temporal and morphological characteristics of real ECG data.

## Key Results
- Auto-TTE achieves AUROC of 0.857, outperforming diffusion (0.834) and Tacotron (0.721) baselines
- CLIP score reaches 0.644, exceeding both diffusion (0.621) and Tacotron (0.371) methods
- FID score of 7.17 improves upon diffusion (9.18) and Tacotron (22.93) baselines
- Human evaluation by three board-certified cardiologists ranked Auto-TTE highest in realism and text-ECG alignment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The VQ-VAE enables efficient discrete representation of continuous ECG signals, making them suitable for autoregressive modeling. The Transformer architecture effectively captures long-range dependencies in both text and ECG sequences, while the conditional attention mechanism ensures alignment between generated signals and clinical descriptions. HiFi-GAN's adversarial training produces high-fidelity waveforms that preserve clinically relevant features like QRS complexes and ST segments.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Clinical Text -> Tokenizer -> Transformer -> VQ-VAE -> HiFi-GAN -> Generated ECG
**Critical Path**: Text encoding → Patient embedding → Discrete code generation → Waveform reconstruction
**Design Tradeoffs**: VQ-VAE provides efficient compression but may lose fine-grained details; Transformer enables strong conditioning but requires large datasets; HiFi-GAN produces high quality but may introduce artifacts
**Failure Signatures**: Poor text-ECG alignment indicates conditioning issues; waveform artifacts suggest decoder problems; low diversity points to codebook limitations

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on proxy metrics that measure distributional similarity rather than clinical utility
- Human evaluation by only three cardiologists limits generalizability and introduces potential subjectivity
- Does not address safety concerns regarding synthetic ECGs being mistaken for real patient data
