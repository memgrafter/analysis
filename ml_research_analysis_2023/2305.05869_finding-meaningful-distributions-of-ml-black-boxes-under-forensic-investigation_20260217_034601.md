# Finding Meaningful Distributions of ML Black-boxes under Forensic Investigation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05869
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05869
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Proposed method improves model cloning accuracy from 45.5% to 72.0% by selecting meaningful training distributions

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of identifying the training data distribution of a black-box ML model using only hard-label access. The authors propose a novel method that leverages a comprehensive hierarchical corpus (like ImageNet) to select datasets that are semantically and functionally close to the model's true training distribution. By combining functional scores (measuring model responsiveness to transformed inputs) with semantic scores (measuring class closeness in the hierarchy), the method can identify candidate distributions that lead to significantly improved performance in follow-up tasks like model cloning.

## Method Summary
The method takes as input a black-box model with hard-label access and a hierarchical corpus containing both data samples and metadata. It computes functional scores by measuring how consistently the model classifies transformed versions of samples from each class, assuming models trained on a distribution will show transformation invariance. Semantic scores are computed using inverse shortest path distances in the class hierarchy. The method then uses K-medoids clustering to group high-scoring classes and selects the cluster with the best combined objective score. This selected subset serves as the candidate training distribution for downstream forensic investigations.

## Key Results
- Model cloning accuracy improved from 45.5% to 72.0% when using datasets selected by the proposed method
- The method successfully identifies meaningful distributions even when starting from unrelated datasets like Caltech 101
- Both functional and semantic components of the objective function contribute to selection quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Functional score captures how well the model's predictions align with the semantic structure of the hierarchical corpus.
- **Mechanism:** The method measures the consistency of model predictions when perturbed samples are fed through the model. If the model was trained on a distribution close to a node in the corpus, it will consistently classify perturbed samples from that node's data as belonging to the same class.
- **Core assumption:** The target model exhibits transformation invariance on data from its training distribution but not on unrelated data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The core of our method is an objective function that considers two criteria on the selected samples: the model functional properties (derived from the dataset), and semantics (derived from the metadata)."
  - [section 4.1.1] "A sensible model should be trained to classify a group of similar objects into a fixed class with high accuracy. Therefore, most samples from the same or very similar distributions should be classified into the same class."
- **Break condition:** If the model was trained on highly domain-specific data not well represented in the corpus, the functional score will not distinguish between candidate distributions effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Semantic score leverages hierarchical structure to measure semantic meaningfulness of candidate distributions.
- **Mechanism:** By computing pairwise closeness between samples in the candidate set using inverse shortest path distance in the hierarchy, the method ensures selected samples form a semantically coherent group rather than a random collection.
- **Core assumption:** The hierarchical corpus (e.g., ImageNet tree) accurately captures semantic relationships between classes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.1.2] "We use CL_T(·, ·) to denote the closeness between the classes of two samples according to the metadata T supplied by the hierarchical corpus."
  - [section 4.1.2] "The closeness of two instances in the selection is measured by using the inverse of distance of the shortest path between their respective nodes in the tree."
- **Break condition:** If the corpus hierarchy is too coarse or contains incorrect semantic relationships, the semantic score will mislead the selection process.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** K-medoids clustering on functional scores and closeness matrix effectively finds meaningful subsets.
- **Mechanism:** After computing functional scores for all leaf nodes, the method clusters nodes with high functional scores using their pairwise closeness. This groups semantically similar, high-scoring classes together.
- **Core assumption:** Semantically similar classes with high functional scores are likely to represent the true training distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] "In our implementation, we run K-medoids [11] algorithm on the pairwise distance matrix."
  - [section 4.2] "We then choose the cluster with the highest functional score."
- **Break condition:** If the number of clusters is poorly chosen or the threshold η is inappropriate, the method may merge unrelated classes or split related ones.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Hierarchical data organization (trees, ontologies)
  - **Why needed here:** The method relies on hierarchical relationships between classes to measure semantic similarity. Understanding how tree structures encode semantic relationships is crucial for implementing and debugging the semantic score.
  - **Quick check question:** Given a tree where "vehicle" has children "car" and "truck", what is the closeness between "car" and "truck" versus "car" and "airplane"?

- **Concept:** Transformation invariance in neural networks
  - **Why needed here:** The functional score depends on the model's invariance to transformations like translation and rotation. Knowing when and why models exhibit such invariance helps interpret functional scores.
  - **Quick check question:** If a model was trained only on centered, upright images, how would its functional score change when tested on rotated versions of those images?

- **Concept:** K-medoids clustering algorithm
  - **Why needed here:** The method uses K-medoids to group semantically similar, high-scoring classes. Understanding this algorithm is necessary for implementing the search step and tuning parameters like number of clusters.
  - **Quick check question:** How does K-medoids differ from K-means, and why might it be more appropriate for this application?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Black-box model -> Functional score computation -> Semantic score computation -> K-medoids clustering -> Selected subset
- **Critical path:** Compute functional scores → Compute pairwise closeness → Run K-medoids → Select best cluster → Filter samples
- **Design tradeoffs:** Using only functional score vs. combining with semantic score; number of clusters in K-medoids; threshold for filtering high-scoring classes
- **Failure signatures:** Low functional scores across all classes; clusters containing semantically unrelated classes; selected subset performs poorly in downstream tasks
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test functional score on a model trained on CIFAR-10 using ImageNet as corpus; verify top-scoring classes match semantically
  2. Run full method with both scores enabled on the same CIFAR-10 model; compare selected subset to ground truth
  3. Use selected subset for model cloning; measure accuracy improvement versus using random or hand-picked datasets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How effective are the proposed countermeasures against adaptive attacks that can detect and bypass the backdoor planting strategy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses a custom countermeasure involving adaptive backdoor planting but does not evaluate its robustness against sophisticated attacks.
- Why unresolved: The proposed defense is only theoretically described without empirical validation against real adversarial techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the countermeasure against known adversarial techniques like adversarial training or detection-based methods would demonstrate its effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the method be extended to handle non-hierarchical datasets or different data types such as text and graphs effectively?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the method is theoretically compatible with different data types but does not provide experimental validation beyond image datasets.
- Why unresolved: The method's applicability to non-image domains like text and graphs remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the method to text classification tasks or graph neural networks and evaluating its performance would provide empirical support.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of the weighting parameter α in the objective function affect the balance between functional and semantic scores, and what is the optimal strategy for selecting it?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses α as a hyperparameter but does not explore its sensitivity or provide guidelines for optimal selection.
- Why unresolved: The impact of α on the final performance and the method's robustness to its choice is not analyzed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting ablation studies with varying α values and analyzing the resulting performance would clarify its role and optimal selection strategy.

## Limitations
- Method effectiveness depends heavily on the quality and comprehensiveness of the hierarchical corpus
- Assumes target models exhibit transformation invariance on training distribution data
- Key hyperparameters (K-medoids clusters, functional score threshold) lack thorough sensitivity analysis

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** The functional score mechanism for measuring model responsiveness to transformations is well-grounded and supported by evidence from the paper.
- **Medium confidence:** The semantic score leveraging hierarchical relationships is plausible but relies heavily on the quality of the corpus hierarchy.
- **Medium confidence:** The K-medoids clustering approach for combining functional and semantic scores is reasonable but depends on appropriate hyperparameter selection.
