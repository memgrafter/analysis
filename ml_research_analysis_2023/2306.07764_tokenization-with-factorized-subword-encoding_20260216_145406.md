# Tokenization with Factorized Subword Encoding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07764
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07764
- Reference count: 22
- Outperforms BPE tokenization on language modeling and morpho-syntactic tasks across 7 diverse languages

## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Factorizer, a novel tokenization method that leverages a VQ-VAE model to factorize subwords into discrete triplets of indices. This approach is designed to preserve character-level information while maintaining sequence length, addressing a key limitation of existing tokenization methods like BPE. Evaluated on language modeling and morpho-syntactic tasks across 7 diverse languages, Factorizer demonstrates superior performance compared to BPE, particularly in part-of-speech tagging, dependency parsing, and lemmatization. The method also shows robustness to noise and data scarcity during pretraining, making it a promising alternative for tokenization in low-resource and noisy environments.

## Method Summary
Factorizer employs a VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder) to factorize subwords into discrete triplets of indices. This factorization process allows the model to maintain character-level information without increasing the length of tokenized sequences, a significant advantage over traditional tokenization methods like BPE. The VQ-VAE learns to encode subwords into a latent space, where they are represented as discrete triplets. These triplets are then used as tokens for downstream tasks. The method is evaluated on language modeling and morpho-syntactic tasks, including part-of-speech tagging, dependency parsing, and lemmatization, across 7 diverse languages. The results demonstrate that Factorizer outperforms BPE in these tasks, highlighting its effectiveness and versatility.

## Key Results
- Factorizer outperforms BPE on part-of-speech tagging, dependency parsing, and lemmatization tasks across 7 diverse languages.
- The method is more robust to noise and data scarcity during pretraining compared to BPE.
- Factorizer maintains character-level information without increasing the length of tokenized sequences.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of Factorizer stems from its ability to factorize subwords into discrete triplets of indices using a VQ-VAE. This factorization preserves character-level information, which is crucial for tasks like lemmatization and part-of-speech tagging. By maintaining sequence length, Factorizer avoids the trade-off between granularity and efficiency that is often encountered with traditional tokenization methods. The VQ-VAE's discrete latent space ensures that the factorization is both interpretable and computationally efficient, enabling the model to handle noise and data scarcity effectively.
