# Deep Stochastic Processes via Functional Markov Transition Operators

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15574
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15574
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces Markov Neural Processes (MNPs) that outperform baseline models including Gaussian Processes, Copula-Based Processes, and standard Neural Processes on various tasks while maintaining theoretical validity as stochastic processes.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Markov Neural Processes (MNPs), a new class of stochastic processes constructed by stacking sequences of neural-parameterized Markov transition operators in function space. The authors prove that these operators preserve the exchangeability and consistency of stochastic processes, providing increased flexibility and expressivity compared to existing neural process models without compromising their fundamental properties. Experiments show that MNPs achieve better marginal log-likelihoods on 1D function regression problems including challenging non-Gaussian data, significantly improve performance on contextual bandit problems, and demonstrate promising results on geological data inference.

## Method Summary
The authors construct MNPs by applying a sequence of Markov transition operators to an initial stochastic process. Each transition operator is parameterized by neural networks and transforms the current process into a more expressive one while preserving the mathematical properties required for a valid stochastic process. The model uses latent variables and normalizing flows to enable flexible transformations, with inference performed using a variational approach that shares parameters with the generative model. The architecture employs set transformers for permutation-invariant encoding of context data and rational quadratic spline flows for invertible transformations.

## Key Results
- MNPs achieve better marginal log-likelihoods on 1D function regression problems compared to Gaussian Processes, Copula-Based Processes, and standard Neural Processes
- The model significantly improves performance on contextual bandit problems, particularly on challenging wheel bandit scenarios
- MNPs demonstrate promising results on geological data inference from the GeoFluvial dataset, showing better likelihood estimates than baseline methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Stacking Markov transition operators in function space creates a valid stochastic process that preserves exchangeability and consistency.
- Mechanism: Each Markov transition operator transforms an existing stochastic process into a more expressive one while maintaining the mathematical properties needed for a valid stochastic process. The composition of multiple such operators creates increasingly flexible distributions over functions.
- Core assumption: The Markov transition operators are properly parameterized neural networks that satisfy the consistency and exchangeability conditions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We prove that these Markov transition operators can preserve the exchangeability and consistency of SPs"
  - [section]: "If the collection of marginals before transition {px1:n(y(0)1:n)}x1:n is consistent and exchangeable... and the collection of MMTOs {px1:n(y1:n|y(0)1:n)}x1:n is also consistent and exchangeable... then the collection of marginals after transition {px1:n(y1:n)} is also consistent and exchangeable, hence defining a valid SP."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the neural network parameterization fails to satisfy the consistency or exchangeability conditions, the resulting model would not be a valid stochastic process.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The auxiliary latent variables allow flexible transformations while maintaining the ability to compute marginal distributions.
- Mechanism: The latent variables z act as conditioning variables that enable complex, non-linear transformations of the function outputs at each step. These transformations can capture complex dependencies while still allowing marginalization over the latents to recover valid marginal distributions.
- Core assumption: The transformation functions Fθ are invertible and the latent variable distribution pθ(z) is properly specified.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We introduce Markov Neural Processes (MNPs), a new class of Stochastic Processes (SPs) which are constructed by stacking sequences of neural parameterised Markov transition operators in function space."
  - [section]: "Equation (9) provides a valid construction of exchangeable and consistent MMTOs by introducing an auxiliary latent variable z."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the transformations are not invertible or the latent variable distribution is misspecified, the model cannot properly marginalize and would lose its validity as a stochastic process.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The inference model with shared parameters enables efficient learning by reusing the generative model's transformations in reverse.
- Mechanism: The inference model uses the same neural network parameters as the generative model but applies them in reverse order with reversed data flow. This allows the model to efficiently approximate the posterior distribution over latent variables without requiring separate, potentially incompatible networks.
- Core assumption: The generative model's transformations are sufficiently expressive to capture the posterior distribution when applied in reverse.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Therefore, we share the parameters of the normalising flows F(1:T)θ between the generative and inference models, leading to better performance."
  - [section]: "In practice, the inference model qx1:n(z(1:T)|y1:n; ϕ) provides an approximate prior/posterior."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the generative model is insufficiently expressive or the reverse application fails to capture the posterior structure, the inference approximation will be poor and training will be ineffective.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Initial SP -> T transition operators -> Final SP
- Critical path: Initial Stochastic Process → T transition operators → Final SP
  The data flows through T sequential transformations, each parameterized by neural networks and conditioned on both the previous state and context information.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Expressivity vs. computational cost: More transition steps increase flexibility but require more computation and memory
  - Latent variable complexity vs. inference difficulty: More complex latent distributions allow better modeling but make inference harder
  - Shared vs. separate parameters: Sharing parameters between generative and inference models reduces memory but may limit expressiveness

- Failure signatures:
  - Underfitting: Model performs similarly to baseline NPs, indicating insufficient expressiveness
  - Training instability: Poor convergence or exploding/vanishing gradients during training
  - Posterior collapse: Latent variables become uninformative, reducing model flexibility
  - Memory issues: Running out of GPU memory with larger context sizes or more transition steps

- First 3 experiments:
  1. 1D function regression with GP data: Start with simple Gaussian Process data to verify the model can recover known distributions and compare performance against baseline GPs.
  2. Ablation study on number of transition steps: Test how performance changes with 1, 3, 5, and 7 transition steps to find the optimal balance between expressivity and computational cost.
  3. Contextual bandit problem: Test on the wheel bandit problem to verify the model can handle sequential decision-making tasks where uncertainty quantification is critical.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can continuous-time Markov transitions (stochastic differential equations in function space) be effectively implemented to reduce memory usage during backpropagation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "To alleviate this, an interesting direction for future work would be to consider continuous-time Markov transitions, i.e. stochastic differential equations in function space, which require less memory by using adjoint methods [9, 37]."
- Why unresolved: The authors only mention this as a future direction without providing implementation details or experimental results. Continuous-time approaches would require developing new numerical methods and testing their effectiveness compared to the current discrete-time approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: A working implementation showing reduced memory consumption during training while maintaining comparable or improved performance metrics compared to the discrete-time approach.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can other forms of marginal Markov transition operators (MMTOs) that do not require latent variables be developed while maintaining exchangeability and consistency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "While Equation (9) provides a valid general construction of exchangeable and consistent MMTOs with latent variables, it may be feasible to investigate other forms of MMTOs that do not necessitate latent variables, thereby eliminating the need for variational approaches."
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores the latent variable approach for MMTOs and suggests this as a future direction without providing concrete alternatives or demonstrating their feasibility.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical proof and practical implementation showing a different construction of MMTOs that preserves exchangeability and consistency without requiring auxiliary latent variables.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do group equivariant convolutional layers improve empirical performance of MNPs when applied to preserve inherent symmetries in data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "There are often inherent symmetries in data (e.g. translational symmetries for stationary SPs) and it may be possible to improve empirical performance of MNPs using (group equivariant) convolutions that preserve certain symmetries [18, 25, 29]."
- Why unresolved: The authors identify this as an orthogonal improvement that could be combined with their approach but do not implement or evaluate these methods, leaving the potential performance gains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing standard MNPs against versions incorporating group equivariant convolutions on datasets with known symmetries, demonstrating improved performance or sample efficiency.

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation on real-world datasets beyond synthetic 1D functions and one geological dataset
- Theoretical proofs assume idealized conditions that may not hold in practice with finite data and neural network approximations
- No ablation studies on the relative importance of different architectural components
