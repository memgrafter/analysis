# Ten Lessons We Have Learned in the New "Sparseland": A Short Handbook for Sparse Neural Network Researchers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02596
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02596
- Reference count: 20

## Executive Summary
This article provides a comprehensive overview of the rapidly evolving field of sparse neural networks (SNNs), addressing common confusions and misconceptions. It categorizes SNNs into various types based on when sparsity is introduced (before, during, or after training), the structure of sparsity (unstructured vs. structured), and the training approach (dense-to-sparse vs. sparse-to-sparse). The authors clarify the differences between these categories and emphasize the importance of fair comparisons among different SNN algorithms. They also highlight the practical relevance of unstructured sparsity, despite its limited hardware support on commodity GPUs, and discuss the potential for converting unstructured sparsity to structured sparsity. Overall, this article serves as a valuable resource for researchers entering or working in the SNN field.

## Method Summary
This paper does not propose a new method but instead summarizes and clarifies common confusions in sparse neural network (SNN) research. It categorizes SNNs by when sparsity is introduced (before, during, or after training), structure (unstructured vs. structured), and training approach (dense-to-sparse vs. sparse-to-sparse). The paper provides recommendations for fair and accurate evaluation of SNNs, including the use of diverse datasets, controlling confounding variables, and reporting error bars with multiple random seeds. The focus is on conceptual clarification and best practices for evaluating SNNs rather than presenting specific experimental results or protocols.

## Key Results
- Structured pruning can achieve similar accuracy to unstructured pruning while enabling hardware acceleration
- Dynamic sparse training can train sparse networks from scratch without sacrificing accuracy compared to dense training
- Unstructured sparsity provides a valuable research platform and can be converted to structured sparsity for deployment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Structured pruning can achieve similar accuracy to unstructured pruning while enabling hardware acceleration.
- Mechanism: Structured pruning removes entire groups of weights (e.g., channels, filters, neurons), creating sparsity patterns compatible with specialized hardware accelerators that can exploit these regular structures.
- Core assumption: The grouped structure removal doesn't critically damage the model's representational capacity for the given task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states "structured pruning involves the selective removal of an entire group of weights... amenable to hardware speedup"
  - [section] explains "structured pruning... encompasses various more types of sparse patterns like block-wise, neuron-wise, channel/ﬁlter-wise"
- Break condition: If the group structure is too coarse-grained, removing entire groups may cause disproportionate accuracy loss compared to fine-grained unstructured pruning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Dynamic sparse training (DST) can train sparse networks from scratch without sacrificing accuracy compared to dense training.
- Mechanism: DST starts with a sparse network and dynamically adjusts the sparse mask during training using pruning and regrowth criteria, maintaining sparsity while adapting to training signals.
- Core assumption: The sparse mask evolution can find and maintain important connections throughout training without needing the full dense network as a reference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] describes DST as "starts from a randomly initialized sparse neural network and dynamically adjusts the sparse topology in one training run"
  - [section] explains "DST starts from a (random, or PaI-generated) sparse neural network and allows the sparse connectivity to evolve dynamically during training"
- Break condition: If the pruning/growth criteria are poorly designed, the sparse mask may converge to a suboptimal configuration that limits model capacity.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Unstructured sparsity, despite hardware limitations, provides a valuable research platform and can be converted to structured sparsity for deployment.
- Mechanism: Unstructured sparsity serves as a fine-grained testbed for SNN algorithms, and recent methods can convert unstructured solutions to structured patterns without significant accuracy loss.
- Core assumption: The conversion process preserves the essential connectivity patterns that determine model performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] states "Chenet al. (2022a) introduce weight reﬁlling and weight regrouping... converting unstructured LTH solutions into ﬁne-grained group-wise patterns"
  - [section] mentions "Jianget al.(2022a)... convert unstructured sparsity to structured sparsity, during dynamic sparse training"
- Break condition: If the conversion introduces significant approximation errors or the regrouping disrupts critical weight dependencies, accuracy may degrade substantially.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pruning criterion -> Growth criterion -> Mask management -> Hardware backend
- Critical path: Initialize sparse network → Apply mask updates during training → Convert to structured format (if needed) → Deploy on hardware
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fine-grained (unstructured) vs coarse-grained (structured) sparsity for accuracy vs hardware efficiency
  - Dynamic vs static masks for adaptation vs implementation simplicity
  - Memory efficiency vs computational efficiency depending on sparsity pattern
- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy degradation despite high sparsity suggests poor mask quality or overly aggressive pruning
  - Training instability in DST indicates poorly tuned growth criteria
  - Poor hardware utilization suggests mismatch between sparsity pattern and accelerator architecture

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can unstructured sparsity patterns be effectively converted to structured sparsity without significant accuracy loss, and what are the optimal algorithms for this conversion process?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that several algorithms support the conversion from unstructured sparsity (at training time) to structured sparsity (at inference time), citing Chen et al. (2022a) and Jiang et al. (2022a) as examples.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the convertibility but does not provide a comprehensive comparison of different conversion methods or their effectiveness across various model architectures and sparsity levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the accuracy, computational efficiency, and hardware compatibility of different conversion algorithms across diverse neural network architectures and sparsity levels.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the theoretical foundations and learning theory principles that rigorously relate sparsity to neural network representation, architecture, optimization, or generalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper lists "Learning theory foundations, rigorously relating sparsity to neural network representation, architecture, optimization, or generalization" as one of the important research questions in the SNN field.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed discussion of the theoretical underpinnings of sparsity in neural networks, instead focusing on practical aspects and common confusions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analyses and mathematical proofs establishing the relationships between sparsity patterns and neural network properties, supported by empirical validations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we develop standardized evaluation protocols and metrics for fair and trusted comparisons among different sparse neural network algorithms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper dedicates a section to discussing the importance of fair comparisons among SNN algorithms and provides recommendations for positioning, datasets, configurations, and metrics.
- Why unresolved: Despite the recommendations, there is no widely accepted standard for evaluating and comparing SNN algorithms, leading to inconsistent and potentially unfair comparisons in the literature.
- What evidence would resolve it: A consensus on evaluation protocols and metrics, validated through community-wide adoption and consistent results across different studies.
