# Measuring Inductive Biases of In-Context Learning with Underspecified Demonstrations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13299
- Reference count: 10
- This paper investigates the inductive biases of in-context learning (ICL) in large language models (LLMs) by measuring their feature biases when given underspecified demonstrations.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the inductive biases of in-context learning (ICL) in large language models (LLMs) by measuring their feature biases when given underspecified demonstrations. The authors construct prompts where two features are equally predictive of the labels and evaluate the model's tendency to use one feature over another. They find that LLMs exhibit clear feature biases, such as a strong preference for sentiment over other features in sentiment analysis tasks. The study also evaluates the effectiveness of various interventions, including natural language instructions, semantically relevant label words, and template explanations, in steering the model towards a particular feature. Results show that while these interventions can influence the model's feature preference, they are less effective when the model has a strong prior bias. The findings highlight the challenges of using ICL as a general-purpose machine learning method and provide insights into how to better align model biases with intended tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors measure feature biases in ICL by constructing underspecified demonstrations where two features are equally predictive of labels. They create test sets where these features disagree and measure which feature the model prefers using h-accuracy. The study uses GPT-3 models (Davinci and Text-Davinci-002) with prompts containing 16 demonstration examples, evaluating on 1,200 test examples balanced between feature pairs. Various interventions are tested including natural language instructions, semantic verbalizers, and template explanations to assess their effectiveness in steering model behavior towards intended features.

## Key Results
- LLMs exhibit strong feature biases, with sentiment analysis models showing a clear preference for sentiment over other features
- Instruction-tuned models are generally more aligned with dataset labels compared to base models
- Semantic verbalizers can effectively override prior feature biases by providing explicit semantic context for labels
- Interventions are less effective when the model has strong prior feature biases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: In-context learning models generalize based on feature biases that emerge from pretraining, even when demonstrations are underspecified.
- Mechanism: When demonstrations contain two equally predictive features, the model relies on prior learned associations from pretraining to select which feature to generalize from, rather than learning the mapping from examples.
- Core assumption: The pretraining distribution contains correlations that create strong feature preferences that persist during ICL.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we characterize the feature biases of GPT-3 models by constructing underspecified demonstrations from a range of NLP datasets and feature combinations"
  - [section 2.1] "We measure whether f is more consistent with h1 or h2 by comparing the predictions of f, h1, and h2 on Dtest"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus shows related work on ICL biases but no direct evidence about pretraining feature correlations
- Break condition: If pretraining data lacks strong correlations between features and labels, or if model architecture changes prevent transfer of these biases to ICL.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Instruction-tuned models show stronger alignment with dataset labels compared to base models due to exposure to similar task formats during tuning.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning on instruction datasets creates stronger associations between specific feature patterns and their semantic interpretations, making models more likely to select the intended feature even in ambiguous contexts.
- Core assumption: Instruction tuning provides sufficient exposure to semantic features that the model develops robust feature representations beyond shallow patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "the instruction-tuned model is generally more aligned with standard dataset labels" and "the TEXT-DAVINCI -002 model, which was trained with human feedback, always prefers the original task hypothesis"
  - [section 5.2] "For the TEXT-DAVINCI -002 model, instructions are much more effective"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus contains related work on instruction tuning but no specific evidence about feature preference changes
- Break condition: If instruction tuning dataset lacks sufficient diversity or if the model overfits to specific instruction patterns without generalizing feature understanding.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Semantic verbalizers can override prior feature biases by providing explicit semantic context for labels, making them more effective than instructions or explanations in some cases.
- Mechanism: By mapping labels to words semantically related to the intended feature, verbalizers create stronger feature-label associations that compete with the model's prior biases from pretraining.
- Core assumption: The model's internal representations connect semantic words to specific feature patterns learned during pretraining.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2] "For the DAVINCI model, the best-performing interventions include semantic verbalizers"
  - [section 5.1] "the verbalizer selects label words that are semantically related to a chosen feature in order to hint at the intended task"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus shows related work on label word effects but no direct evidence about semantic verbalizers overriding biases
- Break condition: If the semantic words are too distant from the intended feature or if the model relies primarily on shallow patterns that ignore semantic context.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Feature bias measurement through disambiguating datasets
  - Why needed here: The core methodology requires creating test sets where two features disagree to measure which feature the model prefers
  - Quick check question: How do you construct a test set where h1(x) ≠ h2(x) for all examples?

- Concept: Prompt template decomposition (instance template, label verbalizer, instruction, explanation)
  - Why needed here: Understanding how different prompt components affect model behavior is essential for designing effective interventions
  - Quick check question: What are the four components of the prompt decomposition described in section 5.1?

- Concept: Label calibration to control for order effects
  - Why needed here: ICL is sensitive to demonstration order, so calibration ensures fair comparison across different prompts
  - Quick check question: Why do the authors randomly shuffle demonstration examples and perform label calibration?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GPT-3/InstructGPT model with prompt construction pipeline → feature bias measurement system → intervention evaluation framework
- Critical path: Prompt construction → model inference → accuracy calculation → bias analysis
- Design tradeoffs: Simple prompt formats vs. complex interventions; base model vs. instruction-tuned model; semantic vs. shallow features
- Failure signatures: Strong prior feature bias prevents intervention effectiveness; instruction-tuned models may overfit to specific task formats; shallow features remain difficult to override
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Measure baseline feature biases using underspecified demonstrations without any interventions
  2. Apply semantic verbalizers to test if explicit semantic context can override prior biases
  3. Compare instruction-tuned vs base model responses to the same ambiguous prompts

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do feature biases in large language models (LLMs) arise from pretraining, and what specific aspects of pretraining data contribute to these biases?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from the limitations section, which states that while empirical findings shed light on feature bias patterns, the paper does not conclude how these biases arise from pretraining.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the connection between pretraining data and feature biases, nor does it provide a theoretical account of how in-context learning develops these biases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the pretraining data for patterns that align with the observed feature biases, or developing a theoretical framework that explains how pretraining influences feature biases in ICL.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the effectiveness of intervention methods in steering LLMs towards specific features be improved for semantically irrelevant features like sentence lengths or lexical overlap?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from the limitations section, which mentions that features not related to semantics are difficult to intervene across all conditions.
- Why unresolved: The paper finds that semantically irrelevant features are harder to steer towards, but does not explore methods to improve intervention effectiveness for these features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing new intervention methods that are specifically designed to handle semantically irrelevant features, and comparing their effectiveness to existing methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the feature bias of LLMs vary across different scales of models and different pretraining data sources?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from the limitations section, which suggests that it would be interesting to study feature bias patterns on other large language models like OPT and BLOOM, trained with different data sources and scales.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on GPT-3 models and does not explore how feature biases might differ in other models with different training data or scales.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with other large language models to compare their feature biases and identifying patterns or differences related to their pretraining data and model scales.

## Limitations
- The study focuses on binary classification tasks and may not generalize to multi-class or regression problems
- The effectiveness of interventions varies significantly across different model versions, suggesting results may not transfer across all LLM architectures
- The paper does not explore how pretraining data influences feature biases or provide a theoretical account of bias development in ICL

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The measurement methodology for feature biases is sound, and the general finding that ICL models exhibit persistent feature biases is well-supported by the experimental results. The comparative effectiveness of different intervention types (instructions, explanations, semantic verbalizers) is reasonably well-established.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that instruction-tuned models are more aligned with intended tasks is supported but shows significant variation across different feature combinations. The effectiveness of semantic verbalizers in overriding prior biases is demonstrated but may be context-dependent.

**Low confidence**: The assertion that semantic verbalizers are universally more effective than other interventions requires more extensive validation across diverse tasks and model architectures. The generalizability of findings to non-binary classification tasks and different prompt formats remains uncertain.

## Next Checks

1. **Replicate feature bias measurements across diverse NLP tasks**: Test the methodology with multi-class classification and regression tasks to assess generalizability beyond binary classification.

2. **Systematic investigation of demonstration sensitivity**: Conduct controlled experiments varying demonstration order, example selection, and quantity to quantify the impact on feature bias measurements.

3. **Cross-model validation**: Apply the same experimental protocol to different LLM architectures (e.g., LLaMA, Claude) to determine if feature biases and intervention effectiveness are consistent across model families.
