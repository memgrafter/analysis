# XuanYuan 2.0: A Large Chinese Financial Chat Model with Hundreds of Billions Parameters

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12002
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12002
- Reference count: 7
- The largest Chinese financial chat model with 176 billion parameters

## Executive Summary
XuanYuan 2.0 is a large-scale Chinese financial chat model built on the BLOOM-176B architecture, featuring 176 billion parameters. It addresses the scarcity of open-source Chinese financial conversational models by introducing a hybrid-tuning methodology that combines pre-training and fine-tuning stages to preserve both general conversational capabilities and domain-specific financial knowledge. The model is trained on a comprehensive dataset of unstructured and structured financial data, demonstrating robust performance in financial domain conversations through manual evaluation on internally constructed datasets.

## Method Summary
XuanYuan 2.0 employs a novel hybrid-tuning approach that integrates pre-training and fine-tuning to mitigate catastrophic forgetting while maintaining domain expertise. The model is built upon the BLOOM-176B architecture and trained on mixed unstructured (news, reports) and structured (company data) financial datasets. This methodology allows the model to retain general conversational abilities while specializing in financial domain knowledge, addressing the challenge of domain-specific language models losing general capabilities during fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- 176 billion parameter Chinese financial chat model, largest in its domain
- Demonstrates robust financial domain knowledge through manual evaluation
- Hybrid-tuning methodology successfully mitigates catastrophic forgetting

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hybrid-tuning methodology works by strategically interleaving general pre-training with domain-specific fine-tuning, creating a balanced knowledge integration that prevents the model from losing general conversational capabilities while acquiring financial expertise. This approach addresses the fundamental challenge of catastrophic forgetting in domain adaptation, where models typically lose previously learned knowledge when specialized to new domains.
