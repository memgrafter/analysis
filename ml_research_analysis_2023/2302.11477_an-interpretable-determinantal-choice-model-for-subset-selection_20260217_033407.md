# An Interpretable Determinantal Choice Model for Subset Selection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11477
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11477
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Proves equivalence between DPPs and random-utility-maximizing subset choice, with applications to wireless interference modeling

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a theoretical bridge between determinantal point processes (DPPs) and interpretable random utility models for subset selection. The authors prove that any DPP can be represented as a random-utility-maximizing choice over subsets, where the utility function has a log-determinant form with additive structure plus similarity-dependent corrections. They develop a parametric determinantal choice model that connects to familiar discrete choice models, reducing to logistic regression under independence and multinomial logit under maximal negative dependence. The model is validated through simulations and applied to LoRaWAN experimental data, successfully capturing wireless interference patterns and producing interpretable parameters about spreading factor orthogonality and collision dynamics.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a determinantal choice model that bridges DPPs with interpretable discrete choice theory. They prove that any DPP is equivalent to a random-utility-maximizing subset choice model with a log-determinant utility function. This utility has an additive structure where each item contributes independently, but with corrections that depend on pairwise similarities between items. The model parameters capture both individual item utilities and their interactions. Through a parametric specification, the model smoothly interpolates between logistic regression (when items are independent) and multinomial logit (when items are maximally negatively dependent). The authors validate their approach through simulations across the dependence spectrum and apply it to LoRaWAN interference data.

## Key Results
- Proves theoretical equivalence between DPPs and random-utility-maximizing subset choice models
- Shows parametric model reduces to logistic regression under independence and multinomial logit under maximal negative dependence
- Applied to LoRaWAN data, learns interpretable parameters revealing non-orthogonality of spreading factors and higher success rates for later-arriving packets in collisions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works because determinantal point processes naturally encode negative dependence between items, which translates directly into competitive interactions in random utility models. The log-determinant utility function captures both individual item utilities and pairwise similarity-based corrections, creating an interpretable structure where parameters can be directly associated with physical phenomena like interference strength.
