# Meet in the Middle: A New Pre-training Paradigm

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07295
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07295
- Reference count: 17
- Improves training data efficiency and in-filling capabilities of language models through bidirectional agreement regularization

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Meet in the Middle (MIM) pre-training paradigm that trains two autoregressive language models in opposite directions (left-to-right and right-to-left) and encourages them to agree on their predictions using an agreement regularizer. The approach improves training data efficiency by providing denser supervision signals than standard language modeling and enables better in-filling capabilities through a bidirectional inference procedure that leverages both models. Experiments on code and natural language models demonstrate that MIM outperforms strong baselines in terms of perplexity and code completion tasks while maintaining the ability to generate sequences in standard left-to-right fashion.

## Method Summary
MIM uses two decoder-only transformer models sharing all parameters, with one trained to predict next tokens (forward) and the other to predict previous tokens (backward). An agreement regularizer using total variation distance between the models' probability distributions provides additional supervision during training. For inference, MIM employs a bidirectional generation procedure where both models generate tokens simultaneously from opposite ends, checking for n-gram matches (4-grams) to find meeting points. A parallel verification procedure confirms valid stitchings before returning the completed sequence. The method optionally includes synchronous bidirectional attention to enhance in-filling capabilities while maintaining autoregressive compatibility.

## Key Results
- MIM achieves better perplexity on code datasets compared to standard autoregressive baselines
- Improves pass@k scores on code completion benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, APPS)
- Maintains or improves inference latency over existing FIM baselines
- Shows consistent improvements across model sizes (350M, 1.3B, 2.7B parameters)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Co-regularization through agreement improves data efficiency by providing denser supervision signals than standard language modeling
- Mechanism: The agreement regularizer compares probability distributions from forward and backward models for each token, using total variation distance to measure disagreement. This creates a supervision signal based on all possible tokens rather than just the observed next token
- Core assumption: Agreement between models trained on complementary views of the data (prefix vs suffix) provides meaningful supervision
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The first is a training objective that aligns the predictions of a left-to-right LM with those of a right-to-left LM"
  - [section 3.1]: "The benefit of this regularizer is two-fold. First, it can provide the models with a denser supervision signal which improves data efficiency"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus contains related work on bidirectional training but no direct evidence about agreement regularizers specifically
- Break condition: If the agreement regularizer forces models to converge on incorrect predictions, or if the forward and backward models become too similar to provide complementary information

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The bidirectional inference procedure improves in-filling performance by allowing context from both sides and early termination
- Mechanism: Two models generate tokens in opposite directions simultaneously, checking for n-gram matches (4-grams in experiments) to find meeting points. A parallel verification procedure confirms the stitching is valid
- Core assumption: Models trained to agree will generate compatible sequences when meeting in the middle
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The second is a bidirectional inference procedure that enables both LMs to meet in the middle"
  - [section 3.2.1]: "Our approach can terminate as quickly as a unidirectional LM. Moreover, with enough parallelism, it can even be faster"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - the corpus includes FiLM which also uses bidirectional context but without the meeting mechanism
- Break condition: If n-gram matching produces too many false positives, or if the verification procedure fails to catch invalid stitchings

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sharing all parameters between forward and backward models maintains autoregressive capabilities while enabling bidirectional training
- Mechanism: A single decoder-only transformer architecture runs both forward and backward generation flows using the same weights, with sentinel tokens distinguishing the directions
- Core assumption: The model can learn to generate in both directions without interference when using the same parameters
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "We use two decoder-only language models that share all of their parameters"
  - [section A.1]: "We adopt standard transformer-based autoregressive language models... and train it to predict the next token in both directions"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - XLNet also shares parameters across factorization orders, though for different purposes
- Break condition: If the model becomes biased toward one direction or fails to maintain quality in standard autoregressive generation

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Single decoder-only transformer with shared parameters
  - Forward generation flow (left-to-right)
  - Backward generation flow (right-to-left)
  - Agreement regularizer (total variation distance)
  - Parallel verification module
  - Synchronous bidirectional attention (optional enhancement)

- Critical path:
  1. Load prefix into forward model, suffix into backward model
  2. Generate tokens simultaneously from both ends
  3. Check for n-gram matches between generated sequences
  4. Run parallel verification on candidate meeting points
  5. Return completed sequence or fallback to standard generation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sharing parameters enables bidirectional training but requires careful initialization
  - Agreement regularizer improves efficiency but may slow convergence if β is too high
  - Parallel verification reduces latency but adds complexity
  - Optional bidirectional attention improves in-filling at the cost of autoregressive compatibility

- Failure signatures:
  - High disagreement between forward and backward models during training (poor agreement regularizer)
  - N-gram matching fails to find valid meeting points (verification procedure issues)
  - Forward model quality degrades compared to standard autoregressive training
  - Generation latency increases significantly over FIM baselines

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train MIM-350M on a small code dataset and compare perplexity to standard autoregressive baseline
  2. Test in-filling inference on a single example with known prefix/suffix to verify n-gram matching works
  3. Vary the agreement regularizer strength β and measure impact on both perplexity and in-filling performance

## Limitations

- Limited analysis of how the agreement regularizer affects generalization to out-of-distribution data
- Lack of thorough evaluation of the bidirectional inference procedure's robustness to edge cases
- No investigation of potential interference when sharing parameters between forward and backward models
