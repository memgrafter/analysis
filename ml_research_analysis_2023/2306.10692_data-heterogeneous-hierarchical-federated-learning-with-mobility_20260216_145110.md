# Data-Heterogeneous Hierarchical Federated Learning with Mobility

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10692
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10692
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Mobility improves maximum test accuracy by up to 15.1% in edge non-i.i.d. case

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a convergence analysis of hierarchical federated learning (HFL) in vehicular networks with mobility. The key finding is that vehicle mobility can improve learning performance by reducing data heterogeneity across edge servers. The authors derive an upper bound on the convergence rate that depends on mobility-induced changes in data distribution, showing that mixing heterogeneous data through mobility can accelerate convergence and improve final accuracy.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes a hierarchical federated learning framework with edge servers and mobile vehicles using the CIFAR-10 dataset. The Mob-HierFAVG algorithm implements a 3-layer CNN with learning rate 0.1 and batch size 20, aggregating models across 4 edge servers and 32 vehicles over 600 cloud epochs. The method includes local training on vehicles, edge-level aggregation, and less frequent cloud-level aggregation. Data heterogeneity is simulated with non-i.i.d. distributions where each edge server has access to only 2 of 8 classes.

## Key Results
- Mobility improves maximum test accuracy by up to 15.1% in edge non-i.i.d. scenarios
- Higher mobility rates accelerate convergence speed
- Convergence benefits saturate beyond v=6 mobility rate
- Theoretical upper bound shows mobility reduces gradient difference bounds

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Mobility reduces data heterogeneity across edge servers, improving convergence.
- Mechanism: Vehicle movement redistributes data samples across edge servers, reducing statistical divergence between local datasets and the global dataset.
- Core assumption: Sufficient mixing of data samples occurs during training.
- Break condition: Insufficient mobility or sparse class distribution prevents adequate mixing.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Mobility-induced dataset mixing lowers the edge-level gradient difference ∆[j] over iterations.
- Mechanism: The convergence upper bound includes ∑∆[j], which decreases with mobility, tightening the bound and accelerating convergence.
- Core assumption: Edge gradient difference ∆[j] is positively correlated with convergence upper bound.
- Break condition: Unrealistic mobility models or small edge coverage areas limit dataset mixing.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mobility increases convergence speed up to a saturation point.
- Mechanism: Higher mobility rates lead to faster dataset mixing, reducing heterogeneity and tightening the convergence bound.
- Core assumption: A threshold mobility rate exists beyond which benefits saturate.
- Break condition: Unrealistic mobility models may provide inaccurate saturation points.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Vehicles -> Edge Servers -> Cloud Server
- Critical path:
  1. Vehicles sample data and initialize local models
  2. Vehicles perform local updates using their datasets
  3. Edge servers aggregate local models from vehicles within coverage
  4. Cloud server aggregates edge models less frequently
  5. Aggregated models are distributed back to vehicles
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Communication overhead vs. model accuracy: More frequent aggregations improve accuracy but increase communication costs
  - Edge server coverage vs. mobility impact: Larger coverage areas may reduce mobility's impact on data heterogeneity
  - Learning rate vs. convergence speed: Higher rates accelerate convergence but may cause instability
- Failure signatures:
  - Slow convergence or low accuracy: May indicate insufficient mobility or high data heterogeneity
  - Communication failures: May occur due to unstable connections or high mobility rates
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement HFL without mobility and measure convergence speed and accuracy
  2. Introduce mobility and compare convergence speed and accuracy to no-mobility case
  3. Vary mobility rates and measure impact on convergence speed and accuracy to find saturation point

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the convergence speed of hierarchical federated learning with mobility vary with different levels of data heterogeneity in edge servers?
- Basis in paper: The paper analyzes the convergence upper bound of HFL with respect to data heterogeneity and mobility, showing that mobility influences the data heterogeneity, and thus, the convergence upper bound.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides a theoretical framework for understanding the impact of mobility on convergence, it does not quantify the relationship between convergence speed and varying levels of data heterogeneity in a rigorous manner.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting extensive simulations or experiments that systematically vary the level of data heterogeneity across edge servers and measure the corresponding convergence speeds of HFL with mobility.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal aggregation period for edge servers in hierarchical federated learning with mobility to maximize learning performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the impact of mobility on the performance of HFL, including convergence speed and accuracy. It is implied that the aggregation period for edge servers plays a role in determining the effectiveness of mobility in improving learning performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explicitly investigate or optimize the aggregation period for edge servers in the context of mobility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that vary the aggregation period for edge servers and measure the resulting learning performance in terms of accuracy and convergence speed, particularly in scenarios with different levels of mobility.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the mobility pattern of vehicles (e.g., random walk, deterministic path) affect the convergence and accuracy of hierarchical federated learning?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the mobility of vehicles causes the network topology to be constantly changing and that mobility can potentially improve the learning performance by mitigating data heterogeneity.
- Why unresolved: While the paper acknowledges the influence of mobility on HFL, it does not explore the nuances of different mobility patterns and how they might differently affect the convergence and accuracy of the learning process.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing and testing HFL with mobility under various mobility patterns (e.g., random walk, deterministic paths) and comparing the convergence and accuracy results to identify which mobility patterns are most beneficial for HFL performance.

## Limitations
- Simplifying assumptions about mobility patterns and network dynamics
- Limited empirical validation of theoretical gradient difference metrics
- Single-model evaluation limits generalizability
- No exploration of variable connection quality or packet loss
