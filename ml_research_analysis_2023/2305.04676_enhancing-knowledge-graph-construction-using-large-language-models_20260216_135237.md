# Enhancing Knowledge Graph Construction Using Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04676
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04676
- Reference count: 27
- Large Language Models (LLMs) can effectively construct Knowledge Graphs from unstructured sustainability news articles, with ChatGPT showing particular promise for ontology generation when provided detailed prompts.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) to construct Knowledge Graphs from sustainability-related news articles. It compares two approaches: a specialized pretrained model (REBEL) and a foundational model (ChatGPT). Both methods successfully extract entities and relations to build structured knowledge representations, with ChatGPT showing particular promise in generating ontologies and linking concepts to specific organizations and practices. The second experiment with ChatGPT, which involved detailed prompts for ontology creation, yielded higher-quality knowledge graphs with more meaningful concepts and instances. While REBEL extracted 805 entities and 105 relations, ChatGPT identified 1158 entities and 677 relations. The findings demonstrate that advanced LLMs can improve the accuracy and relevance of Knowledge Graph construction from unstructured text, with ChatGPT offering potential for automated ontology generation when provided with detailed instructions.

## Method Summary
The method involves collecting 94 sustainability-related news articles and applying two LLM approaches to extract entities and relations for Knowledge Graph construction. REBEL uses seq2seq generation with BART to output structured triples, while ChatGPT processes natural language prompts to identify concepts and instances. Both approaches employ Entity Linking to normalize extracted entities to DBpedia URLs. The second experiment with ChatGPT specifically focuses on generating ontologies through detailed domain-specific prompts, outputting structured RDF/Turtle format representations.

## Key Results
- REBEL extracted 805 entities and 105 relations from sustainability news articles
- ChatGPT identified 1158 entities and 677 relations, showing higher extraction capacity
- Detailed prompts for ChatGPT ontology generation yielded more meaningful concepts and instances compared to basic extraction approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT can generate structured knowledge graphs when provided with detailed, domain-specific prompts.
- Mechanism: The LLM processes natural language instructions to identify concepts (organizations, actions, practices, policies) and instances, then outputs them in a structured RDF/Turtle format.
- Core assumption: ChatGPT can interpret domain-specific prompts and translate them into ontological structures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT offering potential for automated ontology generation when provided with detailed instructions."
  - [section III.B.2] "Experiment 2 focuses on creating a prompt that would directly generate the entire Knowledge Base and write an ontology describing the concepts identified in the texts."
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers with average neighbor FMR=0.497, indicating moderate relevance in the corpus.
- Break condition: If prompts lack sufficient domain specificity, ChatGPT outputs unstructured phrases instead of formal ontological classes and instances.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: REBEL achieves higher precision in relation extraction due to its specialized training for joint entity and relation extraction.
- Mechanism: REBEL uses seq2seq generation with BART to output triples in a structured format with special tokens (<triplet>, <subj>, <obj>) for parsing.
- Core assumption: Specialized models outperform general-purpose LLMs on narrow NLP tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "specialized pretrained models, like REBEL, for joint entity and relation extraction."
  - [section II.A] "REBEL (Relation Extraction By End-to-end Language generation)... performs end-to-end relation extraction for more than 200 different relation types."
  - [corpus] Corpus includes papers on RAKG and graph similarity metrics, suggesting broader context for relation extraction.
- Break condition: When input text exceeds 512 tokens, REBEL's batching approach may split sentences, reducing extraction accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Entity Linking post-processing improves knowledge graph quality by normalizing extracted entities to DBpedia.
- Mechanism: After extraction, entities are matched to DBpedia URLs to ensure consistency and enable reasoning.
- Core assumption: External knowledge bases provide reliable entity normalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.B.1] "we perform Entity Linking... utilize DBpedia as our Knowledge Base and consider two entities identical if they share the same DBpedia URL."
  - [abstract] "Findings demonstrate that advanced LLMs can improve the accuracy and relevance of Knowledge Graph construction from unstructured text."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence for DBpedia usage; weak signal in related papers.
- Break condition: If entities are not present in DBpedia, normalization fails, leaving inconsistencies in the graph.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data Collection → Text Preprocessing → Relation Extraction (REBEL/ChatGPT) → Entity Linking → Knowledge Graph Construction → Ontology Generation (ChatGPT Experiment 2)
- Critical path: Text input → Relation Extraction → Triplet Generation → Entity Linking → Graph Output
- Design tradeoffs: REBEL offers higher precision but limited input size; ChatGPT offers flexibility but requires careful prompt engineering and may hallucinate.
- Failure signatures: REBEL produces incomplete triples when batching splits sentences; ChatGPT outputs unstructured phrases without detailed prompts; Entity Linking fails for entities not in DBpedia.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run REBEL on single short news articles (≤512 tokens) and verify triple extraction quality.
  2. Use ChatGPT with basic prompts on the same articles and compare entity/relation counts.
  3. Implement Entity Linking on both outputs and measure DBpedia match rate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the ontologies generated by ChatGPT from different sustainability articles be effectively unified to create a comprehensive and consistent knowledge base?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that while ChatGPT generates ontologies for individual articles, these ontologies may have inconsistencies and limited scope. The authors mention that future work will explore methods for unifying these ontologies across all articles to enhance the overall definition of concepts and their interrelationships in the sustainability domain.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a solution for unifying the ontologies generated by ChatGPT from different articles. It only states that future research will explore this direction.
- What evidence would resolve it: A methodology or algorithm that can effectively merge and reconcile the ontologies generated by ChatGPT from different articles, ensuring consistency and completeness in the resulting unified knowledge base.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the quality of knowledge graphs generated by ChatGPT be assessed and improved automatically, without manual inspection?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the evaluation of a knowledge base cannot be created in an automated way based on some metric when ground truth data is not available. The authors manually inspected the knowledge graphs generated with the proposed methods and concluded that the second ChatGPT approach creates a knowledge graph of greater quality. However, they also mention that a few steps of refining the answers from ChatGPT are needed, and sometimes the produced output is erroneous and needs to be corrected before proceeding.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a solution for automatically assessing and improving the quality of knowledge graphs generated by ChatGPT. It only mentions the need for such a method.
- What evidence would resolve it: A framework or set of metrics that can automatically evaluate the quality of knowledge graphs generated by ChatGPT, along with techniques for automatically refining and correcting the output to improve its quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the scalability of knowledge graph construction using ChatGPT be improved to handle large volumes of unstructured text data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that due to the limitations of the length of the input prompt passed to ChatGPT, it was not possible to prompt the model first to define an ontology based on all articles on sustainability and then create instances from all the other articles using the same ontology. This limitation suggests that the current approach may not scale well to handle large volumes of unstructured text data.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a solution for improving the scalability of knowledge graph construction using ChatGPT. It only mentions the limitation of the current approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Techniques or strategies for breaking down large volumes of unstructured text data into manageable chunks, processing them efficiently using ChatGPT, and then merging the resulting knowledge graphs to create a comprehensive and scalable knowledge base.

## Limitations

- Evaluation methodology relies on qualitative assessment rather than quantitative metrics for comparing REBEL and ChatGPT performance.
- Entity Linking process lacks validation metrics for DBpedia matching accuracy and handling of entities not present in the knowledge base.
- Limited generalizability due to narrow domain focus (sustainability news) and small corpus size (94 articles).

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: REBEL's relation extraction capabilities and its limitations with long documents (token limits, batching issues).
- **Medium Confidence**: ChatGPT's ability to generate ontologies with detailed prompts, based on qualitative observations of improved structure.
- **Low Confidence**: Comparative performance between REBEL and ChatGPT, due to incomplete reporting and lack of standardized evaluation metrics.
