# Run-Off Election: Improved Provable Defense against Data Poisoning Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02300
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02300
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces Run-Off Election (ROE) aggregation method that improves certified accuracy against data poisoning attacks by 3.54-4.73% over baseline methods

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of provable defense against data poisoning attacks, where adversaries manipulate training data to corrupt model predictions. The authors propose Run-Off Election (ROE), a novel two-round aggregation method that improves upon simple majority voting for ensemble-based defenses. ROE first selects the top two classes from base model votes, then performs a run-off election using logits information to determine the final prediction. The method is integrated with Deep Partition Aggregation (DPA) and Finite Aggregation (FA) frameworks, introducing 1v1 and 2v1 certificate concepts for calculating provable robustness. Empirical results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and GTSRB datasets.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces Run-Off Election (ROE) as an improved aggregation method for ensemble defenses against data poisoning. ROE operates in two rounds: first, base models vote for their preferred class and the top two classes are selected; second, a run-off election between these two classes is conducted using logits layer information. The method is implemented within two existing frameworks - Deep Partition Aggregation (DPA) and Finite Aggregation (FA) - creating DPA+ROE and FA+ROE defenses. The authors develop theoretical guarantees through 1v1 and 2v1 certificates that quantify the robustness of the ensemble against poisoning attacks. The approach leverages ensemble diversity and a more sophisticated aggregation strategy to improve certified accuracy while maintaining provable guarantees.

## Key Results
- ROE improves certified accuracy by 4.73% on MNIST, 3.63% on CIFAR-10, and 3.54% on GTSRB datasets compared to baseline methods
- The method establishes new state-of-the-art performance for pointwise certified robustness against data poisoning attacks
- ROE demonstrates consistent improvements across different aggregation frameworks (DPA and FA)
- Theoretical certificates (1v1 and 2v1) provide provable guarantees for the enhanced robustness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ROE improves defense against poisoning attacks by introducing a more sophisticated aggregation mechanism than simple majority voting. The two-round election process first narrows down to the most competitive classes, then uses detailed logits information to make a more informed final decision. This approach leverages the diversity of ensemble members more effectively, reducing the impact of poisoned data that might fool individual models. By incorporating logits layer information in the second round, ROE captures richer information about model confidence and inter-class relationships that simple voting ignores.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Training data -> Data partitioner -> Multiple base models -> Logits collection -> Run-Off Election (Round 1: voting, Round 2: logits comparison) -> Final prediction

**Critical path**: Base model inference → Logits extraction → First-round voting → Top-2 class selection → Second-round logits comparison → Final class prediction

**Design tradeoffs**: ROE trades increased computational complexity (two rounds of processing) for improved robustness. The method requires more computation per prediction but provides stronger provable guarantees and better empirical performance against poisoning attacks.

**Failure signatures**: The method may fail when: poisoned data successfully influences both rounds of voting, ensemble diversity is insufficient to provide meaningful run-off options, or the adversary can craft poisons that specifically exploit the two-round mechanism.

## Limitations
- Empirical improvements are modest (3.54-4.73% certified accuracy gains) and absolute robustness levels remain relatively low on complex datasets
- Computational overhead of the two-round voting mechanism is not fully characterized in terms of training and inference time impacts
- Theoretical guarantees rely on specific assumptions about adversary capabilities and base model ensemble behavior that may not hold in all scenarios
- Effectiveness against sophisticated poisoning strategies beyond standard label-flipping and trigger-based attacks is not thoroughly explored
