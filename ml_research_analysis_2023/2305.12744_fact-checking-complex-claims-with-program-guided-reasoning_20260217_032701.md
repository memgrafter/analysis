# Fact-Checking Complex Claims with Program-Guided Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12744
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12744
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: ProgramFC outperforms seven baselines on HOVER and FEVEROUS datasets, with improvements growing as claim complexity increases

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Program-Guided Fact-Checking (ProgramFC), a novel approach that addresses complex fact-checking by decomposing claims into simpler sub-tasks guided by reasoning programs. The model leverages large language models to generate programs that outline verification steps, then executes these using specialized functions for answering questions, verifying claims, and performing logical reasoning. Evaluated on HOVER and FEVEROUS datasets, ProgramFC demonstrates superior performance compared to seven baselines across gold evidence and open-book settings, particularly for claims requiring multi-step reasoning.

## Method Summary
ProgramFC employs a two-stage approach: program generation and program execution. First, it uses the in-context learning ability of large language models (specifically Codex) to generate reasoning programs that break down complex claims into a sequence of sub-tasks. These programs are then executed by delegating each sub-task to specialized handlers including question answering, claim verification, and logical reasoning functions. The model aggregates predictions from multiple reasoning programs using majority voting to improve robustness. ProgramFC is evaluated in both closed-book (relying on language model's parametric knowledge) and open-book settings (accessing external corpus via BM25 retrieval).

## Key Results
- ProgramFC achieves F1 scores of 77.22 on 2-hop claims and 60.49 on 4-hop claims on HOVER, with smaller performance drops as complexity increases compared to baselines
- Program-guided retrieval achieves recall@10 of 78.3 on HOVER, outperforming baselines by 5.2% to 11.3%
- Aggregating predictions from N=5 reasoning programs improves performance by an average of 1.5% over single-program approaches

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input claim -> Program Generator (Codex) -> Program Executor -> Sub-task Handlers (QA, Verification, Logical Reasoning) -> Knowledge Source (external corpus or parametric) -> Final Verdict
- Critical path: Input claim → Program Generation → Program Execution → Final Verdict
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using program-guided reasoning vs. end-to-end models: Program-guided reasoning provides more interpretability and data efficiency, but may have higher computational cost.
  - Open-book vs. closed-book setting: Open-book setting allows access to external knowledge, but may require more complex retrieval mechanisms.
- Failure signatures:
  - Incorrect program generation: Syntactic errors, semantic errors (token, structure, subtask), or incorrect execution.
  - Failure in sub-task handlers: Incorrect answers from question answering, fact verification, or logical reasoning modules.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the model's performance on simple claims (1-hop) to establish a baseline.
  2. Evaluate the model's performance on increasingly complex claims (2-hop, 3-hop, 4-hop) to assess the effectiveness of program-guided reasoning for multi-step reasoning.
  3. Compare the model's performance with and without program aggregation to understand the impact of this design choice.

## Limitations
- The paper's claims are primarily supported by experimental results on two datasets (HOVER and FEVEROUS), with limited external validation of the proposed mechanisms
- The approach depends on large language models (Codex, FLAN-T5) which introduces significant computational costs and potential access limitations
- The complexity metrics used (hop counting) may not capture all dimensions of reasoning difficulty, and evaluation focuses on controlled settings rather than practical deployment

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The experimental results showing ProgramFC outperforming baselines on HOVER and FEVEROUS datasets are well-documented with clear methodology and statistical measures. The performance improvements on multi-hop claims are particularly convincing.

**Medium confidence**: The interpretability benefits of program-guided reasoning are plausible given the structured output, but the paper doesn't provide user studies or quantitative measures of how much the programs actually aid human understanding of the reasoning process.

**Low confidence**: The mechanism explanations for why program decomposition improves performance beyond the observed correlation lack direct experimental validation. The claims about data efficiency and the comparison to end-to-end approaches are asserted rather than empirically demonstrated.

## Next Checks

1. **Mechanism validation study**: Design controlled experiments that isolate the impact of program decomposition versus other factors. For instance, compare ProgramFC against a variant that uses the same specialized functions but without the program-guided structure to quantify the specific contribution of the decomposition approach.

2. **Generalization benchmark**: Test ProgramFC on additional fact-checking datasets beyond HOVER and FEVEROUS, particularly those with different domain characteristics or claim structures. Include datasets that require different types of reasoning (temporal, causal, quantitative) to assess robustness.

3. **Resource efficiency analysis**: Measure and compare the computational overhead of ProgramFC (including program generation latency and inference costs) against baseline models. Quantify the trade-off between performance gains and resource requirements, and explore whether smaller language models could provide similar benefits with reduced costs.
