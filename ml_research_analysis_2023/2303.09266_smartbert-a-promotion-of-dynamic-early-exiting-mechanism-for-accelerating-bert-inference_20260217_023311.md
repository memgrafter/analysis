# SmartBERT: A Promotion of Dynamic Early Exiting Mechanism for Accelerating BERT Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09266
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09266
- Reference count: 5
- Achieves 2-3× computation reduction with minimal accuracy drops on GLUE benchmarks

## Executive Summary
SmartBERT introduces a dynamic early exiting mechanism combined with layer skipping to accelerate BERT inference. The method adds skipping gates and exiting operators to each BERT layer, enabling adaptive layer skipping and early exiting based on sample complexity. The authors address the challenge of inconsistent skipping gate usage between training and inference through a hard weight mechanism and special training strategy. Cross-layer contrastive learning is introduced to boost intermediate layers and classifiers, improving early exiting efficiency. Experimental results on eight GLUE benchmark datasets demonstrate 2-3× computation reduction with minimal accuracy drops compared to BERT, outperforming previous dynamic early exiting methods.

## Method Summary
SmartBERT enhances BERT with dynamic early exiting and layer skipping capabilities. The core innovation involves inserting a skipping gate and exiting operator into each layer, which makes adaptive decisions about whether to skip subsequent layers or exit early based on sample complexity. To handle the inconsistency between training (where skipping gates are used) and inference (where they're not), the authors implement a hard weight mechanism with a specialized training strategy. Additionally, cross-layer contrastive learning is employed to strengthen intermediate layers and classifiers, further improving the efficiency of early exiting. This approach particularly excels on complex datasets like RTE and WNLI, where traditional entropy-based early exiting methods fail to reduce computation effectively.

## Key Results
- Achieves 2-3× computation reduction on GLUE benchmark datasets
- Outperforms previous dynamic early exiting methods in both efficiency and accuracy
- Skipping mechanism shows particular effectiveness on complex datasets RTE and WNLI

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The skipping gate mechanism enables adaptive computation allocation by identifying simpler samples that can exit early or skip layers, while allowing complex samples to proceed through more layers. The hard weight mechanism resolves the training-inference inconsistency by maintaining consistent behavior during both phases. Cross-layer contrastive learning strengthens intermediate representations, making early exits more reliable and reducing the need for full computation on simpler samples.

**Failure Signatures**: Inconsistent skipping decisions between training and inference, poor early exit accuracy on complex samples, or insufficient computation reduction on simpler samples.
