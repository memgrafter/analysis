# An Evaluation of Persian-English Machine Translation Datasets with Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00321
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00321
- Reference count: 8
- This paper establishes transformer-based baselines for Persian-English machine translation across eight datasets, showing that model performance does not scale predictably with dataset size.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates transformer-based models for Persian-English machine translation using eight parallel corpora. The researchers fine-tuned multilingual T5 and NLLB models, reporting BLEU scores for both translation directions. Results demonstrate that data quality can be more important than quantity for translation performance, with the Bible corpus achieving the highest scores despite being the smallest dataset. Meta's NLLB model consistently outperformed mT5 variants across all tested conditions.

## Method Summary
The researchers fine-tuned mT5-small, mT5-base, and NLLB-distilled models on eight Persian-English parallel corpora with predefined train/dev/test splits. Models were trained for 7 epochs using AdamW optimizer with 1e-3 learning rate and sequence lengths covering 92% of each dataset. Evaluation used SacreBLEU with N-grams 3,4,5,6,7 for both translation directions. The study established baseline performance metrics without applying preprocessing to maintain consistency across datasets.

## Key Results
- NLLB model consistently outperformed mT5 variants across all datasets and translation directions
- Bible corpus achieved highest BLEU scores despite being the smallest dataset
- Translation from Persian to English yielded higher BLEU scores than English to Persian

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transformer-based models can establish strong baseline performance for low-resource Persian-English translation despite small training data.
- Mechanism: The self-attention mechanism in transformers captures long-range dependencies in parallel corpora, allowing effective representation learning even with limited parallel text.
- Core assumption: The pre-trained multilingual model has sufficient coverage of Persian linguistic features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Meta's NLLB model consistently outperforms mT5 variants"
  - [section]: "fine-tuned multilingual T5 and NLLB models, reporting BLEU scores for both translation directions"
- Break condition: If the pre-trained model lacks adequate Persian vocabulary or grammar patterns, performance degrades regardless of fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data quality impacts BLEU score more than dataset size for Persian-English translation.
- Mechanism: Higher quality parallel corpora with accurate alignments and fewer noise instances produce more reliable model learning signals.
- Core assumption: Parallel sentences are properly aligned and contain minimal errors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "data quality can be more important than quantity for translation performance"
  - [section]: "Mizan, OPUS-100, and Quran...lower or almost the same BLEU score compared to smaller datasets, such as Bible, TEP, TEP++, and PEPC variants"
- Break condition: If datasets contain significant noise or misaligned sentences, increasing size could harm rather than help model performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Translation direction affects BLEU scores differently for Persian-English pairs.
- Mechanism: Language-specific structural differences and corpus characteristics create asymmetric performance between translation directions.
- Core assumption: The source and target languages have different syntactic and morphological complexities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "the evaluation metric for translation from Persian to English is higher than the evaluation metric for translation from English to Persian"
  - [section]: "the Bible dataset performed significantly better in English-to-Persian translation by near 20% higher BLEU score"
- Break condition: If corpus characteristics and language pair complexities are similar in both directions, performance differences may not be significant.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-trained models (mT5-small, mT5-base, NLLB-distilled) -> Parallel corpora (8 datasets) -> Evaluation framework (SacreBLEU) -> Training infrastructure (PyTorch, Transformers, Hugging Face)

- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess parallel corpus
  2. Fine-tune pre-trained model on training split
  3. Evaluate on development set each epoch
  4. Select best model based on validation performance
  5. Evaluate final model on test set

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. computational resources
  - Dataset size vs. quality
  - N-gram parameter selection for BLEU evaluation
  - Sequence length limits during inference

- Failure signatures:
  - BLEU scores plateau or decrease during training
  - Validation perplexity increases while training perplexity decreases
  - Significant performance differences between translation directions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune mT5-small on Bible corpus (smallest dataset, highest BLEU scores)
  2. Fine-tune NLLB on TEP++ corpus (refined version of noisy corpus)
  3. Compare performance on OPUS-100 using different N-gram parameters (10.81, 10.46, 11.62 for 3,4,5-grams)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does model performance vary when using domain-specific fine-tuning on specialized datasets versus general datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that the Bible corpus achieved the highest BLEU scores despite being the smallest dataset, suggesting domain specificity may play a crucial role in performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not directly test domain-specific fine-tuning against general dataset fine-tuning, leaving the comparative impact of domain adaptation unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing domain-specific fine-tuning on specialized datasets like Bible with general dataset fine-tuning would clarify the impact of domain adaptation on model performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of preprocessing techniques such as data cleaning and noise reduction on the final BLEU scores?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that MT systems can be improved by applying data-cleaning approaches but did not apply any preprocessing to establish baselines, indicating this as a potential area for improvement.
- Why unresolved: The study intentionally avoided preprocessing to establish baselines, leaving the impact of data cleaning and noise reduction unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying various preprocessing techniques to the datasets and measuring their impact on BLEU scores would provide insights into the importance of data quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of Persian-English translation models compare to other low-resource language pairs under similar experimental conditions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on Persian-English translation and establishes baselines, but does not compare these results with other low-resource language pairs, leaving the relative performance unexplored.
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to Persian-English translation and does not include comparisons with other low-resource language pairs, making it difficult to assess the relative performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting similar experiments on other low-resource language pairs and comparing the results with the Persian-English baselines would provide insights into the relative performance.

## Limitations
- The study uses relatively small Persian-English parallel corpora, with the largest dataset containing only 1 million sentence pairs
- Evaluation relies solely on BLEU scores, which may not fully capture translation quality nuances for morphologically complex languages like Persian
- The paper does not provide systematic quality assessment of datasets beyond noting TEP++ is a refined version of TEP

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The observation that NLLB consistently outperforms mT5 variants is well-supported by systematic evaluation across all datasets and translation directions
- Medium Confidence: The finding that Bible achieves the highest BLEU scores despite being the smallest dataset is supported but requires further investigation into what specific characteristics drive this performance
- Low Confidence: The claim that translation direction significantly affects performance differently for Persian-English pairs lacks sufficient statistical analysis and significance testing

## Next Checks
1. Perform paired t-tests or bootstrap confidence interval estimation on BLEU scores across translation directions and datasets to determine whether observed performance differences are statistically significant
2. Implement systematic quality scoring for each parallel corpus using automatic metrics and human evaluation to empirically validate the hypothesis that Bible's superior performance stems from higher data quality
3. Conduct experiments transferring models fine-tuned on high-quality small datasets to test sets from lower-quality larger datasets to directly test whether data quality in training data improves generalization
