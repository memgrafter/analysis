# Re-Weighted Softmax Cross-Entropy to Control Forgetting in Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05260
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05260
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: WSM improves federated learning performance, especially in highly heterogeneous settings, by reducing local client forgetting through re-weighted softmax cross-entropy.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of local client forgetting in federated learning, where models diverge from the global model due to heterogeneous data. The authors propose a modification to the cross-entropy loss by re-weighting the softmax logits based on each client's class distribution. This approach shields classes outside a client's label set from abrupt representation changes, allowing the model to focus on the classes present in the local data. The method, called Re-weighted Softmax Cross-Entropy (WSM), is evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and FEMNIST datasets with ResNet-18 and LeNet architectures, showing consistent improvements over standard FedAvg.

## Method Summary
The method modifies the cross-entropy objective on a per-client basis by re-weighting the softmax logits prior to computing the loss. This is done using per-client class proportions βc, which scale the logits for each class. Classes absent from a client's data have βc≈0, effectively removing them from the loss computation. This shields absent classes from being aggressively suppressed during local training, reducing forgetting. The approach is compatible with various federated learning algorithms and improves hyperparameter robustness, particularly for learning rate selection.

## Key Results
- WSM consistently improves performance over standard FedAvg, especially in highly heterogeneous settings with low client participation.
- WSM makes hyperparameter tuning easier, performing well across a wider range of learning rates.
- The method is compatible with various federated learning algorithms, providing consistent performance gains when combined with FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, FedNova, and FedProx.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Re-weighting the softmax logits based on per-client class proportions reduces bias toward classes outside a client's label set, thereby mitigating local client forgetting.
- Mechanism: In standard cross-entropy, the denominator of the softmax includes all classes, encouraging the model to suppress predictions for classes absent in the client's data. By scaling logits with class proportions βc, the contrast term for absent classes is weakened, so local training does not aggressively "forget" those classes.
- Core assumption: The proportion βc for classes absent from a client's data is near zero, so their contribution to the loss is negligible.
- Evidence anchors:

- Break condition: If client data distributions change rapidly or classes reappear, static βc weights may become stale, reducing effectiveness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The modified loss function focuses optimization on classes present in the client's data, preventing the model from drifting toward a solution that is too tailored to local classes at the expense of global generalization.
- Mechanism: By ignoring classes not present in the local dataset (via βc ≈ 0), the objective becomes aligned with the client's true task, reducing pressure to suppress absent classes and maintaining representation stability for them.
- Core assumption: Classes absent locally are still relevant to the global task and should not be completely suppressed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "modifies the cross-entropy objective on a per-client basis by re-weighting the softmax logits prior to computing the loss."
  - [section]: "The re-weighted softmax approach modifies the original local objective function to avoid excessive pressure that drives up the loss of other client data."
  - [corpus]: No direct citation; described only in paper text.
- Break condition: If the global model requires adaptation to new classes not seen in any client's local data, this method may slow that adaptation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: WSM improves hyperparameter robustness, especially for learning rate selection, by reducing sensitivity to local drift.
- Mechanism: By stabilizing local representations and reducing forgetting, WSM allows a wider range of learning rates to converge to good solutions, as observed empirically across multiple rates.
- Core assumption: Local client forgetting is a major source of instability in hyperparameter tuning for federated learning.
- Evidence anchors:

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed re-weighted softmax cross-entropy method affect the convergence rate of federated learning algorithms compared to standard FedAvg?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that WSM provides consistent improvements to standard federated learning algorithms, especially in highly heterogeneous settings with low client participation. It also states that WSM makes hyperparameter tuning easier, performing well across a wider range of learning rates.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides results on the final accuracy achieved by WSM and FedAvg across different learning rates and datasets, but does not explicitly compare the convergence rates of the two methods. Understanding the impact of WSM on the convergence rate would provide valuable insights into its practical applicability and potential benefits in real-world federated learning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: To resolve this question, additional experiments could be conducted to measure and compare the convergence rates of WSM and FedAvg under various settings, such as different data heterogeneity levels, client participation rates, and model architectures. These experiments should track the accuracy or loss values at regular intervals throughout the training process, allowing for a direct comparison of the convergence speeds between the two methods.

- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that WSM improves the final accuracy of federated learning models, it does not explicitly investigate its impact on the generalization ability of these models. Understanding how WSM affects the model's ability to generalize to unseen data is crucial for assessing its practical effectiveness and potential limitations.
- What evidence would resolve it: To address this question, additional experiments could be conducted to evaluate the generalization performance of WSM and FedAvg on held-out test sets or in cross-dataset settings. These experiments should measure metrics such as test accuracy, F1-score, or area under the ROC curve to assess the models' ability to generalize to new, unseen data.

- What evidence would resolve it: To address this question, additional experiments could be conducted to measure and compare the communication costs of WSM and FedAvg under various settings, such as different data heterogeneity levels, client participation rates, and model architectures. ## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to static class distributions, which may not hold in dynamic or evolving data scenarios.
- While hyperparameter robustness is claimed, the experimental sweep is limited to a few learning rates and may not generalize to all federated learning configurations.
- The computational overhead of per-client class proportion computation is noted but not quantified in terms of wall-clock or communication costs.
