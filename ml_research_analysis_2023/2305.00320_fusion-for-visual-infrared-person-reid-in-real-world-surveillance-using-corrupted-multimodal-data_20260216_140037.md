# Fusion for Visual-Infrared Person ReID in Real-World Surveillance Using Corrupted Multimodal Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00320
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00320
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Multimodal fusion models with Masking and Local Multimodal Data Augmentation (ML-MDA) significantly outperform unimodal approaches under corrupted surveillance conditions, with MMSF achieving the best accuracy-complexity trade-off.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of visible-infrared person re-identification (V-I ReID) in real-world surveillance scenarios with corrupted multimodal data. The authors introduce a novel Multimodal Middle Stream Fusion (MMSF) architecture that preserves both modality-specific and modality-shared features, along with adaptations of three attention-based fusion models. To enable realistic evaluation, they propose two new datasets with 20 corruptions applied to visible and infrared images: an uncorrelated corrupted dataset (UCD) for not co-located cameras and a correlated corrupted dataset (CCD) for co-located cameras. A Masking and Local Multimodal Data Augmentation (ML-MDA) strategy is also introduced to improve model robustness. Experiments demonstrate that multimodal models trained with ML-MDA significantly outperform unimodal approaches under corruption, with MMSF achieving the best accuracy-complexity trade-off.

## Method Summary
The method involves developing multimodal fusion architectures for V-I ReID under corrupted conditions, specifically targeting both co-located (CL) and not co-located (NCL) camera settings. The approach uses ResNet-18 backbones with batch hard triplet loss and cross-entropy with label smoothing for training. The MMSF architecture preserves modality-specific and modality-shared features through separate CNN streams, while attention-based models dynamically weight feature vectors. ML-MDA augments training data with multimodal soft random erasing and modality masking. The study evaluates on SYSU-MM01 (NCL), RegDB, and ThermalWORLD datasets with clean and corrupted versions containing 20 corruption types.

## Key Results
- MMSF architecture with ML-MDA achieved superior performance across all tested datasets, demonstrating best accuracy-complexity trade-off
- Multimodal models trained with ML-MDA significantly outperformed unimodal approaches under corruption, with up to 30% improvement in mAP
- The study validated that modality-specific feature preservation and multimodal augmentation are critical for sustaining high accuracy in challenging operational conditions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** MMSF improves robustness to corrupted multimodal images by preserving modality-specific and modality-shared features.
- **Mechanism:** The model uses two modality-specific CNN streams to extract modality-specific information and a middle CNN stream to exploit modality-shared information. Each stream is independent and optimized through its specific loss functions, allowing it not to influence a stream representation from direct knowledge exchanges among streams.
- **Core assumption:** Modality-specific and modality-shared features are both important for person re-identification and should be preserved.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "preserves modality-specific knowledge for improved robustness to corrupted multimodal images"
  - [section] "Assuring the conservation of the modality supplementary information, while taking advantage of the modality-shared information, we propose the Multimodal Middle Stream Fusion (MMSF)."
- **Break Condition:** If the middle stream fusion location is not optimized for the camera configuration (NCL vs CL), the model may not effectively leverage modality correlations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Attention-based models improve robustness to corrupted data through dynamic feature selection.
- **Mechanism:** Attention-based models dynamically weight feature vectors from each modality before fusing them, allowing the model to give more importance to the discriminant modality features in the final embedding.
- **Core assumption:** Dynamic weighting of modality features can help handle corrupted data by focusing on the most relevant information.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "three state-of-art attention-based multimodal fusion models are adapted to address corrupted multimodal data in V-I ReID, allowing for dynamic balancing of the importance of each modality."
  - [section] "Attention approaches are expected to address image corruptions through a dynamic feature selection, dealing with the varying availability of modality information."
- **Break Condition:** If the attention mechanism focuses too much on modality-shared features, it may lose some capacity to discriminate between individuals.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** ML-MDA improves robustness to real-world image corruptions through multimodal data augmentation.
- **Mechanism:** ML-MDA is based on two components: multimodal soft random erasing (MS-REA) and modality masking. These augmentations are used together during the learning process to make the learned co-learning model robust and accurate in a challenging inference environment.
- **Core assumption:** Augmenting multimodal data with local occlusions and modality masking can help the model emphasize modality-specific feature importance and learn how to cancel a modality's influence on the final prediction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "the benefits of our Masking and Local Multimodal Data Augmentation (ML-MDA) strategy are explored to improve the robustness of ReID models to multimodal corruption."
  - [section] "Augmenting the multimodal data with local occlusions may help the model to emphasize modality-specific feature importance, as some features will be available only from one or another modality."
- **Break Condition:** If the augmentation probability is too high, it may lead to overfitting to augmented data and poor generalization to real-world data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** ResNet-18 backbone -> Modality-specific CNN streams -> Middle CNN stream (MMSF) -> Attention modules (attention models) -> Fusion layers -> Loss functions
- **Critical path:** Extract features from each modality using backbone networks -> Process features through modality-specific and middle streams -> Apply attention mechanisms (if using attention-based models) -> Fuse features using concatenation or element-wise sum -> Compute similarity between fused features for person re-identification
- **Design tradeoffs:** Modality-specific vs. modality-shared features: Balancing the importance of each type of feature for effective person re-identification; Attention vs. non-attention models: Choosing between dynamic feature selection and fixed feature fusion; Concatenation vs. element-wise sum: Deciding on the fusion strategy based on the camera configuration (NCL vs CL) and data characteristics
- **Failure signatures:** Poor performance on corrupted data: Indicates insufficient robustness to real-world image corruptions; Overfitting to augmented data: Suggests excessive reliance on data augmentation techniques; Inconsistent performance across camera configurations: Implies suboptimal fusion strategy for the given camera setup

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the multimodal middle stream fusion (MMSF) model perform when using more advanced backbone architectures like ResNet-50 or Vision Transformers?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses ResNet-18 backbones for MMSF and other models. It mentions that different backbones could be explored for better accuracy/complexity ratio in the conclusion.
- **Why unresolved:** The study only evaluated MMSF with ResNet-18 backbones. Testing with more powerful backbones could reveal if MMSF's advantages extend to larger models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing MMSF with ResNet-50 and Vision Transformer backbones against their unimodal counterparts on clean and corrupted datasets.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the impact of varying the fusion layer location (l value) in MMSF on its performance across different corruption types?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper evaluates MMSF with different fusion layer locations (l=0 to l=4) for NCL and CL settings, but doesn't analyze how this affects performance on specific corruption types.
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper finds optimal l values for overall performance, it doesn't examine if certain corruption types benefit more from earlier or later fusion.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Detailed analysis of MMSF performance with different l values on each corruption type, identifying which corruptions benefit most from early vs late fusion.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the Masking and Local Multimodal Data Augmentation (ML-MDA) strategy affect the model's ability to handle severe unilateral corruptions (where one modality is heavily corrupted while the other remains clean)?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper mentions that MMSF has shown weakness while facing strongly and unilaterally corrupted data, and that ML-MDA was crucial for multimodal models to handle corruption.
- **Why unresolved:** While ML-MDA improves overall robustness, the paper doesn't specifically analyze its effectiveness on severe unilateral corruptions where one modality is significantly degraded.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing model performance on unilateral corruptions with and without ML-MDA, focusing on scenarios where one modality is heavily corrupted while the other remains clean.

## Limitations
- Focus on ResNet-18 backbone limits potential performance gains from larger architectures
- Specific corruption set (20 visible, 19 infrared) may not cover all real-world degradation types
- Evaluation primarily on RGB-thermal scenarios without testing alternative modality pairs
