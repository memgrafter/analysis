# OF-AE: Oblique Forest AutoEncoders

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00880
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00880
- Reference count: 34
- Key outcome: Unsupervised ensemble method using oblique trees for image reconstruction

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Oblique Forest AutoEncoders (OF-AE), an unsupervised ensemble method for auto-encoding using oblique decision trees. The method replaces axis-parallel splits with multivariate linear combinations of features, formulating encoding-decoding as a constrained optimization problem. Experiments on tabular and image datasets demonstrate effective feature and image reconstruction, with oblique splits providing compact representations. However, training time increases significantly with image dimensions and number of estimators.

## Method Summary
OF-AE uses an ensemble of oblique decision trees (RandCART and HHCART) as encoders, where each tree provides path constraints through linear inequalities. The decoder solves an l1-minimization problem subject to these constraints to obtain sparse reconstructions. The method is trained on datasets like MNIST, CIFAR10, and Oxford Flowers, with images resized to 28×28 or 32×32. The key innovation lies in using oblique splits (linear combinations of features) instead of axis-parallel splits, which capture richer geometric relationships in the data.

## Key Results
- Effective reconstruction of both tabular and image datasets
- Oblique splits provide more compact representations than axis-parallel splits
- Good reconstruction quality with relatively small number of estimators compared to eForest encoder
- Training time increases significantly with image dimensions and number of estimators

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Oblique splits encode richer geometric relationships than axis-aligned splits
- Mechanism: Linear combinations of features at each node capture oblique decision boundaries directly in the tree structure
- Core assumption: The underlying data distribution has decision boundaries that are not axis-aligned
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "by employing oblique splits consisting in multivariate linear combination of features" and [section II] "partitions the input space X using multivariate tests"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The constrained l1-minimization formulation enables sparse reconstruction
- Mechanism: Framing decoding as l1-minimization with inequality constraints naturally produces sparse solutions
- Core assumption: Sparse solutions are desirable for efficient representation and reconstruction
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "through the computation of a sparse solution of a set of linear inequalities" and [section III] "minimize ||x||1 subject to Ax≥b"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble averaging of oblique trees provides robust encoding-decoding
- Mechanism: Multiple oblique trees create diverse linear constraint systems, and their combination through averaging provides stability
- Core assumption: Diversity among individual tree constraints improves overall reconstruction quality
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "an unsupervised ensemble method consisting of oblique trees" and [section II] "The RF method consists of different randomized decision trees"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Oblique trees (encoder) -> Path extraction (weights and thresholds) -> Constraint matrix construction (Ax≥b) -> l1-minimization solver (decoder) -> Reconstructed output
- Critical path: Training oblique trees -> Encoding input through all trees -> Building constraint system -> Solving l1 problem -> Output reconstruction
- Design tradeoffs: Number of estimators vs. training time vs. reconstruction quality; choice of oblique tree algorithm (HHCART vs RandCART) vs. computational efficiency; l1 regularization strength vs. reconstruction fidelity
- Failure signatures: High MSE values indicate poor reconstruction; extremely long training times suggest curse of dimensionality issues; inconsistent SSIM values across runs suggest instability
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reconstruct a simple 2D tabular dataset (like Diabetes) with varying numbers of estimators to observe the trade-off between quality and training time
  2. Compare reconstruction quality between HHCART and RandCART on a small image dataset to understand the impact of oblique tree choice
  3. Test model reuse by training on CIFAR10 and decoding images from a completely different dataset (like Oxford Flowers) to evaluate generalization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the OF-AE method be extended to neural-type trees as in [13] and [14] while maintaining its computational efficiency?
- Basis in paper: The authors suggest investigating the extension of their methodology to neural-type trees as a potential future direction.
- Why unresolved: Neural-type trees typically require gradient-based optimization which may conflict with the constraint optimization approach used in OF-AE.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and benchmarking of an OF-AE variant using neural-type trees on standard datasets, comparing both reconstruction quality and training time against the original OF-AE.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the number of oblique trees (estimators) and the reconstruction quality in OF-AE, and how does it compare to axis-parallel trees?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that oblique trees provide more compact representations than axis-parallel trees, but doesn't provide a theoretical analysis of this relationship.
- Why unresolved: While empirical results show benefits, the paper doesn't explain why oblique splits lead to better reconstruction with fewer trees from a theoretical perspective.
- What evidence would resolve it: A mathematical proof or extensive empirical study demonstrating the convergence rate and sample complexity differences between oblique and axis-parallel tree autoencoders.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of OF-AE compare to other autoencoder variants (VAE, denoising AE) on the same datasets when accounting for both reconstruction quality and training time?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that OF-AE is computationally expensive compared to neural network autoencoders, but doesn't provide a comprehensive comparison.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating OF-AE's capabilities but doesn't benchmark against established autoencoder methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing OF-AE to VAE, denoising AE, and other autoencoder variants on the same datasets using identical hardware and evaluation metrics.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation on complex real-world datasets beyond standard benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR10)
- No ablation studies on the impact of ensemble size vs. reconstruction quality trade-off
- Computational scalability remains unclear for very high-dimensional data (curse of dimensionality concerns)
- No comparison with state-of-the-art autoencoder variants beyond eForest

## Confidence

- High confidence in the mathematical formulation and constrained optimization approach
- Medium confidence in the empirical results given limited dataset diversity
- Low confidence in scalability claims without larger-scale experiments
