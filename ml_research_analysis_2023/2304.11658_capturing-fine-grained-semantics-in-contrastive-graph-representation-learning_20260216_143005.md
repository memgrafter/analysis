# Capturing Fine-grained Semantics in Contrastive Graph Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11658
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11658
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: FSGCL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods, with large improvements on Amazon-Photos and Coauthor datasets, demonstrating superior ability to capture diverse semantics through motif-based graph construction and semantic-level contrastive learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitation of existing graph contrastive learning methods that ignore fine-grained semantics, resulting in coarse-grained node embeddings. The proposed FSGCL model introduces motif-based graph construction to extract diverse semantic contexts from input data, and employs semantic-level contrastive tasks to enhance the utilization of fine-grained semantics during training. Experimental results on five real-world datasets demonstrate FSGCL's superiority over state-of-the-art methods, with significant improvements particularly on Amazon-Photos and Coauthor datasets.

## Method Summary
FSGCL combines motif-based graph construction with contrastive learning to capture fine-grained semantics in graph-structured data. The method constructs multiple semantic graphs using motif co-occurrence matrices combined with feature similarity, selecting top-K neighbors to preserve both structural and attribute-based semantic distinctions. Separate contrastive objectives are applied on each semantic graph embedding, forcing the model to preserve motif-specific semantic relationships. The model uses a slow-moving average approach to achieve contrastive tasks without negative samples, preserving semantic similarity while maintaining instance discrimination.

## Key Results
- FSGCL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across five real-world datasets
- Large improvements observed on Amazon-Photos and Coauthor datasets
- Visualization analysis on synthetic graphs verifies superior ability to capture diverse semantics
- Ablation studies confirm effectiveness of motif-based graph construction and semantic-level contrastive tasks

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Original graph + T motif-derived semantic graphs -> T+1 semantic-wise GNNs -> Per-view MLPs (projectors and predictors) -> Instance-level and semantic-level contrastive losses -> Slow-moving average target network

- Critical path:
  1. Construct motif-based semantic graphs (preprocessing)
  2. Generate two augmented graph views (PPR diffusion + feature dropout)
  3. Run semantic-wise GNNs to get embeddings
  4. Apply projectors and predictors
  5. Compute instance-level and semantic-level losses without negatives
  6. Update online network, slowly update target network

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Motif selection vs. computational cost: More motifs capture more semantics but increase graph construction and encoding overhead.
  - Top-K neighbor selection: Balances semantic relevance with sparsity; too large K reduces motif specificity, too small may miss important neighbors.
  - Slow-moving average decay τ: Larger τ stabilizes training but slows adaptation; smaller τ risks instability.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor downstream performance despite training: likely motif co-occurrence matrices are too sparse or redundant with features.
  - Training collapse: likely τ too small or predictor too powerful relative to encoder.
  - Out-of-memory: likely too many motif instances or high top-K values on dense graphs.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with single motif (e.g., Motif-A) to verify semantic graph construction and basic contrastive training.
  2. Add second motif and compare performance to assess contribution of additional semantics.
  3. Replace slow-moving average with random negative sampling to confirm its role in preserving semantic similarity.
