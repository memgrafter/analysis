# CML-TTS A Multilingual Dataset for Speech Synthesis in Low-Resource Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10097
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10097
- Reference count: 40
- A 3,176-hour multilingual TTS dataset for Dutch, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Polish, and Spanish

## Executive Summary
CML-TTS is a multilingual TTS dataset derived from Multilingual LibriSpeech, covering seven languages with 3,176 hours of high-quality speech. It adapts the source data through punctuation normalization, audio segmentation, and quality validation to meet TTS requirements. The authors train YourTTS, a multilingual zero-shot multi-speaker model, using CML-TTS plus LibriTTS, achieving Speaker Encoder Cosine Similarity (SECS) scores of 0.77–0.85 across languages for both seen and unseen speakers, and Word/Character Error Rates of 0.19–0.41 and 0.08–0.20, respectively. The dataset is publicly available under CC-BY 4.0 and demonstrates suitability for multilingual TTS research.

## Method Summary
CML-TTS is constructed by adapting Multilingual LibriSpeech through a three-stage pipeline: punctuation normalization and text cleaning, forced alignment-based audio segmentation, and automated quality validation. The dataset covers Dutch, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Polish, and Spanish. The authors train YourTTS, a multilingual zero-shot multi-speaker model, using CML-TTS combined with LibriTTS. The model architecture employs speaker embeddings, multilingual phonetic representations, and attention-based synthesis modules. Training involves multi-task learning with speaker verification and TTS objectives.

## Key Results
- CML-TTS covers seven languages with 3,176 hours of speech data
- YourTTS achieves SECS scores of 0.77–0.85 across languages for both seen and unseen speakers
- WER and CER metrics range from 0.19–0.41 and 0.08–0.20, respectively

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CML-TTS works by providing high-quality, aligned multilingual speech data that enables zero-shot multi-speaker synthesis through speaker embedding transfer learning. The forced alignment ensures precise audio-text correspondence, while punctuation normalization standardizes input text across languages. The combination with LibriTTS provides additional speaker diversity and data volume. YourTTS leverages this data through a speaker encoder that learns transferable speaker representations across languages, enabling synthesis for unseen speakers in seen languages and seen speakers in unseen languages.

## Foundational Learning
- **Forced alignment**: Synchronizes audio with text transcripts by aligning phonemes to audio frames. Needed to ensure precise audio-text correspondence for TTS training. Quick check: Verify alignment accuracy using known benchmark datasets.
- **Speaker embedding transfer**: Encodes speaker characteristics into fixed-dimensional vectors that can be transferred across languages. Needed to enable zero-shot synthesis for unseen speakers. Quick check: Test embedding similarity between same speaker across languages.
- **Multilingual phonetic representation**: Maps language-specific phonemes to a shared phonetic space. Needed to enable cross-lingual synthesis with shared acoustic models. Quick check: Verify phonetic coverage across all target languages.
- **Attention-based synthesis**: Generates spectrograms conditioned on phonetic inputs and speaker embeddings. Needed to produce natural-sounding speech with appropriate speaker characteristics. Quick check: Evaluate synthesis quality with ABX tests.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Raw audio/text -> Forced alignment -> Quality validation -> CML-TTS dataset -> YourTTS speaker encoder -> TTS synthesis module -> Output audio

**Critical path:** Text normalization → Forced alignment → Quality validation → Dataset creation → Model training → Evaluation

**Design tradeoffs:** The dataset prioritizes data quantity and quality over perfect linguistic coverage, accepting some segmentation errors to achieve 3,176 hours. The model trades architectural complexity for zero-shot capability, requiring larger training datasets but enabling synthesis for unseen speakers.

**Failure signatures:** Poor alignment quality manifests as garbled speech or incorrect word pronunciations. Speaker embedding collapse appears as speaker confusion across different voices. Phonetic representation errors show as incorrect phoneme mappings causing unnatural prosody.

3. Evaluate alignment quality by comparing forced alignment outputs against manual transcriptions

## Limitations
- Dataset validation process relies on automated pipeline without explicit discussion of residual alignment errors
- Evaluation is limited to YourTTS model without demonstration of generalization to other TTS architectures
- Coverage of truly low-resource languages is partial, focusing on languages with existing speech data
