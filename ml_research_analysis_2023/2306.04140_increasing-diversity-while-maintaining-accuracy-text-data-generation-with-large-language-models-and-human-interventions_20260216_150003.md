# Increasing Diversity While Maintaining Accuracy: Text Data Generation with Large Language Models and Human Interventions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04140
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04140
- Reference count: 40
- Large language models can generate text data for training and evaluating classification models, but ensuring both high diversity and accuracy in the generated data is challenging.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates human-AI partnerships for creating diverse and accurate text classification datasets using LLM-based text generation. The study examines two technical approaches to diversify text generation: logit suppression and temperature sampling. While these methods increase data diversity, they often hurt accuracy. To address this, the paper explores two human interventions: label replacement (correcting misaligned labels) and out-of-scope filtering (removing irrelevant instances). Oracle studies show that label replacement increases model accuracy by 14.4% when used with diversified datasets, while out-of-scope filtering was not effective in increasing model accuracy.

## Method Summary
The study generates text data using GPT-3 with logit suppression and temperature sampling approaches to increase diversity. The generated datasets are then used to train BERT classifiers, and their performance is evaluated using metrics such as accuracy, diversity (measured using Remote-Clique), and similarity to oracle datasets. Two human interventions are applied: label replacement to correct misaligned labels and out-of-scope filtering to remove irrelevant instances. The effectiveness of these interventions is assessed through oracle studies and by comparing model performance with and without interventions.

## Key Results
- Logit suppression and temperature sampling increase data diversity but often hurt accuracy
- Label replacement intervention increases model accuracy by 14.4% when used with diversified datasets
- Out-of-scope filtering was not effective in increasing model accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Logit suppression increases diversity by reducing token frequency bias in generation.
- Mechanism: The method tracks token frequencies during generation and applies a negative bias to suppress high-frequency tokens, making less frequent tokens more likely to be sampled.
- Core assumption: The distribution of token frequencies in generated text reflects redundancy that can be reduced without harming semantic coherence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "logit suppression, which minimizes the generation of languages that have already been frequently generated"
  - [section]: "logit suppression decreases the probability of high-frequency tokens"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus neighbors include papers on entropy equilibrium and auxiliary hyperparameter-free sampling, suggesting relevance to frequency-based token control mechanisms
- Break condition: If suppression removes tokens essential for maintaining prompt coherence or if the frequency tracking is inaccurate due to small sample sizes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High temperature sampling increases diversity by flattening the probability distribution over tokens.
- Mechanism: Temperature scaling transforms the token probability distribution to make less likely tokens more probable, enabling more varied generation paths.
- Core assumption: The base probability distribution is peaked around common tokens, and flattening it will expose diverse but valid alternatives.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "temperature sampling, which flattens the token sampling probability"
  - [section]: "High temperature leads to 'flatter' token sampling probabilities (Figure 1b), increasing the probability of sampling 'less likely' tokens"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - papers on entropy equilibrium and contrastive search relate to distribution flattening techniques
- Break condition: If flattening becomes extreme enough that sampled tokens become nonsensical or completely unrelated to the prompt.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Label replacement intervention corrects accuracy degradation caused by diversification approaches.
- Mechanism: Human annotators or proxy models identify mismatched labels and replace them with correct ones, restoring label accuracy without changing the text content.
- Core assumption: Label misalignment is the primary source of accuracy loss in diversified generation, and correcting labels directly improves model performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "label replacement (LR), correcting misaligned labels"
  - [section]: "LR increases the absolute accuracy of models trained with diversified datasets by 14.4%"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors don't directly address label correction mechanisms
- Break condition: If label replacement becomes impractical at scale or if text quality degrades independently of label issues.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: LLM text generator (GPT-3) -> Diversification modules (logit suppression and temperature control) -> Human intervention interfaces (label replacement and OOS filtering) -> Proxy model trainers -> Downstream BERT classifiers
- Critical path: Generation -> Diversification -> Quality Assessment -> Human Intervention -> Model Training. Each step depends on the previous one's output quality.
- Design tradeoffs: Balancing diversity against accuracy requires careful tuning of diversification parameters. Using human interventions improves quality but increases cost and latency. Proxy models reduce human effort but introduce their own accuracy limitations.
- Failure signatures: Model accuracy plateaus or degrades despite diversification efforts, indicating label accuracy problems. Extremely high diversity with poor similarity to target domain suggests over-aggressive parameter settings. Proxy models with poor performance on minority classes indicate training data imbalance.

## Limitations
- The scalability of human interventions remains unclear, with no evidence about practical implementation challenges at scale.
- The study's findings are based on eight specific text classification datasets, which may not generalize to other domains or languages.
- The relationship between diversity metrics and actual model performance could be more nuanced than presented.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The effectiveness of label replacement in improving model accuracy when used with diversified datasets (14.4% improvement shown in oracle studies)
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of logit suppression and temperature sampling in increasing data diversity, though with trade-offs to accuracy
- **Medium confidence**: The ineffectiveness of out-of-scope filtering in improving model accuracy, though the mechanism for why this occurs is not fully explained

## Next Checks
1. **Scale test for label replacement**: Implement label replacement across the full dataset sizes used in the experiments (not just oracle studies) to measure the practical accuracy improvements and identify implementation bottlenecks

2. **Domain generalization study**: Apply the diversification and human intervention approaches to datasets from different domains (e.g., legal, medical, or technical documentation) to assess whether the observed patterns hold across diverse application areas

3. **Alternative model architecture evaluation**: Train and evaluate downstream models using different architectures (e.g., RoBERTa, GPT-2, or transformer variants) to determine if the diversification-human intervention patterns are consistent across model families
