# Aiming towards the minimizers: fast convergence of SGD for overparametrized problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02601
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02601
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: SGD with large step sizes achieves linear convergence in the interpolation regime if the aiming condition holds, with width requirements of m = Ω(nr⁶⁺ˡ/λ²) for neural networks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the slow convergence of SGD in overparametrized settings where the number of parameters far exceeds the number of data points. While SGD typically converges slowly in such regimes due to small step size requirements, the authors propose a regularity condition called the aiming condition that ensures gradients point toward the solution set. Under this condition combined with quadratic growth, SGD achieves linear convergence rates comparable to deterministic gradient descent, even with large step sizes. The authors prove that sufficiently wide feedforward neural networks satisfy these conditions with high probability, enabling fast convergence in practice.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze SGD convergence in the interpolation regime where models perfectly fit the training data. They introduce the aiming condition, which requires that the negative gradient direction correlates with the direction toward the solution set. Combined with quadratic growth (a form of Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality), this ensures one-step contraction on a tube around the solution. For neural networks, they use Neural Tangent Kernel analysis to show that wide networks satisfy these conditions with high probability when the width m scales as Ω(nr⁶⁺ˡ/λ²), where n is the number of samples, r bounds the initialization, l is network depth, and λ is the minimal NTK eigenvalue. They also provide a stopping time argument to ensure iterates remain in the initialization ball.

## Key Results
- SGD with large step sizes achieves linear convergence in the interpolation regime when the aiming condition holds
- Wide feedforward neural networks satisfy the aiming and quadratic growth conditions with high probability
- The aiming condition is weaker than quasar convexity but sufficient for fast convergence
- Rejection sampling can boost success probability to be independent of failure probability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SGD with large step sizes can converge linearly in the interpolation regime if the aiming condition holds.
- Mechanism: The aiming condition ensures that the negative gradient direction correlates with the direction toward the solution set, enabling fast progress even with large steps. This is combined with quadratic growth to bound the distance to the solution set.
- Core assumption: The aiming condition ⟨∇L(w), w − projS(w)⟩ ≥ θ · L(w) holds locally around the solution set.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose a regularity condition within the interpolation regime which endows the stochastic gradient method with the same worst-case iteration complexity as the deterministic gradient method, while using only a single sampled gradient (or a minibatch) in each iteration."
  - [section 2.1]: Lemma 2.3 shows one-step contraction on a tube Sr under the aiming condition.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus; weak signal.
- Break condition: If the aiming condition fails (e.g., gradients don't point toward the solution), convergence degrades or fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: For wide neural networks, the aiming and quadratic growth conditions hold with high probability when the network width m is sufficiently large.
- Mechanism: Transition to linearity and the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) ensure that wide networks behave nearly linearly in function space, satisfying PŁ conditions and aiming behavior locally.
- Core assumption: The network width m = Ω(nr⁶⁺ˡ/λ²) ensures the NTK has positive minimal eigenvalue and the aiming condition holds.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3]: Theorem 3.3 states aiming and quadratic growth conditions hold for wide networks under width conditions.
  - [section 1.2]: Explains that the aiming condition is reasonable because it holds for wide feedforward networks.
  - [corpus]: Weak or no direct evidence; the corpus neighbors discuss related SGD convergence but not specifically the aiming condition.
- Break condition: If m is too small or initialization fails, the conditions may not hold and convergence is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SGD iterates remain in the initialization ball with high probability, enabling local convergence guarantees.
- Mechanism: A stopping time argument bounds the probability of escape from the ball Br(w0). Quadratic growth ensures the distance to the solution set shrinks while uniform aiming prevents large deviations from the center.
- Core assumption: The uniform aiming condition ⟨∇L(w), w − v⟩ ≥ θL(w) − ρ · dist(w, S) holds for all v in the ball intersection with S.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2]: Theorem 2.5 proves convergence on a ball using uniform aiming and a stopping time argument.
  - [section 3]: Uniform aiming is verified for wide neural networks.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; the corpus neighbors discuss SGD convergence but not the specific stopping time argument.
- Break condition: If the uniform aiming condition fails or the initial distance to S is too large, iterates may escape the ball and convergence fails.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Loss function L(w) = E[ℓ(w,z)] over data distribution P
  - SGD update: wt+1 = wt − η∇ℓ(wt,zt)
  - Regularity conditions: aiming, quadratic growth, uniform aiming
  - Convergence analysis: stopping time, one-step contraction, rejection sampling
  - Neural network instantiation: wide networks, NTK, transition to linearity

- Critical path:
  1. Verify regularity conditions (aiming, quadratic growth) on a ball or tube
  2. Run SGD with constant learning rate η = Θ(1)
  3. Monitor iterates to ensure they stay in the ball (via uniform aiming)
  4. If needed, apply rejection sampling to boost success probability

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Larger step size η improves convergence rate but risks instability if regularity fails
  - Wider networks increase probability of regularity but require more computation
  - Rejection sampling removes dependence on failure probability but adds overhead

- Failure signatures:
  - Iterates leave the initialization ball (uniform aiming fails)
  - Aiming condition not satisfied (e.g., narrow network, bad initialization)
  - Quadratic growth fails (e.g., PŁ constant too small)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a wide fully-connected network on MNIST; verify aiming condition holds empirically.
  2. Vary network width m; measure how convergence rate changes with m.
  3. Implement rejection sampling; compare success probability vs. naive SGD reruns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the aiming condition be generalized to more complex neural network architectures beyond feedforward networks with linear output layers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that the aiming condition holds for sufficiently wide feedforward neural networks with a linear output layer.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore other architectures like convolutional neural networks or networks with nonlinear output layers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the theoretical analysis to other network architectures and verifying the aiming condition empirically.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of activation functions on the aiming condition and convergence of SGD?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors assume smooth activation functions (Lipschitz and beta-smooth) but do not explore the impact of different activation functions.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate how different activation functions affect the aiming condition or convergence rates.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the aiming condition and convergence for various activation functions and comparing their effects.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the small ball property be relaxed or replaced with a different condition to achieve high probability guarantees?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors use the small ball property to boost the probability of success to be independent of δ1.
- Why unresolved: The small ball property may be too restrictive in practice, and other conditions might be sufficient.
- What evidence would resolve it: Exploring alternative conditions that could replace the small ball property while still ensuring high probability guarantees.

## Limitations

- Width requirements may be prohibitive: The theoretical width requirement m = Ω(nr⁶⁺ˡ/λ²) may be too large for practical applications
- Restricted to interpolation regime: Analysis does not cover problems with label noise or misspecified models
- Strong regularity assumptions: Relies on aiming and quadratic growth conditions that may not hold for general overparametrized models

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Aiming condition enables fast convergence): **High confidence** - The theoretical framework is well-established and the one-step contraction proof is rigorous.
- Mechanism 2 (Wide networks satisfy aiming condition): **Medium confidence** - NTK analysis provides theoretical justification, but empirical validation is limited.
- Mechanism 3 (Uniform aiming ensures ball confinement): **Medium confidence** - The stopping time argument is sound, but practical verification requires careful implementation.
