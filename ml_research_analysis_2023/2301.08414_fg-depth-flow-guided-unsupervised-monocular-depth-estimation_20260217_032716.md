# FG-Depth: Flow-Guided Unsupervised Monocular Depth Estimation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08414
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08414
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised monocular depth estimation with Abs Rel of 0.088 on KITTI and 0.269 on NYU-Depth-v2

## Executive Summary
This paper presents FG-Depth, a novel unsupervised monocular depth estimation method that addresses the limitations of traditional photometric loss-based approaches. The authors propose a flow distillation loss that replaces the typical photometric loss, guiding the optimization with prior knowledge from a pretrained Flow-Net. This loss combines depth regression and flow-guided photometric loss, enabling more effective optimization and achieving more accurate global minima. Additionally, a prior flow-based mask is introduced to remove invalid pixels that introduce noise during training. The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance on both KITTI and NYU-Depth-v2 datasets, with an absolute relative error of 0.088 and 0.269 respectively, surpassing previous methods.

## Method Summary
FG-Depth proposes a flow distillation loss that replaces the traditional photometric loss in unsupervised monocular depth estimation. The method uses a pretrained Flow-Net to guide the optimization process, combining depth regression loss and flow-guided photometric loss. A prior flow-based mask is introduced to remove invalid pixels that introduce noise during training. The method is trained on stereo pairs using the Adam optimizer for 20 epochs, achieving state-of-the-art performance on KITTI and NYU-Depth-v2 datasets.

## Key Results
- Achieves Abs Rel of 0.088 on KITTI dataset
- Achieves Abs Rel of 0.269 on NYU-Depth-v2 dataset
- Surpasses previous state-of-the-art methods on both datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed flow distillation loss addresses the limitations of photometric loss, which can get trapped in local minima due to violations of assumptions like static scenes and Lambertian surfaces. By using prior knowledge from a pretrained Flow-Net, the flow distillation loss guides the optimization towards more accurate global minima. The prior flow-based mask further improves the training process by removing invalid pixels that introduce noise.
