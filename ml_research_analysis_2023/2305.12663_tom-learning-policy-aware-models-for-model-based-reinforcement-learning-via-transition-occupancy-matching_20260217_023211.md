# TOM: Learning Policy-Aware Models for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning via Transition Occupancy Matching

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.12663
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12663
- **Authors:** [Authors not provided]
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Key outcome:** TOM improves MBRL efficiency by focusing model learning on policy-relevant transitions, achieving up to 60% more reward than plain MBPO on Humanoid.

## Executive Summary
TOM addresses the inefficiency in standard model-based reinforcement learning where models are trained on all past experiences, including irrelevant data. The core innovation is Transition Occupancy Matching (TOM), which learns policy-aware dynamics models by focusing on transitions that the current policy experiences. TOM derives a lower bound on the RL objective that includes both policy search and a novel f-divergence model learning objective to match policy footprints between the real environment and the learned model. This is optimized via importance-weighted maximum-likelihood estimation, automatically identifying policy-relevant experiences from a replay buffer. TOM is a plug-and-play model learning subroutine compatible with any MBRL algorithm and demonstrates improved sample efficiency on Mujoco continuous control tasks.

## Method Summary
TOM introduces a novel model learning objective for MBRL that matches the transition occupancy distribution of the current policy in both the real environment and the learned model. The method derives a lower bound on the RL objective that includes an f-divergence term measuring the mismatch between these distributions. This is optimized via importance-weighted maximum likelihood estimation, where transitions from the replay buffer are weighted based on their relevance to the current policy. TOM reduces to an offline imitation learning problem, enabling stable optimization without requiring online model rollouts. The approach is compatible with any backbone MBRL algorithm and focuses model capacity on learning the dynamics that matter most for the current policy.

## Key Results
- TOM achieves at least 60% more reward than plain MBPO on Humanoid tasks
- The method shows consistent sample efficiency improvements across Mujoco environments
- TOM successfully focuses model learning on task-relevant experience regions while de-emphasizing irrelevant areas

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** TOM improves MBRL efficiency by learning dynamics models that match the policy's transition occupancy distribution rather than all past data.
- **Mechanism:** TOM optimizes an f-divergence between the transition occupancy distributions of the current policy in the real environment and in the learned model. This is implemented via importance-weighted maximum likelihood estimation where past transitions are weighted by their relevance to the current policy.
- **Core assumption:** The policy's transition occupancy distribution contains the most relevant information for improving the current policy, and matching this distribution in the learned model leads to better policy optimization.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "TOM offers a plug-and-play model learning sub-routine that is compatible with any backbone MBRL algorithm"
  - [section]: "We instead propose a new 'transition occupancy matching' (TOM) objective for MBRL model learning: a model is good to the extent that the current policy experiences the same distribution of transitions inside the model as in the real environment"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus lacks direct evidence about TOM's specific f-divergence approach
- **Break condition:** If the current policy's transition occupancy is not a good indicator of what the model needs to learn (e.g., if the policy is stuck in a local optimum or if exploration is critical).

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** TOM reduces the TOM objective to an offline imitation learning problem, enabling stable optimization without requiring online model rollouts.
- **Mechanism:** By regularizing the transition occupancy distribution of the learned model to that of the replay buffer empirical policy, TOM casts the problem as an offline imitation learning task. This allows using dual reinforcement learning techniques to compute optimal importance weights from the replay buffer alone.
- **Core assumption:** The Bellman transition flow constraint allows reformulating the TOM objective in a way that depends only on the replay buffer and current policy, without requiring model-generated samples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "We observe that optimizing (13) requires estimating dπT((s,a),s′); however, doing so requires rolling out π in T̂ , which suffers from the compounding error... To circumvent these issues, we propose a practical algorithm that can learn a policy-aware model tailored to the visitation distribution of π without any additional samples from the real environment."
  - [section]: "This dual problem learns a Q-function... that informs the relevance of state-action pairs for learning dπT*((s,a),s′) according to reward r(s,a,s′)"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus lacks direct evidence about the dual optimization approach
- **Break condition:** If the replay buffer does not adequately represent the state-action space or if the empirical policy distribution is too different from the current policy.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** TOM's importance weighting automatically identifies policy-relevant experiences without requiring hand-crafted schedules or value gradients.
- **Mechanism:** TOM computes importance weights based on the log-ratio of transition occupancy distributions between the current policy and the replay buffer empirical policy. This automatically upweights transitions that are more likely under the current policy while downweighting irrelevant or outdated data.
- **Core assumption:** The log-ratio of transition occupancy distributions provides a principled measure of policy relevance that adapts to the current policy's behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "We adapt a recently proposed offline imitation approach (Ma et al., 2022a) to reduce the TOM model-learning objective to importance-weighted maximum likelihood model learning, where higher weights are assigned to past transitions that lie in the footprint of the current policy."
  - [section]: "TOM's importance weights for transitions in various parts of the heatmap, shown in Figure 2 (right), confirm that TOM successfully focuses the model on the task-relevant road regions, de-emphasizing the highly non-linear rocky regions."
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus lacks direct evidence about TOM's automatic importance weighting compared to heuristic approaches
- **Break condition:** If the importance weights become too concentrated on a small subset of transitions, leading to overfitting or loss of exploration capability.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Transition occupancy distribution
  - **Why needed here:** TOM operates on transition occupancy distributions rather than state-action occupancy distributions, requiring understanding of how policies interact with environment dynamics at the transition level.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the difference between state-action occupancy dπ(s,a) and transition occupancy dπ((s,a),s')?

- **Concept:** f-divergence and dual optimization
  - **Why needed here:** TOM uses f-divergence to measure the difference between transition occupancy distributions and employs dual reinforcement learning to optimize the objective without model rollouts.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the choice of f-divergence affect the TOM objective and its optimization?

- **Concept:** Importance-weighted maximum likelihood estimation
  - **Why needed here:** TOM reduces to importance-weighted MLE for model learning, requiring understanding of how to weight past experiences based on their relevance to the current policy.
  - **Quick check question:** How are the importance weights computed in TOM, and what do they represent?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Policy network (πφ) -> Dynamics model (pω) -> Discriminator (cψ) -> Value network (Qθ) -> Replay buffer

- **Critical path:**
  1. Collect transitions using current policy
  2. Train discriminator to estimate transition relevance
  3. Compute optimal importance weights using dual optimization
  4. Train dynamics model using weighted regression on replay buffer
  5. Use learned model for policy optimization

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - TOM trades off model capacity for policy relevance by focusing on transition occupancy matching rather than full dynamics reconstruction
  - The choice of f-divergence affects both the theoretical properties and practical performance
  - Regularization strength between current policy and replay buffer distributions impacts stability

- **Failure signatures:**
  - If importance weights become too uniform, TOM degenerates to standard MLE
  - If importance weights become too concentrated, the model may overfit to a small subset of transitions
  - If the discriminator fails to distinguish policy-relevant transitions, the model learning will be ineffective

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement TOM on a simple grid-world environment with known optimal policy to verify that importance weights correctly identify relevant transitions
  2. Compare TOM's performance to standard MLE on a continuous control task (e.g., Hopper) to measure sample efficiency gains
  3. Analyze the learned importance weights on a replay buffer with known structure to verify TOM focuses on task-relevant regions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does TOM's performance scale with increasingly complex environments beyond the Mujoco suite, such as in visual control tasks or tasks with long-horizon dependencies?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions TOM's effectiveness on Mujoco environments and notes larger gains in environments with more complex dynamics (e.g., Humanoid), but does not test on visual control tasks or explicitly complex temporal dependencies.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on standard Mujoco benchmarks which are primarily state-based and relatively short-horizon compared to real-world applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical evaluation of TOM on visual control benchmarks (e.g., DeepMind Control Suite with pixel observations) and long-horizon tasks (e.g., with sparse rewards or delayed effects).

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the choice of f-divergence in TOM and the sample complexity or convergence rate of the learned policy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that TOM can use any f-divergence that upper bounds the KL divergence, but does not analyze how different choices affect performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides a practical algorithm, it does not offer theoretical analysis on how the divergence choice impacts learning efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical bounds or empirical studies comparing different f-divergences (e.g., KL, reverse KL, χ²) on sample efficiency and final policy performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does TOM handle non-stationary policies during online learning, where the policy changes rapidly between model updates?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper describes TOM as a plug-and-play model learning subroutine compatible with any MBRL algorithm, but does not discuss challenges with rapidly changing policies.
- Why unresolved: The importance weights in TOM are computed based on the current policy, but rapid policy changes could make these weights stale or misaligned with the actual policy distribution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing TOM's performance with rapidly varying policies, or theoretical analysis of how weight staleness affects the lower bound and policy improvement.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can TOM be extended to model-based offline RL settings where the replay buffer contains highly suboptimal or diverse behavior policies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that TOM can learn using solely the replay buffer and demonstrates this on a curated dataset, but does not test on challenging offline RL benchmarks.
- Why unresolved: The offline experiments in the paper use a carefully ordered buffer with a clear progression of policy quality, which is not representative of real-world offline datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluation of TOM on standard offline RL benchmarks (e.g., D4RL) with highly suboptimal or diverse datasets, and comparison to state-of-the-art offline MBRL methods.

## Limitations
- TOM's performance gains are modest across most Mujoco tasks, with Humanoid being the standout exception
- The method's dependence on the quality of the replay buffer's empirical policy distribution introduces potential brittleness
- While presented as plug-and-play, TOM's performance relative to carefully-tuned domain-specific MBRL approaches has not been thoroughly evaluated

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the core algorithmic contribution and its theoretical foundations
- **Medium confidence** in the empirical claims regarding sample efficiency improvements
- **Low confidence** in the generalization claims to diverse MBRL settings
