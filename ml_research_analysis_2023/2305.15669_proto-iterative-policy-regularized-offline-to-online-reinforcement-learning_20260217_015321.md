# PROTO: Iterative Policy Regularized Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15669
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15669
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Iterative policy regularization framework bridges stable initial finetuning with optimal final performance on D4RL benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
PROTO addresses the fundamental challenge in offline-to-online reinforcement learning by introducing an iterative policy regularization framework that gradually relaxes constraint strength during training. The method augments the standard RL objective with a KL-divergence regularization term that evolves iteratively, enabling stable policy learning while avoiding the over-conservatism that plagues existing methods. Extensive experiments on D4RL benchmarks demonstrate PROTO's superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines across MuJoCo locomotion, AntMaze navigation, and Adroit manipulation tasks.

## Method Summary
PROTO implements an iterative policy regularization approach for offline-to-online reinforcement learning. The method starts with a pretrained policy π0 from offline data, then performs online finetuning with an augmented objective that includes KL-divergence regularization between the current policy πk and previous policy ¯πk. The regularization coefficient α is gradually annealed from an initial value to zero, allowing controlled exploration while maintaining stability. The method uses Polyak averaging with speed τ to update both policy and value networks, and conservatism annealing speed η to control the rate of constraint relaxation. PROTO achieves this with minimal computational overhead compared to ensemble-based approaches.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on D4RL benchmarks across MuJoCo, AntMaze, and Adroit tasks
- Demonstrates superior performance compared to fixed-constraint regularization methods
- Maintains computational efficiency with negligible overhead from regularization term
- Provides theoretical convergence guarantees while avoiding suboptimality gaps

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Iterative policy regularization achieves both stable initial finetuning and optimal final performance by gradually relaxing conservatism.
- Mechanism: PROTO augments the standard RL objective with an iteratively evolving KL-divergence regularization term. During initial iterations, the regularization term constrains the finetuning policy to remain close to the pretrained policy π0, preventing distributional shift. As training progresses, the regularization term gradually relaxes as πk evolves, allowing exploration while maintaining stability through trust-region-style updates.
- Core assumption: The iterative evolution of πk creates a trust region that starts conservative but expands appropriately, unlike fixed-constraint methods that remain overly restrictive.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Performing a trust-region-style update, PROTO yields stable initial finetuning and optimal final performance by gradually evolving the regularization term to relax the constraint strength."
  - [section 3.3]: "Performing a trust-region-style update, PROTO constrains the policy w.r.t.an iteratively evolving policy πk, which smartly serves dual purposes by ensuring that: 1) the finetuned policy remains close to the pre-trained policy π0 during the initial finetuning stage, to avoid distributional shift; 2) gradually allowing the policy to deviate far from the potentially suboptimal constraint induced by π0 at the later stage, to find the optima as long as it stays within the trust region."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in related papers about this specific iterative evolution mechanism.
- Break condition: If the annealing schedule is too aggressive or too conservative, the method could either destabilize early training or remain overly conservative throughout.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The iterative policy regularization induces no suboptimality gap unlike fixed policy constraints.
- Mechanism: Unlike fixed policy constraints that maintain a suboptimal constraint set Π throughout training (causing non-eliminable suboptimality gaps), PROTO's iterative constraint set evolves with πk. The regularization term can theoretically converge to zero suboptimality as k → ∞, allowing optimal policy learning while maintaining stability.
- Core assumption: The iteratively evolving constraint set allows sufficient deviation from suboptimal regions while maintaining the benefits of regularization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3]: "The comparison between Theorem 1 and Eq. (7)-(8) demonstrates that PROTO serves as a seamless bridge between fixed policy regularization and no regularization, allowing for stability while retaining the optimality of finetuning performance."
  - [section B.2]: "Theorem 1 provides an intuitive insight that the suboptimality gap can be minimized to zero as k → ∞ even with the existence of iterative policy regularization."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in related papers about the theoretical suboptimality gap elimination.
- Break condition: If the approximation error ϵj is too large or accumulates rapidly, the convergence guarantee may not hold in practice.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PROTO achieves high computational efficiency while maintaining strong performance.
- Mechanism: The method only requires calculating an additional regularization term (negligible computational overhead) compared to ensemble-based methods or transformer-based approaches. This minimal modification enables integration with diverse offline pretraining and online finetuning methods while retaining efficiency.
- Core assumption: The KL-divergence regularization term can be computed efficiently without requiring additional model ensembles or complex architectures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Simple yet elegant, PROTO imposes minimal additional computation and enables highly efficient online finetuning."
  - [section 3.3]: "In addition, calculating the additional regularization term introduces negligible computational cost compared to ensemble networks [30] or transformer-based finetuning approaches [61], enabling agile and lightweight applications."
  - [section 4.5]: "PROTO only requires calculating the additional regularization term, computational overhead of which is negligible, therefore enjoys the same computational efficiency as standard off-policy RL methods."
- Break condition: If the regularization term calculation becomes expensive in high-dimensional action spaces, the efficiency advantage may diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pretrained policy π0 (from offline RL/IL) -> Current policy πk (being finetuned) -> Delayed policy ¯πk (for stability via Polyak averaging) -> Regularization coefficient α (annealed over time) -> Conservatism annealing speed η -> Polyak averaging speed τ -> Standard actor-critic components (Q-network, policy network, target networks)

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize with pretrained policy π0 and corresponding Q0
  2. At each iteration, update policy using objective with KL regularization
  3. Update target networks using Polyak averaging
  4. Anneal regularization coefficient α according to schedule
  5. Continue until convergence or maximum iterations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed vs. iterative regularization: Fixed regularization (Frozen variant) causes over-conservatism; iterative regularization balances stability and optimality
  - Ensemble methods vs. regularization: Ensembles provide stability but are computationally expensive; regularization is efficient but requires careful scheduling
  - Polyak averaging speed τ: Faster updates may cause instability; slower updates may slow convergence

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance plateaus below optimal: Likely indicates insufficient relaxation of regularization
  - Initial performance drop: Likely indicates too aggressive regularization or insufficient annealing schedule
  - Slow convergence: Likely indicates overly conservative regularization or too slow annealing schedule
  - High variance in results: Likely indicates unstable Polyak averaging or inappropriate initial regularization strength

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement PROTO with SAC finetuning on HalfCheetah-random-v2 from D4RL, comparing with SAC from scratch
  2. Test different Polyak averaging speeds (τ = 1e-3, 1e-4, 1e-5) on Walker2d-medium-v2 to find optimal stability
  3. Implement the Frozen variant (fixed policy regularization) on Hopper-medium-v2 to demonstrate benefits of iterative approach

## Limitations

- Theoretical convergence guarantees rely on assumptions about approximation error ϵj that may not hold in practice for complex high-dimensional control tasks
- Computational efficiency claims lack rigorous empirical validation against ensemble-based alternatives in terms of wall-clock time
- Performance depends critically on proper hyperparameter tuning with limited guidance on interaction effects

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The basic mechanism of iterative KL regularization improving over fixed-constraint methods is well-supported by both theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) and experimental results showing consistent performance gains.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim about "no suboptimality gap" is theoretically sound but practically limited by approximation errors and hyperparameter sensitivity that could reintroduce gaps.
- **Medium Confidence**: Computational efficiency advantages are supported by design arguments but lack direct empirical comparison with ensemble methods.
