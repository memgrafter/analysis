# Malafide: a novel adversarial convolutive noise attack against deepfake and spoofing detection systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07655
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07655
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Introduces adversarial convolutive noise attack degrading spoofing countermeasure performance by up to 22.0% EER in white-box settings

## Executive Summary
Malafide introduces a novel adversarial attack against deepfake and spoofing detection systems using optimized linear time-invariant filters tuned to specific spoofing attack artifacts. Unlike traditional additive noise attacks, Malafide filters operate independently of input utterances and require optimization of only a small number of coefficients. The attack demonstrates significant vulnerability across multiple countermeasure architectures, achieving up to 22.0% equal error rate in white-box settings and 23.9% in black-box settings against a RawNet2 CM, while preserving speech quality and ASV system vulnerability.

## Method Summary
Malafide optimizes linear time-invariant (LTI) filters to introduce adversarial convolutive noise that suppresses artifacts detectable by spoofing countermeasures. The attack uses gradient descent to optimize filter coefficients that maximize the countermeasure's score for spoofed utterances, with filters initialized using He initialization and constrained to preserve speech fidelity. Filters are attack-specific and utterance-independent, requiring optimization only on a subset of spoofed utterances from each spoofing algorithm. The attack was evaluated on the ASVspoof 2019 logical access dataset using three countermeasure architectures (RawNet2, AASIST, and SSL-based) and measured using equal error rate and spoofing-aware speaker verification error rate.

## Key Results
- Degrades spoofing countermeasure performance by an order of magnitude, achieving up to 22.0% EER in white-box settings
- Maintains effectiveness in black-box settings with 23.9% EER against RawNet2 CM
- Integrated CM-ASV systems using self-supervised learning CMs showed greater robustness but remained vulnerable
- Filter optimization requires only a small number of coefficients compared to utterance-specific perturbations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Malafide filters introduce adversarial convolutive noise that degrades spoofing countermeasure performance by targeting attack-specific artifacts.
- Mechanism: The linear time-invariant (LTI) filter is optimized to maximize the countermeasure's score for spoofed utterances, effectively suppressing the frequency bands where artifacts are detected. Unlike additive noise, this convolutional approach is utterance-independent and robust to time-domain shifts.
- Core assumption: The countermeasure relies on specific frequency patterns that can be attenuated without destroying the spoofing attack's ability to fool the ASV system.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By introducing convolutional noise using an optimised linear time-invariant filter, Malafide attacks can be used to compromise CM reliability while preserving other speech attributes such as quality and the speaker's voice."
  - [section 3] "The filter should then be tuned to counter the reliance of the CM upon these same artefacts."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors discuss related attacks but don't specifically validate the frequency-targeting mechanism of Malafide.
- Break condition: If the CM uses time-domain or non-frequency-based features, or if the spoofing attack itself doesn't create detectable artifacts, the filter's effectiveness diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Malafide filters are universal within a spoofing attack type, requiring optimization for only a small number of coefficients.
- Mechanism: The filter is optimized on a subset of spoofed utterances from a specific attack algorithm and then applied to all utterances from that attack, reducing computational overhead compared to utterance-specific perturbations.
- Core assumption: Artifacts from a given spoofing algorithm are consistent enough across utterances that a single filter can effectively suppress them.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Malafide filters are optimised independently of the input utterance and duration, are tuned instead to the underlying spoofing attack, and require the optimisation of only a small number of filter coefficients."
  - [section 3] "Different to all previous work, our approach involves the learning of an adversarial linear, time-invariant (LTI) filter which can be applied in real-time to a spoofed utterance through time domain convolution."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors discuss universal perturbations but don't specifically validate the small-coefficient optimization approach.
- Break condition: If artifacts vary significantly across utterances from the same attack algorithm, or if the optimization requires more coefficients than feasible, the attack loses universality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Malafide attacks preserve speech fidelity enough to maintain ASV vulnerability while degrading CM performance.
- Mechanism: By initializing the filter with a Dirac-like impulse response and constraining the central coefficient, the attack balances artifact suppression with speech quality preservation, ensuring the spoofing attack still fools the ASV system.
- Core assumption: The CM can be fooled without introducing distortion levels that would cause the ASV system to reject the spoofed utterance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Malafide attacks can be used to compromise CM reliability while preserving other speech attributes such as quality and the speaker's voice."
  - [section 3] "We have found that such a suitable balance can be achieved by initialising m to resemble a convolutive identity, i.e. an impulse response which exhibits a dominant Dirac (delta) function."
  - [section 6] "Informal listening tests revealed that shorter-length filters better preserve speech fidelity whereas longer-length filters result in detectable reverberation."
- Break condition: If the filter optimization prioritizes CM degradation over speech quality too heavily, or if the spoofing attack is sensitive to the introduced distortion, the ASV system may reject the utterance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Spoofing attack generator (A01-A19 algorithms) -> Malafide filter optimizer -> Linear time-invariant filter -> Countermeasure (RawNet2, AASIST, SSL-based) -> ASV system -> Score fusion module
- Critical path: Spoofed utterance → Malafide filter convolution → Modified utterance → Countermeasure evaluation → Score fusion with ASV → SASV decision
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Filter length vs. speech fidelity: Longer filters provide stronger attacks but introduce more distortion
  - White-box vs. black-box: Attacks optimized for one CM may or may not transfer to others
  - Artifact suppression vs. ASV vulnerability: The filter must degrade CM performance without making the spoofing attack detectable by ASV
- Failure signatures:
  - High CM EER but low SASV-EER: The filter degrades CM but introduces distortion that the ASV system detects
  - Low CM EER in black-box: The attack doesn't transfer between different CM architectures
  - ASR rejection: The introduced noise makes the speech unintelligible to automatic systems
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test Malafide filter on a single spoofing attack type with one CM to verify basic functionality
  2. Compare white-box vs. black-box performance across different CM architectures
  3. Evaluate SASV-EER to confirm the attack maintains ASV vulnerability while degrading CM performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How effective are Malafide attacks against spoofing countermeasures that use alternative self-supervised learning architectures beyond wav2vec 2.0, such as HuBERT or WavLM?
- What is the optimal trade-off between filter length and speech fidelity preservation for real-world deployment scenarios where both ASV and CM performance matter?
- How do Malafide attacks perform against spoofing countermeasures in real-world scenarios with background noise, varying channel conditions, and different recording environments?
- Can integrated defense strategies combining multiple countermeasure approaches effectively mitigate Malafide attacks?

## Limitations

- Effectiveness relies heavily on optimized filters for specific spoofing algorithms with uncertain generalization to unseen attack types
- Black-box transferability (23.9% EER) suggests moderate generalization that may not hold against diverse countermeasure architectures
- Real-world artifact variability and environmental conditions were not tested, limiting practical deployment assessment
- Attack effectiveness against non-neural or ensemble-based countermeasures remains unexplored

## Confidence

- High confidence: Malafide's basic mechanism (convolutional noise filtering) and white-box attack effectiveness against RawNet2 CM
- Medium confidence: Black-box transferability across different CM architectures and the universal filter optimization approach
- Medium confidence: The claim that speech fidelity is preserved enough to maintain ASV vulnerability

## Next Checks

1. Cross-algorithm generalization test: Apply filters optimized for A01-A10 spoofing attacks to A11-A19 attacks to quantify effectiveness degradation and identify which spoofing algorithms are most vulnerable to cross-algorithm filter application.

2. Real-world artifact variability assessment: Test Malafide filters on spoofing attacks generated with different synthesis conditions (varying speaker demographics, recording environments, or synthesis parameters) to evaluate robustness beyond the controlled ASVspoof 2019 dataset.

3. Ensemble countermeasure robustness evaluation: Apply Malafide filters against ensemble-based countermeasures that combine multiple CM architectures to assess whether the attack can bypass multi-layered defense systems.
