# SupSiam: Non-contrastive Auxiliary Loss for Learning from Molecular Conformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07754
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07754
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Non-contrastive Siamese auxiliary task improves manifold smoothness and robustness of E3NNs for molecular conformer-based drug activity prediction

## Executive Summary
This work introduces SupSiam, a supervised Siamese network approach that incorporates a positive-pair-only auxiliary task during training of Euclidean neural networks (E3NNs) on molecular conformers. By minimizing cosine distance between embeddings of augmented conformer pairs, the method increases manifold smoothness around molecular geometries while maintaining strong performance on drug activity prediction tasks. Experiments on PGP_BROCATELLI (classification) and CLEARANCE_HEPATOCYTE_AZ (regression) datasets demonstrate that SupSiam achieves better robustness to input coordinate perturbations compared to standard supervised training, with particular benefits in small-data regimes common in drug discovery.

## Method Summary
SupSiam trains E3NNs with a combined loss function consisting of supervised target loss, non-contrastive Siamese auxiliary loss on augmented conformer pairs, and l2 regularization. The method randomly samples single conformers per molecule per epoch and applies Gaussian noise augmentation to create positive pairs. Task weights (λs for Siamese loss, λr for regularization) control the balance between manifold smoothness and representation compactness. The approach aims to learn smoother embedding manifolds that better capture the underlying structure of molecular conformers while maintaining predictive performance for drug activity tasks.

## Key Results
- SupSiam increases manifold smoothness (measured via KL divergence) around molecular geometries while maintaining or improving ROC-AUC and Spearman correlation metrics
- Task weighting (λs, λr) has substantial effects on partial dimensional collapse, with higher λs generally increasing smoothness but also increasing collapse
- Random sampling of single conformers per epoch provides computational efficiency while approximating Boltzmann-weighted conformer distributions
- The method shows particular promise for small-data regimes where traditional contrastive methods may be less effective

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Non-contrastive Siamese auxiliary task increases manifold smoothness around molecular geometries.
- **Mechanism:** By training the model to minimize cosine distance between embeddings of augmented conformer pairs (positive-only), the network learns to treat similar 3D structures as nearby in latent space, leading to smoother predictions under small geometric perturbations.
- **Core assumption:** Molecular conformers that are close in 3D space (after augmentation) should have similar property predictions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "non-contrastive (positive-pair only) auxiliary task aids in supervised training of Euclidean neural networks (E3NNs) and increases manifold smoothness (MS) around point-cloud geometries."
  - [section 3.2] "Siamese task Ls... translates to predicting zc_a from zc_1... gradients detached from the other."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific smoothness claim; based on internal experimental results.
- **Break condition:** If augmentations produce conformers that are too dissimilar (large τ), the smoothness effect breaks down and the auxiliary task no longer aligns with the target task.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Task weighting (λs, λr) controls the balance between manifold smoothness and representation compactness.
- **Mechanism:** λs controls the strength of the smoothness-inducing Siamese loss, while λr controls latent space regularization via l2 norm. Higher λs increases smoothness but may cause partial dimensional collapse; higher λr prevents collapse but can inhibit learning.
- **Core assumption:** Both smoothness and compactness are necessary for good generalization, and their optimal balance is task-dependent.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "substantial effects of task-weighting... on representation collapse."
  - [section 4.2] "We expect that Ls is not simply serving to compactify latent space... while Ls does push related embeddings to closer cosine distances... with an increased expansion of latent space."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus does not directly discuss this tradeoff in the context of SupSiam.
- **Break condition:** If λs is too high relative to λr, partial dimensional collapse occurs (high Γ), degrading model performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Random sampling of single conformers per epoch prevents overfitting to specific conformer sets and marginalizes conformer selection effects.
- **Mechanism:** By sampling one conformer per molecule per epoch rather than using full ensembles, the model sees a diverse distribution of conformers over training, approximating a Boltzmann-weighted view and reducing dependence on any single conformer's geometry.
- **Core assumption:** Conformational diversity is beneficial, but full ensemble modeling per epoch is computationally prohibitive.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 3.1] "we randomly sample a single conformer c ∈ C_m for each molecule at each pass through models... has the added benefit of computational efficiency."
  - [section 3.1 footnote] "since a random conformer is sampled in each epoch, models are roughly exposed to a Boltzmann-weighted distribution of conformations."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific sampling strategy.
- **Break condition:** If the sampled conformer set is too biased (e.g., all low-energy conformers), the model may not learn to handle diverse geometries.

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: E3NNs are equivariant to SE(3) transformations, making them suitable for modeling molecular geometries where 3D structure directly influences properties.

  - Why needed here: Smoothness ensures that small perturbations in molecular geometry lead to small changes in predictions, which is critical for robustness in drug discovery where structures may vary slightly between experiments.

  - Why needed here: Understanding collapse helps diagnose when the model is not using the full latent space effectively, which can degrade performance.
  - Quick check question: What metric (Γ) is used to detect partial dimensional collapse, and what does Γ = 0.5 signify?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Conformer → E3NN trunk (4 interaction blocks) → Global mean pooling → Readout MLP → Prediction; Augmented conformer → E3NN → Siamese projection MLP → Cosine distance loss
- **Critical path:** Conformer → E3NN → Global pooling → Readout → Prediction; augmented conformer → E3NN → Siamese projection → Cosine distance loss
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Using positive-only pairs avoids forcing unrelated molecules apart but may limit discriminative power
  - Sampling single conformers per epoch trades computational efficiency for marginal conformer selection effects
  - Probabilistic outputs add uncertainty quantification but increase model complexity
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Erratic training curves with high variance in early epochs
  - Divergence of cosine embedding distance for augmented pairs
  - High partial dimensional collapse (Γ close to 1.0) indicating collapse to few dimensions
  - Low manifold smoothness (high KL divergence between augmented pairs) indicating sensitivity to noise
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train with λs = 0.0, λr = 0.0 (pure supervised) to establish baseline performance and manifold smoothness
  2. Train with small λs = 0.1, λr = 0.0 to test smoothness effect without regularization
  3. Train with λs = 1.0, λr = 0.1 to find balanced task weighting that maintains performance while improving smoothness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of conformer generation method (e.g., ETKDG vs QM-based methods) affect the performance and robustness of SupSiam-trained E3NNs in drug activity prediction tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that conformer selection remains an outstanding challenge and that their choice of ETKDG was motivated by computational efficiency, not necessarily optimal conformer quality.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses ETKDG for conformer generation but doesn't compare to higher-quality conformers from QM methods. This is justified by computational constraints and the desire to isolate the effects of the Siamese auxiliary task.
- What evidence would resolve it: A direct comparison of SupSiam-trained E3NNs using conformers generated by ETKDG versus those generated by QM methods (e.g., DFT) on the same drug activity prediction tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal task-specific balance of the Siamese loss weight (λs) and the l2 regularization weight (λr) for maximizing both manifold smoothness and target task performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that increasing λs generally increases manifold smoothness but also increases partial dimensional collapse (PDC), while increasing λr reduces PDC but can inhibit target task learning. The authors suggest a task-specific balance might be optimal.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides an ablation study over λs and λr but doesn't systematically optimize their combination for each task. The observed trade-offs suggest the optimal balance may be task-dependent.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study optimizing the combination of λs and λr for each task using a method like Bayesian optimization, reporting both manifold smoothness and target task performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does increasing the embedding dimension (d) beyond 256 continue to increase partial dimensional collapse (PDC), or is there a point of diminishing returns?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes a positive correlation between embedding dimension (d) and PDC, which is contrary to findings in Li et al. (2022) where increasing capacity was used to reduce PDC.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores d values up to 256. The authors speculate that an information encoding limit may be reached at relatively small d, but this is not empirically verified.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the ablation study to larger embedding dimensions (e.g., d = 512, 1024) and analyzing the trend in PDC and other latent properties.

## Limitations

- The manifold smoothness improvements lack comparison to established baselines beyond standard supervised training
- The sampling strategy's effectiveness in marginalizing conformer selection effects is theoretically justified but not empirically validated against full ensemble modeling
- The optimal task weighting (λs, λr) appears highly dataset-dependent with no clear guidance for hyperparameter selection in new applications
- The method's computational efficiency claims relative to full ensemble modeling are not quantified

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The fundamental mechanism of using positive-pair-only Siamese auxiliary tasks to improve smoothness around molecular geometries is well-supported by the experimental results on both classification and regression tasks.

**Medium Confidence:** The claims about partial dimensional collapse and its relationship to task weighting are supported by internal metrics (Γ values) but lack comparison to established collapse metrics in the broader ML literature.

**Low Confidence:** The assertion that random conformer sampling approximates Boltzmann-weighted distributions is theoretically plausible but lacks empirical validation.

## Next Checks

1. **External Validation:** Test SupSiam on a third, independent molecular dataset (e.g., PDBbind or QM9) to verify generalizability of smoothness improvements and performance metrics.

2. **Ablation Study Extension:** Systematically vary augmentation noise magnitude τ beyond the reported [0.1,1] range to determine robustness boundaries and identify the critical point where smoothness effects break down.

3. **Baseline Comparison:** Compare SupSiam against established contrastive methods (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo) on the same molecular conformer tasks to quantify the relative advantages of the non-contrastive approach.
