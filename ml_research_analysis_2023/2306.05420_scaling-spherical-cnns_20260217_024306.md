# Scaling Spherical CNNs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05420
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05420
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: First large-scale implementation of spherical CNNs, achieving state-of-the-art performance on molecular and weather forecasting tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first large-scale implementation of spherical CNNs, scaling them by an order of magnitude in model capacity and input resolution compared to previous work. The key innovations include an efficient JAX implementation optimized for TPUs, novel activation functions (phase collapse nonlinearity) and normalization layers (spectral batch normalization), and application-specific input representations for molecular and weather data. The scaled spherical CNNs achieve state-of-the-art performance on the QM9 molecular benchmark, outperforming previous models including equivariant graph neural networks and transformers on 8 out of 12 targets. On weather forecasting tasks, they demonstrate competitive performance against specialized models, with particularly strong results in temperature prediction. The work demonstrates that spherical CNNs can be effectively scaled to handle real-world scientific applications while maintaining their desirable properties of rotation equivariance and computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The authors developed a JAX implementation of spherical CNNs optimized for TPU hardware, enabling scaling by an order of magnitude in model capacity and resolution. Key innovations include the phase collapse nonlinearity, which addresses issues with traditional nonlinearities in spherical harmonic representations, and spectral batch normalization, which normalizes across both batch and frequency dimensions. The implementation supports both sampled spherical input representations for molecules and spectral representations for weather data. Model architectures were designed with depthwise separable convolutions and efficient pooling operations to maintain computational tractability at high resolutions.

## Key Results
- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on QM9 molecular benchmark, outperforming equivariant GNNs and transformers on 8/12 targets
- Demonstrated competitive weather forecasting performance, particularly excelling in temperature prediction
- Scaled spherical CNNs by an order of magnitude in both model capacity and input resolution compared to prior work
- Showed that spherical CNNs can effectively handle real-world scientific datasets while maintaining rotation equivariance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Spherical CNNs leverage the mathematical properties of spherical harmonics to achieve rotation equivariance, meaning their outputs transform predictably under rotations of the input. This is particularly valuable for scientific applications where rotational symmetry is inherent. The key mechanism enabling scaling is the transition from expensive sampling-based operations to efficient spectral domain computations using fast Fourier transforms. The phase collapse nonlinearity preserves the equivariant structure while enabling effective feature learning, and spectral batch normalization stabilizes training across different frequency components. Together, these innovations allow spherical CNNs to scale to much larger models and higher resolutions than previously possible.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input data -> Spectral transformation -> Convolutional layers with phase collapse nonlinearity -> Spectral batch normalization -> Pooling -> Output layer

Critical path: The spectral transformation followed by rotation-equivariant convolutions forms the core computational path, with the phase collapse nonlinearity and spectral batch normalization being critical for performance.

Design tradeoffs: The choice between sampled and spectral representations involves tradeoffs between flexibility and computational efficiency. Spectral methods are faster but require band-limited data, while sampling allows for arbitrary functions but is computationally expensive.

Failure signatures: Performance degradation typically manifests as loss of rotation-equivariance, training instability due to spectral normalization issues, or spectral leakage artifacts in the frequency domain.

First experiments:
1. Verify rotation-equivariance by rotating inputs and checking if outputs transform correctly
2. Test spectral normalization stability across different frequency bands

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions regarding the long-term scalability of spherical CNNs beyond the demonstrated improvements, the generalizability of the proposed phase collapse nonlinearity and spectral batch normalization across diverse domains, and the potential computational bottlenecks when scaling to even higher resolutions or more complex datasets.

## Limitations
- Scalability challenges beyond the demonstrated order-of-magnitude improvement remain unexplored
- Performance gains may be partially attributed to specific architectural choices rather than fundamental advantages of spherical CNNs
- Computational bottlenecks at extremely high resolutions or with more complex datasets are not fully characterized

## Confidence
High confidence in the implementation details and computational optimizations presented for scaling spherical CNNs on TPUs
Medium confidence in the state-of-the-art claims on QM9 and weather forecasting tasks, given the competitive landscape and potential for unreported baselines
Low confidence in the long-term scalability and efficiency of spherical CNNs for extremely large-scale problems without further architectural innovations
