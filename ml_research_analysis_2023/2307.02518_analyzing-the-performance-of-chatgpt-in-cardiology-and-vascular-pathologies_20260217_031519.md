# Analyzing the Performance of ChatGPT in Cardiology and Vascular Pathologies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2307.02518
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02518
- Reference count: 5
- ChatGPT achieved 92.10% accuracy on medical questions, outperforming two medical students

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates ChatGPT's performance in cardiology and vascular pathology education by comparing its accuracy on 190 multiple-choice questions against two well-ranked medical students. ChatGPT achieved 175 correct answers (92.10% accuracy), outperforming the students who scored 163 (85.78%) and 159 (82.63%) respectively. The analysis spanned seven lessons including abdominal aortic aneurysm, antihypertensive medications, ECG interpretation, and other cardiovascular topics. ChatGPT demonstrated strong performance across most topics but faced challenges with questions involving numerical values and unit conversions. The results indicate ChatGPT's potential as a valuable tool in medical education, though further refinement is needed for specific medical domains.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated ChatGPT's performance on 190 multiple-choice questions from the Siamois-QCM platform, covering seven cardiology and vascular pathology lessons. Questions were directly submitted to ChatGPT-3.5 or ChatGPT-4, with responses compared against two well-ranked medical students. Accuracy was measured as the percentage of correct answers, with ChatGPT achieving 92.10% compared to student scores of 85.78% and 82.63%.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT scored 175/190 correct answers (92.10% accuracy)
- Outperformed two medical students who scored 163 (85.78%) and 159 (82.63%)
- Strong performance across most topics except numerical values and unit conversions
- Tested on seven lessons including ECG interpretation, antihypertensive medications, and vascular pathologies

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would ChatGPT perform on medical questions that require complex numerical calculations and unit conversions beyond the cardiovascular domain?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that ChatGPT faced challenges with questions involving numerical values and unit conversions, as seen in Figure 5 where it incorrectly converted mm/s to mm/min.
- Why unresolved: The study only tested ChatGPT on cardiology and vascular pathology questions, which may have limited numerical complexity compared to other medical domains like pharmacology or radiology.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative testing of ChatGPT's accuracy on medical questions with complex numerical calculations across multiple medical specialties, with specific focus on unit conversion tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would providing ChatGPT with additional medical training data specifically from medical education platforms improve its performance on residency exam questions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests "further refinement and improvement in its performance for specific medical domains" and plans to expand evaluation to more materials.
- Why unresolved: The current ChatGPT model was trained on general text data, and its performance on specialized medical questions, while impressive, could potentially be enhanced with domain-specific fine-tuning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing ChatGPT's performance on the same medical question sets before and after fine-tuning on medical education datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does ChatGPT's performance compare to other AI models or educational tools specifically designed for medical education?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study only compares ChatGPT to two medical students, without benchmarking against other AI tools or educational technologies.
- Why unresolved: The paper establishes ChatGPT's effectiveness but doesn't provide context about how it compares to existing or emerging medical education technologies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparisons of ChatGPT with other AI medical education tools on the same question sets, measuring accuracy, response time, and explanation quality.

## Limitations
- Results limited to 190 questions from one medical education platform covering specific curriculum
- Comparison only against two medical students, limiting statistical power
- Evaluation focused on multiple-choice format without testing other assessment methods

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** ChatGPT's superior performance on multiple-choice questions compared to medical students (175/190 correct vs. 163 and 159) is a direct empirical observation from the study data. The accuracy difference is substantial and consistent across multiple cardiology and vascular pathology topics.

**Medium Confidence:** The claim that ChatGPT faces challenges with numerical values and unit conversions is supported by the study's observations, but the specific frequency and severity of these errors are not quantified. Without knowing which questions were missed due to numerical issues versus conceptual misunderstandings, the exact scope of this limitation remains uncertain.

**Low Confidence:** The broader assertion that ChatGPT has "potential as a valuable tool in medical education" extends beyond the study's specific findings. While the results are promising, the study did not evaluate practical educational outcomes, student learning gains, or long-term knowledge retention when using ChatGPT as a learning aid.

## Next Checks

1. **Quantitative Error Analysis:** Conduct a detailed breakdown of ChatGPT's incorrect answers to determine what percentage resulted from numerical errors versus conceptual misunderstandings, and test whether these errors persist across different ChatGPT model versions or with enhanced prompting strategies.

2. **Cross-Curriculum Validation:** Replicate the evaluation using multiple medical education platforms and curricula from different institutions to assess whether ChatGPT's performance remains consistent across varied medical education contexts and question styles.

3. **Educational Impact Study:** Design a randomized controlled trial comparing medical student learning outcomes when using ChatGPT as a study tool versus traditional methods, measuring not just test performance but also knowledge retention, clinical reasoning skills, and student confidence in cardiology topics.
