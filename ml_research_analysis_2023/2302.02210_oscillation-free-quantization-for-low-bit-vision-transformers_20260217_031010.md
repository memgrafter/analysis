# Oscillation-free Quantization for Low-bit Vision Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02210
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02210
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: 2-bit DeiT-T/S achieve 9.88% and 7.72% improvements over state-of-the-art on ImageNet

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses weight oscillation during quantization-aware training (QAT) of vision transformers, a critical problem that leads to unstable training and sub-optimal models. The authors identify that learnable scaling factors and quantized query-key interdependence in self-attention layers exacerbate oscillation. They propose three complementary techniques: Statistical Weight Quantization (StatsQ) to replace learnable scaling factors with statistically computed ones, Confidence-Guided Annealing (CGA) to freeze and fine-tune oscillating weights, and Query-Key Reparameterization (QKR) to decouple the negative mutual influence between quantized query and key weights. Experimental results demonstrate significant accuracy improvements, with 2-bit DeiT-T/S achieving 9.88% and 7.72% improvements over state-of-the-art on ImageNet.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three techniques to eliminate weight oscillation in low-bit vision transformer quantization. First, StatsQ replaces learnable scaling factors with statistically computed ones based on maximum information entropy theory, preventing disproportionate influence from outlier weights. Second, CGA freezes weights outside a boundary range and fine-tunes oscillating weights to eliminate oscillation through confidence-guided annealing. Third, QKR decouples the negative mutual influence between quantized query and key weights in self-attention layers by reordering their multiplication. These methods are evaluated on DeiT-Tiny and DeiT-Small architectures using ImageNet-1K, with models trained for 300 epochs using knowledge distillation followed by 25 epochs of CGA fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- 2-bit DeiT-Tiny achieves 9.88% improvement over state-of-the-art on ImageNet
- 2-bit DeiT-Small achieves 7.72% improvement over state-of-the-art on ImageNet
- Oscillation eliminated in both training and inference phases for quantized models
- CGA effectively stabilizes training with robust performance across different boundary range selections

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Learnable scaling factors in QAT cause weight oscillation by amplifying noise gradients, leading to unstable training.
- Mechanism: Weights near quantization thresholds experience frequent gradient direction flips. These weights influence the learnable scaling factor, which determines quantization thresholds. The scaling factor's oscillation then exacerbates weight oscillation, creating a vicious cycle.
- Core assumption: The gradient of the learnable scaling factor is sensitive to outlier weights, causing it to oscillate and destabilize nearby weights.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We discover that the learnable scaling factor, a widely-used de facto setting in quantization aggravates weight oscillation."
  - [section 4.1] "The outlier weight contributes more to the gradient of the learnable scaling factor and moves the scaling factor towards a more optimal value for this outlier. As a result, this leads to the oscillation of the other two weights..."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; the corpus does not contain explicit discussion of learnable scaling factor oscillation.
- Break condition: If the learnable scaling factor becomes static or is replaced with a statistically computed alternative.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Quantized query-key interdependence in self-attention layers causes co-oscillation, leading to gradient misestimation.
- Mechanism: The quantized query weights affect the gradient estimation for key weights and vice versa. This mutual influence amplifies oscillations in both, destabilizing the attention mechanism.
- Core assumption: The quantization of query and key weights creates a feedback loop where oscillation in one propagates to the other.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we also found that the interdependence between quantized weights in query and key of a self-attention layer makes ViT vulnerable to oscillation."
  - [section 4.2.4] "WK optimization would be affected by the inaccurate estimation from oscillation inFq(WQ), and the resulting fluctuation in Fq(WK) will, in turn, aggravate Fq(WQ) oscillation."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; the corpus does not discuss query-key interdependence in quantization.
- Break condition: If query and key weights are decoupled or reparameterized to eliminate mutual influence.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Statistical weight quantization (StatsQ) stabilizes training by using a fixed scaling factor computed from weight statistics, reducing oscillation.
- Mechanism: StatsQ replaces the learnable scaling factor with a statistically computed one based on maximum information entropy theory. This fixed scaling factor equally counts the contribution from each weight, preventing disproportionate influence from outliers.
- Core assumption: A fixed scaling factor based on weight statistics will be more stable than a learnable one, reducing oscillation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "statistical weight quantization (StatsQ) to improve quantization robustness compared to the prevalent learnable-scale-based method"
  - [section 5.1] "αs is calculated based on the maximum information entropy theory that when the quantized weights are evenly distributed in quantized levels, the highest entropy is preserved."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; the corpus does not mention statistical weight quantization.
- Break condition: If the statistical scaling factor becomes unstable or if the weight distribution changes significantly during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Vision Transformer (ViT) with quantized weights -> Learnable scaling factors for quantization -> Self-attention mechanism with quantized query and key weights -> Proposed techniques (StatsQ, CGA, QKR)

- Critical path:
  Quantize ViT weights using StatsQ -> Apply CGA to stabilize training and eliminate oscillation -> Use QKR to decouple query-key interdependence -> Evaluate accuracy improvement on ImageNet

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed vs. learnable scaling factors: Fixed factors (StatsQ) are more stable but may not adapt to changing weight distributions.
  - Boundary range selection in CGA: Larger ranges may require more iterations but can be more effective in eliminating oscillation.
  - Query-key reparameterization: Reduces the number of quantization operations but may increase computational complexity.

- Failure signatures:
  - Persistent oscillation in learnable scaling factors
  - Accuracy degradation due to co-oscillation in query and key weights
  - Sub-optimal local minima due to unstable training

## Limitations

- The core mechanisms rely heavily on empirical observations without rigorous mathematical proofs of why the proposed techniques definitively solve oscillation.
- The paper demonstrates correlation between proposed methods and reduced oscillation, but does not establish causation through ablation studies that isolate each mechanism's contribution.
- The statistical foundations for StatsQ (maximum information entropy theory) are mentioned but not rigorously derived in the context of quantization.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The empirical demonstration that oscillation exists and negatively impacts accuracy is well-supported through visualization and quantitative metrics.
- Medium confidence: The StatsQ method's effectiveness is reasonably demonstrated, though the theoretical justification could be stronger.
- Medium confidence: CGA's mechanism is plausible but the boundary range selection (BRx) appears heuristic rather than theoretically grounded.
- Medium confidence: QKR's effectiveness is shown empirically, but the theoretical basis for why this specific reparameterization resolves the oscillation issue is not fully explained.
