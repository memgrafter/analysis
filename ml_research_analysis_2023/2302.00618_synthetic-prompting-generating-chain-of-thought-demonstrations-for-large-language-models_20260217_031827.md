# Synthetic Prompting: Generating Chain-of-Thought Demonstrations for Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00618
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00618
- Reference count: 35
- Up to 15.6% absolute gains over state-of-the-art prompting methods

## Executive Summary
Synthetic Prompting is a method that augments a small set of handcrafted demonstrations with self-generated examples to improve large language model reasoning performance. The method alternates between backward and forward processes: the backward process generates a question conditioned on a topic word, target complexity, and a reasoning chain, ensuring the question is answerable and well-defined; the forward process produces a more detailed reasoning chain for the synthesized question, improving the example quality. During inference, demonstrations are selected based on in-cluster complexity to ensure diversity and informativeness. Evaluated on numerical, symbolic, and algorithmic reasoning tasks, Synthetic Prompting achieved up to 15.6% absolute gains over state-of-the-art prompting methods like PAL prompting. The approach demonstrates that self-synthesized demonstrations can effectively elicit better reasoning in language models compared to using seed demonstrations alone.

The approach addresses the labor-intensive nature of crafting high-quality demonstrations by automating their generation while maintaining quality through the iterative backward-forward process.

## Key Results
- Achieved up to 15.6% absolute gains over state-of-the-art prompting methods like PAL prompting
- Improved reasoning performance on numerical, symbolic, and algorithmic reasoning tasks
- Demonstrated that self-synthesized demonstrations can effectively elicit better reasoning than seed demonstrations alone

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Synthetic Prompting works by addressing the labor-intensive nature of crafting high-quality demonstrations for chain-of-thought prompting. The backward process ensures questions are well-defined and answerable by generating them conditioned on topic words, complexity targets, and reasoning chains. The forward process then elaborates on these questions with more detailed reasoning chains, improving the quality of generated examples. By selecting demonstrations based on in-cluster complexity during inference, the method ensures diversity and informativeness in the prompts provided to the model. This approach effectively reduces the burden of manual demonstration creation while maintaining or improving reasoning performance.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Chain-of-Thought Prompting**: Why needed: Provides step-by-step reasoning demonstrations to improve model performance on complex tasks. Quick check: Does the model show improved reasoning on multi-step problems?
2. **Demonstration Selection**: Why needed: Ensures diversity and informativeness in prompts. Quick check: Are selected demonstrations from different complexity clusters?
3. **Backward Generation**: Why needed: Creates answerable questions from reasoning chains. Quick check: Can the generated questions be solved by the model?
4. **Forward Elaboration**: Why needed: Improves quality of reasoning chains. Quick check: Do elaborated chains show more detailed reasoning steps?
5. **Complexity-Based Clustering**: Why needed: Organizes demonstrations by difficulty level. Quick check: Does clustering separate easy and hard examples?
6. **Topic Word Conditioning**: Why needed: Guides question generation toward specific domains. Quick check: Are generated questions relevant to specified topics?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Seed Demonstrations -> Backward Generator -> Generated Questions -> Forward Generator -> Elaborated Demonstrations -> Complexity-Based Clustering -> Selected Demonstrations

**Critical Path**: Seed Demonstrations → Backward Generator → Generated Questions → Forward Generator → Selected Demonstrations → Model Inference

**Design Tradeoffs**: Manual demonstration creation vs. automated generation quality; complexity specification vs. generality; backward generation constraints vs. question diversity

**Failure Signatures**: Poor seed demonstrations leading to low-quality generation; incorrect complexity specification causing unbalanced clusters; backward generation producing unanswerable questions

**First Experiments**:
1. Generate questions using backward process with seed demonstrations on simple arithmetic problems
2. Apply forward elaboration to generated questions and evaluate reasoning chain quality
3. Test complexity-based demonstration selection on a small task cluster

## Limitations
- The backward and forward generation processes may not transfer effectively to domains requiring extensive real-world knowledge or open-ended reasoning
- The method's reliance on topic words and complexity specifications introduces a human-in-the-loop requirement that could limit scalability
- Quality of self-generated examples depends heavily on the seed demonstrations, creating a potential bootstrap problem

## Confidence
- 15.6% absolute gain claim: Medium
- Cross-domain generalization: Medium
- Bootstrap problem with seed demonstrations: Medium
