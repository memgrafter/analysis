# Argoverse 2: Next Generation Datasets for Self-Driving Perception and Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.00493
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00493
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces three datasets (Sensor, Lidar, Motion Forecasting) with 1,000 annotated scenarios, 20,000 unlabeled sequences, and 250,000 forecasting scenarios across 6 cities

## Executive Summary
Argoverse 2 introduces three complementary datasets designed to advance self-driving research across perception, point cloud forecasting, and motion forecasting tasks. The datasets span six diverse cities (Austin, Detroit, Miami, Palo Alto, Pittsburgh, Washington D.C.) and emphasize challenging scenarios with underrepresented objects, noteworthy weather conditions, and interesting behaviors. With the largest self-driving taxonomy to date (30 categories, 26 with ≥6,000 cuboids), the datasets provide comprehensive coverage for training and evaluating next-generation autonomous driving systems.

## Method Summary
Argoverse 2 comprises three distinct datasets: the Sensor Dataset with 1,000 15-second scenarios containing multimodal data (7 ring cameras, 2 stereo cameras, 2 lidars) with 3D cuboid annotations for 26 object categories; the Lidar Dataset with 20,000 30-second unlabeled sequences for self-supervised learning; and the Motion Forecasting Dataset with 250,000 11-second scenarios featuring track histories and future motion predictions for 5 dynamic and 5 static object types. The datasets were manually curated to focus on challenging scenarios including crowded scenes, underrepresented objects, noteworthy weather, and interesting behaviors, while maintaining geographic and seasonal diversity across six North American cities.

## Key Results
- Geographic diversity across 6 cities captures varying traffic behaviors and environmental conditions (e.g., Miami has 15× motorcycle frequency vs Pittsburgh)
- Sensor Dataset contains largest self-driving taxonomy with 30 categories and stereo imagery capability
- Baseline results demonstrate dataset utility for 3D object detection, point cloud forecasting, and motion forecasting tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The dataset's geographic diversity across 6 cities ensures that learned models are not overfitted to a single urban environment. By spanning Austin, Detroit, Miami, Palo Alto, Pittsburgh, and Washington D.C., the dataset captures varying traffic behaviors, road geometries, and environmental conditions (e.g., Miami has 15× the frequency of motorcycles/mopeds compared to Pittsburgh).

### Mechanism 2
The inclusion of stereo imagery in the Sensor Dataset enables depth estimation tasks that are not possible with single-view datasets. Stereo imagery provides binocular disparity information, which can be used to compute depth maps and improve 3D object detection accuracy by providing additional geometric constraints.

### Mechanism 3
The dataset's focus on "interesting scenarios" improves model robustness by training on challenging and diverse situations. By mining for crowded scenes, under-represented objects, noteworthy weather, and interesting behaviors, the dataset forces models to learn from rare but critical situations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data collection across 6 cities → Manual scenario selection for "interesting" cases → Multimodal sensor fusion (cameras + lidars) → Annotation pipeline → Dataset creation → Model training and evaluation
- **Critical path**: Data collection → Preprocessing → Annotation → Dataset creation → Model training and evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Geographic diversity vs. data collection cost and complexity; focus on "interesting scenarios" vs. representativeness of normal driving conditions; inclusion of stereo imagery vs. data storage and processing requirements
- **Failure signatures**: Poor model performance on rare object categories or unusual driving scenarios; overfitting to specific geographic regions or weather conditions; inability to generalize to new sensor configurations or data formats
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train a 3D object detection model on the Sensor Dataset and evaluate its performance on the validation split
  2. Use the Lidar Dataset for self-supervised learning and compare the learned representations to those obtained from supervised learning
  3. Train a motion forecasting model on the Motion Forecasting Dataset and evaluate its performance on the test split

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the Argoverse 2 Sensor Dataset's 3D object detection performance compare to the Waymo Open Dataset? The paper states that Argoverse 2 has a larger taxonomy than nuScenes and compares favorably to other datasets, but does not provide a direct comparison to Waymo Open Dataset.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of using the Argoverse 2 Lidar Dataset for self-supervised learning compared to using the ONCE dataset? The paper mentions that Argoverse 2 Lidar Dataset is larger than ONCE and has HD maps, but does not provide a direct comparison of self-supervised learning performance.

### Open Question 3
How does the Argoverse 2 Motion Forecasting Dataset's performance compare to the nuScenes and Waymo Open Motion Datasets for multi-agent forecasting? The paper states that Argoverse 2 has a richer taxonomy and covers a larger mapped area than existing datasets, but does not provide a direct comparison to nuScenes and Waymo Open Motion Datasets.

## Limitations
- Geographic diversity claims lack empirical evidence showing improved generalization to unseen environments
- Stereo imagery benefits are asserted but not validated through comparative experiments
- Focus on "interesting scenarios" may create unrepresentative sampling of normal driving conditions

## Confidence
- Geographic diversity claims: Medium confidence - supported by stated diversity goals but lacking empirical generalization evidence
- Stereo imagery benefits: Low confidence - mechanism described but no validation experiments presented
- Interesting scenarios focus: Medium confidence - clear design rationale but potential representativeness concerns
