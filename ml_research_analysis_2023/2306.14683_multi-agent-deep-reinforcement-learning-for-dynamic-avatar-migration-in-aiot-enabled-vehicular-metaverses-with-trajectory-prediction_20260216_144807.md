# Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning for Dynamic Avatar Migration in AIoT-enabled Vehicular Metaverses with Trajectory Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.14683
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14683
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Reduces avatar task latency by ~25% without prediction and ~30% with prediction using Hybrid-MAPPO with LSTM-based trajectory prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of dynamic avatar migration in AIoT-enabled Vehicular Metaverses (AeVeM), where resource-intensive avatar tasks must be efficiently offloaded from vehicles with limited on-board resources to Road Side Units (RSUs) or cloud servers. The authors propose a novel framework combining LSTM-based trajectory prediction with a Hybrid-MAPPO multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithm that handles both discrete RSU selection and continuous task portion decisions. The approach predicts future vehicle trajectories and RSU workloads to inform pre-migration decisions, achieving significant latency reductions compared to baseline methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses a two-stage approach: first, an LSTM network predicts future vehicle trajectories and corresponding RSU workloads based on historical data; second, a Hybrid-MAPPO algorithm with mixed discrete and continuous action spaces makes pre-migration decisions. Each vehicle agent observes partial information about current RSU workloads and predicted future workloads, then selects both which RSU to migrate to (discrete action) and what portion of the task to migrate (continuous action). The MAPPO algorithm uses centralized training with decentralized execution, allowing agents to share a global value network during training while maintaining independent policies at runtime.

## Key Results
- LSTM trajectory prediction achieves MSE of 6.3×10^-5 on validation dataset
- Hybrid-MAPPO reduces average avatar service latency by ~25% compared to baseline without prediction
- Hybrid-MAPPO reduces latency by ~30% compared to baseline with prediction
- Centralized training with decentralized execution improves agent collaboration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LSTM-based trajectory prediction improves pre-migration decisions by forecasting RSU workloads.
- Mechanism: Historical vehicle trajectories are input into an LSTM network to predict future vehicle positions. These predicted positions are mapped to RSU coverage regions to estimate future traffic volumes, which are converted to workload predictions for each RSU.
- Core assumption: Future vehicle locations can be reliably predicted from historical trajectories, and traffic volume correlates directly with RSU workload.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Specifically, we propose a model to predict the future trajectories of intelligent vehicles based on their historical data, indicating the future workloads of RSUs."
  - [section] "Based on the identified future trajectories of intelligent vehicles, we propose a method to predict the future workload of RSUs."
  - [corpus] Weak - No direct corpus evidence supporting LSTM prediction accuracy in this specific vehicular Metaverse context.
- Break condition: Prediction error exceeds acceptable thresholds, causing inaccurate workload estimates and poor pre-migration decisions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Hybrid action spaces (discrete RSU selection + continuous task portion) enable more granular and adaptive pre-migration decisions.
- Mechanism: Agents use discrete actions to select target RSUs and continuous actions to determine the proportion of tasks to pre-migrate, allowing fine-grained optimization based on current and predicted RSU resources.
- Core assumption: The optimal pre-migration strategy requires both discrete choices (which RSU) and continuous adjustments (how much to migrate).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose a MAPPO-based scheme with hybrid action spaces, i.e., Hybrid-MAPPO, for the non-uniform RSU deployment and the resource-intensive avatar task."
  - [section] "In contrast to MADRL with only one type of action space, MADRL with mixed action spaces can equip the agent with the appropriate type of decision space according to the properties of the control object, enabling more accurate decisions."
  - [corpus] Weak - No corpus evidence specifically validating hybrid action space effectiveness for this problem.
- Break condition: Action space becomes too complex for agents to learn effectively, leading to poor convergence or suboptimal decisions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-agent PPO with centralized training and decentralized execution improves collaboration and performance.
- Mechanism: Each vehicle agent has independent policies but shares a common value network during training, allowing coordination while maintaining decentralized execution at runtime.
- Core assumption: Centralized training with decentralized execution provides better collaboration than fully independent learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Unlike the decentralized training and decentralized execution framework used by the independent PPO algorithm, the MAPPO algorithm employs centralized training and decentralized execution."
  - [section] "This scheme allows all agents to share the global valuation. This improves collaboration between agents and enables better performance in a multi-agent collaborative environment."
  - [corpus] Weak - No corpus evidence specifically validating MAPPO vs independent PPO for this problem.
- Break condition: Shared value network becomes a bottleneck or agents' policies diverge too much during training.

  - Why needed here: The pre-migration problem requires both discrete decisions (which RSU) and continuous adjustments (task portion), which standard RL algorithms cannot handle simultaneously.

  - Why needed here: Vehicle trajectories form sequential data where future positions depend on past movements, requiring models that can capture temporal dependencies.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Environment → Vehicle agents (observation, action, policy networks) → LSTM predictor → RSU resource manager → Reward calculator → Replay buffer → Hybrid-MAPPO trainer
- Critical path: Vehicle observation → LSTM prediction → Hybrid-MAPPO decision → Action execution → Environment update → Reward calculation → Training update
- Design tradeoffs: Prediction accuracy vs. computational overhead; action space granularity vs. learning complexity; centralized training benefits vs. communication overhead
- Failure signatures: High prediction MSE (>10^-4); slow convergence (>200 episodes); reward plateaus below -30; action distributions become uniform

## Limitations
- LSTM trajectory prediction may not generalize well to rare or unprecedented traffic scenarios
- Performance improvements depend heavily on precise hyperparameter tuning that isn't fully specified
- Evaluation focuses primarily on latency reduction without addressing computational overhead or energy consumption

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core premise that dynamic avatar migration requires intelligent pre-migration decisions in mobile vehicular environments is well-established
- **Medium Confidence**: The trajectory prediction mechanism and its integration with workload estimation is plausible but lacks sufficient empirical validation
- **Low Confidence**: The specific performance improvements (~25-30% latency reduction) are difficult to verify without access to the exact simulation parameters and dataset
