# Multimodal Propaganda Processing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08709
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08709
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: Proposes task of multimodal propaganda processing with 6 annotation tasks and argument tree representation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces multimodal propaganda processing as a new task that aims to automatically analyze propaganda content by extracting information from both text and images, identifying persuasion devices and tactics, and generating the conveyed messages. The authors propose a comprehensive annotation scheme consisting of six tasks that together form an argument tree representation of propagandistic articles. They discuss technical challenges including multimodality integration, background knowledge requirements, and domain transferability, while outlining potential applications in propaganda detection and ethical considerations.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves creating a corpus of multimodal propagandistic articles and annotating them according to a six-task framework: identifying propaganda devices and tactics (Tasks 1-2), detecting domain-independent and domain-specific messages (Tasks 3-4), generating main messages (Task 5), and providing background knowledge and image captions (Tasks 6). The annotation scheme uses argument trees to represent the argumentative structure of documents. Models would be trained on these annotations using multimodal architectures combining visual encoders (ResNet, ViLBERT) with text encoders (SpanBERT, RoBERTa), potentially incorporating background knowledge through pre-training.

## Key Results
- Proposes novel task of multimodal propaganda processing combining text and image analysis
- Introduces 6-task annotation scheme forming argument tree representation
- Outlines technical challenges and potential solutions for propaganda processing

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multimodal propaganda processing works because combining text and image modalities provides complementary information for understanding persuasion tactics.
- Mechanism: Visual cues (e.g., exaggerated image scaling) and textual rhetoric (e.g., "SOVIET WAY - USA WAY") together reveal binary reduction tactics that neither modality alone could detect.
- Core assumption: Propaganda devices in text and images are often correlated and mutually reinforcing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "propaganda content ... is often multimodal, comprising both text and images"
  - [section] "The large font size associated with the title and the subtitle as well as the oversimplifying language in them signal the use of the Binary Reduction tactic"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors focus on meme detection and single-modal propaganda detection; no direct evidence of multimodal synergy.
- Break condition: If text and image modalities contain contradictory or uncorrelated information, joint processing may introduce noise rather than clarity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Detecting propaganda devices works because models can learn from annotated examples of known devices and their textual/visual patterns.
- Mechanism: Supervised learning on argument tree annotations (Tasks 1-6) enables models to map input patterns to propaganda device labels and rationales.
- Core assumption: Sufficient labeled examples exist to capture the diversity of propaganda devices and tactics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We propose to annotate each propagandistic article in the form of an argument tree"
  - [section] "The annotations we obtain via these tasks will provide the data needed to train models for processing propaganda content"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors show propaganda detection but not the detailed device-level annotation scheme described.
- Break condition: If propaganda devices are too subtle or context-dependent, supervised learning may fail to generalize beyond training examples.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Background knowledge improves propaganda understanding because it reveals hidden messages and cultural context.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa) acquire general knowledge that can be fine-tuned with propaganda-specific annotations.
- Core assumption: General knowledge from large-scale pre-training transfers effectively to domain-specific propaganda interpretation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "While we have access to background knowledge through the manual annotations obtained as part of Task 5, it is conceivable that the amount of background knowledge we need will far exceed what these annotations can provide"
  - [section] "A potential solution to this background knowledge acquisition bottleneck is pre-training"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence for background knowledge requirements; neighbors focus on detection rather than knowledge integration.
- Break condition: If propaganda relies heavily on niche or rapidly changing background knowledge, static pre-training may become obsolete.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Propaganda devices and tactics (Band Wagon, Card Stacking, Visual Scaling, etc.)
  - Why needed here: These are the core labels the system must predict; understanding their definitions is prerequisite to building annotation schemes.
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes "Card Stacking" from "Glittering Generalities" in propaganda?

- Concept: Multimodal representation learning (visual encoders + text encoders)
  - Why needed here: The system must fuse information from both modalities; understanding encoder architectures is critical for design choices.
  - Quick check question: How does ViLBERT differ from using separate ResNet and BERT encoders followed by concatenation?

- Concept: Argument mining and tree structures
  - Why needed here: The annotation scheme uses argument trees to represent relationships between claims and evidence; understanding this structure is essential for data modeling.
  - Quick check question: In an argument tree, what is the relationship between a premise and its parent node?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Visual encoder (ResNet, ViLBERT) → Image features
  - Text encoder (SpanBERT, RoBERTa) → Text features
  - Background knowledge encoder → Knowledge features
  - Fusion layer → Combined representation
  - Task-specific heads → Propaganda device, tactic, message detection, captioning

- Critical path:
  1. Input image and text → encode separately
  2. Generate caption (Task 6) if using unimodal pipeline
  3. Fuse encoded modalities + background knowledge
  4. Predict propaganda devices and tactics (Task 1)
  5. Predict domain-independent/domain-specific messages (Tasks 2-3)
  6. Generate main message (Task 4)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Multimodal joint model: More parameters, better interaction capture, higher risk of overfitting
  - Unimodal pipeline: Simpler, modular, but prone to error propagation from image captioning step
  - Pre-training: Requires large unlabeled data; may capture irrelevant patterns

- Failure signatures:
  - Low precision on device detection: Possible domain shift or insufficient training examples
  - Caption errors cascade to downstream tasks: Unimodal pipeline too fragile
  - Model overfits to En Guardia: Limited domain transferability

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train image-only and text-only baselines for device detection; compare to multimodal fusion
  2. Evaluate caption quality (BLEU, CIDEr) and its impact on downstream task accuracy
  3. Test domain adaptation by fine-tuning on small labeled data from a new propaganda domain

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can multimodal propaganda processing models effectively combine information from text and images while accounting for their different levels of interpretability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states this as an open question in the "Multimodality" section, noting that "How to combine the information derived from the two modalities to properly understand propaganda is an open question."
- Why unresolved: This question remains unresolved because existing models struggle to bridge the gap between the explicit nature of text and the often more implicit, context-dependent nature of images in propaganda. Additionally, the paper highlights the challenge of understanding the interplay between text and images, where images may be "eye-catching and create the biggest psychological impact" while text plays a "supporting role."
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful models that demonstrate improved performance in propaganda processing tasks when incorporating multimodal information compared to unimodal approaches. Empirical studies comparing different fusion techniques and their effectiveness in capturing the nuanced relationships between text and images in propaganda.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can background knowledge be effectively integrated into multimodal propaganda processing models to improve understanding and interpretation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies the need for background knowledge in processing propaganda content and proposes annotating it as part of the task. However, it also acknowledges the challenge of acquiring sufficient background knowledge beyond manual annotations.
- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests pre-training language models on relevant corpora, it remains unclear how to effectively acquire and integrate diverse forms of background knowledge, including historical, cultural, and domain-specific knowledge, into the model's reasoning process.
- What evidence would resolve it: Models that demonstrate improved performance in propaganda processing tasks when incorporating various forms of background knowledge, such as factual knowledge bases, historical context, and cultural understanding. Studies comparing different approaches to background knowledge integration and their impact on model interpretability and accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can multimodal propaganda processing models be adapted to new domains with limited labeled data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the challenge of domain transferability, stating that "Since the models described thus far are trained on domain-specific annotations, they are necessarily domain-specific."
- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests exploring domain adaptation techniques, it does not provide specific solutions for adapting models to new domains with limited labeled data, especially when the propaganda techniques and messaging may differ significantly across domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Models that demonstrate successful transfer learning or domain adaptation capabilities, achieving comparable performance in new domains with limited labeled data. Studies comparing different domain adaptation techniques and their effectiveness in handling domain shifts in propaganda content.

## Limitations
- No experimental results provided to validate the proposed annotation scheme or model architectures
- Relies on theoretical framework without empirical demonstration of effectiveness
- Does not address potential biases in annotation process or cultural context differences

## Confidence
- High: The identification of multimodal propaganda as an important research problem and the general architecture sketch (visual + text + knowledge encoders)
- Medium: The proposed 6-task annotation scheme and its potential utility for model training
- Low: The effectiveness of argument trees for propaganda representation and the transferrability of background knowledge from general pre-training
