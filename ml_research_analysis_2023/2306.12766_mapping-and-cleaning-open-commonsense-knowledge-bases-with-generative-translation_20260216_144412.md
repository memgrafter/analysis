# Mapping and Cleaning Open Commonsense Knowledge Bases with Generative Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12766
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12766
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Generative translation model outperforms baselines on mapping open KBs to ConceptNet, achieving higher precision, recall, and typicality.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a generative translation approach to map open commonsense knowledge bases to fixed-schema closed knowledge bases like ConceptNet. The method automatically aligns open triples to closed triples, then finetunes GPT-2 to translate between them. This addresses limitations of manual mapping, rule mining, and classification-based approaches while avoiding the noise of purely generative KB construction. Experiments demonstrate superior performance on both automated and manual evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The approach involves three main stages: (1) automatic alignment of open triples to closed triples using rule-based and LM-based methods, (2) finetuning a GPT-2 model on the aligned data to learn translation patterns, and (3) generating and ranking closed triples for each open triple using beam search and a scoring function that combines frequency and rank. This creates a mapped KB that preserves recall while improving precision and ranking quality compared to previous methods.

## Key Results
- Outperforms rule-based and classification baselines on precision, recall, and typicality metrics
- Achieves 10-15% improvement in typicality and ranking quality over COMET baseline
- Manual evaluation shows 60% of generated triples have higher ranking scores than original open triples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Generative translation improves precision while preserving recall by leveraging LM's ability to adapt subjects/objects and correct errors.
- Mechanism: Finetuning GPT-2 on aligned open/closed triples enables generation of multiple equivalent closed triples per open triple, increasing chances of overlap with target KB.
- Core assumption: LM retains commonsense knowledge from pretraining and can flexibly map predicates while adjusting arguments.
- Evidence anchors:

Moreover, it produces higher mapping accuracy than the former while avoiding the association-based noise of the latter."
  - [section]: "This can be seen as a translation problem... we used the GPT-2 model... The generative nature of the LM allows it to adapt to the abovementioned problems while keeping a high faithfulness w.r.t. the source KB."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Automatic alignment methods create sufficient training data without manual effort, enabling scalable KB mapping.
- Mechanism: Rule-based and LM-based alignment generate weak labels by matching open triples to closed triples using syntactic rules or embedding similarity.
- Core assumption: Even noisy alignments provide enough signal for LM to learn general mapping patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We decided to adopt two automatic approaches to generate a broader dataset, even if they contain some noise... LM-Based Alignment... We used Scikit-learn K-nearest neighbor algorithm to find the nearest neighbor in a closed KB for each triple in an open KB."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multiple generations per open triple and ranking by frequency/rank improves final KB quality.
- Mechanism: Beam search generates top K closed triples per open triple, then scores by occurrence count and inverse rank to surface most reliable mappings.
- Core assumption: Correct mappings will appear frequently across different open triples and rank highly in generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We used a beam search... to obtain the top 𝐾 results... Using the score in the original KB, the generation score, and the rank of the generated alignment, we create a final score for each closed triple."
  - [section]: "It is essential to notice that the score of an open triple is provided by the open KB. Therefore, if the open KB is not good at scoring triples, we will inherit negative signals that we hope to compensate with the ranks."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Open KB → Alignment module (Rule-based/LM-based) → Finetuning module (GPT-2) → Generation module (Beam search) → Ranking module (Frequency + Rank) → Closed KB

- Critical path:
  Open KB → Alignment → Finetuning → Generation → Ranking → Closed KB

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Alignment method: Rule-based (high precision, low recall) vs LM-based (higher recall, more noise)
  - Number of generations K: More generations increase recall but computational cost
  - Training data size: Larger datasets improve generalization but increase training time
  - Ranking function: Balance between frequency and rank scores affects precision

- Failure signatures:
  - Low recall: Alignment not capturing enough correct mappings
  - Low precision: Generated triples diverging from target schema
  - Poor generalization: Model overfitting to training alignments
  - Slow inference: Beam search with high K or inefficient ranking

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test alignment quality by manually annotating a sample of rule-based vs LM-based alignments
  2. Vary K in generation and measure impact on recall/precision tradeoff
  3. Compare GenT@1 vs GenT@10 on automated metrics to assess benefit of multiple generations

## Limitations
- Manual evaluation covers only 100 triples, limiting generalizability of qualitative improvements
- No comparison to recent neural canonicalization methods from 2021-2024
- Does not quantify the "association-based noise" claimed to be avoided compared to COMET

## Confidence
- High: The core mechanism of using generative translation for KB mapping is well-supported by experimental results and ablation studies
- Medium: The claim of superior precision-recall tradeoff is supported but limited by small manual evaluation sample size
- Low: The assertion that this approach avoids COMET's "association-based noise" is not directly tested or quantified
