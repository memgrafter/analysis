# Reliability Scores from Saliency Map Clusters for Improved Image-based Harvest-Readiness Prediction in Cauliflower

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15149
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15149
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Achieved 88.14% overall accuracy and 88.52% average class accuracy, representing 15.72% and 15.44% improvements over baseline classification

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a post-hoc framework for deriving reliability scores for cauliflower harvest-readiness predictions using clustering of saliency maps. The approach combines a ResNet18 classifier with spectral clustering of saliency maps (Grad-CAM, OSM, LIME) to identify clusters with high false prediction rates. For unseen data, reliability scores are assigned based on cluster membership, enabling improved farmer decision-making and enhanced prediction accuracy through adjustment of classification results. Using the GrowliFlower dataset, the framework achieved an 88.14% overall accuracy and 88.52% average class accuracy, representing improvements of 15.72% and 15.44% respectively over baseline classification. Grad-CAM proved most effective among the saliency mapping approaches, with cluster 5 containing ~95% false predictions in validation data.

## Method Summary
The framework trains a ResNet18 classifier on augmented cauliflower images from the GrowliFlower dataset, then generates saliency maps using Grad-CAM, OSM, and LIME for validation data. Spectral clustering (q=8 clusters) is applied to the saliency maps to identify groups with similar feature importance patterns. Reliability scores are calculated based on the percentage of false predictions in each cluster. For test data, predictions from clusters exceeding a 75% false prediction threshold are adjusted, improving overall accuracy without requiring model modification or retraining.

## Key Results
- Achieved 88.14% overall accuracy and 88.52% average class accuracy on test set
- Grad-CAM saliency maps produced the most interpretable clusters and best reliability scores
- Cluster 5 contained approximately 95% false predictions in validation data
- Method improved accuracy by 15.72% and 15.44% over baseline classification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Spectral clustering of saliency maps reveals clusters where most predictions are false, enabling targeted reliability scoring.
- Mechanism: The framework computes saliency maps for validation data, applies spectral clustering to identify groups of maps with similar feature importance patterns, then correlates cluster membership with false positive/negative rates to assign reliability scores.
- Core assumption: Saliency maps from false predictions share distinctive clustering patterns that differ from correct predictions.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "By identifying clusters of saliency maps, we derive reliability scores for each classification result"; [section 2.5] "we employ spectral clustering to identify groups of saliency maps computed on the validation data and derive reliability scores"
- Break condition: If saliency maps from false and correct predictions are indistinguishable in feature space, clustering cannot separate them reliably.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Reliability scores from cluster membership improve overall prediction accuracy when used to adjust uncertain classifications.
- Mechanism: For each cluster, the framework calculates the percentage of false predictions (FP+FN). If this percentage exceeds a threshold (75%), predictions in that cluster are flipped, effectively correcting systematic errors.
- Core assumption: Clusters with high false prediction rates consistently contain misclassified samples across validation and test data.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "For unseen data, the reliability can be used to...increase the model prediction accuracy"; [section 2.6] "If rq exceeds a threshold t in cluster q of the validation set, we swap the predicted class for all samples in cluster q"
- Break condition: If threshold tuning is too aggressive or conservative, accuracy gains may not materialize or may degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Grad-CAM saliency maps provide more interpretable and reliable clustering results than OSM or LIME for this cauliflower harvest-readiness task.
- Mechanism: Grad-CAM highlights object-level regions (cauliflower head area) in a compact, human-interpretable manner, leading to clusters that align with prediction correctness, whereas OSM produces scattered noise and LIME fails to generate semantically meaningful superpixels.
- Core assumption: The interpretability of saliency maps correlates with their effectiveness in distinguishing reliable from unreliable predictions.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.3] "Grad-CAM and OSM to be the most suitable approaches" and "Grad-CAM approach results in more interpretable maps"; [section 3.4] "Grad-CAM proves to be the most useful in our scenario"
- Break condition: If the underlying model's decision process doesn't align with human interpretability, even interpretable saliency maps may not yield reliable clusters.

  - Why needed here: Different saliency methods (Grad-CAM, OSM, LIME) highlight different aspects of model decisions; understanding their mechanisms is crucial for selecting the right approach.
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between gradient-based (Grad-CAM) and perturbation-based (OSM, LIME) saliency methods?

  - Why needed here: The framework assigns reliability scores based on cluster false prediction rates and adjusts predictions when scores exceed a threshold, requiring understanding of precision-recall tradeoffs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  ResNet18 classifier → Saliency map generator (Grad-CAM/OSM/LIME) → Spectral clustering → Reliability score assignment → Prediction adjustment

- Data flow: Raw images → augmented training set → model training → validation/test saliency maps → clustering → reliability scores → adjusted predictions

- Critical path:
  1. Train ResNet18 on GrowliFlower, generate Grad-CAM saliency maps for validation set, apply spectral clustering with q=8, visualize cluster prototypes and false prediction distribution
  2. Repeat experiment with OSM and LIME saliency methods, compare clustering results and prototype interpretability
  3. Tune threshold t from 60% to 90% in 5% increments, measure accuracy improvement and identify optimal threshold for prediction adjustment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Saliency method choice: Grad-CAM offers interpretability but may miss subtle features; OSM captures occlusion sensitivity but produces noise; LIME provides model-agnostic explanations but struggles with overlapping plant structures
  - Cluster number (q=8): Balances granularity with generalization; too few clusters may obscure reliability patterns, too many may overfit
  - Threshold (t=75%): Empirically determined; higher thresholds reduce false corrections but may miss improvement opportunities

- Failure signatures:
  - No accuracy improvement after adjustment: Likely due to poor cluster separation or inappropriate threshold
  - Clusters dominated by single class: Suggests saliency maps don't capture prediction-relevant features
  - High variance in reliability scores: May indicate unstable clustering or insufficient validation data

## Limitations
- Framework's effectiveness depends heavily on the assumption that saliency map clusters reliably separate false from correct predictions
- Single dataset validation limits generalizability to other crops or classification tasks
- Method's applicability to more complex multi-class scenarios remains untested

## Confidence
- High confidence in the clustering mechanism's ability to identify problematic predictions within the cauliflower dataset
- Medium confidence in the 15.72% accuracy improvement claim due to single dataset validation
- Medium confidence in Grad-CAM superiority given limited comparison across different saliency methods
- Low confidence in the method's applicability to more complex multi-class scenarios

## Next Checks
1. Conduct k-fold cross-validation on the GrowliFlower dataset to assess stability of cluster reliability scores and accuracy improvements across different data splits
2. Test the framework on a different crop species (e.g., broccoli or cabbage) to evaluate generalizability beyond cauliflower harvest-readiness prediction
3. Compare the clustering-based reliability approach against alternative uncertainty quantification methods like Monte Carlo dropout or ensemble-based prediction variance
