# Artificial Intelligence: 70 Years Down the Road

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02819
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02819
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: Historical and philosophical analysis identifying AI development patterns and proposing human-machine collaboration as the sustainable path forward

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive analysis of AI development over the past 70 years, identifying a universal pattern across computer vision, natural language processing, and machine learning from rule-based to statistical to data-driven methods. The authors argue that AI's fundamental limitation stems from inadequate mathematical tools to model real-world complexity, necessitating empirical approaches and human-machine collaboration. They propose that sustainable AI development should focus on computing power as the central driver, with humans providing knowledge, improving algorithms, and giving feedback, rather than pursuing autonomous machine intelligence.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a systematic historical analysis of AI development across multiple domains, examining the evolution from rule-based to statistical to data-driven methods. It incorporates philosophical analysis using Marxist dialectics and practice theory to understand the unity of AI between natural and social sciences. The methodology involves synthesizing historical patterns, identifying fundamental contradictions in AI development, and proposing a sustainable development path centered on human-machine collaboration and computing power enhancement.

## Key Results
- AI development follows a universal pattern from rule-based to statistical to data-driven methods across different domains
- The fundamental limitation of AI is inadequate mathematical tools for modeling real-world complexity
- Human-machine collaboration is essential for sustainable AI development, with computing power as the central driver

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: AI development follows a predictable pattern of evolution from rule-based to statistical to data-driven methods across different domains
- Mechanism: Each AI field encounters fundamental limitations in mathematical modeling of real-world complexity, forcing a progression through increasingly empirical approaches
- Core assumption: Mathematical tools are inherently inadequate for modeling complex real-world phenomena, necessitating reliance on data-driven approaches
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The development of AI in different fields, including computer vision, natural language processing, and machine learning, follows a pattern from rules to statistics to data-driven methods."
  - [section] "From a technical perspective, the dilemma of AI is fundamentally due to the lack of mathematical ability, and we are still unable to effectively model the complex real world."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on applications rather than developmental patterns
- Break condition: Discovery of mathematical tools capable of modeling real-world complexity without empirical approximation

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Human-machine collaboration is the inevitable path for sustainable AI development
- Mechanism: Machines cannot practice or judge truth of knowledge independently, requiring human feedback and interaction for knowledge validation and system improvement
- Core assumption: Practice is the only criterion for testing truth, and machines cannot practice autonomously
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "machine activity is essentially mathematical reasoning without self-awareness, while practice is a conscious human activity."
  - [section] "Therefore, human participation is an essential part of AI."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - corpus focuses on applications rather than philosophical foundations
- Break condition: Development of machines capable of autonomous practice and truth validation

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Computing power is the central driver of AI advancement, with human knowledge serving to enhance it
- Mechanism: As computing power expands, previously uncomputable human knowledge becomes achievable, creating a spiral of advancement
- Core assumption: There are fundamental contradictions between human knowledge complexity and computational capabilities that can only be resolved through increasing computing power
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We can analyze the fundamental contradictions of AI in two aspects: first, computing power is constant, so we can only use human knowledge to improve the computational performance of intelligent machines; second, human knowledge is too complex and difficult to teach machines through computation, so computing power can continue to expand."
  - [section] "The development of artificial intelligence technology must always be centered on computing power."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus mentions AI applications but not the power-knowledge dynamic
- Break condition: Breakthrough in computational methods that makes current computing power limitations irrelevant

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific mathematical frameworks or tools would be required to properly model real-world complexity in AI systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "the dilemma of AI development, from a technical perspective, is fundamentally due to the lack of mathematical tools" and that "we are still unable to effectively model the complex real world"
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a fundamental limitation but does not propose specific mathematical frameworks that could overcome this challenge
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstration of mathematical frameworks that can effectively model complex real-world phenomena in AI systems, showing superior performance compared to current statistical approaches

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can human feedback mechanisms be systematically incorporated into AI systems to ensure genuine practice-based validation rather than superficial metrics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper argues that "implementing artificial intelligence requires human feedback, rather than simply allowing machines to independently achieve intelligence" and criticizes current systems where "users do not have true freedom of choice and decision-making"
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem with current feedback mechanisms but does not specify how to design effective feedback systems that align with practice theory
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical validation of AI systems with feedback mechanisms that demonstrate genuine human practice-based validation and improved alignment with human values

### Open Question 3
- Question: What specific metrics or indicators would demonstrate that human-machine collaboration is more effective than pursuing autonomous machine intelligence?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes that "human-machine interaction is the inevitable path of AI development" and that AI should be "a technology path centered on computing power" with humans providing knowledge, improving algorithms, and giving feedback
- Why unresolved: The paper advocates for human-machine collaboration but does not specify how to measure its effectiveness compared to autonomous approaches
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the performance, robustness, and adaptability of human-machine collaborative systems versus autonomous AI systems across various domains and tasks

## Limitations

- The paper's claims about AI development patterns lack detailed empirical validation across all AI domains
- The philosophical framework based on Marxist dialectics and practice theory is not empirically tested against alternative approaches
- The assertion that computing power is the central driver requires more quantitative analysis of the power-knowledge relationship

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The identification of common development patterns (rule-based → statistical → data-driven) across different AI fields is well-supported by historical evidence
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that mathematical tools are inherently inadequate for modeling real-world complexity is plausible but requires more rigorous mathematical analysis
- **Medium confidence**: The human-machine collaboration thesis is supported by current AI practice but the philosophical justification using practice theory needs further validation
