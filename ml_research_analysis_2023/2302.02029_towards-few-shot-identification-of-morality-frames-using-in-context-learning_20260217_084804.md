# Towards Few-Shot Identification of Morality Frames using In-Context Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02029
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02029
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Few-shot in-context learning with LLMs can identify morality frames in text, with better performance on moral roles than subtle moral foundations

## Executive Summary
This paper explores few-shot identification of morality frames in text using in-context learning with large language models (LLMs). The authors propose prompting-based approaches that rely on few-shot exemplars to identify moral foundations and roles without updating model parameters. They compare their LLM-based methods with few-shot RoBERTa classifiers on a dataset of political tweets and find that while LLMs underperform on subtle moral foundations, they excel at identifying moral roles directed towards entities. The results demonstrate the potential of in-context learning for computational social science tasks and suggest promising directions for future research.

## Method Summary
The study uses GPT-J-6B LLM with few-shot in-context learning to identify morality frames in political tweets. The approach involves prompting the LLM with task instructions, definitions of moral foundations and roles, and few-shot training examples. The authors decompose the task into subtasks: identifying moral foundations and identifying moral roles of entities. They use one-pass and one-vs-all prompting strategies for moral foundation identification, and one-pass and two-step prompting for moral role identification. The LLM generates labels directly from examples without parameter updates. Performance is compared against few-shot RoBERTa classifiers using macro F1 scores and accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- LLM prompting-based techniques outperform RoBERTa in identifying moral roles directed towards entities
- LLMs underperform RoBERTa in identifying subtle moral foundations like Care/Harm or Fairness/Cheating
- Task decomposition into smaller subtasks improves LLM performance, particularly for entity-related moral roles

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Large language models can leverage few-shot in-context learning to identify morality frames by generating labels directly from examples.
- Mechanism: The LLM is prompted with task instructions, definitions of moral foundations and roles, and few-shot training examples. It then generates the appropriate label for the test instance by recognizing patterns in the examples without parameter updates.
- Core assumption: The LLM has sufficient world knowledge and pattern recognition capability to map input text to correct morality frame labels when given a small number of exemplars.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Few-shot in-context learning using pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) has been recently applied successfully in many NLP tasks."
  - [section] "In this paper, we propose prompting-based approaches using pretrained Large Language Models for identification of morality frames, relying only on few-shot exemplars."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus shows related works on morality frames and LLMs but no direct evidence of few-shot success in this specific task.
- Break condition: If the moral concepts are too subtle or nuanced, the LLM may fail to generate correct labels despite few-shot examples.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Breaking the task into smaller, more focused subtasks improves LLM performance in morality frame identification.
- Mechanism: Instead of predicting the full morality frame in one step, the task is decomposed into (1) identifying the moral foundation, and (2) identifying the moral role of entities. This allows the model to focus on simpler, more discriminative aspects of the text.
- Core assumption: The LLM can more accurately predict simpler, more evident aspects (like entity roles) than complex, subtle ones (like moral foundations), and decomposing the task reduces cognitive load.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We found that prompting-based techniques underperform RoBERTa in identification of subtle concepts like moral foundations, but in case of moral role identification, the prompting-based techniques outperforms RoBERTa by a large margin."
  - [section] "As a result it is easier for the LLMs to identify them."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus includes related works on morality and LLMs but lacks evidence of task decomposition benefits.
- Break condition: If the subtasks are still too complex or interdependent, decomposition may not help and could introduce errors.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Providing definitions and clear task instructions in the prompt improves LLM accuracy by guiding its generation process.
- Mechanism: Each prompt includes definitions of moral foundations and roles, and the task is framed as a text generation problem where the model generates the label directly. This reduces ambiguity and anchors the model's predictions to the intended categories.
- Core assumption: LLMs can use provided definitions as context to constrain their output to the intended label space, especially when the labels are well-defined and the definitions are clear.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We provide the description of the expected labels as task instruction in the prompt."
  - [section] "Note that we do not encounter the problem of hallucination when generating labels for moral foundation and moral roles as the labels were well-defined in the prompt."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus mentions related prompting work but not specific to morality frame definitions.
- Break condition: If definitions are ambiguous or the label space is too large, the model may still generate incorrect or hallucinated outputs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of in-context learning for morality frame identification scale with the size of the underlying language model (e.g., comparing GPT-J-6B to GPT-3)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "Experiment with larger language models" is a future direction and notes that larger models like GPT-3 could potentially perform better due to more parameters and diverse training data.
- Why unresolved: The authors were constrained by resource limitations and lack of open-source access to larger models like GPT-3, preventing them from testing this hypothesis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Running the same experiments with larger models like GPT-3 and comparing their performance metrics (e.g., F1 scores) to those obtained with GPT-J-6B.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would incorporating contextual information (e.g., social context, speaker demographics) into the prompts affect the performance of morality frame identification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper lists "Incorporation of context in prompt" as a future direction, noting that social context and speaker demographics play an important role in complex concepts like moral foundations.
- Why unresolved: The authors did not experiment with adding contextual information to their prompts, leaving the potential impact on performance unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing and testing prompts that include contextual information (e.g., user demographics, temporal context) and measuring the resulting performance improvements or changes in accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of prompt selection strategies on the performance of in-context learning for morality frame identification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests "Prompt selection" as a future direction, citing previous work that showed appropriate prompt selection based on test data points can improve in-context learning performance.
- Why unresolved: The authors used a fixed set of prompts without exploring dynamic prompt selection techniques that could potentially enhance performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing and comparing different prompt selection strategies (e.g., selecting prompts based on similarity to test data) and measuring their impact on task performance metrics.

## Limitations
- Limited to political tweets dataset, potentially limiting generalizability to other domains
- Inconsistent performance - excels at moral role identification but underperforms on subtle moral foundations
- Cannot update model parameters, potentially limiting capacity to learn complex patterns
- Does not address potential bias in LLM-generated outputs or computational cost of repeated inference

## Confidence
- High confidence: The LLM's superior performance on moral role identification over RoBERTa is well-supported by experimental results.
- Medium confidence: The claim that task decomposition improves performance is supported but could benefit from additional ablation studies.
- Low confidence: The generalizability of these findings to other domains and languages remains uncertain due to the single dataset evaluation.
