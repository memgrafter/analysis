# Large Language Models as Corporate Lobbyists

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01181
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01181
- Reference count: 0
- Demonstrates proof-of-concept using LLMs for corporate lobbying activities

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a proof-of-concept demonstrating how large language models can be used for corporate lobbying activities. The authors employ OpenAI's text-davinci-003 to evaluate the relevance of proposed U.S. Congressional bills to specific public companies and draft persuasive letters to bill sponsors. The model achieves 75.3% accuracy in relevance assessment, outperforming both a baseline of always predicting irrelevance (70.9%) and the previous state-of-the-art GPT-3 model (51.8%). The research raises concerns about AI systems potentially disrupting democratic alignment if they begin to significantly influence lawmaking processes.

## Method Summary
The authors use OpenAI's text-davinci-003 model to assess the relevance of 485 proposed U.S. Congressional bills to specific public companies and draft persuasive lobbying letters to bill sponsors. The model's performance is evaluated against a baseline that always predicts irrelevance (achieving 70.9% accuracy) and compared to the previous state-of-the-art GPT-3 model (text-davinci-002, achieving 51.8% accuracy). The study focuses on automated assessment of legislative relevance and generation of persuasive communication content.

## Key Results
- text-davinci-003 achieves 75.3% accuracy on 485 bills, outperforming baseline (70.9%) and GPT-3 (51.8%)
- Model successfully assesses bill relevance and drafts persuasive lobbying letters
- Proof-of-concept demonstrates technical feasibility of AI-assisted lobbying

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper doesn't explicitly detail the mechanism by which the LLM performs lobbying tasks. However, the approach leverages the model's natural language understanding and generation capabilities to analyze legislative text and craft persuasive arguments tailored to specific companies and bill sponsors.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Bill Text -> LLM Analysis -> Relevance Score -> Letter Generation -> Output

**Critical Path**: The critical path flows from bill text input through the LLM's analysis to produce both a relevance score and a drafted letter, with the relevance assessment being the primary performance metric.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The approach prioritizes automation and scalability of lobbying activities over the nuanced, relationship-based nature of traditional lobbying, potentially sacrificing depth for breadth.

**Failure Signatures**: Model may generate irrelevant or off-target assessments, produce unpersuasive letters, or misinterpret legislative intent due to limitations in understanding complex political contexts.
