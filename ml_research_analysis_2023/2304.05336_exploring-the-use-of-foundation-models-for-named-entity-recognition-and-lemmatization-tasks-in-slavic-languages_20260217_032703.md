# Exploring the Use of Foundation Models for Named Entity Recognition and Lemmatization Tasks in Slavic Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05336
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05336
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: AMU's foundation model approach achieved F1 scores of 85.37 (Polish), 89.70 (Czech), and 86.16 (Russian) for NER, and 82.37 (Polish), 76.89 (Czech), and 81.27 (Russian) for lemmatization.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Adam Mickiewicz University's solution for the 4th Shared Task on SlavNER, exploring foundation models for named entity recognition and lemmatization in Slavic languages. The approach utilized BERT and T5 architectures with both monolingual and multilingual variants, complemented by external datasets. The methodology demonstrates that foundation models are effective for these tasks across Polish, Czech, and Russian, with monolingual models performing better for languages with sufficient training data, while multilingual models showed advantages for Russian due to data scarcity. The addition of CRF layers consistently improved performance, and larger T5 models yielded better lemmatization results.

## Method Summary
The methodology involved fine-tuning pre-trained BERT and T5 models for both NER and lemmatization tasks. For NER, token classification was performed using both the token itself and its context representation, with experiments using both monolingual (Polish, Czech, Russian) and multilingual (SlavicBERT, multilingual BERT) variants. A CRF layer was added to some models to improve sequence labeling performance. For lemmatization, a text-to-text approach was employed using T5 models of different sizes (small, base, large). External datasets were utilized to enhance model training, particularly for Russian where in-task data was limited. The evaluation followed the shared task's official metrics with both case-sensitive and case-insensitive F1 scores.

## Key Results
- Monolingual BERT models outperformed multilingual variants for Polish and Czech NER, while multilingual models showed advantages for Russian
- Adding CRF layers significantly improved NER performance across all languages in most cases
- Larger T5 models consistently achieved better lemmatization results
- Best system achieved case-insensitive F1 scores of 85.37, 89.70, and 86.16 for Polish, Czech, and Russian NER respectively
- Best system achieved case-insensitive F1 scores of 82.37, 76.89, and 81.27 for Polish, Czech, and Russian lemmatization respectively

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Foundation models like BERT and T5 capture rich linguistic representations through pre-training on large corpora, enabling effective transfer learning for downstream tasks. The contextual embeddings generated by these models encode both syntactic and semantic information necessary for distinguishing named entities and generating accurate lemmas. For Slavic languages with complex morphology, the transformer architecture's attention mechanism effectively models long-range dependencies and captures morphological patterns. The CRF layer improves sequence labeling by enforcing label consistency constraints, while larger T5 models provide more capacity to handle the morphological complexity of lemmatization tasks.

- **CRF layers**: Necessary for enforcing valid label transitions in NER; quick check: compare performance with and without CRF

- **Text-to-text modeling**: Enables flexible input-output formats for lemmatization; quick check: verify decoder generates valid lemmas
- **Monolingual vs multilingual models**: Important tradeoff for resource-rich vs resource-poor languages; quick check: analyze cross-lingual transfer potential
- **External data utilization**: Critical for improving performance on low-resource languages; quick check: measure impact of additional training data

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Input text -> BERT/T5 encoder -> Token embeddings/CRF layer -> Entity labels/lemmas

**Critical path**: Text preprocessing → Model fine-tuning → Inference → Post-processing

**Design tradeoffs**: Monolingual models offer language-specific optimization but require more resources, while multilingual models provide cross-lingual benefits but may underperform on specific languages

**Failure signatures**: Poor performance on morphologically complex words, incorrect entity boundary detection, generation of invalid lemmas

## Limitations
- Evaluation constrained by shared task data format which may not reflect real-world usage patterns
- Performance comparisons influenced by data availability rather than purely architectural differences
- Limited exploration of alternative sequence labeling approaches beyond baseline fine-tuning
- Surface-level analysis of foundation model limitations for Slavic languages' morphological complexity

## Confidence

**Major claim clusters confidence:**
- **High confidence**: Effectiveness of foundation models for Slavic NER and lemmatization tasks is well-supported by quantitative results across multiple languages
- **Medium confidence**: Superiority of monolingual models for well-resourced languages and multilingual models for Russian is supported but may be influenced by data quantity
- **Medium confidence**: Consistent benefit of CRF layers and larger T5 models is demonstrated, though analysis could benefit from more extensive hyperparameter exploration
