# Utterance Classification with Logical Neural Network: Explainable AI for Mental Disorder Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03902
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03902
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: Achieved AUC ROC of 0.79 for depression classification using Logical Neural Networks with predicate pruning for explainability

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a Neuro-Symbolic AI approach for mental disorder diagnosis using Logical Neural Networks (LNN) to address the critical need for explainable AI in clinical settings. The proposed system takes predicates extracted from clinical interviews as input and outputs mental disorder classifications with interpretable logical rules. The approach employs predicate pruning techniques to achieve scalability and higher classification scores, along with insight extraction methods to aid therapists in diagnosis.

## Method Summary
The system uses AMR parser-generated predicates from clinical interview transcripts as input to an LNN architecture with 4 AND logic gates. The LNN is trained with supervised learning (learning rate 0.05, 50 epochs) on samples generated from the predicate set. Three predicate pruning methods are applied: similarity pruning to remove redundant predicates, frequency pruning to eliminate infrequent predicates, and exclusive pruning to retain predicates unique to each disorder class. The trained model outputs weighted predicates and bound scores for each mental disorder class, enabling both classification and explanation of the diagnostic reasoning.

## Key Results
- Achieved AUC ROC of 0.79 for depression disorder classification
- Demonstrated effective predicate pruning for scalability (reduced 19000+ predicates significantly)
- Extracted significant predicates for each disorder class providing explainability in diagnosis process
- Showed potential for clinical application through interpretable logical rules

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Predicate pruning improves scalability and model performance by reducing the number of predicates, which leads to faster training and better generalization.
- Mechanism: By removing redundant, infrequent, or non-exclusive predicates, the model focuses on the most relevant features for classification, reducing noise and computational complexity.
- Core assumption: A subset of predicates contributes significantly to accurate classification, while others are redundant or introduce noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "different predicate pruning techniques are used to achieve scalability and higher scores"
  - [section] "Table 4 shows that 48 transcripts result in more than 19000 predicates... for a linear increase in number of predicates, LNN requires exponential increase in training time"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Exclusive predicates improve model performance by reducing contradictory information between classes.
- Mechanism: Predicates that are unique to each class eliminate ambiguity during training, allowing the model to learn clearer decision boundaries.
- Core assumption: Predicates that appear across multiple classes introduce confusion and reduce classification accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Exclusive pruning... predicates repeating between classes predicates that are repeated only once have been removed"
  - [section] "Exclusive predicates model have shown a good performance overall. It reached AUC of 0.79 for the depression class"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Logical Neural Networks provide explainability through weighted logical rules that map directly to input predicates.
- Mechanism: Each LNN neuron represents a weighted logical formula, and after training, the weights indicate the importance of each predicate for classification.
- Core assumption: The 1-to-1 relationship between LNN neurons and logical formulae preserves interpretability while maintaining learning capabilities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The LNN is a Recurrent Neural Network architecture that combines the learning capabilities of neural networks with the reasoning capabilities of classical logic-based AI"
  - [section] "After the training model outputs set of weight for each predicate and outputs a tensor of lower and upper bounds as a score"

## Limitations

- The exponential increase in training time with predicate count limits scalability to larger datasets
- The exclusive pruning approach may remove important discriminative features since mental disorders share symptoms
- The relatively small dataset size (12 sessions per disorder class) raises concerns about generalizability

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that predicate pruning reduces computational complexity and improves training efficiency is well-supported by empirical evidence showing exponential training time increases with predicate count.

**Medium Confidence**: The assertion that LNNs provide explainable AI through weighted logical rules is conceptually sound but the practical interpretability depends heavily on predicate quality and pruning choices.

**Low Confidence**: The scalability of this approach to larger, more diverse clinical datasets remains uncertain given the exponential complexity relationship between predicates and training time.
