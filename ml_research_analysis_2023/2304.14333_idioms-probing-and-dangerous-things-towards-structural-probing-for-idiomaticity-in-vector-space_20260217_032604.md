# Idioms, Probing and Dangerous Things: Towards Structural Probing for Idiomaticity in Vector Space

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.14333
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14333
- **Reference count**: 30
- **Primary result**: Probing study reveals idiomaticity information is localized in specific dimensions of both static and contextual embeddings, but norm encoding evidence is conflicting.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how idiomaticity is structurally encoded in word embeddings through a comparative probing study of GloVe and BERT. The authors construct a novel probing dataset by repurposing the VNC-tokens dataset and apply probing with noise methodology to determine whether idiomaticity information is encoded in vector norms or dimensions. Experiments show that both embedding types encode some idiomaticity information, but yield conflicting evidence about norm encoding, while dimension deletion experiments reveal localization in the first half of dimensions. The study highlights both the promise and challenges of structural probing for linguistic phenomena.

## Method Summary
The study employs probing with noise methodology to investigate whether idiomaticity information is encoded in the vector norm or dimensions of embeddings. The VNC-tokens dataset is repurposed into an idiomatic usage classification task with three labels (Idiomatic, Literal, Unknown). Sentence embeddings are generated using GloVe (300-dim) and BERT (768-dim via mean pooling), then subjected to ablation experiments that remove information from either the norm (abl.N) or dimensions (abl.D), or both (abl.D+N). An MLP classifier evaluates the remaining information's capacity to distinguish idiomatic from literal usage, with performance measured via micro-average AUC-ROC across 50 random seeds.

## Key Results
- Both GloVe and BERT encode some idiomaticity information, with BERT showing stronger baseline performance
- Conflicting evidence about whether idiomaticity is encoded in vector norms, with correlation analysis suggesting weak negative correlation but ablation experiments showing no clear signal
- Dimension deletion experiments reveal idiomaticity information is localized in the first half of dimensions in both embeddings
- Performance spikes observed when deleting second half of dimensions suggest potential dataset confounds
- VNC-tokens dataset limitations (small size, narrow scope, quality issues) may affect experimental results

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Probing with noise ablates information in either the vector norm or dimensions to reveal structural encoding patterns.
- Mechanism: The method applies targeted noise functions that scale vector norms uniformly from the min/max range of norms in the dataset, or replace dimension values uniformly from min/max dimension ranges, effectively removing information from one container while preserving the other.
- Core assumption: Information encoded in embeddings is separable into norm and dimension containers, and ablating one reveals whether the other contains relevant task information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method is described in detail in Klubiˇcka and Kelleher (2022): in essence it applies targeted noise functions to embeddings that have an ablational effect and remove information encoded either in the norm or dimensions of a vector."
  - [section 4.2] "We remove information from the norm (abl.N) by sampling random norm values and scaling the vector dimensions to the new norm. To ablate information encoded in the dimensions (abl.D), we randomly sample dimension values and then scale them to match the original norm of the vector."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for this mechanism.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Idiomaticity information is localized in specific dimensions of embeddings, with preference for first half of dimensions.
- Mechanism: Through dimension deletion experiments where half the vector dimensions are removed and the probe is retrained, the method identifies which dimension ranges contain idiomaticity information by observing performance changes.
- Core assumption: Linguistic properties can be localized in specific dimensions of embeddings, and deleting certain dimension ranges will disproportionately affect performance if those dimensions contain the relevant information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.2] "In principle this interpretation is consistent with the findings of Torroba Hennigen et al. (2020) and Durrani et al. (2020), who showed that certain linguistic properties are localised in dimensions of contextual embeddings."
  - [section 5] "We observe quite a large performance spike... we observe the same behaviour in both IUF and IUR in BERT, where del.2h causes a significant performance drop, but del.1h causes a significant spike."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - only 5 related papers found with average FMR 0.462, suggesting limited corpus support for this specific mechanism.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The VNC-tokens dataset limitations create confounding effects that obscure true idiomaticity encoding patterns.
- Mechanism: Dataset issues including small size (two orders of magnitude smaller than established probing datasets), limited scope (only one type of verbal MWE), and quality problems (no sentence length control, typographical errors, incorrect annotations) introduce confounds that affect experimental results.
- Core assumption: Probing results reflect dataset properties as much as or more than the actual embedding models' idiomaticity encoding capabilities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 7.1] "While constructing and experimenting with the VNC-tokens dataset we have become aware of some of its shortcomings. Our main concern is that it is two orders of magnitude smaller than more established probing datasets."
  - [section 7.1] "The VNC-tokens dataset is also very limited in scope, containing only a single type of verbal MWE... It is also worth noting that both idiomatic and literal usages of the VMWEs present in the dataset are relatively frequent in English when compared to other more niche idiomatic phrases."
  - [section 7.1] "Furthermore, at this point the VNC-Tokens dataset is a relatively older benchmark and there are indications that it has not been as meticulously crafted as more recent MWE datasets."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dataset preprocessing -> Embedding generation (GloVe/BERT) -> Noise application (abl.N, abl.D, abl.D+N) -> MLP classifier training -> AUC-ROC evaluation -> Statistical analysis with confidence intervals
- Critical path: The core experimental flow is embedding generation -> noise application -> classifier training -> evaluation, with dataset construction as prerequisite and statistical analysis as post-processing
- Design tradeoffs: Using sentence-level vs. word-level probing (sentence-level may dilute signals), fixed vs. resampled test splits (fixed is harder but more controlled), GloVe vs. BERT comparison (static vs. contextual representations)
- Failure signatures: Performance drops to random baseline levels indicate complete information removal, while performance spikes in dimension deletion suggest unexpected localization patterns, and inconsistent norm vs. correlation results indicate dataset confounds
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run vanilla probing without noise to establish baseline idiomaticity encoding for both GloVe and BERT
  2. Apply norm ablation (abl.N) to test whether idiomaticity information survives when dimensions are preserved
  3. Apply dimension ablation (abl.D) to test whether idiomaticity information survives when norms are preserved

These experiments establish the fundamental pattern of whether information is in norms, dimensions, or both, which is the core research question.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Do word-level embeddings contain more salient idiomaticity information than sentence-level embeddings?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that their sentence-level experiments may have diluted the idiomaticity signal and suggest word-level probing could yield different results.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments only probe sentence-level embeddings averaged from word vectors, which may obscure fine-grained information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Repeating the probing experiments at the word level (e.g., probing individual token embeddings) and comparing the strength of idiomaticity signals.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is idiomaticity information encoded in the vector norm of BERT embeddings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The probing with noise experiments found no signal in the norm, but correlation analysis suggested a weak negative correlation between norm length and idiomaticity labels.
- Why unresolved: The norm ablation experiments did not reveal a significant effect, yet the correlation analysis hinted at a possible relationship, leaving the role of the norm unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing experiments that isolate and test norm effects more directly, or using alternative methods to detect subtle norm-encoded information.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are the observed dimension localization effects in GloVe and BERT due to the dataset or a true property of how these models encode idiomaticity?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The dimension deletion experiments showed surprising performance spikes when deleting the second half of dimensions, which the authors suspect may be an artifact of the VNC-tokens dataset rather than a genuine encoding property.
- Why unresolved: The authors are uncertain whether these effects reflect the actual encoding mechanism or are induced by dataset-specific biases or limitations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replicating the experiments on a larger, more diverse idiomaticity dataset (e.g., MAGPIE or SemEval-2022 Task 2) to see if the localization effect persists.

## Limitations

- Dataset size and quality issues: The VNC-tokens dataset is two orders of magnitude smaller than established probing datasets and has known quality problems including typographical errors and lack of sentence length control
- Limited scope: Contains only 56 verbal multi-word expressions of one type, limiting generalizability to other idiomatic constructions
- Conflicting evidence about norm encoding: Probing with noise found no signal in norms, but correlation analysis suggested weak negative correlation, leaving the role of norms unclear

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The probing methodology itself is sound and well-established in the literature. The observation that dimension deletion experiments show localization patterns in the first half of dimensions is reproducible and consistent with prior work on linguistic property localization.

**Medium confidence**: The finding that both GloVe and BERT encode some idiomaticity information is robust, but the specific claim about whether information is in norms or dimensions has conflicting evidence that requires further investigation.

**Low confidence**: The conclusion that idiomaticity is localized in specific dimensions (particularly the first half) is tentative due to the unexpected performance spike observed in deletion experiments, which may indicate dataset confounds rather than genuine embedding properties.

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset replication**: Replicate the core experiments using a larger, more diverse idiom dataset (such as the STREUSLE corpus or recent SemEval idiom datasets) to determine if the observed patterns hold beyond the limited VNC-Tokens dataset.

2. **Control for confounds**: Run experiments with additional control conditions including sentence length normalization, duplicate removal, and comparison with random baseline splits to isolate whether performance patterns reflect genuine embedding properties versus dataset artifacts.

3. **Cross-model comparison**: Extend the probing analysis to additional embedding models (ELMo, RoBERTa, GPT) and static embeddings of different dimensionalities to determine whether the observed norm/dimension encoding patterns are model-specific or general properties of how embeddings represent idiomaticity.
