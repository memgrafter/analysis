# Using Active Learning Methods to Strategically Select Essays for Automated Scoring

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00628
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00628
- Reference count: 9
- Three active learning methods achieved 95% accuracy with only 1.8-5.1% of essays needing human scoring

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates three active learning methods—uncertainty-based, topological-based, and hybrid—to minimize human scoring requirements for automated essay scoring (AES). Using the ASAP dataset and BERT-based classification, the research demonstrates that strategic essay selection can dramatically reduce the number of essays requiring human scoring while maintaining high accuracy. The topological method proved most efficient, requiring only 1.8% of the dataset to achieve 95% accuracy, compared to 5.1% for uncertainty sampling and 4.6% for the hybrid approach.

## Method Summary
The study employed three active learning strategies to select essays for human scoring: uncertainty sampling (selecting hardest-to-classify essays), topological sampling (selecting most representative essays from class clusters), and hybrid sampling (combining both approaches). BERT was fine-tuned on the ASAP dataset to generate 765-dimensional feature vectors from essay embeddings. The selected essays were scored by humans and used to train the classifier, with performance measured using Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK) across different sample sizes (1%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%).

## Key Results
- Topological method achieved highest efficiency: 1.8% of essays needed for 95% accuracy
- Hybrid method required 4.6% of essays for 95% accuracy
- Uncertainty sampling needed 5.1% of essays for 95% accuracy
- All methods showed varying efficiency depending on sample size allocation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Active learning methods can dramatically reduce the number of essays needing human scoring while maintaining high accuracy.
- Mechanism: By selecting essays that are either uncertain (hardest to classify) or most representative of class clusters, the model learns from the most informative data points rather than random samples.
- Core assumption: The most uncertain or representative essays provide maximum information gain for model training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the topological method was most efficient (1.8% of data needed for 95% accuracy), followed by hybrid (4.6%) and uncertainty (5.1%)"
  - [section] "The AL method that achieves the optimal performance with the smallest number of essays is considered to be efficient"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains related AES papers but none directly comparing active learning efficiency metrics
- Break condition: If the selected essays don't represent the true distribution of essay difficulty or quality, the model may not generalize well.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning BERT on the ASAP dataset significantly improves scoring accuracy compared to using the baseline BERT model.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning adapts the pre-trained language model to the specific domain of essay scoring, incorporating language context into feature vectors which improves discrimination between score levels.
- Core assumption: The ASAP dataset contains sufficient linguistic patterns to improve BERT's understanding of essay quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "BERT was fine-tuned on the ASAP dataset in order to improve its ability to score the essays"
  - [section] "Table 1 compares the BERT language model's classification accuracy on the ASAP dataset before and after fine-tuning. QWK increases noticeably after the context is added"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus - no BERT fine-tuning studies found
- Break condition: If the fine-tuning dataset is too small or unrepresentative, the model may overfit or fail to capture important linguistic features.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different active learning methods show varying efficiency depending on sample size allocation.
- Mechanism: Each AL method has different strengths - uncertainty sampling excels at small sample sizes, while topological methods work better at larger sample sizes, and hybrid methods balance both approaches.
- Core assumption: The relationship between sample size and method efficiency is non-linear and method-dependent.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Different AL methods showed varying efficiency depending on sample size, demonstrating that strategic essay selection can dramatically reduce human scoring requirements"
  - [section] "Figure 4 demonstrates that different percentages results in different levels of efficiency meaning that the growth rate accuracy across the three methods is not linear"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains AES papers but none specifically analyzing sample size effects on AL method efficiency
- Break condition: If the relationship between sample size and efficiency becomes linear or changes pattern, the current understanding of method strengths would be invalid.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  ASAP dataset (12,978 essays) → Pre-processing → BERT feature extraction (765-dimensional vectors) → AL method selection → Human scoring of selected essays → Model training → Validation → QWK evaluation
  Three AL components: Uncertainty sampling, Topological sampling, Hybrid sampling

- Critical path: ASAP dataset → BERT fine-tuning → AL method application → Human scoring → Model training → QWK evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. efficiency: Higher accuracy requires more essays to be scored (95% accuracy needs 1.8-5.1% vs 85% needs <1%)
  - Method selection: Different AL methods work better at different sample sizes
  - Fine-tuning vs. baseline: Fine-tuned BERT is more accurate but requires additional computational resources

- Failure signatures:
  - Low QWK scores despite large training sets
  - Inconsistent performance across different essay prompts
  - One AL method consistently underperforming others
  - Feature vectors not capturing important linguistic features

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline experiment: Train BERT on full ASAP dataset, evaluate QWK without AL to establish performance ceiling
  2. Method comparison: Run all three AL methods (uncertainty, topological, hybrid) at 1%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20% sample sizes, measure QWK and efficiency
  3. Cross-prompt validation: Test trained models on essays from different prompts than training data to assess generalizability

## Limitations
- Study conducted using a single, well-structured dataset (ASAP), may not generalize to more diverse or noisy real-world essay collections
- Only evaluated three specific active learning methods, potentially missing other approaches that could be more efficient
- Computational resources required for fine-tuning BERT may limit practical deployment in resource-constrained environments

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The effectiveness of active learning in reducing human scoring requirements (supported by concrete efficiency metrics showing 1.8-5.1% of data needed for 95% accuracy)
- **Medium Confidence**: The superiority of topological sampling over other methods (based on single dataset results, requires validation on other datasets)
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of fine-tuning benefits across different essay scoring domains (no external validation studies found in corpus)
