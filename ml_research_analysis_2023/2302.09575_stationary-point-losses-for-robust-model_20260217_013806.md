# Stationary Point Losses for Robust Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09575
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09575
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SP losses significantly improve adversarial robustness without sacrificing accuracy across multiple datasets and architectures

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces stationary point (SP) losses to address the fundamental limitation of cross-entropy loss in learning robust decision boundaries. The key insight is that CE loss lacks stationary points, causing neural networks to converge to near-boundaries that are vulnerable to adversarial attacks rather than robust positions. SP losses introduce regularization terms that guarantee at least one stationary point on the correct classification side, theoretically ensuring convergence to more robust boundaries. Extensive experiments on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CINIC-10, Clothing1M, and VOC2007 using ResNet, GoogleNet, and DenseNet architectures demonstrate significant improvements in adversarial robustness under white-box attacks (FGSM, BIM, PGD, UPGD) without accuracy loss.

## Method Summary
SP loss is implemented as a modified loss function that adds a regularization term to standard classification losses. The framework is: L = Lcls(θ) + ηR(θ,ξ), where Lcls(θ) is the base classification loss, R(θ,ξ) is the regularization term that introduces a stationary point, and η is a positive regularity coefficient. The key mechanism ensures at least one stationary point exists on the correct classification side of the decision boundary. The paper demonstrates SP focal loss as a specific implementation: L = -α(1-ξ)^γlog(ξ) + η(||ξ||^2), where ξ is the softmax output, α=0.25, γ=2, and η is tuned per dataset.

## Key Results
- SP focal loss improved CIFAR-10 accuracy under PGD attack by approximately 50% compared to CE loss
- Significant robustness gains across all tested architectures (ResNet-18, ResNet-34, GoogleNet, DenseNet-121)
- Consistent improvements across multiple datasets including imbalanced scenarios like Clothing1M
- Robustness improvements maintained under various attack methods (FGSM, BIM, PGD, UPGD)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SP loss guarantees convergence to a robust boundary by introducing stationary points that prevent the model from settling at near-boundaries
- Mechanism: By adding a regularization term that creates at least one stationary point on the correct classification side, SP loss ensures the gradient descent algorithm has a local minimum corresponding to a robust boundary
- Core assumption: The existence of a stationary point in the loss landscape directly corresponds to a robust decision boundary position
- Evidence anchors:
  - "CE loss sharpens the neural network at the decision boundary to achieve a lower loss, rather than pushing the boundary to a more robust position"
  - "We propose a family of new losses, called stationary point (SP) loss, which has at least one stationary point on the correct classification side"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SP loss prevents the decision boundary from moving too close to training samples by maintaining a loss minimum away from sample points
- Mechanism: The regularization term creates a loss landscape where the minimum occurs at a location that maximizes margins between classes
- Core assumption: The position of the loss minimum directly determines the location of the decision boundary
- Evidence anchors:
  - "With SP loss, larger perturbations are required to generate adversarial examples"
  - "We propose a family of new losses, called stationary point (SP) loss, which has at least one stationary point on the correct classification side"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SP loss prevents weight divergence in the final layer by providing gradient signals that lead to convergence
- Mechanism: By introducing a stationary point in the loss landscape, SP loss provides gradient signals that guide the weights toward convergence rather than divergence
- Core assumption: The behavior of the loss gradient with respect to the network parameters determines whether those parameters converge or diverge during training
- Evidence anchors:
  - "SP loss has at least one stationary point on the correct classification side. We proved that robust boundary can be guaranteed by SP loss without losing much accuracy"
  - "The region of low prediction confidence means that the inference function is very sharp at the decision boundary"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Stationary points in loss landscapes
  - Why needed here: Understanding stationary points is crucial for grasping why SP loss works. Stationary points represent locations in the parameter space where the gradient is zero, which can correspond to local minima, maxima, or saddle points
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between a stationary point and a global minimum in a loss landscape?

- Concept: Adversarial robustness and decision boundaries
  - Why needed here: The paper's core contribution is improving adversarial robustness by changing how decision boundaries are learned
  - Quick check question: Why does a decision boundary positioned in the middle between classes provide better robustness than one close to the samples?

- Concept: Regularization in neural network training
  - Why needed here: SP loss is essentially a form of regularization that modifies the loss landscape
  - Quick check question: How does L2 regularization differ from the regularization used in SP loss?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base classification loss (CE, focal, etc.) -> SP regularization term -> Combined loss -> Standard optimization (SGD, Adam)
- Critical path: 1) Compute network output before softmax, 2) Apply softmax to get class probabilities, 3) Compute base classification loss, 4) Add SP regularization term, 5) Compute gradients and update weights using standard optimization methods
- Design tradeoffs: The regularization coefficient (η) needs careful tuning - too high and it may dominate classification loss, too low and stationary point effect may be insufficient. SP loss framework is more computationally expensive than standard CE loss
- Failure signatures: Poorly chosen regularization coefficient leads to either suboptimal boundaries (coefficient too low) or classification failure (coefficient too high). May show slower convergence during training
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement SP loss on binary classification with two-layer fully connected network to verify boundary positioning effect
  2. Test SP loss on MNIST with CNN architecture, comparing adversarial robustness against CE loss under FGSM and PGD attacks
  3. Experiment with different regularization coefficients on CIFAR-10 to find optimal balance between accuracy and robustness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SP loss performance scale with the number of classes in classification tasks?
- Basis in paper: "In future work, it is necessary to design stationary point losses suitable for classification problems with large number of classes"
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on datasets with limited class numbers (10 classes each)
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments on benchmark datasets with varying numbers of classes and theoretical analysis of high-dimensional classification spaces

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal value for the regularization coefficient η in SP loss for different network architectures and datasets?
- Basis in paper: Uses different values (0.03 for focal loss, 0.5 for CE loss) without systematic analysis
- Why unresolved: Paper doesn't explore sensitivity to different η values or provide selection guidelines
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive ablation studies varying η across datasets and architectures with recommendations for hyperparameter selection

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can SP loss be effectively combined with other adversarial defense techniques like adversarial training or defensive distillation?
- Basis in paper: Demonstrates SP loss effectiveness independently but doesn't explore combination with existing defense methods
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses solely on SP loss as standalone method
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments combining SP loss with various adversarial training approaches, defensive distillation, or other robustness techniques

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees for stationary point losses remain partially unproven in practical settings
- Regularization coefficient η requires careful tuning for each dataset and architecture
- Computational overhead introduced by SP losses is not quantified
- Relationship between stationary points and adversarial robustness is primarily empirical rather than fully theoretical

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Empirical results demonstrating improved robustness against various attacks across multiple datasets and architectures
- **Medium confidence**: Theoretical framework connecting stationary points to robust decision boundaries
- **Medium confidence**: Claim that SP losses work well on imbalanced datasets

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on regularization coefficient**: Systematically vary η across multiple orders of magnitude to determine sensitivity and identify optimal ranges for different datasets

2. **Comparison with state-of-the-art adversarial training**: Compare SP losses against established adversarial training methods (like TRADES or adversarial logit pairing) on the same architectures and datasets to benchmark relative performance

3. **Transferability analysis**: Evaluate whether models trained with SP losses show improved robustness against black-box attacks or transfer-based attacks, indicating more general robustness beyond white-box scenarios
