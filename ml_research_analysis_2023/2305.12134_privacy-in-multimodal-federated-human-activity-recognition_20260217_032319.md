# Privacy in Multimodal Federated Human Activity Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12134
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12134
- Reference count: 30
- Key outcome: Federated learning for HAR is significantly impacted by privacy constraints at user, environment, and sensor levels, with modality-level separation causing the largest accuracy drop (19-42%).

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how different levels of privacy constraints affect federated learning for human activity recognition when using multimodal data from radio-frequency sensors. The authors systematically partition data among clients to simulate privacy scenarios at user, environment, and sensor modality levels, then evaluate three neural network models under federated training. They demonstrate that while privacy-preserving federated learning is achievable, performance degrades significantly as privacy constraints increase, particularly when sensor modalities are separated across clients. To address this challenge, they propose a mutual learning approach between a global federated model and per-modality group models, which substantially reduces accuracy loss while maintaining privacy guarantees.

## Method Summary
The authors use a multimodal HAR dataset collected from radio-frequency sensors and partition it across clients to simulate three privacy levels: user-level (data from different users on separate clients), environment-level (data from different environments separated), and modality-level (different sensor modalities on separate clients). They evaluate three neural network architectures under federated learning: a convolutional neural network, a long short-term memory network, and a fusion vision transformer (FViT). To mitigate the significant accuracy degradation observed at the modality level, they introduce a mutual learning framework where a global federated model and per-modality group models teach each other during training, enabling knowledge transfer while preserving the privacy constraints.

## Key Results
- Accuracy degrades by 5-7% when data is partitioned by user or environment level
- Modality-level separation causes 19-42% accuracy reduction, the most severe impact
- FViT architecture shows the smallest performance drop under high heterogeneity conditions
- Mutual learning approach reduces modality-level accuracy loss to only 7-13%
- The proposed framework enables effective federated HAR while preserving privacy across diverse sensor hardware

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mutual learning approach works by creating a knowledge-sharing mechanism between the global federated model and per-modality group models. Each model learns from the predictions of others during training, effectively transferring complementary information across modality boundaries without directly sharing raw data. This cross-pollination of knowledge compensates for the missing modality information at each client, allowing the models to develop more robust representations despite the privacy constraints. The FViT architecture's inherent ability to handle heterogeneous input patterns makes it particularly resilient to the distribution shifts caused by data partitioning.

## Foundational Learning
- **Federated Learning**: Distributed machine learning where models train on decentralized data without direct data sharing; needed to enable privacy-preserving HAR across multiple devices
- **Multimodal Data Fusion**: Combining information from multiple sensor types to improve recognition accuracy; essential for robust HAR but challenging under privacy constraints
- **Knowledge Distillation**: Technique where a larger model transfers knowledge to smaller ones; forms the basis of the mutual learning approach
- **Privacy-Accuracy Tradeoff**: Fundamental tension between data protection and model performance; central to understanding federated HAR limitations
- **Non-IID Data Distributions**: Real-world scenario where data distributions vary across clients; critical consideration for federated learning effectiveness

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Global FL Server -> Client Groups (User/Env/Modality) -> Local Models -> Mutual Learning Updates -> Global Model

**Critical Path:**
Data partitioning → Local model training → Mutual learning exchange → Global aggregation → Performance evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Privacy vs. accuracy: Stricter partitioning causes greater performance degradation
- Model complexity vs. communication efficiency: More complex models may require more bandwidth
- Centralization vs. decentralization: Mutual learning introduces some coordination overhead

**Failure Signatures:**
- Accuracy plateaus below acceptable threshold when privacy constraints are too strict
- Model convergence issues when client data distributions are highly non-IID
- Communication bottlenecks during mutual learning exchange phases

**3 First Experiments:**
1. Compare baseline FL performance against mutual learning under identical privacy constraints

3. Evaluate model performance when gradually relaxing privacy constraints at each level

## Limitations
- Results are based on a single radio-frequency sensor dataset, limiting generalizability to other HAR modalities
- The study uses controlled partitioning scenarios that may not reflect real-world data distributions
- Performance evaluation focuses on accuracy metrics without comprehensive privacy quantification
- The mutual learning approach adds computational and communication overhead not fully characterized

## Confidence

**High:**
- Performance degradation patterns under different privacy levels are well-supported by experimental results

**Medium:**
- The effectiveness of the mutual learning approach to mitigate modality-level losses, while promising, is demonstrated on a single dataset

**Low:**
- Generalization of findings to diverse sensor types, real-world environments, and larger-scale federated networks

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the experiments on additional HAR datasets with different sensor modalities (e.g., accelerometers, gyroscopes, vision-based sensors) to assess generalizability
2. Test the mutual learning approach under more heterogeneous network conditions, including non-IID data distributions and varying client participation rates
3. Evaluate the proposed framework's performance against established differential privacy mechanisms (e.g., DP-SGD) to quantify privacy-accuracy tradeoffs more rigorously
