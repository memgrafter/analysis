# MedGPTEval: A Dataset and Benchmark to Evaluate Responses of Large Language Models in Medicine

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07340
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07340
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: MedGPTEval dataset and benchmark evaluates three Chinese-language medical chatbots; Doctor PuJiang outperforms ChatGPT and ERNIE Bot on both multi-turn dialogues and case reports.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MedGPTEval, a comprehensive evaluation system for large language models (LLMs) in medical contexts. The system comprises standardized evaluation criteria, novel Chinese medical datasets, and a benchmarking framework for medical chatbots. The evaluation criteria, developed through expert consensus using the Delphi method, cover 16 indicators across four capability categories. Experiments with three chatbots (ChatGPT, ERNIE Bot, and Doctor PuJiang) on 27 medical dialogues and 7 case reports show that Doctor PuJiang achieves the highest overall scores, particularly excelling in social and professional contexts.

## Method Summary
The study designed evaluation criteria through literature review and Delphi method consensus among five medical and engineering experts. Medical datasets were created, including 27 multi-turn medical dialogues and 7 case reports. Three chatbots (ChatGPT, ERNIE Bot, and Doctor PuJiang) were prompted with these datasets, and their responses were collected. Five licensed medical experts performed blind evaluations using the established criteria. Robustness testing involved generating perturbed versions of questions (rephrased, typo-introduced, etc.) and measuring semantic consistency rates. Scores were aggregated and analyzed to compare model performance.

## Key Results
- Doctor PuJiang (Dr. PJ) achieved the highest overall score (13.95) in multi-turn medical dialogues, outperforming ChatGPT (13.41) and ERNIE Bot (12.56).
- In case report scenarios, Dr. PJ scored 10.14, surpassing ChatGPT (8.71) and ERNIE Bot (8.0).
- Dr. PJ demonstrated superior performance in social and professional contexts, as measured by the evaluation criteria.
- Robustness testing revealed varying semantic consistency rates across perturbation types, with Dr. PJ generally maintaining higher consistency than competitors.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Delphi method converges expert opinion to produce a standardized evaluation criteria set that is both comprehensive and reproducible.
- Mechanism: Multiple rounds of anonymous feedback and iterative refinement among domain experts reduce individual bias and inconsistency, leading to consensus on the final criteria.
- Core assumption: Experts can objectively evaluate and refine criteria without groupthink if anonymity and structured iteration are enforced.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "existing candidate criteria were optimized using a Delphi method by five experts in medicine and engineering."
  - [section] "The general process involved sending the criteria to designated experts... obtaining their opinions... making generalizations and corrections... repeated until a unanimous consensus was reached."
  - [corpus] Weak. Related works cite similar expert-based evaluations but do not detail Delphi method specifics.
- Break condition: Consensus is not reached within a reasonable number of rounds, or experts' ratings remain highly divergent despite iteration.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multiple-turn dialogue datasets capture contextual reasoning and anaphora resolution capabilities better than single-turn question-answering benchmarks.
- Mechanism: Iterative question sequences require the model to track context, resolve references, and integrate new information, exposing contextual comprehension weaknesses not visible in isolated questions.
- Core assumption: Real clinical interactions involve multi-step reasoning where previous context influences response quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The dataset with multiple-turn was derived through an iterative process comprising 4 rounds... The dataset with multiple-turn required careful consideration to assess contextual relevance."
  - [corpus] Weak. Most related benchmarks focus on single-turn accuracy or general conversational ability, not multi-turn clinical reasoning.
- Break condition: If the model's performance on single-turn questions is equally strong, the added complexity of multi-turn may not yield additional discriminative power.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Robustness testing via paraphrased and error-introduced prompts exposes the model's ability to maintain semantic consistency under real-world variability.
- Mechanism: By generating multiple rephrasings (adversarial, noisy, typo-introduced), the evaluation measures whether the model's output remains consistent and accurate despite input perturbations common in patient queries.
- Core assumption: Patients will phrase medical questions in diverse, imperfect ways, and models must handle this without performance collapse.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To evaluate the robustness of the LLMs, 5 extended datasets were created based on first-round questions... Rephrasing the question sentence but maintaining the semantics... changing the semantics... introducing punctuation errors... grammatical errors... spelling errors."
  - [corpus] Weak. Related works mention robustness but rarely test with such fine-grained perturbation categories.
- Break condition: If semantic consistency rates are uniformly high across all perturbation types, the perturbation strategy may be insufficiently challenging.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Evaluation Criteria Builder (Delphi method interface)
  - Dataset Generator (multiple-turn dialogue and case report templates)
  - Benchmarking Engine (prompting LLMs, collecting responses)
  - Blind Review Interface (for licensed medical experts to score responses)
  - Robustness Tester (perturbation generation and consistency scoring)
- Critical path:
  1. Generate or import raw criteria.
  2. Run Delphi rounds until consensus.
  3. Create dialogue and case report datasets.
  4. Collect LLM responses to all prompts.
  5. Conduct blind expert scoring.
  6. Aggregate and analyze scores.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Expert consensus vs. speed: Delphi method is thorough but slow; automated metrics are fast but less reliable.
  - Dataset size vs. diversity: Larger datasets improve statistical power but increase expert workload.
  - Robustness perturbation variety vs. relevance: Too many error types may dilute focus on clinically realistic cases.
- Failure signatures:
  - Criteria lack clarity → inconsistent scoring.
  - Dataset bias toward certain specialties → unrepresentative performance.
  - LLM responses not recorded in consistent format → expert review delays.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run a small-scale Delphi round with 3 experts on a subset of criteria; check for convergence speed and clarity.
  2. Generate 5 perturbed versions of a single medical question; verify semantic consistency rate calculation.
  3. Have 2 experts independently score 10 LLM responses; measure inter-rater reliability.

## Limitations
- The evaluation relies on a small expert panel (five licensed medical professionals), which may introduce bias and limit generalizability.
- The medical datasets are limited in size (27 dialogues, 7 case reports), potentially constraining the statistical robustness of the results.
- The study focuses exclusively on Chinese-language models and datasets, limiting direct applicability to other languages.
- The robustness testing, though comprehensive in perturbation types, is based on a small sample size and lacks comparison with established robustness benchmarks.

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the methodological rigor of the Delphi process for criteria development and the blind expert review procedure.
- **Medium confidence** in the benchmarking results, given the small dataset size and limited number of models evaluated.
- **Low confidence** in the robustness findings due to the small perturbation sample size and lack of comparison with established robustness benchmarks.
