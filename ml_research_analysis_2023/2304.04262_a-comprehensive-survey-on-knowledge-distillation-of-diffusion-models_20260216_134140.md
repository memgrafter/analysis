# A Comprehensive Survey on Knowledge Distillation of Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04262
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04262
- Reference count: 21
- This paper provides a comprehensive survey on knowledge distillation of diffusion models, categorizing existing strategies into three main types: diffusion-to-field, diffusion-to-generator, and training-free distillation.

## Executive Summary
This survey paper provides a comprehensive overview of knowledge distillation techniques for diffusion models (DMs), which are powerful generative models that require many neural function evaluations (NFEs) to generate samples. The paper categorizes existing distillation strategies into three main approaches: diffusion-to-field (D2F) distillation, which transfers the generative ODE into another vector field with fewer NFEs; diffusion-to-generator (D2G) distillation, which transfers distributional knowledge into an efficient generator; and training-free (TF) distillation, which achieves accelerated sampling through advanced numerical solvers without training new models. The survey reviews landmark methodologies in each category and their applications, providing valuable insights for researchers interested in improving the efficiency of diffusion models.

## Method Summary
The paper systematically reviews knowledge distillation approaches for diffusion models by categorizing them into three main strategies. D2F distillation involves training a student model to approximate the teacher DM's generative ODE with fewer NFEs, typically using progressive distillation techniques. D2G distillation transfers the teacher DM's learned distributional knowledge into an efficient generator model, such as consistency models or other implicit generative models. TF distillation employs advanced numerical solvers and optimized time step selection to accelerate sampling without training new models. The survey synthesizes methodologies from various papers, including progressive distillation, consistency models, and training-free accelerated sampling algorithms, while providing insights into their mechanisms and applications.

## Key Results
- Knowledge distillation can significantly accelerate diffusion model sampling speed, with some approaches achieving up to 250x efficiency improvements with only 5% drop in generative performance (measured by FID)
- Three main categories of distillation strategies exist: diffusion-to-field, diffusion-to-generator, and training-free approaches
- Progressive distillation can reduce required NFEs to as few as 4 steps while maintaining comparable generative quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge distillation transfers distributional knowledge from a large, trained diffusion model into a smaller, faster model while maintaining comparable generative performance.
- Mechanism: The teacher DM learns fine-grained marginal score functions across many time steps, which can be approximated by a student model using fewer NFEs. The student can be a different vector field (D2F), an implicit generator (D2G), or a subset of time steps (TF).
- Core assumption: The teacher model's learned score functions contain generalizable distributional information that can be effectively approximated by a simpler model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Therefore, a crucial research direction is to explore how to distill the knowledge of DMs and fully utilize their potential."
  - [section] "One of the key motivations for diffusion distillation is to significantly accelerate the sampling speed, which is currently hindered by the large number of neural function evaluations required."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers on distillation mechanisms.
- Break condition: If the student model's architecture cannot express the teacher's learned score functions, or if the teacher model is overfit to training data and lacks generalizable distributional knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Progressive distillation (PD) progressively reduces the number of required NFEs by training a student network to predict multi-step outputs of the teacher model.
- Mechanism: A student network learns to approximate the output of the teacher's DDIM sampler over multiple time steps, effectively learning to "skip" intermediate steps while maintaining generative quality.
- Core assumption: The teacher model's sampling trajectory contains redundant information that can be compressed into fewer steps.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The progressive distillation (PD) strategy proposed by (Salimans and Ho, 2022) aims to train a student neural network that requires half the number of NFEs as the teacher model by learning the two-step prediction of the teacher DM's deterministic sampling strategy."
  - [section] "Successive PD rounds can reduce the required vector fields to only 4 NFEs, making it 250 times more efficient than the teacher diffusion's ODE sampler, with a 5% drop in generative performance as measured by FID."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers on progressive distillation mechanisms.
- Break condition: If the student network cannot accurately predict the teacher's multi-step outputs, or if the teacher model's sampling trajectory is not compressible.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Training-free accelerated sampling algorithms achieve comparable generative performance with fewer NFEs by using advanced numerical solvers and optimized time step selection.
- Mechanism: Instead of training a new student model, these algorithms use the pre-trained teacher model's score functions but simulate the reverse SDE/ODE with fewer time steps using more sophisticated numerical methods.
- Core assumption: The teacher model's score functions contain sufficient information to generate high-quality samples even with fewer time steps.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Recent research has shown that using a small subset of diffusion time levels can significantly accelerate the sampling process with much fewer NFEs."
  - [section] "The most distinguished part of training-free accelerating algorithms is to design faster sampling algorithms with no training of new parametric models but only inferencing by querying pre-trained DMs."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers on training-free accelerated sampling algorithms.
- Break condition: If the numerical solver cannot accurately approximate the reverse SDE/ODE with fewer time steps, or if the teacher model's score functions are not smooth enough for advanced numerical methods.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher DM -> Score network -> Student models (D2F vector field / D2G generator / TF accelerated sampler)

- Critical path:
  1. Train teacher DM using standard diffusion model training objectives
  2. Choose distillation strategy (D2F, D2G, or TF)
  3. Implement appropriate student model architecture
  4. Train student model using teacher's knowledge
  5. Evaluate student model's generative performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - D2F distillation: Faster sampling but may lose some path properties
  - D2G distillation: Can use different latent spaces but may require more complex architectures
  - TF distillation: No training required but may have lower performance gains

- Failure signatures:
  - D2F distillation: Student model's outputs diverge from teacher's at intermediate time steps
  - D2G distillation: Student generator produces samples with artifacts or incorrect distributions
  - TF distillation: Accelerated sampling produces lower quality samples or fails to converge

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement simple D2F distillation using progressive distillation strategy on a small dataset
  2. Compare performance of different student architectures in D2G distillation
  3. Evaluate the impact of different numerical solvers in TF distillation on a pre-trained DM

## Limitations
- The survey primarily reviews existing methodologies without providing extensive experimental validation of the claims made about each distillation strategy's effectiveness
- Lack of quantitative comparisons across different distillation approaches on standardized benchmarks
- Does not address potential negative impacts of distillation, such as loss of fine-grained details in generated samples or degradation of certain conditional generation capabilities

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: The categorization framework (D2F, D2G, TF) and basic mechanisms of knowledge distillation from diffusion models are well-established and supported by multiple papers in the field. The fundamental principles of progressive distillation and training-free acceleration methods are also well-documented.

**Medium Confidence**: Specific claims about relative performance improvements between different distillation strategies are less certain, as these depend heavily on implementation details, datasets, and evaluation protocols that vary across studies. The survey presents these claims but acknowledges variation in reported results.

**Low Confidence**: Predictions about long-term trends and future research directions should be viewed with appropriate skepticism, as the field is rapidly evolving and the survey may not capture emerging approaches or unexpected developments.
