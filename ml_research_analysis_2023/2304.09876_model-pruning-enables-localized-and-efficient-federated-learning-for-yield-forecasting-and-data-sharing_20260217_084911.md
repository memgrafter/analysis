# Model Pruning Enables Localized and Efficient Federated Learning for Yield Forecasting and Data Sharing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09876
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09876
- Reference count: 40
- Improves federated learning inference by 15.5-20% while reducing model size by 84% and communication by 57.1-64.7%

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses federated learning challenges in agri-food by proposing Federated Pruning (FedPruning), which combines neural network pruning with localized model aggregation. The method enables efficient learning on heterogeneous, non-IID data by creating client-specific sparse networks that retain weights important to individual clients. Tested on a soybean yield forecasting dataset, FedPruning significantly outperforms FedAvg baseline in both inference accuracy and communication efficiency while maintaining data privacy.

## Method Summary
The proposed method implements iterative magnitude pruning on client models during federated learning rounds, creating sparse networks that are aggregated using a localization-preserving strategy. Client models are pruned based on weight importance while monitoring loss recovery, then aggregated by updating only overlapping connections. The approach is tested with multiple pruning strategies (iterative, one-shot, and lottery ticket-based) on a soybean yield forecasting task using data from 9 US states spanning 1980-2018.

## Key Results
- FedPruning improves inference performance by 15.5% to 20% compared to FedAvg baseline
- Reduces local model sizes by up to 84% through pruning
- Decreases data volume communicated between clients and server by 57.1% to 64.7%
- Outperforms centralized baseline in most settings
- One-shot pruning with Lottery Ticket initialization achieves competitive results to iterative methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Localized sparse networks improve local inference performance by retaining weights important to individual clients.
- Mechanism: Iterative magnitude pruning removes low-importance weights, creating client-specific subnetworks that preserve data heterogeneity.
- Core assumption: Non-IID data distributions cause global models to underperform on individual clients, and pruning can isolate client-relevant weights.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This method enables local models to be tailored to their respective data distribution and mitigate the data heterogeneity present in agri-food data."
  - [section] "By producing sparse neural networks, the weights important to the client itself are retained."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Communication cost is reduced by transmitting smaller, pruned models between clients and server.
- Mechanism: Smaller model sizes reduce the number of parameters communicated each round, lowering latency and bandwidth usage.
- Core assumption: Data transmission volume directly impacts training efficiency in federated settings, especially in bandwidth-limited rural environments.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "allowing for more compact models that consume less data during transmission."
  - [section] "Reducing the number of parameters in the models can decrease the data exchange volume of the participants."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Localization-preserving aggregation retains client-specific model strengths while still enabling collaborative learning.
- Mechanism: Aggregating only overlapping connections in pruned models preserves unique client parameters, improving overall model diversity.
- Core assumption: Non-IID data leads to sparse model overlap; preserving unique connections prevents information loss during aggregation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "This aggregation strategy updates on only the overlapped connections of the sub-networks while keeping the non-overlapped parts unchanged."
  - [section] "A higher sparsity may reduce the divergence from non-IID data, which causes poor performance."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Client nodes -> Local data/model training/pruning -> Central server -> Aggregation module -> Updated global model -> Back to clients
- Critical path:
  1. Client trains local model
  2. Client prunes model if loss recovered
  3. Client sends sparse model to server
  4. Server aggregates overlapping weights
  5. Server sends updated global model back
  6. Repeat until convergence
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Aggressive pruning → faster communication but higher recovery risk
  - Frequent pruning → better compression but more aggregation noise
  - Larger local epochs → better local convergence but slower global progress
- Failure signatures:
  - Loss spikes after pruning rounds (insufficient recovery)
  - Model divergence across clients (overly sparse overlap)
  - Communication savings plateau early (pruning too late or too shallow)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run FedAvg baseline on soybean dataset, record inference RMSE and model size
  2. Apply iterative pruning with early stopping, measure local RMSE and communication cost
  3. Test one-shot pruning with and without Lottery Ticket initialization, compare recovery speed and final accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of iterative FedPruning compare to one-shot methods in terms of final model performance and communication efficiency across different non-IID data distributions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that while iterative pruning is commonly considered superior, their results show one-shot methods performing comparably, with one-shot-LT even outperforming iterative methods in some cases.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a systematic comparison across varying degrees of data heterogeneity or different datasets to determine when one approach is definitively better.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing both methods across multiple datasets with controlled variations in data heterogeneity, measuring both final performance and communication costs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal pruning schedule (frequency, percentage per round, total rounds) for maximizing FedPruning performance across different neural network architectures and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention they used a generalizable algorithm but acknowledge there may be unexplored approaches to identifying more optimal settings for iterative pruning, such as determining ideal granularity and schedule.
- Why unresolved: The paper tested only one specific pruning schedule per method, and the authors explicitly state this warrants further exploration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies varying pruning frequency, percentage per round, and total rounds across multiple architectures and datasets, measuring final performance and convergence speed.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the combination of model pruning with other compression techniques (like quantization) affect the communication efficiency and inference performance of federated learning in resource-constrained environments?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that model sparsification may be used in conjunction with other techniques and hardware to maximize compression effects, and they aim to develop a pipeline for further efficiency improvements.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses solely on pruning and doesn't explore combinations with other techniques that could provide multiplicative benefits.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing FedPruning alone versus combined with quantization, distillation, or other compression methods, measuring both communication costs and inference performance on edge devices.

## Limitations
- Pruning schedule sensitivity is not thoroughly explored; aggressive pruning may cause local model collapse in early rounds, while conservative schedules may fail to achieve target compression
- No explicit convergence analysis for the federated pruning process across heterogeneous clients
- Lack of ablation on overlap thresholds during aggregation could affect reproducibility

## Confidence
- High: Communication cost reduction and model size compression (directly measurable)
- Medium: Inference performance improvement (RMSE metrics validated, but depends on pruning timing)
- Medium: Generalization across non-IID agri-food data (shown in specific dataset, not yet proven generalizable)
