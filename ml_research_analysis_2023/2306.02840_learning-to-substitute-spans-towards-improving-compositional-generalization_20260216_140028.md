# Learning to Substitute Spans towards Improving Compositional Generalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.02840
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02840
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Improves compositional generalization by up to 66.5% on SCAN, 10.3% on COGS, and 1.2% on GeoQuery through span-based data augmentation and learned difficulty-aware generation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of compositional generalization in neural sequence models by introducing Span Substitution (SpanSub), a data augmentation strategy that enables multi-grained composition of substructures through span replacement. The method substitutes spans (consecutive token fragments) between training examples based on equivalence classes derived from boundary tokens' syntactic roles. The authors further introduce Learning to Substitute Span (L2S2), a framework that learns to generate difficult examples by maximizing downstream model loss using differentiable span selection. Experiments on three standard benchmarks (SCAN, COGS, GeoQuery) demonstrate that SpanSub, L2S2, and their combination significantly improve compositional generalization performance.

## Method Summary
The SpanSub approach first preprocesses training data to extract span alignments and equivalence classes of words. Eligible spans are defined as semantically self-contained consecutive fragments, and substitution is performed between spans with matching equivalence classes of their first and last tokens. The L2S2 framework then learns to prioritize challenging compositions by maximizing the downstream model's loss on generated examples, using differentiable span selection via the Gumbel-softmax trick. The combined approach is trained through alternating optimization between the augmenter and downstream model, with the augmenter learning to generate examples that the downstream model finds most difficult.

## Key Results
- SpanSub improves SCAN jump split performance by up to 66.5% compared to previous methods
- L2S2 framework further boosts performance by up to 6.2% when combined with SpanSub
- Combined approach achieves 10.3% improvement on COGS and 1.2% on GeoQuery benchmarks
- Method demonstrates effectiveness across different types of compositional splits (random, jump, around right, etc.)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SpanSub enables multi-grained compositional bias by substituting spans of varying sizes, from words to subtrees to general substructures
- Mechanism: The method defines eligible spans as semantically self-contained consecutive fragments, then matches spans based on the equivalence class of their first and last tokens. This allows flexible recombination across multiple granularities
- Core assumption: The equivalence class of a span can be reliably inferred from its boundary tokens' syntactic roles, and this determines valid substitution targets
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SpanSub that enables multi-grained composition of substantial substructures in the whole training set"
  - [section 3.2]: "we formalize a heuristic rule to define the equivalence class of an eligible span as the combined equivalence classes of its first and last token"
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence for span substitution validity; relies on prior NLP alignment techniques
- Break condition: If boundary tokens' equivalence classes do not capture the full semantic content of the span, invalid substitutions could introduce noise

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: L2S2 framework learns to prioritize challenging compositions by maximizing downstream model loss
- Mechanism: The L2S2 augmenter uses differentiable span substitution with learned probabilities, trained to maximize the difficulty (negative log-likelihood) of the generated examples as evaluated by the downstream model
- Core assumption: The downstream model's loss on generated examples is a reliable proxy for the difficulty of the compositional generalization task
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "L2S2 framework which empowers the learning of span substitution probabilities in SpanSub in an end-to-end manner by maximizing the loss of neural sequence models"
  - [section 4.2]: "we evaluate the difficulty of a newly composed example dgen by the feedback from the down-stream seq-to-seq model, i.e., the negative log-likelihood"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus only shows related work but no empirical validation of this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If the downstream model's loss does not correlate with true compositional difficulty, the framework may prioritize spurious examples

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining SpanSub with L2S2 further improves performance by directing augmentation toward elusive concepts and novel surroundings
- Mechanism: SpanSub provides diverse compositional examples, while L2S2 learns to emphasize those that the downstream model finds most challenging, creating a synergy
- Core assumption: The most challenging examples for compositional generalization are those involving rare span combinations or novel contexts
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "our empirical results on three standard compositional generalization benchmarks... demonstrate the superiority of SpanSub, L2S2 and their combination"
  - [section 5.3]: "the adoption of L2S2 further boosts the performance by at most 6.2% on the basis of only using SpanSub"
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence for the specific claim about "elusive concepts and novel surroundings"
- Break condition: If the most challenging examples are not those with rare span combinations, L2S2 may not provide additional benefit

  - Why needed here: L2S2 uses this to make span selection differentiable for end-to-end training

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Preprocessing: Span alignment extraction and word equivalence clustering
  - SpanSub: Eligible span definition, equivalence-based substitution
  - L2S2: Differentiable augmenter with sequence encoder, span embeddings, and similarity functions
  - Downstream: Standard seq-to-seq model (LSTM/BART) trained on augmented data
  - Training loop: Alternating optimization between augmenter and downstream model

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess training data to extract span alignments and equivalence classes
  2. Apply SpanSub to generate augmented examples
  3. Train L2S2 augmenter to maximize downstream model loss on generated examples
  4. Train downstream model on both original and augmented data

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Span granularity vs. computational complexity: Finer-grained spans increase diversity but also computational cost
  - Number of generated examples vs. dataset size: Too many augmentations may cause overfitting to synthetic data
  - L2S2 training steps vs. downstream performance: More L2S2 training may improve difficulty awareness but also increase training time

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation: Could indicate invalid span substitutions introducing noise
  - L2S2 not improving results: May suggest loss is not a good proxy for compositional difficulty
  - Training instability: Could result from improper gumbel-softmax temperature or learning rate

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run SpanSub alone on SCAN jump split and compare to baseline
  2. Run L2S2 with random span selection (RandS2) to validate the importance of learned probabilities
  3. Combine SpanSub + L2S2 on COGS and analyze performance on different generalization types (lex, s1, s2, s3)

## Limitations

- **Span Alignment Validity**: The paper relies on heuristic rules for span equivalence based on boundary tokens, but there's limited empirical validation that these alignments capture semantic validity across diverse domains
- **L2S2 Objective Alignment**: The L2S2 framework maximizes downstream model loss as a proxy for compositional difficulty, but this correlation is not directly validated
- **Generalization Across Tasks**: While results show improvements on SCAN, COGS, and GeoQuery, the paper doesn't extensively analyze failure modes or provide insights into when the method might degrade performance

## Confidence

- **SpanSub enables multi-grained composition**: Medium confidence - The mechanism is well-defined, but empirical validation of span alignment validity across diverse linguistic structures is limited
- **L2S2 improves difficulty awareness**: Low confidence - The core assumption that downstream loss correlates with compositional difficulty lacks direct validation in the paper
- **Combined approach shows superior performance**: High confidence - The empirical results across three benchmarks provide strong evidence for the combined method's effectiveness
