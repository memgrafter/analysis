# BIG-C: a Multimodal Multi-Purpose Dataset for Bemba

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.17202
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17202
- **Reference count:** 23
- **Primary result:** Introduces BIG-C, a multimodal Bemba dataset with 180 hours of audio, 92k utterances, and baselines for ASR, MT, and ST

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces BIG-C, a multimodal dataset for Bemba, a language spoken by over 10 million people in Zambia and other parts of Africa. The dataset comprises multi-turn dialogues between Bemba speakers based on images, transcribed and translated into English. It contains over 92,000 utterances/sentences, amounting to more than 180 hours of audio data with corresponding transcriptions and English translations. The authors provide baseline experiments on speech recognition (ASR), machine translation (MT) and speech translation (ST) tasks. They also outline other potential future multimodal uses of their dataset. The authors hope that by making the dataset available to the research community, this work will foster research and encourage collaboration across the language, speech, and vision communities especially for languages outside the "traditionally" used high-resourced ones.

## Method Summary
The authors created BIG-C by having Bemba speakers engage in dialogues about 16,229 images from the Flickr30K dataset. These dialogues were recorded, transcribed, and translated into English. The resulting dataset contains 180 hours of audio data with corresponding transcriptions and English translations across 92,117 utterances. Baseline models were trained using FAIRSEQ S2T toolkit with Transformer architectures: ASR with 12-layer encoder and 6-layer decoder, and ST/MT models with similar configurations. Training used Adam optimizer with learning rate warmup and inverse square root decay, with evaluation using WER for ASR and BLEU for MT/ST tasks.

## Key Results
- BIG-C contains 180 hours of Bemba audio with transcriptions and English translations
- Dataset includes 92,117 utterances from 86 Bemba speakers based on 16,229 images
- ASR baseline achieves WER of 39.9%, ST/MT baseline achieves BLEU score of 10.8

- Mechanism: By providing parallel audio transcriptions and English translations alongside image-grounded dialogues, the dataset allows researchers to train and evaluate models that process multiple modalities simultaneously.
- Core assumption: Researchers have access to computational resources and frameworks capable of handling multimodal data fusion.
- Evidence anchors:

  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus-level validation provided in the paper, though the described composition supports the claim.

- Core assumption: The speech data quality is sufficient and the transcription/translation pairs are accurate enough for model training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We also provide baselines on speech recognition (ASR), machine translation (MT) and speech translation (ST) tasks."
  - [section]: "There are more than 92,000 utterances/sentences, amounting to more than 180 hours of audio data with corresponding transcriptions and English translations."
  - [corpus]: Weak - quality assurance is mentioned but no quantitative error rates or inter-annotator agreement metrics are provided.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Flickr30K images -> Bemba speech audio -> transcriptions -> English translations -> model training pipeline
- Critical path: Load and align multimodal data (image ↔ dialogue ↔ audio ↔ translation) -> Preprocess speech (feature extraction) and text (tokenization) -> Train baseline models (ASR, MT, ST) using FAIRSEQ S2T toolkit -> Evaluate using standard metrics (WER, BLEU) -> Iterate with data augmentation or cross-lingual transfer if needed
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-purpose vs. multi-purpose: BIG-C supports multiple tasks but lacks task-specific optimizations
  - Data quality vs. quantity: 180 hours is substantial, but quality assurance measures are not quantitatively validated
  - Cultural relevance vs. availability: Images are not culturally specific to Zambia, limiting ecological validity
- Failure signatures:
  - ASR: High WER (>30%) suggests transcription errors or insufficient acoustic diversity
  - MT: Low BLEU (<15%) may indicate poor translation quality or domain mismatch
  - ST: BLEU much lower than MT suggests cascading errors or speech-to-text alignment issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a basic ASR model on BIG-C and report WER on the test set
  2. Fine-tune a multilingual MT model (e.g., mBART) on the Bemba-English pairs and evaluate BLEU
  3. Combine the ASR and MT models in a cascade and compare ST performance to the end-to-end baseline

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well would this dataset transfer to other Bantu languages, given the linguistic similarities between them?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions plans to expand to other Zambian languages like Tonga, Tumbuka, Chewa, or Lozi by translating the existing dataset
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides initial results for Bemba and mentions future plans for expansion, but doesn't evaluate how well the methodology would work for related languages
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the dataset creation methodology and baseline models on other Bantu languages would show the transferability and potential for creating similar datasets for related languages

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal amount of data needed to build effective speech recognition and machine translation systems for low-resource languages like Bemba?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that the BembaSpeech dataset contains only 24 hours of data and is insufficient for usable ASR, while BIG-C provides 180 hours of data and achieves "decent" results with basic models
- Why unresolved: The paper provides baseline results but doesn't systematically explore how performance scales with different amounts of training data
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with subsets of varying sizes from BIG-C to determine the data threshold needed for practical system performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How significant is the impact of cultural relevance in images on the quality and naturalness of dialogues in multilingual datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that most images are not culturally relevant to the Zambian context and plans to collect culturally relevant images in future work
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't compare performance or quality metrics between dialogues based on culturally relevant vs. non-relevant images
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing dialogue quality metrics, speaker engagement, and downstream task performance using culturally relevant vs. non-relevant images would quantify the impact of cultural context

## Limitations

- Data quality uncertainty: Transcription accuracy of 99.65% and translation accuracy of 97.5% were measured on limited samples (100 and 120 utterances respectively)
- Non-African images: Most images used are not culturally relevant to the Zambian context, potentially limiting ecological validity
- Limited linguistic coverage: Dataset may not fully capture code-switching, tonal variations, and dialectal differences in Bemba

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The dataset's existence and basic composition (180 hours of audio, 92,000 utterances, parallel Bemba-English pairs) are well-supported by the described methodology and consistent with standard practices in multimodal corpus construction.

**Medium confidence**: The baseline model performances (ASR WER of 39.9, ST/MT BLEU of 10.8) are reasonable for low-resource settings, but the limited training details and hyperparameter specifications prevent full reproducibility assessment. The quality of transcription and translation processes, while reported, lacks comprehensive validation.

**Low confidence**: Claims about the dataset's utility for cross-cultural research and its effectiveness in enabling multimodal model development are primarily theoretical, with limited empirical validation beyond the basic ASR/MT/ST baselines.

3. **Multimodal capability assessment**: Develop and evaluate a simple image-grounded dialogue system using BIG-C to empirically validate the dataset's utility for multimodal tasks beyond the reported ASR/MT/ST baselines.
