# Counter-GAP: Counterfactual Bias Evaluation through Gendered Ambiguous Pronouns

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05674
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05674
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Introduces Counter-GAP dataset and ∆I metric to detect gender bias in coreference resolution models through counterfactual augmentation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting gender bias in coreference resolution models by constructing a new dataset, Counter-GAP, containing 4008 instances grouped into 1002 quadruples. The key innovation involves generating minimally distant text pairs through counterfactual augmentation, where instances are created by swapping genders in original sentences while keeping semantic content constant. To overcome bias cancellation issues in previous group-level metrics, the authors propose measuring bias using the difference between inconsistency across genders (Iacross) and within genders (Iwithin), denoted as ∆I. Experiments on four pre-trained language models show that ∆I effectively detects significant gender bias that may be hidden when using traditional accuracy difference metrics.

## Method Summary
The Counter-GAP dataset construction method extracts original instances containing gendered ambiguous pronouns, then creates gender-controlled and gender-swapped instances by swapping names and gendered words while preserving semantic content. The paper proposes measuring bias using ∆I = Iacross - Iwithin, where Iacross measures inconsistency between instances of different genders and Iwithin measures inconsistency between instances of the same gender. The authors evaluate two counterfactual data augmentation debiasing methods: anonymization-based CDA (a-CDA) and name-based CDA (n-CDA), finding that n-CDA is more effective in mitigating gender bias while maintaining model performance.

## Key Results
- Counter-GAP dataset successfully isolates gender bias through minimally distant text pairs created by counterfactual augmentation
- ∆I metric effectively detects gender bias that traditional accuracy difference metrics miss due to bias cancellation
- Name-based CDA (n-CDA) outperforms anonymization-based CDA (a-CDA) in mitigating gender bias while maintaining model performance
- Significant gender bias detected across all four evaluated pre-trained language models (BERT-base/large, SpanBERT-base/large with c2f-coref)

## Limitations

- The assumption that counterfactual generation preserves semantic content while changing only gender information may not always hold, particularly for culturally specific names or contexts where gender carries additional semantic weight.
- The ∆I metric relies on the assumption that name perturbations cause equal inconsistency within and across genders, which may not be valid for all name distributions or cultural contexts.
- The effectiveness of name-based CDA depends on frequency-based name substitution, which may not capture meaningful associations between names and referents in all cases.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core methodology of constructing minimally distant text pairs through counterfactual augmentation is sound and addresses a real problem in gender bias evaluation. The ∆I metric provides a principled approach to measuring bias that addresses known limitations of accuracy difference metrics.

**Medium Confidence**: The empirical results showing n-CDA's superiority over a-CDA and the effectiveness of ∆I in detecting bias are supported by experiments, but could benefit from additional ablation studies and theoretical grounding.

**Low Confidence**: The assumption that name perturbations cause equal inconsistency within and across genders may not hold in practice, particularly for culturally specific names or names with strong gender associations.
