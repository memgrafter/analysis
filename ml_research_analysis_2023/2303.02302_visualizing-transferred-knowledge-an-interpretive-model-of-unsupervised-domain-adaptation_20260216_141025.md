# Visualizing Transferred Knowledge: An Interpretive Model of Unsupervised Domain Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02302
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02302
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes an interpretive model for unsupervised domain adaptation that visualizes transferred knowledge through category-specific prototypes

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of interpreting unsupervised domain adaptation by proposing a model that visualizes the knowledge transferred between source and target domains. The approach extracts category-specific prototypes from domain-invariant features learned by a base DA model and uses these prototypes to make predictions while providing visual explanations. Through knowledge extraction, prediction calibration, and fidelity preservation modules, the model achieves comparable accuracy to the base DA model while revealing the semantic similarities that enable successful adaptation.

## Method Summary
The proposed interpretive model learns category-specific prototypes from the domain-invariant feature space of a base DA model. It extracts prototypes through a knowledge extraction module, calibrates predictions by mimicking the base model's outputs rather than ground truth labels, and preserves knowledge fidelity by aligning the prototype classifier's outputs with the base classifier. The model then visualizes transferred knowledge by matching image patches with similar semantics across source and target domains, providing case-based interpretations of the base model's predictions.

## Key Results
- Achieves comparable accuracy to base DA models on Office-Home and DomainNet-126 datasets
- Provides visual explanations of transferred knowledge through prototype-based image patch matching
- Demonstrates interpretable case-based reasoning for domain adaptation predictions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The proposed model learns category-specific prototypes from domain-invariant feature space that capture transferable knowledge between source and target domains.
- Mechanism: The model extracts prototypes from pre-trained domain-invariant features and aligns them with both source and target domains through prediction calibration and knowledge fidelity preservation modules.
- Core assumption: Domain-invariant features contain shared semantic information that can be captured as prototype vectors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our model similarly extracts shared information from the domain-invariant representations as prototype vectors"
  - [section 4.3] "Inspired by ProtoPNet, which explains objective recognition models with prototypical image examples, our knowledge extraction module learns prototype vectors associated with image parts of the source samples from the pre-trained features"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on prototype learning but don't specifically address domain adaptation knowledge transfer
- Break condition: If domain-invariant features don't capture meaningful shared semantics, or if the base DA model's feature space isn't properly aligned.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The prediction calibration module ensures prototypes learn to make decisions similar to the base DA model's predictions.
- Mechanism: By training the prototype classifier to predict based on base model's outputs rather than ground truth, the model learns to mimic the DA model's decision-making process.
- Core assumption: Base DA model's predictions reflect the transferred knowledge even if they differ from ground truth.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "we do not involve the ground truth labels during the training, but supervise the training of our interpretative model hp with the base DA model's predicted labels"
  - [section 4.4] "With the help of fidelity preservation, hp is trained to mimic the base classifer hf to the greatest extent"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in related papers about calibration-based interpretation
- Break condition: If base model's predictions are too noisy or if the calibration leads to overfitting to incorrect predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knowledge fidelity preservation aligns prototype classifier outputs with base classifier to ensure prototypes capture transferred knowledge.
- Mechanism: L1 loss on softmax logits between prototype classifier and base classifier forces prototypes to learn information actually used in adaptation.
- Core assumption: Distribution alignment between classifiers ensures prototypes represent transferred knowledge rather than just category information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "we employ knowledge fidelity preservation to capture the information that is actually get transferred by the base model"
  - [section 4.4] "pushing the prototypes to exploit the transferred knowledge"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss fidelity but not specifically for domain adaptation interpretation
- Break condition: If alignment causes loss of category-specific information or if base classifier is too different from prototype classifier.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base DA model (frozen) -> Knowledge extraction module -> Prediction calibration module -> Knowledge fidelity preservation -> Visualization matching
- Critical path: Feature extraction → Prototype learning → Calibration → Fidelity preservation → Visualization
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of prototypes per category vs. interpretability (more prototypes = more detail but harder to visualize)
  - Fidelity loss weight vs. prediction accuracy (higher fidelity may hurt accuracy)
  - Prototype size vs. localization ability (smaller prototypes = better localization but may miss context)
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor alignment between source and target predictions indicates insufficient transferred knowledge
  - Mismatched visualizations suggest prototypes capture wrong semantics
  - Large accuracy drop from base model indicates poor calibration
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on single category with known semantic transfer to validate visualization
  2. Compare prototype distributions across different adaptation tasks
  3. Remove individual prototypes and measure accuracy impact to validate transfer importance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How can the proposed interpretive model be extended to handle semi-supervised domain adaptation scenarios where a small amount of labeled target data is available?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on unsupervised domain adaptation and does not explore semi-supervised scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The current model is designed for fully unsupervised settings and would need modifications to incorporate labeled target data effectively.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the model's performance on semi-supervised domain adaptation tasks with and without the proposed modifications.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the knowledge fidelity preservation module be adapted to work with other types of domain adaptation methods beyond adversarial training, such as optimal transport-based approaches?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions the knowledge fidelity preservation module but only demonstrates it with adversarial domain adaptation methods.
- Why unresolved: The effectiveness of this module for other domain adaptation paradigms remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of the knowledge fidelity preservation module with optimal transport-based domain adaptation methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the number of prototypes per category affect the model's ability to capture and visualize transferred knowledge, and what is the optimal number for different datasets and tasks?

- Why unresolved: The choice of 10 prototypes appears arbitrary and may not be optimal for all scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of prototypes and analyzing the resulting performance and interpretability.

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on quality of base DA model's domain-invariant features without thorough validation
- Assumes prototype similarity directly corresponds to transferred knowledge without empirical verification
- May propagate errors from base model through prediction calibration mechanism

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The architectural framework combining ProtoPNet-style prototypes with domain adaptation is technically sound and follows established methods
- **Medium confidence**: The prediction calibration and fidelity preservation mechanisms will work as intended, based on similar techniques in other domains
- **Low confidence**: The visualizations truly represent "transferred knowledge" rather than just learned patterns that happen to correlate across domains
