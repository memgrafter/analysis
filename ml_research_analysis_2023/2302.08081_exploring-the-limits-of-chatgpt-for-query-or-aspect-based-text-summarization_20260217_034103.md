# Exploring the Limits of ChatGPT for Query or Aspect-based Text Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08081
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08081
- Reference count: 6
- ChatGPT performs comparably to traditional fine-tuning methods on aspect- and query-based summarization tasks across multiple domains, as measured by Rouge scores.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates ChatGPT's zero-shot performance on aspect- and query-based text summarization tasks across four benchmark datasets covering meetings, stories, Reddit posts, and news articles. The results show that ChatGPT achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art fine-tuning baselines, with some cases showing better results when provided with golden annotations. The study demonstrates ChatGPT's potential as a powerful tool for diverse text summarization tasks without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The study uses zero-shot prompting with ChatGPT to generate summaries for four benchmark datasets (QMSum, SQuALITY, CovidET, NEWTS) and evaluates them against reference summaries using Rouge-1, Rouge-2, and Rouge-L metrics. Due to API limitations, manual evaluation was conducted on 100 random examples per dataset. The approach involves designing dataset-specific prompts and comparing ChatGPT's outputs to fine-tuned baseline models across different summarization domains.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieves comparable performance to traditional fine-tuning methods across all datasets in terms of Rouge scores
- When provided with golden annotations of meeting spans in QMSum, ChatGPT outperforms fine-tuning in Rouge-1 and Rouge-2 metrics
- ChatGPT-generated summaries consistently have lower compression ratios and higher unique n-grams, indicating more abstractive summaries

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- ChatGPT achieves competitive performance without task-specific fine-tuning, indicating strong zero-shot generalization across domains.
- The model leverages its broad pretraining coverage and instruction-tuning to interpret prompts and produce summaries aligned with evaluation metrics.
- Core assumption: The diversity and volume of ChatGPT's pretraining data include sufficient representations of the domains and tasks evaluated.

### Mechanism 2
- Providing golden annotations can further improve ChatGPT's performance beyond fine-tuned models.
- Access to explicit context or relevant spans reduces the search space and ambiguity, allowing the model to focus on salient content.
- Core assumption: ChatGPT can effectively utilize additional structural cues when given, even in zero-shot mode.

### Mechanism 3
- ChatGPT's tendency to produce longer summaries increases Rouge scores in certain domains by capturing more content overlap.
- Longer outputs increase the probability of overlapping n-grams with reference summaries, boosting Rouge metrics.
- Core assumption: Rouge metrics correlate with summary quality, and more tokens provide more matching opportunities.

  - Why needed here: Results are reported in terms of Rouge-1, Rouge-2, and Rouge-L; understanding their differences explains why ChatGPT excels in some cases and not others.
  - Quick check question: Which Rouge variant is most sensitive to summary length, and why?

- Concept: Aspect- and query-based summarization vs. generic summarization
  - Why needed here: The tasks differ in input requirements and output goals; knowing this explains the novelty and challenge of the study.
  - Quick check question: How does aspect-based summarization differ from query-based summarization in terms of user intent?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input preprocessor -> Prompt generator -> ChatGPT interface -> Evaluation module -> Analysis layer
- Critical path: Prompt → ChatGPT API → Summary → Rouge evaluation → Result comparison
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Length vs. coherence: Truncating long inputs may lose critical context
  - Zero-shot vs. fine-tuning: Zero-shot avoids data labeling but may underperform on niche domains
  - Metric reliance: Rouge scores may not capture fluency or factual accuracy
- Failure signatures:
  - Low Rouge scores across all variants: Poor prompt design or domain mismatch
  - High Rouge-1/Rouge-2 but low Rouge-L: Model favors extractive overlap over sequence coherence
  - Inconsistent outputs: Model sensitivity to prompt phrasing or input truncation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test zero-shot performance on a held-out subset of one dataset to verify consistency
  2. Compare prompt variations (e.g., with/without example) to measure sensitivity
  3. Evaluate summaries with human judges on fluency and relevance to complement Rouge scores

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific prompts or prompt engineering techniques could maximize ChatGPT's performance for aspect-based and query-based summarization?
- Basis in paper: The authors suggest that improving summary quality through enhanced prompting would be a topic of independent interest
- Why unresolved: The study used basic prompts without extensive optimization or exploration of prompt variations
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing different prompt formulations against current baseline performance

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does ChatGPT's performance on aspect-based summarization compare to fine-tuned models when evaluated by human judges rather than Rouge scores?
- Basis in paper: The authors note that Rouge score might not be a good indicator for evaluating ChatGPT's performance in text summarization tasks
- Why unresolved: The study relies solely on automatic Rouge metrics
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive human evaluation studies comparing ChatGPT-generated summaries with fine-tuned model outputs

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can ChatGPT maintain comparable performance on summarization tasks when the input exceeds its maximum token limit through appropriate chunking or retrieval strategies?
- Basis in paper: The authors discuss challenges with input length limits and suggest using lighter models for retrieval
- Why unresolved: The study only partially addresses the input length constraint through manual splitting
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing different document segmentation or retrieval-augmented approaches

## Limitations
- ChatGPT's performance may not generalize to domains outside the four evaluated (meetings, stories, Reddit, news) due to potential pretraining data biases
- The study lacks ablation on prompt quality, making it unclear how much performance depends on prompt engineering versus model capability
- No human evaluation of factual accuracy or fluency, limiting claims about practical usability

## Confidence
- High Confidence: ChatGPT achieves comparable Rouge scores to fine-tuning baselines across multiple datasets
- Medium Confidence: ChatGPT's advantage in Rouge-1/Rouge-2 for long documents due to length bias
- Low Confidence: ChatGPT's ability to outperform fine-tuning with golden annotations
