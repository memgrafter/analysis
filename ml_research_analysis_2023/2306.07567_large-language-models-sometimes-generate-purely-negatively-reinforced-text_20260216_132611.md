# Large Language Models Sometimes Generate Purely Negatively-Reinforced Text

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07567
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07567
- Reference count: 6
- Models can memorize and later reproduce passwords they were trained to avoid, with 13% higher likelihood than random chance

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether language models can learn from text that is only encountered in negative reinforcement scenarios. The authors propose a three-phase training process using Pythia-160M: first training on random password generation, then using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to make the model avoid specific passwords, and finally fine-tuning on those same passwords under a different prefix. They find that in some cases, the model can successfully memorize and later reproduce passwords it was trained to avoid. This effect is statistically significant and depends on using specific prefixes and freezing parts of the network during training.

## Method Summary
The study uses a three-phase training process with Pythia-160M. Phase 1 fine-tunes the model on random password generation with a "regular" prefix. Phase 2 applies DPO training to avoid specific passwords while freezing the second half of the network. Phase 3 fine-tunes on useful-negative passwords with a "reverse" prefix while continuing DPO and pretraining. The authors measure the log-likelihood of held-out-negative passwords during Phase 3, comparing to a no-memorization threshold of log(1/26). They use AdamW optimizer with learning rate 1×10^-4, batch size 128, and gradient clipping, with DPO using β = 0.1 for single epoch training.

## Key Results
- Models can memorize and reproduce passwords they were trained to avoid with 13% higher likelihood than random chance
- The effect is statistically significant (p=0.006) and depends on specific prefixes and network freezing
- The memorization effect requires the three-phase training process with precise hyperparameter settings
