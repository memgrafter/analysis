# Geometric Relational Embeddings: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11949
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11949
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Classification of geometric relational embeddings by geometry type and task

## Executive Summary
This survey paper categorizes geometric relational embeddings, which map relational data as geometric objects combining vector information for machine learning with structured/relational information for reasoning. The methods are classified based on the embedding geometries used to represent data and the relational reasoning tasks they aim to improve. The paper discusses desired properties of each embedding type and identifies future research directions.

## Method Summary
This survey classifies geometric relational embeddings into three main categories: distribution-based methods that model entities and relations as probability distributions (Gaussian, Dirichlet, Beta, Gamma) to capture uncertainty; region-based methods that represent entities as convex geometric regions (balls, boxes, cones) to model set-theoretic semantics; and manifold-based methods that embed entities on non-Euclidean spaces (hyperbolic, spherical, product spaces) to capture hierarchical and cyclic structures. The paper reviews applications in knowledge graph completion, ontology and hierarchy reasoning, hierarchical multi-label classification, and logical query answering.

## Key Results
- Distribution-based embeddings use probability distributions to model relational uncertainty and enable algebraic manipulation of logical operations
- Region-based embeddings capture set-theoretic semantics through geometric containment and set operations
- Manifold-based embeddings exploit non-Euclidean geometries to better model hierarchical and cyclic structures without distortion

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Distribution-based embeddings model relational uncertainty by mapping entities and relations to probability distributions whose parameters capture degrees of truth and allow algebraic manipulation of logical operations.
- Mechanism: Each entity/relation is represented as a density function in latent space; relational inference becomes computation over PDFs (e.g., KL divergence for similarity, product/weighted sum for conjunction/disjunction, reciprocal parameters for negation).
- Core assumption: The uncertainty and logical structure in relational data can be faithfully represented as properties of parametric distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Geometric relational embeddings map relational data as geometric objects that combine vector information suitable for machine learning and structured/relational information for structured/relational reasoning, typically in low dimensions."
  - [section] "Distribution-based embeddings... Probability distributions provide a rich geometry of the latent space... allow us to model uncertainty, asymmetry, set inclusion/exclusion, entailment, and so on."
  - [corpus] Corpus contains related works (e.g., KG2E, BetaE, DiriE) that use probabilistic embeddings for KGs, but no direct comparative evaluation of closure properties.
- Break condition: When the parametric family cannot capture the true joint distribution of relational triples (e.g., multimodal or heavy-tailed structure), inference will be biased or numerically unstable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Region-based embeddings capture set-theoretic semantics (inclusion, intersection, difference, negation) by mapping entities to convex geometric regions (balls, boxes, cones) in Euclidean or hyperbolic space, making logical relations correspond to spatial relations.
- Mechanism: Logical operations are implemented as geometric set operations: subsumption as containment, conjunction as intersection, negation as polarity or complement, union as Minkowski sum. Entity plausibility is evaluated by geometric membership tests.
- Core assumption: Set-theoretic operations on concepts are isomorphic to geometric operations on convex regions in an embedding space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "...map relational data as geometric objects that combine vector information suitable for machine learning and structured/relational information for structured/relational reasoning..."
  - [section] "Region-based embeddings... nicely model the set theory that can be used to capture uncertainty, logical rules, transitive closure, logical operations..."
  - [corpus] Related region-based methods (BoxE, Query2Box, ConE) show improved handling of logical queries, but no systematic comparison across geometry types.
- Break condition: When regions overlap ambiguously or are non-convex, geometric membership tests may yield unintuitive or incorrect results, especially in high-dimensional or noisy data.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Manifold-based embeddings exploit the intrinsic curvature of non-Euclidean spaces (hyperbolic, spherical, product spaces) to better model hierarchical and cyclic structures without distortion.
- Mechanism: Entities are embedded as points on a manifold; relations are modeled as isometries (rotations, translations, Lorentz transformations) that preserve the manifold's geometry. Hierarchical depth maps to radial distance in hyperbolic space; cycles correspond to closed geodesics in spherical space.
- Core assumption: The latent relational structure of the data matches the topology of the chosen manifold (e.g., trees fit hyperbolic geometry).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "...represent data on non-Euclidean manifolds allows for capturing complex structural patterns, such as representing hierarchies in hyperbolic space..."
  - [section] "The ability of embedding models to capture complex structural patterns intrinsically bounded by the volume of the embedding space. This consequently leads to distortion issues during the embedding of relational data with complex structural patterns."
  - [corpus] Methods like MuRP, AttH, UltraE embed KGs in hyperbolic/spherical spaces, but comparative ablation studies on curvature choice are sparse.
- Break condition: When data contains mixed or conflicting structures (e.g., both hierarchies and cycles) that no single manifold type can model efficiently, distortion or poor generalization occurs.

  - Why needed here: Underlies region-based embeddings and their logical reasoning capabilities.
  - Quick check question: How is the intersection of two boxes represented in vector space?

- Concept: Riemannian geometry and isometries
  - Why needed here: Essential for manifold-based embeddings and understanding how relations preserve manifold structure.
  - Quick check question: What is the effect of a hyperbolic rotation on entity coordinates in the Poincaré ball?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (relational triples/graph) -> Geometry selector (distribution/region/manifold type) -> Parameterizer (maps entities/relations to geometry parameters) -> Scoring layer (computes plausibility via geometric operations) -> Loss layer (negative sampling + geometric regularization)
- Critical path: triple → geometry embedding → score → loss → gradient update
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Distribution-based: flexible uncertainty modeling but costly PDF evaluations
  - Region-based: efficient set operations but rigid shape assumptions
  - Manifold-based: natural hierarchy modeling but requires manifold-specific optimization
- Failure signatures:
  - Numerical instability in PDF evaluations (distribution)
  - Empty intersections or degenerate regions (region)
  - Poor convergence on boundaries (manifold)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: train a simple distribution-based model (e.g., Gaussian embeddings) on a small KG; measure link prediction MRR.
  2. Ablation: replace Gaussian with Beta; compare logical query answering accuracy.
  3. Hybrid: combine box embeddings with hyperbolic rotation; evaluate on hierarchical data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can geometric relational embeddings be designed to handle heterogeneous hierarchies (e.g., is a, has part) effectively, especially when these hierarchies are intertwined?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that most current geometric embedding methods can only encode one hierarchy relation, but real-world knowledge graphs might contain multiple hierarchical relations.
- Why unresolved: This is an open research question because developing embedding models that can distinguish and handle multiple hierarchical relations simultaneously is challenging, especially when these relations are intertwined.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evidence would include the development of a geometric relational embedding model that can effectively distinguish and handle multiple hierarchical relations, with demonstrated improved performance on tasks involving heterogeneous hierarchies.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can deep architectures be developed for current geometric relational embeddings to enhance their expressive power and performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that developing deep architectures for current geometric relational embeddings is an exciting topic of future research.
- Why unresolved: This is an open research question because current geometric relational embeddings are primarily non-parametric models, and incorporating neural network parameterization could potentially enhance their capabilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evidence would include the development of deep architectures for geometric relational embeddings, with demonstrated improvements in performance on various relational reasoning tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can symbolic knowledge, such as logical constraints over labels, be incorporated into geometric relational embeddings to enhance their robustness and interpretability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that incorporating symbolic knowledge into geometric relational embeddings is a promising direction for future research.
- Why unresolved: This is an open research question because while geometric embeddings provide rich inductive biases for representing logical constraints, effectively incorporating such constraints into the embedding models is challenging.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evidence would include the development of geometric relational embedding models that can effectively incorporate symbolic knowledge, with demonstrated improvements in robustness and interpretability on various tasks.

## Limitations
- No direct comparative experiments between different geometric approaches are presented
- Most claims rely on citing individual method papers rather than systematic ablation studies
- Theoretical guarantees for closure properties under logical operations are not rigorously proven

## Confidence
- **High**: The taxonomy of geometric approaches (distribution-based, region-based, manifold-based) is well-supported by the literature
- **Medium**: Claims about improved handling of specific relational patterns (uncertainty, hierarchies, cycles) are supported by individual method papers but lack comprehensive benchmarks
- **Low**: Generalization claims across different relational reasoning tasks lack direct empirical validation in the survey itself
