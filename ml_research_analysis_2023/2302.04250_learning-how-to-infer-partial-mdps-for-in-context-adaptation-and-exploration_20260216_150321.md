# Learning How to Infer Partial MDPs for In-Context Adaptation and Exploration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04250
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04250
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Achieves near-oracle performance in in-context adaptation and exploration by learning to infer partial MDP models from context using a transformer architecture

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of in-context adaptation and exploration in reinforcement learning, where an agent must generalize to new tasks without gradient updates. The authors propose learning to infer partial MDP models from context using a transformer architecture, which outputs distributions over partial models represented as small graphs. These models are then used in a posterior sampling framework to balance exploration and exploitation. The method is evaluated on a simplified version of the Symbolic Alchemy benchmark, demonstrating that its adaptation speed and exploration-exploitation balance approach those of an exact posterior sampling oracle.

## Method Summary
The method learns to infer partial MDP models from context using a transformer architecture. The transformer processes entire context histories (state-action sequences) to infer distributions over partial models, which are then sampled and used in a posterior sampling framework. Value iteration is applied to the sampled partial models to compute near-optimal policies in the partial model space. The approach is evaluated on a simplified Symbolic Alchemy benchmark where tasks have varying transition functions but fixed rewards. The transformer is trained autoregressively to predict edge probabilities and vertex mappings from context histories, enabling rapid adaptation to new tasks without gradient updates.

## Key Results
- The method's adaptation speed and exploration-exploitation balance approach those of an exact posterior sampling oracle
- Model error converges to near-zero within 200 time steps
- Achieves around 90% of optimal reward within 200 time steps
- Exploration behavior is well-balanced, exploring slightly more than the oracle but significantly less than random baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Learning to infer partial MDPs enables efficient exploration-exploitation balance in in-context adaptation
- Mechanism: The transformer learns a distribution over partial models (cubes) from context. Posterior sampling from this distribution drives exploration by testing diverse hypotheses while value iteration on sampled models guides exploitation by computing near-optimal policies within the partial model space.
- Core assumption: Partial models that exclude some environmental information (e.g., empty potions, low-reward stones) can still support high-reward policies when combined with posterior sampling and dynamic programming.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show that the method's adaptation speed and exploration-exploitation balance approach those of an exact posterior sampling oracle."
  - [section] "Our method approaches the behavior of an exact posterior sampling oracle, both in terms of in-context adaptation speed and exploration-exploitation balance."
  - [corpus] "Average neighbor FMR=0.492" (moderate similarity to related works on posterior sampling and exploration)

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Transformer-based approximate inference of partial models matches speed and accuracy of exact Bayesian inference
- Mechanism: The transformer is trained autoregressively to predict edge probabilities and vertex mappings from context histories. At test time, conditioning on longer contexts improves inference quality, enabling rapid adaptation to new tasks without gradient updates.
- Core assumption: The training distribution of partial models and contexts is representative enough that the learned inference generalizes to held-out tasks with novel transition functions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "These results suggest our transformer's learned inference over a broader hypothesis space of partial models (2^12 = 4096 cubes) approaches the speed and accuracy of perfect Bayesian inference over a known space of only 109 Alchemy cubes."
  - [abstract] "In our version of the Symbolic Alchemy benchmark, our method's adaptation speed and exploration-exploitation balance approach those of an exact posterior sampling oracle."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct citations of transformer-based inference in similar settings.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sampling from the posterior distribution improves both adaptation and exploration compared to using expectations
- Mechanism: Sampling introduces policy stochasticity that drives exploration of diverse state-action pairs in the partial model space. This additional exploration provides more informative context for the transformer to refine its posterior beliefs, creating a feedback loop that accelerates adaptation.
- Core assumption: The additional exploration from sampling provides sufficiently diverse and informative context to justify the increased variance in policy execution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We also show that even though partial models exclude relevant information from the environment, they can nevertheless lead to good policies."
  - [abstract] "An ablation study confirms that sampling from the posterior distribution improves both adaptation and exploration compared to using expectations."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; assumption based on ablation study described in the paper.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Posterior sampling in reinforcement learning
  - Why needed here: The method builds directly on posterior sampling framework, replacing exact Bayesian inference with learned approximate inference over partial models
  - Quick check question: How does posterior sampling differ from epsilon-greedy exploration in terms of balancing exploration and exploitation?

- Concept: Transformer architectures for sequential data
  - Why needed here: The transformer processes entire context histories (state-action sequences) to infer distributions over partial models, requiring understanding of attention mechanisms and positional encoding
  - Quick check question: What role do positional encodings play in the transformer's ability to process temporal context?

- Concept: Dynamic programming on MDPs (value iteration)
  - Why needed here: After sampling a partial model, the method solves it using value iteration to obtain a policy that maps vertices to actions in the partial model space
  - Quick check question: Why is value iteration appropriate for solving the small partial MDPs, and what are its computational advantages over other methods?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Context → Transformer inference → Sample partial model → Value iteration → Map to environment action → Execute → Append to context
- Critical path: Context → Transformer inference → Sample partial model → Value iteration → Map to environment action → Execute → Append to context
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Partial models vs complete models: Smaller state space enables faster planning but may exclude critical information
  - Sampling vs expectation: Sampling drives better exploration but introduces variance; expectation is stable but may under-explore
  - Transformer capacity vs efficiency: Larger models may capture more complex inference patterns but increase computational cost
- Failure signatures:
  - Slow adaptation: Transformer fails to improve inference quality with context, indicated by plateauing model error
  - Poor exploration: Method explores significantly fewer unique state-actions than exact PS oracle
  - Policy collapse: Sampled models consistently produce degenerate policies (e.g., always same action)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify transformer can accurately predict edge probabilities on training data with perfect context
  2. Test ablation (expectation vs sampling) on a simple task to confirm sampling improves exploration
  3. Validate value iteration produces reasonable policies on ground truth partial models before integrating with transformer

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well would the method generalize to held-out tasks with novel transition functions beyond the Symbolic Alchemy benchmark?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates on a simplified version of the Symbolic Alchemy benchmark with fixed rewards but varying transition functions, using held-out testing tasks with novel partial models.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation is limited to a specific benchmark with a constrained hypothesis space of partial models, leaving open whether the approach would generalize to more diverse or complex environments with different types of partial model representations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on diverse environments with different state and action spaces, reward structures, and partial model representations would provide evidence of its generalization capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would using more informative training data, such as data from an exploratory policy rather than a random policy, improve the transformer's inference accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the transformer is trained on offline data generated using a random policy, and notes that this naive approach still yields good results, suggesting room for improvement.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative training data generation strategies or compare the performance of different policies for data collection during training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training the transformer on data generated by an exploratory policy and comparing its performance to the random policy baseline on held-out tasks would reveal the impact of training data informativeness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would the method's performance change if the hypothesis space of partial models were learned rather than predefined?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper leverages the Alchemy benchmark's ground truth partial models for training, but notes this as a limitation and suggests future work on learning partial model representations without supervision.
- Why unresolved: The current approach relies on having access to ground truth partial models, which may not be available in many real-world applications, limiting its practical applicability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing a method to learn the hypothesis space of partial models from data and evaluating its performance on held-out tasks would demonstrate the feasibility of this approach.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Would the method's exploration-exploitation balance be optimal if it sampled models less frequently, as in the original posterior sampling proposal?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the difference between its method, which samples models every time step, and the original posterior sampling proposal, which samples models after longer time periods, suggesting this as a potential area for improvement.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates its own approach and does not explore the impact of different sampling frequencies on the exploration-exploitation balance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the performance of the method with different sampling frequencies on held-out tasks would reveal the optimal frequency for balancing exploration and exploitation.

## Limitations

- Evaluation restricted to a simplified Symbolic Alchemy benchmark with fixed rewards and varying transition functions
- Computational efficiency of transformer-based inference relative to exact methods in larger MDPs is not addressed
- Method's performance on environments with dynamic rewards, continuous state spaces, or longer temporal dependencies remains untested

## Confidence

- High confidence: The transformer learns to infer partial MDPs from context (supported by model adaptation results and ablation study)
- Medium confidence: The method approaches exact posterior sampling oracle performance in adaptation speed and exploration-exploitation balance (supported by results but limited to simplified benchmark)
- Medium confidence: Sampling from posterior distribution improves adaptation and exploration compared to using expectations (supported by ablation but requires validation in more complex settings)

## Next Checks

1. **Transfer to Complex Environments:** Evaluate the method on standard RL benchmarks (e.g., MiniGrid, Atari) with varying reward structures and longer episode lengths to assess generalization beyond the simplified Alchemy domain.

2. **Computational Efficiency Analysis:** Measure wall-clock time for transformer inference and value iteration compared to exact posterior sampling methods, particularly as partial model size increases, to quantify practical scalability.

3. **Robustness to Context Quality:** Systematically vary the quality and quantity of context provided to the transformer (e.g., using suboptimal policies or limited trajectories) to determine sensitivity to context reliability and inform requirements for effective in-context adaptation.
