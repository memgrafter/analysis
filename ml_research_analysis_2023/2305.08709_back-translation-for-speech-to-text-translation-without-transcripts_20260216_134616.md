# Back Translation for Speech-to-text Translation Without Transcripts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08709
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08709
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BT4ST achieves 2.3 BLEU improvement over strong baseline without source transcripts

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training speech-to-text translation (ST) models for unwritten languages where source language transcripts are unavailable. The proposed BT4ST method synthesizes pseudo ST data by cascading a target-to-unit model with a unit-to-speech model, using self-supervised discrete units as intermediate representations. This approach effectively handles the short-to-long generation challenge and one-to-many mapping problem in speech synthesis. Experiments on MuST-C En→De, En→Fr, and En→Es datasets demonstrate consistent improvements over baselines, with particularly strong performance in low-resource settings.

## Method Summary
The BT4ST approach synthesizes ST data without source transcripts by generating discrete units from target text using a target-to-unit Transformer model, then converting these units to speech using a HiFi-GAN vocoder with duration prediction and speaker encoding. The method uses self-supervised discrete units to alleviate the short-to-long generation challenge and handle the one-to-many mapping problem in speech synthesis. Data selection based on round-trip BLEU scores filters low-quality synthetic samples, and a two-stage training procedure (pre-training on synthetic data followed by fine-tuning on real data) maximizes the benefits of the augmented dataset.

## Key Results
- BT4ST achieves 2.3 average BLEU improvement over strong baseline across three language pairs
- In low-resource setting (100h data), BT4ST improves BLEU by 5.6 points
- Model ensemble with diverse synthetic datasets further improves performance by 4.0 BLEU
- Data selection using round-trip BLEU improves results by filtering out low-quality samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Cascading target-to-unit and unit-to-speech models alleviates the short-to-long generation challenge in speech synthesis. Discrete units are generated at ~50 Hz, making the unit sequence length comparable to target text (~hundreds of units) rather than waveform samples (~tens of thousands per second). This reduces the gap between source and target sequence lengths.

### Mechanism 2
Discrete units disentangle content from speaker variation, easing the one-to-many mapping problem. Discrete units capture phonetic content while speaker-specific information (pitch, timbre) is handled separately by the speaker encoder, allowing the unit-to-speech model to focus on content reconstruction.

### Mechanism 3
Data selection based on BLEU scores of round-trip translation removes low-quality synthetic data. Target-to-unit model generates units from target text, unit-to-speech model synthesizes speech, then unit-to-target model translates units back to text. BLEU score between round-trip text and original text indicates quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Target text -> Target-to-unit Transformer -> Reduced units -> Duration predictor -> Full units -> Speaker encoder -> Unit-to-speech HiFi-GAN -> Synthetic speech -> ST model

- **Critical path:**
  1. Target text → Target-to-unit model → Reduced units
  2. Reduced units + duration predictor → Full units
  3. Full units + speaker embedding → Unit-to-speech model → Synthetic speech
  4. Synthetic speech → ST model training

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Using discrete units vs. direct text-to-speech synthesis: Discrete units simplify the generation problem but add complexity to the pipeline
  - Multi-speaker vs. single-speaker synthesis: Multi-speaker data better matches real data distribution but requires speaker encoder and increases diversity
  - Beam search vs. sampling for unit generation: Beam search produces higher quality but potentially less diverse data

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low round-trip BLEU scores indicate poor synthesis quality
  - Synthetic speech that doesn't match target transcript indicates model misalignment
  - ST model performance degradation when using synthetic data suggests data quality issues
  - Training instability when combining synthetic and real data suggests data distribution mismatch

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train target-to-unit and unit-to-speech models on small subset, verify round-trip BLEU and synthesis quality
  2. Generate synthetic data with different data selection ratios (0%, 50%, 75%, 100%) and measure impact on ST model performance
  3. Compare multi-speaker vs. single-speaker synthetic data for ST model training

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the quality of back-translated speech change when using different levels of self-supervised discrete units (e.g., different K values in K-means clustering)? The paper uses K=100 clusters but does not explore the impact of varying this parameter on synthetic speech quality.

### Open Question 2
How does the proposed method perform on unwritten languages with very different phonological systems compared to English (e.g., tonal languages or languages with non-Latin scripts)? All experiments use English as source language, which has a relatively simple phonological system compared to many unwritten languages.

### Open Question 3
What is the impact of iterative back-translation on speech-to-text translation performance compared to single-pass back-translation? The authors mention interest in exploring iterative BT techniques in the future but do not implement or evaluate them.

## Limitations

- The method relies on high-quality discrete units as intermediate representations, but the quality and linguistic completeness of these units are not thoroughly validated
- The approach assumes that round-trip BLEU score is a reliable proxy for synthetic data quality, which may not always correlate with actual usefulness for ST training
- The method has not been tested on actual unwritten languages with different phonological properties, only on English as a proxy

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** The core methodology of using back translation for ST without transcripts is well-founded and technically sound. The experimental results showing consistent improvements across three language pairs are reliable.
- **Medium confidence:** The data selection mechanism using round-trip BLEU scores is reasonable but may not be optimal. The specific threshold of 75% for data selection appears effective but could be dataset-dependent.
- **Low confidence:** The assumption that discrete units perfectly disentangle content from speaker variation is not fully validated. The long-term stability of the cascaded generation pipeline under different data conditions is uncertain.

## Next Checks

1. **Unit quality validation:** Generate synthetic speech from discrete units and measure alignment quality between original text and synthesized speech using dynamic time warping. Verify that the monotonic alignment assumption holds across different speakers and content types.

2. **Data selection sensitivity analysis:** Systematically vary the data selection threshold (50%, 60%, 70%, 80%, 90%) and measure the impact on ST model performance. This will determine whether the 75% threshold is optimal or dataset-specific.

3. **Speaker variation robustness test:** Train the unit-to-speech model with different numbers of speakers (5, 10, 20, 50) and measure the impact on synthetic speech quality and ST model performance. This will validate whether the speaker encoder effectively handles the one-to-many mapping problem.
