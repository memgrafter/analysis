# Rethink DARTS Search Space and Renovate a New Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06852
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06852
- Authors: Chaoji Zhang, Yuze Zhang, Yizhou Zhou, Boxiang Tang, Chao Qian
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes LHD, a larger and harder DARTS search space benchmark with 0.29% AMAR on CIFAR-10 compared to 0.012% on DSS

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a critical limitation in the current DARTS search space (DSS) benchmark: its narrow accuracy range makes it difficult to distinguish between neural architecture search (NAS) methods. To address this, the authors propose LHD, a new search space that is both larger and more challenging while maintaining high search efficiency. They construct a comprehensive benchmark that evaluates 12 NAS baselines across 12 different conditions, combining transductive robustness and discretization policies. The results demonstrate that LHD provides significantly better method discernibility and reveals unstable method rankings across different evaluation conditions, making it a more challenging and reliable benchmark for NAS research.

## Method Summary
The authors propose LHD, a new DARTS search space that addresses the narrow accuracy range problem in DSS through three main improvements: input-softmax enhancement for fair operation comparison across edges, polynary searchable blocks that enable searching of addition and concatenation operations, and unified convolution primitives that consolidate different convolution variants. They construct a benchmark by evaluating 12 NAS baselines (including DARTS, MiLeNAS, DrNAS, and others) across 12 conditions combining three datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN) with four discretization policies (Base, 1M, 3ops, 4out). The evaluation uses metrics like AMAR (average accuracy margin) and TMAR (t-test margin) to measure method discernibility, with results showing LHD achieves 0.29% AMAR on CIFAR-10 compared to 0.012% on DSS.

## Key Results
- LHD achieves 0.29% AMAR on CIFAR-10, significantly larger than DSS's 0.012% AMAR
- The overall ranking of NAS methods is unstable across different evaluation conditions
- LHD search efficiency is 40% better than DSS despite larger search space complexity
- Evaluation results on ImageNet-1k under mobile regime are competitive against a wide range of baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The input-softmax enhancement addresses the limitation of sub-graph comparisons in DSS by enabling fair comparison of operation significance across edges.
- Mechanism: Instead of applying softmax on each compound edge individually (local-softmax), the input-softmax is placed before node aggregation, normalizing operation significance across all incoming edges to a node simultaneously.
- Core assumption: Operations on different incoming edges to the same node can be meaningfully compared through their normalized weights.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose and orchestrate a suite of improvements to frame a larger and harder DSS, termed LHD"
  - [section 2] "This way, the significance of any operation om i,j toward node j can be fairly compared through the value of am i,j for all the combinations of m ∈ [1, M] and i ∈ [1, j − 1]"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If the operations on different edges are not comparable due to fundamentally different input characteristics or computational requirements

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The polynary s/e block enables searching of non-unary operations like addition and concatenation that were previously fixed in DSS.
- Mechanism: By associating a learnable parameter with each path in a compound edge, the block can weight and aggregate feature maps from multiple inputs through various operations.
- Core assumption: The significance of different paths (representing different operations or input combinations) can be expressed as learnable weights optimized through gradient descent.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "new searchable blocks: searchable polynary operations"
  - [section 2] "Generalizing the merit of DARTS to search polynary operations is straightforward by associating parameter with each path"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for polynary operations
- Break condition: If the computational graph becomes too complex to optimize effectively or if the gradients become unstable

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The unified convolution primitive simplifies the operation space while maintaining expressiveness, reducing human bias in architecture design.
- Mechanism: By consolidating different convolution variants (dilated, non-dilated, channel mixing) into a single unified structure, the search space becomes more flexible and less constrained by predefined architectural patterns.
- Core assumption: A unified primitive structure can effectively represent the diversity of useful convolution operations without sacrificing performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "primitive refinement: unified convolution primitive"
  - [section 2] "We first unify the dilated and non-dilated depth-wise (d/w) primitives... We step forward to incorporate the d/w primitive and 1×1 channel mixer into an unified structure"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for unified primitives
- Break condition: If the unified primitive cannot adequately represent certain important operations or if it introduces optimization difficulties

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input-softmax layer: Normalizes operation significance across incoming edges to each node
  - Polynary s/e blocks: Searchable blocks that can perform addition, concatenation, and other multi-input operations
  - Unified convolution primitive: Simplified convolution operation structure
  - Cell output blocks: Searchable output paths using softmax (SSB) and sigmoid (CSB) gating
  - Inter-cell connections: Searchable skip connections between cells

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize supernet with LHD components
  2. Alternate optimization of architecture parameters (α) and operation weights (ω)
  3. Apply input-softmax for fair operation comparison
  4. Search polynary operations and cell outputs
  5. Evaluate final architectures across multiple conditions

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Larger search space vs. computational efficiency
  - Simplified primitives vs. expressiveness
  - Searchable outputs vs. fixed architectural patterns
  - Multiple evaluation conditions vs. benchmark accessibility

- Failure signatures:
  - Narrow accuracy range (AMAR < 0.1%) indicating poor method discernibility
  - High correlation between parameter count and validation accuracy
  - Unstable rankings across different evaluation conditions
  - Failure to find valid architectures during search

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement LHD search space with input-softmax enhancement only, evaluate on CIFAR-10
  2. Add polynary s/e blocks to the previous implementation, compare performance and search efficiency
  3. Implement unified convolution primitive, evaluate overall search space complexity and method discernibility

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the LHD search space perform on tasks beyond image classification, such as object detection or semantic segmentation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on evaluating LHD on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN datasets for image classification tasks. However, the authors mention that DSS has inspired follow-up researches in various forms, including applications beyond image classification.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any experimental results or analysis of LHD's performance on tasks other than image classification. It is unclear whether the improvements and characteristics of LHD that benefit image classification would translate to other computer vision tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to evaluate LHD on object detection, semantic segmentation, or other computer vision tasks and comparing the results with DSS and other search spaces would provide insights into LHD's performance beyond image classification.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the improvements in LHD affect the search efficiency compared to DSS, especially when considering the increased search space complexity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the overall time overhead of the search phase of vanilla DARTS on CIFAR-10 is 40% lower than that on DSS (5.2h→3.1h on RTX 3090) despite the increased search space complexity of LHD. However, it is not clear how this efficiency gain scales with the increased complexity of LHD.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the search efficiency of LHD compared to DSS across different search space complexities or network architectures. It is unclear whether the 40% efficiency gain observed on CIFAR-10 would hold for more complex search spaces or different datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to measure the search efficiency of LHD and DSS across different search space complexities and datasets would provide insights into the scalability of LHD's efficiency improvements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the results of the LHD benchmark generalize to larger-scale image classification tasks, such as ImageNet?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the evaluation results on ImageNet-1k under mobile regime are competitive against a wide range of baselines. However, the evaluation is limited to a specific architecture search setting and does not explore the full potential of LHD on ImageNet.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive evaluation of LHD on ImageNet across different architecture search settings, such as searching for different cell types, network depths, or widths. It is unclear whether the improvements observed on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN would translate to larger-scale image classification tasks like ImageNet.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to evaluate LHD on ImageNet across different architecture search settings and comparing the results with DSS and other search spaces would provide insights into the generalizability of LHD's improvements to larger-scale image classification tasks.

## Limitations
- The benchmark evaluation is limited to image classification tasks on CIFAR and SVHN datasets
- Computational efficiency gains may not scale to more complex search spaces or larger datasets
- The generalizability of LHD improvements to other NAS settings remains unverified

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The identification of narrow accuracy range in DSS as a benchmarking limitation (supported by AMAR statistics)
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of LHD improvements in creating a more challenging benchmark (limited to evaluated conditions)
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about the generalizability of LHD's improvements to other NAS settings and datasets
