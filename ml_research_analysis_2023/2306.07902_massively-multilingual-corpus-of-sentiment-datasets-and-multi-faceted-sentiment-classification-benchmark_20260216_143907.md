# Massively Multilingual Corpus of Sentiment Datasets and Multi-faceted Sentiment Classification Benchmark

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07902
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07902
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: 79 high-quality sentiment datasets covering 27 languages and 6 language families, with over 6 million samples total

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the largest open multilingual corpus of sentiment datasets, encompassing 79 high-quality datasets across 27 languages and 6 language families. The corpus contains over 6 million samples and includes strict quality criteria for dataset selection. A comprehensive multi-faceted benchmark was developed, conducting hundreds of experiments across different base models, training objectives, and fine-tuning strategies. The corpus and benchmark are publicly available via HuggingFace, enabling extensive research in multilingual sentiment analysis.

## Method Summary
The authors compiled a multilingual corpus of sentiment datasets by applying strict quality criteria including dataset size, publication venue, and source type. The corpus was filtered based on linguistic features to enable targeted research. For the benchmark, hundreds of experiments were conducted using different base models (XLM-R, LaBSE, MPNet), training objectives, and fine-tuning strategies. The evaluation focused on F1 scores across multiple languages, with particular attention to performance differences between model types and training approaches.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning improves performance significantly, with F1 scores reaching up to 0.7
- XLM-R, LaBSE, and MPNet models consistently outperform other models across languages
- Larger models achieve better results, though fine-tuning reduces performance gaps between models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness stems from the combination of high-quality multilingual data and comprehensive experimental coverage. The strict quality criteria ensure dataset reliability, while the diverse language coverage enables robust cross-lingual analysis. The extensive experimentation across different models and training strategies provides comprehensive performance insights, with fine-tuning emerging as a particularly effective approach for sentiment classification.
