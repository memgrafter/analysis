# FeedbackMap: a tool for making sense of open-ended survey responses

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15112
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15112
- Reference count: 21
- Key outcome: FeedbackMap is a web-based tool that uses natural language processing to help researchers analyze open-ended survey responses through multiple perspectives.

## Executive Summary
FeedbackMap is a web-based tool designed to help researchers analyze open-ended survey responses by providing multiple perspectives on the data. The tool addresses the challenge of manual coding, which is time-consuming and costly, by using natural language processing techniques including language models for summarization and embeddings for visualization. Users can upload survey data, select a question, and explore responses through various lenses including abstractive summaries, topic scatterplots, interesting examples, cluster summaries, and top words/phrases. Initial feedback from three users was positive, though more rigorous evaluation is planned for future work.

## Method Summary
FeedbackMap is implemented as a Streamlit web application that accepts CSV survey data as input. The tool processes open-ended responses using configurable language models for summarization tasks and sentence embedding models for visualization. For datasets exceeding 5000 rows, the tool samples responses to maintain performance. The analysis pipeline involves generating embeddings, projecting to 2D space using UMAP, clustering with HDBSCAN, and creating multiple presentation modes including overall summaries, cluster-specific summaries, and interactive visualizations. The tool allows customization of models and prompts for different deployment contexts.

## Key Results
- Initial feedback from three users (school administrator, non-profit founder, political scientist) was positive
- The tool successfully provides multiple analytical perspectives on survey responses
- Language models can generate coherent summaries and identify interesting response examples
- Embedding-based visualization effectively organizes responses by topic for exploration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Providing multiple perspectives on survey responses reduces bias from any single summarization approach.
- Mechanism: By offering different views (abstractive summary, topic scatterplot, interesting examples, cluster summaries, top words/phrases), the tool allows users to cross-validate findings and notice when a particular view might be missing important information.
- Core assumption: Different summarization methods capture complementary aspects of the data, and viewing them together gives a more complete picture than any single method alone.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section]
- Break Condition: If all summarization methods share the same underlying bias (e.g., all trained on similar datasets), multiple views won't reduce systematic bias.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Embedding-based visualization helps users understand the topical structure of responses.
- Mechanism: By computing sentence embeddings and projecting to 2D space, responses about similar topics cluster together, allowing users to visually identify response patterns and outliers.
- Core assumption: Sentence embeddings capture meaningful semantic similarity that translates well to 2D projection for human interpretation.
- Evidence anchors: [section]
- Break Condition: If the embedding model fails to capture relevant semantic distinctions for the survey domain, the visualization will mislead rather than inform.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Language model summaries can efficiently surface key themes from large numbers of responses.
- Mechanism: The tool uses LMs to generate both overall summaries and cluster-specific summaries, extracting coherent themes that would be time-consuming for humans to identify manually.
- Core assumption: LMs can effectively summarize survey responses in a way that captures meaningful themes without introducing significant hallucination or bias.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section]
- Break Condition: If the LM prompt engineering is poor or the model hallucinates, summaries may contain fabricated information or miss critical themes.

- Design tradeoffs: Random sampling of responses when dataset exceeds 5000 rows (performance vs. completeness), choice between API-based vs. local models (cost vs. control)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How effective is FeedbackMap at mitigating researcher biases compared to traditional manual coding methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the potential biases introduced by summarization methods and the need for critical evaluation of representation and omission of respondent voices.
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions that FeedbackMap's multiple data presentations seek to mitigate biases, but it does not provide a rigorous evaluation of its effectiveness compared to traditional methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study between FeedbackMap and traditional manual coding methods, measuring the impact on researcher biases and the quality of insights generated.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of language model (LM) and summarization techniques affect the quality and biases of the summaries generated by FeedbackMap?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that organizations can customize the language models to use for writing summaries and computing embeddings, and that different summarization methods may introduce biases.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how different LMs and summarization techniques impact the quality and biases of the generated summaries.
- What evidence would resolve it: An empirical study comparing the performance of different LMs and summarization techniques in terms of summary quality, bias, and user satisfaction.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does FeedbackMap handle non-response biases in open-ended survey questions, particularly for underrepresented demographic groups?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper cites a Pew Research study that found non-response rates on open-ended survey questions vary by demographic group, and discusses the potential for hidden biases in the summarization model to exclude certain types of responses.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how FeedbackMap addresses non-response biases or ensures the representation of underrepresented groups in the summaries.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study examining the representation of different demographic groups in the summaries generated by FeedbackMap, and the impact of non-response biases on the insights generated.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on informal feedback from only three initial users rather than rigorous field studies
- No systematic evaluation of potential hallucinations or inaccuracies in language model-generated summaries
- Sampling approach for large datasets (>5000 responses) may miss important patterns or outliers
- Limited validation of whether embedding-based visualization actually improves user understanding
