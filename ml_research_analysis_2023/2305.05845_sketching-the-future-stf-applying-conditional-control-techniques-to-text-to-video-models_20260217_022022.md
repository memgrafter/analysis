# Sketching the Future (STF): Applying Conditional Control Techniques to Text-to-Video Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05845
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05845
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: Combines zero-shot text-to-video generation with ControlNet to improve output quality and consistency when guided by sketched frames

## Executive Summary
Sketching the Future (STF) introduces a novel approach that enhances text-to-video generation by incorporating user-provided sketched frames as guidance through ControlNet conditioning. The method interpolates between sketched frames to create intermediate guidance, then uses these as conditioning input for video generation while maintaining object consistency through cross-frame attention. Experiments demonstrate that STF produces high-quality, consistent video content that better aligns with user-intended motion compared to baseline text-to-video methods.

## Method Summary
STF combines Text-to-Video Zero with ControlNet to create a conditional video generation framework. The method takes multiple sketched frames as input, interpolates intermediate frames between them, and uses these interpolated frames as conditioning for ControlNet during text-to-video generation. A cross-frame attention mechanism maintains foreground and background object identity by comparing each frame against the first frame. This approach enables high-quality video generation that follows the user's intended motion trajectory without requiring additional training data.

## Key Results
- STF excels at producing high-quality and remarkably consistent video content that more accurately aligns with user's intended motion
- The approach generates video output that matches the flow of input sketched frames
- Method demonstrates effectiveness without requiring additional training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Interpolating between sketched frames enables ControlNet to enforce temporal consistency across generated video
- Mechanism: Creates intermediate frames between user-provided sketches via interpolation, which serve as conditioning input to ControlNet during text-to-video generation
- Core assumption: Interpolated frames accurately represent intermediate motion and ControlNet can effectively use these as spatial-temporal conditioning
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states "By first interpolating frames between the inputted sketches and then running Text-to-Video Zero using the new interpolated frames video as the control technique"; section confirms interpolation followed by ControlNet processing

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Cross-frame attention mechanism maintains foreground object identity and background consistency across frames
- Mechanism: Adds cross-frame attention layers that compare each frame against the first frame to preserve visual features throughout the video sequence
- Core assumption: First frame contains sufficient information about object identity that can be propagated through cross-frame attention
- Evidence anchors: Section states "we add a cross-frame attention layer on each frame against the ﬁrst frame. This maintains the identity of the foreground and background objects"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ControlNet provides robust conditioning that enables high-quality video generation without additional training data
- Mechanism: ControlNet learns task-specific conditions in an end-to-end manner and can be trained on small datasets (<50k) while working with existing pretrained diffusion models
- Core assumption: ControlNet's ability to learn conditioning from small datasets extends to video generation when using interpolated frames as conditioning
- Evidence anchors: Section mentions "ControlNet learns task-speciﬁc conditions in an end-to-end manner and exhibits robust learning even with small training datasets (< 50k)"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Sketched frames -> Interpolation module -> ControlNet -> Text-to-Video Zero -> Cross-frame attention -> Generated video

- Critical path:
  1. Receive sketched frames and text prompt
  2. Interpolate frames between sketches
  3. Generate latent codes with motion dynamics
  4. Apply cross-frame attention (frame 1 as reference)
  5. ControlNet processes interpolated frames as conditioning
  6. Generate final video frames
  7. Output video sequence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using interpolated frames vs. direct sketch-to-video generation: Interpolation provides smoother temporal transitions but adds computational overhead and potential error propagation
  - Cross-frame attention vs. self-attention only: Cross-frame attention maintains consistency but increases computational complexity
  - ControlNet conditioning vs. pure text-to-video: ControlNet provides better control but requires additional architecture and potential training

- Failure signatures:
  - Motion inconsistency: Interpolated frames poorly represent intended motion
  - Object drift: Cross-frame attention insufficient to maintain object identity
  - Prompt mismatch: Text conditioning dominates over sketch conditioning
  - Temporal artifacts: Frame interpolation creates unnatural transitions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test interpolation quality: Generate interpolated frames from simple sketch pairs and visually inspect for motion plausibility
  2. Validate ControlNet conditioning: Run ControlNet with sketch-based conditioning on image generation to verify it follows sketch guidance
  3. Cross-frame attention ablation: Generate video with and without cross-frame attention to measure impact on object consistency

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the interpolation quality between sketched frames affect the final video output quality and motion smoothness?
- Basis in paper: Paper mentions interpolating frames between inputted sketches but does not analyze the impact of interpolation quality on final output
- Why unresolved: Paper does not provide experiments or analysis on how different interpolation methods or quality affect generated video output
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing generated video quality using different interpolation techniques and quantitative metrics on motion smoothness

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of sketch quality and detail level on the final video generation results?
- Basis in paper: Paper uses simple stick figure sketches but does not explore how varying sketch quality affects output
- Why unresolved: Experiments only used basic sketches without varying the detail level or quality to test model's robustness to different sketch inputs
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing video outputs generated from sketches of varying detail levels with quantitative metrics on output quality and accuracy

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does STF perform on more complex scenes with multiple moving objects or interactions between subjects?
- Basis in paper: Paper mentions future research directions including "more complex and dynamic scenes" but does not test this capability
- Why unresolved: Experiments focused on a single subject in a simple beach scene without testing complex multi-object scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing STF on videos with multiple subjects, object interactions, and complex background elements

### Open Question 4
- Question: What are the computational requirements and processing times for STF compared to other text-to-video generation methods?
- Basis in paper: Paper claims "low overhead" but does not provide detailed computational analysis or timing comparisons
- Why unresolved: Paper does not include benchmarks, processing time measurements, or memory usage comparisons with other methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed benchmarking data showing processing times, memory usage, and computational complexity compared to Text2Video-Zero and other methods

## Limitations
- Evaluation methodology relies on qualitative assessments rather than quantitative metrics, making improvements subjective
- Demonstrated effectiveness limited to specific types of motion sketches; generalization to diverse motion patterns and complex scenes is unclear
- Computational overhead from interpolation step and cross-frame attention mechanism not fully characterized

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Core architectural concept of using ControlNet with interpolated frames for sketch-based video generation is technically sound
- Medium Confidence: Claim that STF "excels at producing high-quality and remarkably consistent video content" supported by qualitative examples but lacks quantitative validation
- Low Confidence: Assertion that approach works "without additional training data" is questionable as ControlNet typically requires adaptation
