# Is ChatGPT a Good Teacher Coach? Measuring Zero-Shot Performance For Scoring and Providing Actionable Insights on Classroom Instruction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03090
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03090
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: ChatGPT generates relevant but often non-novel feedback on classroom instruction, with 82% of suggestions pointing to existing teacher practices and low correlation (0.03-0.14) with expert ratings

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates ChatGPT's zero-shot performance on three teacher coaching tasks: scoring classroom transcript segments, identifying instructional highlights and missed opportunities, and providing actionable suggestions for eliciting student reasoning. Using elementary math classroom transcripts and expert teacher ratings as ground truth, the study reveals that while ChatGPT consistently generates relevant responses, it struggles to produce novel or insightful feedback. The model frequently identifies areas where teachers already implement suggested practices (82% of suggestions) and shows weak correlation with human expert scores across all observation items. These findings highlight significant challenges in developing generative AI systems capable of providing meaningful instructional coaching.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate ChatGPT's performance on three teacher coaching tasks using elementary math classroom transcripts. Expert teachers scored these transcripts across 10 observation items, which served as ground truth. ChatGPT was prompted to score transcript segments, identify highlights and missed opportunities, and provide actionable suggestions. Responses were compared against expert ratings using correlation analysis and evaluated for relevance, novelty, and faithfulness to the transcript. The study employed zero-shot prompting without any model fine-tuning or task-specific training.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT generates relevant responses but lacks novelty, with 82% of suggestions pointing to areas where teachers already implement the suggested practice
- Correlation between ChatGPT predictions and human expert scores ranges from 0.03 to 0.14 across all 10 observation items
- Hallucination is significant, with 15-30% of model responses being unfaithful to the transcript content

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does ChatGPT's performance on teacher coaching tasks vary across different subjects (e.g., mathematics vs. history vs. English)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that "what counts as helpful feedback may be different for each teacher, and look different in other subjects, eg. History and English" and proposes future work to adapt the approach beyond mathematics.
- Why unresolved: The current study only evaluates ChatGPT's performance on elementary math classroom transcripts, limiting generalizability to other subjects.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting similar experiments on classroom transcripts from other subjects and comparing ChatGPT's performance across different domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can automated feedback systems be designed to reduce hallucination and provide more accurate interpretations of classroom events?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies hallucination as a significant issue, with 15-30% of model responses being unfaithful to the transcript. It suggests future work on "improved prompting methods" and "engineering the model's prompt collaboratively with teachers and coaches."
- Why unresolved: While the paper highlights the problem, it does not provide specific solutions or experimental results for addressing hallucination in automated feedback systems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing novel prompting strategies or model architectures that explicitly reduce hallucination in educational contexts.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can ChatGPT's ability to generate novel and insightful feedback be improved, particularly for complex pedagogical tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper finds that ChatGPT struggles to generate insightful and novel feedback, with 82% of suggestions pointing to places where the teacher already implements the suggestion. It suggests involving "coaches and educators in the reinforcement learning stage of model fine-tuning" as a potential solution.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but does not explore specific methods for improving ChatGPT's feedback generation capabilities beyond suggesting future work directions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing and evaluating fine-tuning approaches that incorporate feedback from experienced educators and coaches, then measuring improvements in the novelty and insightfulness of generated suggestions.

## Limitations
- Low correlation (0.03-0.14) between model predictions and expert human scores across all observation items
- High rate of hallucination (15-30% of responses) where model provides information not present in transcripts
- Limited generalizability to subjects beyond elementary mathematics
- Zero-shot evaluation prevents optimization of model performance for specific coaching tasks
- Lack of exploration into potential architectural modifications or fine-tuning approaches that could improve performance

## Confidence
- Confidence in ChatGPT's relevance claims: Medium
- Confidence in novelty and insight claims: Low
- Confidence in automated scoring reliability: Medium
- Confidence in generalizability to other subjects: Low
