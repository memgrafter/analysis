# Wavelets Beat Monkeys at Adversarial Robustness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09403
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09403
- Reference count: 19
- The VOneBlock architecture achieves only 9.47% PGD accuracy on CIFAR-10, far below adversarially trained models (31.49%), while a wavelet scattering transform with Gaussian noise achieves up to 24.84% PGD accuracy without adversarial training.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the claim that biologically-inspired VOneBlock architectures provide meaningful adversarial robustness. Through rigorous testing and ablation studies, the authors demonstrate that VOneBlock's previously reported robustness is negligible under standard attack settings. More importantly, they show that none of the VOneBlock's individual components contribute to robustness when isolated. The key contribution is replacing the complex VOneBlock with a parameter-free wavelet scattering transform combined with simple uniform Gaussian noise, which achieves significantly higher robustness without requiring adversarial training or extensive parameter tuning.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate adversarial robustness on CIFAR-10 using a standard 3-layer CNN backend (width 64) with either VOneBlock or wavelet scattering transform as the first layer. Models are trained for 200 epochs using Adam optimizer (lr=1e-3). Robustness is measured using FGSM, PGD-20 (epsilon=8/255), and AutoAttack benchmarks, with each experiment repeated 3 times to report mean and standard deviation. The key innovation is replacing the biologically-inspired VOneBlock with a wavelet scattering transform from the Kymatio library, followed by uniform Gaussian noise injection.

## Key Results
- VOneBlock achieves only 9.47% PGD accuracy on CIFAR-10 under standard attack settings, compared to 31.49% for adversarially trained models
- None of the VOneBlock components (Gabor filters, non-linearities, stochastic noise) contribute to robustness when isolated
- Wavelet scattering transform with Gaussian noise achieves up to 24.84% PGD accuracy without adversarial training
- Simple uniform Gaussian noise outperforms complex neuronal noise modeling in the VOneBlock

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Wavelet scattering transforms combined with Gaussian noise achieve adversarial robustness without requiring adversarial training.
- Mechanism: The wavelet scattering transform acts as a parameter-free feature extractor that captures geometric invariants and stable representations. When combined with uniform Gaussian noise injection, it creates stochasticity that prevents gradient-based attacks from finding consistent adversarial directions.
- Core assumption: The wavelet scattering transform's multiscale analysis and local invariance properties provide robustness to small perturbations, and uniform Gaussian noise can replace the need for complex biologically-inspired noise modeling.
- Evidence anchors:

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: None of the individual components of the VOneBlock contribute to robustness when isolated.
- Mechanism: The VOneBlock's robustness emerges from the delicate interplay between Gabor filters, non-linearities, and stochastic noise. When any component is removed or replaced with simpler alternatives, the synergistic effect that provides slight robustness disappears entirely.
- Core assumption: The robustness is not due to any single component but rather the specific combination and interaction of all components as designed to mimic primate V1 responses.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we demonstrate that none of the components of VOne alone induce any robustness... when we remove any one of the components of the VOneBlock the model looses any robustness whatsoever"

- Claim: Biologically-inspired architectures are less effective than physics-inspired structures for achieving adversarial robustness.
- Mechanism: Physically-motivated structures like wavelet scattering transforms have inherent mathematical properties (multiscale analysis, translation invariance) that provide robustness, while biologically-inspired architectures require extensive parameter tuning based on experimental data without guaranteed robustness benefits.
- Core assumption: Mathematical and physical principles can capture essential features for robustness more effectively than empirical modeling of biological systems.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Wavelet scattering transform → Gaussian noise injection → Convolutional backend → Output
- Critical path: Input → Wavelet scattering transform → Gaussian noise injection → Convolutional backend → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Parameter-free vs. learned features: Scattering transform is parameter-free but may miss dataset-specific patterns that learned features could capture
  - Noise level selection: Too little noise provides insufficient protection; too much destroys useful signal
  - Backend complexity: Simple backend works with scattering transform but may limit overall model capacity

- Failure signatures:
  - Low clean accuracy: Scattering transform may not capture sufficient discriminative information
  - Low robust accuracy: Noise level may be insufficient or scattering transform properties may not align with adversarial perturbation patterns
  - High variance across runs: Insufficient averaging of stochastic gradients during attack

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline test: Implement scattering transform with no noise, measure clean and robust accuracy
  2. Noise sensitivity: Test various Gaussian noise levels (std 0.1, 0.2, 0.35) while keeping scattering transform fixed
  3. Component ablation: Remove scattering transform entirely, use only Gaussian noise with random features to isolate noise contribution

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the robustness of wavelet scattering transforms compare to adversarially trained models under standard attack settings (epsilon=8/255)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that replacing the VOneBlock with a wavelet scattering transform followed by Gaussian noise achieves significantly higher robustness (up to 24.84% PGD accuracy) compared to the VOneBlock's 9.47% PGD accuracy.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates superior robustness compared to VOneBlock, it doesn't provide a direct comparison to adversarially trained models under the same attack settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments comparing the wavelet scattering transform's robustness to that of adversarially trained models under standard attack settings (epsilon=8/255) would provide a clear answer.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical explanation for why physically-inspired structures like wavelet scattering transforms achieve superior adversarial robustness compared to biologically-inspired architectures?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper observes that wavelet scattering transforms combined with Gaussian noise achieve higher robustness than the biologically-inspired VOneBlock, but doesn't provide a theoretical explanation for this phenomenon.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents empirical results but doesn't explore the underlying reasons for the difference in robustness between physically and biologically-inspired approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a theoretical framework that explains the properties of wavelet scattering transforms that make them more robust to adversarial attacks compared to biologically-inspired architectures would address this question.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different types of stochasticity (neuronal noise vs. uniform Gaussian noise) affect the robustness of wavelet scattering transforms and VOneBlock architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares the effects of neuronal noise and uniform Gaussian noise on both architectures, finding that uniform Gaussian noise leads to higher robustness in wavelet scattering transforms.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that uniform Gaussian noise is more effective for wavelet scattering transforms, it doesn't explore why this is the case or how different types of stochasticity might affect the VOneBlock architecture.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that systematically vary the type and parameters of stochasticity in both architectures and analyzing their effects on robustness would provide insights into this question.

## Limitations

- Experimental results show high variance across random seeds, suggesting sensitivity to initialization and training dynamics
- All robustness claims are based on a single attack setting (epsilon=8/255), with no exploration of different attack strengths or threat models
- The binary ablation approach may miss nuanced interactions between components, and doesn't test whether simplified versions of individual components might retain some robustness properties

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The finding that standard VOneBlock achieves minimal robustness (9.47% PGD accuracy) under the tested attack settings
- Medium Confidence: The claim that wavelet scattering transforms can replace VOneBlock components while maintaining or improving robustness
- Low Confidence: The broader claim that physics-inspired structures are inherently superior to biologically-inspired architectures for adversarial robustness

## Next Checks

1. **Multi-epsilon validation**: Test both VOneBlock and wavelet scattering approaches across a range of epsilon values (2/255, 4/255, 8/255, 16/255) to verify whether the relative performance relationship holds under different attack strengths.

2. **Dataset generalization**: Evaluate the wavelet scattering approach on at least one additional dataset (e.g., SVHN or CIFAR-100) to determine if the robustness gains transfer beyond CIFAR-10.

3. **Component modification ablation**: Instead of binary removal, test whether simplified or modified versions of VOneBlock components (e.g., using standard convolutions instead of Gabor filters) retain any robustness properties, which would provide more nuanced understanding of component contributions.
