# Generating Synthetic Documents for Cross-Encoder Re-Rankers: A Comparative Study of ChatGPT and Human Experts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02320
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02320
- Reference count: 40
- Human-generated training data slightly outperforms ChatGPT-generated data in supervised settings, while ChatGPT-trained models excel in zero-shot scenarios

## Executive Summary
This study explores the use of generative large language models (LLMs) to create synthetic documents for training cross-encoder re-rankers, challenging the conventional approach of generating synthetic queries. The researchers introduce the ChatGPT-RetrievalQA dataset and compare the effectiveness of cross-encoders fine-tuned on human-generated versus ChatGPT-generated responses. The findings reveal that models trained on ChatGPT responses outperform human-trained models in zero-shot settings across multiple datasets, while human-trained models slightly outperform in supervised settings. Domain-specific analysis indicates that human-trained models perform better in specialized domains like medicine. The study demonstrates the potential of generative LLMs for augmenting training data in information retrieval tasks, particularly for zero-shot scenarios.

## Method Summary
The researchers conducted a comparative study using the ChatGPT-RetrievalQA dataset, which contains synthetic documents generated by ChatGPT. They fine-tuned cross-encoder models on both human-generated and ChatGPT-generated responses and evaluated their performance across various datasets. The study employed a controlled experimental setup where models were trained on different combinations of human and synthetic data, then tested in both supervised and zero-shot settings. Domain-specific experiments were also conducted to assess performance variations across different fields of knowledge.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT-trained models outperform human-trained models in zero-shot settings across multiple datasets
- Human-trained models slightly outperform ChatGPT-trained models in supervised settings
- Domain-specific analysis shows human-trained models perform better in specialized domains like medicine

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study leverages the generative capabilities of LLMs to create synthetic training documents that capture diverse language patterns and information structures. By using synthetic documents instead of synthetic queries, the approach addresses the challenge of domain-specific vocabulary and factual accuracy in generated responses. The mechanism works because cross-encoders can learn from the rich semantic content in the synthetic documents while maintaining the ability to generalize to unseen queries in zero-shot settings.

## Architecture Onboarding

ChatGPT (synthetic document generator) -> Cross-encoder model -> Evaluation datasets

**Critical path:**
1. Generate synthetic documents using ChatGPT
2. Fine-tune cross-encoder on human-generated and synthetic data
3. Evaluate performance in supervised and zero-shot settings

**Design tradeoffs:**
The study balances between using human expertise for quality control and leveraging LLM scalability for data augmentation. The choice of synthetic documents over queries prioritizes semantic richness over query diversity.

**Failure signatures:**
- Poor performance in specialized domains suggests limitations in ChatGPT's domain knowledge
- Zero-shot performance gaps indicate potential overfitting to training data patterns
- Supervised setting results may be influenced by human annotation biases

**Three first experiments:**
