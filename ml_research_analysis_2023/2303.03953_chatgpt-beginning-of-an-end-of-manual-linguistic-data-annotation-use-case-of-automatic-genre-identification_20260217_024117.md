# ChatGPT: Beginning of an End of Manual Linguistic Data Annotation? Use Case of Automatic Genre Identification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03953
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03953
- Reference count: 15
- ChatGPT shows strong zero-shot performance on genre classification, outperforming fine-tuned models on English and performing comparably on Slovenian when prompted in English

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates ChatGPT's potential for automatic genre identification as a text classification task, comparing its zero-shot performance against a multilingual XLM-RoBERTa model fine-tuned on manually annotated datasets. The study evaluates performance on both English and Slovenian, finding that ChatGPT outperforms the fine-tuned model on an unseen English test set and performs comparably to English when prompted in English for Slovenian. However, performance drops significantly when prompted in Slovenian, highlighting language-dependent limitations. These findings suggest that large language models like ChatGPT could potentially reduce the need for labor-intensive manual annotation campaigns for text classification tasks, even for smaller languages.

## Method Summary
The study compares ChatGPT's zero-shot genre classification performance against a fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa model using manually annotated datasets in English and Slovenian. ChatGPT was prompted with a simple instruction to classify texts into one of four genre categories (news, reports, reviews, and miscellaneous). The fine-tuned model was trained on the same data and evaluated on both seen and unseen test sets. The experiments tested performance across different prompt languages (English and Slovenian) to assess language dependency. The study focused on single-label classification without exploring prompt optimization techniques or few-shot learning approaches.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT outperformed the fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa model on unseen English test data
- On Slovenian, ChatGPT performed comparably to English when prompted in English but significantly worse when prompted in Slovenian
- The study demonstrates ChatGPT's potential for reducing manual annotation needs, particularly for high-resource languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ChatGPT's strong performance stems from its large-scale pretraining on diverse text data, enabling it to generalize genre classification patterns without task-specific fine-tuning. The model's ability to understand contextual cues and linguistic patterns across languages allows for zero-shot classification. However, the performance drop with Slovenian prompts suggests that language-specific linguistic patterns and cultural context may impact the model's effectiveness when operating outside its dominant training language.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Zero-shot learning** - Classification without task-specific training; needed to evaluate ChatGPT's generalization ability; quick check: test on completely unseen categories
2. **Genre identification** - Classifying texts by communicative purpose; needed as the core task; quick check: verify human agreement on genre labels
3. **Multilingual modeling** - Cross-language performance comparison; needed to assess language dependency; quick check: test same model across multiple languages
4. **Prompt engineering** - Impact of instruction format on model output; needed to optimize performance; quick check: vary prompt structure systematically
5. **Fine-tuning vs. zero-shot** - Trade-offs between task-specific adaptation and generalization; needed for baseline comparison; quick check: measure performance across dataset sizes
6. **Cross-lingual transfer** - Performance when prompted in different languages; needed to understand language dependency; quick check: prompt in multiple languages and compare

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Human annotators -> Manual dataset creation -> Fine-tuning (XLM-RoBERTa) -> Classification model; ChatGPT (pre-trained LLM) -> Prompt input -> Classification output

**Critical Path:** Input text → Prompt processing → Genre classification → Output label

**Design Tradeoffs:** Zero-shot learning offers flexibility without annotation costs but may underperform on specialized tasks; fine-tuning provides task-specific accuracy but requires manual data; multilingual models must balance language coverage with performance

**Failure Signatures:** Performance degradation on under-resourced languages; inconsistent classifications across similar texts; sensitivity to prompt phrasing

**Three First Experiments:**
1. Test ChatGPT with varied prompt structures (few-shot examples, different instruction formats)
2. Evaluate model consistency by running multiple classifications on the same texts
3. Compare performance across different genre distributions in training data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does ChatGPT's performance on genre classification vary with prompt engineering techniques beyond the basic prompt used in this study?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions experimenting with more advanced prompting techniques as future work, suggesting that current results may not represent the full potential of ChatGPT for this task.
- Why unresolved: The study only used a basic prompt structure, leaving the impact of more sophisticated prompting strategies unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using techniques like few-shot learning, chain-of-thought prompting, or dynamic prompting to determine if they significantly improve ChatGPT's genre classification accuracy.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can ChatGPT effectively handle multi-label genre classification and identification of text spans within documents, as suggested by the paper's preliminary observations?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that ChatGPT assigned multiple classes to some texts and recognized document parts, but these capabilities were not systematically evaluated.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on single-label classification, leaving the model's ability to handle more complex genre identification tasks unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing ChatGPT's performance on documents with multiple genres or text segments, comparing its ability to identify and label different parts accurately.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size of manually annotated training datasets impact the performance of fine-tuned models compared to ChatGPT's zero-shot performance on genre classification?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests exploring the impact of dataset sizes as future work, indicating that this relationship is not yet fully understood.
- Why unresolved: The study compared ChatGPT to a model trained on a specific dataset size without examining how varying this size would affect performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the size of manually annotated datasets used to fine-tune models, comparing their performance against ChatGPT's zero-shot results to identify the threshold where fine-tuning becomes more effective.

## Limitations
- Limited language coverage (only English and Slovenian)
- No exploration of prompt optimization strategies
- Unclear impact of genre distribution in training data
- No assessment of consistency across multiple ChatGPT runs

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: ChatGPT's superior performance on English zero-shot classification
- **Medium confidence**: ChatGPT's comparable performance on Slovenian when prompted in English
- **Low confidence**: Generalizability of findings to other language pairs and text domains
