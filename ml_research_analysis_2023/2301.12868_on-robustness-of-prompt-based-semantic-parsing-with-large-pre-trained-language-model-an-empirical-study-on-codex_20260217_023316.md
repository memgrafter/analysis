## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the robustness of large prompt-based language models for semantic parsing, specifically examining Codex. The authors find that these models are vulnerable to adversarial attacks, particularly sentence-level perturbations, despite their strong performance in few-shot settings. To improve robustness without extensive labeled data or heavy computational resources, they explore several approaches including using more demonstration examples, adversarial in-context learning, and active learning-based sampling. Their experiments show that increasing the number of standard examples improves both standard and robust accuracy, while adversarial in-context learning has limited effect. They also find that sampling strategies with higher language complexity (measured by lexical diversity metrics) lead to stronger robustness. The study highlights that even large language models like Codex have non-negotiable vulnerabilities to adversarial examples in semantic parsing tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct an empirical study on Codex's robustness for semantic parsing tasks using various attack methods including character-level (TB), word-level (RD, RS, CS, CI), and sentence-level (RB, DB) perturbations. They evaluate three approaches to improve robustness: increasing the number of standard examples in prompts, adversarial in-context learning, and active learning-based sampling strategies. The experiments are conducted on three datasets: Scholar, and two GeoQuery variants. Robustness is measured as the ratio of robust accuracy to standard accuracy across multiple attack types.

## Key Results
- Prompt-based models are significantly more vulnerable to sentence-level perturbations than word-level perturbations
- Increasing the number of standard demonstration examples consistently improves both standard and robust accuracy
- Adversarial in-context learning shows limited effectiveness in improving robustness
- Sampling strategies with higher lexical diversity produce stronger robustness in semantic parsing tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of increasing demonstration examples stems from providing more context and patterns for the model to learn from, reducing overfitting to specific input formats. The vulnerability to sentence-level perturbations likely arises because these attacks fundamentally alter the input structure and meaning, which is harder for the model to recover from compared to word-level substitutions that maintain overall sentence structure. Lexical diversity in sampling strategies improves robustness by exposing the model to a wider range of linguistic patterns and variations during training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of adversarial in-context learning vary across different types of perturbations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "adversarial in-context learning has limited effect in improving the robustness of prompt-based parsers" but does not specify whether this limitation varies across perturbation types.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides overall average results for adversarial in-context learning without breaking down the effectiveness by perturbation type.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of how adversarial in-context learning performs against each specific perturbation type (TB, RD, RS, CS, CI, RB, DB) would reveal whether certain perturbations are more susceptible to this approach than others.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between lexical diversity of in-context examples and robustness to sentence-level versus word-level perturbations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that sampling strategies with higher language complexity lead to stronger robustness, and that prompt-based models are more vulnerable to sentence-level perturbations.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't specifically examine whether lexical diversity has different effects on robustness to sentence-level versus word-level attacks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing robustness against sentence-level and word-level perturbations across sampling strategies with varying lexical diversity would reveal if this relationship differs by perturbation type.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the number of in-context examples needed for robustness improvement vary by dataset complexity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that more examples improve robustness, with GeoQuery requiring up to 50 examples while Scholar only needed 10 due to schema complexity differences.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't investigate whether this difference in required examples is consistent across other datasets or what factors beyond schema complexity determine the necessary number of examples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing multiple datasets with varying characteristics (size, complexity, domain specificity) while measuring robustness improvements at different example counts would identify patterns in how dataset properties affect the number of examples needed for robustness.

## Limitations
- The study focuses on a single large language model (Codex), limiting generalizability to other model architectures
- Only a limited set of attack types were examined, potentially missing other vulnerability patterns
- The active learning sampling strategy shows promise but needs validation on larger-scale experiments
