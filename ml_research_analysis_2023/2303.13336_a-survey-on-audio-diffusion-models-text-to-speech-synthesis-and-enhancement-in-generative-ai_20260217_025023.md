# A Survey on Audio Diffusion Models: Text To Speech Synthesis and Enhancement in Generative AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13336
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13336
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Comprehensive survey of diffusion-based methods for text-to-speech synthesis and speech enhancement

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive survey of audio diffusion models for text-to-speech synthesis and speech enhancement tasks. The survey covers the development of diffusion-based methods for acoustic modeling, vocoder, and end-to-end frameworks in text-to-speech synthesis, as well as speech enhancement by removing perturbations or adding missing parts. The authors categorize various speech enhancement tasks and provide comparisons of experimental results, highlighting the effectiveness of diffusion models in generating high-quality speech and their potential for adaptive modeling in multi-speaker settings.

## Method Summary
The survey categorizes diffusion-based approaches into three main categories for text-to-speech: acoustic modeling, vocoder, and end-to-end frameworks. For speech enhancement, it covers denoising, bandwidth extension, and dereverberation tasks. The methods are evaluated using standard speech quality metrics including MOS (Mean Opinion Score), PESQ, STOI, and SI-SDR. The survey synthesizes findings from 40+ references to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of audio diffusion models.

## Key Results
- Diffusion models effectively generate high-quality speech through iterative denoising from Gaussian noise
- Integration of diffusion models at multiple TTS stages (acoustic model, vocoder, end-to-end) improves synthesis quality
- Diffusion models show strong performance in speech enhancement by modeling complex noise distributions and generating clean speech from degraded inputs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Diffusion models generate high-quality speech by iteratively denoising from Gaussian noise toward the data distribution
- Mechanism: Forward process adds Gaussian noise in T steps, reverse process learns to denoise using estimated score function, converging to clean speech
- Core assumption: The score function can be accurately learned to guide denoising from any noise level
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The success of the diffusion model in computer vision has inspired numerous works for speech generation"
  - [section] "DDPM [23] consists of a forward process and a reverse process... During the forward process, DDPM adds noise to a clean image step by step until the image is destroyed"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct diffusion model citations in corpus, but related audio diffusion works exist
- Break condition: If score function estimation fails, denoising cannot converge to realistic speech

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Text-to-speech quality improves when diffusion models are integrated at multiple stages (acoustic model, vocoder, end-to-end)
- Mechanism: Different diffusion model placements handle different aspects - acoustic models generate Mel-spectrograms, vocoders generate waveforms, end-to-end models skip intermediate representations
- Core assumption: Each stage can benefit from diffusion's iterative refinement while maintaining coherent overall synthesis
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we divide the methods into three categories based on the stage where diffusion model is adopted: acoustic model, vocoder and end-to-end framework"
  - [section] "Most recent text-to-speech works on diffusion model follow the two-stage framework... which first generate acoustic features with a acoustic models, and then output waveform with a vocoder"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus - diffusion model citations focus on audio enhancement rather than text-to-speech
- Break condition: Stage-specific diffusion models may introduce inconsistencies between generated components

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Speech enhancement benefits from diffusion models' ability to model complex noise distributions and generate clean speech from degraded inputs
- Mechanism: Diffusion models can be conditioned on degraded speech and learn to reverse noise addition process, handling both time-frequency and time-domain enhancements
- Core assumption: The degradation process can be effectively modeled as noise addition, allowing reverse denoising to recover clean speech
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "diffusion models have also been widely used in improving the quality of existing degraded audio"
  - [section] "DiffuSE [61] is a pioneering work to apply diffusion model to speech enhancement, also known as audio denoising"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains related audio diffusion works but not specifically speech enhancement
- Break condition: If degradation is too severe or non-Gaussian, reverse process may fail to recover meaningful speech

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text encoder -> Duration predictor -> Diffusion-based acoustic model -> Diffusion-based vocoder/waveform generator
- Critical path: Text encoding and alignment → Diffusion-based acoustic feature generation → Diffusion-based waveform generation → Quality assessment and refinement
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Speed vs quality: More sampling steps improve quality but reduce inference speed
  - Conditioning strength: Stronger conditioning improves controllability but may limit diversity
  - Model capacity vs computational efficiency: Larger models capture more detail but require more resources
- Failure signatures:
  - Artifacts in generated speech: Often indicates score function estimation issues
  - Inconsistent speaker characteristics: May result from insufficient conditioning
  - Poor handling of rare phonetic contexts: Suggests training data limitations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate Mel-spectrograms from text using a pre-trained diffusion acoustic model
  2. Compare waveform quality using different sampling step counts
  3. Test cross-speaker adaptation by conditioning on reference audio embeddings

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can diffusion models be further optimized for real-time applications in text-to-speech synthesis?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The survey mentions that ProDiff [27] made diffusion models applicable to interactive, real-world speech synthesis applications at low computation, and BDDM [47] generated indistinguishable samples from human speech with only seven steps.
- Why unresolved: While some progress has been made in optimizing diffusion models for efficiency, there is still a need for further improvements to make them truly real-time and suitable for various interactive applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies evaluating the real-time performance of different diffusion-based text-to-speech models on various hardware platforms and application scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can diffusion models effectively handle audio restoration tasks with non-Gaussian noise characteristics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The survey mentions that CDiffuSE [62] formulated a generalized conditional diffusion model to incorporate observed noisy data, which facilitates estimating both Gaussian noise and non-Gaussian noise.
- Why unresolved: While CDiffuSE [62] showed some success in handling non-Gaussian noise, the effectiveness of diffusion models in various audio restoration tasks with different types of non-Gaussian noise remains to be fully explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluations of diffusion-based audio restoration models on diverse datasets with various types of non-Gaussian noise, comparing their performance to other state-of-the-art methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can diffusion models be extended to handle more complex audio tasks beyond speech synthesis and enhancement?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The survey discusses the application of diffusion models in source separation and voice conversion tasks, but there is potential for exploring their use in other audio domains.
- Why unresolved: The current survey focuses primarily on speech synthesis and enhancement, leaving room for investigating the potential of diffusion models in other audio domains such as music generation, audio inpainting, or audio classification.
- What evidence would resolve it: Novel applications of diffusion models to various audio tasks, along with comparative studies demonstrating their effectiveness against existing methods in those domains.

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on subjective MOS evaluations limits objective comparison capabilities
- Limited discussion of computational efficiency and inference speed trade-offs
- Field is rapidly evolving with many methods lacking extensive comparative validation

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: The survey's categorization of diffusion-based approaches into acoustic modeling, vocoder, and end-to-end frameworks is well-supported by the literature and provides a useful organizational structure for understanding the field's development.

**Medium Confidence**: Claims about diffusion models' effectiveness in generating high-quality speech are supported by emerging experimental results, though comprehensive comparative studies with established methods like WaveNet or Tacotron are still limited.

**Low Confidence**: Specific claims about controllability aspects (emotional intensity, speaker adaptation) require more rigorous validation, as most published works demonstrate these capabilities through limited examples rather than systematic evaluation.
