# On the contribution of pre-trained models to accuracy and utility in modeling distributed energy resources

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11679
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11679
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: Pre-trained models without fine-tuning can achieve comparable or superior performance to local models in distributed energy resource modeling

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether pre-trained models (PTMs) can accelerate data-driven modeling of distributed energy resources using quarter-hourly data from 24 heat pump hot water storage systems. The study compares PTMs against purely local models in terms of predictive accuracy and downstream utility. Results show that large pre-trained models without fine-tuning can achieve comparable or superior performance to local models, even with limited observational data. The research demonstrates that careful selection of PTM source corpus significantly impacts accuracy and enables near-optimal control strategies while reducing privacy concerns by keeping local data on-premises.

## Method Summary
The study uses quarter-hourly time series data from 24 identical heat pump hot water storage systems over one year, with 8 systems forming a source corpus and 16 as targets. Models include local neural networks trained on individual building data and pre-trained models trained on the source corpus with optional fine-tuning. The neural network architecture consists of two fully connected hidden layers with ReLU activation and L2 regularization. Performance is evaluated using mean absolute error (MAE) on holdout test sets and downstream control optimization for optimal heating demand. Models are compared across different data regimes (4, 8, 16, and 32 weeks of training data) to assess sample efficiency.

## Key Results
- Large pre-trained models without fine-tuning outperform local models in low-data regimes
- PTM performance significantly depends on source corpus selection
- Careful PTM selection enables near-optimal control strategies for distributed energy resources
- Privacy preservation is achieved by minimizing local fine-tuning requirements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-trained models without fine-tuning can match or outperform local models in low-data regimes
- Mechanism: The PTM has already learned the underlying system dynamics from a large corpus, so initial fine-tuning with small amounts of biased local data can actually degrade performance by overfitting to that bias
- Core assumption: The source corpus is sufficiently representative of the target system's dynamics, even if not its exact conditions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "large pre-trained models without fine-tuning can achieve comparable or superior performance to local models, even with limited observational data"
  - [section 3.1] "the best model in the low observational data regime is actually the large PTM without any fine-tuning"
- Break condition: If the source corpus is too small or unrepresentative of target system dynamics

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Careful selection of PTM source corpus is critical for optimal performance
- Mechanism: Different source models capture different aspects of the system dynamics; selecting the right one based on similarity in feature space can yield near-optimal results without extensive fine-tuning
- Core assumption: Similarity in input feature distributions between source and target systems correlates with transfer learning success
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The choice of PTM significantly affects accuracy, with careful selection enabling near-optimal control strategies"
  - [section 3.2] "we see that the model trained on the system's data itself is usually, but not always the best performing model"
- Break condition: If feature space similarity doesn't correlate with transfer success

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PTMs enable privacy-preserving modeling by keeping local data on-premises
- Mechanism: Since PTMs can achieve high accuracy without extensive local fine-tuning, the local data can be used solely for validation rather than model training, eliminating the need to share it
- Core assumption: The accuracy requirements for validation are lower than for training, allowing minimal local data usage
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "PTMs to reduce privacy concerns by keeping local data on-premises while maintaining high accuracy"
  - [section 4] "local data can stay on the user's premises, leading to a simultaneous improvement in both downstream utility and reduction in privacy leakage"
- Break condition: If validation requires substantial local data that still poses privacy risks

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data ingestion layer: quarter-hourly time series from heat pump systems
  - Feature extraction: time since last reheat, hot water demand, initial temperature
  - Model training: two fully connected hidden layers with normalization
  - Evaluation: holdout test set from last 3 months of each system's data

- Critical path:
  1. Collect source corpus data from multiple systems
  2. Train initial PTM on source corpus
  3. Deploy PTM to target systems
  4. Evaluate performance on holdout test sets
  5. Optionally fine-tune on local data

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Large source corpus vs. computational cost
  - Fine-tuning aggressiveness vs. overfitting to local bias
  - Model complexity vs. sample efficiency

- Failure signatures:
  - Large uncertainty bands in cross-system performance
  - Local models failing to meet user comfort constraints
  - PTM performance degrading with fine-tuning in low-data regimes

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare large PTM vs small PTM vs local model performance on holdout test set
  2. Test PTM performance with and without fine-tuning at different data volumes
  3. Evaluate cross-system PTM performance to identify selection criteria

## Limitations
- Limited generalizability to heterogeneous distributed energy resources beyond heat pump systems
- Uncertainty about optimal source corpus composition and its relationship to target systems
- Simplified control optimization framework may not capture real-world operational constraints

## Confidence
- PTM accuracy claims: Medium confidence (well-documented results but small sample size)
- Privacy preservation claims: High confidence (follows directly from architectural choices)
- Downstream utility claims: Medium confidence (simplified control framework used)
