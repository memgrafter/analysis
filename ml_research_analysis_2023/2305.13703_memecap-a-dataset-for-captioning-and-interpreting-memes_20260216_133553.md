# MemeCap: A Dataset for Captioning and Interpreting Memes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13703
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13703
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: State-of-the-art VL models (Flamingo, MiniGPT4, LLaMA) perform substantially worse than humans on meme captioning task

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MEME CAP, a novel dataset for meme captioning containing 6,384 memes with titles, meme captions, literal image captions, and visual metaphors. The task requires models to generate concise captions describing meme meaning by recognizing and interpreting visual metaphors with respect to the text. Experiments with state-of-the-art vision-and-language models show they struggle significantly with this task, performing worse than humans across multiple evaluation metrics. Models tend to ignore important visual or textual elements, repeat text inside memes, or hallucinate content.

## Method Summary
The MEME CAP dataset contains 6,384 memes with multiple textual and visual annotations. The paper tests three state-of-the-art VL models (Flamingo, MiniGPT4, LLaMA) in various setups: zero-shot, few-shot (4, 8, 12 shots), and fine-tuning for MiniGPT4. Models are evaluated with different input combinations including meme images, titles, image captions, and OCR-extracted text. Evaluation uses automatic metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-L, BERTScore) and human evaluation on correctness, length appropriateness, visual completeness, textual completeness, and faithfulness.

## Key Results
- VL models perform substantially worse than humans on meme captioning task
- Models struggle with visual metaphors, often ignoring important elements or making up content
- Best performance achieved with maximum input redundancy (image + title + caption + OCR text)
- Fine-tuning MiniGPT4 actually decreased performance compared to few-shot learning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Visual metaphors require joint reasoning across image and text modalities to capture intended meaning
- Mechanism: Memes combine literal visual content with metaphorical meaning, requiring models to ignore literal content and focus on inferred relationships between visual elements and textual context
- Core assumption: Models can learn to separate visual metaphors from literal image content through exposure to labeled examples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "interpreting visual metaphors with respect to the text inside or around the meme"
  - [section] "recognizing and interpreting visual metaphors with respect to the text inside or around the meme, often while employing background knowledge and reasoning abilities"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly address mechanism, only shows related work on memes
- Break condition: If models cannot distinguish between literal and metaphorical visual elements, performance will degrade significantly

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Chain-of-thought reasoning with explicit metaphor annotations improves model performance
- Mechanism: Providing explicit mappings between visual elements and their metaphorical meanings guides models through multi-step reasoning process
- Core assumption: Explicit annotation of metaphors acts as effective scaffolding for model reasoning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We also tested models in a Chain of Thought (CoT) style prompting (Wei et al., 2022)"
  - [section] "In our case, we elicit multi-step reasoning from the LLM by providing the visual metaphors"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus shows related work but doesn't confirm CoT effectiveness for visual metaphors
- Break condition: If model's language component cannot effectively process the additional metaphor annotations

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Redundant textual inputs (image captions + OCR text) improve performance by providing complementary information
- Mechanism: Multiple textual representations of the same visual content provide different perspectives that help models disambiguate meaning
- Core assumption: Different textual representations capture different aspects of visual content useful for metaphor interpretation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the best performance is achieved with as many of the inputs as possible, i.e. including both the image caption and the OCR text"
  - [section] "This might be due to suboptimal cross-modal interaction in VL models"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly address redundancy benefits
- Break condition: If model's cross-modal integration capabilities cannot effectively combine redundant information

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Visual metaphor recognition and interpretation
  - Why needed here: Memes rely heavily on visual metaphors that require specialized understanding beyond literal image description
  - Quick check question: Can you identify when a visual element is being used metaphorically vs. literally?

- Concept: Multimodal reasoning and integration
  - Why needed here: Meme meaning emerges from combining visual and textual elements, requiring models to reason across modalities
  - Quick check question: How would you combine information from an image and its accompanying text to infer meaning?

- Concept: Chain-of-thought reasoning with explicit reasoning steps
  - Why needed here: Complex metaphor interpretation benefits from explicit multi-step reasoning rather than direct inference
  - Quick check question: Can you break down how you would explain a visual metaphor using intermediate reasoning steps?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Image → Vision encoder (CLIP ViT/L-14 or BLIP-2) → Cross-attention → Combined representation → Language encoder (LLaMA or Vicuna) → Generation head

- Critical path:
  Image → Vision encoder → Cross-attention → Combined representation → Caption generation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Frozen vs. fine-tuned components: Frozen pre-trained models preserve general knowledge but may miss meme-specific patterns
  - Input redundancy vs. efficiency: Multiple textual inputs improve performance but increase computational cost
  - Zero-shot vs. few-shot learning: Zero-shot avoids data requirements but few-shot provides task-specific guidance

- Failure signatures:
  - Copying text from memes without interpretation indicates literal processing
  - Missing visual elements in generated captions suggests poor cross-modal integration
  - Hallucinated content indicates model filling knowledge gaps inappropriately

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation test: Remove OCR text input and measure performance drop
  2. Input ordering: Test different sequences of text inputs to find optimal arrangement
  3. Few-shot scaling: Test performance across 1, 4, 8, and 16 shots to find sweet spot

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of VL models on meme captioning compare to their performance on other multimodal tasks like image captioning or visual question answering?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "Despite the recent success of vision and language (VL) models on tasks such as image captioning and visual question answering, our extensive experiments using state-of-the-art VL models show that they still struggle with visual metaphors, and perform substantially worse than humans."
- Why unresolved: While the paper establishes that VL models perform worse on meme captioning compared to humans, it doesn't provide a direct comparison of their performance on meme captioning versus other established multimodal tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: A direct comparison of VL model performance metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) on meme captioning versus image captioning and VQA tasks using the same models and datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific aspects of visual metaphors do VL models struggle with the most - recognizing the metaphorical elements, interpreting their meaning, or generating appropriate captions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that models "tend to ignore important visual or textual elements, and instead, repeat the text inside the meme or make up fake elements."
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies general weaknesses in model performance, it doesn't break down which specific aspects of metaphor interpretation (recognition, understanding, or generation) are most problematic.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed error analysis categorizing model failures by type (recognition errors, interpretation errors, generation errors) and measuring performance on subtasks like metaphor identification vs. meaning inference.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would fine-tuning VL models on larger, more diverse meme datasets improve their performance on visual metaphor interpretation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that MiniGPT4's fine-tuning actually decreased performance, but this was only tested on the relatively small MEME CAP dataset. The authors note this is "consistent with Zhou et al. (2023), according to which most knowledge in LLM is learned during the pre-training stage."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests fine-tuning on the MEME CAP dataset, which may be too small or homogeneous to effectively teach models about visual metaphors. The question of whether larger, more diverse training data would help remains open.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments fine-tuning VL models on much larger meme datasets (e.g., 100K+ memes from multiple sources) and measuring performance gains on MEME CAP and other metaphor-rich test sets.

## Limitations
- MEME CAP dataset represents only a subset of meme types and cultural contexts
- Human evaluation conducted with only five annotators
- Focus exclusively on English-language memes
- Evaluation metrics may not fully capture creative and culturally nuanced aspects

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Current VL models struggle with meme captioning; models tend to copy text or hallucinate content
- Medium Confidence: Visual metaphors require specialized reasoning; effectiveness of Chain-of-Thought prompting
- Low Confidence: Specific ranking of model performance; input redundancy improving performance
