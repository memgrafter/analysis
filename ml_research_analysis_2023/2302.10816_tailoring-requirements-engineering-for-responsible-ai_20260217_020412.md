# Tailoring Requirements Engineering for Responsible AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10816
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10816
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: RE should be tailored for Responsible AI systems through six key areas including quality requirements, prototyping, data focus, terminology, tradeoff analysis, and testing foundations

## Executive Summary
This paper argues that traditional Requirements Engineering (RE) approaches are inadequate for developing Responsible AI systems, which frequently fail after deployment due to insufficient understanding of user, technical, and societal requirements. The authors propose that RE activities and templates need significant adjustment to address the unique challenges of AI development, particularly around data requirements and quality metrics. They identify six critical areas where RE must be tailored for AI contexts and call for further empirical research to validate these approaches in practice.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a conceptual framework based on literature review and expert analysis of existing challenges in AI system development. The authors synthesize current knowledge about Responsible AI failures and identify gaps in traditional RE practices when applied to AI systems. Through this analysis, they develop six proposed areas for tailoring RE to better support the development of responsible, reliable AI systems that meet both technical and societal requirements.

## Key Results
- Six key areas identified for tailoring RE to AI: quality requirements specification, prototyping bridge, data requirement focus, terminology integration, tradeoff analysis, and requirements-based testing
- Proposal for a common vocabulary for Responsible AI concepts to make them more concrete and testable
- Recognition that data and quality requirements need particular attention in AI RE processes
- Call for empirical studies to validate the effectiveness of tailored RE approaches in practice

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by recognizing that AI systems have fundamentally different requirements than traditional software, particularly around data quality, explainability, and fairness. By tailoring RE processes to address these unique characteristics, teams can better capture and validate the complex, often conflicting requirements that Responsible AI systems must satisfy. The mechanism involves making abstract Responsible AI concepts concrete through standardized terminology and testing criteria, while expanding traditional RE to include data-centric considerations throughout the development lifecycle.

## Foundational Learning

**Quality Requirements Specification**: Needed because AI systems require quantifiable thresholds for accuracy, fairness, and explainability rather than binary pass/fail criteria. Quick check: Can you specify measurable targets for explainability in your AI system requirements?

**Data-Centered Requirements**: Essential since AI system performance is fundamentally dependent on data quality, representativeness, and bias characteristics. Quick check: Does your RE process explicitly capture data requirements alongside functional requirements?

**Tradeoff Analysis Framework**: Required because AI systems often involve conflicting objectives (accuracy vs. explainability, fairness vs. performance). Quick check: Can you systematically evaluate and document the tradeoffs between competing AI system objectives?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: RE Process -> Quality Requirements -> Data Requirements -> Tradeoff Analysis -> Testing Framework -> Deployment Validation

**Critical Path**: The most critical path involves translating abstract Responsible AI concepts into concrete, testable requirements that can guide development and evaluation throughout the AI system lifecycle.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Balancing specificity of requirements against flexibility needed for AI system adaptation, versus capturing sufficient detail to enable meaningful testing and validation of Responsible AI properties.

**Failure Signatures**: Systems failing post-deployment due to: unaddressed bias in training data, insufficient explainability for end-users, unrealistic accuracy expectations, or inability to handle edge cases identified during requirements gathering.

**First Experiments**:
1. Map existing RE templates to AI-specific requirements categories to identify gaps
2. Develop prototype templates for specifying quality requirements with measurable thresholds
3. Create a vocabulary mapping exercise linking Responsible AI concepts to testable criteria

## Limitations

- The paper is largely conceptual without empirical validation of proposed approaches
- Lacks concrete examples demonstrating how tailored RE would work in actual AI projects
- Insufficient detail on how to measure success of tailored RE approaches in practice

## Confidence

**High confidence**: AI systems often fail post-deployment due to inadequate requirements understanding
**Medium confidence**: Proposed RE tailoring areas address known AI development challenges
**Low confidence**: Specific implementation recommendations lack validation and detail

## Next Checks

1. Conduct case studies in industry settings to evaluate whether proposed RE tailoring approaches reduce post-deployment failures compared to standard RE practices
2. Develop and validate metrics for measuring effectiveness of Responsible AI requirements, including how to quantify acceptable levels of explainability and fairness
3. Create pilot implementation of proposed common vocabulary for Responsible AI concepts and test its effectiveness in improving communication across interdisciplinary AI development teams
