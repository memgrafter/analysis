# Schema-Guided Semantic Accuracy: Faithfulness in Task-Oriented Dialogue Response Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.12568
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12568
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Key outcome:** Proposes SGSAcc, a metric using NLI to evaluate task-oriented dialogue response generation for both categorical and non-categorical slots, showing good human agreement and improved coverage over SER

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Schema-Guided Semantic Accuracy (SGSAcc), a new metric for evaluating task-oriented dialogue response generation that uses natural language inference to check if generated utterances entail references constructed from dialogue actions. SGSAcc addresses the limitation of traditional Slot Error Rate (SER) by evaluating both categorical and non-categorical slots through textual entailment. The authors also propose an ensemble method combining prefix-tuning and fine-tuning with SGSAcc reranking, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the Schema Guided Dialogue (SGD) dataset.

## Method Summary
The authors construct entailment references from dialogue actions using predefined rules and schema information, then use an NLI model (RoBERTa-large MNLI) to check if generated utterances entail these references. They train three models: fine-tuned T5-small, prefix-tuned T5-small (with prefix length 10), and an ensemble that uses SGSAcc to rerank outputs from both models. The ensemble selects the generation with the highest fidelity score, combining the strengths of both approaches to achieve high SGSAcc and low SER.

## Key Results
- SGSAcc shows good agreement with human judgment on the SGD dataset
- Prefix-tuning significantly improves faithfulness in unseen domains compared to fine-tuning
- The SGSAcc ensemble achieves the lowest SER reported and high SGSAcc on the SGD dataset
- SGSAcc improves coverage for non-categorical slots compared to traditional SER

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SGSAcc evaluates utterances from both categorical and non-categorical slots by checking textual entailment.
- Mechanism: The system converts dialogue actions into fluent sentences (entailment references) and uses an NLI model to check if generated utterances entail these references.
- Core assumption: NLI models trained on free-running texts can perform RTE correctly without further fine-tuning when given properly constructed entailment references.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose Schema-Guided Semantic Accuracy (SGSAcc) to evaluate utterances generated from both categorical and non-categorical slots by recognizing textual entailment."
  - [section]: "Following their design, we first convert the dialogue actions into fluent sentences to serve as the hypothesis, so that the NLI model trained on free-running texts can perform RTE correctly without further fine-tuning."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for NLI RTE capability without fine-tuning.
- Break condition: The NLI model fails to correctly recognize entailment between generated utterances and entailment references, or the entailment references are poorly constructed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prefix-tuning significantly improves faithfulness in unseen domains compared to fine-tuning.
- Mechanism: Prefix-tuning inserts trainable key-value pairs at each attention layer while fixing other parameters during training, enhancing generalization to unseen data.
- Core assumption: Prefix-tuning maintains better generalization to unseen domains than fine-tuning because it doesn't overwrite the pre-trained model's knowledge as extensively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We empirically show that prefix-tuning significantly improves faithfulness in unseen domains on the Schema Guided Dialogue dataset."
  - [section]: "We found that prefix-tuning significantly improved SGSAcc in unseen domains, whereas the fine-tuning model achieved lower SER in comparison."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for prefix-tuning's generalization advantage over fine-tuning.
- Break condition: Prefix-tuning fails to generalize to unseen domains or performs worse than fine-tuning in terms of faithfulness metrics.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The SGSAcc ensemble with fidelity reranker combines the advantages of fine-tuning and prefix-tuning models.
- Mechanism: The ensemble uses SGSAcc to assign fidelity scores to each generation from fine-tuning and prefix-tuning models, selecting the generation with the highest fidelity score.
- Core assumption: The fidelity scores assigned by SGSAcc accurately reflect the faithfulness of generated utterances, allowing effective selection between fine-tuning and prefix-tuning outputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We build an ensemble of prefix-tuning and fine-tuning models that achieves the lowest SER reported and high SGSAcc on the SGD dataset."
  - [section]: "We further build an ensemble of prefix-tuning and fine-tuning models that achieves the lowest SER reported and high SGSAcc on the SGD dataset."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for ensemble effectiveness in improving both SER and SGSAcc.
- Break condition: The fidelity scores do not accurately reflect faithfulness, leading to poor selection between model outputs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Construct entailment references -> Check entailment using NLI model -> Evaluate faithfulness using SGSAcc -> Select best generation using ensemble reranker

- **Critical path:** Dialogue actions → Entailment reference construction → NLI model evaluation → SGSAcc scoring → Model selection (ensemble)

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Using NLI model without fine-tuning vs. fine-tuning on specific dataset
  - Constructing entailment references using rules vs. manual templates
  - Prefix-tuning vs. fine-tuning for unseen domains
  - Ensemble approach vs. single best model

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low agreement between SGSAcc and human judgment
  - Poor performance of generative models on unseen domains
  - High error rates in constructing entailment references
  - Ineffective selection by fidelity reranker

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Validate SGSAcc by comparing its evaluations with human judgments on a sampled dataset.
  2. Compare prefix-tuning and fine-tuning models on seen and unseen domains using SER and SGSAcc metrics.
  3. Evaluate the effectiveness of the SGSAcc ensemble by comparing its performance with individual models on the full test set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several implicit research directions emerge from the limitations and findings.

## Limitations

- SGSAcc relies on schema information, making it inapplicable to datasets without schema definitions
- Weak corpus support for the core assumption that NLI models can perform RTE without fine-tuning on task-oriented dialogue data
- Limited evaluation of prefix-tuning's generalization advantage across multiple datasets
- No systematic evaluation of how different entailment reference construction rules affect SGSAcc performance

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- SGSAcc shows good agreement with human judgment on the SGD dataset
- SGSAcc significantly improves coverage over SER for non-categorical slots
- The ensemble approach achieves state-of-the-art SER on the SGD dataset

**Medium Confidence:**
- NLI models can accurately perform RTE without fine-tuning on task-oriented dialogue data
- Prefix-tuning provides superior generalization to unseen domains compared to fine-tuning
- Fidelity scores from SGSAcc reliably select the best generation between models

## Next Checks

**Validation Check 1:** Test SGSAcc agreement with human judgments on a different task-oriented dialogue dataset (e.g., MultiWOZ) to verify generalizability beyond SGD.

**Validation Check 2:** Conduct ablation studies on the NLI model fine-tuning, comparing SGSAcc performance with and without fine-tuning on task-oriented dialogue specific data.

**Validation Check 3:** Evaluate the prefix-tuning and fine-tuning comparison across multiple unseen domains and datasets to verify the generalization claims beyond the single SGD validation.
