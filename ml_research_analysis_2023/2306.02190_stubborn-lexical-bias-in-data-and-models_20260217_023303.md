# Stubborn Lexical Bias in Data and Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.02190
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02190
- **Reference count**: 23
- **Primary result**: Statistical framework using permutation tests reveals strong lexical biases in SNLI, QNLI, QQP datasets; reweighting reduces bias in data but models remain biased, especially for bigram features.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a statistical framework to detect and quantify lexical biases in both data and trained models. Using a permutation test, the authors measure whether models exploit spurious correlations between word features and labels, finding strong evidence of bias across three datasets (SNLI, QNLI, QQP). To mitigate these biases, they develop an optimization-based reweighting method that adjusts instance weights to balance label distributions conditioned on lexical features. While reweighting reduces bias in the data, models trained on the reweighted data still exhibit bias, particularly for bigram features, suggesting that data-level interventions alone are insufficient for debiasing NLP models.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a permutation test to statistically detect lexical biases in datasets and trained models. For a given lexical feature and label, they compare the observed correlation in the data or model predictions to a null distribution generated by randomly permuting labels. This allows quantification of bias strength and statistical significance. To mitigate bias, they formulate an optimization problem that reweights training instances to minimize the difference between label distributions conditioned on lexical features. The reweighting is applied during training, and models are evaluated for residual bias post-training using the same permutation test framework.

## Key Results
- Strong lexical biases detected in SNLI, QNLI, and QQP datasets using permutation tests
- Reweighting successfully reduces bias in training data but not fully in trained models
- Bigram features show particularly stubborn bias, persisting even after reweighting
- Data-level interventions alone are insufficient for complete debiasing

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The permutation test provides a principled statistical method to detect whether lexical features are spuriously correlated with labels beyond what would be expected by chance. By comparing observed correlations to a null distribution, the test quantifies the strength and significance of bias. The reweighting method works by adjusting instance weights to balance label distributions conditioned on lexical features, effectively reducing the signal available to models to exploit these spurious correlations. However, the persistence of bias in models, especially for bigrams, suggests that models may learn more complex, higher-order correlations or that the optimization landscape makes it difficult to fully eliminate bias.

## Foundational Learning
- **Permutation test**: Statistical test comparing observed statistic to null distribution from random permutations; needed to quantify bias significance without distributional assumptions
- **Spurious correlation**: Non-causal statistical relationship between features and labels; critical concept for understanding why models exploit lexical biases
- **Instance reweighting**: Adjusting training sample importance to modify learned distributions; technique for data-level bias mitigation
- **Label distribution balancing**: Ensuring labels are evenly distributed across lexical feature values; goal of reweighting to reduce bias
- **Bigram features**: Two-word sequences used as lexical features; shown to be particularly challenging for bias mitigation

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Permutation test → Bias quantification → Reweighting optimization → Trained model → Bias evaluation

**Critical path**: The permutation test identifies biased feature-label pairs, which inform the reweighting optimization. The reweighted data is then used to train models, which are evaluated for residual bias using the permutation test.

**Design tradeoffs**: The permutation test is computationally expensive but provides statistical rigor. Reweighting balances bias reduction with maintaining data utility, but aggressive reweighting may hurt model performance. Focusing on unigrams and bigrams simplifies analysis but may miss higher-order biases.

**Failure signatures**: If reweighting is too aggressive, model performance on downstream tasks may degrade. If the permutation test is not properly calibrated, false positives/negatives in bias detection may occur. Residual bias in models suggests limitations of data-level interventions alone.

**First experiments**: 1) Apply permutation test to new datasets to assess bias prevalence, 2) Evaluate reweighting impact on model performance to quantify bias-utility tradeoff, 3) Test reweighting with different lexical feature granularities (e.g., trigrams) to understand bias complexity

## Limitations
- Reweighting reduces bias in data but not fully in models, especially for bigrams
- Limited to unigram and bigram lexical features, potentially missing higher-order biases
- Does not explore complementary debiasing strategies beyond data reweighting
- Evaluation focuses on bias detection rather than downstream task performance

## Confidence

**Medium Confidence**: Data reweighting reduces bias in training data but models remain biased, especially for bigrams

**Low Confidence**: Data-level interventions alone are insufficient for complete debiasing; needs further validation across diverse datasets and models
