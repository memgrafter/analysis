# Causal-Discovery Performance of ChatGPT in the context of Neuropathic Pain Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13819
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13819
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: ChatGPT achieved 0.12 precision and 0.21 recall on causal discovery questions about neuropathic pain diagnosis

## Executive Summary
This study evaluated ChatGPT's ability to answer causal discovery questions in the context of neuropathic pain diagnosis. Using a medical benchmark with 100 sampled causal pairs (50 positive, 50 negative), the authors tested ChatGPT's performance by asking questions in the format "X causes Y. Answer true or false." The results showed high precision (0.12) but low recall (0.21), with the model tending to make false negative mistakes. Qualitative analysis revealed that ChatGPT could identify causal relationships in some cases but failed to understand underlying medical knowledge, such as the anatomical regions affected by specific nerve conditions.

## Method Summary
The authors sampled 100 causal pairs from a medical benchmark (50 positive, 50 negative) and queried ChatGPT with each pair formatted as "X causes Y. Answer true or false." They compared ChatGPT's responses against ground truth to calculate precision, recall, and F-score. The evaluation focused on ChatGPT's ability to identify causal relationships in neuropathic pain diagnosis without any training or fine-tuning of the model.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieved precision of 0.12 and recall of 0.21 on causal discovery tasks
- The model showed high false negative rates, tending to incorrectly answer "false" for true causal relationships
- Performance was inconsistent across different test days, with fewer positive answers on the second day

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT's causal inference is limited to linguistic pattern matching rather than genuine medical knowledge integration.
- Mechanism: The model maps input phrases to learned associations in its training corpus but does not construct internal causal graphs or understand anatomical relationships. This leads to high precision (few false positives) but low recall (many false negatives) because it only answers confidently when it recognizes exact textual patterns.
- Core assumption: The model's architecture lacks explicit causal reasoning modules and relies solely on distributional language patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT tends to make false negative mistakes" and "it provides the answer based on the trained content but does not understand the human body's nervous system"
  - [section] "It only understands the languages that are typically used to describe the situations but not the underlying knowledge"
  - [corpus] No strong causal reasoning benchmarks in neighbors; average neighbor FMR 0.533 suggests moderate topical overlap but not causal modeling.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Performance inconsistency stems from internal model updates and language detection failures.
- Mechanism: The model's inference behavior changes across days due to potential fine-tuning or prompt interpretation shifts. Additionally, when it fails to recognize non-English terms (e.g., Swedish), it incorrectly treats them as irrelevant, increasing false negatives.
- Core assumption: ChatGPT's session state and language detection are non-deterministic.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Its performance is not consistent and not stable... different answers to the same question" and "the answers on the second day are much more conservative to claim a causal relationship"
  - [section] "ChatGPT can correctly identify Swedish in some cases... but fails in some other cases"
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence of language detection mechanisms; must be inferred from observed behavior.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Benchmark design biases against causal discovery because it relies on known ground truth rather than novel causal inference.
- Mechanism: The evaluation uses pairs with known causal status, forcing the model to retrieve rather than discover. This mismatch means good performance would imply memorization, not true causal discovery capability.
- Core assumption: Causal discovery should work on novel data, not pre-labeled pairs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Causal benchmarks may be biased towards utilizing existing knowledge for evaluation, which is against the goal of causal discovery"
  - [section] "Causal discovery aims to uncover the underlying unknown causal relationships based purely on observational data. In contrast, applying ChatGPT... is fundamentally different"
  - [corpus] No neighbor papers discuss causal discovery benchmarks; weak corpus support.

- Why unresolved: The study only observes this inconsistency without investigating its root causes or proposing solutions to improve stability.

## Limitations
- The dataset composition and sampling methodology are not fully specified, making it difficult to assess representativeness
- The evaluation only tested ChatGPT on pre-existing causal pairs rather than its ability to discover novel causal relationships from observational data
- Performance inconsistency across different test days suggests non-deterministic behavior influenced by model updates or session-specific factors

## Confidence

- High confidence: The observed precision (0.12) and recall (0.21) values are mathematically correct given the stated methodology.
- Medium confidence: The qualitative interpretation that ChatGPT relies on pattern matching rather than genuine causal reasoning is supported by the results but requires additional controlled experiments for definitive validation.
- Medium confidence: The performance inconsistency across days is well-documented but the underlying causes (model updates vs. session state) remain speculative.
