# Optimizing Non-Autoregressive Transformers with Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13667
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13667
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Non-autoregressive Transformers (NATs) are faster than autoregressive models but struggle to learn the multi-modal nature of the data distribution, resulting in performance gaps. This paper proposes a contrastive learning framework to ease modality learning by sampling from the model distribution instead of the data distribution. The approach derives contrastive constraints to stabilize training and integrates them with the state-of-the-art DA-Transformer architecture. Experiments on machine translation, summarization, and paraphrasing tasks with five benchmarks show that the proposed method significantly outperforms previous NAT baselines and establishes new state-of-the-art results. On WMT14 En↔De, the method achieves BLEU scores of 27.40/31.64, outperforming the previous best NAT results by a large margin.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the performance gap between non-autoregressive Transformers (NATs) and autoregressive models by proposing a contrastive learning framework. NATs struggle to capture the multi-modal nature of the data distribution, leading to lower performance. The proposed approach samples from the model distribution instead of the data distribution, easing modality learning and improving the model's ability to rank high-quality hypotheses. The method is integrated with the DA-Transformer architecture and evaluated on machine translation, summarization, and paraphrasing tasks, establishing new state-of-the-art results for NATs.

## Method Summary
The method proposes a contrastive learning objective derived from the reverse KL divergence between the data distribution and the model distribution. Instead of sampling from the data distribution, the approach samples from the model distribution, which tends to be single-modal and easier for NATs to learn. Contrastive constraints are derived from the data distribution and imposed on the model distribution to guide the ranking of hypotheses based on a reward function like BLEU. The objective is combined with the DP training loss from DA-Transformer for stabilization. The model is trained by first pretraining with DP loss for 120 epochs, then fine-tuning with the combined DP and contrastive loss for 5 epochs. During inference, multinomial sampling is used to maintain the speedup advantage of NATs.

## Key Results
- The proposed method significantly outperforms previous NAT baselines on machine translation, summarization, and paraphrasing tasks.
- On WMT14 English↔German, the method achieves BLEU scores of 27.40/31.64, establishing new state-of-the-art results for NATs.
- The approach effectively reduces the performance gap between NATs and autoregressive models while maintaining the inference speedup advantage of NATs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Optimizing the model distribution directly instead of the data distribution reduces the multi-modality problem in NATs.
- Mechanism: The contrastive learning objective D(q∥p) focuses the model on samples from the model distribution q(·|x), which tend to be single-modal, instead of the multi-modal data distribution p(·|x). This is achieved by deriving contrastive constraints that penalize violations of ranking conditions based on reward functions like BLEU.
- Core assumption: The model distribution q(·|x) can capture high-quality, single-modal samples that are easier for NATs to learn than the full multi-modal data distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose to ease the difficulty of modality learning via sampling from the model distribution instead of the data distribution."
  - [section 3.1]: "We suggest that the above problem can be alleviated by replacing the samples from p(·|x) with the samples from q(·|x), therefore dropping some modalities that are difficult for the NATs to capture in the learning procedure."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. No explicit discussion of multi-modality in corpus neighbors, though related papers on NATs exist.
- Break condition: If the model distribution q(·|x) fails to capture high-quality, single-modal samples, the contrastive learning objective will not effectively reduce the multi-modality problem.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The contrastive constraints derived from the data distribution guide the model to rank high-quality hypotheses higher.
- Mechanism: The contrastive objective penalizes pairs of hypotheses (y'i, y'j) where R(y'i, y) > R(y'j, y) but q(y'i|x) < q(y'j|x), encouraging the model to rank higher-reward hypotheses higher in probability. This is implemented by sampling K hypotheses from the model distribution and applying the constraints to pairs.
- Core assumption: The reward function R(·, ·) (e.g., BLEU) provides a meaningful ranking of hypotheses that correlates with their quality and modality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "We derive a set of constraints from the data distribution and impose them to the model distribution. We find that in theory leads to a contrastive learning objective."

- Break condition: If the reward function R(·, ·) does not provide a meaningful ranking of hypotheses, or if the model fails to learn the ranking, the contrastive constraints will not effectively guide the model.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The DP training loss acts as a regularizer to prevent the model from collapsing to a single, low-quality modality.
- Mechanism: The contrastive learning objective D(q∥p) is combined with the KL divergence KL(p∥q) between the data and model distributions. KL(p∥q) acts as a regularizer, preventing the model from collapsing to a single, low-quality modality by ensuring it still assigns reasonable probability to samples from the data distribution.
- Core assumption: The DP training loss provides a stable baseline that prevents the model from collapsing during contrastive learning.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Encoder -> DA-Transformer decoder with extended decoder length (8x encoder length) -> Contrastive loss -> DP loss -> Adam optimizer

- Critical path:
  1. Warmup DA-Transformer with DP loss for 120 epochs.
  2. Train with combined DP loss and contrastive loss for 5 epochs.
  3. Sample K hypotheses from the model distribution using nucleus sampling.
  4. Apply contrastive constraints to pairs of hypotheses based on reward function.
  5. Update model parameters using Adam optimizer.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sampling size K vs. training/inference efficiency: Larger K provides more diverse hypotheses but increases computational cost.
  - Decoder length vs. sampling space: Longer decoder length provides larger sampling space but increases computational cost.
  - Reward function R(·, ·) vs. correlation with quality: Different reward functions may have different correlations with hypothesis quality.

- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability: If the contrastive loss causes the DP loss to increase significantly, the model may collapse.
  - Poor performance: If the model fails to learn the ranking or the reward function does not correlate with quality, the contrastive constraints will not improve performance.
  - Low efficiency: If the sampling size K or decoder length is too large, the model may become computationally expensive.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary the sampling size K and measure its impact on performance and efficiency.
  2. Vary the decoder length and measure its impact on performance and efficiency.
  3. Try different reward functions R(·, ·) and measure their impact on performance.

## Limitations
- The experimental validation is primarily focused on translation tasks, with limited evaluation on summarization and paraphrasing.
- The sampling-based inference mechanism introduces complexity and may not be practical for all deployment scenarios.
- The contrastive learning objective is derived from theoretical considerations but lacks empirical ablation studies to isolate the contribution of individual components.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The integration of contrastive learning with the DA-Transformer architecture is sound and well-implemented. The experimental results demonstrate significant performance improvements over previous NAT baselines on the tested benchmarks. The sampling-based inference mechanism provides a practical speedup compared to autoregressive models.
- Medium Confidence: The contrastive learning objective effectively reduces the multi-modality problem in NATs by focusing on single-modal samples from the model distribution. The contrastive constraints derived from the data distribution guide the model to rank high-quality hypotheses higher. The DP training loss acts as a regularizer to prevent the model from collapsing to a single, low-quality modality.
- Low Confidence: The Oracle BLEU score improvement is solely attributed to the contrastive learning approach and not other factors such as model architecture or training procedure. The contrastive learning objective is universally effective across all sequence generation tasks, including those not evaluated in the paper. The sampling-based inference mechanism is the optimal approach for deploying NATs in real-world applications.
