# Generating robust counterfactual explanations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12943
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12943
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: CROCO framework generates counterfactual explanations that outperform PROBE in both proximity and robustness while meeting user-specified robustness targets.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CROCO, a framework for generating robust counterfactual explanations for machine learning models. The key innovation is the soft recourse invalidation rate, which provides a differentiable upper bound on the true recourse invalidation rate, enabling gradient-based optimization while ensuring robustness guarantees. CROCO addresses the challenge of balancing proximity to the original example with robustness to input perturbations, outperforming existing methods on three tabular datasets.

## Method Summary
CROCO introduces a novel estimator for an upper bound of the recourse invalidation rate that is differentiable and can be directly optimized. The framework uses Monte Carlo sampling to estimate the soft recourse invalidation rate and enforces an almost-sure upper bound guarantee through confidence intervals. The method is implemented as a gradient-based optimization that balances proximity to the original input with validity and robustness constraints, without requiring assumptions about the noise distribution.

## Key Results
- CROCO achieves better trade-offs between proximity and robustness compared to PROBE and Wachter methods
- The framework successfully maintains user-specified upper bounds on recourse invalidation rates across three datasets
- CROCO demonstrates particular effectiveness on datasets with mixed numerical and categorical features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The soft recourse invalidation rate Θ provides a tighter, differentiable upper bound on the true recourse invalidation rate Γ than the Monte Carlo estimator used in PROBE.
- Mechanism: By replacing the non-differentiable step function h with the differentiable model f in the expectation, Θ(˘x;pε) = Eε∼pε [1−f (˘x +ε)] can be optimized directly in the loss function, enabling gradient-based updates.
- Core assumption: The model's predicted probability f(x) is a good proxy for the true classification boundary, so 1−f(˘x +ε) approximates the invalidation risk.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] Proposition 1 states Γ (˘x;pε) ≤ Θ (˘x;pε)/(1−t), and the estimator ˜Θ is differentiable.
  - [abstract] "Our framework introduces the novel concept of soft recourse invalidation rate, as well as an estimator of it."
- Break condition: If f is poorly calibrated or outputs probabilities near 0 or 1, the approximation 1−f(x) may not reflect true invalidation risk, weakening the bound.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The almost-sure upper bound with confidence m ensures user-specified robustness targets are met with high probability.
- Mechanism: Proposition 2 gives P(Γ (˘x;pε) ≤ m + ˜Θ(˘x;K,pε)/(1−t)) ≥ 1−exp(−2m²K), so with large K and chosen m, the bound holds almost surely.
- Core assumption: The Monte Carlo estimator ˜Θ converges sufficiently fast and is unbiased for the expectation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Proposition 2 shows that our estimator, ˜Θ, defines an almost-sure upper bound of the true recourse invalidation rate."
  - [section] "If we enforce m + ˜Θ(˘x;K,pε)/(1−t) to be lower than a given threshold ¯Γt, then we are almost-sure that the true recourse invalidation rate is lower than ¯Γt."
- Break condition: If the noise distribution pε is heavy-tailed or K is too small, the exponential bound may not hold, breaking the robustness guarantee.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Directly optimizing the squared error ( ˜Θ + m)/(1−t) − ¯Γt)² enforces the upper bound constraint while balancing proximity and validity.
- Mechanism: This loss term penalizes solutions whose soft upper bound exceeds the target, steering optimization toward counterfactuals that satisfy the robustness constraint and are close to the original input.
- Core assumption: The differentiable surrogate ( ˜Θ + m)/(1−t) approximates the true invalidation risk closely enough for optimization to succeed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The first term encourages our new estimator to be close to a target value ¯Γt, i.e. the target upper-bound of the recourse invalidation rate."
  - [abstract] "CROCO uses a novel concept called 'soft recourse invalidation rate' and an associated estimator to derive an almost-sure upper bound on the true recourse invalidation rate."
- Break condition: If the loss landscape is too flat or has many local minima, gradient descent may not find a solution meeting both robustness and proximity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data loader → Neural net model f → Gradient-based optimizer (CROCO loop) → Output counterfactual
  Auxiliary: Sampling module for ε, validation metrics (Γ, distance, validity)

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize δ=0.
  2. Sample K perturbations ε from pε.
  3. Compute ˜Θ via f(x+δ+ε).
  4. Check if f(x+δ)≥t and (m+˜Θ)/(1−t)≤¯Γt.
  5. If not, update δ via ∇δLCROCO and repeat.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Larger K → tighter bound but higher computation.
  - Larger m → easier to satisfy bound but looser guarantee.
  - λ → controls proximity vs. validity trade-off.

- Failure signatures:
  - Infinite loop: δ not converging to satisfy constraints.
  - Divergence: δ growing unbounded, indicating poor λ or step size.
  - High invalidation rates despite target: m too small or K too small.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run CROCO on Adult dataset with σ²=0.01, K=500, m=0.1, verify convergence and that all counterfactuals are valid.
  2. Compare Γ vs. target for PROBE and CROCO on GSC dataset, confirm CROCO's robustness guarantee holds.
  3. Vary m and K systematically, measure how the bound tightness changes and compute runtime overhead.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper mentions future work directions including extending CROCO to handle categorical variables, but does not explicitly call out open questions in the main text.

## Limitations
- The framework only handles numerical features, with categorical feature extension noted as future work
- Performance depends on model calibration quality for the soft recourse invalidation rate approximation to be effective
- Computational cost increases with larger K values needed for tighter bounds

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Θ as differentiable upper bound): High confidence - theoretically grounded with clear mathematical derivation
- Mechanism 2 (almost-sure upper bound guarantee): Medium confidence - relies on Monte Carlo convergence assumptions that may not hold in all scenarios
- Mechanism 3 (direct optimization of squared error): Medium confidence - practical effectiveness depends on loss landscape characteristics
