# Revisiting the Noise Model of Stochastic Gradient Descent

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02749
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02749
- Reference count: 40
- This paper demonstrates that stochastic gradient noise (SGN) in deep neural networks is better characterized by heavy-tailed SαS Lévy distributions rather than the commonly assumed Gaussian distribution.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the conventional Gaussian assumption of stochastic gradient noise (SGN) in deep learning by providing extensive empirical evidence that SGN follows heavy-tailed SαS Lévy distributions. Through experiments on ResNet and BERT architectures across multiple datasets, the authors show that different network parameters have distinct noise characteristics characterized by individual stability parameters αᵢ. They develop a novel framework using Lévy-driven stochastic differential equations to model SGD dynamics near local minima, enabling analysis of escape times and trapping probabilities. The framework reveals that learning rate decay primarily affects noise amplitude rather than step size, and parameters with lower α values are more likely to facilitate escape from local minima.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct extensive experiments training ResNet variants (18, 34, 50) and BERT-base models using SGD without momentum and weight decay. They sample random parameters from pre-trained models, compute gradients across all mini-batches to estimate SGN, and fit these to different distributions (Gaussian, SαS with constant α, and SαS with parameter-specific αᵢ). The framework uses Lévy-driven stochastic differential equations with N independent Lévy processes to model SGD dynamics near local minima. Escape time experiments are conducted on 3-layer neural networks trained on UCI datasets, measuring mean escape time across different learning rates and batch sizes. Theoretical predictions from the SDE framework are validated against empirical observations.

## Key Results
- SGN is empirically shown to be heavy-tailed and better modeled by SαS distributions than Gaussian, with fitting errors significantly lower for SαS models
- Different DNN parameters exhibit distinct SGN characteristics with varying stability parameters αᵢ, rather than sharing a single α value
- Learning rate decay primarily attenuates SGN amplitude rather than merely reducing step size, affecting convergence through noise reduction
- Parameters with lower α values are more likely to aid in escaping local minima, with escape direction determined by relative α values between dimensions
- Theoretical predictions for mean escape time, trapping probability, and escape direction align well with empirical observations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The heavy-tailed SαS Lévy distribution of stochastic gradient noise (SGN) better captures the true noise structure compared to Gaussian.
- Mechanism: SGN is generated from minibatch sampling and exhibits jump discontinuities and tail heaviness that violate central limit theorem assumptions for finite samples.
- Core assumption: SGN noise samples across multiple minibatches follow SαS rather than Gaussian.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states "SGN is heavy-tailed and better depicted by the SαS distribution."
  - [section 3.1] derives the covariance matrix assuming SαS noise and compares it with Gaussian.
  - [corpus] neighbors include "Revisiting Gradient Normalization and Clipping for Nonconvex SGD under Heavy-Tailed Noise."
- Break condition: If empirical SGN histograms are symmetric and light-tailed, SαS assumption fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Different DNN parameters have distinct SGN characteristics, each with its own stability parameter αᵢ.
- Mechanism: Parameter-specific noise arises from varying gradient sensitivities across layers and dimensions, leading to heterogeneous αᵢ values.
- Core assumption: SGN for each parameter i is independent and characterized by its own αᵢ.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] claims "different parameters in a DNN hold distinct SGN characteristics throughout training."
  - [section 5] shows empirical histograms of αᵢ for multiple parameters.
  - [corpus] neighbors include "Momentum Does Not Reduce Stochastic Noise in Stochastic Gradient Descent."
- Break condition: If all parameters exhibit similar noise behavior, single α assumption suffices.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Learning rate decay primarily attenuates SGN amplitude rather than merely reducing step size.
- Mechanism: Lower learning rate reduces the magnitude of large jumps in SαS noise, improving convergence without changing deterministic drift.
- Core assumption: The scheduler sₜ scales both gradient step and noise amplitude proportionally.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states "LRdecay effect may stem from the reduction of the SGN and not the decrease in the step size."
  - [section 5] shows empirical SGN amplitude reduction after decay.
  - [corpus] neighbors include "Noise Balance and Stationary Distribution of Stochastic Gradient Descent."
- Break condition: If step size reduction is the dominant effect, noise amplitude change is secondary.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do different parameters in a deep neural network hold different SGN distributions characterized by distinct α values?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe empirically that different parameters have noise that distributes differently during training, with varying αi values across the network.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates this phenomenon exists, it doesn't provide theoretical justification for why this occurs. The paper notes this as an open question requiring future research.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the relationship between parameter positions in the network architecture, their role in gradient computation, and their αi values could reveal patterns. Comparative studies across different architectures might identify systematic factors.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the α parameter evolve during training?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes this as an unclear aspect, stating "how α evolves in time is still unclear and demands future research."
- Why unresolved: The framework models SGN with fixed αi values, but real training involves dynamic changes. The temporal evolution of αi would impact the accuracy of escape time predictions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Tracking αi values throughout training epochs for various architectures and datasets would reveal patterns. Correlating αi evolution with training stages, learning rate schedules, or other hyperparameters could provide insights.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the specific roles of different parameters in the optimization process?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "It is also unclear why different parameters hold different SGN distributions, and what are the roles of each parameter in the optimization process."
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows parameters with lower αi are more likely to aid escape from local minima, it doesn't explain the broader functional significance of parameter-specific noise characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments perturbing individual parameters and measuring effects on training dynamics, generalization, and convergence would clarify their roles. Network pruning studies focusing on parameters with different αi values could reveal their importance.

## Limitations

- The universality of SαS characterization across all DNN architectures and optimization regimes remains uncertain, as the paper primarily demonstrates this on ResNets and BERT
- The framework relies on continuous-time approximations that may not fully capture discrete SGD dynamics, potentially limiting the accuracy of escape-time predictions
- Finite-sample estimation of α from heavy-tailed distributions can be unstable, introducing uncertainty in the characterization of parameter-specific noise

## Confidence

- **High**: Empirical histograms showing heavier tails than Gaussian for CIFAR/CoLA SGN samples; clear improvement in fit when moving from Gaussian to SαS per parameter
- **Medium**: Claims that LR decay mainly attenuates noise amplitude (mechanism clear, but quantitative attribution to step-size vs noise is indirect)
- **Low**: Generalization of SαS universality to all DNN architectures and optimization regimes without further validation
