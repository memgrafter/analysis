# Predictive Coding as a Neuromorphic Alternative to Backpropagation: A Critical Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02658
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02658
- Reference count: 14
- This work proves that predictive coding variants have worse time complexity than backpropagation under identical problem settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper critically evaluates whether modified predictive coding (PC) variants can serve as practical alternatives to backpropagation for deep learning, particularly in neuromorphic hardware contexts. Through rigorous theoretical analysis and empirical validation, the authors demonstrate that PC variants like FPA-PC and Z-IL, despite appearing to offer local and parallel updates, actually have provably worse time complexity than backpropagation. The study reveals that achieving backpropagation-equivalent weight updates requires specific modifications that break the generative variational Bayes framework that originally motivated PC. These findings suggest that PC may have more limited potential as a direct replacement for backpropagation than previously envisioned.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze various PC variants (FPA-PC, Z-IL, G-IL) by deriving their computational complexity and comparing them to standard backpropagation. They implement FPA-PC inference equations with fixed prediction assumptions and test on MLP networks using MNIST. The analysis includes time complexity bounds using VJP computation costs, empirical validation by varying inference steps and learning rates, and cosine similarity measurements between PC parameter updates and BP gradients. The theoretical proofs establish lower bounds on PC complexity relative to backpropagation, while empirical results validate these bounds across different network architectures and inference configurations.

## Key Results
- FPA-PC and Z-IL variants achieve backpropagation-equivalent weight updates through specific inference sequences, but require L sequential steps for an L-layer network
- PC variants have provably worse time complexity than backpropagation, with total complexity ≥ 3w∑Ci compared to backpropagation's 2w∑Ci
- Modified PC variants lose the generative variational Bayes interpretation of standard PC by fixing or decoupling dynamics from the variational objective

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
FPA-PC and Z-IL achieve backpropagation-equivalent weight updates through a specific sequence of inference steps. FPA-PC fixes feed-forward predictions and Jacobians during inference, while Z-IL triggers layer-specific updates at precise inference time-steps (t = L - ℓ). This mimics the backpropagation recursion but with additional or unused computation. The mechanism assumes latent states remain close to their feed-forward initializations throughout inference. The abstract states these variants require "specific modifications that diverge from the generative variational Bayes framework," and the analysis shows FPA-PC can be interpreted as an Euler integration scheme. This breaks if latent states deviate significantly from feed-forward values or if inference step size ≠ 1.

### Mechanism 2
Predictive coding variants have provably worse time complexity than backpropagation under identical problem settings. Despite ostensibly parallel computation, error information must propagate sequentially from output to input, requiring at least L inference steps for an L-layer network. Each inference step includes VJP computations, leading to total complexity ≥ 3w∑Ci. The core assumption is that each VJP computation dominates inference latency and cannot be fully parallelized across time-steps. The abstract notes these PC variants are "lower-bounded by backpropagation," and the time complexity analysis shows TIME{FPA-PC(F )} ≥w∑i=0 Ci +w∑i=0 2Ci ≥ TIME{backprop(F )}. This fails if parallel hardware can eliminate VJP latency per step or if convergence occurs in < L steps.

### Mechanism 3
Modified PC variants lose the generative variational Bayes interpretation of standard PC. FPA-PC and Z-IL fix or decouple dynamics from the variational Bayes objective, removing uncertainty over latent states and turning inference into a deterministic process rather than MAP estimation. The core assumption is that standard PC's strength lies in maintaining uncertainty over latent hidden states; removing this undermines the original theoretical motivation. The abstract suggests PC "may have more limited potential as a direct replacement of backpropagation than previously envisioned," and the analysis concludes that "learning does not correspond to learning of a latent probabilistic model via variational Bayes." This breaks if modified PC can be reinterpreted within a probabilistic framework or if uncertainty is reintroduced.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  Forward pass -> Latent state updates -> Error computation -> Learning step

- **Critical path**:
  1. Forward pass through all layers (O(Ci) for each fi)
  2. L inference steps for error propagation (each step includes all VJPs)
  3. Single parameter update (one VJP per layer)

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - FPA-PC and Z-IL offer local, parallel updates but require L sequential inference steps
  - Standard PC maintains uncertainty but is not equivalent to backpropagation
  - Modified PC loses variational interpretation but achieves backpropagation-like updates

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Cosine similarity between PC and BP updates drops if inference steps < L
  - Test/validation accuracy degrades with insufficient inference steps
  - Convergence fails for inference learning rates ≠ 1 (Z-IL) or > 1

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify FPA-PC equivalence: Run FPA-PC and backpropagation on a small MLP, compare weight updates (cosine similarity).
  2. Measure time complexity: Profile FPA-PC inference steps vs backpropagation for networks of varying depth.
  3. Test failure modes: Reduce inference steps or change learning rate, observe drop in cosine similarity and accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does the improved learning rate stability and online learning performance observed in G-IL and related energy-based models persist when combined with advanced optimizers like Adam or SGD with momentum? This question requires empirical testing against modern optimizers that weren't part of the original G-IL study's comparisons. Controlled experiments comparing G-IL's learning rate stability and online learning performance against standard backpropagation with Adam and momentum-based SGD under identical conditions would resolve it.

### Open Question 2
What specific architectural or algorithmic modifications to standard PC would enable it to achieve competitive classification performance on complex tasks while maintaining its generative probabilistic interpretation? The paper identifies potential reasons for poor performance but doesn't explore specific modifications that could address these limitations while preserving the theoretical framework. Demonstrations of modified PC variants achieving state-of-the-art classification performance while maintaining generative probabilistic interpretations and uncertainty estimates would resolve this question.

### Open Question 3
Can neuromorphic hardware implementations achieve practical performance advantages with any PC variant when accounting for the additional or unused computation identified in this analysis? The theoretical complexity analysis assumes traditional von Neumann architectures; neuromorphic implementations may have different bottlenecks and advantages. Empirical comparisons of neuromorphic implementations of PC variants versus backpropagation running on the same neuromorphic hardware, measuring actual energy efficiency, latency, and throughput for real-world tasks would resolve this question.

## Limitations
- The analysis focuses on specific PC variants (FPA-PC, Z-IL) while other approaches exist
- Proofs assume standard computational models and do not account for specialized neuromorphic hardware
- Empirical validation uses relatively simple MLP architectures on MNIST, which may not capture complexities of deeper networks

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The time complexity lower bounds for FPA-PC and Z-IL relative to backpropagation are mathematically rigorous and well-supported.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that modified PC variants lose the variational Bayes interpretation is well-argued but relies on specific assumptions about what constitutes "standard" PC.

**Low confidence**: The broader implication that PC has limited potential as a BP replacement, while plausible, extends beyond what is proven for the specific variants analyzed.

## Next Checks
1. **Hardware-aware analysis**: Evaluate whether specialized neuromorphic hardware could reduce the effective time complexity of PC inference steps through true parallelization.
2. **Broader architecture testing**: Validate the theoretical claims on deeper convolutional networks and more complex datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, ImageNet) to assess scalability.
3. **Alternative PC variants**: Test other PC approaches not covered in the analysis to determine if they might overcome the identified limitations.
