# 3HAN: A Deep Neural Network for Fake News Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12014
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12014
- Reference count: 18
- Achieves 96.77% accuracy in fake news detection

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces 3HAN, a deep learning model for detecting fake news that employs a three-level hierarchical attention network. The model processes words, sentences, and headlines separately through three layers of attention mechanisms to capture the differential importance of textual elements in fake news detection. By pre-training on headlines alone, 3HAN exploits their distinctive features as a proxy for fake news characteristics. Experiments on a large real-world dataset demonstrate superior performance compared to traditional and neural baselines, with attention weights providing interpretable heatmaps for manual fact-checking.

## Method Summary
3HAN employs a three-level hierarchical attention network architecture that processes news articles through word, sentence, and headline layers. Each level incorporates attention mechanisms to identify the most relevant features for fake news detection. The model is pre-trained using only headlines, based on the premise that headlines contain distinctive features indicative of fake news. This pre-training approach serves as an effective initialization strategy before fine-tuning on full articles. The attention weights at each level generate interpretable heatmaps, enabling manual verification of the model's decision-making process.

## Key Results
- Achieves 96.77% accuracy in fake news detection
- Outperforms traditional and neural network baselines
- Attention heatmaps enable manual fact-checking of important article components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical attention network captures fake news characteristics at multiple granularities - individual words, sentence structures, and headline patterns. The three-layer attention mechanism identifies the most salient features at each level, allowing the model to focus on deceptive language patterns, sensational headlines, and misleading sentence constructions. Pre-training on headlines leverages their typically exaggerated or misleading nature as an effective proxy for fake news characteristics, providing a strong initialization for the full model.
