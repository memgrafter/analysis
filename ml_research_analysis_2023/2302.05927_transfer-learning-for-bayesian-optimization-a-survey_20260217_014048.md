# Transfer Learning for Bayesian Optimization: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05927
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05927
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Systematic survey categorizing transfer learning methods for Bayesian optimization into four approaches based on what is transferred: initial points, search space, surrogate model, and acquisition function.

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive categorization of transfer learning methods for Bayesian optimization, organizing them into four main approaches based on what knowledge is transferred between tasks. The paper introduces methodological aspects and technical details for each category, covering a wide range of applications from hyperparameter tuning to robotics. It serves as a roadmap for researchers by systematically describing existing approaches and proposing promising future directions for the field.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a literature review of 40 references on transfer learning in Bayesian optimization, categorizing methods based on what knowledge is transferred. The authors propose a general framework for transfer learning in BO and provide detailed descriptions of methodological aspects and technical details for each approach. The survey covers applications across various domains and identifies future research opportunities in combining transfer components and leveraging additional knowledge types.

## Key Results
- Categorizes transfer learning methods for BO into four main approaches: initial points design, search space design, surrogate model transfer, and acquisition function transfer
- Introduces methodological aspects and technical details for each transfer approach
- Identifies challenges in fair comparison across methods and proposes the need for comprehensive benchmarks
- Highlights opportunities for combining different transfer learning components into unified frameworks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transfer learning improves BO convergence by reusing source-task observations to reduce initial uncertainty in the target task.
- Mechanism: Historical evaluations from source tasks are incorporated into the surrogate model (e.g., GP, BNN, NP) as additional data or priors, effectively giving the target task a "warm start."
- Core assumption: Source tasks share similar underlying response surfaces or kernel structure with the target task, so their data is informative.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "incorporate the spirit of transfer learning to accelerate optimization process, which could borrow strength from the past tasks (source tasks) to accelerate the current optimization problem (target task)."
  - [section 2.3.1] "Our observations are taken from these 𝐾 + 1 tasks as input, in which 𝐷𝑡1𝑛1, ..., 𝐷𝑡𝐾𝑛𝐾 are training history from 𝐾 source tasks and 𝐷𝑡𝑇𝑡 is the observations in the target task at 𝑡-th iteration."
- Break condition: If source and target tasks are too dissimilar, transferred knowledge can mislead and slow convergence.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Kernel design methods model task similarity explicitly to enable data sharing across tasks in a unified GP model.
- Mechanism: A multi-task kernel combines a base kernel for input similarity with a task kernel that encodes inter-task relationships (e.g., via Cholesky factor, similarity matrices). This allows observations from all tasks to inform predictions at any input.
- Core assumption: Task similarity can be learned or estimated from data, and a parametric kernel can represent this structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1.1-A] "They define the multi-tasks kernel between different tasks by considering the correlation of tasks...𝐾𝑚𝑢𝑙𝑡𝑖 (( 𝒙𝑡𝑖 , 𝑡𝑖 ), (𝒙𝑡 𝑗 , 𝑡𝑗 )) = 𝐾𝑡 (𝑡𝑖, 𝑡𝑗 ) ⊗ 𝐾𝑥 (𝒙𝑡𝑖 , 𝒙𝑡 𝑗 )"
  - [section 4.1.1-B] "Joy et al. [50] first proposed a method called Envelope-BO...view target task in a noisy envelope of source task, and the size of the envelope depends on the correlation between source and target task."
- Break condition: Incorrect similarity estimation leads to inappropriate data sharing and poor predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble methods combine independent surrogate models trained per task, weighting them by similarity or performance.
- Mechanism: Each task gets its own GP; predictions for a query point are a weighted sum of the individual task predictions. Weights can be static, similarity-based, or learned via ranking or MAB selection.
- Core assumption: Combining predictions is more robust than forcing a single joint model, especially under heterogeneous scales/noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1.4] "Schilling et al. [89] propose POGPE to compute 𝐾 different source tasks as individual GP model and then compute the weighted product of all individual likelihoods"
  - [section 4.1.4] "Feurer et al. [26] propose Ranking-Weighted Gaussian Process Ensembles (RGPE)...use the posterior mean function to compute the number of misranked pairs between one source task and the target task"
- Break condition: Poor similarity estimation or weight computation leads to suboptimal predictions.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Gaussian Process posterior computation (mean/variance update given observations).
  - Why needed here: All surrogate transfer methods (kernel, prior, ensemble) rely on GP inference to combine source and target data.
  - Quick check question: Given a GP with kernel k, observations (X, y), and new point x*, can you write the posterior mean formula?

- Concept: Kernel similarity and covariance modeling.
  - Why needed here: Multi-task kernel design hinges on modeling inter-task and input similarities via kernels.
  - Quick check question: How does the Kronecker product in a multi-task kernel encode independence across tasks?

- Concept: Meta-feature extraction for task similarity.
  - Why needed here: Initialization and search-space methods use meta-features to measure task similarity and choose initial points or search bounds.
  - Quick check question: If two tasks have similar meta-features, why might their optimal hyperparameters be near each other?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data Ingestion: Source task datasets (hyperparameters, evaluations) + target task evaluations
  - Surrogate Layer: GP / BNN / NP model with task-aware kernels or priors
  - Transfer Module: Kernel design, ensemble weighting, meta-feature similarity, or search-space pruning
  - Acquisition Engine: EI, UCB, MES, or learned AF, possibly transfer-informed
  - Initialization & Space Design: Warm-start points, pruned or shaped search space

- Critical path:
  1. Load source and target data
  2. Build surrogate model with transfer mechanism (kernel/ensemble/etc.)
  3. Generate initial points via meta-features or learned strategy
  4. Optimize acquisition function to select next evaluation
  5. Update surrogate and repeat until budget exhausted

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Joint GP vs. ensemble: Joint is simpler but sensitive to scale/noise; ensemble is robust but requires similarity computation
  - Meta-feature vs. learned similarity: Meta-features are interpretable but may miss task nuances; learned similarity is flexible but needs data
  - Fixed vs. adaptive weights: Fixed is cheap; adaptive can adapt to changing target-task landscape

- Failure signatures:
  - Slow convergence: Source tasks too dissimilar or weights poorly chosen
  - Unstable predictions: Overfitting in joint model or poorly regularized ensemble
  - High overhead: Expensive similarity computation or large ensemble

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run vanilla BO on a target task, record convergence
  2. Apply a simple ensemble GP with uniform weights, compare
  3. Add meta-feature-based warm start, compare again

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we design a comprehensive empirical analysis or benchmark to fairly compare different transfer learning methods for Bayesian optimization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions in Section 9.1 that "it is hard to measure the performance gap between methods based on different experimental setups" and that "a comprehensive empirical analysis or a general benchmark is required to perform a fair comparison among transfer learning methods."
- Why unresolved: Existing works typically compare themselves to only a few baselines on specific problems, making it difficult to assess the relative performance of different methods across various scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: A standardized benchmark suite with diverse optimization problems and a unified evaluation framework that allows for direct comparison of different transfer learning approaches.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we combine different transfer learning components (surrogate model, acquisition function, initial points, search space) into a comprehensive Bayesian optimization framework?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 9.2 discusses the opportunity to combine different methods into a comprehensive TLBO framework, noting that "there is an opportunity to combine different methods into a comprehensive TLBO framework."
- Why unresolved: Most existing work focuses on individual components rather than integrating multiple transfer learning techniques, and challenges remain in how to effectively combine these components without introducing additional complexity or computational overhead.
- What evidence would resolve it: A unified framework that demonstrates improved performance over individual component approaches across various optimization tasks, with theoretical guarantees on convergence and efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we leverage other types of transferable knowledge beyond observations and meta-features in transfer learning for Bayesian optimization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 9.3 mentions that "other types of knowledge can also be potential information to transfer, e.g., low-fidelity results" and asks how to make use of such knowledge.
- Why unresolved: Current methods primarily focus on transferring observations and meta-features, while other forms of transferable knowledge like low-fidelity results or model parameters have not been fully explored in the context of Bayesian optimization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful integration of additional knowledge types into transfer learning frameworks that demonstrate improved optimization efficiency and performance across various application domains.

## Limitations

- The categorization framework is descriptive rather than prescriptive, lacking formal criteria for method placement
- The paper lacks quantitative benchmarking across surveyed methods, making it difficult to assess relative performance
- Transfer mechanism effectiveness heavily depends on source-target task similarity, which is often estimated rather than learned
- Many methods rely on Gaussian processes, limiting applicability where GP assumptions don't hold

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The categorization framework itself and basic transfer mechanisms (kernel design, ensemble methods, meta-feature similarity) are well-supported by literature
- **Medium Confidence**: Claims about transfer learning accelerating BO convergence are supported by individual method papers but lack systematic validation across diverse settings
- **Low Confidence**: Proposed future directions make speculative claims about emerging approaches (neural processes, learned acquisition functions) with limited empirical validation in BO transfer context

## Next Checks

1. **Empirical Benchmarking**: Implement and compare at least three representative methods from different categories (kernel design, ensemble, meta-feature initialization) on standardized transfer learning scenarios with controlled task similarity to quantify performance gains and identify failure modes.

2. **Similarity Metric Validation**: Design experiments testing whether learned task similarity (from data) outperforms heuristic meta-feature similarity across heterogeneous optimization problems, measuring both transfer success rate and computational overhead.

3. **Scale and Noise Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically vary source-target task scale differences and noise levels in synthetic optimization problems to determine under which conditions each transfer approach degrades and whether adaptive weighting strategies can mitigate these issues.
