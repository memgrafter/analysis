# Bridging the Gap: A Survey on Integrating (Human) Feedback for Natural Language Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00955
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00955
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Comprehensive overview of human feedback integration for NLG with taxonomy of feedback types and mechanisms

## Executive Summary
This survey paper systematically examines how human feedback has been leveraged to improve natural language generation systems. The authors establish a formal framework for understanding different types of human feedback and how they can be integrated into NLG pipelines. The paper covers two primary approaches: direct optimization against human feedback and training feedback models to approximate human judgments, while also introducing the emerging field of AI feedback that aims to reduce human involvement.

## Method Summary
The paper formalizes human feedback integration by creating a taxonomy of feedback formats (numerical scores, rankings, natural language) and objectives (helpfulness, harmlessness). It describes two main implementation approaches: directly optimizing model parameters against human feedback functions using reinforcement learning or supervised learning, and training feedback models that approximate human judgments to provide scalable optimization signals. The survey also examines feedback collection datasets and discusses emerging AI feedback methods that leverage large language models to minimize human annotation requirements.

## Key Results
- Human feedback can be systematically categorized by format (numerical, ranking, natural language) and objective (helpfulness, harmlessness)
- Two main approaches exist for using human feedback: direct optimization of model parameters or training feedback models as proxies
- AI feedback represents an emerging paradigm that uses large language models to reduce human annotation burden
- Dataset availability and collection methods significantly impact the effectiveness of feedback integration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Direct human feedback optimization improves model alignment by providing explicit signals on output quality.
- Mechanism: The model parameters are optimized directly against a human feedback function h(x, y) using techniques like reinforcement learning or supervised learning on positively-labeled outputs.
- Core assumption: Human feedback can be expressed as a differentiable or sampleable objective that correlates with desired model behavior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "human feedback is an invaluable signal for evaluating and improving models"
  - [section] "we can create the following optimization problem: θ⋆ = arg max Ex∼D[h(x, Mθ(x))]"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers focus on evaluation rather than direct optimization mechanisms
- Break condition: If human feedback is too noisy, sparse, or contradictory, the optimization signal becomes unreliable and may degrade model performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Feedback models provide scalable proxies for expensive human feedback by learning to predict human judgments.
- Mechanism: A feedback model ˆhφ is trained on collected human feedback data to approximate h(x, y), then used to optimize or rank model outputs without further human input.
- Core assumption: The feedback model can generalize from limited human feedback to provide accurate proxy judgments across diverse inputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the nascent field of AI feedback, which exploits large language models to make judgments"
  - [section] "Given a feedback function h : X × Y 1 × · · · × Yn → F, we want to learn a parametric (numerical) feedback model ˆhφ"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors don't directly address feedback model training approaches
- Break condition: If the feedback model overfits to the specific human annotators' biases or the training distribution, it may provide misleading optimization signals.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Feedback-conditioned decoding allows iterative refinement without changing model parameters.
- Mechanism: During generation, the model maintains a memory of past human feedback and uses it to condition future outputs or iteratively refine them based on real-time human input.
- Core assumption: Past feedback patterns are predictive of current generation quality and can guide the decoding process effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "leveraging human feedback during decoding plays a critical role in enhancing LLMs's performance"
  - [section] "Feedback Memory: Feedback Memory Utilization involves maintaining a repository of feedback from prior sessions"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers don't specifically address feedback memory or iterative refinement approaches
- Break condition: If the feedback memory becomes too large or contains contradictory signals, the conditioning mechanism may introduce confusion rather than improvement.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data collection pipeline (human annotators, feedback collection interface) -> Feedback model training module (converts human feedback to learnable signals) -> Optimization engine (RL or supervised learning against feedback) -> Decoding enhancement layer (reranking, memory-based conditioning) -> Evaluation framework (human-in-the-loop assessment of improvements)

- Critical path: Human feedback → Feedback model training → Model optimization → Evaluation with human feedback
- Design tradeoffs: Direct human feedback optimization vs. feedback model proxy approach (accuracy vs. scalability)
- Failure signatures: Overoptimization to feedback model, degradation in general language capabilities, inconsistent feedback interpretation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement simple RLHF with preference rankings on a small summarization dataset
  2. Train a feedback model on existing human evaluation data and use it for reranking
  3. Set up a feedback memory system for iterative refinement on a dialogue task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the most effective ways to reduce the need for human feedback in natural language generation by leveraging AI feedback from large language models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the emerging field of AI feedback, which exploits large language models to make judgments based on a set of principles and minimize the need for human intervention.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions AI feedback as a promising approach, it does not provide a comprehensive evaluation of its effectiveness compared to traditional human feedback methods. Further research is needed to determine the best practices and limitations of AI feedback.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies between AI feedback and human feedback in various natural language generation tasks, assessing the quality and efficiency of the generated text.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we effectively collect and leverage richer types of human feedback beyond rankings and numerical scores to improve natural language generation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that humans prefer to provide richer feedback than labeling and discusses the need for more detailed feedback to capture important information for improving generation systems.
- Why unresolved: Most existing datasets and research focus on ranking or numerical feedback, leaving a gap in understanding how to collect and utilize more detailed feedback types. Further investigation is needed to explore the potential benefits and challenges of richer feedback formats.
- What evidence would resolve it: Studies that experiment with different feedback formats, such as natural language feedback or multi-aspect feedback, and evaluate their impact on the quality of generated text and the efficiency of the feedback collection process.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the best practices for addressing the overoptimization problem when using feedback models to improve natural language generation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the overoptimization problem, where optimizing a system with a feedback model can lead to systems that receive good feedback from the model but not humans.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions the overoptimization problem and suggests regularization techniques, it does not provide a comprehensive solution or evaluation of different approaches to mitigate this issue.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different regularization techniques and optimization strategies, assessing their effectiveness in reducing overoptimization while maintaining the quality of generated text.

## Limitations

- The survey lacks empirical validation and systematic comparisons of different feedback integration approaches
- Limited coverage of emerging AI feedback methods due to the nascent nature of this field
- Does not address critical questions about feedback efficiency, diminishing returns, or feedback-induced model collapse

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the taxonomy of feedback types and formats (numerical scores, rankings, natural language)
- **Medium confidence** in the described mechanisms (direct optimization, feedback models, decoding enhancement) based on the literature coverage
- **Low confidence** in the practical scalability and generalization of these approaches, as the survey doesn't provide systematic comparisons of real-world performance

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset diversity validation**: Compare performance across multiple human feedback datasets with varying formats (numerical, ranking, natural language) to identify which formats provide the most reliable optimization signals.

2. **Feedback model generalization test**: Train feedback models on one domain/task and evaluate their effectiveness on unseen tasks to measure cross-domain generalization capabilities.

3. **Human-in-the-loop efficiency analysis**: Measure the amount of human feedback required to achieve specific performance improvements across different feedback mechanisms (direct optimization vs. feedback models vs. decoding enhancement).
