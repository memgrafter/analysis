# ESCOXLM-R: Multilingual Taxonomy-driven Pre-training for the Job Market Domain

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.12092
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12092
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: ESCOXLM-R outperforms XLM-Rlarge on 6 out of 9 job-related datasets, particularly on sequence labeling tasks involving short spans

## Executive Summary
ESCOXLM-R is a multilingual language model based on XLM-Rlarge that uses domain-adaptive pre-training on the ESCO taxonomy to improve performance on job-related tasks. The model incorporates two pre-training objectives: dynamic masked language modeling and a novel ESCO relation prediction task. Evaluated on 9 datasets across 4 languages, ESCOXLM-R shows consistent improvements over the baseline, particularly on tasks involving short spans of text related to skills and occupations.

## Method Summary
The authors pre-train ESCOXLM-R on the ESCO taxonomy (version 1.0.9) using two objectives: standard masked language modeling and a novel ESCO relation prediction task that learns relationships between occupations and skills. The pre-training uses a base XLM-Rlarge model and involves concatenating an anchor concept with its description, then sampling additional concepts in three ways (random, linked, grouped) to create training pairs. The model is then fine-tuned on 9 downstream datasets covering skill extraction, skill classification, job title classification, and de-identification tasks, using the MaChAmp toolkit with dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning.

## Key Results
- ESCOXLM-R outperforms XLM-Rlarge on 6 out of 9 datasets
- The model shows particular strength on sequence labeling tasks, achieving higher entity-level and surface-level span-F1 scores
- ESCOXLM-R performs better on short spans (1-4 tokens) compared to longer spans
- Improvements are consistent across multiple languages including English, Danish, German, and French

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Domain-adaptive pre-training on the ESCO taxonomy improves model performance on job-related tasks by incorporating structured knowledge about occupations, skills, and their relationships. This domain-specific knowledge helps the model better understand and extract job-related information from text.

### Mechanism 2
The ESCO relation prediction task improves the model's ability to understand relationships between occupations and skills by training it to predict the relationship between two sampled concepts based on their descriptions during pre-training.

### Mechanism 3
The improved performance on short spans is due to the focus on token-level annotation in the ESCO taxonomy, which contains short skill and occupation titles that help the model better recognize and extract short spans of text related to skills and occupations in job postings.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: XLM-Rlarge -> ESCO taxonomy -> MLM objective + ESCO relation prediction objective -> Downstream job-related tasks

- **Critical path**: 1. Pre-train ESCOXLM-R on ESCO taxonomy using MLM and ERP objectives. 2. Fine-tune on downstream job-related tasks. 3. Evaluate performance on the tasks.

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using XLM-Rlarge provides strong multilingual capabilities but requires more computational resources; the ESCO relation prediction task adds complexity but may improve understanding of relationships.

- **Failure signatures**: Poor downstream performance may indicate pre-training issues or taxonomy quality problems; overfitting to ESCO may reduce performance on tasks that don't align with taxonomy knowledge.

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Pre-train with only MLM objective and evaluate. 2. Pre-train with both MLM and ERP objectives and compare. 3. Analyze performance on short vs. long spans to assess taxonomy impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does ESCOXLM-R's performance on non-European job market data compare to its performance on European job market data? The paper observes slight improvement on BHOLA (Singapore) but lacks comprehensive evaluation outside Europe.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of the ESCO relation prediction task on relation-centered tasks? The paper acknowledges no job-related datasets containing relations between skill/occupation concepts exist for benchmarking this specific objective.

### Open Question 3
How does performance on longer spans compare to shorter spans? The paper finds performance declines on spans of 7-10 tokens but doesn't provide detailed analysis of contributing factors.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation of the ESCO relation prediction objective due to lack of ablation studies
- Incomplete specification of preprocessing steps for several downstream datasets
- Moderate relevance in citation neighborhood (25 related papers, average FMR=0.484)
- Evaluation restricted to only 4 languages despite claiming multilingual capabilities

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: ESCOXLM-R outperforms XLM-Rlarge on 6 out of 9 datasets
- **Medium confidence**: Improved performance on short spans due to ESCO taxonomy focus
- **Low confidence**: Specific contribution of the ESCO relation prediction objective to overall performance

## Next Checks

1. Conduct an ablation study comparing ESCOXLM-R with only the MLM objective to isolate the effect of the ESCO relation prediction task.

2. Extend evaluation to additional languages beyond the current 4 to better assess multilingual capabilities.

3. Perform a taxonomy coverage analysis to validate how well ESCO covers vocabulary and concepts in downstream datasets.
