# Electricity Price Prediction for Energy Storage System Arbitrage: A Decision-focused Approach

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00362
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00362
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes decision-focused approach achieving 90.91% less regret and 46.8% higher economic benefits compared to MSE-based models

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a decision-focused approach for electricity price prediction in energy storage system (ESS) arbitrage that bridges the gap between upstream prediction and downstream optimization. The method incorporates decision error into the training of prediction models by measuring regret and transforming it into tractable surrogate regret. A hybrid loss function combining mean-square-error (MSE) and surrogate regret is designed, along with a hybrid stochastic gradient descent learning method to train prediction models for both prediction and decision accuracy.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach transforms regret into a tractable surrogate regret function and derives its gradients with respect to predicted prices. A hybrid loss function is created by combining MSE and surrogate regret, which is then optimized using a hybrid stochastic gradient descent method. This allows the prediction model to be trained not just for prediction accuracy but also for decision accuracy in ESS arbitrage. The method is evaluated using the PJM dataset and compared against traditional MSE-based models.

## Key Results
- Achieves 90.91% less regret compared to MSE-based models
- Generates 46.8% higher economic benefits through ESS arbitrage
- Improves decision accuracy by flattening the time distribution of prediction errors

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by directly incorporating decision error into the prediction model training process. By measuring regret (the difference between optimal and actual decisions) and transforming it into a tractable surrogate function, the model learns to predict prices that not only minimize prediction error but also optimize downstream decision-making in ESS arbitrage.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Price Prediction Model -> Regret Calculation -> Surrogate Regret Transformation -> Hybrid Loss -> SGD Optimization

**Critical path**: Input price data → Prediction model → Regret calculation → Surrogate regret transformation → Hybrid loss computation → Parameter update via SGD

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- MSE vs surrogate regret weighting: Higher MSE weight improves prediction accuracy but may reduce decision performance
- Model complexity vs training efficiency: More complex models may capture price patterns better but require more computational resources

**Failure signatures**:
- High regret despite low MSE indicates poor alignment between prediction and decision objectives
- Unstable training suggests inappropriate surrogate regret formulation or learning rate issues

## Limitations
- Mathematical transformation of regret to surrogate regret lacks detailed explanation
- Assumption that flattened time distribution of prediction errors improves decision accuracy may not hold universally
- Results based solely on PJM dataset may not generalize to other electricity markets
