# Utilization of Multinomial Naive Bayes Algorithm and Term Frequency Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF Vectorizer) in Checking the Credibility of News Tweet in the Philippines

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00018
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00018
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Multinomial Naive Bayes with TF-IDF achieved 99.46% training accuracy and 88.98% test accuracy for detecting fake news in Philippine election-related tweets

## Executive Summary
This study develops a machine learning model to detect fake news in Philippine election-related tweets using Multinomial Naive Bayes (MNB) with TF-IDF feature extraction. The model was trained on a balanced dataset of 790 news articles and tweets (395 real, 395 fake) and achieved strong performance metrics including 99.46% accuracy on the training set and 88.98% on test data. The research demonstrates the feasibility of using NLP techniques for fake news detection in the Philippine context, with particular attention to local language processing needs including Tagalog stop words.

## Method Summary
The researchers collected 790 balanced news articles and tweets from Twitter using RapidMiner and the Twitter API, with ground truth labels obtained from credible sources and fact-checking accounts. The dataset was pre-processed through cleaning (removing URLs, hashtags, mentions), tokenization, stemming, and stop word removal (including Tagalog stop words). TF-IDF vectorization was applied to convert text features into numerical representations, and a Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier was trained on 70% of the data. The model was evaluated on the remaining 30% using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score metrics, revealing high precision but lower recall indicating a tendency to misclassify fake news as real.

## Key Results
- Achieved 99.46% accuracy on training data but 88.98% on test data, indicating some overfitting
- High precision of 95.76% suggests reliable identification of real news
- Lower recall of 84.33% indicates tendency to misclassify fake news as real news
- F1 score of 89.68% balances precision and recall performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier works effectively for fake news detection by leveraging the probabilistic framework of Bayes' theorem, where it calculates the likelihood of a tweet being fake or real based on word frequencies. The TF-IDF vectorizer enhances this by weighting terms according to their importance across documents, giving higher weights to distinctive words that appear frequently in a document but rarely in the corpus. This combination allows the model to identify characteristic patterns in fake news language while filtering out common terms that don't contribute to classification decisions.

## Foundational Learning
- Multinomial Naive Bayes: A probabilistic classifier based on Bayes' theorem that assumes feature independence, well-suited for text classification tasks where word frequencies matter
- TF-IDF Vectorization: Transforms text into numerical features by weighting terms based on their importance across documents, balancing term frequency with inverse document frequency to highlight distinctive words
- Text Preprocessing: Cleaning and normalization steps including tokenization, stemming, and stop word removal that standardize text data and reduce noise for better model performance
- Class Imbalance Handling: Techniques to address unequal distribution of classes in training data, crucial for maintaining model performance across both fake and real news categories
- Cross-validation: Model evaluation technique that partitions data into multiple folds to assess generalization performance and reduce overfitting risks

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: Raw Tweets -> Preprocessing Pipeline -> TF-IDF Vectorizer -> Multinomial Naive Bayes Classifier -> Performance Metrics

Critical Path: Data Collection → Preprocessing (Cleaning, Tokenization, Stemming, Stop Word Removal) → TF-IDF Feature Extraction → Model Training (MNB) → Evaluation (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1)

Design Tradeoffs: The choice of Multinomial Naive Bayes prioritizes simplicity and speed over the potential accuracy gains from more complex models like neural networks. TF-IDF provides interpretable features but may miss semantic relationships that word embeddings could capture. The 70/30 train-test split offers a reasonable evaluation but could benefit from k-fold cross-validation for more robust assessment.

Failure Signatures: Large gap between training (99.46%) and test accuracy (88.98%) indicates overfitting to training data. Low recall (84.33%) compared to high precision (95.76%) suggests the model is biased toward classifying news as real, potentially missing fake news instances. Poor performance on test data may indicate insufficient generalization to unseen examples.

- Lower recall (84.33%) compared to precision (95.76%) indicates the model tends to misclassify fake news as real news
- Balanced dataset of 790 samples, while adequate, may limit generalizability to broader contexts and evolving fake news patterns
- Focus on election-related content may not capture fake news patterns in other domains
- Limited temporal coverage of data collection may not capture evolving fake news tactics
