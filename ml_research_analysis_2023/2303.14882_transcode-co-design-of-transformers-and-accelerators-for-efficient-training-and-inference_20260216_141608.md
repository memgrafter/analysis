# TransCODE: Co-design of Transformers and Accelerators for Efficient Training and Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14882
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14882
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TransCODE achieves 0.3% higher accuracy than SOTA while incurring 5.2× lower latency and 3.0× lower energy consumption

## Executive Summary
TransCODE introduces a co-design framework for transformers and accelerators that achieves state-of-the-art performance with significant efficiency gains. The framework combines DynaProp, a dynamic pruning method that removes ineffectual computations at runtime, with ELECTOR, a simulator for diverse accelerator configurations, and BOSHCODE, a Bayesian optimization framework. By jointly optimizing transformer architectures and accelerator designs, TransCODE delivers superior accuracy-latency-energy trade-offs compared to existing approaches.

## Method Summary
TransCODE employs DynaProp for runtime pruning of activations, gradients, and weights using magnitude-based thresholding to eliminate unnecessary computations. ELECTOR simulates transformer inference and training across a vast design space of 14.85 million accelerator configurations, mapping computational graphs to specialized hardware modules. BOSHCODE uses Bayesian optimization with a heteroscedastic surrogate model to co-design transformers and accelerators, predicting performance metrics and guiding the search for optimal configurations. The framework leverages the FlexiBERT 2.0 design space of heterogeneous transformer architectures and evaluates performance using the GLUE benchmark.

## Key Results
- Achieves 0.3% higher accuracy than state-of-the-art transformer-accelerator pairs
- Reduces latency by 5.2× compared to existing approaches
- Decreases energy consumption by 3.0× while maintaining competitive accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** DynaProp dynamically prunes activations, gradients, and weights at runtime to reduce computational overhead during transformer training and inference.
- **Mechanism:** DynaProp introduces sparsity by setting elements below a runtime threshold τ to zero, thereby skipping ineffectual multiply-and-accumulate (MAC) operations. This is implemented in parallel across matrix tiles for efficiency.
- **Core assumption:** Sparsity introduced by thresholding will not significantly degrade model accuracy, and the overhead of pruning is negligible compared to the savings in compute and memory.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] DynaProp is described as a "low-overhead pruning method that prunes activations and gradients at runtime."
  - [section] "It then prunes values with a magnitude less than a given threshold τ... We implement each such comparison in parallel, thus requiring only one clock cycle for the pruning process."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; corpus papers mention sparsity but not runtime pruning with minimal accuracy loss.
- **Break condition:** If the threshold τ becomes too aggressive, model accuracy will drop significantly, or if the parallel pruning implementation adds non-negligible latency.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** ELECTOR is a flexible accelerator framework that supports diverse transformer architectures and integrates DynaProp for efficient hardware execution.
- **Mechanism:** ELECTOR maps transformer computational graphs to specialized hardware modules (MAC lanes, softmax, layer-norm) and tiles operations for efficient resource allocation. It implements DynaProp within processing elements to prune matrices before computation.
- **Core assumption:** The design space of accelerators (14.85 million configurations) is sufficiently broad to find a high-performance configuration for any transformer in the FlexiBERT 2.0 space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] ELECTOR is described as a "framework that simulates transformer inference and training on a design space of accelerators."
  - [section] "ELECTOR supports various accelerators in its design space... ELECTOR stores all data in a compressed format... The PEs employ the compressed data and associated masks to perform the main compute operations."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related work mentions accelerator design spaces but not one as large or flexible as ELECTOR.
- **Break condition:** If the mapping from computational graph to hardware operations is inefficient or if the tiling strategy leads to poor resource utilization.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** TransCODE uses Bayesian optimization with a surrogate model (BOSHCODE) to co-design transformers and accelerators for optimal performance.
- **Mechanism:** BOSHCODE leverages second-order gradient-based optimization on a heteroscedastic surrogate model combining a natural parameter network, teacher, and student networks. It predicts performance metrics (latency, energy, area, accuracy) and queries the next transformer-accelerator pair to evaluate.
- **Core assumption:** The surrogate model can accurately predict performance for unseen transformer-accelerator pairs, and the optimization landscape is smooth enough for gradient-based methods to converge.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We use this simulator in conjunction with the proposed co-design technique, called TransCODE, to obtain the best-performing models with high accuracy on the given task and minimize latency, energy consumption, and chip area."
  - [section] "BOSHCODE supports co-design with any two search spaces... It leverages second-order gradient-based optimization... on an actively-trained surrogate model for performance prediction."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; corpus papers mention hardware-aware NAS but not the specific BOSHCODE approach.
- **Break condition:** If the surrogate model predictions are inaccurate, leading the optimizer to query poor configurations, or if the design space is too rugged for gradient methods.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** FlexiBERT 2.0 design space -> DynaProp pruning -> ELECTOR accelerator simulation -> BOSHCODE optimization -> optimal transformer-accelerator pair
- **Critical path:** Transformer embedding -> FlexiBERT 2.0 -> computational graph -> ELECTOR mapping -> accelerator simulation -> performance prediction -> BOSHCODE optimization -> next query
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Sparsity level vs. accuracy in DynaProp
  - Number of PEs, buffer sizes, and memory type in ELECTOR (performance vs. area/energy)
  - Exploration vs. exploitation in BOSHCODE's optimization
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Accuracy degradation in DynaProp (threshold too high)
  - Poor resource utilization in ELECTOR (mismatch between transformer and accelerator design)
  - Optimizer converging to local optima or suboptimal configurations in TransCODE
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement DynaProp on a small transformer (e.g., BERT-Tiny) and measure accuracy vs. sparsity tradeoff
  2. Simulate ELECTOR with a fixed transformer architecture and vary key design parameters (e.g., number of PEs, memory type) to observe performance trends
  3. Run BOSHCODE with a small subset of the design space to validate the surrogate model's prediction accuracy and the optimizer's convergence behavior

  - Why unresolved: The authors did not explore the integration of reconfigurable processors into their framework.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Implementing reconfigurable processors in the ELECTOR framework and evaluating the performance gains and trade-offs in terms of latency, energy consumption, and accuracy for various transformer models and workloads.

- Question: How does the accuracy-latency trade-off of DynaProp training change when using complex optimizers like Adam with second-order gradients instead of simple gradient descent?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that DynaProp can be extended to support complex optimizers like Adam, but notes that second-order gradients would add significant computational overhead and are left for future work.
  - Why unresolved: The authors did not implement or test DynaProp with complex optimizers like Adam.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Implementing DynaProp with Adam and evaluating the accuracy-latency trade-off for various transformer models and tasks, comparing it to the current implementation with simple gradient descent.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on simulated performance metrics rather than real silicon measurements, which may not capture all hardware implementation overheads.
- DynaProp's generalization across diverse tasks and model scales remains to be thoroughly tested.
- The ELECTOR simulator's accuracy in predicting real hardware performance across the full 14.85 million accelerator configurations is uncertain without silicon measurements.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The core mechanisms of DynaProp pruning and ELECTOR's accelerator design space are well-specified and implementable. The 5.2× latency and 3.0× energy improvements over state-of-the-art are reported with specific benchmark comparisons.
- **Medium Confidence:** The BOSHCODE co-design optimization methodology is described in detail, but its effectiveness depends on the accuracy of the surrogate model and the smoothness of the design space. The claimed 0.3% accuracy improvement over SOTA requires verification on independent datasets.
- **Low Confidence:** The scalability of DynaProp to larger models (beyond BERT-Base) and its impact on training convergence stability are not thoroughly validated.

## Next Checks
