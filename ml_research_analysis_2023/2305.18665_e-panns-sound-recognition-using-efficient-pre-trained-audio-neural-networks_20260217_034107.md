# E-PANNs: Sound Recognition Using Efficient Pre-trained Audio Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.18665
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18665
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Primary result**: E-PANNs reduces PANNs-CNN14 parameters by 70% and MACs by 36% while slightly improving audio tagging performance from 0.431 to 0.442 mAPs

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational and memory inefficiencies of the PANNs audio tagging model for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The authors propose E-PANNs, a pruned version of PANNs-CNN14 that reduces parameters by 70% and MACs by 36% while slightly improving audio tagging performance from 0.431 to 0.442 mAPs. The pruning approach removes 25-50% of filters from the six largest convolutional layers based on their importance. Experiments on AudioSet show E-PANNs maintains performance with fewer iterations, making it suitable for real-time applications. The code is open-sourced.

## Method Summary
E-PANNs is developed through structured pruning of the PANNs-CNN14 architecture, specifically targeting the six largest convolutional layers. The pruning strategy removes 25-50% of filters based on importance metrics, significantly reducing the parameter count by 70% and computational operations by 36%. This approach maintains or slightly improves audio tagging performance on AudioSet while enabling more efficient deployment on resource-constrained devices. The model achieves these efficiency gains without requiring additional training iterations compared to the original PANNs architecture.

## Key Results
- 70% reduction in model parameters compared to PANNs-CNN14
- 36% reduction in multiply-accumulate operations (MACs)
- Audio tagging performance improves slightly from 0.431 to 0.442 mAP on AudioSet
- Maintains performance with fewer training iterations required

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The pruning approach works by systematically removing less important filters from the convolutional layers while preserving the most critical features for audio classification. By targeting only the six largest convolutional layers for pruning (25-50% of filters), the method achieves substantial computational savings without compromising the model's ability to learn discriminative audio features. The efficiency gains come from reducing both the parameter count and the computational graph complexity, which directly translates to faster inference and lower memory requirements.
