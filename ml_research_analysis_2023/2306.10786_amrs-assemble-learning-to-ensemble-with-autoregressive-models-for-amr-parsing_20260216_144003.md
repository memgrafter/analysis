# AMRs Assemble! Learning to Ensemble with Autoregressive Models for AMR Parsing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.10786
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10786
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** Proposed ensemble strategies improve robustness to structural constraints while reducing computational time compared to previous AMR ensemble models.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses limitations in current AMR ensemble parsing methods, which often violate AMR structural constraints and rely on the SMATCH metric, leading to corrupted outputs. To overcome these challenges, the authors propose two novel ensemble strategies based on Transformer models. The first strategy trains a seq2seq model to combine multiple predictions using both the original sentence and graph predictions. The second strategy selects the best graph from a set of predictions using perplexity scores. The proposed methods improve robustness to structural constraints while reducing computational time. The results show that the proposed methods outperform previous ensemble models, achieving higher SMATCH scores and fewer structural issues in the generated graphs.

## Method Summary
The authors propose two ensemble strategies for AMR parsing. The first strategy employs a seq2seq model that takes both the original sentence and multiple graph predictions as input, learning to generate an improved AMR graph that combines the strengths of individual predictions. The second strategy uses a perplexity-based selection method, where a pretrained language model scores each candidate graph, and the graph with the lowest perplexity is selected as the ensemble output. Both approaches leverage Transformer architectures and are designed to maintain AMR structural constraints while improving overall parsing quality.

## Key Results
- The proposed ensemble methods achieve higher SMATCH scores compared to previous ensemble models
- Structural constraint violations are significantly reduced in the generated AMR graphs
- Computational efficiency is improved compared to traditional ensemble approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The ensemble strategies work by addressing two fundamental challenges in AMR parsing: structural constraint violations and metric dependency issues. The seq2seq ensemble model learns to integrate information from multiple graph predictions, effectively smoothing out individual parser errors while maintaining graph validity. The perplexity-based selection method provides a principled way to evaluate graph quality beyond the SMATCH metric, capturing semantic coherence that traditional metrics might miss. By using Transformer architectures, both methods can effectively model the complex relationships between sentence meaning and AMR graph structure.

## Foundational Learning

**AMR Graph Structure** - Abstract Meaning Representation uses directed acyclic graphs to represent sentence semantics. *Why needed:* Understanding the graph-based nature of AMR is essential for recognizing why structural constraints matter. *Quick check:* Verify that generated graphs are acyclic and have proper concept-role relationships.

**SMATCH Metric** - A metric for evaluating AMR parsing quality based on triple matching between gold and predicted graphs. *Why needed:* Recognizing SMATCH's limitations helps understand why alternative evaluation methods are valuable. *Quick check:* Compare SMATCH scores with human judgments of semantic quality.

**Perplexity Scoring** - A measure of how well a language model predicts a given sequence. *Why needed:* Understanding perplexity is crucial for the selection-based ensemble strategy. *Quick check:* Validate that lower perplexity correlates with higher semantic quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Sentence -> Multiple Parsers -> Seq2seq Ensemble Model OR Perplexity Scorer -> Final AMR Graph

**Critical Path:** Input sentence flows through multiple parsers, then either through the seq2seq model or perplexity evaluation, resulting in the final ensemble output.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The seq2seq approach offers potentially better integration of parser outputs but requires more computational resources for training. The perplexity-based approach is computationally lighter but depends heavily on the quality of individual parser outputs.

**Failure Signatures:** Structural constraint violations in output graphs, poor SMATCH scores relative to individual parsers, or high perplexity scores indicating semantic incoherence.

## Limitations
- The perplexity-based selection method's performance may be sensitive to variations in parser reliability across different domains or languages
- The seq2seq ensemble model requires substantial computational resources for training
- Evaluation focuses primarily on SMATCH scores and structural constraint violations without extensive exploration of other metrics like concept stability or semantic fidelity
