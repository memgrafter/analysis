# Simple Yet Effective Neural Ranking and Reranking Baselines for Cross-Lingual Information Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01019
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01019
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SPLADE-based approaches with reranking achieve state-of-the-art results on TREC 2022 NeuCLIR Track for Persian and Russian.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a conceptual framework for organizing cross-lingual information retrieval methods and implements reproducible baselines for the TREC 2022 NeuCLIR Track in Persian, Russian, and Chinese. The authors adapt mono-lingual retrieval architectures to the cross-lingual setting, exploring document translation, query translation, and language-independent representations using modern neural models. Experiments with BM25, SPLADE, and xDPR retrievers show that SPLADE-based approaches are most effective, with pseudo-relevance feedback improving recall but not nDCG. Reranking significantly boosts effectiveness, and fusion of document and query translation SPLADE models achieves state-of-the-art results, outperforming individual methods in Persian and Russian.

## Method Summary
The paper explores cross-lingual retrieval through three main approaches: document translation, query translation, and language-independent representations. First-stage retrievers include BM25, SPLADE, and xDPR models, with reranking using monoT5 and mT5 cross-encoders. The framework adapts mono-lingual multi-stage architectures to cross-lingual settings, with documents indexed in source languages or translated to English. Experiments use the TREC 2022 NeuCLIR corpora with English queries, measuring effectiveness using nDCG@20 and recall@1000. The authors also explore pseudo-relevance feedback with Rocchio and fusion techniques including reciprocal rank fusion.

## Key Results
- SPLADE-based approaches outperform BM25 and xDPR retrievers in cross-lingual settings
- Reranking with monoT5 and mT5 significantly improves retrieval effectiveness
- Fusion of document and query translation SPLADE models achieves state-of-the-art results on Persian and Russian
- Pseudo-relevance feedback improves recall but not nDCG@20

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The conceptual framework successfully organizes cross-lingual retrieval methods by leveraging mono-lingual retrieval architecture as a scaffold.
- Mechanism: The framework maps cross-lingual retrieval approaches (document translation, query translation, language-independent representations) onto the well-understood stages of mono-lingual retrieval (first-stage retrieval and reranking). This provides a structured way to understand and compare different cross-lingual methods.
- Core assumption: The design principles and effectiveness patterns observed in mono-lingual retrieval can be meaningfully extended to cross-lingual settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we provide a conceptual framework for organizing different approaches to cross-lingual retrieval using multi-stage architectures for mono-lingual retrieval as a scaffold"
  - [section] "Our work makes two important contributions: First, we provide a conceptual framework for organizing different approaches to cross-lingual retrieval using multi-stage architectures for mono-lingual retrieval as a scaffold"
- Break condition: If the effectiveness patterns in cross-lingual settings diverge significantly from mono-lingual patterns, or if cross-lingual methods require fundamentally different architectural considerations that don't map cleanly onto the mono-lingual framework.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SPLADE-based approaches outperform other methods in cross-lingual retrieval due to their learned sparse representations and query expansion capabilities.
- Mechanism: SPLADE models learn term weights through neural networks rather than using heuristics like BM25, allowing them to capture more nuanced term importance and relationships. The query expansion component helps improve recall by incorporating related terms.
- Core assumption: Learned sparse representations can better capture cross-lingual semantic relationships than traditional bag-of-words models or dense representations in the context of retrieval.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments with BM25, SPLADE, and xDPR retrievers show that SPLADE-based approaches are most effective"
  - [section] "We chose SPLADE++ Ensemble Distil [12] due to its zero-shot capabilities. The SPLADE family of models is a sparse neural retrieval model that learns both document and query expansion controlled by a regularization term"
- Break condition: If the learned term weights in SPLADE fail to generalize across languages, or if the query expansion introduces noise that degrades precision.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Reranking significantly improves cross-lingual retrieval effectiveness by refining candidate rankings from first-stage retrievers.
- Mechanism: Cross-encoders like monoT5 and mT5 process query-document pairs holistically, capturing interactions between query terms and document content that first-stage retrievers miss. This is particularly valuable in cross-lingual settings where translation introduces additional semantic gaps.
- Core assumption: The additional computational cost of reranking is justified by the improvement in ranking quality, even when first-stage retrieval is already effective.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Reranking significantly boosts effectiveness"
  - [section] "In a standard multi-stage ranking architecture, the first-stage retriever generates a ranked list of candidates that are then processed by one or more reranking stages that aim to improve the ranking. Reranking is also applicable in the cross-lingual case"
- Break condition: If the computational overhead of reranking outweighs the effectiveness gains, or if the reranker overfits to the training data and doesn't generalize well to new queries.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Documents -> First-stage retrievers (BM25, SPLADE, xDPR) -> Rerankers (monoT5, mT5) -> Fusion
- Critical path: Documents are indexed in their source language (or translated to English). Queries are processed through the chosen first-stage retriever, candidates are generated, and then reranked. For fusion, results from multiple first-stage retrievers are combined before or after reranking.
- Design tradeoffs: Document translation provides a solid baseline but requires upfront translation costs. Query translation is more efficient but may suffer from translation quality issues. Language-independent representations avoid translation but may not perform as well. Dense representations are more computationally expensive than sparse ones.
- Failure signatures: Poor retrieval effectiveness due to translation errors, inadequate cross-lingual semantic alignment in embeddings, or mismatched query and document representations. Fusion may not improve effectiveness if the individual runs are too similar or if one run is significantly better than the others.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement document translation with BM25 on a small subset of the Persian corpus to verify the basic pipeline works.
  2. Compare BM25 and SPLADE retrieval effectiveness on the Russian corpus with query translation to assess the impact of learned representations.
  3. Apply reranking to the best first-stage retriever from experiment 2 and measure the improvement in nDCG@20.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different pseudo-relevance feedback methods (Rocchio vs RM3) compare in cross-lingual retrieval settings when applied to dense representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that RM3 is a popular choice for PRF and has been well studied in the context of neural methods, but chose Rocchio for consistency across dense and sparse models. It notes that Rocchio has been demonstrated to be effective for dense vector representations.
- Why unresolved: The paper chose Rocchio over RM3 primarily for methodological consistency rather than effectiveness comparison. No direct comparison between these PRF methods was conducted.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing RM3 and Rocchio PRF on cross-lingual dense retrieval tasks, measuring both effectiveness (nDCG@20, recall) and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal fusion strategy (early vs late) for combining cross-lingual retrieval models, and does this depend on the specific models being fused?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper conducted experiments comparing early and late fusion strategies but found the results inconclusive, with only a few significant differences observed. It notes that early fusion performed better for dense models while results were mixed for other configurations.
- Why unresolved: The experimental results showed no clear winner between early and late fusion across all conditions. The significance testing revealed only limited cases where differences mattered.
- What evidence would resolve it: Larger-scale experiments with more diverse model combinations, different dataset sizes, and comprehensive significance testing to determine if fusion strategy effectiveness varies by model type or dataset characteristics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does pseudo-relevance feedback improve recall but not nDCG in cross-lingual retrieval, and what does this imply about the nature of errors being corrected?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that PRF consistently improves recall across all conditions but does not improve nDCG@20. This behavior is noted as expected since PRF is primarily a recall-enhancing device.
- Why unresolved: The paper accepts this as expected behavior without exploring the underlying reasons or implications for retrieval system design.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of PRF's impact on different recall ranges, examination of which documents are being added to the top-k results, and investigation of whether PRF helps with recall at the expense of precision in the top positions.

## Limitations

- Translation quality dependence on machine translation performance across language pairs
- Limited language coverage focusing only on Persian, Russian, and Chinese with distinct scripts
- Computational overhead of reranking with cross-encoders not fully explored

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The framework for organizing cross-lingual retrieval methods and the observation that SPLADE-based approaches outperform others with reranking boosting effectiveness.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific superiority of language-independent representations over translation-based approaches and the effectiveness of fusion strategies.

**Low Confidence**: The observation that pseudo-relevance feedback improves recall but not nDCG requires more validation.
