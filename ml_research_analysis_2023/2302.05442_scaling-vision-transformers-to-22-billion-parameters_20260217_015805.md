# Scaling Vision Transformers to 22 Billion Parameters

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05442
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05442
- Reference count: 40
- Largest dense vision transformer model to date at 22 billion parameters

## Executive Summary
This paper presents ViT-22B, a 22-billion-parameter Vision Transformer that achieves state-of-the-art results across image classification, dense prediction, and video classification tasks. The authors address critical training instability issues that arise when scaling ViT models by introducing architectural modifications including parallel layers, query/key normalization, and omitted biases on certain projections. They also develop efficient model-parallel training techniques that enable high hardware utilization. The model demonstrates improved fairness, robustness, and alignment with human perception, achieving a shape bias of 87% and notable trade-offs between fairness and performance.

## Method Summary
ViT-22B uses parallel layers where attention and MLP blocks are computed simultaneously rather than sequentially, query/key normalization to prevent training divergence, and omits biases on QKV projections and LayerNorms to improve hardware utilization. The model is trained using sophisticated model-parallel techniques across 1024 TPU v4 chips with a 2D logical mesh organization. Pre-training occurs on a 4B image JFT dataset for 177K steps, followed by fine-tuning on downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art performance on ImageNet and other vision benchmarks
- Shape bias of 87%, the highest reported for vision models
- Improved fairness metrics with performance trade-offs
- High hardware utilization (67% MFU) during training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Parallel layers in ViT-22B improve training efficiency without quality degradation
- Mechanism: The MLP and Attention blocks are computed in parallel instead of sequentially, allowing fusion of linear projections and reducing redundant computations
- Core assumption: The parallel computation preserves the representational power of the original sequential design
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Parallel layers. As in Wang and Komatsuzaki (2021), ViT-22B applies the Attention and MLP blocks in parallel, instead of sequentially as in the standard Transformer"
  - [abstract] "We present a recipe for highly efficient and stable training of a 22B-parameter ViT (ViT-22B)"
  - [corpus] "Getting ViT in Shape: Scaling Laws for Compute-Optimal Model Design" discusses similar parallel layer approaches
- Break condition: If the parallel computation fails to preserve model capacity, performance would degrade despite efficiency gains

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Query/Key normalization prevents training instability at scale
- Mechanism: Applying LayerNorm to queries and keys before dot-product attention prevents extreme attention logit values that cause divergence
- Core assumption: The normalization doesn't interfere with the attention mechanism's ability to learn meaningful relationships
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In scaling ViT beyond prior works, we observed divergent training loss after a few thousand steps... To solve this, we adopt the approach of Gilmer et al. (2023)"
  - [abstract] "We present a recipe for highly efficient and stable training of a 22B-parameter ViT (ViT-22B)"
  - [section] "The effect on an 8B parameter model is shown in Figure 1, where normalization prevents divergence due to uncontrolled attention logit growth"
- Break condition: If normalization reduces the model's ability to attend to distant tokens effectively, performance may suffer despite stability

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Omitting biases on QKV projections and LayerNorms improves hardware utilization without quality loss
- Mechanism: Removing bias terms reduces memory usage and improves computation efficiency on accelerators
- Core assumption: The bias terms in these specific layers are not critical for model performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Following PaLM (Chowdhery et al., 2022), the bias terms were removed from the QKV projections and all LayerNorms were applied without bias and centering"
  - [abstract] "We present a recipe for highly efficient and stable training of a 22B-parameter ViT (ViT-22B)"
  - [corpus] "Scaling Recommender Transformers to One Billion Parameters" shows similar bias removal strategies in large-scale transformers
- Break condition: If certain bias terms are actually important for model expressiveness, removing them could limit performance

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Embedding layer -> 48 parallel encoder blocks (parallel MLP/Attention, LayerNorm, QK normalization) -> Pooling head -> Linear classifier
- Critical path: The forward pass goes through patch embedding → 48 parallel encoder blocks → pooling head → linear classifier. The most compute-intensive part is the parallel attention and MLP operations within each block.
- Design tradeoffs: The parallel architecture trades some theoretical model expressiveness for training efficiency and stability. Omitting biases improves hardware utilization but assumes those biases aren't critical. The QK normalization adds stability but may slightly alter the attention mechanism.
- Failure signatures: Training instability (diverging loss), poor hardware utilization (low MFU), or degraded downstream performance despite scaling would indicate issues with these architectural choices.

  3. Measure hardware utilization with and without bias removal to quantify the 3% improvement claimed in the paper

## Limitations

- Performance gains are heavily dependent on massive pre-training datasets, making the approach resource-intensive
- Architectural modifications may be scale-specific and not generalize to smaller models
- Hardware-specific optimizations limit portability across different accelerator types
