# Coupled Variational Autoencoder

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02565
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02565
- Reference count: 14
- This paper proposes the Coupled Variational Auto-Encoder (C-VAE), which reformulates the VAE problem as an optimal transport problem between the prior and data distributions.

## Executive Summary
The Coupled Variational Auto-Encoder (C-VAE) addresses key limitations in standard VAEs by reformulating the generative modeling problem as an optimal transport problem between prior and data distributions. By enforcing coupling between these distributions through entropic optimal transport, the model naturally resolves the prior hole problem and enables greater flexibility in prior distributions. The approach uses primal, dual, and semi-dual formulations of entropic optimal transport to jointly learn the decoder (as a cost function) and posterior distribution, achieving improved sample quality and more expressive latent representations compared to standard VAEs, WAE, and InfoVAE.

## Method Summary
The C-VAE reformulates the VAE objective as an optimal transport problem with entropy regularization, coupling the prior and aggregate posterior distributions. Unlike standard VAEs that assume parametric posteriors (typically Gaussian), C-VAE uses optimal transport to find the best coupling between data points and latent codes without assuming a form for the posterior. The model jointly learns the likelihood function pθ(x|z) and posterior distribution q(z|x) through alternating optimization between primal (decoder) and dual (posterior) formulations. Two training strategies are proposed: Primal Strategy (optimizing q(z|x) with dual, pθ(x|z) with primal) and Dual Strategy (optimizing both with dual). The method is evaluated on synthetic data (mixture of 25 Gaussians) and MNIST using visual inspection of samples, reconstructions, and latent representations.

## Key Results
- C-VAE resolves the prior hole problem by enforcing coupling between prior and data distributions
- Achieves more expressive posterior approximations without parametric constraints
- Outperforms alternatives including VAE, WAE, and InfoVAE in sample quality and latent representation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The C-VAE resolves the prior hole problem by enforcing a coupling constraint between the prior and the aggregate posterior through entropic optimal transport.
- Mechanism: By reformulating the VAE objective as an optimal transport problem with entropy regularization, the model explicitly minimizes the discrepancy between the prior distribution and the aggregate posterior distribution. This coupling ensures that regions of high prior density also have high aggregate posterior density, eliminating "holes" where the decoder has not been trained.
- Core assumption: The prior and data distributions are absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, and the optimal transport coupling can be computed efficiently through the dual formulation.
- Evidence anchors:

  - [section] "The prior hole problem is resolved naturally via marginal constraints of EOT"

- Break Condition: If the optimal transport solver fails to converge or the entropy regularization parameter is set too high, the coupling constraint may not be properly enforced, leading to residual prior holes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The C-VAE achieves more expressive posterior approximations by solving the optimal transport problem without assuming a parametric form for the posterior distribution.
- Mechanism: Instead of restricting the approximate posterior to a fixed family like Gaussian distributions, the C-VAE uses optimal transport to find the best coupling between data points and latent codes. This allows the posterior to adapt its shape to the data distribution, potentially capturing multi-modal or skewed structures that standard VAEs cannot.
- Core assumption: The cost function used in optimal transport (negative log likelihood) provides sufficient flexibility to learn complex posterior structures without parametric constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "allows greater flexibility in priors and naturally resolves the prior hole problem"
  - [section] "we can solve for the approximated posterior q(z|x) or more generally joint distribution π(z, x) using optimal transport solvers... without assuming the form of approximate distributions"

- Break Condition: If the optimal transport solver is limited to discrete approximations or the cost function is too restrictive, the posterior may still be constrained to simple forms despite the theoretical flexibility.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The C-VAE improves sample quality by optimizing both the decoder (as a cost function) and the posterior distribution simultaneously through the primal-dual formulation of optimal transport.
- Mechanism: The model jointly learns the likelihood function pθ(x|z) and the posterior distribution q(z|x) by alternating between optimizing the primal OT objective with respect to the likelihood and the dual OT formulation with respect to the posterior. This coordinated optimization ensures that the decoder is trained to handle the specific latent codes that the posterior generates, leading to better sample quality.
- Core assumption: The alternating optimization between primal and dual formulations converges to a good solution and that the learned cost function adequately represents the reconstruction loss.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "enables flexible optimization through the primal, dual, and semi-dual formulations of entropic OT"
  - [section] "We use the dual and semi-dual OT formulation solving for the approximate posterior in the continuous prior case and the Sinkhorn algorithm in the discrete prior case"

- Break Condition: If the alternating optimization doesn't converge properly or the step sizes are poorly tuned, the decoder and posterior may become misaligned, degrading sample quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prior distribution -> OT Solver -> Posterior -> Decoder -> Samples
- Critical path: Prior → OT Solver → Posterior → Decoder → Samples
  The model generates samples by first sampling from the prior, then using the learned posterior to find the corresponding latent codes, and finally decoding these codes through the trained decoder.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Flexibility vs. Computational Cost: More flexible priors and posteriors increase model expressiveness but also increase computational burden of the optimal transport solver.
  - Entropy Regularization Strength: Higher entropy regularization makes the problem more stable and easier to solve but may lead to blurrier results and less sharp couplings.
  - Alternating vs. Joint Optimization: Alternating between primal and dual optimization can be more stable but may converge slower than joint optimization.

- Failure signatures:
  - Degenerate couplings: If the entropy regularization is too low, the optimal transport coupling may become degenerate, assigning all probability mass to a few points.
  - Posterior collapse: If the decoder is too flexible or the cost function optimization is not properly balanced, the posterior may collapse to the prior, losing the ability to capture data-specific structure.
  - Mode dropping: If the optimal transport solver is not properly initialized or the step sizes are poorly tuned, the model may miss some modes of the data distribution.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the coupling constraint: Check that the marginal of the optimal transport coupling matches the prior distribution and that the conditional coupling q(z|x) produces reasonable posterior distributions for individual data points.
  2. Test sample quality: Generate samples from the model and visually inspect them for quality and diversity. Compare against samples from standard VAEs to verify the claimed improvements.
  3. Analyze the effect of entropy regularization: Train the model with different entropy regularization strengths and analyze the tradeoff between sample quality, posterior expressiveness, and computational cost.
