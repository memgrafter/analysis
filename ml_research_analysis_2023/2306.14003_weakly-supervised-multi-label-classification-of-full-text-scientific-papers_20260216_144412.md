# Weakly Supervised Multi-Label Classification of Full-Text Scientific Papers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14003
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14003
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes FuTex, a weakly supervised multi-label text classification framework for scientific papers that leverages both the cross-paper citation network structure and the in-paper hierarchy structure.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of classifying scientific papers into a large and fine-grained label space without requiring human-annotated training data. FuTex leverages both cross-paper citation network structure and in-paper hierarchy structure to improve classification performance. The framework uses network-aware contrastive fine-tuning to leverage citation links across papers, hierarchy-aware aggregation to exploit the hierarchical structure within each paper, and a self-training strategy to improve classification performance. Experiments on MAG-CS and PubMed datasets demonstrate that FuTex significantly outperforms competitive baselines, including scientific PLMs and weakly supervised text classifiers.

## Method Summary
FuTex processes scientific papers by first constructing a cross-paper citation network and parsing each paper's hierarchical structure. It then applies network-aware contrastive fine-tuning using SPECTER as the base PLM, creating positive pairs from papers connected via citation meta-paths and negative pairs from unconnected papers. The framework uses a Bi-Encoder architecture for paragraph embeddings and a Cross-Encoder for abstracts, aggregating embeddings bottom-up through the paper's hierarchy. A self-training strategy with Parabel classifier is employed to generate pseudo labels and refine predictions. The final output combines initial MRR-based rankings with self-trained classifier results to produce multi-label predictions.

## Key Results
- FuTex significantly outperforms competitive baselines including scientific PLMs, weakly supervised text classifiers, and structure-enhanced PLMs on MAG-CS and PubMed datasets
- FuTex achieves performance on par with fully supervised classifiers trained on 1,000 to 60,000 ground-truth training samples without requiring any annotated training data
- Ablation studies show that network-aware contrastive fine-tuning, hierarchy-aware aggregation, and self-training each contribute substantially to FuTex's superior performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The cross-paper network structure provides semantic proximity signals that improve fine-grained classification. By linking papers through citation meta-paths (e.g., Paper→Paper, Paper→Paper←Paper), FuTex uses contrastive fine-tuning to make paragraph embeddings from linked papers more similar. This encourages the model to detect subtle semantic relationships across papers. The core assumption is that two papers connected via a citation meta-path share more relevant topics than two randomly chosen papers.

### Mechanism 2
Hierarchy-aware aggregation allows efficient full-text utilization without exceeding sequence length limits. Instead of concatenating full texts into one long sequence, FuTex aggregates embeddings bottom-up from paragraphs to sections to the entire paper. This respects the structural containment relationship and prevents dilution of important abstract signals. The core assumption is that parent nodes in the hierarchy semantically contain their children, so simple averaging of child embeddings preserves meaningful semantic structure.

### Mechanism 3
Self-training with pseudo labels compensates for the retrieval stage's limited recall. After initial MRR-based ranking, the top-N predictions are used as pseudo labels to train a bag-of-words classifier (Parabel). This classifier then ranks additional labels not selected in retrieval, enriching the final prediction set. The core assumption is that confident initial predictions are accurate enough to serve as training signals for a complementary classifier.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Contrastive learning
  - **Why needed here**: To fine-tune the PLM so it can distinguish between fine-grained labels by pulling together semantically similar paragraphs from linked papers and pushing apart dissimilar ones.
  - **Quick check question**: In the contrastive loss, what is the role of the "anchor" paragraph versus the "positive" and "negative" paragraphs?

- **Concept**: Hierarchical text representation
  - **Why needed here**: To aggregate information from fine-grained paragraphs to coarser sections without exceeding PLM input limits, while preserving the importance of abstracts.
  - **Quick check question**: Why does bottom-up aggregation avoid the problem that Longformer encounters when more tokens are included?

- **Concept**: Weak supervision with label descriptions
  - **Why needed here**: Since no training labels exist, the model must learn to match paper content to label semantics using only names and descriptions.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the retrieval stage using exact name matching complement the PLM-based scoring?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input Pipeline -> Cross-Paper Network Builder -> Hierarchy Parser -> PLM (SPECTER) + Contrastive Fine-Tuning -> Bi-Encoder + Cross-Encoder Ensemble -> MRR-based Initial Ranking -> Self-Training (Parabel) -> Final Prediction Output

- **Critical path**: Paper → Hierarchy parsing → PLM encoding (abstract vs full text) → Contrastive fine-tuning → Bi-Encoder/Cross-Encoder scoring → MRR ranking → Self-training → Final ranking

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using Bi-Encoder for full-text paragraphs trades context for speed; Cross-Encoder reserved for abstracts where accuracy matters most. Retrieval stage reduces computation but may miss relevant labels; self-training mitigates this at the cost of additional training time. Simple averaging in hierarchy aggregation is efficient but may not capture complex semantic interactions.

- **Failure signatures**: If P@1 is high but P@5 is low → likely retrieval stage filtering out relevant labels. If overall performance drops with more training data → possible overfitting or noise in pseudo labels. If training time explodes → check if hierarchy aggregation or retrieval stage is misconfigured.

- **First 3 experiments**: 
  1. **Ablation test**: Run FuTex-NoNetwork, FuTex-NoHierarchy, FuTex-NoSelfTrain separately to quantify each module's contribution.
  2. **Retrieval recall check**: Measure what fraction of ground-truth labels are retrieved by exact name matching to assess retrieval stage effectiveness.
  3. **Contrastive learning sanity**: Visualize paragraph embeddings before and after fine-tuning to confirm linked papers' embeddings become more similar.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can the FuTex framework be extended to leverage other in-paper structural signals, such as the relationship between paragraphs and figures/tables, to further improve classification performance? The paper mentions this as an interesting future direction but doesn't provide implementation details or experimental validation.

### Open Question 2
How can large language models, such as GPT-4, be effectively utilized for weakly supervised fine-grained paper classification while addressing the maximum input length limit given the paper full text and tens of thousands of label names? The paper suggests this as a future direction but doesn't address the input length challenge or provide experimental results.

### Open Question 3
How does the performance of FuTex vary across different domains and datasets, and what are the factors that influence its effectiveness in different settings? The paper evaluates on only two datasets (MAG-CS and PubMed) without exploring performance across diverse scientific domains or analyzing domain-specific factors.

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness depends on the quality and availability of citation networks, which may not be comprehensive across all scientific domains
- The self-training component may propagate errors if initial predictions are noisy, but the paper doesn't analyze failure cases or provide confidence thresholds
- The evaluation compares against weakly supervised methods and fully supervised models trained on different data distributions, making direct performance comparison challenging

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core architectural components (network-aware contrastive fine-tuning, hierarchy-aware aggregation, self-training strategy) are well-motivated and supported by ablation results
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that FuTex is "on par with fully supervised classifiers" is supported by P@5 metrics but less so for P@1, where FuTex underperforms MICoL
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of FuTex across different scientific domains is uncertain, as experiments are limited to computer science and biomedical fields

## Next Checks

1. Conduct domain transfer experiments by evaluating FuTex on scientific papers from fields outside computer science and biomedical research to test generalizability.
2. Implement a noise analysis for the self-training component by measuring how performance degrades when initial predictions have varying levels of accuracy.
3. Compare FuTex against weakly supervised multi-label classification methods from non-scientific domains to establish whether the hierarchical and network structures provide domain-specific advantages.
