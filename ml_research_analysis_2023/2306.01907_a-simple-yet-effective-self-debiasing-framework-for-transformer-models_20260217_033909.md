# A Simple yet Effective Self-Debiasing Framework for Transformer Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01907
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01907
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: This paper addresses the problem of dataset bias in Transformer-based natural language understanding (NLU) models, which causes poor performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) test sets. The authors propose a simple yet effective self-debiasing framework that leverages the differences in feature learning between low and high layers of Transformer models. Specifically, they add a classifier on a low layer to capture common biased features, and introduce a residual connection to feed the low-layer sentence representation to the top-layer classifier. This encourages the top-layer representation to ignore biased features and focus on task-relevant unbiased features. ## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of dataset bias in Transformer-based natural language understanding (NLU) models, which causes poor performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) test sets. The authors propose a simple yet effective self-debiasing framework that leverages the differences in feature learning between low and high layers of Transformer models. Specifically, they add a classifier on a low layer to capture common biased features, and introduce a residual connection to feed the low-layer sentence representation to the top-layer classifier. This encourages the top-layer representation to ignore biased features and focus on task-relevant unbiased features. Experiments on three NLU tasks show that the proposed framework achieves new state-of-the-art results on all OOD test sets, with significant improvements over previous methods. For example, on the PAWS dataset for paraphrase identification, the framework improves accuracy by 26.3% compared to BERT.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework adds a classifier on a selected low layer of a Transformer model to capture common biased features, and introduces a residual connection that feeds the low-layer sentence representation to the top-layer classifier. This encourages the top-layer representation to ignore biased features and focus on task-relevant unbiased features. The model is trained with a combined loss for both classifiers, and during inference, the residual connection is removed to use only the top-layer representation for predictions. The framework is evaluated on three NLU tasks: Natural Language Inference (MNLI), Fact Verification (FEVER), and Paraphrase Identification (QQP), with their respective OOD test sets.

## Key Results
- The proposed framework achieves new state-of-the-art results on all OOD test sets for the three NLU tasks.
- On the PAWS dataset for paraphrase identification, the framework improves accuracy by 26.3% compared to BERT.
- The framework demonstrates consistent performance improvements across different Transformer-based models and tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Low-layer sentence representations encode common biased features that are also encoded by high-layer representations.
- Mechanism: During training, both low- and high-layer classifiers learn similar patterns for biased features, leading to similar performance on biased validation instances.
- Core assumption: Biased features are captured early in the model's feature hierarchy and persist through higher layers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "1) both low- and high-layer sentence representations encode common biased features during training"
  - [section 3.2]: "From Figure 2, we can find that all classifiers exhibit similar performance on biased validation instances"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on different aspects of sentence representation learning
- Break condition: If biased features are highly task-specific and not captured in low layers, or if layer-wise feature specialization differs significantly from the studied model.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High-layer representations encode more task-relevant unbiased features than low-layer ones.
- Mechanism: The residual connection allows the top-layer classifier to access low-layer biased features, encouraging it to focus on unbiased features that are not present in the low-layer representation.
- Core assumption: Task-relevant features are learned progressively through the network hierarchy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "2) the low-layer sentence representations encode fewer unbiased features than the high-layer ones"
  - [section 3.2]: "low-layer classifiers fl (1≤l≤5) perform worse than high-layer ones on anti-biased validation instances"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss different aspects of sentence representation learning
- Break condition: If the model architecture or pretraining objective changes the feature learning hierarchy, or if unbiased features are equally distributed across layers.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The residual connection encourages top-layer representations to ignore common biased features.
- Mechanism: By providing the low-layer representation as input to the top-layer classifier, the model learns to focus on features not already captured by the low layer, which are more likely to be task-relevant unbiased features.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively learn to distinguish between biased and unbiased features based on their presence/absence in different layers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we introduce a residual connection that feeds the low-layer sentence representation to the top-layer classifier"
  - [section 4]: "the top-layer sentence representation is encouraged to ignore the common biased features"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors don't directly address residual connections for debiasing
- Break condition: If the residual connection interferes with the learning of unbiased features, or if the low-layer representation becomes too dominant in the combined representation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Transformer encoder (L layers) -> Low-layer classifier (on layer lb) -> Residual connection -> Top-layer classifier (on layer L)

- Critical path:
  Input -> Transformer layers -> Low-layer representation -> Residual connection -> Top-layer representation -> Prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Choice of low layer (lb): Too low may miss important biased features; too high may not provide sufficient debiasing
  - Gradient flow: Residual connection gradients are turned off during training to prevent interference with low-layer learning
  - Inference simplicity: Residual connection is removed during inference to use only top-layer representation

- Failure signatures:
  - No improvement on OOD test sets: Residual connection not effective or wrong layer chosen
  - Significant performance drop on validation set: Over-debiasing or residual connection interfering with unbiased feature learning
  - Slow convergence: Additional classifier and residual connection increasing training complexity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary the choice of low layer (lb) and measure impact on validation and OOD test performance
  2. Compare performance with and without residual connection during inference to verify its necessity
  3. Analyze feature importance using interpretability methods to confirm that top-layer focuses on unbiased features after debiasing

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness depends on the availability of OOD test sets for validation, which may not always be available in practical applications.
- The layer selection strategy (lb=4) may need task-specific tuning and is not fully justified across all three tasks.
- The interpretability analysis using token-level metrics provides interesting insights but doesn't conclusively prove that the model is truly focusing on "unbiased" features.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The experimental results showing consistent improvements across all three NLU tasks on their respective OOD test sets.
- **Medium confidence**: The proposed mechanism explaining how layer-wise feature learning and the residual connection work together for debiasing.
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of the framework to other model architectures (e.g., RoBERTa, GPT variants) or to NLU tasks not studied in this paper.
