# Full Stack Optimization of Transformer Inference: a Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14017
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14017
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Full-stack co-design of Transformer inference can yield up to 88.7× speedup with minimal accuracy degradation

## Executive Summary
This comprehensive survey analyzes Transformer inference optimization across the full stack, from hardware design to model architecture. The authors perform a detailed case study using Gemmini, a DNN accelerator generator, demonstrating that nonlinear operations like LayerNorm, Softmax, and GELU dominate runtime despite minimal FLOPs due to memory bandwidth limitations and lack of dedicated hardware support. The survey reveals that memory hierarchy tuning, particularly larger accumulators for output reuse, can reduce matmul latency by 36%. Most significantly, the study shows that full-stack co-design combining hardware optimization, model compression, and neural architecture search can achieve up to 88.7× speedup while maintaining model accuracy.

## Method Summary
The survey analyzes Transformer inference optimization through systematic examination of four key areas: hardware design (accelerator generators like Gemmini), model optimization (quantization and pruning techniques like I-BERT), scheduling and mapping strategies, and neural architecture search. The case study methodology involves profiling BERT inference to identify bottlenecks, particularly in nonlinear operations, then iteratively optimizing the Gemmini accelerator's memory hierarchy and implementing specialized hardware units for these operations. The authors also explore NAS to discover efficient Transformer architectures and validate findings through quantitative measurements of latency, energy, and accuracy on benchmark datasets like WikiText-2.

## Key Results
- Nonlinear operations (LayerNorm, Softmax, GELU) can consume up to 96% of execution time despite minimal FLOPs due to memory bandwidth constraints
- Memory hierarchy optimization with larger accumulators reduces total matmul latency by 36% through improved output reuse
- Full-stack co-design combining hardware optimization, model compression, and NAS achieves up to 88.7× speedup with minimal accuracy loss

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Nonlinear operations in Transformers dominate runtime latency despite small FLOPs due to repeated passes over data and lack of hardware support.
- **Mechanism**: These operations require multiple memory passes (e.g., Softmax needs three passes for max subtraction trick), have low arithmetic intensity, and are often offloaded to CPU, causing high overhead.
- **Core assumption**: The accelerator lacks dedicated hardware units for these operations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "nonlinear operations... can result in less than 1% hardware utilization"
  - [section 3.4.2]: "96% of execution is spent on non-matmul operations"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no direct mention of nonlinear operations dominating latency.
- **Break condition**: Dedicated hardware units for these operations reduce latency by up to 39.6×.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Memory hierarchy tuning (larger accumulator, smaller scratchpad) improves Transformer performance due to high output reuse needs.
- **Mechanism**: Transformer act-to-act matmuls (e.g., query×key) have large output activations (𝑙 × 𝑙), benefiting from larger accumulators for output reuse. Convolutions in CNNs have higher arithmetic intensity and benefit from larger scratchpads.
- **Core assumption**: Output reuse is more critical than weight reuse for Transformers.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.4.3]: "Increasing the accumulation buffer size therefore allows for improved output reuse"
  - [section 3.4.3]: "36% reduction in total matmul latency"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; weak support.
- **Break condition**: Optimal memory hierarchy depends on operation characteristics (matmul vs. convolution).

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Scheduling matmuls in Transformers is as challenging as scheduling convolutions in CNNs despite fewer loops.
- **Mechanism**: Matmuls have 3 nested loops vs. 6 for convolutions, but the mapspace is still vast with similar ranges of EDP (Energy-Delay Product) and similar percentages of near-optimal mappings.
- **Core assumption**: Loop count doesn't directly correlate with scheduling difficulty.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 5.5.1]: "mapspaces for BERT matmuls and ResNet50 convolutions are similarly vast"
  - [section 5.5.1]: "BERT matmuls... are as challenging to schedule as CNNs"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; weak support.
- **Break condition**: Near-optimal mappings exist for both matmuls and convolutions with similar percentages.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: DRAM -> local scratchpad -> PE array (systolic array) -> accumulator -> output (with optional specialized units for nonlinear operations)
- **Critical path**: Data flows from off-chip DRAM through local scratchpad to PE array for matmuls, then to accumulator for partial sums, with potential intermediate steps for nonlinear operations
- **Design tradeoffs**: Balancing memory hierarchy (scratchpad vs. accumulator size), hardware support for nonlinear operations (area vs. latency), and mapping strategies (fusion vs. no fusion)
- **Failure signatures**: Low hardware utilization (<1%), high latency in nonlinear operations, poor memory reuse, and suboptimal mapping choices
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Profile BERT inference on baseline Gemmini to identify bottlenecks (non-matmul operations)
  2. Adjust memory hierarchy (e.g., increase accumulator size) and measure impact on latency
  3. Implement I-BERT-style nonlinear operation units and assess performance improvement

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal number of attention heads for balancing accuracy and hardware efficiency in Transformer models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that a hypothetical BERT model with fewer attention heads (4 instead of 12) reduces memory operations and improves arithmetic intensity for activation-to-activation matmuls, suggesting a tradeoff between accuracy and hardware efficiency.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore the accuracy impact of varying the number of attention heads or provide a definitive optimal number.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing model accuracy and hardware efficiency (latency, energy) across different numbers of attention heads on various Transformer architectures and tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of activation function (e.g., GELU vs ReLU) impact the efficiency of Transformer inference on hardware accelerators?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the computational cost of GELU in Transformers and how it can be approximated with integer polynomial functions for hardware efficiency. It also mentions ReLU as a common activation function in CNNs.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't compare the hardware efficiency of different activation functions in Transformers or provide a comprehensive analysis of their impact on accuracy and performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of Transformer models using different activation functions, evaluating their accuracy, latency, and energy consumption on various hardware platforms.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective strategies for scheduling and fusing LayerNorm and Softmax operations with preceding matmuls in Transformers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenges of fusing LayerNorm and Softmax with matmuls due to runtime statistics and tiling constraints. It presents a case study showing that fusion can sometimes hurt performance depending on accumulator size and sequence length.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a definitive answer on the best strategies for scheduling and fusing these operations, and the impact of fusion depends on various factors.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments exploring different scheduling and fusion strategies for LayerNorm and Softmax with matmuls, considering various hardware configurations, sequence lengths, and Transformer architectures.

## Limitations
- Limited quantitative validation across different hardware platforms and Transformer variants
- Case study relies on Gemmini's performance characteristics with unspecified hardware parameters
- Some NAS findings lack empirical validation across diverse Transformer architectures

- **Medium confidence**: Memory hierarchy optimization recommendations (based on limited case study)
- **Low confidence**: Some NAS findings due to limited empirical validation across diverse Transformer architectures
