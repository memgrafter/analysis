# BanglaCoNER: Towards Robust Bangla Complex Named Entity Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09306
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09306
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: BanglaBERT (large) achieves F1 score of 0.79 on validation for complex named entity recognition in Bangla

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses complex named entity recognition (CNER) in Bangla, a low-resource language with rich morphology. The authors compare two approaches: Conditional Random Fields (CRF) with handcrafted features and fine-tuning the large BanglaBERT transformer model on a synthetic dataset. The dataset contains 15,300 training and 800 validation sentences with 7 NER tags and significant English content. Results show BanglaBERT outperforms CRF, achieving an F1 score of 0.79 on validation. The work highlights the advantage of deep learning for CNER in synthetic, low-resource settings where linguistic patterns may not be immediately apparent to humans.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate two approaches for Bangla CNER: CRF with handcrafted features (POS tags, suffixes, prefixes, neighboring words, k-means clustering of embeddings, and gazetteers from NMT) and fine-tuning BanglaBERT (large) with various hyperparameters. The synthetic BanglaCoNER dataset contains 15,300 training and 800 validation sentences in CONLL format with 7 NER tags. For CRF, features were carefully selected to address Bangla-specific challenges like lack of title case and code-switching. BanglaBERT was fine-tuned with sequence lengths 64-128 and batch sizes 16-32 for 3-6 epochs. Weighted loss and oversampling were also experimented with but did not improve results.

## Key Results
- BanglaBERT (large) achieves F1 score of 0.79 on validation, outperforming CRF
- Ablation studies show handcrafted features improve over baseline but are less effective than contextualized representations
- Weighted loss and oversampling approaches did not improve performance
- Gazetteer augmentation using NMT improved recall for creative word category

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BanglaBERT's contextualized embeddings capture linguistic patterns that handcrafted features miss, especially for synthetic, transliterated, and compound entities
- Mechanism: Transformer-based models learn contextual relationships between tokens across the full sequence, allowing disambiguation of ambiguous entities (e.g., "CW" tags) that depend on surrounding context
- Core assumption: The pretraining corpus of BanglaBERT sufficiently covers the linguistic phenomena in the synthetic dataset
- Evidence anchors:
  - "not all linguistic patterns are immediately apparent or even intuitive to humans, which is why Deep Learning based models has proved to be the more effective model in NLP, including CNER task"
  - "BanglaBERT (large) outperforms CRF, achieving an F1 score of 0.79 on validation. Ablations show handcrafted features improve over baseline but are less effective than contextualized representations."
- Break condition: If the synthetic dataset contains domain-specific jargon not well-represented in the pretraining corpus

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Feature engineering for Bangla is challenging due to linguistic differences from English, necessitating careful selection of relevant features
- Mechanism: Bangla-specific morphological patterns (e.g., location suffixes like "aloy", "pur") are captured through adapted features like POS tags, suffixes, prefixes, and neighboring words
- Core assumption: Bangla-specific morphological patterns are reliable indicators for NER and can be captured by handcrafted features
- Evidence anchors:
  - "choosing features for Bangla Language turned out to be somewhat challenging compared to English... So, we had to choose the features carefully"
  - "We leveraged the suffix information for this kind of case. And our experiment shows that the macro F1 score increases by a significant margin in our validation set"
- Break condition: If the dataset contains significant code-switching or non-standard Bangla dialects

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Synthetic datasets with English content and transliteration benefit from gazetteers generated via NMT
- Mechanism: The CSE BUET Bangla t5 NMT model generates Bangla gazetteers for English entity names, allowing the CRF to recognize transliterated entities
- Core assumption: The NMT model's transliteration capability is accurate and consistent for the entity types in the dataset
- Evidence anchors:
  - "We used the CSE BUET Bangla t5 model which was fine-tuned for Neural Machine Translation (NMT) task excels at this kind of task... We had score improvements in the creative word category using this method"
- Break condition: If the NMT model produces inconsistent or incorrect transliterations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data pipeline: Load CONLL-formatted dataset → EDA → Split into train/validation
  - Feature extraction (CRF): POS tagging → Suffix/prefix extraction → k-neighbor words → Clustering → Gazetteers
  - Model training: CRF with scikit-learn → BanglaBERT finetuning with Hugging Face
  - Evaluation: Compute precision, recall, F1 for each NER tag and overall

- Critical path:
  - EDA → Feature engineering (CRF) or model selection (BanglaBERT) → Hyperparameter tuning → Training → Evaluation → Error analysis

- Design tradeoffs:
  - CRF: Interpretable, lightweight, but limited by handcrafted features and local context
  - BanglaBERT: Powerful contextual representations, but requires more compute and may overfit on small datasets
  - Feature selection: Balancing between capturing linguistic patterns and avoiding overfitting

- Failure signatures:
  - CRF: Low recall on complex/compound entities, poor performance on transliterated English words
  - BanglaBERT: Overfitting on training data, poor generalization to unseen entity types, high computational cost

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train CRF with basic features (POS, suffix) and evaluate on validation set to establish baseline
  2. Train BanglaBERT (base) with default hyperparameters and compare performance to CRF
  3. Perform ablation study on CRF features to identify most impactful features (e.g., remove gazetteers, observe performance drop)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How do the performance of CRF-based models compare to deep learning models when applied to non-synthetic, real-world Bangla CNER datasets?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on a synthetic dataset likely created via translation, and the authors note that feature-based models struggle with creative word (CW) tags, which depend on context
  - Why unresolved: The study only uses a synthetic dataset, so the generalizability of the findings to real-world data is unknown
  - What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating both CRF and deep learning models on a real-world, non-synthetic Bangla CNER dataset would provide a direct comparison

- Question: Would a different weighting function for the CrossEntropyLoss improve the performance of BanglaBERT on imbalanced CNER datasets?

  - Why unresolved: The paper only tested one specific weighting function and did not explore alternative approaches
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimenting with various weighting functions, such as inverse frequency weighting or focal loss

- Question: Can knowledge base augmentation improve the identification of Creative Word (CW) tags in Bangla CNER tasks without significantly increasing computational costs?

  - Why unresolved: The experiment was not completed due to resource constraints, leaving the potential benefit of knowledge base augmentation untested
  - What evidence would resolve it: Implementing efficient knowledge retrieval methods, such as entity linking or contextual embeddings, and evaluating their impact on CW tag identification while managing computational costs

## Limitations

- The synthetic nature of the BanglaCoNER dataset may limit generalizability to naturally occurring Bangla text
- The paper does not provide detailed hyperparameter configurations for either CRF or BanglaBERT, limiting reproducibility
- Limited error analysis beyond noting that Creative Word (CW) tags remain challenging due to context-dependency

## Confidence

- High confidence: The relative performance comparison between CRF and BanglaBERT (with BanglaBERT achieving higher F1 score) is well-supported by the reported results
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of handcrafted features for Bangla, while demonstrated, may be sensitive to dataset-specific characteristics and the quality of external tools used for feature generation
- Low confidence: The generalizability of results to real-world Bangla text and the optimality of the reported hyperparameters, given the lack of detailed experimental specifications
