# Wasserstein Auto-encoded MDPs: Formal Verification of Efficiently Distilled RL Policies with Many-sided Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12558
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12558
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: WAE-MDPs distill RL policies up to 10× faster than VAE-MDPs with better abstraction quality and enable formal verification

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of deploying deep reinforcement learning policies in safety-critical scenarios by introducing Wasserstein Auto-encoded Markov Decision Processes (WAE-MDPs). WAE-MDPs learn discrete latent space models from any RL policy that enable formal verification with bisimulation guarantees. Unlike previous approaches that rely on variational inference, WAE-MDPs directly minimize the optimal transport distance between the original and latent policies, learning both the distilled policy and latent model simultaneously. The framework overcomes key limitations of previous Variational MDPs by providing more efficient distillation, better latent model quality, and compatibility with model checking techniques.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a framework that learns a discrete latent MDP representation of any pre-trained RL policy through a Wasserstein distance minimization approach. The method uses relaxed Bernoulli and Gumbel-softmax distributions to learn discrete latent states and actions, while simultaneously optimizing transition dynamics with masked autoregressive flows. The training objective combines reconstruction loss with dual Wasserstein regularizers that enforce steady-state and transition distribution similarity between the original and latent policies. The approach enables extraction of a distilled policy and latent MDP that can be formally verified using model checking techniques like value iteration for properties such as time-to-failure.

## Key Results
- WAE-MDPs distill policies up to 10× faster than VAE-MDPs while achieving better latent model quality
- The approach provides PAC local loss bounds that guarantee bisimulation between original and distilled policies
- Formal verification techniques like time-to-failure properties can be applied to the learned latent MDPs
- Distilled policies maintain comparable performance to the original policies across multiple OpenAI Gym environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by learning a discrete latent representation that preserves the essential behavior of the original RL policy while enabling efficient formal verification. By minimizing the optimal transport distance rather than relying on variational inference, the method directly optimizes for policy similarity in both steady-state and transition distributions. The discrete latent space enables tractable model checking while the relaxation techniques allow gradient-based optimization of discrete structures.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Original Policy -> WAE-MDP Encoder -> Discrete Latent MDP -> Distilled Policy

**Critical Path**: Input states → Latent encoding → Transition dynamics → Action selection → Policy evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs**: Binary latent states enable efficient verification but may limit expressiveness; dual Wasserstein regularizers provide strong guarantees but increase computational cost

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Posterior collapse (latent states collapse to single state)
- High PAC local losses (poor bisimulation guarantees)
- Gradient instability in dual optimization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can WAE-MDPs effectively handle environments with high-dimensional observation spaces like images, where the choice of distance metrics for states, actions, and rewards becomes crucial?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the choice of distance metrics (dS, dA, dR) and mentions that high-dimensional observations like images might require tuning these metrics. It also notes that the Euclidean distance used in experiments might not be optimal for all cases.
- Why unresolved: The experiments were conducted on environments with relatively low-dimensional state spaces. The paper acknowledges the potential challenges but doesn't provide experimental evidence or solutions for high-dimensional cases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on environments with image-based observations (e.g., Atari games) using WAE-MDPs, comparing performance and latent space quality with different distance metrics and dimensionality reduction techniques.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of temperature parameters (λ) for continuous relaxation of discrete distributions impact the performance and learning dynamics of WAE-MDPs across different environments and latent space sizes?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the use of temperature-controlled relaxations for discrete latent spaces and mentions that the choice of temperature parameters is important. It also notes that different environments and latent space sizes might require different temperature settings.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions the importance of temperature parameters and provides a hyperparameter search range, it doesn't thoroughly analyze the impact of these choices on performance across different environments and latent space sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying temperature parameters across multiple environments with different characteristics and latent space sizes, analyzing the impact on learning speed, model quality, and distilled policy performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the WAE-MDP framework be extended to handle non-episodic, continuous interaction settings, and how would this affect the stationary distribution estimation and formal verification process?
- Basis in paper: The paper assumes episodic RL environments and uses a recursive ϵ-perturbation trick to sample from the stationary distribution. It also mentions that the ergodicity assumption is compliant with episodic RL but doesn't explicitly address continuous interaction settings.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on episodic environments and doesn't provide a clear extension or analysis for non-episodic, continuous interaction settings. The impact on stationary distribution estimation and formal verification in such cases is unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: An extension of the WAE-MDP framework to handle continuous interaction settings, with experiments demonstrating the impact on stationary distribution estimation, learning dynamics, and the ability to perform formal verification in these settings.

## Limitations
- Binary latent state representation may not capture full complexity of high-dimensional continuous state spaces
- Computational complexity of dual Wasserstein optimization requires careful hyperparameter tuning
- Assumes episodic RL environments, limiting applicability to continuous interaction settings

## Confidence
