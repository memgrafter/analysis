# Can Language Models Understand Physical Concepts?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.14057
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14057
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: Language models struggle with embodied concepts, but vision-augmented models and knowledge distillation can improve understanding.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces VEC, a benchmark for evaluating language models' understanding of physical concepts, including visual attributes (color, shape, material, size, height) and embodied concepts (mass, temperature, hardness). The study reveals that while scaling up language models improves understanding of certain visual concepts, they struggle with embodied knowledge. Vision-augmented models like CLIP and BLIP perform better on embodied concepts due to rich semantics in visual representations. The paper proposes a knowledge distillation method to transfer embodied knowledge from VLMs to LMs, achieving gains comparable to scaling up model parameters 134×, though significant gaps remain compared to human performance.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates language models on the VEC benchmark using zero-shot and few-shot prompting tailored to different model architectures. Text-only LMs (BERT, RoBERTa, OPT family) are compared against vision-augmented models (CLIP, BLIP, DeCLIP). For embodied concept transfer, a knowledge distillation method using Neuron Selectivity Transfer (NST) aligns activation patterns between teacher VLMs and student LMs. All models are re-trained on the YFCC-15M dataset for fair comparison, and human performance is measured using Cohen's kappa.

## Key Results
- Scaling up LMs improves understanding of certain visual concepts but fails on embodied concepts
- Vision-augmented models significantly outperform text-only LMs on embodied concepts
- Knowledge distillation from VLMs to LMs achieves performance gains comparable to 134× parameter scaling
- Significant gaps remain between LM performance and human understanding of physical concepts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Scaling up language models improves understanding of certain visual concepts through co-occurrence statistics in text.
- Mechanism: As model size increases, LMs better capture statistical patterns between visual attributes (like color-material co-occurrence) that frequently appear in text corpora.
- Core assumption: Visual concepts with strong linguistic co-occurrence patterns can be learned from text alone without visual grounding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our zero (few)-shot prompting results show that the understanding of certain visual concepts emerges as scaling up LMs"
  - [section] "It is because the combination of color and material frequently occurs (e.g., red apples) in raw texts. The co-occurrence statistics of color and material are well captured by large LMs"
  - [corpus] "Average neighbor FMR=0.426" (moderate relevance in related work, suggesting this is a recognized but not fully explored mechanism)
- Break condition: Scaling law fails for visual concepts that lack strong linguistic co-occurrence patterns, as shown by the height dataset where OPT-175B shows negligible improvement.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Vision-augmented models (VLMs) acquire embodied knowledge through rich semantics in visual representations.
- Mechanism: Image representations contain embodied knowledge signals (like mass and temperature) that are learned through contrastive training with textual descriptions.
- Core assumption: Visual representations encode embodied properties that can be transferred to language understanding through multimodal training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Analysis indicates that the rich semantics in visual representation can serve as a valuable source of embodied knowledge"
  - [section] "A case study demonstrates that embodied knowledge in the VLM of CLIP is potentially learned from image representations"
  - [corpus] "PhysVLM: Enabling Visual Language Models to Understand Robotic Physical Reachability" (related work showing VLMs can learn physical concepts)
- Break condition: VLMs struggle with ambiguous text descriptions where images are biased toward specific meanings, limiting knowledge transfer.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knowledge distillation can transfer embodied knowledge from VLMs to LMs by aligning activation patterns.
- Mechanism: Neuron Selectivity Transfer (NST) aligns spatial activation patterns between teacher VLMs and student LMs, effectively transferring embodied knowledge without requiring image inputs.
- Core assumption: Activation patterns in VLMs contain embodied knowledge that can be distilled to LMs through representation alignment rather than direct label supervision.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Inspired by this, we propose a distillation method to transfer embodied knowledge from VLMs to LMs, achieving performance gain comparable with that by scaling up parameters of LMs 134×"
  - [section] "We utilize Neuron Selectivity Transfer (Huang and Wang, 2017) which transfers the inner states such as spatial activation patterns of teacher neurons to student neurons"
  - [corpus] "Weak evidence - related work shows knowledge distillation works in other domains, but embodied concept transfer is novel"
- Break condition: Vanilla alignment on predicted word distributions fails, requiring specialized NST approaches with MMD loss.

  - Why needed here: Different LM architectures (masked, causal, vision-augmented) require different prompting strategies to elicit knowledge
  - Quick check question: Why can't we use [MASK] tokens for causal language models like GPT?

  - Why needed here: Transferring complex multimodal knowledge from VLMs to LMs requires specialized distillation methods beyond simple logits matching
  - Quick check question: What makes Neuron Selectivity Transfer more effective than standard distillation for embodied concepts?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - VEC benchmark: Evaluation framework for visual and embodied concepts
  - Text-only LMs: BERT, RoBERTa, OPT family (125M to 175B parameters)
  - Vision-augmented LMs: CLIP, BLIP, DeCLIP with text encoders
  - Knowledge distillation pipeline: Teacher VLMs → Student LMs with NST alignment
  - Prompt generation system: Task-specific templates for different model architectures

- Critical path:
  1. Evaluate baseline LMs on VEC benchmark
  2. Evaluate VLMs on VEC benchmark
  3. Analyze scaling effects and concept-specific performance
  4. Implement knowledge distillation from VLMs to LMs
  5. Validate distilled models on VEC benchmark
  6. Compare performance gains with scaling effects

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Text-only vs. vision-augmented: VLMs perform better but require image inputs; text-only models are more flexible but learn less embodied knowledge
  - Scaling vs. distillation: 134× parameter scaling vs. knowledge transfer - distillation is more parameter-efficient but may not capture all knowledge
  - Prompt complexity: More diverse prompts improve robustness but increase evaluation complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - Random-level performance on embodied concepts indicates missing multimodal grounding
  - High variance across prompts suggests prompt sensitivity or incomplete knowledge
  - Poor performance on ambiguous descriptions reveals limitations in VLM semantic understanding

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate OPT-175B on all VEC tasks to establish scaling baseline
  2. Implement and test Neuron Selectivity Transfer from CLIP-ViT/L-14 to OPT-1.3B
  3. Compare distilled model performance against scaled-up baseline (OPT-175B) on embodied concepts only

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does scaling up language models affect their understanding of embodied concepts compared to visual concepts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that scaling up LMs improves understanding of certain visual concepts, but they still struggle with embodied knowledge, even with models as large as OPT-175B.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that scaling improves performance on visual concepts, it does not provide a clear explanation for why scaling fails to improve understanding of embodied concepts. The relationship between model size and the ability to learn embodied knowledge remains unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing the performance of various-sized models on both visual and embodied concepts, along with an analysis of the types of errors made by larger models, could provide insights into the limitations of scaling for embodied knowledge.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can knowledge distillation effectively transfer embodied knowledge from vision-augmented models to text-only models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes a distillation method to transfer embodied knowledge from VLMs to LMs, achieving gains comparable to scaling up model parameters 134×. However, the improved LMs still exhibit significant gaps with human performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that knowledge distillation can improve LMs' understanding of embodied concepts, it does not explore the limitations of this approach or investigate alternative methods for transferring embodied knowledge. The effectiveness of distillation for different types of embodied knowledge and the factors influencing its success remain unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of distilled LMs on various embodied concepts, along with an analysis of the knowledge transferred from VLMs, could provide insights into the effectiveness and limitations of knowledge distillation for embodied knowledge transfer.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do vision-augmented language models learn embodied knowledge from visual representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that rich semantics in visual representations can serve as a valuable source of embodied knowledge, and a case study demonstrates that image representations in CLIP exhibit embodied knowledge.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides evidence that visual representations contain embodied knowledge, it does not explore the mechanisms by which VLMs learn this knowledge or investigate the types of visual information that are most useful for understanding embodied concepts. The relationship between visual representations and embodied knowledge remains unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis of the visual features learned by VLMs and their correlation with embodied knowledge, along with experiments manipulating the visual information available to VLMs, could provide insights into the mechanisms of embodied knowledge acquisition from visual representations.

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't fully quantify what fraction of visual knowledge can be learned from text statistics versus requiring multimodal grounding
- The analysis of how visual representations encode embodied knowledge is somewhat speculative without characterizing specific visual features
- The knowledge distillation approach shows impressive results but lacks systematic comparison with simpler baselines or robustness analysis

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The empirical finding that text-only LMs struggle with embodied concepts while VLMs perform better. This is directly supported by the benchmark results and multiple ablation studies.

**Medium confidence**: The mechanism explanation that co-occurrence statistics in text enable scaling benefits for certain visual concepts. While the data supports this pattern, the causal mechanism isn't fully established.

**Low confidence**: The specific effectiveness of the NST-based knowledge distillation method. While results show gains, the paper doesn't provide systematic ablations or comparison with alternatives.

## Next Checks

1. **Systematic scaling analysis**: Conduct controlled experiments varying dataset size, domain diversity, and co-occurrence patterns to quantify exactly what fraction of visual concept understanding comes from text statistics versus requiring visual grounding.

2. **VLM knowledge transfer mechanism**: Perform ablation studies on the contrastive learning components of VLMs to identify which specific training signals are most critical for learning embodied concepts.

3. **Knowledge distillation robustness**: Test the NST approach against simpler distillation baselines across multiple teacher-student pairs and datasets to assess generalizability and effectiveness.
