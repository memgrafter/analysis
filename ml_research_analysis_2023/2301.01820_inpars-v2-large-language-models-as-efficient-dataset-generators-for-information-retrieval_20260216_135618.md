# InPars-v2: Large Language Models as Efficient Dataset Generators for Information Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01820
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01820
- Reference count: 10
- New state-of-the-art average nDCG@10 of 0.536 on BEIR benchmark

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces InPars-v2, an improved method for using large language models to generate synthetic query-document pairs for training information retrieval systems. Unlike its predecessor InPars-v1 and the recently published Promptagator, InPars-v2 uses an open-source LLM (GPT-J with 6B parameters) to generate queries and employs a monoT5 reranker to filter and select the most relevant query-document pairs. The method generates one synthetic query per document in a corpus, then uses the reranker to score and select the top 10k pairs as positive examples, pairing them with BM25-retrieved negative examples. These synthetic pairs are used to finetune a monoT5-3B reranker, which is then applied to rerank BM25 retrieval results. On the BEIR benchmark, this approach achieves new state-of-the-art average nDCG@10 performance of 0.536, surpassing both InPars-v1 and Promptagator, particularly on datasets like TREC-News, Climate-FEVER, Robust, and Touche. The authors make their code, synthetic data, and models publicly available.

## Method Summary
InPars-v2 generates synthetic query-document pairs for training information retrieval systems by first using GPT-J-6B with few-shot prompting to create one query per document in a BEIR corpus. The generated pairs are then scored by a monoT5-3B reranker trained on MS MARCO, with only the top 10k pairs retained as positive examples. These are combined with BM25-retrieved negative examples and used to finetune monoT5-3B, which is then applied to rerank BM25 results. The entire pipeline uses open-source models and is designed for reproducibility, with the authors releasing their code, synthetic data, and trained models.

## Key Results
- Achieves new state-of-the-art average nDCG@10 of 0.536 on BEIR benchmark
- Outperforms both InPars-v1 and Promptagator baselines
- Shows particularly strong performance on TREC-News, Climate-FEVER, Robust, and Touche datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Open-source LLMs with few-shot prompting can generate high-quality synthetic queries for IR training.
- Mechanism: GPT-J-6B is prompted with 3 MS MARCO examples using the "gbq" template to generate one query per document, producing diverse and relevant query variants.
- Core assumption: A small number of high-quality few-shot examples is sufficient to guide the LLM toward generating relevant queries for arbitrary documents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "uses an open-source LLM (GPT-J with 6B parameters) to generate queries and employs a monoT5 reranker to filter and select the most relevant query-document pairs"
  - [section] "we use the open-source GPT-J [8] with 6B parameters to replace OpenAI’s curie model used in InPars-v1. For each dataset in the BEIR benchmark, we sample 100k documents from its corpus and generate one synthetic query per document using GPT-J prompted with 3 examples from MS MARCO."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence about few-shot quality from corpus; only related work exists.
- Break condition: If the LLM fails to capture domain-specific terminology or document nuances, generated queries will be irrelevant.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A monoT5 reranker trained on MS MARCO can effectively filter synthetic query-document pairs.
- Mechanism: After generating 100k synthetic pairs, monoT5-3B scores each pair and selects the top 10k highest-scoring pairs as positive training examples.
- Core assumption: The relevance signal learned from MS MARCO generalizes well enough to rank synthetic pairs for other domains.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "employs a monoT5 reranker to filter and select the most relevant query-document pairs"
  - [section] "we use monoT5-3B [4] already finetuned on MS MARCO for one epoch to estimate a relevancy score for each of the 100k query-document pairs. Then, we keep only the top 10k pairs with the highest scores as our positive query-document pairs for training."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus contains no direct evidence about filtering efficacy; only mentions related filtering work.
- Break condition: If MS MARCO relevance patterns diverge significantly from target domain, filtering may keep irrelevant pairs.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Finetuning monoT5-3B on synthetic data improves BM25 reranking performance on BEIR.
- Mechanism: monoT5-3B is first finetuned on MS MARCO, then further finetuned on the filtered synthetic pairs (10k positive + 10k BM25 negatives), creating a reranker that boosts nDCG@10.
- Core assumption: The synthetic data distribution approximates real query-document relevance well enough for effective transfer learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "These synthetic pairs are used to finetune a monoT5-3B reranker, which is then applied to rerank BM25 retrieval results"
  - [section] "The rerankers are finetuned in the same manner as in InPars-v1: monoT5-3B is finetuned on MS MARCO for one epoch and then further finetuned for one epoch on the synthetic data."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus does not provide evidence about finetuning effectiveness; only related methods are mentioned.
- Break condition: If synthetic data is too noisy or domain-shifted, finetuning may degrade or not improve performance.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Few-shot prompting with LLMs
  - Why needed here: Enables query generation without large supervised datasets; crucial for InPars-v2's data augmentation.
  - Quick check question: What prompt template and number of examples does InPars-v2 use for GPT-J query generation?

- Concept: Reranking and relevance scoring
  - Why needed here: Filters synthetic pairs and improves final retrieval quality; monoT5 is the core model.
  - Quick check question: How many synthetic query-document pairs are kept after monoT5 filtering?

- Concept: Transfer learning in IR
  - Why needed here: Allows MS MARCO-pretrained monoT5 to adapt to new domains using synthetic data.
  - Quick check question: What is the two-stage finetuning process for monoT5 in InPars-v2?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GPT-J-6B → monoT5-3B (filter) → monoT5-3B (reranker) → BM25 pipeline
- Critical path: Document → GPT-J query generation → monoT5 filtering → monoT5 finetuning → BM25 + rerank
- Design tradeoffs: Open-source LLM (GPT-J) vs proprietary (GPT-3); fewer synthetic pairs (10k) vs higher recall; monoT5-3B vs smaller models
- Failure signatures: Low nDCG improvement despite synthetic data; high variance across BEIR datasets; slow query generation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate synthetic queries for one BEIR dataset with GPT-J; verify output quality and diversity.
  2. Apply monoT5 filter on generated pairs; check top-10k selection distribution.
  3. Finetune monoT5-3B on MS MARCO then synthetic data; evaluate nDCG@10 vs BM25 baseline.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of InPars-v2 vary with different sizes of synthetic query-document pairs beyond the 10k top pairs used in the current implementation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that only the top 10k pairs with highest monoT5 scores are kept as positive examples, but doesn't explore the impact of using different numbers of pairs.
- Why unresolved: The authors chose 10k pairs as a threshold without systematic comparison to other values, leaving uncertainty about the optimal number for different datasets or model sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of retained synthetic pairs (e.g., 5k, 10k, 20k, 50k) across different BEIR datasets, showing nDCG@10 performance curves and computational cost trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the synthetic query quality from GPT-J-6B compare to proprietary models like GPT-3 or FLAN when evaluated using human relevance judgments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that InPars-v2 uses GPT-J-6B as an open-source alternative to proprietary models like GPT-3 used in InPars-v1 and FLAN used in Promptagator.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates competitive performance on BEIR, it doesn't directly compare the quality of synthetic queries generated by different LLMs through human evaluation or query clarity metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled study where human annotators rate the relevance and clarity of queries generated by GPT-J-6B, GPT-3, and FLAN for the same document corpus, with inter-annotator agreement scores and qualitative analysis.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of using dataset-specific prompts for synthetic query generation across all BEIR datasets, not just ArguAna as mentioned in preliminary experiments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that in preliminary experiments, dataset-specific prompts improved ArguAna by more than 10 nDCG@10 points and note that custom prompts are used in Promptagator.
- Why unresolved: The paper only reports results using a generic "gbq" prompt template from InPars-v1, leaving the potential gains from tailored prompts unexplored across the full BEIR benchmark.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments where each BEIR dataset is paired with a carefully designed prompt template, with performance comparisons showing nDCG@10 improvements over the generic prompt baseline and analysis of prompt characteristics that correlate with performance gains.

## Limitations
- Limited exploration of optimal number of synthetic pairs retained after filtering
- No direct evaluation of synthetic query quality beyond downstream IR performance
- Significant performance variance across BEIR datasets suggests method may not generalize equally well to all domains

## Confidence
- Confidence: Low for synthetic query quality claims due to lack of direct evaluation metrics beyond downstream nDCG@10 performance
- Confidence: Medium for monoT5 filtering effectiveness, as filtered synthetic data improves nDCG@10 on BEIR datasets
- Confidence: Medium for overall method superiority, as InPars-v2 achieves state-of-the-art average nDCG@10 but with variable improvements across datasets

## Next Checks
1. **Automated synthetic query evaluation**: Implement automated metrics (e.g., query coherence, relevance to document content) to assess the quality of GPT-J-generated queries independently of downstream IR performance, providing direct evidence for Mechanism 1.

2. **Domain transfer validation for filtering**: Test monoT5 filtering performance on held-out synthetic pairs from target domains to directly measure how well MS MARCO-trained relevance scores generalize, validating the core assumption of Mechanism 2.

3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary key hyperparameters (number of few-shot examples, synthetic pairs kept, finetuning epochs) to identify optimal configurations and assess method robustness across different choices, addressing potential overfitting and performance variance concerns.
