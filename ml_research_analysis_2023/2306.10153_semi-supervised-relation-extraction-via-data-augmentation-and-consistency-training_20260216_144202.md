# Semi-supervised Relation Extraction via Data Augmentation and Consistency-training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10153
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10153
- Reference count: 24
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on three out of four benchmark datasets for semi-supervised relation extraction

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of semi-supervised relation extraction by proposing a novel method that combines constrained back-translation for data augmentation with consistency training. The key innovation lies in using lexically constrained decoding to preserve entity integrity during translation, while entity type markers help leverage pre-trained language model knowledge. The method is evaluated on four benchmark datasets and demonstrates significant improvements over existing approaches, particularly when leveraging unlabelled data.

## Method Summary
The proposed method tackles semi-supervised relation extraction through a three-pronged approach: (1) constrained back-translation using lexically constrained decoding to preserve entities during translation, (2) REMix - latent space interpolation for generating additional training data, and (3) entity type markers to enhance the model's ability to capture entity-specific context. The model employs consistency training by combining supervised and unsupervised loss components, with the unsupervised loss derived from consistency between original and augmented examples. Experiments use varying proportions of labelled data (3%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 30%) with 50% unlabelled data across four benchmark datasets.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art F1 scores on three of four benchmark datasets (TACRED, RE-TACRED, KBP37)
- Demonstrates significant improvements when leveraging unlabelled data compared to supervised baselines
- Ablation studies show effectiveness of each component: data augmentation, entity type markers, and modified model architecture

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by addressing the fundamental challenge in semi-supervised RE where standard data augmentation techniques fail to preserve entity integrity and sentence meaning. By using lexically constrained decoding, the back-translation process maintains entity positions and semantic content while generating diverse training examples. The entity type markers provide explicit signals to the model about entity roles, improving relation extraction performance. Consistency training ensures the model learns robust representations by enforcing agreement between predictions on original and augmented examples.

## Foundational Learning
- **Lexically constrained decoding**: Required for preserving entities during back-translation; quick check: verify augmented sentences retain original entities
- **Latent space interpolation (REMix)**: Generates synthetic training examples; quick check: ensure interpolated examples maintain valid relation patterns
- **Entity type markers**: Explicit signals for entity roles; quick check: confirm markers are properly tokenized and incorporated
- **Consistency training**: Enforces agreement between original and augmented examples; quick check: monitor consistency loss during training
- **Semi-supervised learning**: Leveraging unlabelled data; quick check: verify stratified sampling maintains label distribution
- **BERT-based relation extraction**: Foundation model architecture; quick check: ensure proper tokenization and input formatting

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Input Sentence -> Entity Type Markers -> BERT Encoder -> Relation Encoder -> MLP Classifier -> Output Relations

Critical Path: Data Augmentation (constrained back-translation + REMix) → Model with Entity Type Markers → Consistency Training → Final Predictions

Design Tradeoffs:
- Constrained back-translation vs. simple synonym replacement: Higher quality augmentation at computational cost
- Entity type markers vs. entity position only: Better entity-specific context but increased input complexity
- Consistency training weight: Balance between supervised and unsupervised objectives

Failure Signatures:
- Poor performance due to inadequate constrained decoding leading to entity corruption
- Suboptimal entity type marker integration causing loss of entity-specific context
- Incorrect consistency training implementation resulting in noisy gradient updates

Three First Experiments:
1. Implement constrained back-translation and manually inspect augmented examples for entity preservation
2. Train baseline model without data augmentation and consistency training for comparison
3. Conduct ablation study on entity type markers by training models with and without them

## Limitations
- Implementation details of lexically constrained decoding not fully specified, making exact reproduction challenging
- Precise MLP classifier architecture (layers, dimensions) not provided, affecting direct comparison
- Specific hyperparameters for consistency training beyond learning rate and warmup ratio are unspecified

## Confidence

High confidence: The overall approach combining data augmentation, entity type markers, and consistency training is sound and well-motivated.

Medium confidence: The reported improvements over baselines on three out of four benchmark datasets are likely reproducible, but exact numbers may vary.

Low confidence: The effectiveness of each individual component (constrained back-translation, REMix, entity type markers) as demonstrated in ablation studies, due to lack of detailed implementation information.
