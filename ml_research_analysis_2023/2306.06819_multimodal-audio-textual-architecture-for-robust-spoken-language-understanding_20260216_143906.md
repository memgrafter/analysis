# Multimodal Audio-textual Architecture for Robust Spoken Language Understanding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06819
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06819
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: Multimodal fusion of Wav2Vec audio and BERT/RoBERTa text improves robustness against ASR errors in SLU tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a multimodal language understanding (MLU) architecture that combines audio and text modalities to mitigate ASR error propagation in SLU systems. The approach uses Wav2Vec for speech encoding and BERT/RoBERTa for text processing, fusing their logits via late fusion. Experiments on five SLU tasks across three datasets demonstrate that MLU outperforms unimodal baselines on degraded ASR transcripts while maintaining performance on clean transcripts. Noise injection during training further enhances robustness.

## Method Summary
The MLU architecture encodes audio with Wav2Vec and text with BERT/RoBERTa, then fuses their logits through late fusion (averaging or fine-tuning). Wav2Vec extracts deep semantic features from speech, while BERT/RoBERTa processes text transcripts. The system is trained with optional noise injection to simulate ASR errors. Experiments evaluate robustness on academic (CMU Sphinx) and commercial (Google ASR API, WIT) ASR outputs across FSC, SNIPS, and SLURP datasets.

## Key Results
- MLU outperforms BERT/RoBERTa on academic ASR transcripts while maintaining performance on commercial ASR outputs.
- Fine-tuned late fusion outperforms simple averaging of logits for robustness.
- Noise injection during training consistently improves performance on degraded transcripts.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Late fusion of audio and text logits improves robustness against ASR errors by combining complementary information.
- Mechanism: The system separately encodes audio with Wav2Vec and text with BERT/RoBERTa, then averages or fine-tunes their logits before classification. This allows the model to rely on clean speech embeddings when text is corrupted.
- Core assumption: Speech embeddings carry sufficient semantic information to compensate for degraded ASR transcripts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The MLU benefits from self-supervised features learned from both audio and text modalities, specifically Wav2Vec for speech and Bert/RoBERTa for language."
  - [section]: "Our MLU combines an encoder network to embed the audio signal and a text encoder to process text transcripts followed by a late fusion layer to fuse audio and text logits."
- Break condition: If speech embeddings are too noisy or not semantically aligned with text, the fusion may degrade rather than improve performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Wav2Vec provides robust speech representations that mitigate ASR error propagation.
- Mechanism: Wav2Vec is pre-trained on large amounts of unlabeled audio, producing contextual embeddings that encode phonetic and semantic information beyond what ASR transcripts capture.
- Core assumption: Wav2Vec embeddings are sufficiently discriminative for downstream SLU tasks and can bypass reliance on ASR output.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We use the wav2vec model to extract deep semantic features from speech... wav2vec is an self-supervised pre-trained model trained on a large amount of unlabelled audio."
  - [corpus]: Limited direct evidence in nearby literature; assumption that Wav2Vec's pre-training generalizes well for SLU.
- Break condition: If the domain or accent of the speech differs significantly from Wav2Vec's pre-training data, the embeddings may not generalize.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Noise injection during training improves generalization to ASR errors.
- Mechanism: Random word replacement, deletion, or insertion simulates ASR errors in the training text, forcing the model to learn robustness.
- Core assumption: The types of noise injected approximate real ASR errors sufficiently to improve test-time performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We injected noise into the training set... We used lexical replacement which consists of proposing one or more words that can replace a given word."
  - [section]: "Table 4 presents results without and with noise injection... Noise injection was found to be beneficial for both systems."
- Break condition: If noise patterns do not match real ASR error distributions, training may not transfer to actual degraded transcripts.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Wav2Vec self-supervised pre-training
  - Why needed here: Provides speech embeddings without requiring labeled audio, enabling multimodal fusion even when ASR transcripts are unreliable.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between Wav2Vec's pre-training objective and a supervised ASR model's objective?

- Concept: Late fusion vs. early fusion in multimodal architectures
  - Why needed here: Late fusion allows independent optimization of audio and text encoders before combining, simplifying training and preserving modality-specific strengths.
  - Quick check question: Why might early fusion of audio and text embeddings lead to suboptimal performance in this SLU context?

- Concept: ASR error propagation in cascade SLU systems
  - Why needed here: Understanding how errors in ASR transcripts affect downstream NLU motivates the need for robustness mechanisms like multimodal fusion.
  - Quick check question: What are the two main reasons ASR error propagation degrades NLU performance in traditional pipelines?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Wav2Vec speech encoder -> Convolutional + LSTM fine-tuning layers -> Logits
  - BERT/RoBERTa text encoder -> Logits
  - Late fusion layer (average or fine-tuned) -> Classification head
  - Optional noise injection module during training

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess audio and text inputs
  2. Extract Wav2Vec embeddings and pass through conv-LSTM encoder
  3. Encode text with BERT/RoBERTa
  4. Fuse logits from both modalities
  5. Apply classification head

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Late fusion simplifies training but may miss early interactions between modalities
  - Fine-tuning vs. averaging logits: fine-tuning may overfit to clean text during training but performs better when text is noisy
  - Adding noise injection improves robustness but increases training time and hyperparameter tuning complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - Degradation when both modalities are noisy
  - Over-reliance on text when fine-tuning is used, leading to poor performance with low-quality transcripts
  - Poor generalization if Wav2Vec embeddings do not align semantically with text

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train and evaluate NLU baselines (BERT/RoBERTa) on golden vs. ASR transcripts to quantify error propagation.
  2. Train MLU with late fusion (averaging logits) on golden transcripts, then test on ASR transcripts to assess robustness.
  3. Compare MLU with fine-tuned logits vs. averaging logits on noisy ASR transcripts to determine optimal fusion strategy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

## Limitations
- ASR error simulation validity: Lexical replacement may not capture real ASR error patterns like homophone confusion or phonetic proximity errors.
- Modality fusion optimality: Late fusion may miss early cross-modal interactions that could improve performance for tasks where audio prosody is critical.
- Wav2Vec generalization: Robustness claims rely on Wav2Vec's pre-training capturing sufficient semantic and phonetic information across diverse domains.

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core claim that multimodal fusion mitigates ASR error propagation is supported by consistent improvements across three datasets and multiple ASR engines.
- Medium confidence: The specific advantage of fine-tuned late fusion over simple averaging is shown but may be task-dependent.
- Low confidence: The exact mechanisms by which Wav2Vec compensates for ASR errors are not fully characterized.

## Next Checks
1. Error type analysis: Categorize ASR errors (deletions, insertions, substitutions) and measure MLU performance degradation for each type compared to text-only baselines.
2. Domain transfer robustness: Test MLU on a held-out domain or accent not represented in pre-training data to assess Wav2Vec's generalization limits.
3. Alternative fusion strategies: Implement and compare early fusion against late fusion to evaluate whether early fusion captures complementary information missed by late fusion.
