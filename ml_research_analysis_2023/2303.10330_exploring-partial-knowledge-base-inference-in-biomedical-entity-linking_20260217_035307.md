# Exploring Partial Knowledge Base Inference in Biomedical Entity Linking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10330
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10330
- Reference count: 21
- We explore partial KB inference for biomedical entity linking (EL), where models are trained on one KB but infer on a subset without retraining. Three EL paradigms show dramatic performance drops due to NIL mentions being incorrectly linked, and we propose post-pruning and thresholding redemption methods that improve performance with little computational overhead.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the challenge of partial knowledge base inference in biomedical entity linking, where models trained on a full KB must perform inference on a subset without retraining. The authors find that all three major EL paradigms (NER-NED, NED-NER, and Simultaneous Generation) suffer dramatic precision degradation when applied to partial KBs, primarily due to NIL mentions being incorrectly linked to entities within the partial KB. They propose two simple redemption methods - post-pruning and thresholding - that significantly improve performance with minimal computational overhead.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three biomedical entity linking paradigms on partial KB inference: NER-NED (sequential NER then NED), NED-NER (sequential NED then NER), and Simultaneous Generation (end-to-end generative). The authors construct benchmarks using BC5CDR and MedMentions datasets with various knowledge bases (UMLS, MeSH, MEDIC, SNOMED, T038, T058) to simulate partial KB settings. They propose two redemption methods: post-pruning (removing entities not in the partial KB after linking) and thresholding (removing mention-entity pairs below a confidence threshold). The evaluation measures entity linking and mention detection performance through precision, recall, and F1 scores.

## Key Results
- All three EL paradigms show significant precision degradation when inferring on partial KBs, with F1 drops of 6.38-24.09 points
- NIL mentions are incorrectly linked to entities within the partial KB, causing the performance degradation
- Post-pruning and thresholding redemption methods improve performance with minimal computational overhead
- EntQA paradigm shows better robustness to partial KB inference due to its concept retrieval stage filtering out-of-KB concepts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The dramatic performance degradation in partial KB inference is primarily caused by NIL (unseen in partial KB) mentions being incorrectly linked to entities within the partial KB.
- Mechanism: When a mention refers to an entity outside the partial KB, standard EL models will still attempt to link it to the best available concept in the partial KB, resulting in false positives. This causes precision to drop sharply because many of the linked mentions are incorrect.
- Core assumption: The model does not distinguish between mentions that are truly in the partial KB and those that are not; it always produces a link.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our findings reveal these EL paradigms can not correctly handle unlinkable mentions (NIL), so they are not robust to partial KB inference."
  - [section] "Our experimental findings reveal the different implicit mechanisms and performance bottlenecks within each paradigm which shows partial KB inference is challenging."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: EntQA is more robust to partial KB inference because it filters out irrelevant concepts before attempting mention detection.
- Mechanism: EntQA uses a two-stage approach: first it retrieves candidate concepts from the partial KB, then it detects mentions associated with those concepts. This design inherently filters out concepts not in the partial KB, reducing the chance of linking NIL mentions.
- Core assumption: The concept retrieval step effectively excludes out-of-KB concepts, so the NER stage only deals with in-KB concepts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our findings reveal these EL paradigms can not correctly handle unlinkable mentions (NIL), so they are not robust to partial KB inference."
  - [section] "EntQA detects mentions according to retrieved concepts. This explicit modeling makes it more robust since it handles out-of-KB mentions by filtering out irrelevant concepts in the retrieving stage."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The thresholding and post-pruning methods improve performance by removing NIL-related false positives.
- Mechanism: Thresholding removes mention-entity pairs with low confidence scores, which often correspond to NIL mentions. Post-pruning removes entities not in the partial KB after linking, directly eliminating false positives caused by NIL mentions.
- Core assumption: NIL-related mention-entity pairs tend to have lower confidence scores or refer to entities outside the partial KB.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also propose two simple-and-effective redemption methods to combat the NIL issue with little computational overhead."
  - [section] "Thresholding removes entities with scores below a threshold. These two redemption methods are all designed to reduce the impact of NIL entities and boost EL performances."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: NER-NED -> NED-NER -> Simultaneous Generation (three distinct paradigms)
- Critical path: For NER-NED and Simultaneous Generation, the critical failure point is the NED step mislinking NIL mentions. For NED-NER, the critical point is the concept retrieval step.
- Design tradeoffs: NER-NED is simple but vulnerable to NIL mentions. NED-NER is more robust but requires efficient concept retrieval. Simultaneous Generation is elegant but struggles with partial KB inference.
- Failure signatures: Sharp precision drop with stable recall indicates NIL mentions being incorrectly linked. Mention detection performance drop indicates the model is detecting mentions that should be NIL.

  1. Run partial KB inference on a small dataset to observe precision degradation patterns.
  2. Implement thresholding on the baseline model to test if it improves precision.
  3. Compare EntQA's performance with NER-NED baseline on partial KB inference to confirm robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of post-pruning and thresholding vary across different biomedical entity linking paradigms beyond the three investigated?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates three paradigms (NER-NED, NED-NER, Simultaneous Generation) and proposes redemption methods, but does not explore other potential paradigms.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on three specific paradigms and does not test the proposed redemption methods on other entity linking approaches that may exist in the literature.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments applying post-pruning and thresholding to a broader range of biomedical entity linking paradigms would clarify their generalizability and effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can incorporating hierarchical information from biomedical knowledge bases improve the handling of NIL entities in partial KB inference?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The limitations section mentions that a hierarchical structure exists in biomedical KBs and suggests mapping NILs to hypernym concepts as a potential solution.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not experiment with using hierarchical information to address NIL entities, focusing instead on simple post-processing methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance when using hierarchical mappings for NIL entities versus the proposed post-pruning and thresholding methods would demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of partial KB inference change with different proportions of partial KB annotations in the training data?

- Why unresolved: While the correlation is observed, the study does not systematically investigate how varying the proportion of partial KB annotations affects performance across different paradigms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments with varying proportions of partial KB annotations in training data, measuring performance across different paradigms, would quantify the impact of annotation distribution on partial KB inference.

## Limitations
- The paper's claims about partial KB inference robustness hinge on the assumption that NIL mentions are the primary source of performance degradation, though other contributing factors may exist
- The redemption methods (post-pruning and thresholding) are presented as simple and effective, but their optimality is not rigorously established
- The evaluation focuses on two biomedical datasets; generalization to other domains or larger-scale KBs remains untested

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The observation that all three EL paradigms suffer precision degradation during partial KB inference is well-supported by the experimental results. The mechanism explaining this through NIL mention mislinking is consistent with the data.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that EntQA is more robust to partial KB inference due to its two-stage approach is supported but not definitively proven. While the paper shows EntQA performs better, the exact contribution of the concept retrieval stage versus other architectural differences is not isolated.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of thresholding and post-pruning redemption methods is demonstrated, but the paper doesn't explore parameter sensitivity or compare against alternative NIL handling strategies, limiting confidence in their general applicability.

## Next Checks

1. **NIL Detection Analysis**: Implement an oracle NIL detector to measure the upper bound of performance improvement achievable through better NIL handling, helping quantify how much of the performance gap is truly due to NIL mentions versus other factors.

2. **Cross-Domain Generalization**: Evaluate the partial KB inference performance and redemption methods on non-biomedical datasets (e.g., Wikipedia-based EL datasets) to test whether the observed patterns and solutions generalize beyond the biomedical domain.

3. **Alternative Redemption Methods**: Compare the proposed thresholding and post-pruning approaches against more sophisticated NIL handling strategies, such as uncertainty estimation techniques or out-of-distribution detection methods, to establish whether the simple methods are near-optimal or if significant improvements are possible.
