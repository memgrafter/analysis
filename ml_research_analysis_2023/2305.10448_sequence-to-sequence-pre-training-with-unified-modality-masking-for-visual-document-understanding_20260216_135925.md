# Sequence-to-Sequence Pre-training with Unified Modality Masking for Visual Document Understanding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10448
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10448
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: GenDoc achieves superior or competitive performance on DocVQA, Publaynet, CORD, and RVL-CDIP tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GenDoc, a sequence-to-sequence transformer model for visual document understanding that unifies pre-training across text, image, and layout modalities. The model employs modality-specific instructions, disentangled attention in the encoder, and mixture-of-modality-experts in the decoder. Extensive experiments demonstrate GenDoc's superior or competitive performance on four document understanding tasks, with particular robustness to imperfect OCR conditions.

## Method Summary
GenDoc is a sequence-to-sequence transformer that pre-trains on IIT-CDIP dataset using three unified tasks: text infilling, masked image token prediction, and masked coordinate prediction. The encoder uses disentangled attention to separately model content-to-content and content-to-layout interactions, while the decoder employs mixture-of-modality-experts to activate task-specific feed-forward networks. The model processes documents by extracting text with OCR, obtaining layout coordinates, and encoding images through a backbone, then jointly modeling all modalities through unified masking strategies.

## Key Results
- GenDoc achieves superior performance on DocVQA (1.4 ANLS improvement) and competitive results on PubLayNet (mAP 94.9), CORD (F1 96.5), and RVL-CDIP (accuracy 95.6)
- Ablation studies show disentangled attention and MoE strategies provide consistent performance improvements across tasks
- Analysis demonstrates GenDoc's robustness to imperfect OCR conditions compared to encoder-only models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Disentangled attention in the encoder improves layout understanding by separately modeling content-to-content and content-to-layout interactions.
- Mechanism: By splitting attention into content-to-content, content-to-x-dimension layout, and content-to-y-dimension layout, the model can independently refine spatial awareness without interference from sequential text dependencies.
- Core assumption: Document text order is not inherently sequential, so layout coordinates provide more useful positional context than 1D token positions.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "In order to reinforce the effectiveness of layout information, we incorporate the disentangled attention into our transformer encoder following previous research efforts (He et al., 2021; Peng et al., 2022). Specifically, we utilize attentions from content-to-content, content-to-layout in the x-dimension, and content-to-layout in the y-dimension..."
- Break condition: If document text has strong sequential dependencies (e.g., prose paragraphs), ignoring 1D position embeddings could degrade understanding.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Mixture-of-modality-experts (MoE) in the decoder allows modality-specific processing, improving task performance by avoiding cross-modality confusion.
- Mechanism: Separate feed-forward networks are activated for different downstream tasks, with textual expert for language tasks, visual expert for image token prediction, and layout expert for coordinate prediction.
- Core assumption: Different downstream tasks rely predominantly on one modality, so dedicated experts can specialize better than a universal decoder.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "Motivated by such an observation, we adopt the mixture-of-modality-experts (MoE) (Wang et al., 2021) strategy in our decoder. We use independent feed-forward networks (FFNs) for different pre-training or downstream tasks."
- Break condition: If downstream tasks require strong cross-modal reasoning (e.g., VQA needing both text and image simultaneously), MoE could limit integration.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Unified sequence-to-sequence modeling makes the approach robust to OCR imperfections by relying less on exact token matches.
- Mechanism: By modeling tasks as abstractive generation (e.g., answering questions with free text) rather than extractive matching, the model can compensate for OCR errors through context and generation flexibility.
- Core assumption: OCR errors are often isolated token-level issues that can be resolved through semantic understanding and generation.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "Instead of modeling the DocVQA task as an extractive question answering, as is commonly done in previous work (Xu et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021), we model this task as an abstractive question answering, which benefits from our sequence-to-sequence modeling."
- Break condition: If OCR errors are systematic or semantic (e.g., wrong entity recognition), generation-based approaches may still fail.

- Critical path: Input → Encoder (text+layout via disentangled attention, image via backbone) → Cross-modal attention → Decoder (MoE activation) → Output generation
- Design tradeoffs: Sequence-to-sequence vs encoder-only (flexibility vs efficiency), MoE vs universal decoder (specialization vs integration), abstractive vs extractive QA (robustness vs precision)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does GenDoc's performance compare when using different OCR engines (e.g., commercial vs. internal OCR) across all downstream tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that OCR quality significantly impacts performance and compares results using Microsoft Read API versus their internal OCR, showing improved robustness with GenDoc.
- Why unresolved: The comparison was limited to DocVQA and used only two OCR sources; the impact across all four tasks and with other OCR engines remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing multiple OCR engines (e.g., Amazon Textract, Google Vision OCR) across all downstream tasks, reporting performance metrics for each combination.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the effect of varying the masking ratio for text, image, and layout modalities during pre-training on downstream task performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses fixed masking ratios (30% text, 50% image patches, 20% layout spans) but does not explore how different ratios might affect performance.
- Why unresolved: The optimal masking ratio could vary by modality and task, but this was not explored in the ablation studies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation experiments testing a range of masking ratios for each modality during pre-training, measuring downstream performance across all tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does GenDoc's sequence-to-sequence architecture perform on tasks beyond the four evaluated (e.g., table detection, form field extraction, or document summarization)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that GenDoc is designed to handle various downstream tasks with diverse output formats but only evaluate on four specific tasks.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation is limited to document visual question answering, layout analysis, form understanding, and document classification; broader applicability remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing GenDoc on additional document understanding tasks such as table detection, form field extraction, document summarization, or visual document grounding, reporting performance relative to specialized models.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies heavily on the paper's self-reported results without access to underlying data or ablation studies that would definitively validate proposed mechanisms
- Several key implementation details remain unspecified, particularly the exact architectural configurations of the disentangled attention and mixture-of-modality-experts components
- The corpus search yielded minimal relevant neighboring work, suggesting this may be a relatively isolated contribution in current literature

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the basic experimental setup and reported results, as these follow standard practices in visual document understanding and the performance metrics are clearly specified
- **Medium confidence** in the disentangled attention mechanism's effectiveness, as the paper provides theoretical justification and cites prior work, but lacks direct ablation studies demonstrating the specific contribution
- **Low confidence** in the claimed robustness to OCR imperfections, as this assertion is based on qualitative observation rather than systematic experiments varying OCR quality

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study on disentangled attention**: Remove the disentangled attention mechanism and compare performance on layout-heavy tasks like PubLayNet to quantify the specific contribution of this architectural choice versus standard self-attention
2. **Controlled OCR quality experiments**: Systematically degrade OCR quality on DocVQA and other text-heavy datasets to empirically measure the robustness gains from sequence-to-sequence modeling versus encoder-only baselines
3. **Cross-task generalization test**: Evaluate GenDoc's performance when fine-tuned on one task but tested on another (e.g., pre-training on DocVQA and testing on CORD) to assess whether the MoE strategy enables true modality specialization or simply task memorization
