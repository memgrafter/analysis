# ScandEval: A Benchmark for Scandinavian Natural Language Processing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.00906
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00906
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Key outcome**: Benchmarking platform for Scandinavian NLP that found national language models outperform multilingual models across four tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ScandEval, a comprehensive benchmarking platform for Scandinavian natural language processing that evaluates pretrained models on four tasks across Danish, Swedish, and Norwegian. The authors developed a Python package and CLI tool that enables reproducible benchmarking of any Hugging Face model, constructing two new datasets (ScandiQA for question answering and ScaLA for linguistic acceptability) alongside existing datasets for named entity recognition and sentiment classification. Through benchmarking over 100 models, they demonstrate that language models developed specifically for Scandinavian languages outperform multilingual alternatives like XLM-RoBERTa and mDeBERTaV3, while also revealing substantial cross-lingual transfer within Mainland Scandinavian languages but limited transfer to Insular Scandinavian languages (Icelandic, Faroese).

## Method Summary
The ScandEval benchmark uses a consistent finetuning pipeline with learning rate 2×10⁻⁵, 100 warmup steps, batch size 32, and early stopping after 90 steps of no validation improvement. Each model-dataset combination undergoes 10 bootstrapped runs with fixed random seeds to ensure reproducibility and calculate 95% confidence intervals. The framework evaluates models on named entity recognition (micro F1), sentiment classification (Matthews Correlation Coefficient), linguistic acceptability (binary accuracy), and question answering (F1), aggregating these into overall ScandEval scores. The benchmarking package, scandeval, can evaluate any model uploaded to Hugging Face Hub using a uniform dataset format.

## Key Results
- National language models developed in Norway, Sweden, and Denmark outperform multilingual models like XLM-RoBERTa and mDeBERTaV3 on ScandEval tasks
- Substantial cross-lingual transfer exists among Mainland Scandinavian languages (Danish, Swedish, Norwegian)
- Limited cross-lingual transfer between Mainland and Insular Scandinavian languages (Icelandic, Faroese)
- The investment in Scandinavian language technology has produced competitive specialized models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The benchmarking package ensures reproducible results by fixing random seeds and using bootstrapped test sets
- Mechanism: By setting a fixed random seed for each iteration and sampling with replacement from the test set, the evaluation captures both initialization noise and test set variability, leading to more reliable confidence intervals
- Core assumption: The bootstrapping process adequately represents the true distribution of test set performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "which can benchmark any model that has been uploaded to the Hugging Face Hub, with reproducible results"
  - [section]: "To ensure reproducibility, we enforce a learning rate of 2· 10−5 with 100 warmup steps... We use the AdamW optimiser... Further, random seeds are fixed throughout, to ensure reproducibility"
  - [corpus]: Weak - the corpus does not directly address reproducibility mechanisms
- Break condition: If the test set is too small or not representative, bootstrapping may not capture true performance variability

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Cross-lingual transfer is effective within Mainland Scandinavian languages but limited with Insular Scandinavian languages
- Mechanism: The F-statistic analysis measures between-group and within-group variance to quantify clustering quality, revealing that Mainland Scandinavian languages share more linguistic features enabling better transfer
- Core assumption: The chosen representative models and datasets adequately represent each language's linguistic properties
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The analysis shows that there is substantial cross-lingual transfer among the Mainland Scandinavian languages (Danish, Swedish and Norwegian), with limited cross-lingual transfer between the group of Mainland Scandinavian languages and the group of Insular Scandinavian languages (Icelandic and Faroese)"
  - [section]: "We introduce a way to measure the 'joint cross-lingual transfer' of a group of languages... we aim to find the best split of these languages into two groups"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus does not provide specific cross-lingual transfer evidence
- Break condition: If the Insular languages share more features with Mainland languages than assumed, the transfer limitation may be overstated

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: National language models outperform multilingual models for Scandinavian languages
- Mechanism: Investment in national language technology has produced specialized models that capture language-specific features better than generalized multilingual models
- Core assumption: The specialized training data and objectives for national models provide advantages over multilingual pretraining
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The benchmarking results also show that the investment in language technology in Norway, Sweden and Denmark has led to language models that outperform massively multilingual models such as XLM-RoBERTa and mDeBERTaV3"
  - [section]: "we see that the efforts of the National Libraries in Norway and Sweden, as well as the Danish Foundation Models project in Denmark, have paid off"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus does not discuss model performance comparisons
- Break condition: If multilingual models receive more diverse training data, they may eventually surpass specialized models

- Critical path: Each step must complete successfully for the system to function
- Design tradeoffs: Small training datasets (1,024 samples) enable faster benchmarking but may not fully capture model capabilities
- Failure signatures: Unusually high variance in benchmarking results may indicate issues with random seed handling or dataset quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal training data size for the ScandEval benchmark tasks that balances model differentiation and benchmarking efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors experimented with different training data sizes and found 1,024 samples to be a good balance, but this was based on qualitative assessment and specific datasets
- Why unresolved: The optimal size may vary by task type, language similarity, and model architecture. The current choice was based on limited experimentation and may not be universally optimal
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying training data size across all ScandEval tasks, measuring both model differentiation ability and benchmarking efficiency metrics like training time per epoch

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of ScandEval models compare when evaluated on real-world downstream applications versus the benchmark tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on benchmark performance but acknowledges that the ScandiQA dataset is based on translated data, suggesting potential domain mismatch
- Why unresolved: Benchmark datasets, especially those created through translation, may not reflect real-world Scandinavian language use and domain-specific requirements
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical evaluation of top ScandEval models on real-world Scandinavian NLP applications and domain-specific datasets across various industries

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the cross-lingual transfer potential between Insular and Mainland Scandinavian languages, and can joint training improve performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The cross-lingual transfer experiment showed limited transfer between these groups, but this was based on individual model training rather than joint training approaches
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested transfer through benchmarking models trained separately on each language, not exploring whether joint training or multilingual models could improve cross-group transfer
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with multilingual ScandEval models trained on combined Mainland and Insular Scandinavian datasets, comparing performance to monolingual models on both groups

## Limitations
- Small training dataset size (1,024 samples) may not fully capture model capabilities
- Limited cross-lingual transfer results may be overstated if Insular languages share more features with Mainland languages than assumed
- Current benchmarking conclusions based on specific model versions and training data that may evolve

## Confidence
- Reproducibility of methodology: **High**
- Exact replication of results: **Medium** (due to unknown preprocessing steps and complete model list)
- Cross-lingual transfer analysis: **Medium** (dependent on representative model assumptions)
- National vs multilingual model performance: **Medium** (based on current model versions)
