# A Survey On Few-shot Knowledge Graph Completion with Structural and Commonsense Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01172
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01172
- Reference count: 40
- This paper provides a comprehensive survey of few-shot knowledge graph completion (FKGC) methods, categorizing them into structural and semantic approaches.

## Executive Summary
This survey paper provides a comprehensive overview of few-shot knowledge graph completion methods, organizing approaches into structural and semantic categories. The paper covers meta-learning frameworks, attentive neighbor aggregation, and the use of pre-trained language models for incorporating textual information. It explores applications across various domains including recommendation systems, protein interaction prediction, and commonsense reasoning. While the paper serves as a valuable resource for understanding current FKGC methods, it does not provide specific quantitative results or empirical comparisons between different approaches.

## Method Summary
The survey categorizes FKGC methods into structural and semantic approaches. Structural methods enhance traditional knowledge graph embeddings with meta-learning frameworks, employing techniques like attentive neighbor aggregation and reinforcement learning for multi-hop reasoning. Semantic methods leverage pre-trained language models and prompts to incorporate textual information from entities and relations. The paper systematically reviews these approaches and their applications across different domains, providing a structured overview of the current state of FKGC research.

## Key Results
- Survey organizes FKGC methods into structural (meta-learning, neighbor aggregation) and semantic (LM prompts, text integration) categories
- Applications demonstrated across recommendation systems, protein interaction prediction, and commonsense reasoning
- Future research directions identified include multi-modal knowledge integration and improved zero-shot learning capabilities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The survey's effectiveness stems from its comprehensive categorization of FKGC methods, which helps readers understand the landscape of current research. By distinguishing between structural approaches that focus on graph topology and semantic approaches that leverage textual information, the paper provides a clear framework for understanding different methodological directions. The organization allows readers to identify which approaches might be most suitable for their specific use cases and understand the relationships between different techniques.

## Foundational Learning

**Knowledge Graph Embeddings**: Vector representations of entities and relations in a graph that capture structural information. Why needed: Fundamental building blocks for most FKGC methods. Quick check: Can you explain how TransE or DistMult work?

**Meta-Learning**: Learning to learn from few examples by adapting to new tasks quickly. Why needed: Enables models to generalize from limited training data in FKGC. Quick check: What's the difference between MAML and metric-based meta-learning?

**Attentive Neighbor Aggregation**: Weighting neighboring nodes based on their relevance to the target node. Why needed: Improves structural reasoning by focusing on relevant information. Quick check: How does attention differ from simple averaging in neighbor aggregation?

**Prompt Engineering**: Designing input formats to leverage pre-trained language models for specific tasks. Why needed: Enables semantic approaches to incorporate textual knowledge effectively. Quick check: What's the difference between manual and automatic prompt engineering?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Entity Embeddings -> Meta-Learning Framework -> Knowledge Graph Completion Model (Structural)
                   Entity/Relation Text -> Pre-trained LM -> Prompt-based Completion Model (Semantic)

**Critical Path**: Input (Few-shot triples) -> Embedding Generation -> Model-specific Processing (Meta-learning or LM prompts) -> Score Prediction -> Output (Completed KG)

**Design Tradeoffs**: Structural methods trade computational efficiency for direct graph structure learning, while semantic methods trade domain specificity for rich textual information incorporation.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance on long-tail relations, inability to generalize to unseen entity types, overfitting to training distribution, or failure to incorporate commonsense knowledge effectively.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several future research directions, including integrating multi-modal knowledge sources (text, images, tables) and improving zero-shot learning capabilities for entities and relations not seen during training. However, it does not call out specific open questions for investigation.
