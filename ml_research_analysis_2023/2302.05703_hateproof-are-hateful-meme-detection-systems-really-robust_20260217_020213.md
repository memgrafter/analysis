# HateProof: Are Hateful Meme Detection Systems really Robust?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05703
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05703
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art hateful meme detection models are highly vulnerable to adversarial attacks, with performance drops up to 10% in macro-F1 score.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the vulnerability of multi-modal hateful meme detection systems to adversarial attacks. The authors propose nine different attacks that can be easily integrated with existing memes while maintaining message perception. These attacks target both individual and combined image-text modalities. The research demonstrates that current state-of-the-art hate meme detection models are significantly susceptible to these attacks. To address this vulnerability, the authors propose two countermeasures based on contrastive learning and adversarial training, with an ensemble approach showing the most promising results in improving robustness against various attack types.

## Method Summary
The authors systematically evaluate the robustness of hateful meme detection systems by applying nine different adversarial attacks to three benchmark datasets (Fbhm, HarMeme, and Mami). They test three state-of-the-art models: Uniter, VisualBERT, and Rob+Resnet. To counter these attacks, they implement two countermeasures: a contrastive learning approach (based on SimCLR) and an adversarial training method (VILLA). The ensemble of these two approaches is also evaluated. The performance is measured using macro-F1 score for hate detection across different attack scenarios.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art hateful meme detection models are highly vulnerable to adversarial attacks, with up to 10% drop in macro-F1 performance
- Contrastive learning and adversarial training countermeasures individually improve robustness but have limitations against certain attack types
- Ensemble of contrastive learning and adversarial training countermeasures provides the best overall performance in improving robustness
- The Mami dataset's poor image resolution affects the effectiveness of countermeasures for certain attacks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contrastive learning improves robustness by learning invariant representations across augmented views.
- Mechanism: The model generates synthetic augmentations of each training sample and learns to maximize agreement between these augmented views while minimizing agreement between different samples. This creates more robust embeddings that are less sensitive to small perturbations.
- Core assumption: Small augmentations preserve the class label and semantic meaning while creating challenging but realistic variations.
- Evidence anchors: The authors implement a SimCLR-based contrastive learning approach and observe improved robustness across multiple attack types, though the improvement varies by attack type and dataset.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adversarial training with Gaussian noise perturbations improves robustness by exposing the model to adversarial examples during training.
- Mechanism: During training, small Gaussian noise is added to the embedding space of either the image or text modality while keeping the other modality unchanged. This creates adversarial examples that help the model learn to be robust to small perturbations.
- Core assumption: Gaussian noise perturbations are a realistic approximation of the types of attacks that might be encountered in practice.
- Evidence anchors: The VILLA-based adversarial training approach shows improved robustness to several attack types, though its effectiveness varies across different attacks and datasets.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble methods combine the strengths of contrastive learning and adversarial training to achieve better robustness than either approach alone.
- Mechanism: The model takes the average of the prediction probabilities from both the contrastive learning-based model and the adversarial training-based model, combining their complementary strengths.
- Core assumption: The two approaches learn different aspects of robustness and their combination provides better overall performance.
- Evidence anchors: The ensemble approach consistently outperforms individual countermeasures across multiple datasets and attack types, particularly for attacks where individual approaches show limited effectiveness.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input meme -> OCR extraction -> Feature extraction (image and text) -> Hate meme detection model (Uniter/VisualBERT/Rob+Resnet) -> Contrastive learning module (generates augmented samples and computes contrastive loss) -> Adversarial training module (VILLA, generates adversarial examples) -> Ensemble module (averages prediction probabilities)

- Critical path: The critical path is the data flow from input meme through OCR extraction, feature extraction (image and text), model prediction, and finally ensemble of countermeasure predictions. The most time-consuming steps are likely the feature extraction and model inference, especially for large models like Uniter.

- Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoff is between robustness and accuracy on clean data. The countermeasures may improve robustness to adversarial attacks but could slightly decrease performance on clean data. Another tradeoff is computational cost - the countermeasures require additional training and inference time.

- Failure signatures: The system may fail if the OCR extraction fails to accurately extract text from the meme, if the feature extraction misses important regions of the image, or if the model is unable to learn robust representations that generalize across different types of attacks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific architectural improvements in multimodal hate meme detection systems would make them more resistant to adversarial attacks like Newsprint and Add+SaltPepper-I?
- Basis in paper: The authors note that none of the countermeasures worked for these specific attacks and suggest this as a future direction.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that even ensemble methods fail against these attacks, indicating that current architectures have fundamental limitations that need addressing.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing new model architectures or fusion mechanisms that show significantly improved robustness against these specific attacks, demonstrated through rigorous experimentation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of training data (e.g., resolution, diversity) affect the robustness of hate meme detection models to adversarial attacks?
- Basis in paper: The authors observe that countermeasures fail for the Mami dataset due to poor image resolution, suggesting data quality impacts robustness.
- Why unresolved: While the paper hints at this relationship, it doesn't systematically explore how different aspects of data quality (resolution, diversity, noise levels) affect model robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying data quality parameters and measuring corresponding changes in model robustness to different attack types.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between the number of adversarial examples used during training and the resulting model performance and computational cost?
- Basis in paper: The authors observe that adding up to 5 adversarial examples improves performance, but adding more deteriorates it, and they call for finding this balance.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests a limited range of adversarial examples and doesn't explore the trade-off between performance gains, computational cost, and potential overfitting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of adversarial examples across a wider range, measuring both performance improvements and computational costs, to identify the optimal point.

## Limitations
- The study focuses primarily on white-box attacks with known model architectures, which may not reflect real-world adversarial scenarios where attackers have limited knowledge of the detection system.
- The effectiveness of the proposed countermeasures is evaluated on benchmark datasets, but their performance on real-world, dynamically evolving hate content remains unproven.
- The ensemble approach shows promising results, but the study does not provide insights into which attacks are most effectively mitigated by each component of the ensemble.

## Confidence

- **High**: The observation that state-of-the-art hateful meme detection models are vulnerable to simple adversarial attacks is well-supported by the empirical results across multiple datasets and attack types.
- **Medium**: The effectiveness of the individual countermeasures (contrastive learning and adversarial training) in improving robustness is demonstrated, but the extent of improvement varies across different attack types and datasets.
- **Low**: The superiority of the ensemble approach over the individual countermeasures is claimed, but the study does not provide a detailed analysis of why this combination works better or under what conditions it may fail.
