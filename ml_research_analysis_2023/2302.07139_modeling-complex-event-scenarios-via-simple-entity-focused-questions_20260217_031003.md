# Modeling Complex Event Scenarios via Simple Entity-focused Questions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07139
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07139
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Question-guided event language models generate more diverse event sequences with better entity role control compared to standard approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a question-guided generation framework for event language models that models events as answers to simple entity-focused questions. The method converts event sequences into (Context, Question, Answer) training tuples, enabling models to generate multiple valid next events depending on the question asked. The approach addresses the challenge of controlling event language models to generate diverse and controllable sequences of events for complex scenarios, which is crucial for applications like intelligence analysis.

The key insight is that by conditioning generation on questions about specific entities (what else did X do?, what else happened to X?, what else happened?), the model can explore different paths in a scenario based on user-specified entities of interest. This provides a natural interface for interactive exploration of complex event scenarios while maintaining the ability to generate coherent event sequences.

## Method Summary
The method involves training event language models using question-guided generation, where events are modeled as answers to entity-focused questions. The approach converts standard event sequences into (Context, Question, Answer) triples by identifying entities in each event, generating role-based questions about those entities, and using subsequent events involving those entities as answers. Three models are trained: ELM (standard event language model), EGELM (entity-guided model), and QGELM (question-guided model). The models are fine-tuned on T5-base using these training triples, with QGELM learning to generate events conditioned on both context and questions about specific entities.

## Key Results
- Question-guided model generates more diverse sequences (lower Self-BLEU scores) compared to standard event language models
- Better control for entity roles with fewer failures and less sampling needed compared to entity-guided approaches
- Maintains comparable perplexity and cloze accuracy to standard event language models while improving interactive schema generation quality
- User studies show better quality schemas with more accepted events, fewer resampling steps, and longer exploration paths

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Question-guided training converts standard event sequences into (Context, Question, Answer) triples, enabling models to generate multiple valid next events depending on the question asked.
- Mechanism: For each entity in an event, the framework creates role-based questions and uses subsequent events involving that entity as answers, creating diverse training instances from the same context.
- Core assumption: Subsequent events in a sequence can serve as valid answers to questions about entities in prior events.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The method converts event sequences into (Context, Question, Answer) training tuples"; [section 3.2] "we take a partial sequence of events as context and derive a role-based question involving an entity for which a future event in the sequence can be an answer"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Conditioning on questions rather than just entities provides finer-grained control over generated events, leading to better coverage of scenario participants.
- Mechanism: The model learns P(e|context, question) where the question specifies both the entity and its role (agent/theme), allowing generation of events that specifically involve the entity in that role.
- Core assumption: The question's surface form (what did X do vs what happened to X) is sufficient to control the role of X in the generated event.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "models events in complex scenarios as answers to questions about participants"; [section 5.2] "QGELM fares even better outperforming all methods by a significant margin" when controlling for specific entity roles

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Question-guided training acts as a form of data augmentation, improving model performance on standard event sequence generation tasks.
- Mechanism: By training to generate events that may not be adjacent to the current context (i.e., skipping intermediate events), the model learns more flexible generation patterns that transfer to standard sequence modeling.
- Core assumption: Training with non-adjacent events as answers improves the model's ability to generate plausible next events in standard settings.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.2] "the model also learns to generate events that are not always adjacent to the end of the current sequence, which can be seen as a form of data augmentation"; [section 5.3] "QGELM achieves the lowest perplexity in these settings"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Coreference resolution
  - Why needed here: To identify all mentions of the same entity across different events in a sequence
  - Quick check question: Can you identify all mentions of "John" in a paragraph where he's referred to as "he" and "the suspect"?

- Concept: Dependency parsing
  - Why needed here: To determine the grammatical role (subject/object) of entities in events for creating appropriate questions
  - Quick check question: In the sentence "The police arrested the suspect," can you identify which noun is the subject and which is the object?

- Concept: OpenIE tuple extraction
  - Why needed here: To convert natural language events into structured (predicate, arguments) format for modeling
  - Quick check question: Can you extract the predicate and arguments from "The suspect escaped from the prison"?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: OpenIE extraction -> Coreference resolution -> (Context, Question, Answer) triple generation -> T5-base fine-tuning

- Critical path: 1. Extract OpenIE tuples from document; 2. Identify coreference clusters; 3. For each event and its entities, generate questions based on roles; 4. Find subsequent events involving those entities as answers; 5. Create training triples and fine-tune T5 model

- Design tradeoffs: Granularity of roles (subject/object vs full semantic role labeling); Question types (three fixed templates vs more diverse formulations); Data augmentation (including non-adjacent events vs only adjacent events)

- Failure signatures: Low diversity in generated sequences (high Self-BLEU); Model fails to generate events with specified entities/roles; Poor perplexity/accuracy on standard event sequence prediction

- First 3 experiments: 1. Verify data processing creates expected number of (Context, Question, Answer) triples from sample documents; 2. Compare diversity (Self-BLEU) of ELM vs QGELM on sequences of length 5; 3. Test controllability by measuring % of generated events containing a specified entity for both models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would using a more fine-grained semantic role labeling system (beyond just subject/object) affect the diversity and controllability of the generated event sequences?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that they use simple dependency-based heuristics to determine entity roles (subject for agentive, object for non-agentive), but note this is a limitation.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses two broad categories of roles, which may be too coarse-grained to capture the full complexity of event scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Running experiments with a more sophisticated semantic role labeling system (e.g., identifying agent, patient, theme, manner, etc.) and comparing the diversity and controllability metrics to the current approach.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the question-guided generation approach compare to using control codes derived from automatically generated latent representations of the input?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The related work section mentions that learning latent representations or codes from the input has been explored for diverse generation in other domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores using simple role-based questions as control codes, not learned latent representations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training an event language model using control codes derived from learned latent representations and comparing its performance to the question-guided model on diversity, controllability, and quality metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would the question-guided approach perform on generating events for domains outside of the intelligence analysis community?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The evaluation focuses on domains relevant to the intelligence analysis community, but the approach is not limited to these domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not evaluate the approach on a diverse set of domains to assess its generalizability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the question-guided model on event generation tasks in domains such as healthcare, finance, or sports, and comparing its performance to the ELM and other baselines.

## Limitations

- The paper assumes question surface forms sufficiently encode entity roles, but the model may be learning lexical patterns rather than semantic understanding
- Reliance on OpenIE extraction and coreference resolution systems may introduce noise into training data
- The approach has input length constraints that limit performance on longer event sequences with many participants

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The method's ability to improve diversity in generated sequences (lower Self-BLEU scores) and the general framework of converting event sequences into (Context, Question, Answer) training tuples are well-supported by the results presented.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that question-guided training acts as data augmentation improving standard event sequence generation is plausible but not definitively proven, as the comparison is limited to perplexity and cloze accuracy metrics.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that the method better controls for entity roles is supported by the user study results, but the exact mechanism (role understanding vs. pattern matching) remains uncertain.

## Next Checks

1. **Role understanding test**: Design an experiment where questions use novel entities not seen during training to verify if the model truly understands entity roles or has merely memorized patterns from the training data.

2. **Noise injection analysis**: Systematically introduce errors in coreference resolution and OpenIE extraction to measure the impact on model performance, quantifying the sensitivity of the method to data quality.

3. **Long sequence evaluation**: Test the model's performance on scenarios requiring longer event sequences (beyond T5's input limit) by evaluating on progressively longer contexts and measuring when performance degrades.
