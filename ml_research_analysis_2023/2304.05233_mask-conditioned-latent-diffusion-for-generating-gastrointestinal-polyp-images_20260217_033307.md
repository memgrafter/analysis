# Mask-conditioned latent diffusion for generating gastrointestinal polyp images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05233
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05233
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Generated synthetic GI polyp images improve segmentation performance when combined with real data, achieving micro-imagewise IOU of 0.7751 with DeepLabv3+

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a conditional diffusion probabilistic model framework to generate synthetic gastrointestinal polyp images and corresponding segmentation masks, addressing the challenge of limited annotated medical data. The method uses an improved diffusion model to generate realistic synthetic polyp masks, which are then used as conditions for a latent diffusion model to synthesize high-fidelity polyp images. The generated data was evaluated by training three segmentation models (UNet++, FPN, and DeepLabv3+) on combinations of real and synthetic data, demonstrating that synthetic data can improve segmentation performance depending on the model architecture used.

## Method Summary
The proposed method employs a two-stage pipeline: first, an improved diffusion model generates synthetic polyp masks by reversing noise added to real mask images. These synthetic masks are then used as conditioning inputs for a latent diffusion model, which generates corresponding polyp images in latent space. The pipeline leverages the Kvasir-SEG and HyperKvasir datasets for training. Segmentation models (UNet++, FPN, and DeepLabv3+) are trained on combinations of real and synthetic data, with performance evaluated using micro-imagewise IOU, F1 score, accuracy, and precision metrics.

## Key Results
- Best micro-imagewise IOU of 0.7751 achieved using DeepLabv3+ with mixed real and synthetic data
- Synthetic data improves segmentation performance for some architectures (DeepLabv3+) but not others (FPN, UNet++ perform best with only real data)
- Generated synthetic masks evaluated with similarity measure (SIM) metric, showing realistic diversity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Improved diffusion models generate diverse, realistic synthetic polyp masks by gradually reversing noise added to real mask images.
- Mechanism: The improved diffusion model starts with a real polyp mask and iteratively adds noise over many steps. Training teaches the model to reverse this process, producing synthetic masks that match the distribution of real ones.
- Core assumption: The underlying data distribution of real polyp masks is learnable and can be captured by the denoising process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The study uses "an improved diffusion model to generate realistic synthetic polyp masks" and validates with "similarity measure (SIM) metric."
  - [section] "The improved diffusion model generates synthetic mask images by first adding noise to a randomly selected mask image from the training set. This noise would then be gradually reversed through multiple steps until a synthetic mask image is generated."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. Related work on Polyp-DDPM also uses diffusion-based methods, but no direct similarity metrics reported.
- Break condition: If the mask dataset is too small or lacks variability, the model may overfit and generate only minor variations of training samples.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Latent diffusion models conditioned on synthetic masks can generate high-fidelity polyp images that preserve the mask structure.
- Mechanism: The latent diffusion model takes a synthetic mask as a condition and learns to generate corresponding polyp images. By operating in latent space, it produces more coherent images while preserving fine details from the mask.
- Core assumption: There exists a stable mapping between mask shape and the visual appearance of the corresponding polyp region in real images.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "high-fidelity synthetic polyp images conditioned on pre-generated synthetic polyp masks using a conditional latent diffusion model."
  - [section] "Latent diffusion models are a suitable choice for generating synthetic images of the GI tract...capable of generating a large diversity of high-quality synthetic images."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus; related papers focus on text-guided augmentation or GAN-based synthesis.
- Break condition: If the conditioning signal is weak or the model overfits to the training distribution, generated images may ignore the mask structure.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining real and synthetic data during segmentation model training improves performance more than real data alone, depending on model architecture.
- Mechanism: Synthetic data expands the training distribution, reducing overfitting and improving generalization. Different architectures (UNet++, FPN, DeepLabv3+) respond differently based on their inductive biases and capacity.
- Core assumption: The synthetic data distribution is close enough to real data that it helps the model learn robust features without introducing harmful artifacts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the best micro-imagewise IOU of 0.7751 was achieved from DeepLabv3+ when the training data consists of both real data and synthetic data."
  - [section] "adding synthetic data can improve the results of segmentation models...However, it is not always true because some models like FPN and UNet++ show the best IOU, F1, and accuracy when the training data consists of only the real data."
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence; this is the main experimental finding of the paper.
- Break condition: If synthetic images contain unrealistic artifacts or the ratio of synthetic to real data is too high, performance may degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Improved diffusion model (mask generator) -> Latent diffusion model (image generator) -> Segmentation models (evaluation) -> Evaluation metrics (FID, SIM, IOU, F1, Accuracy, Precision)

- Critical path:
  1. Train improved diffusion model on real polyp masks
  2. Generate synthetic masks
  3. Train latent diffusion model conditioned on synthetic masks
  4. Generate synthetic polyp images
  5. Train segmentation models on combinations of real and synthetic data
  6. Evaluate segmentation performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using latent space diffusion vs. pixel-space diffusion (speed vs. control)
  - Number of synthetic samples vs. training time and potential overfitting
  - Choice of segmentation architecture based on dataset size and complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - Low FID/SIM scores for masks indicate poor mask quality
  - Generated images that ignore mask shape indicate conditioning failure
  - Segmentation models performing worse with synthetic data indicate distribution mismatch

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the improved diffusion model and evaluate mask FID/SIM on held-out real masks
  2. Generate synthetic images conditioned on real masks and visually inspect for structure preservation
  3. Train a simple segmentation model (e.g., UNet++) on only synthetic data and measure baseline IOU

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal balance between real and synthetic training data for maximizing segmentation performance across different model architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows varying performance with different ratios of real to synthetic data (e.g., 700 real + 1000 synthetic vs. 100 real + 200 synthetic), with DeepLabv3+ performing best with maximum synthetic data, while FPN and UNet++ showed best results with only real data
- Why unresolved: The study tested limited combinations (fixed at 100 real images with increasing synthetic data, and 700 real images) and concluded that the optimal ratio depends on model architecture, but did not exhaustively explore all possible combinations
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the ratio of real to synthetic data across multiple model architectures, identifying the specific sweet spot for each architecture type

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do generated synthetic polyps conditioned on the same mask compare in terms of diversity and realism when using different noise inputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions conditioning the latent diffusion model on generated masks and producing multiple samples, showing this in Figure 5, but does not provide quantitative analysis of the diversity between these samples
- Why unresolved: While qualitative inspection was performed, the paper lacks quantitative metrics to assess the variability and realism of multiple generations from the same mask
- What evidence would resolve it: Quantitative metrics comparing diversity between multiple generations from the same mask (e.g., diversity scores, perceptual studies) and statistical analysis of how noise inputs affect the final output

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the improvement in segmentation performance from synthetic data generalize across different polyp datasets and clinical settings?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study uses Kvasir-SEG and HyperKvasir datasets but does not test cross-dataset generalization or evaluate performance in different clinical contexts
- Why unresolved: The experiments were conducted within a single dataset ecosystem without testing how synthetic data trained on one dataset performs on others, or how well it generalizes to different imaging conditions
- What evidence would resolve it: Cross-dataset evaluation experiments showing segmentation performance when models trained on synthetic data from one dataset are tested on completely different polyp datasets, and clinical validation studies across multiple institutions

## Limitations
- Unknown training hyperparameters (batch size, learning rate, epochs) and random seed settings limit reproducibility
- Performance improvements depend on model architecture but reasons for architectural sensitivity are not fully explored
- No clinical validation of synthetic images to assess potential for misleading diagnosis

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The diffusion model pipeline for generating synthetic masks and images is well-established and the reported segmentation improvements are methodologically sound.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that synthetic data improves segmentation performance is supported by experimental results, but the dependence on model architecture requires further investigation.
- **Low confidence**: The paper does not address potential biases in synthetic data generation or provide clinical validation, limiting the practical applicability of the approach.
