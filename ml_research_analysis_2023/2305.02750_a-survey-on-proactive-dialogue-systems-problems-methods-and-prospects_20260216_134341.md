# A Survey on Proactive Dialogue Systems: Problems, Methods, and Prospects

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02750
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02750
- Reference count: 1
- This paper provides the first comprehensive survey of proactive dialogue systems, categorizing them into open-domain, task-oriented, and information-seeking dialogues

## Executive Summary
This survey provides the first comprehensive overview of proactive dialogue systems, which enable conversational agents to lead conversations toward achieving predefined targets or goals. The paper categorizes these systems into three main types: open-domain dialogues (target-guided and prosocial), task-oriented dialogues (non-collaborative and enriched), and information-seeking dialogues (asking clarification questions and user preference elicitation). For each category, the survey summarizes recent advances in problem formulations, methods, datasets, and evaluation protocols. The work identifies key challenges including handling hybrid dialogues, developing robust evaluation protocols, and addressing ethical considerations around privacy and manipulation.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes research across multiple proactive dialogue system categories, organizing findings around problem formulations, methods, datasets, and evaluation protocols. For each problem type, it identifies relevant datasets and evaluation metrics, then reviews methodological approaches including topic-shift detection, topic planning, response generation, and strategy learning. The methods vary by problem type but generally involve pre-trained language models fine-tuned for specific proactive behaviors, reinforcement learning for optimizing dialogue strategies, and knowledge graph reasoning for coherent topic transitions. The survey also discusses evaluation challenges and ethical considerations specific to proactive systems.

## Key Results
- Proactive dialogue systems can lead conversations toward achieving predefined targets while maintaining naturalness and coherence
- Non-collaborative dialogues require strategic reasoning beyond standard intent detection to handle scenarios where users and systems have different goals
- Proactive information-seeking systems can reduce ambiguity through clarification questions, improving search efficiency in conversational search scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Proactive dialogue systems achieve higher user engagement by leading conversations toward predefined targets.
- Mechanism: The system actively shifts conversation topics or introduces new information based on context and goals, rather than passively responding to user input.
- Core assumption: Users find goal-oriented conversations more satisfying and efficient than purely reactive exchanges.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Proactive dialogue systems... equip the conversational agent with the capability of leading the conversation direction towards achieving pre-defined targets or fulfilling certain goals from the system side."
  - [section] "Instead of making consistent responses to the user-oriented topics, the dialogue system is required to proactively lead the conversation topics with either a designated target topic or a pre-defined topic thread."
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers discussing proactive dialogue systems, indicating active research interest and validation of the approach.
- Break condition: If user resistance to topic shifts becomes too high or if the system cannot maintain natural conversational flow, engagement may decrease.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Non-collaborative dialogues require strategic reasoning beyond standard intent detection.
- Mechanism: The system predicts and adapts to user strategies, modeling personality and interests to steer conversations toward mutual agreement.
- Core assumption: Understanding user decision-making processes enables more effective negotiation and persuasion.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Rather than the focus on task completion, there are increasing demands for proactive TOD systems that can either (i) handle non-collaborative tasks where users and systems do not share the same goal, such as negotiation and persuasion dialogues."
  - [section] "He et al. [2018] aim to control the dialogue strategy to achieve different negotiation goals with the same language generator by decoupling strategy and generation."
  - [corpus] Related work on negotiation and persuasion dialogues supports the need for strategic reasoning in non-collaborative settings.
- Break condition: If strategy prediction accuracy drops below a threshold, the system may fail to reach agreements or may appear manipulative.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Proactive information-seeking systems reduce ambiguity and improve search efficiency through clarification questions.
- Mechanism: The system detects when user queries are ambiguous and generates clarification questions to narrow down information needs.
- Core assumption: User queries in conversational search are often too brief for accurate retrieval without additional context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The goal of conversational information-seeking (CIS) systems is to fulfill the user's information needs... Recent years have witnessed several advances on developing proactive CIS systems that can further eliminate the uncertainty for more efficient and precise information seeks by initiating a subdialogue."
  - [section] "Asking clarification questions aims to clarify the potential ambiguity in the user query, since the user query is often succinct and brief in real-world conversational search and question answering."
  - [corpus] Related work on clarification questions in conversational search indicates this is an active research area.
- Break condition: If clarification questions are perceived as annoying or interruptive, user satisfaction may decrease despite improved search accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: User input → Intent/Strategy Classifier → Topic Planner/Strategy Learner → Response Generator → Output. Additional components include Knowledge Graph Interface, User Preference Model, and Evaluation Module.
- Critical path: User utterance → Context understanding → Proactive decision (topic shift/strategy/clarification) → Response generation → User feedback
- Design tradeoffs: Balance between proactivity and user autonomy; between task completion and natural conversation flow; between system initiative and user engagement
- Failure signatures: User disengagement, failed goal completion, unnatural conversation flow, repetitive responses, incorrect topic transitions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can proactive dialogue systems effectively handle hybrid dialogues that incorporate multiple conversational goals within a single conversation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly identifies this as a key challenge in Section 5, noting that current systems assume users have clear single goals, while real-world applications often involve multiple and various goals.
- Why unresolved: This remains unresolved because most current systems are designed for single-goal dialogues, and developing systems that can seamlessly transition between different types of dialogues while maintaining overall quality is complex.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementations of hybrid dialogue systems that can naturally transition between different dialogue types (e.g., from chit-chat to task-oriented to information-seeking) while maintaining user engagement and achieving all goals would resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective evaluation protocols for measuring proactivity in dialogue systems, considering the multidisciplinary nature of this assessment?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 5 discusses the challenges of evaluating proactivity, noting that it involves perspectives from natural language, human-computer interaction, sociology, and psychology.
- Why unresolved: This remains unresolved because current evaluation methods rely heavily on human evaluation or user simulators, which are either expensive or lack the nuance to fully capture proactivity across multiple disciplines.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of comprehensive evaluation frameworks that can effectively measure proactivity across linguistic, social, and psychological dimensions would resolve this question.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can proactive dialogue systems balance the benefits of agent initiative with ethical concerns such as privacy, factuality, and potential aggressiveness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 5 discusses ethical concerns, including factuality, morality, and privacy, specifically noting that proactivity raises more concerns about misusing personal information.
- Why unresolved: This remains unresolved because current systems focus on technical capabilities without sufficient consideration of ethical implications, particularly in non-collaborative dialogues where aggressiveness could be an issue.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstrated frameworks or systems that successfully incorporate ethical guardrails while maintaining proactivity would resolve this question.

## Limitations

- The survey does not address how proactive dialogue systems perform across different cultural contexts or with users having varying communication styles
- The effectiveness of proactive systems in maintaining long-term user engagement versus short-term task completion remains unclear
- The survey acknowledges but does not deeply explore the ethical implications of systems that can manipulate conversation direction

## Confidence

- **Target-guided dialogues**: High confidence - well-studied with established datasets and multiple evaluation methods
- **Prosocial dialogues**: Medium confidence - evaluation protocols less standardized, heavy reliance on human evaluation
- **Non-collaborative dialogues**: Medium confidence - complex evaluation involving persuasion and negotiation success
- **Information-seeking dialogues**: Low confidence - most evaluations rely on simulated environments rather than live user interactions

## Next Checks

1. **Evaluate cross-cultural generalization**: Test proactive dialogue systems across different cultural contexts to assess whether topic-shifting and strategic reasoning behaviors are perceived similarly across diverse user populations.

2. **Longitudinal engagement study**: Conduct a study measuring user engagement and satisfaction with proactive systems over extended interaction periods (multiple sessions over weeks/months) rather than single-session evaluations.

3. **User autonomy impact assessment**: Design experiments to quantify the tradeoff between system proactivity and user sense of control, measuring when proactive behaviors enhance versus diminish user experience.
