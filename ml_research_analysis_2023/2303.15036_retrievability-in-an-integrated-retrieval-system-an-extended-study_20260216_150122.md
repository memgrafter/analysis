# Retrievability in an Integrated Retrieval System: An Extended Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15036
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15036
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: Datasets show significantly higher retrievability bias than publications and variables in GESIS Search, with popularity bias affecting retrieval across document types.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates retrievability patterns across document types (datasets, publications, variables) in the GESIS Search digital library. The research analyzes 92,975 user queries and 5,579 export interactions from Q3 2022, revealing significant differences in how likely different document types are to be retrieved. Datasets exhibit notably higher retrievability bias compared to publications and variables, suggesting certain datasets are disproportionately accessible. The study demonstrates that popularity bias in user queries significantly impacts retrievability, particularly for datasets and publications, while variables show more uniform retrieval patterns.

## Method Summary
The research employs a quantitative analysis of user interactions within the GESIS Search system, focusing on export actions as a proxy for document usefulness. The study examines query-document relationships to measure retrievability - the likelihood of a document being retrieved by the system. Analysis spans three months of user activity, tracking which document types appear in search results and which are subsequently exported by users. The methodology calculates retrievability bias scores for different document types and analyzes correlations between popularity bias in queries and retrieval outcomes.

## Key Results
- Datasets exhibit significantly higher retrievability bias than publications and variables, indicating unequal access patterns
- Variables show more uniform retrievability and usefulness distributions across the corpus
- Popularity bias in user queries has substantial impact on retrievability, particularly for datasets and publications

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The retrievability patterns emerge from the interaction between user query popularity distribution and the integrated search system's ranking algorithms. When certain datasets become disproportionately associated with popular queries, the system's retrieval mechanism amplifies their visibility while marginalizing less popular content. This creates a feedback loop where popular datasets become increasingly retrievable while others become effectively invisible to users.

**Component Map**: User Queries -> Search Engine -> Document Corpus (Datasets, Publications, Variables) -> Retrieval Results -> Export Actions

**Critical Path**: Query Input → Ranking Algorithm → Result Set Generation → User Export Decision → Usefulness Measurement

**Design Tradeoffs**: The integrated approach balances cross-type discovery against potential bias amplification, prioritizing comprehensive search over specialized optimization for individual document types.

**Failure Signatures**: High retrievability bias indicates systematic exclusion of certain content; low export rates despite high retrieval suggest relevance matching problems.

## Limitations
- Findings are specific to GESIS Search system and may not generalize to other platforms
- Three-month observation period may not capture seasonal variations in user behavior
- Corpus composition changes during analysis period not accounted for
- Export actions as usefulness proxy may not fully represent user satisfaction

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: Findings about retrievability bias differences between document types are robust and well-supported by statistical patterns.

**Medium Confidence**: Conclusions about popularity bias effects are reasonable but require longer-term validation.

**Low Confidence**: Recommendations for system improvements are speculative without empirical validation.

## Next Checks
