# Survey of Trustworthy AI: A Meta Decision of AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00380
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00380
- Reference count: 0

## Executive Summary
This survey presents a comprehensive taxonomy for Trustworthy AI (TAI) organized around three domains—articulate, authentic, and basic trust—and ten measurable dimensions such as explainability, fairness, privacy, and robustness. Using PRISMA methodology combined with text-mining, the study systematically surveys over 1000 papers and evaluates 33 implementation models and 106 schemes/tools. The framework maps high-level ethical objectives to measurable dimensions through Marr's levels of analysis, enabling practical deployment of TAI in strategic decision contexts while identifying key paradoxes across ethics, machine learning, and decision-making domains.

## Method Summary
The survey employs PRISMA methodology combined with text-mining techniques in R to screen over 1000 literature sources, focusing on more than 100 relevant papers. The approach uses an inductive method to identify key themes and classifications, then applies a deductive method to organize these findings into a hierarchical taxonomy. The framework consists of three trust domains and ten measurable dimensions, with each paper evaluated for its method, contribution, and limitations across these dimensions.

## Key Results
- Comprehensive taxonomy of TAI based on three domains and ten measurable dimensions

- Identification of paradoxes across ethics, machine learning, and strategic decision-making domains

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Taxonomy Engine -> PRISMA+Text-Mining Pipeline -> Paradox Repository -> Evaluation Module

- Critical path:
  1. Define TAI objectives and select relevant dimensions.
  2. Generate domain vectors and select implementation models/schemes.
  3. Use PRISMA+text-mining to survey literature for each dimension.
  4. Identify and analyze paradoxes affecting TAI objectives.
  5. Synthesize findings into actionable guidance.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Depth vs. breadth: Focusing on fewer dimensions in detail vs. covering more dimensions superficially.
  - Rigor vs. scalability: Using strict PRISMA criteria vs. broader text-mining to include more literature.
  - Generality vs. specificity: Creating a universal taxonomy vs. tailoring it to specific domains (e.g., healthcare, finance).

- Failure signatures:
  - Mismatch between selected dimensions and actual TAI challenges.
  - Over-reliance on literature from a single domain, missing cross-cutting issues.
  - Failure to update taxonomy as new TAI technologies and paradoxes emerge.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply the taxonomy to a simple TAI case (e.g., loan approval) and assess its coverage.
  2. Run the PRISMA+text-mining pipeline on a small, controlled set of papers to validate filtering criteria.
  3. Use the paradox repository to analyze a known TAI dilemma (e.g., transparency vs. privacy) and test if the framework captures the tension.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a practical framework to measure and compare the trustworthiness of different AI systems across diverse applications and contexts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper emphasizes the need for a comprehensive framework to assess Trustworthy AI (TAI) but acknowledges the challenges in quantifying and comparing different TAI techniques and their outcomes.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies various dimensions and metrics for TAI but does not provide a unified method for aggregating and comparing these metrics across different AI systems and applications. The complexity of TAI and the diverse contexts in which it is applied make it difficult to establish a universal standard for measurement.
- What evidence would resolve it: A validated framework that integrates multiple TAI dimensions and provides a standardized method for evaluating and comparing the trustworthiness of AI systems across various applications and contexts.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we address the trade-off between the explainability of AI systems and their performance, particularly in high-stakes decision-making scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the paradox of explainability, where increased transparency can lead to reduced performance, and highlights the challenges in balancing these two aspects of TAI.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the dilemma but does not offer a concrete solution for optimizing both explainability and performance simultaneously. The complexity of AI models and the need for high performance in critical applications make it difficult to achieve both goals effectively.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating effective methods for improving explainability without significantly compromising performance, along with real-world applications where this balance has been successfully achieved.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we ensure the long-term sustainability of AI systems, considering their environmental, economic, and social impacts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the importance of sustainability in TAI but acknowledges the challenges in measuring and mitigating the environmental, economic, and social impacts of AI systems over time.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the need for sustainability but does not provide a comprehensive approach for assessing and addressing the long-term impacts of AI systems. The complexity of sustainability and the diverse factors involved make it difficult to develop a unified strategy.
- What evidence would resolve it: A framework that integrates environmental, economic, and social metrics for AI systems and demonstrates effective strategies for minimizing negative impacts and promoting long-term sustainability.

## Limitations

- The PRISMA + text-mining methodology may exclude emerging TAI approaches not captured by current terminology.
- The framework's alignment between ethical objectives and measurable dimensions assumes trust can be decomposed into standardized components, which may not hold across all contexts or cultures.
- Limited evidence of overlap with related literature suggests potential gaps in coverage of existing TAI research.

## Confidence

- High: The three-domain, ten-dimension taxonomy structure and its alignment with Marr's levels of analysis.
- Medium: The systematic review methodology and literature coverage (based on the large corpus size).
- Low: The identification and analysis of paradoxes across ethics, ML, and strategic decision-making due to limited evidence of overlap with related literature.
