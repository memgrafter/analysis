# Detection of Uncertainty in Exceedance of Threshold (DUET): An Adversarial Patch Localizer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10291
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10291
- Reference count: 28
- This work introduces DUET, an uncertainty-based adversarial patch localizer that identifies and localizes adversarial patches on images.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DUET, an adversarial patch detection method leveraging Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) to quantify prediction uncertainty. DUET identifies and localizes adversarial patches by evaluating whether model confidence exceeds a threshold, flagging uncertain predictions as potential adversarial areas. The method is evaluated on flower, traffic sign, and food datasets under various adversarial attacks, demonstrating superior mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) performance compared to deterministic baselines.

## Method Summary
The method employs Bayesian neural networks with variational inference to approximate posterior distributions over model weights, enabling uncertainty-aware predictions. A DeeplabV3+ segmentation architecture with ResNet-101 backbone is used, with Bayesian layers optionally added to encoder or decoder blocks. The DUET algorithm applies Monte Carlo sampling to generate probability distributions per pixel, calibrates these via Platt scaling, and flags pixels as adversarial if confidence intervals fall below a threshold. Models are trained for 50 epochs using Adam optimizer.

## Key Results
- DUET models achieve higher mIoU on adversarial patch detection compared to deterministic baselines (e.g., 0.921 vs 0.868 on PGD-attacked flower images).
- Bayesian layers in early encoder blocks improve adversarial patch localization, while those in later blocks or decoder enhance clean image segmentation and generalization.
- Two well-performing configurations are proposed: encoder-1110-bayes for top localization accuracy and decoder-only-bayes for balanced performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bayesian neural networks improve generalization by modeling uncertainty in weight space, allowing the model to handle out-of-distribution adversarial patches better than deterministic models.
- Mechanism: Variational inference approximates the posterior distribution over weights, replacing point estimates with probability distributions, which inherently capture epistemic uncertainty and enable uncertainty-aware predictions.
- Core assumption: The posterior over weights can be well-approximated by a tractable variational distribution (e.g., isotropic Gaussian) without losing essential information for localization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Bayesian neural networks (BNN) [6] to provide us with uncertainty estimates of adversarial pixels"
  - [section] "Bayesian neural network (BNN) is a natural choice for the localization of adversarial patches of varied attacks due to its ability to detect out-of-distribution (OOD) samples [17]."
- Break condition: If the variational posterior cannot adequately represent the true posterior (e.g., highly multimodal), the uncertainty estimates will be overconfident or incorrect, reducing robustness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The DUET algorithm uses calibrated confidence intervals to decide if a pixel's classification is trustworthy, reducing false negatives by flagging uncertain predictions as adversarial.
- Mechanism: MC sampling from the variational posterior generates a distribution of probability estimates per pixel; these are calibrated via Platt scaling to correct for underestimation, then compared against a threshold to decide trustworthiness.
- Core assumption: The calibration set (correctly classified non-Bayes outputs) is representative of the true probability space and that calibration improves calibration across all regions of the input space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We quantify our prediction uncertainties with the development of Detection of Uncertainties in the Exceedance of Threshold (DUET) algorithm."
  - [section] "The DUET algorithm attempts to quantify uncertainty by ensuring that the allowable confidence interval (i.e., 90%) of the probability estimates must fall below the classification threshold to be deemed as non-adversarial."
- Break condition: If calibration is poor (e.g., mismatched or biased calibration set), confidence intervals will be misleading, causing either excessive false positives or missed detections.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Placing Bayesian layers in early encoder blocks yields better adversarial patch localization, while placing them in later blocks or decoder improves clean image segmentation and stability.
- Mechanism: Bayesian layers in early blocks capture high-level uncertainty about spatial and semantic features relevant to adversarial patch shape, whereas later or decoder layers focus on precise localization and fine-grained details.
- Core assumption: The feature hierarchy in ResNet-101 is such that early layers capture global, high-level uncertainty and later layers capture local, precise uncertainty; Bayesian uncertainty at different depths complements these roles.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we found that isometric gaussian priors in Bayesian Neural Networks are suitable for patch localization tasks and the presence of Bayesian layers in the earlier neural network blocks facilitates top-end localization performance, while Bayesian layers added in the later neural network blocks contribute to better model generalization."
  - [section] "the addition of bayesian layers to the first two blocks seems to facilitate better top-end performance, achieving much higher predictive scores on detecting adversarial patches. In contrast, the presence of bayesian layers in the last few blocks in the encoder seems to facilitate better stability in clean image prediction performance."
- Break condition: If the assumption about feature hierarchy is wrong (e.g., for different architectures), Bayesian layers at those positions may degrade performance or add unnecessary noise.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Variational inference and evidence lower bound (ELBO) for approximate Bayesian inference.

  - Why needed here: Ensures the confidence intervals derived from Bayesian models are meaningful and not systematically overconfident.
  - Quick check question: Why might raw Bayesian output probabilities underestimate true confidence, and how does Platt scaling help?

  - Why needed here: Primary evaluation metric for patch localization performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → (Bayesian encoder blocks) → ASPP → (Bayesian decoder blocks) → Output logits → DUET calibration → Binary mask
- Critical path: Input → (Bayesian encoder blocks) → ASPP → (Bayesian decoder blocks) → Output logits → DUET calibration → Binary mask
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More Bayesian blocks → better uncertainty but slower inference and potential convergence issues
  - Calibration set choice → impacts confidence reliability; must be representative
  - Prior selection (isotropic Gaussian vs Cauchy vs GMM) → affects model flexibility and performance; Gaussian often best balance
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor calibration → overconfident or underconfident detections
  - Excessive Bayesian layers → slow convergence, unstable training
  - Wrong Bayesian block placement → poor localization or generalization
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replace last encoder block with Bayesian layer, train, evaluate IoU on PGD test set vs baseline.
  2. Swap Gaussian prior to Cauchy prior, retrain, compare mIoU and generalization to Google Patch.
  3. Implement DUET calibration on a trained Bayesian model, measure confidence reliability on clean vs attacked images.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the optimal configurations of Bayesian layers within a segmentation model to balance both adversarial patch localization accuracy and clean image segmentation performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors conclude that no DUET model achieves both top-end adversarial patch localization and near-perfect clean image segmentation concurrently, suggesting a potential trade-off that requires further investigation.
- Why unresolved: The study shows that increasing the number of Bayesian layers improves adversarial detection but degrades clean image performance, and vice versa, indicating a need to find a balance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing various Bayesian layer configurations on both adversarial and clean image datasets to identify models that achieve optimal performance in both tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of prior distribution (e.g., Cauchy vs. Gaussian) affect the performance and generalization of Bayesian neural networks in adversarial patch detection?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compare Cauchy and Gaussian priors, finding that Gaussian priors perform better on average across different datasets and attacks, but the reasons for this are not fully explored.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the superiority of Gaussian priors, it does not delve into the theoretical or empirical reasons behind this observation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed ablation studies and theoretical analysis of how different priors influence the posterior distribution and model performance in adversarial settings.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of applying Bayesian neural networks to the decoder section of a segmentation model versus the encoder section in terms of inference latency and detection accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that decoder-only Bayesian models have lower inference latency and better clean image segmentation performance compared to encoder Bayesian models, but further investigation is needed.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests a trade-off between latency and performance but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how Bayesian layers in different model sections affect these metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies measuring inference time and detection accuracy for models with Bayesian layers in the encoder, decoder, and both sections across various datasets and attack types.

## Limitations
- The core assumption that variational inference with a simple isotropic Gaussian prior adequately captures the posterior over weights for adversarial patch localization remains unverified.
- The DUET algorithm's calibration step relies heavily on the quality and representativeness of the calibration set, which is not fully described in the paper.
- The paper lacks ablation studies showing performance drop when BNN is removed or when calibration is disabled.

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (BNN improves generalization): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (DUET calibration ensures trustworthy predictions): Low
- Mechanism 3 (Bayesian layer placement improves performance): Medium
