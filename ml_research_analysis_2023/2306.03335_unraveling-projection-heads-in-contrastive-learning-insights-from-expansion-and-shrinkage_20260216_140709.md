# Unraveling Projection Heads in Contrastive Learning: Insights from Expansion and Shrinkage

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.03335
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03335
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** This paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why (linear) projectors impact the downstream performance of learned representations, identifying expansion and shrinkage effects induced by contrastive loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the role of projection heads in contrastive learning, addressing the puzzling observation that representations learned before projectors often outperform those learned after, even when projectors are linear. Through both empirical and theoretical analysis, the authors identify two crucial effects—expansion and shrinkage—induced by the contrastive loss on the projectors. They demonstrate that the contrastive loss either expands or shrinks the signal direction in the representations learned by an encoder, depending on factors such as augmentation strength and temperature. The authors propose a family of linear transformations to accurately model the projector's behavior and precisely characterize the downstream linear classification accuracy in the high-dimensional asymptotic limit.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes linear projection heads in contrastive learning by first fixing a well-trained encoder and then training a linear projector under the contrastive loss. The authors use a modified SimCLR loss and vary temperature and augmentation strength parameters to observe their effects on the projector. They calculate singular values and cumulative alignment scores of the projector matrices, then evaluate downstream accuracy using both features and embeddings. The theoretical framework is built on Gaussian mixture models and high-dimensional asymptotic analysis, with empirical validation using the STL-10 dataset.

## Key Results
- Linear projectors operating in the shrinkage regime hinder downstream classification accuracy, while those in the expansion regime improve it
- The contrastive loss induces simultaneous expansion and shrinkage effects when the signal direction and augmentation direction are not perfectly aligned
- The expansion/shrinkage phenomenon is characterized by the projector's singular values and their alignment with the signal direction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The contrastive loss either expands or shrinks the signal direction in the representations learned by the encoder.
- **Mechanism:** The projector (linear transformation) either amplifies or attenuates the signal direction μ based on the temperature τ and augmentation strength σ²_aug. When in the expansion regime, the projector stretches the signal direction, leading to better alignment of normalized features on the hypersphere. In the shrinkage regime, the projector compresses the signal direction, increasing uniformity of normalized features.
- **Core assumption:** The encoder has already learned well-separated clusters (Gaussian mixture model) that can be linearly separated.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "contrastive loss either expands or shrinks the signal direction in the representations learned by an encoder"
  - [section 4] "in the expansion regime, contrastive loss leads to a linear projector W* that maximally expands along the signal direction μ"
- **Break condition:** If the encoder does not produce well-separated clusters, the expansion/shrinkage effect on the projector may not manifest as expected.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Even linear projectors affect downstream linear classification accuracy through their impact on the inductive bias.
- **Mechanism:** The projector reparametrizes the feature space, effectively changing the norm associated with the max-margin classifier. This alters the implicit regularization induced by gradient descent on linearly separable data, thereby changing the generalization error.
- **Core assumption:** In the high-dimensional regime, solving logistic regression on linearly separable data via gradient descent converges to the max-margin classifier.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "linear projectors operating in the shrinkage (or expansion) regime hinder (or improve) the downstream classification accuracy"
  - [section 5.4] "the test accuracy increases with the expansion strength η" and "reparametrization due to expansion or shrinkage affects generalization in high dimensions"
- **Break condition:** If the data is not linearly separable or if a different classifier (e.g., with strong explicit regularization) is used, the effect of the projector on inductive bias may be negligible.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The simultaneous expansion and shrinkage phenomenon occurs when the signal direction μ and spike direction v_aug are not perfectly aligned.
- **Mechanism:** Inhomogeneous augmentations (with a spiked covariance matrix) create two competing effects: expansion along the signal direction μ and shrinkage along the augmentation direction v_aug. The balance between these effects depends on the temperature τ and the cosine similarity between μ and v_aug.
- **Core assumption:** Augmentations introduce feature perturbations with a spiked covariance structure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "simultaneous expansion and shrinkage, which aligns with our empirical discoveries using the STL-10 dataset"
  - [section 6] "the signal μ and spike v_aug exhibit different levels of expansion/shrinkage at the same temperature parameter"
- **Break condition:** If the augmentation does not introduce a spiked covariance structure or if μ and v_aug are perfectly aligned, only one of expansion or shrinkage may dominate.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for feature modeling
  - Why needed here: The paper assumes the encoder outputs features that form well-separated clusters, modeled as a GMM, to decouple the analysis of the projector from the encoder dynamics.
  - Quick check question: If features are generated from a 2-component GMM with means ±μ and covariance I, what is the form of the population loss Ln(W) for a linear projector W?

- **Concept:** Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) and its geometric interpretation
  - Why needed here: The paper uses SVD to analyze how the projector transforms the signal direction, with expansion corresponding to large singular values in the signal direction and shrinkage corresponding to small singular values.
  - Quick check question: Given a projector W with SVD W = UΣV^T, how is the expansion measure T(W) = ||Wμ||²/(||W||²_F||μ||²) related to the alignment of μ with the right singular vectors of W?

- **Concept:** Implicit regularization and max-margin classifier in overparameterized models
  - Why needed here: The paper explains how the projector changes the norm in the max-margin optimization problem, thereby altering the inductive bias and downstream generalization.
  - Quick check question: In a linearly separable high-dimensional dataset, what solution does gradient descent on logistic loss converge to, and how does this relate to the implicit regularization?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Encoder -> Projector -> Loss -> Downstream Classifier
- **Critical path:**
  1. Fix a well-trained encoder producing well-separated clusters
  2. Train the projector W under the contrastive loss with varying τ and augmentation strength
  3. Analyze the SVD of W to determine expansion/shrinkage regime
  4. Evaluate downstream accuracy using features vs. embeddings
  5. Relate expansion/shrinkage to changes in max-margin classifier's inductive bias

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Using a linear projector vs. nonlinear projector: Linear projectors are easier to analyze but may be less expressive; nonlinear projectors can capture more complex transformations but are harder to interpret.
  - Expansion vs. shrinkage regime: Expansion generally improves downstream accuracy but may lead to dimensional collapse; shrinkage may preserve feature space dimensionality but harms accuracy.
  - Temperature τ: Higher τ promotes expansion but may reduce the effective rank of features; lower τ promotes shrinkage and may increase uniformity.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Dimensional collapse in the feature space (many zero singular values in W)
  - Downstream accuracy using embeddings is worse than using features (indicating harmful shrinkage)
  - No clear phase transition in expansion/shrinkage measure as τ varies (suggesting the encoder does not produce well-separated clusters)

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Fix a pretrained encoder, train a linear projector W under SimCLR loss with varying τ (e.g., 0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0), and plot the expansion measure T(W) vs. τ to observe the phase transition.
  2. For each τ, compute the downstream accuracy using embeddings z = Wh and features h, and verify that accuracy improves in the expansion regime and degrades in the shrinkage regime.
  3. Repeat experiment 1 with inhomogeneous augmentations (spiked covariance) and plot the cumulative alignment scores for both the signal direction μ and spike direction v_aug to observe simultaneous expansion/shrinkage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise mechanism by which the projector induces expansion or shrinkage of the signal direction in the representations learned by the encoder?
- Basis in paper: Explicit. The paper identifies two crucial effects—expansion and shrinkage—induced by the contrastive loss on the projectors, but does not provide a detailed explanation of the underlying mechanism.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the empirical and theoretical analysis of the expansion and shrinkage effects, but does not delve into the specific mechanisms that cause these effects.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further theoretical analysis or experiments that isolate and measure the contributions of different components of the contrastive loss (e.g., alignment loss, uniformity loss) to the expansion and shrinkage effects.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the strength of augmentation affect the expansion and shrinkage regimes, and how can this be quantified?
- Basis in paper: Explicit. The paper mentions that the augmentation strength is a factor influencing the expansion and shrinkage effects, but does not provide a quantitative relationship between augmentation strength and the regimes.
- Why unresolved: The paper's analysis focuses on the temperature parameter and its impact on the expansion and shrinkage regimes, but does not extensively explore the role of augmentation strength.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that systematically vary the augmentation strength while keeping other parameters constant, and measure the resulting expansion and shrinkage effects. Additionally, theoretical analysis that incorporates augmentation strength into the existing models.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the expansion and shrinkage phenomenon manifest in non-linear projectors, and how does it affect downstream performance?
- Basis in paper: Inferred. The paper primarily focuses on linear projectors, but mentions that the observed phenomenon of representations learned before projectors outperforming those learned after is even true when the projectors themselves are linear.
- Why unresolved: The paper's analysis and experiments are limited to linear projectors, and the implications for non-linear projectors are not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that train and evaluate models with non-linear projectors (e.g., multi-layer perceptrons) and compare their downstream performance to models with linear projectors. Additionally, theoretical analysis that extends the current models to incorporate non-linear transformations.

## Limitations
- The theoretical analysis relies heavily on high-dimensional asymptotic regime and Gaussian mixture model assumptions
- The simultaneous expansion and shrinkage phenomenon requires further validation across diverse datasets and architectures
- The analysis focuses primarily on linear projectors, leaving the behavior of nonlinear projection heads less understood

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The observation that features learned before projectors outperform those after, even with linear projectors, is well-established through extensive empirical evidence
- **Medium Confidence:** The expansion/shrinkage mechanism and its relationship to temperature and augmentation strength is theoretically sound and empirically supported
- **Medium Confidence:** The explanation of how expansion/shrinkage affects downstream accuracy through changes in inductive bias is compelling but could benefit from additional validation

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-architecture validation:** Test the expansion/shrinkage predictions across different encoder architectures (e.g., Vision Transformers, EfficientNet) to verify if the phenomenon generalizes beyond ResNets.

2. **Non-linear projector analysis:** Extend the theoretical framework to analyze nonlinear projectors (e.g., 2-layer MLPs) and compare their expansion/shrinkage behavior with linear counterparts.

3. **Real-world robustness:** Evaluate the impact of expansion/shrinkage on downstream tasks under distribution shift and varying data conditions to assess practical implications beyond the controlled experimental settings.
