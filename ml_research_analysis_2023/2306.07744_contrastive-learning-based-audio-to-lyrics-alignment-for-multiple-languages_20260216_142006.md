# Contrastive Learning-Based Audio to Lyrics Alignment for Multiple Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07744
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07744
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: End-to-end audio-to-lyrics alignment using contrastive learning achieves <0.2s average absolute error across multiple languages without requiring aligned annotations.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel end-to-end approach for automatic audio-to-lyrics alignment using contrastive learning. Unlike prior methods based on CTC or speech recognition toolkits, the proposed system maps audio and lyrics into a shared embedding space and uses contrastive learning to align them without requiring precise annotations. A text encoder with contextual embeddings allows robust performance across languages, and a line-based decoding step improves alignment accuracy. Evaluated on the JamendoLyrics Multi-Lang dataset, the system achieves an average absolute error below 0.2 seconds, outperforming previous methods while remaining simple and language-agnostic. The approach also generalizes well to German, French, and Spanish with minimal adaptation.

## Method Summary
The system processes 5-second audio segments through a residual CNN encoder to produce phonetic embeddings, while a context-aware text encoder processes lyrics (characters or phonemes) with surrounding context. Contrastive learning aligns these embeddings by pushing matching audio-symbol pairs together and non-matching pairs apart. Line-based decoding refines alignments by constraining tokens to estimated lyrical line intervals. The model is trained on weak supervision (lyrics without timestamps) and evaluated on multiple languages, achieving superior performance to CTC-based baselines while remaining language-agnostic.

## Key Results
- Achieves average absolute error below 0.2 seconds across multiple languages
- Outperforms CTC-based methods while requiring no aligned annotations
- Generalizes effectively to German, French, and Spanish with minimal adaptation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contrastive learning enables robust audio-lyrics alignment without requiring aligned labels
- Mechanism: The model learns to map audio and text embeddings into a shared space where positive pairs (matching audio and lyrics) are pushed together and negative pairs (non-matching audio and lyrics) are pushed apart. This is achieved by sampling positive tokens from the current lyrics and negative tokens from other songs in the dataset.
- Core assumption: The temporal structure of lyrics is sufficient for alignment when embedded in a shared space with audio, even without explicit alignment annotations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "uses contrastive learning to align them without requiring precise annotations"
  - [section 2.3] "we apply contrastive learning, where a positive example s+ is taken from the lyrics for a given audio segment and negative examples s− are sampled from the distribution ps over symbols obtained from all lyrics in the dataset that do not appear in the audio segment."
  - [corpus] Weak - only 1 of 5 related papers mentions contrastive learning explicitly
- Break condition: If the negative sampling strategy fails to provide informative contrastive examples, the alignment performance will degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Contextual text embeddings significantly improve alignment accuracy compared to character-level or phoneme-level processing without context
- Mechanism: The text encoder processes subsequences of symbols (characters or phonemes) with surrounding context, allowing the model to learn pronunciation variations based on neighboring symbols. This creates a data-driven phoneme representation that outperforms handcrafted phoneme dictionaries.
- Core assumption: Pronunciation of a symbol depends on its neighboring symbols, and this dependency can be learned from data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2] "we extend the text encoder to process the subsequence (sn−C , sn−C+1, . . . , sn, . . . , sn+C ) for each symbol sn"
  - [section 3.2] "We see that processing multiple (M5) rather than single (M3) phonemes leads to significantly stronger results... This effect is even stronger for characters, where processing characters independently (M4) leads to poor performance, and using multiple characters (M6) yields an AAE of 0.15s"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of contextual embeddings in related papers
- Break condition: If the context window is too small or too large, the model may fail to capture relevant pronunciation dependencies or introduce noise.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Line-based decoding with estimated line intervals improves alignment accuracy by constraining tokens to their respective lyrical lines
- Mechanism: After initial alignment, the system estimates line intervals based on token positions and durations, then applies a line-mask to constrain subsequent alignment to keep tokens from the same line close together in time.
- Core assumption: Lyrical lines are temporally clustered in audio, and this temporal structure can be exploited to improve alignment accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.4] "we exploit that lyrics are temporally clustered and constrain the model to output all words from the same line close to each other"
  - [section 3.2] "When we skip the line-based decoding (M7) of the best character model, we observe a significantly worse AAE"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of line-based decoding in related papers
- Break condition: If the estimated line durations are inaccurate, the line-mask may incorrectly constrain tokens, degrading alignment quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Audio features → Audio encoder → Text encoder → Contrastive loss → Line-based decoding → Final alignment
- Critical path: Audio features → Audio encoder → Text encoder → Contrastive loss → Line-based decoding → Final alignment
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Character vs phoneme representation: Characters with context work better than phonemes due to data-driven pronunciation modeling
  - Context window size: Small windows (C=1) work well; larger windows may introduce noise
  - Embedding dimension: E=64 balances performance and model size
  - Negative sampling rate: 1000 negatives per positive provides good contrastive signal
- Failure signatures:
  - High AAE but good PCO: Model finds approximate alignment but struggles with precise timing
  - Poor multilingual performance: Model overfits to English pronunciation patterns
  - Degraded performance with line-based decoding: Line interval estimation is inaccurate
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare character-only vs character-with-context vs phoneme-with-context models on English data
  2. Test different context window sizes (C=0, 1, 2, 3) to find optimal balance
  3. Evaluate multilingual performance with and without language conditioning layer

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model perform on low-resource languages that are not in the Latin script, such as Chinese or Arabic?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors discuss the potential for extending the approach to low-resource languages but only evaluate on Latin-script languages.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide data or experiments for non-Latin scripts, and the impact of script differences on the text encoder's performance is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on non-Latin languages, comparisons of character vs phoneme representations, and ablation studies isolating the effect of script on performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the contrastive learning framework be extended to handle overlapping vocal parts, such as duets or ad-libs, without significant changes to the decoding stage?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention this as a potential direction but do not explore it in the experiments.
- Why unresolved: The current decoding assumes a single vocal line per time frame; overlapping lyrics violate this assumption and may require modifications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Results from training and evaluating on audio with overlapping vocals, and a comparison of decoding strategies for such cases.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the effect of increasing the context window size (C) beyond the small values tested, especially for longer-range dependencies in lyrics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that long-range dependencies are not crucial for symbol sound estimation and use small C values.
- Why unresolved: There is no empirical evidence showing how larger context windows would affect performance or robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying C, analyzing alignment accuracy and model generalization with larger context windows.

## Limitations
- Absence of quantitative comparisons to state-of-the-art systems on standardized metrics
- Limited evaluation on non-Latin script languages and highly polyphonic music
- Reliance on weak supervision may not generalize to scenarios requiring exact timestamp alignment

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core mechanism of contrastive learning for audio-text alignment is well-established and the experimental results showing superior performance over CTC-based methods are convincing
- Medium confidence: The multilingual generalization claims are supported by results on three additional languages, but the sample sizes and language diversity are limited
- Low confidence: The claim that this is the first end-to-end system for audio-to-lyrics alignment without requiring aligned labels is difficult to verify given the limited corpus evidence

## Next Checks
1. Cross-linguistic robustness test: Evaluate the system on a diverse set of languages with varying phonetic and orthographic systems to verify the claimed language-agnostic performance
2. Noise and complexity stress test: Test the system on music with varying levels of background instrumentation, polyphony, and vocal styles to determine the limits of the audio encoder's ability to extract relevant phonetic features
3. Temporal precision evaluation: Conduct a detailed analysis of alignment errors at different time scales and correlate error patterns with specific musical features to understand when and why the line-based decoding fails
