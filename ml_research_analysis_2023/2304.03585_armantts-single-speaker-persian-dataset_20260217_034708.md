# ArmanTTS single-speaker Persian dataset

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03585
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03585
- Reference count: 33
- Key outcome: Single-speaker Persian TTS dataset (9 hours) with MOS scores of 4.0 (real), 3.87 (vocoder), 2.98 (TTS model)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ArmanTTS, a single-speaker Persian dataset for text-to-speech synthesis containing 9 hours of studio-recorded speech at 22.05 kHz. The dataset was created to address the lack of Persian TTS resources and enable research in Persian speech synthesis. The authors evaluated the dataset by training a TTS model combining Tacotron 2 and HiFi-GAN, achieving MOS scores that demonstrate the dataset's viability for Persian TTS research, though larger datasets are needed for improved performance.

## Method Summary
The authors created ArmanTTS by recording 9 hours of Persian speech from a single speaker in a studio environment at 22.05 kHz sampling rate with approximately 25 dB SNR. They trained a TTS model using Tacotron 2 to convert phonemes to mel-spectrograms and HiFi-GAN to convert spectrograms to waveforms. Evaluation was conducted using Mean Opinion Score (MOS) with 16 raters scoring 30 audio samples across three conditions: real speech, vocoder-only, and full TTS synthesis.

## Key Results
- MOS of 4.0 for real speech, 3.87 for vocoder prediction, and 2.98 for TTS model output
- Dataset contains 8449 samples with 9 hours of studio-quality Persian speech
- Average signal-to-noise ratio of 25 dB in recordings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ArmanTTS enables Persian TTS by providing clean, single-speaker dataset with studio-quality recordings at 22.05 kHz.
- Mechanism: Deep learning TTS models require paired text-utterance data. ArmanTTS supplies 9 hours of aligned phoneme-to-speech samples, allowing models like Tacotron 2 + HiFi-GAN to learn mapping from Persian phonemes to waveforms.
- Core assumption: The dataset's noise-free recordings and phoneme alignment are accurate enough for supervised model training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ArmanTTS, a single-speaker Persian dataset for text-to-speech (TTS) synthesis. The dataset contains 9 hours of studio-recorded speech at a 22.05 kHz sample rate."
  - [section] "The speech production of this dataset was done in a studio environment with a sampling rate of 22.05 kHz. The average signal to noise ratio is 25 dB."
  - [corpus] Weak: corpus shows neighboring TTS works but no direct verification of ArmanTTS signal quality or phoneme accuracy.
- Break condition: If recordings contain high noise or phoneme-to-text alignment errors, model training will fail to converge or produce intelligible speech.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Tacotron 2 + HiFi-GAN pipeline achieves reasonable MOS by decomposing TTS into two stages: acoustic feature generation and waveform synthesis.
- Mechanism: Tacotron 2 maps phonemes to mel-spectrograms (acoustic model), HiFi-GAN converts those spectrograms to waveforms (vocoder). This two-stage approach isolates modeling complexity and allows each component to specialize.
- Core assumption: Acoustic features (spectrograms) contain sufficient information for HiFi-GAN to reconstruct natural waveforms.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also combined the Tacotron 2 and HiFi GAN to design a model that can receive phonemes as input, with the output being the corresponding speech."
  - [section] "To convert phonemes to acoustic features, the Tacatron 2 [9] model is used and to convert acoustic features to waveforms, HiFi -GAN[3] is used."
  - [corpus] Weak: corpus shows similar two-stage pipelines but no MOS scores for ArmanTTS-trained models.
- Break condition: If Tacotron 2 poorly estimates spectrograms, HiFi-GAN cannot recover natural speech, dropping MOS sharply.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: MOS evaluation validates model performance relative to real speech, with scores of 4.0 (real), 3.87 (vocoder-only), and 2.98 (full TTS) indicating ArmanTTS supports trainable TTS models.
- Mechanism: Human raters score audio naturalness; lower MOS for TTS than real speech signals model gap, but >2.5 MOS shows basic intelligibility. MOS differences also reveal component-level quality (vocoder better than full TTS).
- Core assumption: MOS scores are reliable indicators of TTS naturalness and intelligibility.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "4.0 value of MOS was obtained from real speech, 3.87 value was obtained by the vocoder prediction and 2.98 value was reached with the synthetic speech generated by the TTS model."
  - [section] "We used MOS(mean opinion score) metrics for the evaluation. MOS is a metric used for evaluation of ground-truth audio, TTS models and vocoders. We evaluated 30 samples which results can be seen in Table II."
  - [corpus] Weak: corpus shows no MOS data for neighboring works, so cannot confirm ArmanTTS MOS is comparable to Persian TTS standards.
- Break condition: If raters' criteria are inconsistent or dataset too small, MOS may not reflect true model capability.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Phoneme-to-speech alignment
  - Why needed here: TTS model learns mapping from phoneme sequences to acoustic frames; misalignment corrupts training.
  - Quick check question: Given the Persian sentence "دو ساله که با هم صحبت نکردهاند", can you produce the correct phoneme sequence used in ArmanTTS?

  - Why needed here: Tacotron 2 outputs mel-spectrograms, which HiFi-GAN converts to waveforms; understanding their structure helps debug model outputs.
  - Quick check question: What frequency range does a 22.05 kHz sample rate support in mel-spectrograms?

  - Why needed here: MOS quantifies naturalness of synthesized speech; knowing scoring protocol is essential for interpreting model quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text → Phoneme → Tacotron 2 → Mel-spectrogram → HiFi-GAN → Waveform → Audio
- Critical path: Data → Tacotron 2 → HiFi-GAN → Audio → MOS
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-speaker limits generalization but reduces variability.
  - 22.05 kHz balances quality and model complexity vs. higher rates.
  - Two-stage pipeline isolates failures but adds training overhead.
- Failure signatures:
  - Tacotron 2 outputs noisy spectrograms → HiFi-GAN produces garbled audio.
  - Phoneme errors in dataset → nonsensical speech.
  - MOS drops >1 point → possible misalignment or overfitting.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train Tacotron 2 on ArmanTTS, generate spectrograms, inspect for artifacts.
  2. Train HiFi-GAN on ground-truth spectrograms, generate waveforms, compare to originals.
  3. End-to-end pipeline, evaluate MOS on 10 samples, verify MOS >2.5 for intelligibility.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the TTS model performance improve if the ArmanTTS dataset was expanded to match the duration and speaker diversity of larger datasets like LibriTTS or HiFi-TTS?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "we need larger amount of data to cover the problem domain appropriately to reach better performance" and compares ArmanTTS's 9-hour single-speaker dataset to larger multi-speaker datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates the current 9-hour single-speaker dataset and does not explore how scaling the dataset would affect performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating TTS models on progressively larger versions of ArmanTTS (e.g., 20 hours, 50 hours, 100+ hours) and comparing MOS scores against the current results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of ArmanTTS compare to Persian-text-to-speech when using the same TTS model architecture?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces ArmanTTS as a new Persian dataset while Persian-text-to-speech is currently the only available Persian dataset, but no direct comparison between them is provided.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares ArmanTTS to English and German datasets in terms of characteristics, not in terms of TTS model performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training identical TTS models on both ArmanTTS and Persian-text-to-speech datasets and comparing their MOS scores using the same evaluation methodology.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would incorporating text normalization techniques improve the TTS model performance on ArmanTTS, despite the current model using phonemes as input?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "In the ArmanTTS dataset, the input to the model is phonemes. Therefore, there is no process to normalize the text," but does not explore whether normalization could still be beneficial.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates the current phoneme-based approach without investigating alternative preprocessing methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training TTS models on ArmanTTS using both the current phoneme approach and a normalized text approach (with phoneme conversion as part of the model), then comparing MOS scores.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the signal-to-noise ratio distribution in ArmanTTS affect the overall TTS model quality compared to datasets with higher SNR values?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper reports an average SNR of 25dB for ArmanTTS and shows the SNR histogram, but does not analyze its impact on TTS quality.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides SNR statistics, it does not correlate these with the MOS scores or explore how improving SNR might affect results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Creating cleaner versions of ArmanTTS by re-recording or enhancing audio, then training TTS models on both versions to measure performance differences.

## Limitations
- Single-speaker constraint limits generalization to diverse Persian speech
- 9-hour dataset size is modest compared to larger TTS datasets (10-100+ hours)
- No comparative evaluation against existing Persian TTS approaches
