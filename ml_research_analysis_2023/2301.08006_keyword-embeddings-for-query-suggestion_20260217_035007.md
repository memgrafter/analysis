# Keyword Embeddings for Query Suggestion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08006
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08006
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Keywords2Vec and FastKeywords achieved MAP@20 scores of up to 0.8659 and MRR@100 scores of up to 0.8988 on benchmark datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces novel architectures for generating keyword embeddings that outperform traditional word and sentence embedding models in keyword suggestion tasks. The proposed Keywords2Vec and FastKeywords models adapt Word2Vec and FastText architectures to learn dense representations of keywords based on their co-occurrence patterns in documents. A key innovation is a negative sampling strategy based on connected components in keyword co-occurrence graphs, which improves the quality of learned embeddings for suggesting semantically related keywords.

## Method Summary
The paper presents Keywords2Vec and FastKeywords as adaptations of Word2Vec and FastText architectures, respectively, trained on keyword co-occurrence data rather than word sequences. Keywords2Vec uses a skip-gram model where keywords serve as both context and target, while FastKeywords extends this with sub-keyword information. The novel negative sampling approach identifies negative samples from the same connected component in the keyword co-occurrence graph as the positive sample, improving semantic relevance of suggestions. Both models are trained on document-keyword associations from benchmark datasets and evaluated on tasks including document keyword identification and masked keyword discovery.

## Key Results
- Keywords2Vec achieved MAP@20 of 0.8659 on Inspec dataset for document keyword identification
- FastKeywords achieved MRR@100 of 0.8988 on KP20k dataset for masked keyword discovery
- Both models significantly outperformed state-of-the-art word and sentence embedding baselines across all evaluation metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success stems from directly modeling keyword relationships rather than intermediate word representations. By training on keyword co-occurrence rather than word sequences, the models capture semantic relationships between keywords more directly. The connected component-based negative sampling ensures that negative examples are semantically similar but not identical, creating more informative training signals than random negative sampling.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Documents -> Keyword Extraction -> Co-occurrence Graph -> Connected Components -> Keywords2Vec/FastKeywords -> Keyword Embeddings

**Critical Path:** Keyword co-occurrence graph construction → connected component analysis → negative sampling → embedding training → inference for suggestion

**Design Tradeoffs:** Direct keyword modeling vs word-level modeling (better semantic capture vs larger vocabulary); connected component negative sampling vs random sampling (more relevant negatives vs simpler implementation)

**Failure Signatures:** Poor keyword suggestion quality when keyword vocabulary is sparse; degraded performance on domains with highly specialized terminology; failure to capture cross-domain keyword relationships
