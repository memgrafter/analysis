# ImageCaptioner$^2$: Image Captioner for Image Captioning Bias Amplification Assessment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04874
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04874
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: ImageCaptioner2 outperforms LIC in human alignment (80% vs 54%) and consistency (97% vs 92%) for bias amplification assessment

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ImageCaptioner2, a novel metric for measuring bias amplification in image captioning models. Unlike existing methods that use language classifiers, ImageCaptioner2 leverages the visiolinguistic nature of image captioning by using an image captioner itself to assess bias. The core innovation is measuring bias amplification (the relative increase from data to model bias) rather than absolute bias, providing more actionable insights for fairness evaluation. The metric demonstrates superior human alignment and consistency compared to recent bias metrics like LIC across multiple datasets and protected attributes.

## Method Summary
ImageCaptioner2 measures bias amplification by comparing model bias (Bm) to ground truth dataset bias (Bd) through a prompt-based image captioning approach. The method involves text preprocessing to mask protected-attribute indicative words and append prompt templates, then uses the captioning model to predict the last word (protected attribute) while measuring confidence as the bias score. The bias amplification is calculated as Bamp = Bm - Bd. The metric was evaluated across 11 different image captioning architectures on three datasets (MS-COCO, Artemis V1, Artemis V2) and three protected attributes (gender, race, emotions).

## Key Results
- ImageCaptioner2 achieves 80% correlation with human evaluation vs 54% for LIC on bias amplification assessment
- The metric demonstrates 97% consistency across classifier variations vs 92% for LIC
- Superior performance in capturing visiolinguistic bias compared to language-only classifiers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: ImageCaptioner2 measures bias amplification by comparing model bias to data bias rather than measuring absolute bias
- **Mechanism**: Calculates bias amplification as Bamp = Bm - Bd, quantifying how much the model amplifies existing bias
- **Core assumption**: Model bias estimation through prompt-based evaluation is reliable and comparable to data bias
- **Break condition**: If prompt-based bias estimation is unreliable or introduces new biases

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Using image captioning models as bias classifiers better captures visiolinguistic nature
- **Mechanism**: Reformulates bias measurement as prompt-based image captioning task, using model confidence to predict protected attributes
- **Core assumption**: Confidence scores are valid bias indicators that better match model pre-training objectives
- **Break condition**: If confidence scores don't reliably indicate bias or prompts introduce artifacts

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Masking protected attributes is essential for measuring contextual bias
- **Mechanism**: Replaces protected-attribute words with [MASK] tokens and hides visual clues, forcing context-based inference
- **Core assumption**: If model infers attributes from context after masking, it indicates learned association bias
- **Break condition**: If masking is imperfect or model uses unintended cues

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Prompt-based learning and its application to image captioning
  - **Why needed here**: Better matches underlying pre-training objective of assessed models
  - **Quick check question**: How does prompt-based learning differ from traditional classification in image captioning bias assessment?

- **Concept**: Bias amplification vs. absolute bias measurement
  - **Why needed here**: Provides actionable insights into how models distort existing biases
  - **Quick check question**: What's the key difference between measuring bias amplification and absolute bias?

- **Concept**: Confidence scoring as a bias metric
  - **Why needed here**: High confidence in biased predictions indicates stronger bias than accuracy-based measures
  - **Quick check question**: Why might confidence scores be better bias indicators than accuracy measures?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Image/text → preprocessing → prompt-based captioning → confidence scoring → bias amplification calculation
- **Critical path**: Image/text → preprocessing → prompt-based captioning → confidence scoring → bias amplification calculation
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Same model for assessment vs. separate classifier (potential bias vs. consistency)
  - Prompt-based approach vs. direct classification (objective match vs. complexity)
  - Masking vs. full context (stronger signal vs. information loss)
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High scores across all models (prompt/preprocessing issues)
  - Inconsistent scores across classifiers (classifier sensitivity)
  - Low correlation with human evaluation (misalignment with human perception)
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test on known biased (NIC) and debiased (NIC+) models to verify higher scores for biased model
  2. Test with different prompt templates to ensure result stability
  3. Evaluate with and without image masking to confirm image inclusion improves detection

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does ImageCaptioner2 perform on other types of bias beyond gender, race, and emotions (e.g., age, disability, socioeconomic status)?
- **Basis in paper**: Paper focuses on three specific protected attributes without exploring others
- **Why unresolved**: Only tested on three attributes, leaving generalizability unknown
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Apply metric to datasets with other protected attributes and compare performance

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can ImageCaptioner2 be adapted to handle intersectional forms of bias (e.g., gender-race intersection)?
- **Basis in paper**: Addresses individual attributes but not intersectional bias
- **Why unresolved**: Formulation focuses on single attributes, effectiveness on complex biases unclear
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extend metric to multiple attributes simultaneously and evaluate on intersectional bias datasets

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does performance compare to other metrics across different architectures and model sizes?
- **Basis in paper**: Compares to LIC on specific set of architectures
- **Why unresolved**: Comparison limited to specific models, performance on others unknown
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluate on wider range of architectures and sizes, compare to other metrics

## Limitations

- The reliance on confidence scores may not capture all forms of bias, particularly subtle or context-dependent biases
- Masking approach may not perfectly eliminate all protected attribute clues, potentially leading to false positives
- Performance across diverse real-world scenarios may not generalize beyond evaluated datasets and architectures

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Correlation results (80% vs 54% for human alignment, 97% vs 92% for consistency) based on rigorous experimental evaluation
- **Medium Confidence**: Prompt-based approach matching pre-training objectives is theoretically sound but lacks extensive validation across diverse architectures
- **Medium Confidence**: Masking approach for contextual bias measurement is methodologically justified but sensitive to implementation details

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Evaluate ImageCaptioner2 on additional datasets beyond MS-COCO, Artemis V1, and Artemis V2 to assess performance consistency across diverse scenarios and protected attribute distributions

2. **Robustness to prompt variations**: Systematically test metric sensitivity to different prompt templates and text preprocessing strategies to ensure results aren't artifacts of specific implementation choices

3. **Comparative analysis with alternative bias metrics**: Conduct comprehensive evaluation comparing ImageCaptioner2 against other established bias metrics (e.g., directional bias amplification) to validate superiority claims across multiple bias dimensions and contexts
