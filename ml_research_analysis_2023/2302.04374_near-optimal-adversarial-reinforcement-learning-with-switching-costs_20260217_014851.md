# Near-Optimal Adversarial Reinforcement Learning with Switching Costs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04374
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04374
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes algorithms achieving O((HSA)1/3T2/3) regret in adversarial RL with switching costs, matching lower bound

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of adversarial reinforcement learning where both the loss distribution can change arbitrarily over time and policy switches incur additional costs. The authors establish a regret lower bound of Ω((HSA)1/3T2/3) and develop two novel algorithms that achieve near-optimal performance. Their approach uses a super-episode framework with delayed switching and unbiased loss estimation techniques to balance the trade-off between loss regret and switching costs.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes SEEDS for known transition functions and SEEDS-UT for unknown transition functions. Both algorithms divide episodes into super-episodes of length τ, maintaining the same policy within each super-episode to reduce switching costs. They employ unbiased loss estimation by aggregating losses only from visited state-action pairs, and update occupancy measures using online mirror descent with KL-divergence regularization. The algorithms carefully tune parameters η and τ to balance exploration-exploitation trade-offs and switching costs.

## Key Results
- Establishes regret lower bound of Ω((HSA)1/3T2/3) for adversarial RL with switching costs
- SEEDS achieves O((HSA)1/3T2/3) regret for known transition functions
- SEEDS-UT achieves O(H2/3(SA)1/3T2/3(ln T SA/δ)1/2) regret with probability 1-δ for unknown transitions
- Both algorithms employ super-episode-based policy search with delayed switching
- Novel unbiased loss estimation technique handles random visitation patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Achieves near-optimal regret by delaying policy switches and using unbiased loss estimation across super-episodes
- Mechanism: Divides T episodes into super-episodes of length τ, maintaining the same policy within each super-episode to reduce switching costs from O(T) to O(T/τ). Unbiased loss estimation aggregates losses only from episodes where each state-action pair is actually visited.
- Core assumption: Loss estimation technique provides unbiased estimate despite random visitation patterns and adversarial losses
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "novel unbiased loss estimation techniques"; section describes decomposition approach for combining losses only from visited episodes
- Break condition: High variance or bias in loss estimates due to skewed visitation patterns or rapid loss changes within super-episodes

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Balances exploration and exploitation through occupancy measure updates incorporating estimated losses and regularization
- Mechanism: Updates occupancy measure using online mirror descent with KL-divergence regularizer, balancing adaptation to estimated losses (exploration) while staying close to previous occupancy measure (exploitation)
- Core assumption: Estimated losses are sufficiently accurate to guide updates toward good policies
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "switching-reduced algorithms"; section details occupancy measure update formula with KL-divergence regularizer
- Break condition: High variance or systematic bias in estimated losses causing convergence to suboptimal policies

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Achieves near-optimal regret by carefully tuning parameters to balance loss regret and switching costs
- Mechanism: Sets τ to Θ(T^(1/3)) to achieve O(T^(2/3)) switching costs, while tuning η to balance loss regret
- Core assumption: Parameter tuning achieves claimed balance between loss regret and switching costs
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states algorithms "match our lower bound"; section provides specific parameter formulas
- Break condition: Changes in scaling of T, S, A, H, or β invalidating parameter tuning assumptions

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Episode manager -> Super-episode generator -> Policy executor -> Loss estimator -> Occupancy updater -> Policy generator -> Transition estimator (SEEDS-UT only)

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize policy and occupancy measure
  2. For each super-episode:
     a. Execute policy for all episodes in super-episode
     b. Estimate losses from visited state-action pairs
     c. Update occupancy measure using estimated losses
     d. Generate new policy from updated occupancy measure
  3. Return final policy and regret analysis

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Longer super-episodes reduce switching costs but increase loss estimate variance
  - More aggressive exploration (larger η) speeds learning but increases regret
  - More conservative regularization (larger KL-divergence weight) stabilizes learning but slows adaptation

- Failure signatures:
  - High regret despite low switching costs: Biased loss estimation or suboptimal policy convergence
  - Frequent policy switches: τ too small or loss function changing too rapidly
  - Poor performance on specific state-action pairs: Inaccurate transition function estimation or insufficient exploration

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test unbiasedness of loss estimator on synthetic MDP with known transitions and adversarial losses
  2. Evaluate regret sensitivity to super-episode length τ by measuring resulting regret and switching costs
  3. Test SEEDS-UT robustness to different transition function estimation methods by varying confidence parameter γ

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the regret lower bound of Ω((HSA)1/3T2/3) be further tightened?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper establishes this lower bound and notes it's order-wise optimal
- Why unresolved: While matching upper bounds are provided, the tightness of the lower bound itself isn't proven
- What evidence would resolve it: Proof that no algorithm can achieve better regret, or counterexample achieving better regret

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does SEEDS-UT perform in practice compared to theoretical bounds?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper provides theoretical bounds but no empirical evaluations
- Why unresolved: Theoretical bounds don't always translate to practical performance with large MDPs
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing SEEDS-UT against other RL algorithms on benchmark problems

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can algorithms be extended to handle non-episodic or continuous spaces?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper focuses on episodic MDPs with discrete spaces
- Why unresolved: Many real applications involve non-episodic or continuous domains
- What evidence would resolve it: Modified version handling continuous/non-episodic settings with regret analysis

### Open Question 4
- Question: What's the impact of delayed switching on convergence speed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Uses fixed delay ensuring switches every O(T1/3) episodes
- Why unresolved: Delay parameter choice is heuristic; adaptive strategies might perform better
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis comparing different switching delay strategies including adaptive approaches

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on strong assumptions about known/estimable transition functions and bounded losses
- Lower bound construction may not capture all practical switching cost scenarios
- Assumes perfect policy switching at super-episode boundaries which may be challenging to implement

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Regret lower bound of Ω((HSA)1/3T2/3) and near-optimal regret bounds of proposed algorithms
- **Medium confidence**: Practical effectiveness of unbiased loss estimation technique due to challenges in tracking visitation frequencies
- **Medium confidence**: Parameter tuning for balancing loss regret and switching costs as optimal values depend on unknown parameters
