# Writing user personas with Large Language Models: Testing phase 6 of a Thematic Analysis of semi-structured interviews

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18099
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18099
- Reference count: 8
- The study demonstrates that an LLM can generate credible user personas from themes derived via Thematic Analysis of semi-structured interviews.

## Executive Summary
This study tests the feasibility of using Large Language Models (LLMs) to perform Thematic Analysis (TA) on semi-structured interviews and generate user personas as the final output. Using GPT-3.5-Turbo on farmer interview data, the author shows that phases 2-5 of TA can be automated, with human validation ensuring theme quality. The LLM then synthesizes these themes into realistic personas by composing structured tuples of theme-needs and theme-challenges. The resulting personas demonstrate plausible narratives grounded in the original interview data, validating LLMs as tools for human-AI collaboration in user-centered design.

## Method Summary
The method involves preprocessing 14 semi-structured farmer interviews by splitting them into 700-1600 token chunks. Phases 2-5 of Thematic Analysis are conducted using GPT-3.5-Turbo: codes are generated from chunks, grouped into themes, and evaluated by a human analyst who validates and refines them. For persona generation, random 2-element tuples are selected from 12 theme-needs and 12 theme-challenges, and the LLM composes personas using explicit prompts specifying goals, backgrounds, needs, challenges, and quotes. The pipeline demonstrates automated TA with human oversight for quality control.

## Key Results
- GPT-3.5-Turbo successfully performed phases 2-5 of Thematic Analysis on interview data
- Two credible user personas were generated using randomly selected theme tuples
- Personas included realistic components (goal, background, needs, challenges, quote) grounded in original interview quotes
- Human validation of LLM-generated themes proved essential for maintaining analytical quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The LLM can map interview-derived codes into coherent user personas by composing structured tuples of theme-needs and theme-challenges. The model receives two curated lists (one of theme-needs, one of theme-challenges), each with name, description, underlying codes, and quotes. By randomizing tuples and prompting for persona components (goal, background, needs, challenges, quote), it synthesizes a realistic narrative grounded in the original data. The core assumption is that the LLM's internal knowledge of "what a persona is" aligns with the target persona structure and can be triggered without explicit persona definitions in the prompt.

### Mechanism 2
Human-AI collaboration during thematic analysis (especially phases 3-4) is essential for producing valid themes that can feed persona generation. The human analyst reviews and refines LLM-generated themes, replacing weak single-code themes with stronger ones emerging at higher temperature. This iterative validation ensures themes are analytically sound before persona synthesis. The core assumption is that the LLM's thematic grouping is a useful starting point but needs human judgment to confirm theme validity and replace weak themes.

### Mechanism 3
Randomized tuple selection expands persona variability without compromising data grounding. Python code generates all possible 2-element tuples from the 12 theme-needs and 12 theme-challenges, yielding 78×78 combinations. Random selection ensures each persona is built from a distinct thematic combination, while the underlying codes keep the persona anchored to the interview data. The core assumption is that each tuple combination will still map to a plausible persona because the themes themselves are coherent and validated.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Interview chunks → code extraction → theme consolidation → persona prompt → persona output
- Critical path: Interview chunks → code generation → theme grouping → human validation → tuple selection → persona generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Token limits force chunking, which may fragment context; chunking vs. whole-interview analysis.
  - Randomization maximizes variety but may produce less optimal persona mixes; curated selection vs. pure randomness.
  - Skipping phase 5 keeps pipeline compact but may leave themes less polished; completeness vs. speed.
- Failure signatures:
  - Personas lacking clear connection to data → weak or invalid themes.
  - Repeated or generic personas → insufficient theme diversity or poor tuple selection.
  - Factual errors or hallucinations → LLM overreliance without human oversight.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run the pipeline end-to-end on a small interview set, verify that at least one persona clearly reflects a theme's underlying codes.
  2. Compare persona outputs at T=0 vs. T=1 to assess impact of temperature on realism.
  3. Manually curate a tuple set instead of randomizing, then compare narrative coherence to random outputs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do LLMs prioritize which codes to use when generating persona narratives? The paper mentions that it is unclear how the model prioritizes certain codes over others for building the narrative. This remains unresolved because the paper acknowledges uncertainty about the prioritization mechanism without providing a definitive answer. Empirical studies testing different coding combinations and their impact on persona outputs would resolve this.

### Open Question 2
Can LLMs perform Phase 1 (familiarization) of Thematic Analysis given advancements in token limits and memory? The paper states Phase 1 cannot be done by the LLM due to current limitations in memory and tokens. This remains unresolved because the paper acknowledges this as a current limitation but doesn't explore whether future model improvements could address it. Testing newer LLM models with expanded token limits on complete datasets would resolve this.

### Open Question 3
What is the optimal number and combination of themes to generate realistic personas? The paper used 2 theme-needs and 2 theme-challenges arbitrarily and mentions this may impact realism. This remains unresolved because the paper chose these numbers without systematic testing of different combinations. Comparative studies testing various theme combinations for persona quality and realism would resolve this.

## Limitations
- Findings are based on a single LLM (GPT-3.5-Turbo) and specific farmer interview dataset, limiting generalizability
- Qualitative theme validation relies on subjective human judgment that may not scale consistently
- Random tuple selection may produce less optimal persona combinations compared to curated selection
- Token limits required chunking that risks fragmenting context and potentially distorting thematic coherence

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The LLM can generate structured persona components (goals, backgrounds, needs, challenges) when given validated themes and explicit prompts.
- **Medium Confidence:** Randomized tuple selection produces varied personas that remain grounded in the original interview data.
- **Low Confidence:** The study's method generalizes reliably to other datasets, LLMs, or TA applications without further refinement.
