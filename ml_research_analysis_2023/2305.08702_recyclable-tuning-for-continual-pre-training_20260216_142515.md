# Recyclable Tuning for Continual Pre-training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.08702
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08702
- **Reference count:** 40
- **One-line result:** Proposed recyclable tuning improves convergence and performance of continually pre-trained PLMs through knowledge transfer from outdated adapted weights.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of adapting upgraded pre-trained language models (PLMs) in continual pre-training scenarios. When PLMs are continually pre-trained on different domains, the resulting models may become incompatible with previously adapted weights. The authors propose recyclable tuning, which leverages outdated adapted weights to improve the tuning process for upgraded PLMs. Through pilot studies, they discover that continually pre-trained PLMs maintain some degree of parametric and functional proximity, enabling effective knowledge transfer. Two methods are introduced: initialization-based recycling that uses outdated weights as starting points, and distillation-based recycling that transfers knowledge through distillation.

## Method Summary
The recyclable tuning framework involves two main approaches for leveraging outdated adapted weights when tuning upgraded PLMs. The initialization-based method directly uses the parameters of outdated adapted weights as initialization for tuning the upgraded model, capitalizing on the observed parametric proximity between continually pre-trained models. The distillation-based method employs knowledge distillation to transfer knowledge from the outdated adapted model to the upgraded model, matching both output distributions and intermediate representations. These methods can be combined for complementary benefits. The framework is evaluated across multiple downstream tasks and domains, with continual pre-training performed on RoBERTaBASE across four domains (BIO, REV, CS, NS), followed by adaptation on various task pairs.

## Key Results
- Initialization-based recycling improves convergence speed when source and target tasks are from the same category
- Distillation-based recycling achieves performance gains with limited labeled data by transferring knowledge from outdated adapted weights
- Combining both methods yields better performance than either method alone, demonstrating their complementary nature

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Initialization-based Knowledge Transfer
- Claim: Outdated adapted weights can serve as effective initialization for tuning the upgraded PLM, improving convergence speed and final performance.
- Mechanism: The parametric proximity between continually pre-trained PLMs, revealed by linear mode connectivity, allows the optimization process to start closer to a good solution in the loss landscape.
- Core assumption: The outdated weights remain within a "good" region of the parameter space even after the backbone PLM has been upgraded through continual pre-training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that after continual pre-training, the upgraded PLM remains compatible with the outdated adapted weights to some extent."
  - [section 4.2] "linearly interpolating the parameters of both adapted models could produce a series of checkpoints with high task performance (low loss)"
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus papers focus on continual learning and parameter-efficient tuning but don't directly address weight recycling between upgraded models.
- Break condition: If continual pre-training significantly alters the loss landscape or if the new domain is highly dissimilar to previous domains, the initialization may point to a poor region and hurt performance.

### Mechanism 2: Distillation-based Knowledge Transfer
- Claim: Knowledge distillation can effectively transfer knowledge from outdated adapted weights to the upgraded PLM, improving data efficiency and performance.
- Mechanism: The upgraded PLM can mimic the output distributions and intermediate representations of the outdated adapted weights, capturing task-specific knowledge even with limited labeled data.
- Core assumption: The functional similarity between continually pre-trained PLMs extends to their adapted versions, allowing effective knowledge transfer through distillation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "knowledge distillation can effectively facilitate knowledge transfer between continually pre-trained PLMs"
  - [section 4.3] "their corresponding attention heads exhibit similar patterns given the same input" (functional similarity)
  - [section 5.2] "Using only a small number of labeled examples, the upgraded PLM can outperform the original PLM when trained with far more examples"
- Break condition: If the continual pre-training causes catastrophic forgetting of task-relevant knowledge, the outdated weights may not contain useful information to distill.

### Mechanism 3: Complementary Parameter and Functional Knowledge
- Claim: Initialization-based and distillation-based methods are complementary, and combining them yields better performance than either method alone.
- Mechanism: Parameter initialization provides a good starting point in the loss landscape, while distillation provides additional supervision through output and intermediate representations, addressing both parametric and functional knowledge transfer.
- Core assumption: The benefits of initialization and distillation are additive rather than redundant, addressing different aspects of the knowledge transfer problem.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also show that both methods can be combined to further improve the performance"
  - [section 5.2] "Lfinal+Init. leads to better performance thanLfinal"
  - [section 5.2] "knowledge transfer through parameter space and model outputs are complementary to each other"
- Break condition: If one method already captures most of the transferable knowledge, adding the other may provide diminishing returns.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Original PLM (Mi) -> Outdated adapted weights (ΔTj i) -> Upgraded PLM (Mi+1)
- Critical path: For initialization-based method: Load outdated weights → Initialize new model parameters → Fine-tune on target task. For distillation-based method: Load outdated and new model weights → Compute distillation loss → Optimize new model parameters.
- Design tradeoffs: Initialization is computationally cheaper but requires compatible model architectures. Distillation is more flexible but requires more computation during training. Combining both adds complexity but improves performance.
- Failure signatures: Poor performance despite using outdated weights may indicate catastrophic forgetting during continual pre-training. Slow convergence despite good initialization may suggest the outdated weights point to a suboptimal region of the new loss landscape.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test initialization-based method on a simple task (e.g., sentiment analysis) using adapter tuning to verify convergence improvements
  2. Test distillation-based method on the same task to verify performance improvements with limited data
  3. Combine both methods on the same task to verify complementary benefits

## Limitations

- The parametric proximity between continually pre-trained PLMs may not generalize across all domain combinations and pre-training strategies
- The effectiveness of distillation-based methods depends on the degree of catastrophic forgetting during continual pre-training
- Findings are primarily validated on adapter-based tuning methods, limiting generalization to other parameter-efficient approaches

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The empirical demonstration that initialization-based recycling improves convergence speed has strong support through controlled experiments comparing with random initialization baselines. The linear mode connectivity observations are methodologically sound.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that combining initialization and distillation methods yields complementary benefits is supported by experimental results, but the statistical significance of the improvements and their consistency across different task categories could be stronger.

**Low Confidence**: The generalization of findings to PLMs other than RoBERTaBASE, particularly larger models or those from different families, remains untested. The paper's claims about computational efficiency benefits are qualitative rather than quantitative.

## Next Checks
