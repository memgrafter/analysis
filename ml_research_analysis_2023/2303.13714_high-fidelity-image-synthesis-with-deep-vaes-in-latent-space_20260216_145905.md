# High Fidelity Image Synthesis With Deep VAEs In Latent Space

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13714
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13714
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Achieves FID of 9.34 on ImageNet-256, competitive with BigGAN

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a two-stage approach for high-fidelity image generation using hierarchical variational autoencoders (VAEs) trained in the latent space of a deterministic autoencoder. The method first compresses images into semantic features using a pretrained deterministic autoencoder, then trains a deep VAE to model these features. By focusing on structural components rather than fine-grained details, this approach enables VAEs to generate more realistic images on large, multimodal datasets like ImageNet-256. The method achieves competitive Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores while addressing key limitations of traditional VAEs in generating high-quality images.

## Method Summary
The proposed method employs a two-stage framework for image generation. First, a deterministic autoencoder compresses images into a semantic latent space, capturing meaningful features while discarding pixel-level noise. This pretrained autoencoder serves as a feature extractor for the second stage. In the second stage, a hierarchical VAE is trained on the latent representations from the autoencoder, learning to model the distribution of these compressed features. The hierarchical structure allows the VAE to capture multi-scale dependencies in the latent space, improving its ability to generate diverse and realistic images. By separating feature extraction from generative modeling, the approach enables VAEs to focus on structural aspects of image generation rather than pixel-level reconstruction.

## Key Results
- Achieves FID of 9.34 on ImageNet-256 dataset
- Outperforms baseline hierarchical VAEs in image quality
- Demonstrates competitive performance compared to BigGAN

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The two-stage approach works by decoupling feature extraction from generative modeling. The deterministic autoencoder learns to compress images into a meaningful latent space, removing pixel-level noise and focusing on semantic content. This preprocessing allows the VAE to operate in a more structured latent space where structural components are emphasized over fine details. The hierarchical VAE can then model multi-scale dependencies in this compressed representation, learning to generate diverse and realistic images by capturing the distribution of semantic features rather than raw pixels.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Deterministic Autoencoder -> Hierarchical VAE -> Image Generator

Critical Path:
Image → Deterministic Autoencoder → Latent Representation → Hierarchical VAE Sampling → Generated Image

Design Tradeoffs:
- Separation of feature extraction and generation improves VAE focus on structure
- Additional computational overhead from two-stage training
- Dependence on quality of pretrained deterministic autoencoder

Failure Signatures:
- Poor quality autoencoder leads to suboptimal latent representations
- VAE collapse results in mode dropping or repetitive outputs
- Hierarchical structure too shallow/deep for target dataset complexity

First Experiments:
1. Train deterministic autoencoder on ImageNet-256 and visualize latent space structure
2. Train baseline VAE directly on pixel space for comparison
3. Implement hierarchical VAE on autoencoder latent space with varying depth

## Limitations
- Reliance on pretrained deterministic autoencoder limits domain adaptability
- Still lags behind state-of-the-art GANs in sample diversity and fine detail preservation
- Evaluation primarily through FID may not capture full perceptual quality
