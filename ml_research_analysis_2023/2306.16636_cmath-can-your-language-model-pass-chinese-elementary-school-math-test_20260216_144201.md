# CMATH: Can Your Language Model Pass Chinese Elementary School Math Test?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16636
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16636
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: GPT-4 is the only model achieving over 60% accuracy across all six elementary school grades on CMATH

## Executive Summary
This work introduces CMATH, a dataset of 1.7k elementary school-level Chinese math word problems, to evaluate the mathematical capabilities of large language models (LLMs). The dataset is sourced from actual Chinese workbooks and exams, annotated with grade information, answers, and complexity measures like reasoning steps and digits involved. Evaluations on various popular LLMs reveal that GPT-4 is the only model achieving over 60% accuracy across all six elementary school grades, while others struggle significantly. Furthermore, GPT-4 demonstrates robustness against distracting information, maintaining performance when irrelevant details are added to problems, whereas other models' accuracy drops sharply. This study highlights the current limitations of LLMs in arithmetic and reasoning tasks, providing insights for future advancements.

## Method Summary
The study collected 1.7k elementary school math word problems from actual Chinese workbooks and exams, then annotated each problem with grade level, answers, reasoning steps, and digit counts. Models were evaluated using a zero-shot approach where problems were presented without supplementary prompting. An automated evaluation procedure using regular expressions extracted and compared numerals from model responses with ground truth answers. The study also tested model robustness by adding distracting information to problems and measuring performance degradation.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieves over 60% accuracy across all six elementary school grades, while other models struggle significantly
- Reasoning complexity has a larger impact on LLM performance than arithmetic complexity
- GPT-4 demonstrates robustness to distracting information, maintaining performance when irrelevant details are added, unlike other models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CMATH's fine-grained grade-level annotation enables precise measurement of LLM mathematical proficiency by mapping performance to human grade standards.
- Mechanism: By annotating each problem with specific grade level, the dataset creates a direct correspondence between LLM accuracy and human elementary school grade achievement.
- Core assumption: Grade-level classification accurately reflects problem difficulty and required reasoning skills in a way that correlates with LLM capabilities.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "This dataset aims to provide a benchmark tool for assessing the following question: to what grade level of elementary school math do the abilities of popular large language models (LLMs) correspond?"; [section 2.3.1] "We annotate the elementary school grade to which each collected math word problem belongs. This information can be used to create subsets of problems specific to a particular grade, enabling more targeted, fine-grained evaluation results."
- Break condition: If grade-level annotations don't accurately capture problem difficulty or if LLM performance doesn't correlate with human grade-level expectations, the benchmark would lose its interpretability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The dataset's complexity measures (reasoning steps and digits) provide orthogonal dimensions for analyzing LLM failures beyond simple accuracy metrics.
- Mechanism: By annotating problems with number of reasoning steps and number of digits, the dataset enables analysis of whether LLM failures stem from reasoning complexity or arithmetic complexity, allowing targeted model improvements.
- Core assumption: These two measures effectively capture the key dimensions of mathematical problem complexity that affect LLM performance.
- Evidence anchors: [section 2.3.3] "We use the number of reasoning steps as a suitable proxy for a problem's reasoning complexity, which relates to the level of logical analysis and problem-solving strategies needed for an LLM to arrive at the correct solution."; [section 2.3.4] "This quantity is a practical and easily quantifiable measure of the computational demands placed on an LLM when tackling a problem."; [section 4.2] "it is pertinent to say that the reasoning complexity of the problem has generally a larger impact than the arithmetic complexity."
- Break condition: If these measures don't correlate with actual difficulty for LLMs or if other dimensions of complexity are more important, the analysis would be incomplete.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The distractor robustness test reveals whether LLMs truly understand problems or are merely pattern-matching.
- Mechanism: By adding irrelevant information to problems and observing performance degradation, the test distinguishes between LLMs that understand relevant information versus those that are misled by numerical cues.
- Core assumption: Robustness to distractors indicates genuine problem comprehension rather than superficial pattern matching.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.3] "This type of robustness is of particular interest because real-world problems seldom manifest in an idealized manner where all provided information is useful."; [section 4.3] "Our findings reveal that GPT-4 is able to maintains robustness, while other model fail."; [table 4] Shows qualitative differences in how models handle distractors, with GPT-4 maintaining focus while others produce erroneous reasoning.
- Break condition: If distractor robustness doesn't correlate with real-world performance or if the test methodology is flawed, the conclusions about model understanding would be invalid.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Zero-shot evaluation methodology
  - Why needed here: Ensures the benchmark measures baseline LLM capabilities without artificial prompting enhancements that wouldn't be available in real applications
  - Quick check question: Does the evaluation approach allow any form of supplementary prompting, few-shot examples, or chain-of-thought techniques?

- Concept: Automated answer extraction from LLM responses
  - Why needed here: Enables scalable evaluation across thousands of problems without manual review of each response
  - Quick check question: How does the system identify and extract the final numerical answer from potentially multi-paragraph LLM responses?

- Concept: Problem complexity measurement
  - Why needed here: Allows decomposition of performance failures into reasoning vs arithmetic components for targeted model improvement
  - Quick check question: How are the number of reasoning steps and number of digits calculated for each problem, and what do these metrics represent?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection pipeline (PDF/Word → text conversion → deduplication → cleaning → validation) → Annotation system (Grade classification + answer extraction + reasoning steps + digit counting) → Evaluation framework (Problem presentation → LLM response generation → automated answer extraction → correctness checking) → Analysis tools (Accuracy calculation by grade/complexity → distractor robustness testing → visualization)

- Critical path: Problem annotation → evaluation execution → automated answer extraction → accuracy calculation → result analysis

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Manual vs automated annotation: Manual provides accuracy but limits scale; automated enables scale but may miss nuances
  - Zero-shot vs prompted evaluation: Zero-shot measures baseline capability but may underestimate potential; prompted evaluation may overestimate real-world performance
  - Automated vs manual answer checking: Automated enables scale but has precision/recall limitations; manual is accurate but doesn't scale

- Failure signatures:
  - Low precision in automated answer extraction → inflated accuracy scores
  - Inconsistent grade-level annotation → meaningless comparisons across models
  - Poor distractor design → invalid robustness conclusions
  - Data leakage between training and evaluation → inflated performance metrics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run evaluation on a small subset (50 problems) with manual answer verification to validate automated extraction accuracy
  2. Test grade-level consistency by having multiple annotators classify problems and measuring inter-annotator agreement
  3. Validate distractor robustness by manually reviewing LLM responses on augmented problems to ensure the automated analysis captures actual failure modes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do LLMs' performances on CMATH correlate with their performance on other math reasoning benchmarks, and what does this reveal about the transferability of mathematical skills across different domains and languages?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates LLMs on a Chinese elementary school math dataset but does not compare results to other math benchmarks or explore cross-domain/cross-linguistic performance correlations.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses solely on Chinese elementary math problems without benchmarking against other datasets or exploring how performance on CMATH might generalize to other math domains or languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of LLM performance on CMATH versus other math datasets (e.g., GSM8K, MATH) and analysis of performance correlations across different languages and complexity levels.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific architectural or training modifications could improve LLMs' robustness to distracting information in math word problems, and how can these improvements be systematically measured?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies GPT-4's robustness to distractors as a key differentiator but does not explore the underlying reasons or propose methods to improve other models' robustness.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the problem of distractor susceptibility, it does not investigate the technical reasons behind this weakness or propose concrete solutions to address it.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic analysis of model architectures and training approaches that enhance distractor robustness, along with new evaluation metrics to quantify improvements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance gap between GPT-4 and other LLMs on elementary math problems inform our understanding of what constitutes "elementary" mathematical reasoning, and at what point do these gaps begin to close for more advanced mathematical concepts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows GPT-4 significantly outperforms other models on elementary math but does not analyze what specific aspects of reasoning contribute to this gap or how it might change for higher-level mathematics.
- Why unresolved: The study identifies performance differences but does not deconstruct the specific reasoning capabilities that differentiate GPT-4 from other models or examine whether these differences persist at higher mathematical levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed error analysis of different model types on specific reasoning types, and systematic testing across multiple mathematical domains and complexity levels.

## Limitations
- The dataset size (1.7k problems) may not fully represent the breadth of elementary mathematics education in China
- The automated answer extraction methodology using regular expressions may have precision/recall limitations that could inflate accuracy scores
- The distractor augmentation methodology lacks full specification of how distracting information is selected and applied

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The claim that GPT-4 outperforms other models on CMATH across all grade levels is well-supported by the evaluation results
- **Medium Confidence**: The interpretation that reasoning complexity has a larger impact than arithmetic complexity on LLM performance requires further validation
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that CMATH provides a comprehensive benchmark for elementary school mathematics capabilities is limited by the dataset size and lack of validation

## Next Checks
1. **Annotation Quality Validation**: Conduct a blind re-annotation study where independent annotators classify 100 randomly selected problems by grade level and count reasoning steps/digits. Calculate inter-annotator agreement using Cohen's kappa to establish the reliability of the annotations.

2. **Automated Answer Extraction Precision**: Manually verify the accuracy of the automated answer extraction system by reviewing 200 LLM responses across all models. Calculate precision, recall, and F1-score of the regular expression approach to quantify potential inflation in reported accuracy scores.

3. **Distractor Robustness Validation**: Design a controlled experiment where the same LLM responses are evaluated both with and without distractors by human reviewers to validate that the automated analysis correctly identifies when distractors cause erroneous reasoning versus when they're simply ignored.
