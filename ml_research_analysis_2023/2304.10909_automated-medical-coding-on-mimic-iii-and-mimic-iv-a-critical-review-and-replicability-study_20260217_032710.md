# Automated Medical Coding on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV: A Critical Review and Replicability Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10909
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10909
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents a comprehensive reproducibility study of automated medical coding models on the MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive reproducibility study of automated medical coding models on the MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV datasets. The authors find that previous evaluations were flawed due to suboptimal experimental setups, including poor hyperparameters, inadequate decision boundary tuning, and missing classes in the test set. Their corrections significantly improve macro F1 scores. They create new MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV splits with full class representation and perform a revised model comparison using consistent experimental setups. Their error analysis confirms that rare codes are challenging for all models, while long documents have a negligible impact on performance. The study provides the first comprehensive results on MIMIC-IV and offers new training and evaluation pipelines for fair future comparisons.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates six encoder-decoder models (Bi-GRU, CNN, CAML, MultiResCNN, LAAT, PLM-ICD) on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV datasets for automated medical coding. The authors identify flaws in previous evaluations including missing classes in test sets, suboptimal hyperparameters, and unoptimized decision boundaries. They create new stratified train/validation/test splits ensuring full class representation, implement consistent preprocessing (lowercasing, removing non-alphabet characters), and tune hyperparameters and decision boundaries for each model. Performance is measured using micro/macro F1, AUC-ROC, and other metrics, with macro F1 calculated as the arithmetic mean of F1 for each class.

## Key Results
- Correcting macro F1 calculation and addressing missing classes in test sets doubles previously reported macro F1 scores
- Tuned hyperparameters and decision boundaries substantially improve model performance compared to original configurations
- Long documents have negligible impact on performance, contrary to prior assumptions
- PLM-ICD achieves the highest performance among evaluated models on both MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Poor evaluation practices artificially suppressed reported macro F1 scores
- Mechanism: Original macro F1 was computed as the harmonic mean of macro precision and recall, which is sub-optimal for imbalanced datasets. Additionally, missing classes in test sets were assigned zero F1, capping the maximum achievable macro F1 at 46%.
- Core assumption: The harmonic mean formulation biases toward lower values when classes are imbalanced and that setting missing classes to zero significantly underestimates performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In previous work, the macro F1 score has been calculated sub-optimally, and our correction doubles it."
  - [section] "Opitz and Burst [33] analyze macro F1 formulas common in multi-class and multi-label classification. They demonstrate that the above formulation is sub-optimal, as it rewards heavily biased classifiers in unbalanced datasets."
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence directly supporting the harmonic mean claim.
- Break condition: If macro F1 were calculated differently (e.g., micro-averaged) or if missing classes were handled by excluding them from the macro calculation entirely.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Suboptimal hyperparameters and lack of decision boundary tuning caused models to underperform
- Mechanism: Models were trained with original hyperparameters that were not optimized for the MIMIC datasets, and decision boundaries were fixed at 0.5 instead of being tuned on validation data. These factors masked the true performance potential of the models.
- Core assumption: Hyperparameter tuning and decision boundary optimization significantly impact multi-label classification performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that several models underperform due to weak configurations, poorly sampled train-test splits, and insufficient evaluation."
  - [section] "Using the hyperparameters from the original works degraded the performance substantially for CAML, Bi-GRU, and CNN but had a smaller effect on the other models. Not tuning the decision boundary had the largest negative effect on all models except MultiResCNN."
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence directly comparing tuned vs untuned performance.
- Break condition: If model architectures are inherently robust to hyperparameter settings or if decision boundaries are irrelevant for certain loss functions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Long documents have negligible impact on performance, contrary to prior assumptions
- Mechanism: Previous studies claimed long documents degrade performance, but empirical analysis showed only a very small negative correlation between document length and micro F1, which is not significant enough to affect average performance.
- Core assumption: Document length directly impacts model performance in multi-label classification tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The analysis confirms that all models struggle with rare codes, while long documents only have a negligible performance impact."
  - [section] "We observe a very small negative correlation between document length and micro F1 which matches the downward trend in micro F1, starting from approximately 1000 words in Fig. 4."
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence quantifying the impact of document length on other datasets.
- Break condition: If document length correlates with other factors (e.g., number of codes per document) that do impact performance.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Macro vs micro averaging in multi-label classification
  - Why needed here: Understanding the difference is crucial for interpreting evaluation results and why the correction of macro F1 matters.
  - Quick check question: If a dataset has 100 classes with 99 having zero precision and one having perfect precision, what would macro precision and micro precision be?

- Concept: Stratified sampling for multi-label datasets
  - Why needed here: Ensures that the test set contains a representative sample of all classes, avoiding the issue of missing classes that biased previous evaluations.
  - Quick check question: How does stratified sampling differ from random sampling when dealing with imbalanced multi-label data?

- Concept: Decision boundary tuning in multi-label classification
  - Why needed here: Critical for optimizing model performance since default thresholds (0.5) may not be optimal for all classes or datasets.
  - Quick check question: Why might a fixed decision threshold of 0.5 be suboptimal for multi-label classification with imbalanced classes?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Encoder: Word2Vec, CNN, Bi-GRU, BERT (PLM-ICD)
  - Decoder: Max-pooling, LA CAML, LA AT
  - Training pipeline: Data preprocessing, stratified sampling, hyperparameter tuning, decision boundary optimization
  - Evaluation: AUC-ROC, micro/macro F1, Precision@k, EMR, R-precision, MAP

- Critical path: Data preprocessing → Model training with tuned hyperparameters → Decision boundary optimization → Stratified evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pre-trained vs from-scratch embeddings: PLM-ICD uses BERT pre-trained on PubMed but doesn't significantly outperform other models
  - Model complexity vs performance: More complex models (LAAT, PLM-ICD) don't always yield proportional performance gains
  - Document truncation: 4000 words vs 2500 words shows minimal performance difference

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation on rare codes (fewer than 100 training examples)
  - Models failing to predict codes with digits in descriptions due to preprocessing
  - Over-reliance on frequent codes when similar codes have class imbalance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does document length have a causal impact on medical coding performance, or is the observed weak correlation merely correlational?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper found only a weak negative correlation between document length and micro F1, and suggests the correlation might be due to other factors correlated with document length.
- Why unresolved: The analysis was correlational and didn't control for confounding variables like number of codes per document or code frequency.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experimental study that manipulates document length while holding other factors constant, or a causal inference analysis that controls for potential confounders.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the source of the large performance gap between PLM-ICD and other models - is it primarily due to pre-training, model architecture, or some other factor?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that PLM-ICD significantly outperforms other models but questions why pre-trained transformers have been unsuccessful in previous medical coding attempts.
- Why unresolved: The study didn't perform ablation experiments to isolate the contribution of different components (pre-training, architecture, hyperparameters).
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled comparison where PLM-ICD is re-implemented without pre-training or with different pre-training strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would medical coding performance change if the pre-processing step of removing digits from discharge summaries was modified or removed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The error analysis identified that removing digits made certain codes (particularly those with numerical descriptions like BMI codes and gestational age codes) challenging to predict.
- Why unresolved: The study only observed this issue in error analysis but didn't experiment with alternative pre-processing approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance with different pre-processing strategies, such as preserving numerical digits or using specialized tokenization for numerical information.

## Limitations
- The study relies on exact hyperparameter values and preprocessing decisions from previous works that are not fully specified in the original publications
- The impact of document truncation at 4000 words versus 2500 words remains unclear
- The study focuses exclusively on English-language clinical text from US hospitals, limiting applicability to other languages and healthcare systems

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mechanism that poor evaluation practices artificially suppressed macro F1 scores is well-supported by mathematical analysis and the dramatic improvement when corrected (doubling of macro F1). The claim that stratified sampling is essential for fair evaluation is strongly supported by the identification of missing classes in previous test sets.
- **Medium confidence**: The impact of hyperparameter tuning and decision boundary optimization is supported by empirical results showing substantial performance improvements, though the specific hyperparameter ranges and search methodology could affect reproducibility.
- **Low confidence**: The claim that document length has negligible impact on performance is based on correlation analysis showing a very small negative correlation, but this may not hold for different document length distributions or model architectures.

## Next Checks

1. **Reproduce the baseline performance gap**: Implement the original experimental setup with default hyperparameters and 0.5 decision thresholds on both MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV datasets. Compare results with corrected implementations to verify the magnitude of improvement claimed.

2. **Validate class coverage in test sets**: Create new test sets using stratified sampling and verify that all codes present in the full dataset are represented. Compare performance metrics with previous test sets that had missing classes to quantify the impact of this evaluation flaw.

3. **Test document length sensitivity**: Systematically evaluate model performance on documents of varying lengths (short: <500 words, medium: 500-1000 words, long: >1000 words) to confirm that the negligible impact holds across the full distribution of document lengths in MIMIC datasets.
