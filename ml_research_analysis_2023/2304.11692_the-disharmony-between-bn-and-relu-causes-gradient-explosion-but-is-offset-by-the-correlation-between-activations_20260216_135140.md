# The Disharmony between BN and ReLU Causes Gradient Explosion, but is Offset by the Correlation between Activations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11692
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11692
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LALC optimizer outperforms existing methods in large batch training while achieving similar performance to WarmUp in small batch training

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of gradient explosion during early training stages in deep neural networks using ReLU activations and batch normalization. The authors demonstrate that the interaction between ReLU and batch normalization causes gradients to increase exponentially during backpropagation, while forward propagation remains stable. This occurs because ReLU reduces variance more than expected (by (π-1)/(2π) instead of 1/2), and batch normalization amplifies the gradient during recovery. The paper proposes LALC (Layer-wise Adaptive Learning-rate Clipping), an improved adaptive learning rate algorithm that scales the learning rate inversely proportional to the square of the gradient norm, effectively controlling training instability.

## Method Summary
The authors propose LALC (Layer-wise Adaptive Learning-rate Clipping) as an improved adaptive learning rate algorithm inspired by second-order optimization. LALC scales the learning rate inversely proportional to the square of the gradient norm, using the approximation that the Hessian equals the square of the gradient norm for parameters in series. The method is designed to handle the gradient explosion problem caused by the disharmony between ReLU and batch normalization during early training stages. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets using ResNet-50 architecture, comparing LALC against existing learning rate scaling/clipping methods (LARS, CLARS, AGC, LAMBC) and WarmUp scheduling.

## Key Results
- LALC outperforms existing learning rate scaling/clipping methods in large batch training scenarios
- LALC exhibits similar performance to WarmUp in small batch training while reducing the need for explicit warm-up phases
- Experiments demonstrate effective mitigation of gradient explosion during early training stages
- Convolutional layers show smaller explosion rates compared to fully connected layers due to input correlations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The interaction between ReLU and batch normalization causes gradient explosion due to variance amplification
- Mechanism: ReLU reduces variance more than expected (by (π-1)/(2π) instead of 1/2), and batch normalization amplifies the gradient during recovery, causing exponential growth
- Core assumption: Input distributions remain approximately zero-centered and weights maintain stable norms during early training
- Evidence anchors:

  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations discussing this specific variance amplification mechanism
- Break condition: When inputs become highly non-zero-centered or when weight norms become unstable between layers

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Correlation between activations reduces gradient explosion during training
- Mechanism: As training progresses, correlated activations reduce the effective variance of the sum, which decreases gradient norms while maintaining stable forward propagation
- Core assumption: Neural networks tend to generate redundant signals during training, creating correlation between activations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the correlation between activations plays a key role in preventing the gradient explosion from persisting throughout the training"
  - [section] "Most of the assumptions in Theorem 1 seem to remain valid even after the neural network is trained... However, a neural network tends to generate multiple redundant and duplicated signals during training"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations discussing correlation effects on gradient explosion
- Break condition: When activations become decorrelated (e.g., through dropout or aggressive regularization)

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LALC scales learning rates inversely proportional to squared gradient norm to handle curvature
- Mechanism: By approximating the Hessian as the square of the gradient norm, LALC provides larger steps for parameters in parallel and smaller steps for parameters in series
- Core assumption: The curvature of the loss landscape scales with the square of the gradient norm for parameters in series
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose an improved adaptive learning rate algorithm inspired by second-order optimization algorithms"
  - [section] "we used the formula from the basic second-order optimization ∆w = (H +ϵI)⁻¹J, where the Hessian is approximated by the square of the gradient norm"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations discussing LALC's specific second-order approximation approach
- Break condition: When gradient norms become very small (approaching numerical instability)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the correlation between activations provide a more fundamental explanation for gradient explosion than the disharmony between batch normalization and ReLU?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "the correlation between activations seems to contribute to the decrease of the gradient primarily" and propose that this correlation alters variance differently than independent random variables.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides empirical evidence but doesn't offer a rigorous mathematical proof that correlation is the primary factor. The proof of Theorem 2.4 (perfect correlation case) is a special case.
- What evidence would resolve it: A mathematical proof showing that correlation between activations is the dominant factor in preventing gradient explosion across all correlation scenarios, not just perfect correlation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is the LALC algorithm's effectiveness primarily due to its inverse relationship with gradient norm squared, or are there other contributing factors?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose LALC as "inspired by the basic second-order optimization formula" using gradient norm squared, but don't isolate this factor's specific contribution.
- Why unresolved: The experiments compare LALC to other methods but don't perform ablation studies to determine which aspects of LALC are most important for its performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies comparing LALC variants that use different relationships between learning rate and gradient norm (linear, quadratic, etc.) to determine which aspect is most critical.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the gradient explosion phenomenon differ between fully connected layers and convolutional layers, and why?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note "The convolutional layer is observed to exhibit a smaller explosion rate compared to fully connected layers" and speculate this may be due to "correlation between inputs in the CNN."
- Why unresolved: The paper provides only qualitative speculation without rigorous analysis of why convolutional layers show different behavior.
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical analysis comparing gradient explosion rates in convolutional vs fully connected architectures, accounting for spatial correlations in convolutional layers.

## Limitations
- The variance amplification mechanism assumes zero-centered inputs and stable weight norms during early training, which may not hold in practice
- The paper lacks comprehensive ablation studies to isolate the effects of variance amplification, correlation, and LALC separately
- The sensitivity of LALC to hyperparameters like η and ϵ is not thoroughly explored

## Confidence

High confidence: Variance amplification mechanism through ReLU and batch normalization interaction (Theorem 2.2 provides rigorous mathematical proof)
Medium confidence: Correlation-based mitigation effects and LALC algorithm effectiveness (primarily empirical observations with limited mathematical proof)
