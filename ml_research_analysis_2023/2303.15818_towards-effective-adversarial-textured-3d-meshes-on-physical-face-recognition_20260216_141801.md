# Towards Effective Adversarial Textured 3D Meshes on Physical Face Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15818
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15818
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: 91% attack success rate on physical face recognition systems

## Executive Summary
This work presents a novel physical adversarial attack against face recognition systems using 3D textured meshes. The approach optimizes in the low-dimensional coefficient space of 3D Morphable Models (3DMM) rather than high-dimensional mesh space, improving black-box transferability. The method generates 3D-printed textured meshes that can be attached to an attacker's face to simultaneously deceive commercial face recognition APIs, anti-spoofing systems, mobile phones, and automated access control systems.

## Method Summary
The attack framework, named AT3D-P, perturbs the low-dimensional coefficient space of 3DMM to generate adversarial textured meshes. The optimization process involves initializing with the victim's facial coefficients and attacker's pose, then iteratively updating these coefficients through differentiable neural rendering to minimize face recognition similarity scores. The resulting meshes are 3D-printed and physically attached to the attacker's face, creating a physical adversarial example that evades both recognition and anti-spoofing defenses across multiple modalities.

## Key Results
- 91% attack success rate on physical face recognition systems
- AT3D-P outperforms 2D digital attacks when transferred to physical domain
- Effective against RGB, depth, and infrared-based defensive mechanisms
- Evades commercial systems including face recognition APIs, anti-spoofing APIs, mobile phones, and access control systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Low-dimensional coefficient space optimization improves black-box transferability compared to high-dimensional mesh space.
- Mechanism: Optimizing in the 3DMM coefficient space constrains the search to a realistic face manifold, reducing overfitting and improving generalization to unseen models.
- Core assumption: The 3DMM coefficient space sufficiently captures the variability of real faces to generate adversarial examples that transfer across models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose to perturb the low-dimensional coefficient space based on 3D Morphable Model (3DMM)... which significantly improves black-box transferability"
  - [section] "optimizing in the pre-learnt low-dimensional coefficient space of 3DMM can promote more general semantic features of a 3D face"
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct evidence in corpus about 3DMM-based transfer
- Break condition: If 3DMM fails to capture critical facial features needed for adversarial perturbations, transferability would degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Elaborate mesh topology evades defensive mechanisms across RGB, depth, and infrared modalities.
- Mechanism: 3D-printed textured meshes with realistic topology mimic real facial geometry, preventing anti-spoofing systems from detecting them as synthetic.
- Core assumption: Defensive systems rely on detecting anomalies in facial geometry that are absent in well-designed 3D meshes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "adversarial textured 3D meshes (AT3D) with an elaborate topology on a human face, which can be 3D-printed and pasted on the attacker's face to evade the defenses"
  - [section] "experimentally found that the adversarial mesh with elaborate topology can present a similar appearance with the original one among RGB-based, depth-based and infrared-based defensive techniques"
  - [corpus] Weak: no corpus evidence about topology-based defense evasion
- Break condition: If defensive systems incorporate additional verification methods beyond geometry analysis, the attack may fail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Initializing adversarial mesh coefficients with victim's identity and attacker's pose improves optimization convergence and effectiveness.
- Mechanism: Starting from victim's facial coefficients while using attacker's pose creates a closer semantic match between initialization and target, accelerating optimization.
- Core assumption: The semantic similarity between initialization and target affects optimization efficiency and final attack quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we experimentally found that selecting different initialization in optimizing Eq. (5) gives rise to inconsistent black-box performances... Thus, we apply the coefficients of the 3DMM from the victim to initialize the adversarial mesh"
  - [section] "the best performance can be achieved when adopting the pose of the victim to consistently fit the attacker's face"
  - [corpus] Weak: no corpus evidence about initialization strategies
- Break condition: If the victim's facial features are too dissimilar from the attacker's, the initialization may not provide sufficient guidance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  3DMM regression model (shape, texture, illumination, pose coefficients) -> Mesh generation and topology design -> Differentiable renderer -> Face recognition model -> Anti-spoofing detection systems

- Critical path:
  1. 3DMM regression generates initial coefficients from target face
  2. Mesh topology is designed for physical realism
  3. Differentiable renderer converts mesh to image
  4. Recognition model computes similarity
  5. Gradients flow back through renderer to update 3DMM coefficients
  6. Process repeats until attack success or iteration limit

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Mesh resolution vs. 3D printing feasibility
  - Perturbation magnitude vs. naturalness
  - Optimization speed vs. attack effectiveness
  - Topology complexity vs. physical comfort

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor convergence in optimization
  - Recognition system detects mesh as fake
  - 3D printing fails to capture fine details
  - Physical attachment degrades mesh quality

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify 3DMM regression accurately reconstructs faces from 2D images
  2. Test differentiable renderer produces realistic face images from mesh
  3. Evaluate attack effectiveness in controlled digital environment before physical testing

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of AT3D-P compare against adversarial attacks that utilize other low-dimensional manifolds beyond 3DMM, such as autoencoders or generative adversarial networks (GANs)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on using 3DMM for low-dimensional optimization but does not explore other low-dimensional manifolds for comparison.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates AT3D-P against 2D attacks and AT3D-M/AT3D-ML, which are mesh-based optimizations, but does not compare against other low-dimensional optimization methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments comparing AT3D-P against adversarial attacks utilizing other low-dimensional manifolds on the same datasets and evaluation metrics would provide insights into the relative effectiveness of different low-dimensional optimization approaches.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different initialization strategies on the convergence speed and final attack success rate of AT3D-P, particularly when considering attackers with varying degrees of similarity to the victim?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the sensitivity of AT3D-P to initialization, showing that using the victim's coefficients performs better than noise, and further improvement is achieved by using the victim's pose.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the importance of initialization, it does not explore the full spectrum of initialization strategies or analyze the impact of attacker-victim similarity on the optimization process.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically evaluating different initialization strategies, including varying degrees of attacker-victim similarity, and measuring their impact on convergence speed and attack success rate would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the initialization problem.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is AT3D-P against adversarial defenses specifically designed for 3D attacks, such as depth-based or multi-spectral analysis techniques?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that 3D attacks are more feasible for evading face anti-spoofing with multiple modalities due to versatile and realistic characteristics, but does not provide a detailed analysis of AT3D-P's robustness against specific 3D adversarial defenses.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on evaluating AT3D-P against commercial face anti-spoofing APIs, which may not employ the most advanced 3D-specific defenses.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments against a range of 3D adversarial defenses, including depth-based and multi-spectral analysis techniques, would provide insights into the robustness of AT3D-P against state-of-the-art 3D attack detection methods.
