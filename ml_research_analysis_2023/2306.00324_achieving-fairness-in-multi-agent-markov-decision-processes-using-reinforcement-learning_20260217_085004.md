# Achieving Fairness in Multi-Agent Markov Decision Processes Using Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00324
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00324
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes online and offline RL algorithms with sub-linear regret and sub-optimality gap bounds for achieving fairness in multi-agent MDPs with unknown environments

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of achieving fairness in multi-agent Markov Decision Processes where the environment is unknown. The authors introduce a fairness function that ensures equitable rewards across agents rather than maximizing the sum of individual value functions. They develop an online convex optimization approach using state-action occupancy measures with confidence bounds for the unknown environment. The work provides theoretical guarantees through regret bounds and sub-optimality gap bounds for both online and offline algorithms, along with a policy-gradient method for large state spaces. Simulation experiments validate the approach's effectiveness in achieving fair decision-making strategies.

## Method Summary
The authors tackle fairness in multi-agent MDPs by formulating it as an online convex optimization problem. They use state-action occupancy measures as the decision variables and construct confidence bounds for the unknown environment parameters. The online algorithm achieves sub-linear regret through careful bounding of the estimation error and the fairness objective's non-linearity. For offline settings, they develop an algorithm with provable sub-optimality gap bounds. Additionally, they propose a policy-gradient method suitable for large state spaces where exact occupancy measure computation becomes intractable. The fairness function can be tuned for different notions of fairness including α-fairness, proportional fairness, and max-min fairness.

## Key Results
- Online algorithm achieves sub-linear regret bound of O(CF·(H²N√SAK + HN²S^(3/2)A + H²NS²A))
- Offline RL algorithm provides sub-optimality gap bound for fair policies
- Policy-gradient method developed for large state spaces
- Validated through simulation experiments demonstrating effectiveness of fairness guarantees

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by transforming the fairness-constrained multi-agent MDP problem into an online convex optimization framework. By using occupancy measures as the optimization variables and constructing confidence bounds around the unknown transition dynamics, the algorithm can make decisions that balance fairness across agents while maintaining performance guarantees. The non-linear nature of fairness functions is handled through careful mathematical analysis that bounds the estimation error and the impact of the fairness objective on regret. The confidence bounds on the environment parameters allow the algorithm to explore safely while converging to fair policies.

## Foundational Learning

**Multi-Agent MDPs**: Framework for modeling sequential decision-making problems with multiple interacting agents - needed to represent the problem structure where agents share a common environment but may have different objectives - quick check: verify state and action spaces properly account for all agents

**Occupancy Measures**: Probability distribution over state-action pairs induced by a policy - needed as the optimization variables that directly relate to the value functions - quick check: confirm occupancy measures sum to 1 across all state-action pairs

**Convex Optimization with Confidence Bounds**: Online optimization framework that incorporates uncertainty about the environment - needed to handle the unknown transition dynamics while optimizing for fairness - quick check: verify confidence bounds properly capture the estimation uncertainty

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Environment Dynamics -> Occupancy Measure Estimation -> Fairness Objective Computation -> Policy Update -> (back to) Environment Dynamics

Critical Path: The algorithm iteratively estimates occupancy measures based on current policies, computes the fairness objective using these estimates and confidence bounds on environment parameters, then updates policies to improve fairness while maintaining performance guarantees.

Design Tradeoffs: The main tradeoff is between fairness and performance - more aggressive fairness constraints may lead to lower overall rewards. The choice of fairness function (α-fairness, proportional, or max-min) affects both the convergence rate and the final fairness-performance balance.

Failure Signatures: If confidence bounds are too loose, the algorithm may be overly conservative and fail to converge quickly. If the fairness function is too strict, the algorithm may get stuck in local optima with poor overall performance. Estimation errors in occupancy measures can propagate and degrade both fairness and performance.

## Limitations
- Assumes bounded unknown environment parameters, which may not hold in complex real-world scenarios
- Confidence interval construction may be conservative, potentially slowing convergence
- Effectiveness in high-dimensional state spaces requires further validation beyond proposed policy-gradient method
- Trade-off between fairness and performance may be too restrictive for some applications
