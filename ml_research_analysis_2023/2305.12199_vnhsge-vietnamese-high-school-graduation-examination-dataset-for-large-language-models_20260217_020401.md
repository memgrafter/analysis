# VNHSGE: VietNamese High School Graduation Examination Dataset for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.12199
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12199
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** VNHSGE dataset contains 19,000+ multiple-choice questions and 300 literature essays across 9 subjects to benchmark LLM capabilities

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the VNHSGE dataset, a comprehensive collection of Vietnamese high school graduation examination questions across nine subjects. The dataset contains over 19,000 multiple-choice questions and 300 literature essays, designed to evaluate large language models (LLMs) on various tasks such as question answering, text generation, and reading comprehension. Experiments using ChatGPT and BingChat on this dataset reveal that while these models perform at a human level in subjects like literature, English, history, geography, and civic education, they struggle with mathematics, physics, chemistry, and biology. The dataset aims to provide a robust benchmark for assessing LLM capabilities and promote further advancements in addressing their limitations in mathematics and natural sciences.

## Method Summary
The VNHSGE dataset was constructed by collecting official Vietnamese high school graduation examination questions from 2020-2022, converting them to LaTeX format, and structuring them into JSON and Word formats with detailed solutions. GPT-4/ChatGPT was used to translate the Vietnamese dataset (VNHSGE-V) into English (VNHSGE-E). The translated dataset was then used to evaluate GPT-3.5 (ChatGPT) and GPT-4 (BingChat) using standardized prompts with "pre-question" formatting. Performance was measured by accuracy metrics and compared against average Vietnamese student performance.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT and BingChat achieve human-level performance on literature, English, history, geography, and civic education subjects
- Both models struggle significantly with mathematics, physics, chemistry, and biology questions requiring complex computation
- The dataset successfully exposes LLM limitations in multi-step reasoning and domain-specific inference tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: VNHSGE's wide subject coverage and graded difficulty levels provide a robust benchmark for evaluating LLMs' general and specialized reasoning capabilities.
- Mechanism: By including questions ranging from knowledge recall to high-application reasoning across nine subjects, the dataset tests both breadth and depth of LLM performance, exposing limitations in complex computation and domain-specific inference.
- Core assumption: LLMs trained on large text corpora have sufficient baseline knowledge but struggle with domain-specific reasoning and multi-step problem solving.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The dataset assesses LLMs in multitasking situations such as question answering, text generation, reading comprehension, visual question answering, and more by including both textual data and accompanying images."
  - [section]: "The VNHSGE dataset presents a strong challenge to their mathematical aptitude in both breadth and depth."
- Break Condition: If future LLM architectures achieve human-level reasoning across all domains without domain-specific fine-tuning, the dataset's value as a differentiator would diminish.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Translation of the Vietnamese dataset into English using GPT-4/ChatGPT enables multilingual evaluation and accessibility.
- Mechanism: Translating VNHSGE-V to VNHSGE-E allows non-Vietnamese-speaking researchers to evaluate LLMs on a non-English language, testing cross-lingual understanding and generalization.
- Core assumption: GPT-4/ChatGPT are sufficiently accurate translators to preserve semantic and syntactic nuances required for educational assessment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "GPT-4/ChatGPT was used to translate VNHSGE-V into VNHSGE-E."
  - [section]: "According to earlier research [12], [62], and [63], GPT-4/ChatGPT can successfully serve as the appropriate translation engine in this circumstance."
- Break Condition: If translation errors significantly alter question semantics or difficulty, the benchmark's validity for non-Vietnamese users would be compromised.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The inclusion of detailed explanations and step-by-step solutions in the dataset trains LLMs to improve reasoning and answer derivation.
- Mechanism: Providing solutions alongside questions enables supervised learning of problem-solving processes, improving LLM's ability to generate correct and explainable answers.
- Core assumption: LLMs can learn to replicate structured reasoning from examples if solutions are detailed and logically ordered.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The dataset consists of 300 essays on Literature and 19,000 multiple-choice questions on other topics."
  - [section]: "The dataset includes questions and related solutions, which are supplied in a complete step-by-step solution."
- Break Condition: If LLMs fail to generalize reasoning patterns from provided solutions to unseen problems, the dataset's pedagogical value diminishes.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data collection -> LaTeX conversion -> JSON/Word formatting -> GPT-4/ChatGPT translation -> LLM evaluation engine -> Performance visualization

- Critical path:
  1. Collect raw exam data from Vietnamese Ministry of Education sources
  2. Convert symbols/formulas to LaTeX for compatibility
  3. Structure data into JSON/Word formats with explanations
  4. Translate dataset into English using GPT-4/ChatGPT
  5. Design evaluation prompts and scoring rubrics
  6. Run LLM experiments and collect results
  7. Analyze and visualize performance comparisons

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-language vs. multilingual dataset: Broader accessibility vs. translation fidelity
  - Detailed solutions vs. minimal answers: Better training vs. increased dataset size
  - JSON vs. Word formats: Machine readability vs. human usability

- Failure signatures:
  - Inconsistent JSON field formatting leading to parsing errors
  - Translation inaccuracies affecting question semantics
  - LLM responses not matching expected structured format
  - Missing or incorrect answer keys in dataset

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test JSON parsing and answer extraction accuracy with a small subset of questions
  2. Evaluate GPT-4/ChatGPT translation quality on a sample of Vietnamese questions
  3. Run a pilot LLM evaluation on 10 questions from each subject to validate scoring pipeline

## Limitations
- Translation quality from Vietnamese to English remains unverified and may affect question semantics
- Performance comparison to human students lacks statistical rigor and confidence intervals
- Dataset construction methodology for literature essays and visual interpretation questions lacks detailed documentation

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Dataset collection methodology and subject coverage are well-documented and verifiable. JSON/Word formatting specifications are clearly defined.
- **Medium Confidence**: Performance gaps between LLMs and human-level reasoning in mathematics and natural sciences are reported, but statistical significance is not established.
- **Low Confidence**: Translation quality assessment and claims about detailed solutions improving LLM reasoning are not directly validated within this work.
