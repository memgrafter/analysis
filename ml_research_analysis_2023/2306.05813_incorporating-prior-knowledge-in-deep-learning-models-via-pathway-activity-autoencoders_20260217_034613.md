# Incorporating Prior Knowledge in Deep Learning Models via Pathway Activity Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05813
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05813
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents a pathway activity-based deep learning framework that outperforms traditional autoencoders on cancer subtype classification using fewer features and parameters.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a pathway activity autoencoder (PAAE) framework that incorporates biological prior knowledge into deep learning models for cancer subtype classification. The method uses biochemical pathways to construct pathway activity encoders that produce interpretable intermediate representations before further compression into latent space. The PAAE framework outperforms traditional autoencoders despite using fewer features and parameters, achieving significantly better classification accuracy (ROC AUC of 0.914 vs 0.858) on breast cancer datasets. The pathway-constrained approach also enables meaningful interpretation of how specific pathways contribute to disease classification.

## Method Summary
The PAAE framework takes gene expression data as input and processes it through pathway-specific encoders that compress gene expression into pathway activity scores. These scores are concatenated into a pathway activity vector, which is then further compressed by a final encoder into latent space representation. The decoder reconstructs the original input from this compressed representation. The model is trained using reconstruction loss, with pathway activity scores providing an interpretable intermediate representation. The authors also develop a variational variant (PAVAE) and compare both approaches against standard autoencoders on TCGA BRCA and Metabric breast cancer datasets.

## Key Results
- PAAE achieves ROC AUC of 0.914 vs 0.858 for traditional autoencoders on breast cancer classification
- Pathway-constrained models use fewer features and parameters while achieving better performance
- Variational autoencoder variants may negatively impact performance compared to standard autoencoders
- Pathway activity scores provide interpretable intermediate representations that maintain class separability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Using pathway-based feature spaces improves classification performance even with fewer total features and parameters. Pathway activity encoders compress gene expression into interpretable biological scores, reducing dimensionality while preserving biologically meaningful variance. This constrained representation acts as a regularizer that prevents overfitting despite lower model complexity. The core assumption is that biochemical pathways capture relevant biological variance for the classification task and that this variance is more informative than raw gene expression for distinguishing cancer subtypes.

### Mechanism 2
Variational autoencoder variants may negatively impact performance compared to standard autoencoders in this domain. The KL divergence regularization in VAEs forces the latent space to follow a prior distribution, which may discard task-relevant information that is not well-represented by the assumed prior. This is particularly problematic when the biological signal is sparse or when the pathway constraints already provide sufficient regularization. The standard reconstruction loss is sufficient for capturing the relevant information for classification, and the additional KL regularization is unnecessary or harmful in this context.

### Mechanism 3
Pathway activity vectors provide more interpretable intermediate representations that maintain class separability. The pathway activity space directly maps to known biological processes, allowing visualization of how specific pathways contribute to sample classification. This interpretability doesn't come at the cost of performance, as shown by the maintained or improved ROC AUC. The biological pathways used are well-curated and relevant to the disease under study, and the pathway activity scores computed are meaningful representations of the underlying biology.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input (gene expression) → Pathway encoders (one per pathway, linear or nonlinear) → Pathway activity vector (concatenated outputs) → Final encoder → Latent space → Decoder → Output (reconstruction)
- **Critical path**: The pathway activity vector is the key intermediate representation that enables both interpretability and performance. Ensuring these encoders learn meaningful pathway activities is critical.
- **Design tradeoffs**: More pathway encoders increase interpretability but also parameter count; nonlinear pathway encoders may capture more complex relationships but reduce interpretability; VAE variants add generative modeling capability but may hurt discriminative performance.
- **Failure signatures**: Poor reconstruction quality indicates pathway encoders aren't capturing relevant information; lack of class separability in the latent space suggests the pathway representation isn't informative for the classification task; VAE variants diverging during training suggests the KL term is too strong.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train a baseline autoencoder on the same data to establish performance without pathway constraints.
  2. Train the PAAE model with linear pathway encoders and compare reconstruction and classification performance.
  3. Train the PAAE model with nonlinear pathway encoders and assess the tradeoff between performance and interpretability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Why do the PAVAE models consistently perform better using pathway activity vectors (p) rather than the hidden representation (μ)? The paper shows PAVAE models achieved higher performance by using pathway activity vectors (p < 10^-3) instead of their hidden representation μ, and this was the best representation for PAVAE despite μ being optimized during training. This performance difference between representations remains unexplained.

### Open Question 2
What specific characteristics of gene expression data make vanilla variational autoencoders perform worse than standard autoencoders? While the paper identifies that VAEs negatively impact both reconstruction outputs and classification performance, it doesn't fully characterize the data-specific factors causing VAEs to underperform.

### Open Question 3
How transferable are the pathway activity scores across different cancer types and measurement platforms? The paper shows pathway activity scores work well for breast cancer classification across TCGA and Metabric datasets (different technologies), but doesn't test cross-cancer-type applicability or explore how platform differences affect score transferability.

## Limitations

- The study only validates the method on breast cancer datasets, limiting generalizability to other cancer types
- The pathway definitions used (KEGG and Hallmark sets) may not capture all relevant biological processes for cancer classification
- The comparison with variational autoencoder variants is based on limited experimentation with specific hyperparameters

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Performance improvement claims (ROC AUC 0.914 vs 0.858) due to clear numerical comparisons and statistical significance
- **Medium confidence**: Interpretability claims, as pathway activity scores are meaningful but biological validation is limited to correlation with known pathways
- **Low confidence**: Claims about VAE variants being universally harmful, as this may depend heavily on specific hyperparameters and task requirements
