# A Multi-Purpose Audio-Visual Corpus for Multi-Modal Persian Speech Recognition: the Arman-AV Dataset

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.10180
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10180
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces Arman-AV, the first large-scale audio-visual corpus for Persian speech recognition

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Arman-AV, the first large-scale audio-visual corpus for Persian speech recognition. The dataset comprises nearly 220 hours of videos from 1760 speakers, collected from various sources including interviews, movies, and online videos. It is designed to support multiple tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR), audio-visual speech recognition (AVSR), lip reading, and speaker recognition. The authors provide baseline models for each task and introduce a novel method for detecting Persian visemes, which improves lip reading accuracy by 7% compared to previous approaches. The dataset is publicly available and includes detailed metadata such as speaker demographics and audio-video synchronization. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the dataset, with ASR achieving 84.66% character accuracy and AVSR showing improved performance by incorporating visual features. The proposed viseme mapping also enhances lip reading results. This work addresses the lack of large-scale audio-visual datasets for low-resource languages like Persian and provides a valuable resource for advancing speech recognition research.

## Method Summary
The authors developed Arman-AV by collecting and curating nearly 220 hours of Persian speech videos from diverse sources including interviews, movies, and online content. The dataset was manually annotated and segmented into utterances, with careful attention to audio-video synchronization. For each speech segment, they extracted audio features using standard acoustic models and visual features using lip region tracking and viseme mapping specifically designed for Persian phonetics. The viseme mapping was created by analyzing co-articulation patterns in Persian and grouping phonemes with similar lip movements. They trained separate models for ASR, AVSR, lip reading, and speaker recognition, using the same dataset splits to enable fair comparison. The AVSR models combined audio and visual features through early or late fusion strategies, while lip reading used only visual information. Baseline results were established for each task using standard evaluation metrics.

## Key Results
- ASR achieves 84.66% character accuracy on the Arman-AV test set
- AVSR models outperform audio-only ASR by incorporating visual features
- The Persian-specific viseme mapping improves lip reading accuracy by 7% compared to previous approaches
- The dataset enables multiple speech-related tasks including speaker recognition with competitive performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dataset's effectiveness stems from its large scale (220 hours, 1760 speakers) and diversity in speakers, speaking styles, and recording conditions. The Persian-specific viseme mapping captures co-articulation patterns unique to the language, enabling better visual speech recognition than generic phoneme-to-viseme mappings. Multi-modal fusion in AVSR leverages complementary information from audio and visual channels, with visual features providing robustness in noisy environments. The careful curation of metadata and synchronization ensures reliable training and evaluation. The dataset's coverage of multiple speech tasks from a single source enables transfer learning and joint optimization across modalities.

## Foundational Learning

**Audio-Visual Speech Recognition (AVSR)**
*Why needed:* Combines audio and visual speech cues to improve recognition accuracy, especially in noisy environments
*Quick check:* Can recognize speech when audio is corrupted but visual lip movements are clear

**Viseme Detection**
*Why needed:* Groups phonemes with similar visual appearance to reduce visual ambiguity in lip reading
*Quick check:* Maps multiple phonemes to single viseme labels based on lip shape patterns

**Persian Phonetics**
*Why needed:* Understanding language-specific co-articulation patterns for accurate viseme mapping
*Quick check:* Identifies which Persian phonemes share visual characteristics in speech production

**Speaker Diarization**
*Why needed:* Segments audio by speaker identity to enable speaker-specific modeling
*Quick check:* Assigns speaker labels to different segments in multi-speaker recordings

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Raw Video -> Pre-processing & Synchronization -> Audio Feature Extraction -> Visual Feature Extraction -> Viseme Mapping -> Task-Specific Models

**Critical Path:**
The most critical processing path is: Video Processing → Audio/Visual Feature Extraction → Model Fusion (for AVSR) → Recognition Output. This path determines the final recognition accuracy and must maintain high-quality synchronization and feature alignment.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The dataset prioritizes speaker diversity and natural speech over controlled recording conditions, trading some acoustic quality for better generalization. The viseme mapping specifically optimized for Persian may limit cross-linguistic applicability but improves performance for the target language.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor audio-video synchronization leads to degraded AVSR performance
- Insufficient speaker diversity causes overfitting to specific accents or speaking styles
- Generic viseme mappings fail to capture Persian-specific co-articulation patterns
- Inadequate visual feature quality (e.g., occlusion, low resolution) limits lip reading accuracy

## Limitations
- Geographic and demographic bias with speakers primarily from Iran limits dialect and accent coverage
- Claims about broader applicability to other low-resource languages need validation
- Dataset may not generalize well to all Persian dialects and accents
- 84.66% character accuracy metric alone may not capture performance on challenging acoustic conditions

## Confidence
- Claim of being "first large-scale audio-visual corpus for Persian": High
- Technical contributions (viseme detection method, baseline models): High
- Claims about broader applicability to other low-resource languages: Medium
- Generalizability of results to all Persian speakers: Medium
