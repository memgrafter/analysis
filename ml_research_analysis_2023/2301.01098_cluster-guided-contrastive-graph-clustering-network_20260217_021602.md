# Cluster-guided Contrastive Graph Clustering Network

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01098
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01098
- Reference count: 11
- The paper addresses the challenge of constructing reliable positive and negative sample pairs in contrastive graph clustering.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of constructing reliable positive and negative sample pairs in contrastive graph clustering. Existing methods often rely on complex graph data augmentations or well-pretrained models, which can lead to semantic drift or unreliable sample pairs. To overcome these limitations, the authors propose Cluster-guided Contrastive deep Graph Clustering network (CCGC). CCGC employs un-shared Siamese encoders to construct two views of the graph, avoiding complex augmentations and semantic drift. Guided by high-confidence clustering information, CCGC carefully selects positive samples from the same high-confidence cluster in two views and uses the centers of different high-confidence clusters as negative samples. This approach improves the discriminative capability and reliability of the sample pairs. Extensive experiments on six benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of CCGC compared to existing state-of-the-art algorithms, with significant improvements in clustering performance across various metrics.

## Method Summary
CCGC employs un-shared Siamese encoders to construct two views of the graph, avoiding complex augmentations and semantic drift. Guided by high-confidence clustering information, CCGC carefully selects positive samples from the same high-confidence cluster in two views and uses the centers of different high-confidence clusters as negative samples. This approach improves the discriminative capability and reliability of the sample pairs. The model uses K-means clustering on fused embeddings to generate pseudo-labels and confidence scores, which guide the construction of positive and negative sample pairs for contrastive learning.

## Key Results

- The approach shows significant improvements in clustering performance across various metrics.
- CCGC avoids semantic drift caused by inappropriate graph data augmentations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using un-shared Siamese encoders avoids semantic drift caused by inappropriate graph augmentations.
- Mechanism: Instead of perturbing edges or attributes to create two views, the model uses two separate encoders with distinct parameters. This inherently generates two semantically different node embeddings from the same input without introducing noise or drift.
- Core assumption: Parameter asymmetry in Siamese encoders is sufficient to produce diverse yet meaningful views for contrastive learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "instead of conducting complex node or edge perturbation, we construct two views of the graph by designing special Siamese encoders whose weights are not shared between the sibling sub-networks"
  - [section] "we construct two node views with different semantics... thus avoiding semantic drift caused by inappropriate graph data augmentations"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations supporting parameter unsharing as a general substitute for augmentation.
- Break condition: If the encoders collapse to similar representations due to identical architectures or optimization pressures, the contrastive signal weakens.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Selecting positive samples from the same high-confidence cluster across views improves their discriminative quality.
- Mechanism: After clustering the fused embeddings, nodes with high confidence scores are grouped. Positive pairs are formed by matching nodes from the same cluster in the two views, guided by pseudo-labels from the clustering step.
- Core assumption: High-confidence clustering pseudo-labels are accurate enough to serve as reliable supervision for contrastive learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "guided by the high-confidence clustering information, we carefully select and construct the positive samples from the same high-confidence cluster in two views"
  - [section] "we carefully select and construct the positive samples from the same cluster in two views according to high-confidence clustering pseudo labels"
  - [corpus] Weak - no explicit comparison with other pseudo-label selection strategies in the cited works.
- Break condition: If clustering quality is poor (e.g., wrong number of clusters), pseudo-labels become unreliable and propagate errors into contrastive learning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using cluster centers as negative samples increases reliability and reduces false negatives.
- Mechanism: For each cluster, the mean embedding in both views is computed. These centers from different clusters form negative pairs, ensuring negatives are semantically distinct.
- Core assumption: Cluster centers represent meaningful cluster boundaries and are good proxies for contrasting samples from different clusters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "to construct semantic meaningful negative sample pairs, we regard the centers of different high-confidence clusters as negative samples"
  - [section] "we regard the centers of different high-confidence clusters as the negative sample pairs, which are more reliable and semantic meaningful"
  - [corpus] Weak - no comparative ablation of negative sampling strategies using cluster centers.
- Break condition: If clusters are imbalanced or overlapping, centers may not represent cluster boundaries well, leading to noisy negatives.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input: Graph G = (X, A) with node attributes and adjacency -> Preprocessing: Laplacian smoothing → smoothed attributes -> Encoder block: Two un-shared MLPs (Enc1, Enc2) producing Ev1, Ev2 -> Fusion: Average embeddings E = (Ev1 + Ev2)/2 -> Clustering: K-means on E → pseudo-labels + confidence scores -> Sample selection: High-confidence nodes → DPS (same cluster pairs), RNS (different cluster centers) -> Loss: Lpos (MSE between matched positives), Lneg (cosine similarity between negative centers), L = Lpos + α·Lneg -> Output: Final cluster assignment from K-means on learned E
- Critical path: Input → Smoothing → Dual encoders → Fusion → Clustering → Sample selection → Loss → Updated embeddings → Clustering
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Un-shared encoders vs. shared encoders: Un-shared encoders avoid augmentation-induced drift but may increase parameter count and training complexity.
  - High-confidence filtering vs. full graph: Filtering reduces noise in supervision but may discard informative samples.
  - Cluster center negatives vs. random negatives: Centers are semantically meaningful but sensitive to cluster quality.
- Failure signatures:
  - Training loss plateaus early: Likely collapse of encoder representations or poor negative selection.
  - Cluster accuracy remains near random: Clustering confidence may be too low or K is mis-specified.
  - Large variance across runs: Hyperparameter τ (confidence threshold) or α (loss balance) may be unstable.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify encoder diversity: Train with shared vs. un-shared encoders on a small graph and compare embedding cosine similarity.
  2. Test sample selection: Run clustering with τ = 0 (all samples) vs. τ = 0.7 and measure impact on contrastive loss and clustering.
  3. Ablate negative sampling: Replace center-based negatives with random negatives and compare clustering metrics.

## Limitations
- The approach relies heavily on high-confidence clustering pseudo-labels, yet the paper provides limited analysis of clustering quality metrics and their impact on contrastive sample reliability.
- The use of cluster centers as negative samples, while theoretically sound, lacks ablation studies showing performance degradation when clusters are imbalanced or overlapping.
- The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the sensitivity of CCGC to different hyperparameters, which could affect the performance of the algorithm.
