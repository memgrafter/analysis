# Trustworthy Deep Learning for Medical Image Segmentation
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.17456
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17456
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Proposes three methods—label-set losses, hardness-weighted DRO, and a Dempster‑Shafer trustworthy AI framework—that improve segmentation robustness (reducing worst‑case Dice IQR by up to 2%) and expert‑assessed trustworthiness on a large multi‑centric fetal brain MRI dataset.

## Executive Summary
This thesis tackles the lack of robustness and trust in deep learning for medical image segmentation, particularly for fetal brain 3D MRI, where data variability, hidden stratification, and partial annotations hinder reliable deployment. It introduces three methodological contributions: (1) label‑set loss functions that enable training with partially labeled images; (2) hardness‑weighted sampling for distributionally robust optimization (DRO) that optimizes worst‑case performance; and (3) a Dempster‑Shafer‑based framework that combines a deep network with a registration‑based fallback and anatomical/intensity priors to automatically detect and handle unreliable predictions. Evaluated on the largest multi‑centric fetal brain MRI dataset (540 volumes, 13 sites), the methods enhance segmentation accuracy across subpopulations, reduce performance variability, and increase trustworthiness metrics as rated by experts. Additional contributions include the first spatio‑temporal fetal brain atlas for spina bifida aperta and an analysis of manual segmentation variability.

## Method Summary
The work addresses three core challenges: (i) learning from partially labeled data, (ii) ensuring robustness to distributional shifts and hidden stratification, and (iii) building trustworthy AI that signals when it is unreliable.

1.  **Label‑set loss functions:** Extend standard losses (Dice, cross‑entropy) by marginalizing over label‑sets (e.g., a voxel annotated as “WM or GM”). For a voxel with label‑set *g*, predicted probabilities for labels in *g* are averaged, and others remain unchanged. This allows training on images where only a subset of tissue classes are annotated, improving accuracy with limited annotations.
2.  **Hardness‑weighted sampling for DRO:** Replaces empirical risk minimization (ERM) with a DRO objective that minimizes worst‑case loss over a set of distributions. The sampler assigns higher probability to volumes with higher *stale* loss values (loss computed from a fixed backbone network). The loss is computed using a ϕ‑divergence (KL) between the sample weight distribution and a uniform reference, with theoretical convergence guarantees.
3.  **Dempster‑Shafer (DS) trustworthy AI framework:** Combines a backbone nnU‑Net with a multi‑atlas fallback segmentation. For each voxel, DS theory combines basic probability assignments (BPAs) from: (a) the AI’s softmax probability, (b) the fallback probability, (c) an anatomical prior (dilated atlas masks), and (d) an intensity prior (Gaussian mixture model for CSF). When the AI’s prediction contradicts the priors, the combination rules shift belief toward the fallback, effectively switching to the more trustworthy output.

Experiments use 3D T2‑weighted fetal brain MRI (multi‑centric, FeTA, BraTS), with primary metrics: Dice score (DSC), Hausdorff distance at 95th percentile (HD95), interquartile range (IQR) of metrics across subgroups, and expert Likert scores (0–5) for anatomical plausibility.

## Key Results
- Hardness‑weighted DRO reduces the IQR of worst‑case Dice scores by up to 2% on fetal brain MRI and BraTS, indicating improved robustness across pathologies, gestational ages, and acquisition centers.
- The DS framework significantly increases expert‑assessed trustworthiness scores on the largest multi‑centric fetal brain dataset while maintaining or improving segmentation accuracy.
- The work produced the first spatio‑temporal fetal brain atlas for spina bifida aperta and documented high inter‑rater variability in manual fetal brain segmentations, highlighting the need for standardized protocols.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanisms behind each contribution are:

- **Label‑set losses:** The loss function marginalizes over label‑sets by averaging the network's predicted probabilities for all labels within a given set (e.g., {WM, GM}) and comparing this aggregated prediction to a uniform distribution over the set. *Assumption:* The label‑set accurately reflects the annotator's uncertainty; an incorrect set could mislead learning. This mechanism allows training on partial annotations, effectively increasing data utility and reducing annotation bias. *Unknown:* The impact of label‑set granularity on performance in highly ambiguous regions.

- **Hardness‑weighted DRO:** The sampler maintains a stale loss for each training volume computed from a fixed backbone network. Volumes with higher stale loss are sampled more frequently. The training objective minimizes the worst‑case expected loss over a ball of distributions defined by a KL‑divergence constraint. *Assumption:* Stale loss is a reliable proxy for historical hardness; this may not hold for all distribution shifts. *Unknown:* The sensitivity to the β hyperparameter and the stale loss update frequency, which are underspecified in the paper and may cause instability.

- **Dempster‑Shafer framework:** Multiple evidence sources (AI softmax, multi‑atlas fallback, anatomical prior via dilated masks, intensity prior via GMM) are combined using Dempster's rule. Each source assigns basic probability masses to tissue classes and to an "unknown" category. When the AI's prediction conflicts with the priors (e.g., high GM probability in a region typically CSF), the combination rule shifts belief toward the fallback. *Assumption:* The priors are reliable indicators of normal anatomy; this may degrade on severe pathologies. *Unknown:* Robustness to registration errors and parameter η (margin) across diverse datasets.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partially Labeled Training:** Necessary to reduce the extensive time required for full manual segmentation of 3D medical volumes. *Quick check:* Verify that label‑set Dice loss produces valid gradients and matches full‑supervision Dice when all labels are present on a small synthetic dataset.
- **Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO):** Addresses hidden stratification by optimizing for the worst‑case loss over a ball of distributions, rather than the average. *Quick check:* Implement DRO on a toy dataset with known subgroups (e.g., two Gaussian clusters with different means); confirm that worst‑group accuracy improves compared to ERM.
- **Dempster‑Shafer Theory:** Provides a mathematical framework for combining multiple uncertain information sources (AI, fallback, priors) and explicitly modeling conflict and ignorance. *Quick check:* Construct a simple example with two conflicting sources (e.g., AI says “WM”, prior says “GM”); apply DS rule and verify that belief mass is allocated to the uncertain “unknown” set rather than forcing a contradictory decision.
- **Multi‑Atlas Segmentation:** Serves as a robust, anatomy‑driven fallback that is less data‑dependent than deep learning. *Quick check:* Register 3–5 atlases to a test image using a simple algorithm; compute majority‑vote segmentation and its Dice against manual reference to establish a performance baseline.
- **Anatomical Priors:** Incorporate domain knowledge (e.g., tissues occupy specific spatial relationships) to constrain segmentations and detect anomalies. *Quick check:* Dilate atlas masks to create prior regions; measure the rate at which AI segmentations violate these priors on normal vs. pathological cases.
- **Inter‑Rater Variability Analysis:** Quantifies the inherent uncertainty in manual annotations, which affects ground truth quality and model evaluation. *Quick check:* Calculate Dice scores between 2–3 manual raters on a subset of images; report mean and standard deviation as a measure of variability.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:**  
Raw fetal MRI -> Brain extraction (MONAIfbs) -> 3D reconstruction (NiftyMIC) -> Alignment to atlas (NiftyMIC) -> Backbone nnU‑Net -> [Label‑set loss / Hardness sampler / DS framework] -> Final segmentation

**Critical path:**  
1. Preprocessing (brain extraction, reconstruction, alignment) is the most labor‑intensive step and requires careful quality control.  
2. Training the backbone nnU‑Net with either label‑set losses or the DRO sampler is standard but requires hyperparameter tuning (e.g., β for DRO).  
3. For the DS framework, an additional step is building and registering the multi‑atlas set, and tuning anatomical margins η.  
4. Inference with DS combination adds computational overhead due to registration and evidence combination.

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- *Label‑set losses* vs. full supervision: Gains in annotation efficiency but potential loss of information if partial labels are very sparse or biased.  
- *DRO sampler* vs. ERM: Better worst‑case robustness at the cost of higher computational overhead (stale loss management) and sensitivity to the β hyperparameter; may destabilize training if β is too large.  
- *DS framework* vs. standalone AI: Increased trust and safety via fallback, but slower inference due to non‑linear registration and DS combination; performance hinges on fallback atlas quality and prior definitions.

**Failure signatures:**  
- **Out‑of‑memory** during 3D U‑Net training; reduce patch size or batch size.  
- **DRO divergence**: if β is too high, sampling concentrates on a few outliers, causing oscillations. Monitor per‑volume loss distribution and sampling probabilities.  
- **Registration failure**: poor alignment yields inaccurate fallback; visually inspect a sample of registered atlases or compute target registration error on landmarks.  
- **DS contradiction**: if AI and fallback probabilities completely conflict with priors, Dempster’s rule can yield an empty‑set mass; ensure anatomical priors are sufficiently broad (via dilation) and that fallback probabilities are smoothed.

**Three first experiments:**  
1. Label‑set loss reproduction: Train a 3D U‑Net on the FeTA dataset with 30% of volumes partially labeled (e.g., only white matter and cortical gray matter). Compare mean Dice on the test set against a fully supervised baseline using the same backbone.  
2. DRO sampler sensitivity: Integrate HardnessWeightedSampler into nnU‑Net on the BraTS dataset. Sweep β ∈ {10, 100, 1000} and plot the IQR of Dice scores across tumor subclasses (enhancing, non‑enhancing, necrotic) over training epochs.  
3. DS margin robustness: Using a fixed trained backbone and pre‑built atlases, vary the anatomical margin η by ±20% from the default (computed as HDFN_95) on a spina bifida subset. Record (a) expert trustworthiness scores (if available) or a proxy metric (e.g., rate of fallback activation), and (b) mean Dice of final output.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1.  **Robustness to unseen subpopulations:** How can we guarantee robustness for subpopulations absent from the training dataset? DRO can only reweight existing samples and fails on completely unseen conditions. Resolution requires domain generalization techniques or synthetic data.
2.  **Interactive expert feedback:** Can interactive expert feedback be incorporated into the trustworthy AI framework to reduce errors and improve trust? The thesis proposes semi‑automatic segmentation but does not explore how expert corrections affect the theoretical guarantees of DRO and Dempster‑Shafer.
3.  **Generalization to other domains:** Does the proposed trustworthy AI approach extend to other medical imaging domains (e.g., adult brain MRI, cardiac imaging)? The contracts of trust, fallback atlases, and priors are tailored to fetal brain anatomy.
4.  **Standardized labeling protocol:** How can a standardized labeling protocol for fetal brain MRI be established to reduce inter‑rater variability? Current manual segmentations show large differences, harming ground truth quality and model reproducibility.

## Limitations
- **DRO sampler dynamics** are underspecified (exact stale‑loss update frequency and decay), risking instability and unclear convergence behavior in practice.
- **Label‑set loss practicality** lacks details on creating consistent label‑sets across eight tissue classes and diverse pathologies, which could limit real‑world adoption.
- **DS framework parameter sensitivity** (anatomical margins η) is data‑fitted; stability across registration errors, pathologies, and sites is unknown.
- **Trustworthiness metric validity** relies on subjective expert Likert scores without established correlation to clinical safety or patient outcomes.
- **Computational burden** of the full pipeline (registration, fallback) is not quantified relative to standalone inference, hindering assessment of clinical feasibility.
