# From Explicit Communication to Tacit Cooperation:A Novel Paradigm for Cooperative MARL

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14656
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14656
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed TACO method enables transition from explicit communication to tacit cooperation in MARL, achieving performance comparable to or surpassing QMIX and communication-based methods without explicit communication during execution.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of fostering effective cooperation in cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) under partial observability, where lack of information sharing limits performance. Inspired by human team learning, the authors propose a novel paradigm called TACO (From Explicit Communication to Tacit Cooperation). TACO enables a gradual transition from explicit communication to tacit cooperation during training. Initially, agents share relevant information and reconstruct it using local trajectories, obtaining mixed information. As training progresses, the reliance on explicit communication is reduced, enabling fully decentralized execution without communication. Experiments on StarCraft II Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC), SMACv2, and Google Research Football (GRF) demonstrate that TACO achieves performance comparable to or even surpassing that of QMIX and communication-based methods without explicit communication. The method effectively balances practicality and effectiveness in MARL.

## Method Summary
The TACO method introduces a novel paradigm for cooperative MARL that gradually transitions from explicit communication to tacit cooperation during training. The approach involves two key phases: initially, agents share relevant information and reconstruct it using local trajectories, obtaining mixed information. As training progresses, the reliance on explicit communication is reduced, enabling fully decentralized execution without communication. The method employs a reconstruct loss weight (β) to control the transition, allowing agents to learn to cooperate effectively without relying on explicit communication during execution. This approach addresses the challenge of partial observability in MARL by enabling agents to learn cooperation patterns that can be executed in a decentralized manner.

## Key Results
- TACO achieves performance comparable to or surpassing QMIX and communication-based methods on SMAC, SMACv2, and GRF benchmarks
- The method enables fully decentralized execution without explicit communication during inference
- TACO effectively balances practicality and effectiveness in MARL by gradually transitioning from explicit communication to tacit cooperation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The TACO method works by gradually transitioning agents from relying on explicit communication to learning tacit cooperation patterns. Initially, agents share relevant information and reconstruct it using local trajectories, obtaining mixed information. This process allows agents to learn cooperation patterns based on shared information. As training progresses, the reliance on explicit communication is reduced by adjusting the reconstruct loss weight (β). This forces agents to learn to cooperate effectively using only their local observations and the tacit cooperation patterns they have learned. The gradual transition allows agents to develop robust cooperation strategies that can be executed in a decentralized manner without explicit communication during inference.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the TACO method's performance vary across different types of multi-agent relative patterns (e.g., attention weight, distance, velocity)?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions different types of multi-agent relative patterns but only analyzes attention weights in detail.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses solely on attention weights, leaving the effectiveness of TACO with other patterns unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing TACO's performance using different types of relative patterns across various multi-agent scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the reconstruct loss weight (β) on TACO's performance in different scenarios?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses varying β values but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of its impact across scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The paper only shows results for a few β values, without exploring the full range of its influence.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying β across a wide range of scenarios and analyzing the resulting performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the TACO method handle scenarios where agents have completely unrelated trajectories or observations?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions this as a potential limitation in the closing remarks.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any experiments or analysis to address this limitation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results demonstrating TACO's performance in scenarios with unrelated agent trajectories or observations, or theoretical analysis of the method's limitations in such cases.

## Limitations
- Evaluation scope is limited to specific cooperative benchmarks (SMAC, SMACv2, and GRF), which may not generalize to more complex or diverse multi-agent scenarios.
- The claim of achieving performance "comparable to or even surpassing" QMIX and communication-based methods requires careful scrutiny, as the paper does not provide detailed ablations showing whether improvements stem from the communication reduction mechanism or other factors in the architecture.
- Lack of analysis of computational overhead during the transition phase, which could be significant given the reconstruction of mixed information using local trajectories.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the technical feasibility of the transition mechanism, as the method is well-defined and reproducible.
- Medium confidence in the performance claims, as results are promising but limited to specific benchmarks without broader validation.
- Low confidence in the claim about "fully decentralized execution" since the paper does not thoroughly examine edge cases where communication might still be beneficial.
