# Is forgetting less a good inductive bias for forward transfer?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.08207
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08207
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Less forgetful representations in continual learning lead to better forward transfer, higher feature diversity, and improved discriminability.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the relationship between catastrophic forgetting and forward transfer in continual learning. The authors propose a new measure of forward transfer that evaluates how well representations learned on previous tasks can be used to learn new tasks by fixing representations and training temporary classifiers. Through experiments on several benchmarks, they demonstrate that representations with less forgetting lead to better forward transfer, suggesting a strong correlation between retaining past information and learning efficiency on new tasks. The paper also finds that less forgetful representations are more diverse and discriminative, providing insight into why reducing forgetting improves transfer capability.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a novel measure of forward transfer that evaluates continually learned representations independently from the training process. This involves fixing the learned feature extractor and training a temporary linear classifier on a small subset of data from a new task (k-shot learning). The authors implement six continual learning baselines including Finetuning, Experience Replay, AGEM, and FOMAML, testing them on benchmarks like Split CIFAR-10, Split CIFAR-100, and CLEAR variants. They evaluate average forgetting (AvgFgt), average forward transfer (AvgFwtk), and feature diversity (AvgFDiv) across multiple runs with different initialization schemes.

## Key Results
- Representations with less forgetting consistently achieve better forward transfer performance across all tested benchmarks
- Less forgetful representations show higher feature diversity and better discrimination between classes
- The relationship between forgetting and forward transfer holds for both randomly initialized and pre-trained models
- Feature diversity scores correlate with forward transfer performance, suggesting diversity as a key factor in transfer capability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Less forgetful representations lead to better forward transfer because they maintain higher feature diversity and discrimination.
- **Mechanism**: Continual learning methods that preserve performance on previous tasks produce feature representations with higher inter-class separation and lower intra-class variability. This diversity makes it easier to learn a classifier on top of these representations for new tasks.
- **Core assumption**: Feature diversity (measured by log-determinant of covariance matrices) is a good proxy for representational quality and transfer potential.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "Further, we found less forgetful representations to be more diverse and discriminative compared to their forgetful counterparts." [section] "We hypothesize that less forgetful representations maintain more diversity and discrimination in the features making it easy to learn a classifier head on top leading to better forward transfer."
- **Break condition**: If feature diversity scores don't correlate with transfer performance across different benchmarks and initialization schemes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Separating forward transfer evaluation from continual training updates decouples the tradeoff between forgetting and transfer.
- **Mechanism**: Traditional forward transfer measures (learning accuracy on a task after continual training) are confounded by the need to preserve previous knowledge. By fixing representations and only training a temporary classifier, we measure pure representational transfer capability.
- **Core assumption**: The ability to learn a new task from fixed representations is a more appropriate measure of forward transfer than end-to-end learning accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "We argue that the measure of forward transfer to a task should not be affected by the restrictions placed on the continual learner in order to preserve knowledge of previous tasks." [section] "We propose to use auxiliary evaluation of continually trained representations as a measure of forward transfer which is separate from the continual training of the model."
- **Break condition**: If fixed-representation evaluation doesn't correlate with end-to-end transfer performance in practical applications.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Less forgetful representations also retain better performance on upstream tasks, which contributes to forward transfer.
- **Mechanism**: Pre-trained models that forget less on both upstream (pre-training) and downstream tasks produce representations that transfer better to new tasks. This suggests that preserving "previous knowledge" broadly is beneficial.
- **Core assumption**: Upstream task performance retention is indicative of general representational quality that benefits downstream transfer.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "We further analyse the reasons of this better transferability and find that less forgetful models result in more diverse and easily separable representations making it easier to learn a classifier head on top." [section] "From the plot, it can be seen that LP-FT has retained better upstream performance (relatively speaking) compared to the other baselines."
- **Break condition**: If retaining upstream performance doesn't correlate with improved transfer to unrelated downstream tasks.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Catastrophic forgetting in neural networks
  - **Why needed here**: The paper's central claim is that reducing catastrophic forgetting improves forward transfer. Understanding what catastrophic forgetting is and why it occurs is fundamental to grasping the paper's contribution.
  - **Quick check question**: Why does standard SGD training lead to catastrophic forgetting when updating on new tasks?

- **Concept**: Feature diversity and discrimination metrics
  - **Why needed here**: The paper uses a specific metric (log-determinant of covariance matrices) to quantify feature diversity. Understanding how this metric works and what it measures is crucial for interpreting the results.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the feature diversity score (FDiv) mathematically measure inter-class separation and intra-class variability?

- **Concept**: K-shot learning and linear probing
  - **Why needed here**: The paper's novel forward transfer measure involves learning a classifier from k examples per class. Understanding k-shot learning and linear probing methodology is essential for implementing and validating the results.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the difference between k-shot linear probing and k-shot fine-tuning, and why might they yield different transfer results?

- **Design tradeoffs**: Feature extractor capacity vs. memory constraints: Larger models may retain more information but require more compute; Replay buffer size vs. forgetting: Larger buffers reduce forgetting but increase memory requirements; Number of training epochs per task vs. transfer: More epochs may improve task performance but increase forgetting
- **Failure signatures**: High AvgFgt with low AvgFwtk: Model is forgetting but not transferring effectively; Low AvgFgt with low AvgFwtk: Model is retaining knowledge but representations aren't diverse/discriminative; High AvgFDiv with low AvgFwtk: Feature diversity exists but isn't translating to transfer (possible negative transfer)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does the relationship between forgetting and forward transfer change when tasks are negatively related or actively interfere with each other? The paper mentions that negatively related tasks are rare but doesn't study this scenario explicitly.
- **Open Question 2**: How does the proposed measure of forward transfer (k-shot linear probing) compare to end-to-end forward transfer metrics in terms of correlation with practical continual learning performance? The paper doesn't provide a direct comparison between the proposed measure and end-to-end forward transfer metrics on the same benchmarks.
- **Open Question 3**: How does the amount of forgetting on upstream (pre-trained) tasks relate to forward transfer when the pre-trained model is used as initialization? The paper observes that less forgetting on both upstream and downstream tasks improves transferability of representations but doesn't provide a detailed analysis of this relationship.

## Limitations

- The positive correlation between forgetting reduction and forward transfer may not hold for negatively related tasks
- The k-shot linear probing metric may not fully capture end-to-end transfer performance in practical applications
- The feature diversity metrics used may be influenced by specific architectures and datasets rather than representing universal measures of representational quality

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The empirical observation that less forgetful methods achieve better forward transfer (AvgFwtk) on the tested benchmarks
- **Medium confidence**: The proposed k-shot linear probing measure as a cleaner evaluation of forward transfer
- **Medium confidence**: The interpretation that feature diversity and discrimination explain the transfer improvements

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset validation**: Test whether the positive correlation between forgetting reduction and forward transfer holds on more diverse datasets beyond CIFAR and ImageNet variants, particularly in domains with different feature distributions

2. **End-to-end transfer validation**: Compare the proposed k-shot linear probing metric against traditional end-to-end transfer learning accuracy to establish whether the cleaner evaluation correlates with practical transfer performance

3. **Alternative diversity metrics**: Validate whether the log-determinant feature diversity metric captures representational quality better than simpler metrics like inter-class variance or t-SNE-based separability
