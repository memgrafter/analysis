# MM-REACT: Prompting ChatGPT for Multimodal Reasoning and Action

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11381
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11381
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: System integrates ChatGPT with vision experts using text prompts to solve complex visual tasks, showing competitive performance vs. PaLM-E

## Executive Summary
MM-REACT is a novel system that enables ChatGPT to perform multimodal reasoning and action by integrating specialized vision experts through carefully crafted textual prompts. Rather than training a monolithic multimodal model, MM-REACT converts visual inputs into text representations and uses structured prompts with watchwords to invoke appropriate vision experts. The system demonstrates effectiveness across diverse tasks including multi-image reasoning, document understanding, concept recognition, and video analysis, all in a zero-shot manner without additional training.

The approach offers significant flexibility through its modular design, allowing for easy integration of new vision tools and components. By standardizing vision expert outputs as text, MM-REACT can leverage ChatGPT's powerful reasoning capabilities while maintaining the specialized strengths of domain-specific vision models. The system achieves competitive performance compared to joint fine-tuning approaches like PaLM-E, while providing a more adaptable framework for multimodal AI applications.

## Method Summary
The method involves converting images and videos into text placeholders, then using structured prompts containing watchwords to instruct ChatGPT to invoke specific vision experts. These experts (such as captioning, OCR, and face detection models) process the visual inputs and return text-formatted outputs that ChatGPT can understand. The system uses in-context examples and prompt prefixes to enhance ChatGPT's ability to coordinate multiple vision experts and perform multi-step reasoning. The modular architecture allows for easy integration of new tools without additional training, as long as their outputs can be serialized into text format that ChatGPT can process.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot experiments demonstrate MM-REACT's effectiveness across multiple visual understanding capabilities
- Competitive performance compared to PaLM-E on various multimodal tasks
- Flexible system architecture allows easy upgrades and incorporation of new tools without retraining
- Successful handling of complex scenarios including multi-image reasoning and video analysis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The system works by converting non-text inputs into text placeholders and instructing ChatGPT to invoke specific vision experts via structured prompts containing watchwords.
- **Mechanism:** When ChatGPT encounters a watchword like "Assistant, what objects do you see in this image?", it triggers execution of a corresponding vision expert. The expert's output is serialized as text and fed back into ChatGPT for further reasoning or final response generation.
- **Core assumption:** ChatGPT can reliably parse watchwords and execute multi-step reasoning chains when provided with structured prompt templates and in-context examples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "MM-REACT introduces a textual prompt design that can represent text descriptions, textualized spatial coordinates, and aligned file names for dense visual signals such as images and videos."
  - [section] "Inspired by the efficacy of reasoning and acting with LLMs and NLP tools, we explore the integration of vision expert tools with LLMs."
- **Break condition:** If ChatGPT fails to recognize watchwords or if vision expert outputs cannot be serialized into a format ChatGPT can parse, the system breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The system achieves competitive performance by leveraging existing specialized vision experts instead of training new multimodal models.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of training a monolithic multimodal model, MM-REACT composes multiple pre-trained vision experts with ChatGPT, allowing each expert to focus on its specific domain.
- **Core assumption:** Specialized vision experts are sufficiently accurate for the tasks at hand, and ChatGPT can effectively coordinate their outputs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Zero-shot experiments demonstrate MM-REACT's effectiveness in addressing the specified capabilities of interests and its wide application in different scenarios that require advanced visual understanding."
  - [section] "Figure 15-17 shows our MM-REACT can achieve competitive results to PaLM-E."
- **Break condition:** If any vision expert performs poorly or if coordination between experts becomes too complex for ChatGPT to handle, performance degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The system's extensibility allows for easy integration of new tools without additional training by standardizing outputs as text.
- **Mechanism:** New vision tools can be added by ensuring their outputs are formatted as text that ChatGPT can understand, enabling seamless integration into the existing reasoning pipeline.
- **Core assumption:** Text serialization of vision outputs preserves sufficient information for ChatGPT to make meaningful use of the data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Furthermore, we discuss and compare MM-REACT's system paradigm with an alternative approach that extends language models for multimodal scenarios through joint finetuning."
  - [section] "Figure 23 and 24, we explore the enhancement of MM-REACT's LLM from ChatGPT ('gpt-3.5-turbo') to GPT-4 (language-only)."
- **Break condition:** If text serialization loses critical information or if ChatGPT cannot process the standardized text format, the extensibility breaks down.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** User input → Text placeholder conversion → ChatGPT with prompt prefix → Watchword detection → Vision expert execution → Text output serialization → ChatGPT response generation → User output
- **Critical path:** User input → ChatGPT response with watchword → Vision expert execution → Text output → Final ChatGPT response
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Modularity vs. performance: Using specialized experts provides flexibility but may introduce coordination overhead
  - Text serialization vs. information loss: Converting visual outputs to text may lose spatial information
  - Prompt complexity vs. reliability: More complex prompts may improve performance but increase failure modes
- **Failure signatures:**
  - ChatGPT fails to recognize watchwords → No vision expert invocation
  - Vision expert returns unexpected format → ChatGPT cannot process output
  - Token limit exceeded → Incomplete reasoning chain
  - API rate limiting → System timeout
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test basic image captioning with a single image and simple prompt
  2. Test multi-expert coordination with two vision experts on one image
  3. Test watchword recognition with edge cases (misspellings, alternative phrasings)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the performance of MM-REACT be systematically evaluated given the lack of annotated benchmarks for many of its target tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper acknowledges that "it is hard to systematically evaluate the performance with concrete accuracy numbers, due to a lack of annotated benchmarks" and that "it is worth investing efforts towards how to effectively evaluate such system's performance."
- Why unresolved: The multimodal reasoning and action paradigm requires evaluation across diverse vision tasks, many of which lack standardized benchmarks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of comprehensive evaluation protocols that can measure both accuracy of individual vision expert outputs and effectiveness of LLM's reasoning and tool-selection strategies.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal way to represent multimodal information in text format to maximize ChatGPT's understanding while minimizing information loss?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "Visual signals are converted to text words for ChatGPT understanding, which might not be the optimal solution for certain vision tasks."
- Why unresolved: The current approach of textualizing visual information through file paths and coordinate-based descriptions is a pragmatic solution but may not be optimal for all types of visual reasoning tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies testing different text encoding strategies for multimodal information, measuring their impact on task performance across various vision domains.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the manual prompt engineering process for MM-REACT be automated to reduce development overhead and improve generalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that "MM-REACT requires manual prompt engineering" and "We expect research work to automate this process, making the system even easier to develop."
- Why unresolved: The current implementation relies on carefully crafted prompt prefixes that describe vision expert capabilities and provide in-context examples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of automated prompt generation methods that can effectively encode vision expert capabilities and examples without human intervention.

## Limitations
- Limited transparency on watchword effectiveness with no ablation studies on alternative prompting strategies
- Potential information loss in text serialization of visual outputs is not explicitly evaluated
- Performance bounds unknown with specific gaps and failure cases not detailed

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Core architectural design of using text-based prompts to coordinate vision experts with ChatGPT is well-supported
- **Medium confidence:** Claim of competitive performance compared to PaLM-E is supported by visual results but lacks detailed quantitative comparison
- **Low confidence:** Extensibility claims are largely theoretical without demonstrated examples of adding new vision experts
