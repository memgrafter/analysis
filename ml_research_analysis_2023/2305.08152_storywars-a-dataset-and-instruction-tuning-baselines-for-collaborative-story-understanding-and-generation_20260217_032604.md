# STORYWARS: A Dataset and Instruction Tuning Baselines for Collaborative Story Understanding and Generation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.08152
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08152
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Primary result**: Introduces STORY WARS dataset and benchmark with 101 tasks; proposes INSTRUCT STORY model achieving 1.53 point average improvement over single-task fine-tuning baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces STORY WARS, a comprehensive dataset of 40,000+ collaboratively-written stories paired with 101 tasks spanning story understanding and generation across fully-supervised, few-shot, and zero-shot settings. The authors propose INSTRUCT STORY, a two-stage model that first instruction-tunes on story tasks then fine-tunes per-task, outperforming single-task fine-tuning baselines by an average of 1.53 points. The work demonstrates that instruction tuning can enhance both zero-shot performance and fully-supervised results, establishing a new benchmark for collaborative story understanding and generation.

## Method Summary
The authors develop INSTRUCT STORY through a two-stage training approach: first instruction-tuning a T5-large-lm-adapt model on a diverse mix of 12 story understanding and generation task types, then performing single-task fine-tuning on each fully-supervised task. They create STORY WARS dataset from the STORYWARS website containing over 40,000 stories with corresponding tasks. The instruction templates are designed to capture various story-related capabilities, and the model is evaluated across zero-shot, few-shot, and fully-supervised learning scenarios using BERTScore and other metrics.

## Key Results
- INSTRUCT STORY achieves 1.53 point average improvement over single-task fine-tuned T5 baseline across all tasks
- Instruction tuning provides superior performance in zero-shot and few-shot scenarios while also enhancing fully-supervised results
- The model with mixed understanding and generation tasks (IS) outperforms variants with only understanding (ISU) or only generation (ISG) tasks
- INSTRUCT STORY scores higher than T5 baseline in every task type evaluated

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Instruction tuning improves both zero-shot and fully-supervised performance by providing task-agnostic generalization during pre-finetuning.
- Mechanism: By training on a diverse mix of story understanding and generation tasks, the model learns to adapt to various instruction formats, improving its ability to generalize to unseen tasks in zero-shot settings and serving as better initialization for single-task fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: Instruction tuning on task mix with similar distribution to target tasks leads to better downstream performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "instruction tuning, in addition to achieving superior results in zero-shot and few-shot scenarios, can also obtain the best performance on the fully-supervised tasks"
  - [section 4.1]: "INSTRUCT STORY scores higher than T5 in every task type"
- Break condition: If the instruction tuning task mix is too dissimilar from target tasks, the generalization benefit may not transfer.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The two-stage training approach (instruction tuning then single-task fine-tuning) provides performance gains over single-task fine-tuning alone.
- Mechanism: Instruction tuning serves as multitask pre-finetuning, providing better initialization for the subsequent single-task fine-tuning stage, leading to improved performance on fully-supervised tasks.
- Core assumption: Instruction tuning provides better initialization than standard pre-training for task-specific fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1]: "INSTRUCT STORY can achieve a 1.53 point increase in the overall average score compared to the single-task fine-tuned T5 baseline"
  - [abstract]: "when combined with single-task fine-tuning, Instruction Tuning can surpass single-task fine-tuning alone"
- Break condition: If the single-task fine-tuning stage causes catastrophic forgetting of the instruction-tuned generalization abilities.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Including both understanding and generation tasks in the instruction tuning stage is more effective than using only one type.
- Mechanism: The diverse task mix helps the model develop a more comprehensive understanding of story-related capabilities, leading to better overall performance across all task types.
- Core assumption: Multitask learning with diverse task types provides better generalization than single-task or limited-task instruction tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.4]: "IS > ISU > ISG > T5 across all zero-shot, few-shot, and fully-supervised learning scenarios"
  - [abstract]: "12 task types, including both understanding and generation task types"
- Break condition: If the task mix becomes too large or diverse, it may lead to optimization difficulties or interference between tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: STORY WARS dataset preparation → Task template design → Instruction tuning (T5-large-lm-adapt) → Single-task fine-tuning → Evaluation across 101 tasks
- **Critical path**: Prepare STORY WARS dataset → Design instruction templates for 12 task types → Perform instruction tuning on task mix → Fine-tune on individual tasks → Evaluate performance across all task types
- **Design tradeoffs**: Two-stage training trades increased training time and computational resources for improved performance across all learning scenarios. Instruction tuning enables better zero-shot/few-shot performance but risks catastrophic forgetting.
- **Failure signatures**: Ineffective instruction tuning shows no significant improvements in zero-shot/few-shot performance; catastrophic forgetting results in no improvement on fully-supervised tasks compared to instruction-tuned model.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Evaluate zero-shot performance of INSTRUCT STORY on STORY WARS tasks to verify instruction tuning effectiveness
  2. Compare performance with and without single-task fine-tuning stage on fully-supervised tasks
  3. Ablation study comparing IS (mixed tasks), ISU (understanding only), and ISG (generation only) variants

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can instruction tuning be effectively applied to improve fully-supervised performance in addition to zero-shot and few-shot scenarios?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper claims instruction tuning combined with single-task fine-tuning achieves best fully-supervised performance with 1.53 point average improvement, but it's unclear whether this improvement is consistent across all task types and generalizable to other datasets and models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation across different task types, datasets, and models would provide evidence for generalizability and effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the problem of catastrophic forgetting be mitigated when fine-tuning on a new task while preserving multitasking abilities and generalization capabilities?
- Basis in paper: Inferred
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions catastrophic forgetting as a potential issue and suggests techniques like Rehearsal, it doesn't empirically investigate this problem or evaluate these mitigation techniques in fully-supervised learning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research on effectiveness of techniques like Rehearsal in mitigating catastrophic forgetting in fully-supervised learning would provide evidence for feasibility.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the evaluation of collaborative story generation be improved beyond the use of automatic metrics like BERTScore?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges limitations of automatic evaluation metrics for story generation and notes the need for more nuanced methods that capture complexity and diversity of collaborative stories, but doesn't propose specific alternatives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of appropriate evaluation methods (human evaluation or other metrics) that capture complexity and diversity of collaborative stories would provide evidence for effectiveness.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to single base model architecture (T5-large-lm-adapt), unclear if results transfer to other models like GPT or LLaMA
- Focuses exclusively on English-language story tasks with no investigation of cross-lingual generalization
- Evaluation metrics predominantly accuracy-based without consideration for qualitative story quality dimensions like coherence or creativity
- Dataset characteristics not thoroughly analyzed, making generalizability to other collaborative storytelling datasets uncertain

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Instruction tuning provides superior zero-shot performance with consistent improvements across multiple task types
- **Medium Confidence**: Instruction tuning combined with single-task fine-tuning achieves best fully-supervised performance, but dependent on specific task mix and evaluation protocol
- **Low Confidence**: Two-stage training approach is universally superior to alternatives; catastrophic forgetting issue mentioned but not empirically investigated
