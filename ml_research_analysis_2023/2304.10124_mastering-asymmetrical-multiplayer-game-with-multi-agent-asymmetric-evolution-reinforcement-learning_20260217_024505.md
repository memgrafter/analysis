# Mastering Asymmetrical Multiplayer Game with Multi-Agent Asymmetric-Evolution Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10124
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10124
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: Multi-agent deep RL framework (AET) trained cat and mouse agents in Tom & Jerry game, achieving 98.5% mouse win rate against top human players.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel multi-agent deep reinforcement learning framework for training AI agents in asymmetrical multiplayer (AMP) games. The proposed asymmetric-evolution training (AET) method addresses the challenge of training agents with different roles simultaneously without using human data. By introducing adaptive data adjustment (ADA) and environment randomization (ER), the framework effectively manages the imbalance issues inherent in AMP games. The mouse AI achieved a remarkable 98.5% win rate against top human players over 65 matches, demonstrating top-level performance. Ablation experiments confirmed the effectiveness of ADA and ER modules, representing a significant advance in training AI for complex AMP games.

## Method Summary
The framework trains cat and mouse agents simultaneously using asymmetric-evolution training (AET) with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It employs adaptive data adjustment (ADA) to dynamically allocate sampler resources between agents and environment randomization (ER) to introduce controlled difficulty variations. The method uses separate policy and value networks for each agent type, with reward shaping tailored to asymmetric roles. Historical model pools with policy frequency soft play (pFSP) ensure diverse opponent variety during training.

## Key Results
- Mouse AI achieved 98.5% win rate against top human players over 65 matches
- ADA and ER modules significantly improved training stability and performance
- Framework successfully trained both cat and mouse agents simultaneously without human data
- Demonstrated effective handling of asymmetric game dynamics in complex multiplayer environment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Asymmetric-evolution training (AET) enables simultaneous training of agents with different roles without catastrophic imbalance.
- Mechanism: By maintaining a dynamic sampler resource ratio and employing environment randomization (ER), the framework prevents one agent type from dominating and stalling learning.
- Core assumption: The two agent types have sufficiently different learning dynamics that fixed resource allocation leads to imbalance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The mouse AI achieved a 98.5% win rate against top human players... ablation experiments confirmed the effectiveness of ADA and ER modules."
  - [section 3.3] "adaptive data adjustment (ADA) and environment randomization (ER) to optimize the AET process."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related works focus on symmetric games, not AMP-specific imbalance solutions.
- Break condition: If the game dynamics change such that both agents learn at similar rates, the adaptive resource allocation becomes unnecessary.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Reward shaping tailored to asymmetric roles prevents agents from exploiting easy subgoals at the expense of strategic learning.
- Mechanism: Asymmetric reward shaping includes ordinary, guidance, and team rewards, with guidance rewards decaying over time to avoid overfitting to fixed strategies.
- Core assumption: Without asymmetric shaping, agents will converge to degenerate strategies exploiting their role's strengths.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "Due to the characteristics of asymmetric game, two sets of reward shaping are required."
  - [section 3.2] "Parts of guidance reward will gradually decay during the training process which is designed to prevent the agent from overfitting to some fixed strategies."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; most related papers do not discuss asymmetric reward shaping in AMP contexts.
- Break condition: If reward shaping becomes too complex, it may obscure the true learning signal and slow convergence.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Historical model pool with weighted sampling via policy frequency soft play (pFSP) ensures diverse and challenging opponents.
- Mechanism: The win-rate-based weighting function f(x) = (1 - x)^p prioritizes models that the current model struggles against, preventing overfitting to specific strategies.
- Core assumption: Maintaining a sufficiently large and diverse pool of past models provides robust opponent variety.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "We use pFSP [Vinyals et al., 2019] to calculate the probability of choosing the history model from model pool."
  - [section 3.3] "The size of the HMP is 500 in this study... each model can be loaded on about 5 samplers."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; most related works focus on symmetric self-play, not asymmetric historical opponent sampling.
- Break condition: If the pool becomes too large relative to available compute, sampling efficiency drops and training slows.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Samplers -> Trainers -> Model Pool -> Evaluator Server -> ADA/ER Adjustments -> Log Server
- Critical path:
  1. Samplers generate trajectories → replay buffers
  2. Trainers sample batches → update policies via PPO
  3. Updated models deployed to model pool
  4. Evaluator tests models → ADA/ER adjustments
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed vs. adaptive sampler ratios: Adaptive prevents early imbalance but adds complexity
  - Historical model pool size: Larger pool increases diversity but slows sampling
  - Reward shaping complexity: More guidance rewards prevent degenerate behavior but risk overfitting
- Failure signatures:
  - Win rate imbalance > 80/20 persisting for >1M steps → ADA not working
  - Sudden performance drop after ER activation → too aggressive randomization
  - Training collapse → reward shaping too sparse or too dense
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with fixed 1:4 cat:mouse sampler ratio; measure imbalance onset
  2. Disable ER; observe whether the stronger agent forgets disadvantaged strategies
  3. Reduce historical model pool to 100; check if overfitting to recent strategies occurs

## Limitations
- Proprietary game environment restricts full reproducibility and independent validation
- Limited human player evaluation sample size (65 matches) may not capture full strategic diversity
- Framework tested only on single game scenario, raising questions about generalizability to other AMP games

## Confidence
- High confidence in AET framework's ability to train agents in AMP games
- Medium confidence in specific win rate claims against human players
- Low confidence in framework's generalizability beyond Tom & Jerry environment
