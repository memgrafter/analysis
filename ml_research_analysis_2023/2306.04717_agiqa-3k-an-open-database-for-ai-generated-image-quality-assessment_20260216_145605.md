# AGIQA-3K: An Open Database for AI-Generated Image Quality Assessment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04717
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04717
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Presents AGIQA-3K, a large-scale AGI subjective quality database with 2,982 images and 125,244 ratings, addressing the gap between current IQA models and human perception of AI-generated images.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces AGIQA-3K, a comprehensive database for evaluating the quality of AI-generated images (AGIs) that captures both perceptual quality and text-to-image alignment dimensions. The database contains 2,982 images generated from six different AGI models with fine-grained subjective scores from 21 participants. The authors conduct a benchmark experiment showing current IQA models struggle with AGI quality assessment, particularly for alignment evaluation. They propose StairReward, a novel metric that significantly improves alignment assessment by decomposing prompts into morphemes and images into spatial regions.

## Method Summary
The AGIQA-3K database was constructed by generating images from six representative AGI models (AttnGAN, DALLE2, GLIDE, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion v1.5, and Stable Diffusion XL) using a combination of real prompts from DiffusionDB and human-designed prompts. A subjective experiment was conducted following ITU-R BT.500-13 standards with 21 participants rating images on two dimensions: perceptual quality and text-to-image alignment using sliders (0-5 scale, 0.1 intervals). The authors then evaluated existing IQA metrics on this database and proposed StairReward, which improves alignment assessment by breaking prompts into morphemes and cutting images into stair-like regions for more precise one-to-one correspondence between prompt elements and image regions.

## Key Results
- AGIQA-3K provides fine-grained subjective scores capturing both perception and alignment quality dimensions
- Current IQA models show significant gaps in assessing AGI quality, particularly for text-to-image alignment
- StairReward improves alignment assessment performance by decomposing prompts into morphemes and images into spatial regions
- The database covers multiple AGI model types (GAN, auto-regressive, diffusion) providing comprehensive quality representation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: AGIQA-3K bridges the gap between IQA models and human perception for AGIs
- Mechanism: Collects detailed MOS at both perceptual quality and alignment levels from multiple raters, creating ground truth that captures nuanced differences across models, prompts, and parameters
- Core assumption: Human perception of AGI quality is multi-dimensional and varies significantly across different generation models and prompt configurations
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] states "fine-grained subjective scores" for "both perception and alignment levels"; [section] describes collecting MOS at "Perception and Alignment level through two sliders"
- Break condition: If subjective scores don't capture meaningful differences between AGI models or two-dimensional assessment doesn't correlate with user preferences

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: StairReward improves alignment assessment through morpheme-image correspondence
- Mechanism: Breaks prompts into individual morphemes and cuts images into stair-like regions, creating one-to-one correspondence for more precise alignment scoring
- Core assumption: Different morphemes correspond to different spatial regions, with earlier morphemes typically corresponding to more central regions
- Evidence anchors: [section] describes prompt segmentation using prepositions and punctuation as delimiters; [section] explains image cutting strategy using boxes of varying sizes based on morpheme position
- Break condition: If morpheme-image correspondence doesn't hold consistently across different prompt types or spatial segmentation fails to capture relevant regions

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Database coverage of multiple AGI model types provides comprehensive quality representation
- Mechanism: Includes AGIs from six different models across GAN, auto-regressive, and diffusion categories, capturing quality variations across different generation paradigms
- Core assumption: Different AGI model architectures produce systematically different quality characteristics requiring representation in assessment datasets
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] states database considers "various popular AGI models" from different technical routes; [section] describes inclusion of "six representative generative models" from different categories
- Break condition: If certain model types dominate disproportionately or quality differences between models aren't meaningful for assessment

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a more accurate alignment quality model for text-to-image generation that performs well across different AGI models, prompt lengths, and artistic styles?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that current alignment models have a "significant gap" compared to perception assessment tasks and that their performance degrades for long prompts and popular styles. The authors propose StairReward as an improvement but acknowledge more work is needed.
- Why unresolved: Current alignment metrics rely on whole-prompt matching rather than breaking down prompts into individual morphemes, failing to capture nuanced alignment quality humans perceive
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study comparing morpheme-level alignment models against human subjective ratings across diverse AGI outputs, prompt structures, and artistic styles with improved Spearman correlation coefficients

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we develop perception quality models that maintain consistent performance across different AGI model qualities rather than showing significant performance degradation for lower-quality outputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Benchmark results show DL-based perception models achieve good overall SRoCC (~0.8) but performance drops substantially for "bad model" subsets (SRoCC ~0.5) compared to "good model" subsets
- Why unresolved: Current perception models are trained primarily on natural images and high-quality AGI outputs, making them poorly calibrated for diverse quality range in AGI outputs
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and validation of perception models specifically on curated datasets spanning full quality spectrum of AGI outputs with ablation studies showing improved consistency across quality levels

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal approach for collecting and curating prompts that truly represent real-world user inputs for AGI quality assessment?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Authors acknowledge using "real" prompts extracted from DiffusionDB as framework combined with human-designed elements, but don't empirically validate whether this captures full diversity of actual user prompts or creates sampling bias
- Why unresolved: Paper doesn't provide validation that prompt collection methodology (real + human-designed) represents true distribution of user inputs or could affect generalizability of quality assessments
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of prompt distributions between AGIQA-3K and actual user prompt logs from major AGI platforms, along with validation studies showing quality assessments generalize to independently collected user-generated content

## Limitations
- Dataset coverage may not fully represent the rapidly evolving AGI landscape with only six models
- Subjective ratings from only 21 participants may limit statistical robustness for certain assessment tasks
- Prompt selection mechanism might introduce sampling bias affecting generalizability of quality assessment results

## Confidence
**High Confidence Claims**:
- Database construction methodology and subjective experiment procedures are well-documented and follow established standards
- Multi-dimensional quality assessment approach addresses real gaps in current AGI quality evaluation
- Benchmark results showing correlations between subjective scores and objective metrics are reproducible

**Medium Confidence Claims**:
- StairReward's performance improvement over existing alignment metrics
- Representativeness of six AGI models for broader AGI ecosystem
- Sufficiency of 21 participants for reliable subjective quality assessment

**Low Confidence Claims**:
- Generalizability of alignment assessment results to prompts and models not included in AGIQA-3K
- Long-term validity of database as AGI models continue to evolve
- Optimal number of spatial regions for image segmentation in StairReward

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Model Validation**: Test StairReward's alignment assessment performance on AGIs from models not included in AGIQA-3K (e.g., newer diffusion models or fine-tuned variants) to verify its generalizability across different AGI architectures.

2. **Participant Diversity Study**: Conduct a follow-up subjective experiment with a more diverse participant pool (varying demographics, cultural backgrounds, and technical expertise) to assess the robustness of MOS scores and identify potential cultural or demographic biases in quality perception.

3. **Temporal Stability Analysis**: Re-administer the subjective experiment to the same participants after a 6-12 month interval to evaluate the stability of quality ratings over time and assess whether the database captures consistent quality factors or is subject to temporal perception changes.
