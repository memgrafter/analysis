# Fine-Grained Product Classification on Leaflet Advertisements

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03706
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03706
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: 96.4% accuracy with 99.2% Top-3 score using combined image and text inputs

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a fine-grained product classification dataset from European retailer leaflets, containing 41.6k manually annotated product images across 832 classes. The study investigates three approaches for product classification: image-only, text-only, and combined image-text methods. The combined approach achieves state-of-the-art accuracy by leveraging both visual appearance and extracted text from leaflet images, particularly improving classification of visually similar products where textual attributes like package size or flavor provide crucial disambiguation.

## Method Summary
The method employs three classification approaches on leaflet product images. The image-only approach uses a ResNet50 model fine-tuned on ImageNet, trained with SGD optimizer (lr=0.001, momentum=0.9) for 30 epochs with ColorJitter augmentation. The text-only approach extracts product information using Tesseract OCR with multiple preprocessing pipelines and PSM modes, then classifies using SGDClassifier with modified huber loss on TF-IDF encoded text. The combined approach uses weighted probability stacking, giving higher weight to text predictions, achieving 96.4% accuracy and 99.2% Top-3 score.

## Key Results
- Combined image and text model achieves 96.4% accuracy (vs 94.7% image-only, 91.5% text-only)
- Top-3 accuracy reaches 99.2% with weighted probability stacking
- Tesseract OCR with combined preprocessing methods achieves 91.5% text extraction accuracy
- Model enables confidence-based separation for manual review with 99.2% accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Combining image and text inputs improves classification of visually similar products.
- Mechanism: OCR-extracted text captures fine-grained product attributes (package size, flavor) that are not visually distinguishable, while image model captures visual appearance. Weighted probability stacking leverages both modalities.
- Core assumption: OCR-extracted text is sufficiently accurate and aligned with correct class.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The combination of image and text as input improves the classification of visual difficult to distinguish products."
  - [section] "The prediction stacking method by combining the probabilities of the text and image model results in an overall accuracy of 96.4% which is a significant increase from the models alone."
- Break condition: OCR quality drops below ~90% accuracy, causing text model to introduce noise.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Tesseract OCR with multiple preprocessing pipelines extracts more text from varied leaflet layouts.
- Mechanism: Different PSM modes and preprocessing (grayscale, resize, thresholding) capture text from diverse backgrounds, font sizes, and positions.
- Core assumption: Text layout consistency within classes yields complementary information without excessive duplication.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The combination of all developed extraction methods, to extract as much text from the image as possible, boosts the performance to 91.5%."
- Break condition: OCR methods generate too many false positives, reducing precision.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Weighted probability stacking with high/low confidence separation allows selective human review.
- Mechanism: When image and text predictions agree, confidence is high; when they disagree, Top-3 candidates can be manually verified, achieving 99.2% accuracy.
- Core assumption: Disagreement between models correlates with true uncertainty benefiting from human inspection.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "This allows to separate those low confidence predictions to be potentially reviewed manually with the help of a Top-3 selection with a very high accuracy of 99.2%."
- Break condition: Human review becomes a bottleneck or cost outweighs accuracy gains.

  - Why needed here: Leaflet product images contain textual descriptions and package sizes that disambiguate visually similar products; extracting this text is prerequisite to text classification model.

  - Why needed here: Combining image and text probabilities yields higher accuracy than either modality alone; understanding how to weight and merge predictions is essential.
  - Quick check question: What happens to accuracy if you give equal weight to image and text probabilities instead of weighting text higher?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  OCR pipeline (Tesseract + OpenCV preprocessing) → text features (TF-IDF) → text classifier (SGD)
  Image pipeline (ResNet50 fine-tuned) → image features → image classifier
  Fusion layer (weighted probability stacking) → final prediction + confidence flag
  Confidence threshold → manual review queue (Top-3)

- Critical path: OCR extraction → text preprocessing → text model training → image model training → fusion layer → final prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - OCR preprocessing complexity vs. extraction accuracy
  - Model complexity (ConvNeXt vs. ResNet50) vs. training time and deployment constraints
  - Text vs. image weight in fusion vs. modality reliability

- Failure signatures:
  - OCR output contains many non-product words → text model confused
  - Image model misclassifies due to background clutter → low image confidence
  - Fusion weight too high on weak modality → overall accuracy drops

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run OCR extraction with default PSM only; measure accuracy drop vs. multi-PSM pipeline.
  2. Swap ResNet50 with ConvNeXt; compare training time and accuracy.
  3. Adjust text/image fusion weights; plot accuracy vs. weight ratio to find optimal balance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does performance degrade when applied to leaflet images from different regions/countries with varying product packaging and naming conventions?
- Basis in paper: Dataset is based on European retailers; error analysis mentions challenges with similar products from same producer.
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on specific dataset without discussing generalizability to other regions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing models on leaflet images from retailers in different regions/countries and comparing performance metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using more advanced OCR techniques or pre-trained OCR models on text extraction and classification accuracy?
- Basis in paper: Uses Tesseract OCR Engine with various preprocessing steps; final combination achieves 91.5% accuracy.
- Why unresolved: Paper does not explore benefits of more advanced OCR techniques or pre-trained models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing proposed OCR approach with more advanced techniques on same dataset and measuring impact on classification accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does inclusion of additional contextual information (price, retailer-specific info) affect classification performance?
- Basis in paper: Focuses on combining image and text information without considering additional contextual information.
- Why unresolved: Paper does not explore benefits of incorporating additional contextual information available in leaflet images.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating models with additional contextual information and comparing performance metrics.

## Limitations
- Dataset domain limited to European leaflet advertisements, limiting generalizability
- OCR extraction quality heavily dependent on preprocessing pipeline details not fully specified
- Weighted probability stacking mechanism described but not fully detailed for exact reproduction

## Confidence
- Classification accuracy claims (96.4%): High confidence - supported by clear experimental methodology and baseline comparisons
- OCR extraction improvements (91.5%): Medium confidence - results depend heavily on unspecified preprocessing parameters
- Human review workflow benefits: Medium confidence - theoretical benefit stated but not empirically validated
