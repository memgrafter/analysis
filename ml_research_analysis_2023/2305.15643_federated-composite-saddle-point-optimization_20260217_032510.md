# Federated Composite Saddle Point Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.15643
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15643
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: FeDualEx is the first federated algorithm for composite saddle point optimization, achieving O(1/R^(2/3)) and O(1/√(M·R)) convergence rates

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FeDualEx, a novel federated algorithm for composite saddle point optimization that extends dual extrapolation to handle non-smooth regularization and constraints. The algorithm achieves the first convergence rates for this setting under the federated learning paradigm, with O(1/R^(2/3)) and O(1/√(M·R)) dominating terms. By incorporating generalized Bregman divergence into the proximal operator, FeDualEx can handle composite objectives while maintaining convergence guarantees. The method naturally extends to sequential composite saddle point problems, providing new rates for both stochastic (O(1/√T)) and deterministic (O(1/T)) cases.

## Method Summary
FeDualEx is a federated dual extrapolation algorithm that extends dual extrapolation to composite saddle point problems by using generalized Bregman divergence in the proximal operator. The algorithm operates in two phases: local computation on clients and aggregation on the server. Each client performs multiple local updates using a generalized proximal operator that incorporates the composite term through the distance-generating function. The server aggregates dual variable updates from all clients and projects to the primal space for output. The key innovation is replacing the standard Bregman divergence with a generalized version that can handle non-smooth regularization terms by incorporating them into the conjugate of the distance-generating function.

## Key Results
- FeDualEx achieves O(1/R^(2/3)) and O(1/√(M·R)) convergence rates for federated composite saddle point optimization
- The algorithm provides the first convergence rate for stochastic composite saddle point problems (O(1/√T))
- Experimental results show better convergence and solution sparsity/rank compared to FedMiP and FedDualAvg on ℓ1 and nuclear norm regularization tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: FeDualEx extends dual extrapolation to composite saddle point problems by replacing the Bregman divergence in the proximal operator with generalized Bregman divergence.
- **Mechanism**: The proximal operator for saddle functions is defined as the arg min of the inner product plus generalized Bregman divergence. This allows handling of non-smooth regularization terms by incorporating them into the distance-generating function through the conjugate operation.
- **Core assumption**: The composite term ψ(z) can be incorporated into the distance-generating function ℓt(z) = ℓ(z) + tηψ(z) such that the conjugate ℓ∗t remains differentiable.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "FeDualEx, an extra-step primal-dual algorithm, which is the first of its kind that encompasses both saddle point optimization and composite objectives under the FL paradigm"
  - [section 4.1] "we propose a novel technical change to the proximal operator, directly replacing the Bregman divergence in the proximal operator with the generalized Bregman divergence"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct mentions of Bregman divergence or proximal operator modifications in neighboring papers
- **Break condition**: If the conjugate of ℓt is not differentiable, the proximal operator cannot be computed via gradient of conjugate, breaking the algorithm.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: FeDualEx achieves convergence rate O(1/R^(2/3)) and O(1/√(M·R)) for federated composite saddle point optimization.
- **Mechanism**: The convergence analysis bounds the duality gap by separating the smooth term f and the composite regularization term ψ. Telescoping over local and communication rounds yields the stated rates.
- **Core assumption**: The distance-generating function ℓ is 1-strongly convex and the domain Z is compact w.r.t. Bregman divergence.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "The authors provide the first convergence rate for this setting, showing O(1/R^(2/3)) and O(1/√(M·R)) terms dominate communication complexity"
  - [section 4.2] "We provide the convergence rate for FeDualEx under the homogeneous setting, which is, to the best of our knowledge, the first convergence rate for composite saddle point optimization under the FL paradigm"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - neighboring papers focus on other aspects of optimization without composite saddle point analysis
- **Break condition**: If the strong convexity or compactness assumptions fail, the telescoping argument and bound derivations collapse.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: FeDualEx naturally yields algorithms for sequential stochastic and deterministic composite saddle point optimization with rates O(1/√T) and O(1/T) respectively.
- **Mechanism**: By reducing the number of clients M to one (sequential case) and further removing gradient noise, the same analysis applies with simpler terms, yielding the stated rates.
- **Core assumption**: The gradient operator g is Lipschitz and the distance-generating function ℓ is Legendre-type.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "FeDualEx also yields new results for sequential composite saddle point optimization, providing the first convergence rate for stochastic composite saddle point problems (O(1/√T))"
  - [section 5] "By reducing the number of clients to one, we show for the sequential version of FeDualEx that the analysis naturally yields a convergence rate for stochastic composite saddle point optimization"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no neighboring papers discuss sequential composite saddle point optimization rates
- **Break condition**: If the Lipschitz assumption on g fails, the convergence rate proofs relying on Lipschitz continuity break down.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Bregman divergence and its properties**
  - Why needed here: FeDualEx is built on mirror descent and dual extrapolation, which rely on Bregman divergence for non-Euclidean geometry. The generalized version handles composite terms.
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between Bregman divergence and strong convexity of the distance-generating function?

- **Concept: Convex conjugate and its differentiability**
  - Why needed here: The proximal operator is defined via the gradient of the conjugate of the distance-generating function. Differentiability is crucial for the algorithm's correctness.
  - Quick check question: Under what conditions is the convex conjugate of a strictly convex function continuously differentiable?

- **Concept: Saddle point problems and duality gap**
  - Why needed here: The objective is a saddle point problem, and convergence is measured by the duality gap. Understanding this structure is essential for analyzing FeDualEx.
  - Quick check question: How does the duality gap relate to the primal and dual objective values in a convex-concave saddle point problem?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: FeDualEx (Algorithm 1) -> FedMiP (Algorithm 2) -> Lemma 1-3, Theorem 1-4 -> Synthetic experiments -> Figure 2-3

- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize dual variables ςr=0 on server and ςm r,0=ςr on clients
  2. For each local update k: compute zm r,k via generalized proximal operator, zm r,k+1/2 via extra step, update ςm r,k+1
  3. Aggregate client dual updates on server: ςr+1=ςr+ηs·∆r
  4. Project aggregated dual to primal for output: 1/RK·ΣΣ \zr,k+1/2

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Dual aggregation vs primal aggregation: Dual aggregation preserves composite structure but requires dual space operations
  - Extra step vs no extra step: Extra step improves convergence for saddle point problems but increases computation per iteration
  - Generalized Bregman divergence vs vanilla: Generalized handles non-smooth regularization but requires conjugate computation

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Divergence or slow convergence: Likely due to poor choice of step sizes ηc, ηs or violation of Lipschitz/strong convexity assumptions
  - Poor sparsity/rank in solutions: May indicate step sizes too large or regularization parameter λ not properly tuned
  - High variance in experimental results: Could be due to insufficient gradient variance control or client heterogeneity

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run FeDualEx on bilinear problem with ℓ1 regularization and ℓ∞ constraint (Figure 2) with K=1, R=5000 to verify duality gap convergence and sparsity
  2. Test FeDualEx on nuclear norm regularized problem (Figure 3) with K=1, R=100 to check low-rank solution and duality gap
  3. Compare FeDualEx against FedMiP and FedDualAvg on both problems with same hyperparameters to validate effectiveness

## Limitations
- Assumes smooth convex-concave objectives f with Lipschitz gradients, excluding many practical problems with non-smooth losses
- Strong convexity assumption on distance-generating function ℓ may not hold in federated settings with heterogeneous data
- Convergence rates rely on bounded domains Z and finite diameter D, which may not be realistic in high-dimensional settings

## Confidence
- FeDualEx mechanism and algorithm design: High - well-defined and grounded in established optimization theory
- O(1/R^(2/3)) and O(1/√(M·R)) convergence rates: Medium - novel analysis but relies on several assumptions that may not hold in practice
- O(1/√T) and O(1/T) rates for sequential composite saddle point optimization: Low - new results with limited validation and no comparison to existing methods
- Experimental effectiveness on synthetic problems: Medium - shows improved convergence and sparsity/rank but only on synthetic data
