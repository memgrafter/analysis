# Probing Physical Reasoning with Counter-Commonsense Context

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02258
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02258
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Language models struggle to reason from unusual contexts when these contradict commonsense knowledge, performing at chance levels despite high accuracy on commonsense-consistent cases.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CConS, a dataset designed to test whether language models can override their ingrained commonsense knowledge when reasoning about size relationships in unusual contexts. The dataset contains 1,112 examples generated from 139 templates, where contexts either align with or contradict physical commonsense (e.g., "a house is in a light bulb"). The study evaluates both masked language models (BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT) and generative models (T0, GPT-3) on this task, revealing that while models perform well on commonsense-consistent cases (up to 85% accuracy), their performance drops significantly to chance levels when contexts contradict commonsense knowledge.

The findings demonstrate that current language models rely heavily on prior physical commonsense rather than contextual reasoning, even when the context provides clear logical guidance. This highlights a fundamental gap between human reasoning, which can flexibly override prior knowledge when presented with unusual contexts, and machine reasoning, which appears to be constrained by training data biases. The results suggest that improving models' ability to reason from context rather than defaulting to commonsense knowledge remains a significant challenge in natural language processing.

## Method Summary
The study constructs the CConS dataset through systematic template generation, creating 139 unique templates that produce 1,112 examples for binary size comparison tasks. Each template is designed to create contexts that either support commonsense physical relationships or deliberately contradict them. The evaluation protocol tests both masked language models (BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT) and generative models (T0, GPT-3) on their ability to correctly identify which of two objects is larger based solely on the provided context. Performance is measured separately for commonsense-consistent and counter-commonsense contexts to isolate the effect of prior knowledge on reasoning ability.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 achieves 85% accuracy on commonsense-consistent size comparisons but only 41% on counter-commonsense cases
- All evaluated models show significant performance degradation when contexts contradict commonsense knowledge
- Masked language models (BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT) perform worse than generative models overall, but all exhibit similar patterns of reliance on prior commonsense knowledge

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that language models rely on statistical patterns learned during training rather than genuine contextual reasoning. When presented with unusual contexts that contradict commonsense physical relationships, models fail to override their prior knowledge, instead defaulting to predictions based on their training distribution. This suggests that current models lack the flexibility to adapt their reasoning based on context alone, instead treating unusual contexts as noise or errors rather than valid reasoning scenarios.

## Foundational Learning

**Counterfactual Reasoning**: Understanding how to reason about situations that contradict known facts or commonsense knowledge.
*Why needed*: Essential for testing whether models can override prior knowledge when context demands it.
*Quick check*: Can the model correctly answer "Which is larger: a mouse or an elephant?" in both normal and inverted contexts?

**Template-Based Dataset Generation**: Creating controlled experimental conditions through systematic template design.
*Why needed*: Ensures consistent evaluation conditions and isolates the variable of commonsense versus counter-commonsense content.
*Quick check*: Are all templates balanced across the two experimental conditions?

**Masked vs Generative Language Model Evaluation**: Comparing different model architectures on the same reasoning task.
*Why needed*: Determines whether model architecture affects the ability to reason from context.
*Quick check*: Do all model types show similar patterns of performance degradation?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Dataset Generation -> Template Application -> Example Creation -> Model Evaluation -> Performance Analysis

**Critical Path**: The evaluation pipeline flows from controlled dataset creation through systematic model testing to comparative analysis of performance across commonsense and counter-commonsense conditions.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The study prioritizes controlled experimental conditions through template generation over dataset diversity, potentially limiting generalizability but ensuring clear attribution of performance differences to the intended experimental variable.

**Failure Signatures**: Models default to commonsense predictions even when context clearly indicates otherwise, suggesting they treat unusual contexts as noise rather than valid reasoning scenarios requiring logical inference.

**First Experiments**: 
1. Evaluate models on a subset of templates with varying complexity levels
2. Test zero-shot performance with explicit instructions to ignore prior knowledge
3. Compare performance across different size relationship pairs to identify potential biases

## Limitations
- The dataset size (1,112 examples) may not capture the full complexity of physical reasoning across diverse contexts
- Templates may contain implicit biases affecting performance beyond the intended commonsense versus counter-commonsense distinction
- Evaluation focuses exclusively on size comparison tasks, limiting conclusions about broader physical reasoning capabilities
- The study does not explore whether fine-tuning or prompt engineering could improve performance on counter-commonsense contexts

## Confidence

**Core Claim (High)**: Language models struggle to reason from unusual contexts when these contradict commonsense knowledge, performing at chance levels despite high accuracy on commonsense-consistent cases.

**GPT-3 Performance (Medium)**: GPT-3 outperforms other models while still showing substantial limitations, though this finding is reasonable given the experimental design.

**Human-Machine Gap (Low)**: The broader conclusion about the gap between human and machine reasoning extrapolates beyond the specific experimental scope and requires additional behavioral studies for validation.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct ablation studies varying template complexity and context length to determine whether performance degradation stems from counter-commonsense content or increased cognitive load.

2. Test models on zero-shot prompts with explicit instructions to ignore prior knowledge and reason solely from the given context, isolating the effect of explicit reasoning cues.

3. Expand evaluation to include multi-step physical reasoning tasks beyond binary size comparisons to assess whether the observed limitations generalize to more complex reasoning scenarios.
