# Generative Adversarial Symmetry Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00236
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00236
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: LieGAN discovers continuous symmetries from data and improves equivariant network performance

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LieGAN, a generative adversarial approach for automatically discovering symmetries in data. The method learns interpretable Lie algebra bases that represent continuous symmetry groups through adversarial training between a generator and symmetry detector. LieGAN can discover both discrete and continuous symmetries including SO(n) and SO(1,3)+ groups, and demonstrates improved accuracy when the discovered symmetries are incorporated into equivariant neural networks.

## Method Summary
LieGAN uses a generative adversarial framework where a generator learns to produce transformed data samples, while a symmetry detector network tries to distinguish between original and transformed data. Through this adversarial process, LieGAN discovers a group of transformations that preserve the original data distribution. The learned symmetries are represented as interpretable Lie algebra basis, enabling integration with existing equivariant neural networks. The method is trained by balancing multiple objectives: generator loss, symmetry detector loss, and symmetry encoder loss.

## Key Results
- Discovered SO(2) rotation symmetry in n-body trajectory data with cosine correlation of 0.9998 and test MSE of 3.79e-5
- Discovered discrete C7 rotation symmetry in synthetic regression with MAE of 0.003
- Discovered SO(1,3)+ symmetry in top tagging with LieGNN achieving accuracy of 0.938 and AUROC of 0.9848, comparable to LorentzNet

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LieGAN works by leveraging the principle that symmetries preserve data distribution. The adversarial framework forces the generator to learn transformations that the symmetry detector cannot distinguish from original data, thereby discovering true symmetries. The Lie algebra representation provides interpretable continuous transformations that can be composed and inverted, making them practical for integration with existing equivariant networks.

## Foundational Learning
- Lie Groups: Continuous symmetry groups that are also smooth manifolds, needed for representing continuous transformations
- Lie Algebras: Tangent spaces of Lie groups at identity, providing linear approximations of continuous symmetries
- Generative Adversarial Networks: Framework for learning data distributions through competition between generator and discriminator
- Equivariant Neural Networks: Networks that transform predictably under symmetry transformations, improving generalization

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component Map: Generator -> Symmetry Detector -> Symmetry Encoder -> Generator
- Critical Path: The adversarial loop between generator and symmetry detector drives symmetry discovery
- Design Tradeoffs: Balancing generator quality against symmetry discovery quality through hyperparameter α
- Failure Signatures: Poor symmetry discovery when α is too low (focuses on data quality) or too high (finds spurious patterns)
- First Experiments:
  1. Test on simple synthetic data with known SO(2) symmetry
  2. Vary α parameter to observe stability of discovered symmetries
  3. Compare discovered symmetries against analytical ground truth

None

## Limitations
- Sensitive to hyperparameter tuning, particularly the symmetry detector loss weight α
- Limited theoretical guarantees for convergence to true symmetries
- Scalability to higher-dimensional Lie algebras remains untested

## Confidence
- LieGAN can discover low-dimensional continuous symmetries from data: Medium
- The discovered symmetries improve performance of equivariant networks: Medium
- LieGAN outperforms or matches specialized physics models: Low

## Next Checks
1. Test LieGAN on synthetic datasets with known higher-dimensional Lie algebras (e.g., SU(2), SO(4)) to assess scalability
2. Evaluate LieGAN's performance on non-physics datasets with unknown symmetries to test generalization
3. Compare LieGAN's discovered symmetries against established symmetry detection methods like Casimir invariants or invariant polynomials
