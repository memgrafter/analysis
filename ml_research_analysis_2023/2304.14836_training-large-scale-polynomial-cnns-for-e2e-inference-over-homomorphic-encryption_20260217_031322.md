# Training Large Scale Polynomial CNNs for E2E Inference over Homomorphic Encryption

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14836
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14836
- Reference count: 40
- This work tackles the challenge of training large-scale Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) that can be efficiently evaluated under Homomorphic Encryption (HE) for end-to-end secure inference.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training large-scale CNNs that can be efficiently evaluated under homomorphic encryption (HE) for end-to-end secure inference. The core contribution lies in developing a training procedure that minimizes the input ranges to polynomial activation functions, thereby enabling accurate polynomial approximations and reducing the multiplication depth required for HE evaluation. This is achieved through a combination of range-aware regularization loss and empirical range estimation. The paper also provides insights into optimizing Skip-Connection placement and demonstrates the benefits of using the ConvNeXt backbone. The method is validated by successfully training HE-friendly versions of ResNet-18/50/101 and ConvNeXt models on ImageNet, achieving high accuracy and enabling practical secure inference with 128-bit security in minutes on a GPU.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves training CNNs with polynomial approximations of activation functions that are optimized for homomorphic encryption (HE) evaluation. The key innovation is a range-aware regularization loss that minimizes the input ranges to these polynomial activations, enabling more accurate approximations and reducing the computational depth required for HE. The training procedure includes empirical range estimation to further refine the approximation accuracy. Additionally, the paper explores Skip-Connection placement strategies and demonstrates the advantages of using the ConvNeXt architecture as a backbone for HE-friendly models. The approach is validated on ImageNet using ResNet and ConvNeXt architectures, showing high accuracy and practical inference times under 128-bit HE security.

## Key Results
- Successfully trained HE-friendly versions of ResNet-18/50/101 and ConvNeXt models on ImageNet.
- Achieved high accuracy while enabling practical secure inference with 128-bit security in minutes on a GPU.
- Demonstrated the effectiveness of range-aware regularization and empirical range estimation in minimizing input ranges for polynomial activations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by minimizing the input ranges to polynomial activation functions, which are used in place of standard non-polynomial activations to enable homomorphic encryption evaluation. By reducing these input ranges through a combination of range-aware regularization loss and empirical range estimation, the polynomial approximations become more accurate, requiring less computational depth for HE evaluation. This reduction in multiplication depth directly translates to faster and more practical secure inference times. Additionally, optimizing Skip-Connection placement and using the ConvNeXt backbone further enhance the efficiency and accuracy of the HE-friendly models.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Input Data -> CNN Backbone (e.g., ResNet, ConvNeXt) -> Polynomial Activation Functions -> Homomorphic Encryption Evaluation -> Output Predictions

**Critical Path**
The critical path involves the CNN backbone processing the input data, followed by polynomial activation functions, and finally the homomorphic encryption evaluation. The efficiency of this path is determined by the accuracy of the polynomial approximations and the computational depth required for HE evaluation.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Accuracy vs. Computational Efficiency: Higher-degree polynomial approximations may improve accuracy but increase computational overhead for HE evaluation.
- Range Minimization vs. Model Capacity: Aggressive range minimization may limit the model's ability to learn complex features, potentially impacting accuracy.

**Failure Signatures**
- Significant accuracy drop when switching from standard to polynomial activations.
- Excessive computational time or memory usage during HE evaluation, indicating inefficient polynomial approximations or range minimization.

## Limitations

1. The effectiveness of the proposed method relies heavily on the accuracy of the polynomial approximations of activation functions. While the paper demonstrates that minimizing the input ranges improves the accuracy of these approximations, the impact of approximation errors on the overall model accuracy is not fully explored. Additionally, the paper does not discuss the potential trade-offs between the accuracy of the polynomial approximations and the computational efficiency of the HE evaluation.

2. The paper focuses on training HE-friendly CNNs for image classification tasks. The generalizability of the proposed method to other types of neural networks (e.g., recurrent neural networks, transformers) and tasks (e.g., object detection, natural language processing) is not discussed. It is unclear whether the proposed approach can be directly applied to these scenarios or if significant modifications are required.

3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational overhead introduced by the proposed training procedure compared to standard training methods. While the paper mentions that the training procedure is efficient, a quantitative comparison of the training time and resources required for HE-friendly CNNs versus standard CNNs would be valuable.
