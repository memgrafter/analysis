# Curious Replay for Model-based Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15934
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15934
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Curious Replay achieves 19.4 on Crafter benchmark, improving upon DreamerV3's 14.5 score

## Executive Summary
Curious Replay is a prioritized experience replay method for model-based reinforcement learning agents that improves adaptability to changing environments by prioritizing experiences the agent knows the least about. It combines count-based novelty (prioritizing less-frequently visited experiences) with adversarial curiosity (prioritizing experiences with high model prediction error) to form a dual signal that ensures the model trains more on unfamiliar or poorly understood states. The method demonstrates significant improvements in adaptation speed on object interaction assays and achieves a new state-of-the-art score on the Crafter benchmark while maintaining performance on standard Deepmind Control Suite tasks.

## Method Summary
Curious Replay modifies the experience replay mechanism in DreamerV3 by introducing a curiosity-based priority signal that combines count-based novelty and adversarial curiosity. The method computes priority for each experience as a weighted sum of visit count and model loss: pi = cβvi + (|Li| + ε)α, where vi is visit count, Li is model loss, and c, β, α, ε are hyperparameters. This dual prioritization ensures training focuses on both rarely visited states and states the model predicts poorly, enabling faster adaptation to environmental changes while preserving performance on stable tasks. The approach maintains the same computational overhead as standard prioritized replay by using SumTree-based sampling and updating priorities after each training step.

## Key Results
- Achieves new state-of-the-art score of 19.4 on Crafter benchmark, improving upon DreamerV3's 14.5 score by 33%
- Improves adaptation speed on object interaction assay, reaching 5th interaction faster than DreamerV2 baseline and TD-replay variants
- Maintains similar overall performance on Deepmind Control Suite while showing enhanced adaptation to background changes and constraint releases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Curious Replay improves model-based RL adaptability by prioritizing experiences that the agent knows the least about. By combining count-based novelty with adversarial curiosity, the method ensures the model trains more on unfamiliar or poorly understood states, helping it adapt faster to changes in the environment. This dual signal prevents overfitting to well-learned experiences while maintaining performance stability.

### Mechanism 2
Curious Replay maintains performance in unchanging environments while improving adaptability in changing ones. The weighted sum of count-based and adversarial signals avoids over-prioritizing novelty at the expense of stable, well-learned experiences, preserving good performance on unchanging benchmarks like Deepmind Control Suite.

### Mechanism 3
Curious Replay improves learning efficiency in Crafter by focusing training on experiences where the agent holds recently discovered resources. This enables faster progression through achievement chains (e.g., wood → wood pickaxe → stone → stone pickaxe → iron) by rapidly updating the model for new phases of the POMDP.

  - Quick check question: How does PER differ from uniform replay, and why might prioritizing by TD error be less effective for model-based agents in changing environments?

  - Quick check question: What are the main forms of curiosity signals in RL, and how does adversarial curiosity (model error) compare to count-based novelty?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** DreamerV3 (World model, Actor-critic, Experience replay buffer) -> Curious Replay (Priority computation, SumTree sampling, Priority update)
- **Critical path:** 1) Collect transitions with environment, 2) Add to replay buffer with initial max priority, 3) Sample batch using SumTree probabilities, 4) Train world model and policy, 5) Update priorities based on visit count and loss
- **Design tradeoffs:** Count-based vs. adversarial prioritization balances novelty and stability; hyperparameter sensitivity requires careful tuning; computational overhead is minimal due to efficient SumTree implementation
- **Failure signatures:** Over-prioritizing novelty causes catastrophic forgetting; under-prioritizing novelty prevents adaptation; hyperparameter misconfiguration causes performance drops or high variance
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Sanity check: Run DreamerV3 on Deepmind Control Suite with Curious Replay vs. uniform replay, 2) Adaptation test: Use object interaction assay to measure time-to-interaction, 3) Hyperparameter sweep: Vary α and β in Crafter to observe impact on achievement progression

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does Curious Replay consistently improve performance in more complex changing environments beyond Crafter, such as procedurally generated games or robotics tasks? The paper mentions future directions including testing performance in a wider variety of settings but only demonstrates Curious Replay on Crafter and Deepmind Control Suite variants.

### Open Question 2
How does Curious Replay compare to other meta-learning approaches for adaptation in changing environments? The paper mentions meta-learning as a related approach but does not directly compare Curious Replay to meta-learning methods.

### Open Question 3
Can Curious Replay be extended to handle changing reward functions, not just changing state dynamics or observations? The paper notes this as a limitation, mentioning that Curious Replay is not currently tailored to account for changing reward functions as in the LoCA setting.

## Limitations
- Curious Replay has not been tested on more complex domains beyond Crafter and Deepmind Control Suite variants
- The method does not address changing reward functions, only state dynamics and observations
- No ablation studies isolate the individual contributions of count-based versus adversarial prioritization

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** Crafter benchmark improvement (19.4 vs 14.5) and overall Deepmind Control Suite performance maintenance
**Medium Confidence:** Adaptability improvement on object interaction assay and background-swap variant
**Low Confidence:** The specific claim about resource discovery prioritization driving efficiency gains in Crafter

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies on α and β hyperparameters across all tasks to verify the balance between count-based and adversarial signals is robust and necessary for claimed performance
2. Implement versions using only count-based prioritization and only adversarial prioritization to isolate which component drives main improvements
3. Test Curious Replay on additional open-world environments beyond Crafter to validate whether resource discovery prioritization generalizes as a mechanism for learning efficiency
