# A Survey on Event-based News Narrative Extraction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08351
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08351
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Reviews 54 studies on event-based news narrative extraction, identifying three resolution levels and three structural approaches while highlighting coherence optimization as the dominant methodology.

## Executive Summary
This survey systematically reviews 54 studies on event-based news narrative extraction from traditional news sources. The authors develop a comprehensive taxonomy based on three event resolution levels (sentences, documents, clusters) and three structural approaches (linear, graph, tree). They analyze 24 extraction criteria and various evaluation methodologies, finding that most methods use sentence-level resolution with linear structures and rely on coherence optimization. The survey identifies key challenges including computational scalability, lack of standardized evaluation metrics for complex narratives, and limited handling of misinformation. Future research opportunities include multi-resolution methods, interactive narrative exploration, and developing metrics that account for both narrative structure and content.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a systematic review of 900 articles, screening them based on criteria including event-based approaches, narrative structure generation, and text-based news sources. They analyzed 54 relevant studies using a framework that categorizes methods by resolution level (sentence, document, cluster), structural approach (linear, graph, tree), and evaluation methodology. The survey synthesizes findings across these dimensions to identify trends, challenges, and research opportunities in computational news narrative extraction.

## Key Results
- Most narrative extraction methods use sentence-level resolution with linear structures and coherence optimization
- Graph-based representations enable modeling of multiple storylines and their interactions
- Key challenges include computational scalability, lack of standardized evaluation metrics for complex narratives, and limited handling of misinformation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The survey's multi-resolution framework (sentences, documents, clusters) captures narrative extraction at different granularity levels.
- Mechanism: By defining three distinct resolution levels, the authors can systematically categorize and compare methods that represent events at varying levels of abstraction, from fine-grained sentence-level to coarse-grained cluster-level representations.
- Core assumption: Different narrative extraction tasks require different levels of granularity, and a unified framework must accommodate all three levels to be comprehensive.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The authors identify three resolution levels for events (sentences, documents, clusters).
  - [section 1.1.1] Following these exclusion criteria, we removed approximately 10 articles from the final data set.
  - [corpus] The corpus shows related work on narrative classification and LLM-based narrative extraction, supporting the relevance of multi-resolution approaches.
- Break condition: If narrative extraction methods prove equally effective at all resolution levels, the multi-resolution framework becomes unnecessary complexity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Coherence optimization serves as a unifying principle across diverse narrative extraction methods.
- Mechanism: Most methods, regardless of resolution level or structural approach, incorporate coherence metrics (smooth transitions, logical flow) into their objective functions or selection criteria.
- Core assumption: Human-perceived narrative quality correlates with computational measures of coherence, making it a valid optimization target.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The survey finds that most methods use sentence-level resolution with linear structures, and rely on coherence optimization.
  - [section 3] While coherence has a formal definition in narratological terms [1], it is just as complex and ill-defined as relevance in computational terms.
  - [corpus] The corpus neighbors include work on narrative classification and stakeholder detection, suggesting coherence remains relevant for different narrative tasks.
- Break condition: If coherence metrics fail to correlate with human judgments of narrative quality, optimization based on these metrics becomes counterproductive.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Graph-based representations enable modeling of multiple storylines and their interactions, which linear structures cannot capture.
- Mechanism: Graph structures allow for convergent and divergent storylines, enabling the representation of complex narrative interactions that single timelines cannot express.
- Core assumption: Real-world narratives often involve multiple interacting storylines, and graph representations are necessary to capture this complexity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The authors identify three structural approaches (linear, graph, tree) and note that graph-based works are designed to represent multiple storylines.
  - [section 5.1] In contrast, graph-based structures allow the modeling of different interactions between storylines (e.g., convergent and divergent stories).
  - [corpus] The corpus shows work on narrative mapping and stakeholder classification, supporting the need for complex narrative representations.
- Break condition: If most practical applications only require single, linear narratives, the added complexity of graph representations provides diminishing returns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What evaluation metrics can properly account for both order and structure in non-linear narrative representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies that current metrics only consider content without accounting for order and structure, particularly for graph-based narratives.
- Why unresolved: Existing metrics like ROUGE and traditional IR metrics focus on content similarity and don't capture the complex relationships in graph structures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of new metrics that incorporate both structural elements (connectivity, paths) and content similarity, tested against human evaluations of narrative quality.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can narrative extraction methods be made computationally scalable while maintaining quality?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that most methods suffer from computational cost issues when dealing with big data and mentions filtering as a potential solution.
- Why unresolved: While filtering is mentioned as a solution, the paper doesn't provide concrete methods for implementing scalable extraction while preserving narrative quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different computational optimization techniques (online processing, parallel computing, approximate methods) showing trade-offs between speed and narrative quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What framework can be developed to evaluate narrative extraction methods across different resolution levels (sentences, documents, clusters)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the lack of benchmark data sets and evaluation metrics that work across different event resolution levels.
- Why unresolved: Current evaluation approaches are fragmented, with different metrics for each resolution level but no unified framework.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive evaluation framework that standardizes metrics across resolution levels, validated with multiple data sets and extraction methods.

## Limitations
- Focus on traditional news sources excludes emerging narrative extraction from social media and multimedia content
- Classification framework may not fully capture hybrid methods combining multiple resolution levels or structural approaches
- Predictions about future research directions depend on evolving technological capabilities not yet established in literature
