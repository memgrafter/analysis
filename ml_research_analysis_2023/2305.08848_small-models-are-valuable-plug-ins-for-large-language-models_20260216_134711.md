# Small Models are Valuable Plug-ins for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08848
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08848
- Reference count: 6
- This paper introduces Super In-Context Learning (SuperICL), a novel approach that combines locally fine-tuned smaller models with black-box LLMs to improve performance on supervised tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Super In-Context Learning (SuperICL), a novel approach that addresses the limitations of both fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) and using in-context learning (ICL) on supervised tasks. The authors propose combining locally fine-tuned smaller models (e.g., RoBERTa) as plug-ins with black-box LLMs (e.g., GPT-3.5) to improve performance on supervised tasks. SuperICL leverages the task-specific knowledge of the fine-tuned plug-in model and the general language understanding capabilities of the LLM. Experiments on the GLUE benchmark show that SuperICL outperforms both GPT-3.5 ICL and the plug-in model RoBERTa-Large, with an average advantage of 8.58 and 1.22, respectively.

## Method Summary
SuperICL combines locally fine-tuned smaller models with black-box LLMs by constructing a context that includes randomly sampled in-context examples from the training data, along with the plug-in model's predictions and confidence scores for these examples. For test inputs, the LLM's input includes the constructed context, the test input, and the plug-in model's prediction for the test input. The LLM then generates a final prediction, optionally providing an explanation for overriding the plug-in model's prediction. The method involves fine-tuning the plug-in model on the entire training set, constructing the context, and using the LLM to generate final predictions based on the context and plug-in model's prediction.

## Key Results
- SuperICL outperforms both GPT-3.5 ICL and the plug-in model RoBERTa-Large on the GLUE benchmark, with an average advantage of 8.58 and 1.22, respectively.
- On the XNLI benchmark, SuperICL improves the performance of XLM-V (a multilingual model) in most languages.
- The method addresses the instability problem of ICL by leveraging the task-specific knowledge of the fine-tuned plug-in model.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Confidence scores from the plug-in model enable the LLM to decide when to override predictions.
- Mechanism: The plug-in model's confidence score is incorporated into the context, allowing the LLM to assess the reliability of the plug-in's prediction. Low confidence scores signal uncertainty, prompting the LLM to potentially override with its own prediction.
- Core assumption: The LLM can effectively interpret and act upon numerical confidence scores provided in the context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The LLM's input includes the constructed context, the test input, and the plug-in model's prediction for the test input."
  - [section] "confidence scores provide a measure of the plug-in model's uncertainty in its predictions. By incorporating these scores in the context, the LLM can trust predictions where the plug-in model is highly confident and be more cautious when the plug-in model is uncertain."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in neighbors about confidence score usage.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: In-context examples from the training set provide task-specific knowledge transfer to the LLM.
- Mechanism: Randomly sampled in-context examples, along with the plug-in model's predictions for these examples, are concatenated to form the context. This exposes the LLM to task-specific patterns and the plug-in model's understanding of these patterns.
- Core assumption: The LLM can learn from in-context examples in the same way it learns from demonstrations in few-shot learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a context is constructed by randomly sampling from the training data and incorporating the plug-in model's predictions"
  - [section] "the LLM can better understand the relationship among the input examples, ground-truth labels and the plug-in model's expertise."
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbors discuss in-context learning but not specifically this ensemble approach.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The plug-in model acts as a knowledge bridge, transferring task-specific information to the LLM.
- Mechanism: The plug-in model, fine-tuned on the entire training set, provides predictions and confidence scores that are incorporated into the context. This allows the LLM to leverage the plug-in's task-specific knowledge without needing to be fine-tuned itself.
- Core assumption: The plug-in model's fine-tuning captures meaningful task-specific patterns that can be transferred to the LLM.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We use the plug-in model to predict labels with confidence for in-context examples and concatenate them with the input text and ground-truth labels as context."
  - [section] "These plug-in models have been fine-tuned on the task-specific data, they serve as a bridge between the large pre-trained model and the task-specific data, allowing for effective knowledge transfer and improved performance."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in neighbors about this bridging mechanism.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Training data -> Plug-in model (e.g., RoBERTa-large, XLM-V) -> Large Language Model (e.g., GPT-3.5) -> Context construction mechanism -> Inference pipeline

- Critical path:
  1. Fine-tune plug-in model on training data
  2. Construct context with in-context examples and plug-in predictions
  3. Concatenate test input with plug-in prediction
  4. LLM generates final prediction and optional explanation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a smaller plug-in model allows for fine-tuning on the entire training set, but may limit the transfer of knowledge to the LLM.
  - Incorporating confidence scores adds complexity but enables more informed decision-making by the LLM.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance compared to baselines
  - High variance in performance across different runs
  - Inability to improve performance on multilingual tasks

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare SuperICL performance to ICL and plug-in model alone on a single task (e.g., MNLI).
  2. Analyze the effect of removing confidence scores from the context.
  3. Test the impact of different numbers of in-context examples on performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of plug-in model architecture (e.g., RoBERTa vs. DeBERTa) affect the performance and stability of SuperICL across different tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compare SuperICL with RoBERTa and DeBERTa V3-Large plug-in models, showing that SuperICL improves performance with both but the improvement is smaller with DeBERTa.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares two architectures and does not provide a systematic analysis of how different model architectures (e.g., different Transformer variants, model sizes) impact SuperICL's performance, stability, and efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive ablation study varying plug-in model architectures (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, ELECTRA) and sizes, along with their impact on SuperICL's performance, stability, and inference time/cost.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can SuperICL be effectively extended to non-text classification tasks such as text generation, summarization, or semantic parsing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that due to space and budget constraints, they only investigate text classification tasks and suggest that exploring generation tasks would be interesting.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any experimental results or analysis on whether SuperICL can be applied to generation tasks beyond classification, and what modifications might be needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying SuperICL to generation tasks (e.g., summarization, question answering, semantic parsing) with appropriate modifications to the context construction and inference steps, and comparing the performance to ICL and fine-tuning baselines.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does SuperICL's performance and robustness change when the plug-in model is adversarially attacked or when the in-context examples are noisy or irrelevant?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors analyze SuperICL's performance on the ANLI benchmark, which contains adversarial examples, and find that SuperICL underperforms ICL when the plug-in model (RoBERTa) is attacked. They also mention the potential impact of noisy or irrelevant in-context examples.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic analysis of SuperICL's robustness to adversarial attacks on the plug-in model or the impact of noisy/irrelevant in-context examples on its performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating SuperICL's performance and robustness when the plug-in model is adversarially attacked (e.g., using different attack methods) or when the in-context examples are corrupted with noise or made irrelevant, and comparing the results to ICL and fine-tuning baselines.

## Limitations

- Limited evidence that LLMs can effectively interpret numerical confidence scores provided in the context.
- Lack of systematic analysis on how different plug-in model architectures impact SuperICL's performance, stability, and efficiency.
- Potential sensitivity of the method to the quality and diversity of in-context examples.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The core experimental results showing SuperICL outperforms both GPT-3.5 ICL and the plug-in model alone on GLUE benchmarks.
- Medium Confidence: The explanation of how confidence scores are used by the LLM to make decisions.
- Low Confidence: The claim about the plug-in model acting as a knowledge bridge.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct controlled experiments where the plug-in model's confidence scores are systematically manipulated (e.g., inverted, randomized, or removed) to determine whether the LLM actually uses these scores to make decisions about overriding predictions.

2. Use interpretability techniques (e.g., attention visualization, feature attribution) to analyze what specific patterns from the plug-in model are being transferred to the LLM through the context construction mechanism.

3. Compare random sampling of in-context examples against alternative strategies (e.g., diversity-based sampling, difficulty-based sampling, or curriculum learning approaches) to determine if the current sampling method is optimal for the transfer mechanism.
