# Evaluation of Confidence-based Ensembling in Deep Learning Image Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.03185
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03185
- **Reference count**: 12
- **Primary result**: Three-member Conf-Ensemble with modified training heuristic slightly outperforms single model (0.10% improvement) on ImageNet

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the Conf-Ensemble technique, originally developed for binary classification, on the complex ImageNet dataset for multi-class image classification. The study systematically investigates how confidence thresholds for training and inference affect ensemble performance. Results show that while lower training thresholds and higher runtime thresholds improve accuracy, even optimized ensembles struggle to match single model performance on this complex task. A modified three-member ensemble that maintains the full dataset for each member achieves marginal improvement (0.10%) over a single model, highlighting the importance of sufficient training data in complex multi-class scenarios.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates Conf-Ensemble on ImageNet using ResNet50 architecture, comparing single models against two-member and three-member ensembles with varying confidence thresholds. Training thresholds (T1t) determine which samples are passed to subsequent ensemble members based on their classification confidence, while runtime thresholds (Tr) control when predictions are accepted during inference. The modified three-member approach uses the full dataset for all members rather than reducing dataset size. Performance is measured using top-1 accuracy and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across multiple threshold combinations.

## Key Results
- Two-member ensembles with lower training thresholds (0.01) and higher runtime thresholds yield better accuracy but still fall short of single model performance
- Three-member ensembles using the modified training heuristic (full dataset for all members) achieve a 0.10% improvement over single models
- The "most confident" consensus heuristic outperforms the "last member" heuristic, suggesting later ensemble members aren't consistently better at handling difficult samples
- For complex multi-class tasks, maintaining large training datasets for each ensemble member is crucial for achieving competitive performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Lower training thresholds improve performance by including more difficult samples
When training thresholds are low, more samples from the original dataset are included in training subsequent ensemble members, preventing underfitting and enabling complex feature learning. This works because the original training dataset contains sufficient difficult samples to improve ensemble performance when more of them are included in training. The experimental evidence shows that the ensemble with training threshold T L=1 r = 0.01 achieved higher accuracies compared to ensembles with higher thresholds.

### Mechanism 2: Higher runtime thresholds improve accuracy by leveraging earlier models
When the runtime threshold is high, predictions from earlier ensemble members (trained on the full dataset) are accepted more often, reducing reliance on less-trained subsequent members. This mechanism assumes that earlier ensemble members trained on the full dataset are more accurate than subsequent members trained on subsets of difficult samples. The evidence shows that higher run-time thresholds consistently work better across different ensemble configurations.

### Mechanism 3: "Most confident" consensus heuristic selects optimal predictions
The "most confident" consensus heuristic outperforms "last member" because it selects predictions from the model with the highest confidence score across all ensemble members, regardless of model position. This mechanism assumes that confidence scores correlate with prediction accuracy, and higher confidence indicates better predictions. Experimental results demonstrate that this approach consistently outperforms simply using the last ensemble member's prediction.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input layer (224x224x3 RGB images) -> Base model (ResNet50) -> Confidence metric (uncertainty score U) -> Training pipeline (sequential training with confidence thresholds) -> Inference pipeline (sequential evaluation with runtime thresholds and consensus heuristics)

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Train first ensemble member (M0) on full ImageNet training dataset
  2. Calculate uncertainty scores for all training samples using M0
  3. Select samples for next ensemble member based on training threshold
  4. Train subsequent ensemble member on selected samples
  5. Repeat steps 2-4 for additional ensemble members
  6. During inference, evaluate samples sequentially through ensemble members
  7. Accept predictions based on runtime thresholds or use consensus heuristic

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Training threshold vs. dataset size: Lower thresholds include more samples but may include easier samples; higher thresholds focus on difficult samples but may lead to underfitting
  - Runtime threshold vs. model utilization: Higher thresholds favor early models (more data) but may miss benefits of specialized later models; lower thresholds utilize later models but risk using less accurate predictions
  - Consensus heuristic choice: "Most confident" may select from earlier models; "last member" ensures specialized training is utilized but may select from less accurate models

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Performance worse than single model: Indicates that ensemble specialization is not beneficial or that training data is insufficient for specialized models
  - High variance in performance across different threshold combinations: Suggests that optimal thresholds are highly dependent on dataset characteristics and task complexity
  - Calibration errors: Indicates that confidence scores do not correlate well with actual prediction accuracy

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare single ResNet50 model vs. two-member Conf-Ensemble with varying training thresholds (0.2, 0.1, 0.01) and runtime thresholds (0.4, 0.2, 0.1, 0.01)
  2. Evaluate three-member Conf-Ensemble using modified training heuristic (full dataset for all members) with same threshold combinations as experiment 1
  3. Analyze confidence score distributions and calibration metrics for best-performing ensembles from experiments 1 and 2 to understand why they succeed or fail

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can Conf-Ensemble be systematically optimized for multi-class image classification tasks? The paper states that optimal training and runtime thresholds must be empirically determined and that systematic study of their interrelation is yet to be conducted. Current experiments rely on manual threshold selection without a principled optimization framework.

### Open Question 2
What is the fundamental trade-off between focused training on difficult samples and maintaining sufficient training data volume in Conf-Ensemble? The paper identifies a contradiction between "focused" training and the need for large training datasets, noting that the pure Conf-Ensemble approach leads to dataset reduction that limits accuracy. The paper proposes a heuristic modification but doesn't provide a theoretical framework for understanding this trade-off.

### Open Question 3
How does Conf-Ensemble performance scale with increasing model complexity and dataset size in multi-class classification? The paper notes that Conf-Ensemble was successful in binary classification with ~80 features but shows limited benefits in complex image classification with 150,528 features and 1000 classes. The paper only tests ResNet50 on ImageNet, providing limited insight into scalability.

## Limitations
- Conf-Ensemble shows limited effectiveness for complex multi-class tasks compared to simpler binary classification tasks
- Optimal threshold selection requires extensive empirical tuning without systematic optimization framework
- Performance improvements are marginal (0.10%) even with modified training approaches
- Study limited to ResNet50 architecture on ImageNet, limiting generalizability to other model types and datasets

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Lower training thresholds and higher runtime thresholds improve accuracy - well-supported by experimental results across multiple threshold combinations
- **Medium confidence**: Maintaining large training datasets for each ensemble member is crucial for complex tasks - supported by results but could benefit from more extensive ablation studies
- **Low confidence**: Conf-Ensemble is less effective for complex multi-class tasks compared to simpler binary tasks - requires broader comparative studies across different dataset complexities
