# Beware of Instantaneous Dependence in Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05458
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05458
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: Ignoring instantaneous dependence in MBRL can lead to suboptimal policies; a plug-and-play method using non-diagonal covariance matrices improves performance

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how ignoring instantaneous dependence between state variables at the same time step can lead to suboptimal policies in model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL). The authors prove that a lagged model assuming conditional independence is optimality-consistent only under rare conditions, while generally resulting in optimality-inconsistent policies. They propose a simple method that enables existing MBRL algorithms to consider instantaneous dependence by using non-diagonal covariance matrices in the probabilistic environment model. Experiments on dynamics-aware control tasks and MuJoCo environments demonstrate that their method achieves lower model prediction error and higher policy return compared to baseline lagged models.

## Method Summary
The proposed method modifies existing MBRL algorithms by replacing the diagonal covariance matrices typically used in probabilistic environment models with non-diagonal ones. This allows the model to capture instantaneous dependence between state variables at the same time step. The correlation coefficient matrix Γ is learned from prediction errors, enabling the model to make predictions that account for these dependencies. The method is designed as a plug-and-play addition that can be integrated into existing MBRL frameworks without requiring fundamental architectural changes.

## Key Results
- The proposed method achieves lower model prediction error compared to baseline lagged models
- Policy performance improves significantly on MuJoCo benchmarks (Inverted Pendulum, Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d)
- The method effectively captures instantaneous dependence structures, as evidenced by smaller likelihood loss

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Ignoring instantaneous dependence in MBRL leads to suboptimal policies.
- Mechanism: A lagged model assumes future state variables are conditionally independent given past states. When instantaneous dependence exists, this assumption breaks the transition function's true structure, causing the learned policy to deviate from the optimal one.
- Core assumption: Instantaneous dependence between state variables at the same time step affects the optimal policy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we prove that with few exceptions, ignoring instantaneous dependence can result in suboptimal policy learning in MBRL."
  - [section 4.3] "Theorem 4.6... if for any policy π, the stochastic transition function P(st/divides.alt0st−1,π(st−1)) is G−invariant and the reward function R(st,π(st)) as a function of st satisfies that GR(st)≡ 0, then we have β = 0 for any (st,a 0,a 1)."
- Break condition: When the transition function is G-invariant and the reward function satisfies GR(st)≡ 0, the lagged model can still achieve optimality-consistency.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The proposed plug-and-play method enables existing MBRL algorithms to consider instantaneous dependence.
- Mechanism: By using non-diagonal covariance matrices in the probabilistic environment model, the method captures the instantaneous dependence between state variables. The correlation coefficient matrix Γ is learned by leveraging prediction errors.
- Core assumption: Non-diagonal covariance matrices can effectively model the instantaneous dependence between state variables.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose a simple plug-and-play method to enable existing MBRL algorithms to take instantaneous dependence into account. Through experiments on two benchmarks, we... (3) verify that our method effectively enables reinforcement learning with instantaneous dependence and improves policy performance."
  - [section 5] "We introduce non-diagonal covariance matrix predictions... We denote the correlation coefficient matrix as Γ and the resulting model rollout process is presented in Algorithm 1."
- Break condition: When the instantaneous dependence structure is too complex to be captured by the correlation coefficient matrix Γ.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The proposed method improves policy performance by reducing model prediction error.
- Mechanism: By considering instantaneous dependence, the model makes more accurate predictions of future states. This leads to a more accurate environment model, which in turn enables better policy learning.
- Core assumption: More accurate state predictions lead to better policy performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The results show that the proposed method outperforms the baseline using a lagged model, achieving lower model prediction error and higher policy return."
  - [section 6.3] "The results show that our algorithm achieves smaller likelihood loss and higher policy returns, which indicates that our model makes predictions with instantaneous dependence, and it also benefits policy learning."
- Break condition: When the policy performance is not sensitive to small improvements in model prediction accuracy.

## Limitations

- The theoretical framework assumes full observability, limiting its applicability to partially observable environments
- The experimental evaluation is primarily conducted on standard MuJoCo benchmarks, which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world environments with pronounced instantaneous dependencies
- The method's efficiency and scalability in complex environments with high-dimensional state spaces remains to be thoroughly evaluated
