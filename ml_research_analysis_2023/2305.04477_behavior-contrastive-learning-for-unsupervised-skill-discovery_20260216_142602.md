# Behavior Contrastive Learning for Unsupervised Skill Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04477
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04477
- Reference count: 32
- One-line primary result: BeCL achieves 75.38% IQM score and 25.44% OG score on URLB benchmark

## Executive Summary
Behavior Contrastive Learning (BeCL) is a novel unsupervised skill discovery method that learns diverse and far-reaching skills by maximizing the mutual information between different behaviors based on the same skill. Unlike previous methods that often learn simple and static skills, BeCL encourages the agent to produce similar behaviors for the same skill and diverse behaviors for different skills. The method uses contrastive learning to approximate the MI objective in high-dimensional tasks, leading to better state coverage and improved downstream adaptation performance.

## Method Summary
BeCL uses contrastive learning to maximize the mutual information between different states generated by the same skill (I(S(1); S(2))), which serves as an upper bound for the traditional skill discovery MI objective I(S; Z). The method samples positive and negative pairs of states from trajectories, computes a contrastive loss with a temperature parameter κ, and uses this loss as an intrinsic reward for policy training. After pretraining for 2M steps, each skill is finetuned on downstream tasks for 100K steps, achieving competitive performance on the URLB benchmark.

## Key Results
- BeCL achieves the highest Interquartile Mean (IQM) score of 75.38% on the URLB benchmark
- BeCL obtains the lowest Optimality Gap (OG) score of 25.44% on the URLB benchmark
- The method generates diverse and far-reaching skills that outperform state-of-the-art baselines in challenging mazes and continuous control tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The MI between different states generated by the same skill (I(S(1); S(2))) serves as an upper bound for the traditional skill discovery MI objective I(S; Z).
- **Mechanism:** By constructing two views (trajectories) from the same skill and maximizing their mutual information, the method implicitly maximizes I(S; Z) because of the redundancy assumption: S(1) and S(2) share the same skill-relevant information.
- **Core assumption:** The states S(1) and S(2) are mutually redundant to the skill-relevant information (i.e., S(1)⊥ ⊥Z|S(2)).

- **Break condition:** If the redundancy assumption fails (i.e., the skill information shared between S(1) and S(2) is not the same), the upper bound relationship no longer holds.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Maximizing I(S(1); S(2)) implicitly increases state entropy, leading to better state coverage.
- **Mechanism:** Through contrastive estimation, the method pushes states from different skills away from each other in feature space, encouraging a more uniform distribution of states and thus higher entropy.
- **Core assumption:** The state features lie on a hypersphere and can be modeled with a von Mises-Fisher distribution.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Meanwhile, our method implicitly increases the state entropy to obtain better state coverage."
  - [section]: "Our method implicitly increases the state entropy to encourage a better coverage of the environment, which prevents the agent from learning static skills."

- **Break condition:** If the state space is not well-represented by the contrastive features or if the temperature parameter is poorly chosen, the entropy maximization effect may be diminished.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The contrastive loss balances the uniformity-tolerance dilemma, ensuring skills are globally separated while locally clustered.
- **Mechanism:** By adjusting the temperature parameter κ, the method can control the strength of penalties, encouraging skills to explore different regions (uniformity) while maintaining consistency within the same skill (tolerance).
- **Core assumption:** A suitable κ can avoid the gathering of different skills in local areas while maintaining the alignment of positive samples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: Not directly mentioned, but implied by the discussion of diverse skills.
  - [section]: "A suitable κ may avoid the gathering of different skills in local areas, which helps to explore different regions and improves the state coverage."
  - [corpus]: Weak or missing - the temperature's role in balancing uniformity and tolerance is mentioned but not deeply explored in the corpus.
- **Break condition:** If κ is set too low, the method may weaken the discriminability of skills; if too high, it may not sufficiently encourage exploration.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Skill sampling -> Policy (π(a|s,z)) -> Environment -> Encoder (fφ(s)) -> Contrastive loss -> Intrinsic reward
- **Critical path:**
  1. Sample skills and interact with the environment to generate trajectories.
  2. Sample states from these trajectories to form positive and negative pairs.
  3. Compute the contrastive loss and update the encoder.
  4. Use the contrastive loss as an intrinsic reward to update the policy.
  5. Repeat until convergence.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Skill dimensionality vs. exploration efficiency
  - Temperature parameter κ vs. uniformity-tolerance balance
  - Contrastive loss weight vs. policy learning stability
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Skills become too similar (lack of diversity)
  - State coverage is poor (skills don't explore different regions)
  - Policy learning is unstable (due to poor reward signals)
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test the effect of skill dimensionality on state coverage in a simple 2D maze.
  2. Vary the temperature parameter κ to observe its impact on skill diversity and state coverage.
  3. Compare the adaptation efficiency of learned skills in a downstream task with and without contrastive learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the performance of BeCL scale with the dimensionality of the skill space, and what is the optimal dimensionality for different downstream tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper mentions that increasing skill dimensions can benefit adaptation performance in hard downstream tasks like (Walker, Run) and (Walker, Flip), but not in relatively easy tasks like (Walker, Stand).
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not provide a systematic study of the impact of skill dimensions across all tasks and domains. The optimal dimensionality for different tasks and domains remains unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive study varying skill dimensions across all tasks and domains, measuring adaptation performance to determine optimal skill space dimensionality for each scenario.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the temperature parameter κ in the contrastive objective affect the diversity and discriminability of the learned skills?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper discusses that lower temperatures encourage skills to cover more of the state space, but too small temperatures can weaken skill discriminability.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only provides a qualitative analysis of the effect of κ on skill behavior in a 2D maze. A systematic study of the impact of κ on skill diversity and discriminability in complex tasks is needed.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive study varying κ across tasks and domains, measuring skill diversity (e.g., state coverage) and discriminability (e.g., skill classification accuracy) to find the optimal κ for each scenario.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Can BeCL be extended to image-based tasks, and how does its performance compare to state-based tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper mentions initial results of BeCL in pixels-based walker tasks using PIEG encoder, showing better performance than image-based CIC but still underperforming state-based tasks.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only provides initial results for one domain. The performance of BeCL on image-based tasks across all URLB domains and its comparison to state-based performance remains unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive study applying BeCL to all image-based tasks in URLB, measuring performance and comparing it to state-based performance to determine the effectiveness of BeCL in image-based scenarios.

## Limitations

- The theoretical claims about upper bounds and entropy maximization are not empirically validated.
- The optimal temperature parameter κ for balancing uniformity and tolerance is not thoroughly explored.
- The method's performance on image-based tasks is initially lower than state-based tasks, requiring additional techniques like PIEG encoder.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The contrastive learning framework and its implementation details are well-specified and reproducible.
- **Medium Confidence:** The performance improvements on URLB benchmark are demonstrated, but the ablation studies on key design choices (temperature, skill dimension) are limited.
- **Low Confidence:** The theoretical claims about upper bounds and entropy maximization are not empirically validated.
