# Unified Emulation-Simulation Training Environment for Autonomous Cyber Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01244
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01244
- Reference count: 23
- One-line result: Training times reduced from days to minutes while maintaining policy transfer from simulation to real networks

## Executive Summary
This paper presents CyGIL, a unified emulation-simulation training environment for autonomous cyber agents using reinforcement learning. The core innovation addresses the challenge of building high-fidelity simulators for complex cyber operations by automatically generating a simulated environment (CyGIL-S) from data collected in an emulated real network (CyGIL-E). The unified training framework integrates CyGIL-E and CyGIL-S in a cross-training loop to reduce data collection and training time while maintaining policy transferability to real networks.

## Method Summary
CyGIL collects real-world network data during emulation, then uses representation learning to construct a simulator that preserves state transitions and action outcomes. The unified training loop alternates between slow, high-fidelity training in CyGIL-E (to collect representative data) and fast training in CyGIL-S (to quickly refine policies). This iterative approach allows agents to explore promising regions rapidly while maintaining realism. The system uses different reinforcement learning algorithms at different stages: DQN for initial exploration, PPO for fast training in CyGIL-S, and C51 Rainbow for final optimization.

## Key Results
- Agents trained in CyGIL-S achieve optimal policies transferable to CyGIL-E
- Training times reduced from days to minutes compared to training solely in CyGIL-E
- Cross-training between emulation and simulation reduces total data collection requirements
- Representation learning in early segments focuses data collection on relevant state space regions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Automatically generated simulators from emulation data enable faster agent training with high fidelity.
- Mechanism: CyGIL collects real-world network data during emulation (CyGIL-E), then uses representation learning to construct a simulator (CyGIL-S) that preserves state transitions and action outcomes. This avoids the need for manually designing high-fidelity simulators from scratch.
- Core assumption: The collected trace data is sufficient to represent all relevant state transitions needed for optimal policy learning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Unified cross-training between emulation and simulation reduces total data collection and training time.
- Mechanism: The system alternates between slow, high-fidelity training in CyGIL-E (to collect representative data) and fast training in CyGIL-S (to quickly refine policies). This iterative loop allows the agent to explore promising regions rapidly while maintaining realism.
- Core assumption: Fast policy improvements in CyGIL-S are transferable back to CyGIL-E, and data collected in CyGIL-E after transfer remains relevant.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Representation learning in early training segments focuses data collection on relevant state space regions.
- Mechanism: Initial training in CyGIL-E with sample-efficient algorithms like DQN explores the state space broadly but slowly. This exploration identifies valuable transitions, which are then used to build a CyGIL-S that prioritizes these promising regions, avoiding wasted exploration of irrelevant areas.
- Core assumption: Early exploration in CyGIL-E is sufficiently broad to capture the necessary transitions for building a useful CyGIL-S.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Markov Decision Process (MDP) modeling of network environments
  - Why needed here: The paper explicitly models the training environment as an MDP to formalize state transitions, actions, and rewards, enabling rigorous algorithm design and analysis.
  - Quick check question: In an MDP, what does the transition probability P(s'|s,a) represent?

- Concept: Representation learning for state space approximation
  - Why needed here: The true network state space is too large and partially unobservable; representation learning allows approximating it from observable traces, making simulator generation feasible.
  - Quick check question: Why can't we directly use the full network state space for simulator generation?

- Concept: Transfer learning between simulation and emulation environments
  - Why needed here: The paper relies on transferring learned policies from fast simulation back to realistic emulation to maintain both training speed and real-world applicability.
  - Quick check question: What conditions must hold for a policy trained in simulation to transfer successfully to emulation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CyGIL-E (emulated network) -> Data Collection Pipeline -> CyGIL-S (generated simulator) -> Agent Training Node -> Cross-Training Controller -> Back to CyGIL-E
- Critical path: Data collection in CyGIL-E → CyGIL-S generation → Fast training in CyGIL-S → Policy transfer back to CyGIL-E → Repeat until optimal
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fidelity vs. speed: CyGIL-E is slow but realistic; CyGIL-S is fast but potentially incomplete
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: Early CyGIL-E exploration must be broad enough but not wasteful
  - Algorithm selection: Sample-efficient algorithms for early exploration, fast-converging for CyGIL-S refinement
- Failure signatures:
  - Agent performance degrades when transferred from CyGIL-S to CyGIL-E
  - Training loops get stuck in local optima across multiple iterations
  - Unknown state transitions increase dramatically, causing high penalties
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run basic DQN training in CyGIL-E for 10 episodes, collect trace data, generate initial CyGIL-S, train PPO agent in CyGIL-S, measure transfer performance
  2. Vary the trigger threshold for switching between E and S segments, measure impact on total training time and final policy quality
  3. Introduce controlled gaps in trace data collection, observe how unknown state transitions affect CyGIL-S training stability and final performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal balance between data collection in CyGIL-E and training in CyGIL-S to achieve the best performance while minimizing training time?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenge of collecting sufficient data in CyGIL-E to generate a high-fidelity CyGIL-S, and presents a unified training approach that iteratively improves both environments.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that the current parameters for switching between CyGIL-E and CyGIL-S are heuristic and need further research to optimize across different CyOp scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing different strategies for data collection and training iterations, showing the trade-offs between training time and agent performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the issue of unknown state transitions in CyGIL-S be effectively managed to improve the fidelity and performance of the simulator?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that unknown transitions are encountered in CyGIL-S due to unobserved state changes in CyGIL-E, and suggests that managing this problem is critical for reducing data collection.
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions that more data can support better transition approximation, but does not provide a definitive solution for handling unknown transitions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of methods or algorithms that can predict or infer unknown state transitions, validated through experiments showing improved agent training in CyGIL-S.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective reinforcement learning algorithms for different stages of the CyGIL training cycle, and how do they impact the overall performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the use of different algorithms like DQN, PPO, and C51 Rainbow in various segments of the training loop, but notes that the selection is heuristic and requires further research.
- Why unresolved: The paper indicates that the effectiveness of algorithms in handling local optima and improving agent performance is not fully understood and needs more exploration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different RL algorithms applied to the CyGIL framework, demonstrating their impact on training efficiency and agent performance across diverse scenarios.

## Limitations

- The approach assumes trace data collected from CyGIL-E is sufficient to represent all relevant state transitions for optimal policy learning
- Representation learning relies on quality and coverage of collected traces rather than explicitly modeling network dynamics
- Generalization to different network architectures or attack scenarios beyond the specific topology tested remains unverified

## Confidence

- Core claims: Medium
  - Experimental results demonstrate successful policy transfer and training time reduction in the specific network topology tested
  - Lack of ablation studies showing performance with incomplete data or sensitivity to segment switching thresholds
- Mechanism explanations: Medium
  - Clear theoretical justification for cross-training approach
  - Implementation details of representation learning and FSM construction are somewhat abstract
- Data completeness assumption: Low
  - Critical dependency on collected trace data being representative of all relevant state transitions
  - No systematic analysis of minimum data requirements or impact of missing transitions

## Next Checks

1. **Data Completeness Test**: Systematically remove portions of trace data from CyGIL-E before CyGIL-S generation and measure the impact on policy transfer quality. This would reveal the minimum data requirements and identify which types of transitions are most critical for simulator fidelity.

2. **Cross-Environment Generalization**: Test the trained agent on modified network topologies or different attack objectives not seen during training. This would validate whether the learned policies are truly robust or simply overfit to the specific test environment.

3. **Algorithm Ablation Study**: Replace the unified training loop with pure CyGIL-E training and pure CyGIL-S training (without cross-training) to quantify the exact contribution of the cross-training mechanism versus the individual benefits of each environment.
