# Spiking Synaptic Penalty: Appropriate Penalty Term for Energy-Efficient Spiking Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01500
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes spiking synaptic penalty term that directly optimizes energy consumption metric in SNNs, achieving better energy-accuracy trade-off than existing methods while maintaining accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel spiking synaptic penalty term for optimizing energy consumption in spiking neural networks (SNNs). The key innovation is deriving a penalty term whose expected value is precisely proportional to the energy consumption metric (ESNN), enabling direct energy optimization without modifying network architecture. The method demonstrates superior performance compared to existing penalty terms and conversion approaches from artificial neural networks, particularly when using p=1 in the penalty formulation. Experiments on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 datasets show the proposed method achieves better energy-accuracy trade-offs across multiple architectures including CNN7, VGG11, and ResNet18.

## Method Summary
The method introduces a spiking synaptic penalty term Ωsyn into the objective function that directly optimizes energy consumption in SNNs. The penalty term scales each spike contribution by its synaptic weight count, making its expected value proportional to the energy consumption metric ESNN. This allows direct energy minimization during training using backpropagation with surrogate gradients, without requiring architectural modifications. The penalty works independently of the choice of surrogate gradient function and includes a p parameter that controls the energy-accuracy trade-off, with p=1 showing better performance than p=2. The method is compared against baseline models, other penalty terms (Ωtotal, Ωbalance), and conversion approaches from artificial neural networks.

## Key Results
- The spiking synaptic penalty with p=1 achieves better energy-accuracy trade-off than p=2 across all tested architectures and datasets
- The method outperforms conversion approaches from artificial neural networks while maintaining competitive accuracy
- The penalty term works independently of the choice of surrogate gradient function
- Including weight decay helps create neuron sparsification, further reducing energy consumption

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The spiking synaptic penalty term directly optimizes the energy consumption metric by scaling each spike contribution by its synaptic weight count. The penalty term is derived so that its expected value is precisely proportional to the energy consumption metric (ESNN), making it possible to directly minimize energy during training without modifying network architecture. This works because the relationship between spike activity and energy consumption is linear and can be captured by counting outgoing synapses per neuron.

### Mechanism 2
The p=1 option in the penalty term provides better energy-accuracy trade-off than p=2 due to backward signal control. When p=1, the derivative of the penalty term is simpler and doesn't suppress backward gradients for non-firing spikes, allowing better gradient flow during training. This creates more effective optimization dynamics that balance energy reduction with accuracy maintenance.

### Mechanism 3
The spiking synaptic penalty works independently of the choice of surrogate gradient function. The derivation of the penalty term is independent of the specific surrogate gradient function used, making it compatible with various differentiable approximations of the spiking neuron. This generality allows the method to be applied with different training approaches without modification.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Single-step spiking neurons with membrane potential and spike output -> Spiking synaptic penalty (Ωsyn) scaled by synaptic weight count -> Cross-entropy + λΩsyn + λWD weight decay -> Backpropagation with surrogate gradient -> Energy consumption metric (ESNN) and accuracy evaluation

- **Critical path**: 1) Forward pass: Compute membrane potentials, apply surrogate gradient for spikes; 2) Penalty calculation: Compute Ωsyn using synaptic weight counts; 3) Backward pass: Compute gradients using surrogate gradient and penalty term; 4) Parameter update: Apply optimizer with learning rate schedule; 5) Evaluation: Measure energy consumption and accuracy on validation set

- **Design tradeoffs**: p=1 vs p=2 in penalty term: p=1 provides better energy-accuracy trade-off but may be less stable; Weight decay inclusion: Helps with neuron sparsification but adds complexity to hyperparameter tuning; BBN (batch norm weight decay): Including it simplifies implementation but may affect normalization layer behavior

- **Failure signatures**: Training collapse: Accuracy drops to ~20% indicating penalty term is too strong; Energy-accuracy trade-off not improving: Penalty term not effectively reducing energy consumption; Dead neurons not forming: Weight decay or penalty term not creating sufficient sparsity

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Implement single-step spiking neuron with surrogate gradient and verify basic forward/backward pass; 2) Add Ωsyn penalty term with p=1 and verify it reduces energy consumption while maintaining accuracy; 3) Compare p=1 vs p=2 penalty terms and verify the energy-accuracy trade-off difference

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Why does the choice of p = 1 versus p > 1 in the spiking synaptic penalty affect the training outcome differently? The paper mentions that p = 1 is apparently better than p = 2 in experimental results, but the mechanism by which this affects the training result is still unclear and remains a future work. This requires a theoretical analysis explaining how the choice of p affects the surrogate gradient and the subsequent training dynamics.

### Open Question 2
How does the spiking synaptic penalty interact with pruning techniques to create more energy-efficient SNNs? The paper mentions that the high availability of the spiking synaptic penalty should be verified in real datasets, large networks, and other tasks, and notes that the synergy between spike sparsification and pruning was not focused on. This requires experiments comparing energy efficiency with penalty alone versus in combination with pruning techniques.

### Open Question 3
Can the spiking synaptic penalty be adapted to optimize other energy consumption metrics beyond the one used in this study? The paper mentions that if the target metric becomes deformed, the penalty should be accordingly deformed in accordance with their principle, even though it is a metric irrelevant to the energy consumption. This requires a theoretical framework for deriving penalty terms for different energy consumption metrics.

## Limitations

- The experimental scope is limited to image classification tasks on standard datasets without testing on more complex vision tasks or non-vision domains
- The comparison with conversion approaches lacks detail about implementation specifics, making fair assessment difficult
- The energy consumption model assumes specific hardware implementation that may not generalize to all neuromorphic platforms

## Confidence

High confidence: The core mathematical derivation of the spiking synaptic penalty term is sound and the energy consumption metric calculation is well-defined.

Medium confidence: The independence claim regarding surrogate gradient functions is supported by experiments with two different functions, but broader testing would strengthen this claim.

Low confidence: The comparison with conversion approaches lacks detail about implementation specifics, making it difficult to assess whether the comparison is truly fair.
