# Human-like Summarization Evaluation with ChatGPT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02554
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02554
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: ChatGPT can effectively perform human-like evaluation of text summarization with high correlation to human judgments

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates ChatGPT's potential as a human-like evaluator for text summarization tasks. The study explores ChatGPT's ability to perform four common human evaluation methods: Likert scale scoring, pairwise comparison, Pyramid, and binary factuality evaluation across five datasets. Results show that ChatGPT can complete these evaluations with high correlation to human judgments, often outperforming existing automatic evaluation metrics. The paper also analyzes the impact of prompt design on performance and examines the quality of generated explanations and invalid responses.

## Method Summary
The study uses ChatGPT API (gpt-3.5-turbo-0301) with temperature 0 and max_tokens 256 to perform four types of human evaluation methods on five summarization datasets. The evaluation methods include Likert scale scoring for multiple dimensions, pairwise comparison of summaries, Pyramid method for content unit matching, and binary factuality checking. Different prompt designs were tested to optimize performance, and results were compared against human annotations using Spearman's correlation and accuracy metrics. The study also analyzed invalid responses and generated explanations.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieves high correlation with human judgments across all four evaluation methods (Spearman's ρ up to 0.889 for pairwise comparison)
- ChatGPT outperforms existing automatic metrics in pairwise comparison and binary factuality tasks
- Different prompt designs significantly impact performance, with some configurations degrading results
- ChatGPT generates explanations even when not explicitly asked, which can be leveraged for additional insights
