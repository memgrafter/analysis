# Graph Neural Network-Inspired Kernels for Gaussian Processes in Semi-Supervised Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05828
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05828
- Reference count: 40
- This paper establishes a deep connection between graph neural networks (GNNs) and Gaussian processes (GPs) by showing that certain GNNs are equivalent to GPs when their layers are infinitely wide.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a deep connection between graph neural networks (GNNs) and Gaussian processes (GPs) by showing that certain GNNs are equivalent to GPs when their layers are infinitely wide. The authors derive the covariance kernel that incorporates the graph inductive bias as GNNs do, starting with the graph convolutional network (GCN) and extending to other GNN architectures. They analyze the kernel universality and the limiting behavior when the depth also tends to infinity. The paper proposes a computationally efficient approximation of the covariance matrix for scalable posterior inference with large-scale data using a Nystrom-like scheme. The proposed GP model with graph-based kernels leads to competitive classification and regression performance, as well as advantages in computation time, compared with the respective GNNs.

## Method Summary
The authors derive Gaussian process kernels from GNN architectures by analyzing the infinite-width limit where the pre-activations converge to Gaussian random variables via the central limit theorem. They start with GCN and show its equivalence to a multi-output GP with a specific covariance kernel. The kernel is computed recursively through the network layers using the arc-cosine kernel formula for ReLU activations. For scalability, they propose a Nystrom-like low-rank approximation scheme that propagates the approximation through the recursive kernel computation. The GP posterior inference uses these approximated kernels for predictions, avoiding the iterative optimization required for GNN training.

## Key Results
- GCN architecture is equivalent to a GP when layers are infinitely wide, with a closed-form covariance kernel
- The derived GP kernels lead to competitive classification and regression performance compared to the original GNNs
- The GP approach achieves significant computational advantages, being much faster than training and predicting with GNNs
- The low-rank approximation scheme enables scalability to large graphs while maintaining accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: When a graph neural network has infinitely wide layers, its output converges to a Gaussian process whose covariance kernel incorporates the graph structure.
- Mechanism: The central limit theorem applies as layer width tends to infinity, making each coordinate's pre-activation a Gaussian random variable. The post-activation covariance follows from the arc-cosine kernel of the ReLU activation applied to the Gaussian pre-activations.
- Core assumption: Weights and biases in each layer are independent and identically distributed as zero-mean Gaussians.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "a prominent example of GNNs, the graph convolutional network, is equivalent to some GP when its layers are infinitely wide"
  - [section]: "The following theorem states that when the weights and biases in each layer are iid zero-mean Gaussians, in the limit on the layer width, the GCN output z(L)(x) is a multi-output GP"
  - [corpus]: Weak - only general GP and GNN papers, no specific infinite-width equivalence
- Break condition: If weights/biases are not iid Gaussian, the CLT no longer applies and the equivalence breaks.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The kernel matrix for the limiting GP can be computed recursively using low-rank approximations that scale linearly with graph size.
- Mechanism: Starting from a low-rank approximation of the input kernel, each layer's graph convolution and activation are applied to propagate this approximation. The recursion uses the closed-form arc-cosine kernel formula to update the low-rank factors efficiently.
- Core assumption: The covariance matrix at each layer admits a low-rank approximation that can be propagated through the layer operations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We propose a Nystr¨om-like scheme to perform low-rank approximations and apply the approximation recursively on each layer, to yield a low-rank kernel matrix"
  - [section]: "we use the subscript a to denote a set of landmark nodes... The Nystr¨om approximation... is K (0):a(K (0)aa)−1K (0)a:"
  - [corpus]: Weak - only general kernel approximation papers, no specific GNN-GP low-rank scheme
- Break condition: If the landmark set is too small relative to the graph structure, the approximation error may dominate.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The GP kernels derived from GNNs maintain competitive classification and regression performance while being significantly faster to compute than the original GNNs.
- Mechanism: The GP posterior inference uses closed-form updates that avoid the iterative optimization required for GNN training. The low-rank approximation further reduces computational complexity from cubic to linear in graph size.
- Core assumption: The approximation quality is sufficient to maintain predictive accuracy while reducing computation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "demonstrate that these graph-based kernels lead to competitive classification and regression performance, as well as advantages in computation time, compared with the respective GNNs"
  - [section]: "We demonstrate through numerical experiments that the GP posterior inference is much faster than training a GNN and subsequently performing predictions"
  - [corpus]: Weak - only general GP and GNN papers, no specific timing comparison
- Break condition: If the approximation error grows with graph size or depth, the performance advantage may disappear.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Central Limit Theorem and its application to neural network layer outputs
  - Why needed here: The infinite-width equivalence relies on each layer's output being Gaussian due to the CLT
  - Quick check question: Why does the CLT apply to the pre-activation values in a neural network layer when the width tends to infinity?

- Concept: Gaussian Process kernels and their properties (positive definiteness, universality)
  - Why needed here: The limiting GP's kernel must be well-defined and capture the graph structure effectively
  - Quick check question: What properties must a kernel function have to define a valid Gaussian process?

- Concept: Low-rank matrix approximations and Nyström method
  - Why needed here: The scalability of the approach depends on efficiently approximating the kernel matrix
  - Quick check question: How does the Nyström approximation work, and what determines its quality?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input features -> Input kernel computation -> Recursive kernel composition through GNN layers -> Low-rank approximation (Nyström) -> GP posterior inference

- Critical path: For a new dataset, the engineer should: (1) construct the graph adjacency matrix with normalization, (2) choose landmark nodes for low-rank approximation, (3) compute the input kernel matrix (e.g., inner product or squared exponential), (4) apply the recursive kernel composition for the desired GNN architecture, (5) perform posterior inference using the approximated kernel.

- Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoff is between approximation quality and computational efficiency - using more landmark nodes improves accuracy but increases computation. Another tradeoff is between using a simple kernel (like inner product) versus a more complex one (like squared exponential) for the input.

- Failure signatures: Poor performance may indicate: (1) insufficient landmark nodes leading to low approximation quality, (2) inappropriate kernel choice for the data, (3) numerical instability in the recursive kernel computation, or (4) mismatch between the GNN architecture and the problem structure.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the basic GCN-GP equivalence on a small dataset (like Cora) with full kernel computation to check if the accuracy matches the original GCN.
  2. Test the low-rank approximation on a medium dataset (like PubMed) to find the minimum number of landmark nodes needed for acceptable accuracy.
  3. Compare the running time of the GP approach versus the original GNN on a large dataset (like Reddit) to quantify the speedup.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- What is the precise convergence behavior of the kernel matrix as depth approaches infinity when the weight scaling parameter is between the two known regimes?
- How does the choice of activation function affect the universality and limiting behavior of the derived GP kernels?
- What is the theoretical relationship between the number of landmark nodes and the approximation quality in the Nystrom-like scheme?

## Limitations
- The infinite-width equivalence relies on strict assumptions about weight distributions that may not hold in practical implementations
- The low-rank approximation scheme, while computationally efficient, lacks detailed validation of approximation quality across diverse graph structures
- The empirical evaluation shows competitive performance but doesn't thoroughly explore failure modes or robustness to hyperparameter choices

## Confidence
- High confidence in the theoretical derivation of the GCN-GP equivalence under idealized conditions
- Medium confidence in the practical applicability of the low-rank approximation scheme
- Low confidence in the generalizability of performance claims across diverse graph datasets
