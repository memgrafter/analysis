# On the Convergence of Stochastic Gradient Descent in Low-precision Number Formats

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01651
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01651
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Provides deterministic and stochastic analysis of SGD convergence under low-precision formats, showing how numerical precision affects convergence rates and deriving bounds for optimal step size selection.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) convergence under low-precision number formats, deriving theoretical bounds that explicitly capture the impact of numerical precision on convergence rates. The authors provide both deterministic and stochastic analyses for quasi-convex loss functions, establishing finite-iteration bounds and convergence guarantees. The work demonstrates that accumulator precision is critical for SGD convergence and provides practical guidelines for selecting optimal step sizes under precision constraints. Experimental validation using logistic regression on MNIST confirms the theoretical predictions, showing that controlled numerical errors allow convergence to points near the optimum.

## Method Summary
The authors present a comprehensive analysis of SGD under low-precision formats by first establishing deterministic convergence bounds for quasi-convex loss functions with explicit precision-dependent terms. They then extend this analysis to stochastic settings with mild assumptions on noise moments. The theoretical framework derives finite-iteration bounds showing how rounding errors in weight updates and gradient computations affect convergence rates. The analysis identifies accumulator precision as a critical factor and provides formulas for optimal step size selection under precision constraints. Experimental validation is performed using logistic regression on MNIST to verify the theoretical predictions.

## Key Results
- Finite-iteration bounds derived for deterministic SGD showing explicit dependence on numerical precision
- Convergence guarantees established for stochastic SGD under mild noise moment assumptions
- Accumulator precision identified as critical factor for SGD convergence under low-precision constraints
- Experimental validation confirms theoretical bounds with logistic regression on MNIST

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The analysis works by explicitly modeling the numerical errors introduced by low-precision arithmetic operations and incorporating these into the convergence analysis. By treating rounding errors as bounded perturbations and analyzing their propagation through the SGD update steps, the authors derive bounds that show how precision loss directly impacts the convergence rate. The key insight is that numerical errors accumulate differently depending on whether they occur in gradient computation versus weight accumulation, with accumulator precision playing a particularly crucial role.

## Foundational Learning

**Quasi-convex functions**: A broader class of functions where any local minimum is also a global minimum. Why needed: The analysis assumes quasi-convexity to guarantee convergence to the global optimum. Quick check: Verify that the loss function satisfies the quasi-convexity condition.

**Stochastic Gradient Descent**: Optimization algorithm that uses noisy gradient estimates to minimize loss functions. Why needed: Forms the foundation for the convergence analysis under low-precision arithmetic. Quick check: Ensure SGD update equations are correctly implemented.

**Numerical precision and rounding errors**: The finite representation of real numbers in computer systems leading to computational errors. Why needed: Central to understanding how low-precision formats affect convergence. Quick check: Quantify the magnitude of rounding errors in gradient and weight updates.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Data -> Gradient Computation -> Weight Update (low-precision) -> Accumulator (high-precision) -> Loss Evaluation -> Next iteration

**Critical path**: The weight update and accumulation steps are critical, as numerical errors here directly affect convergence. The accumulator precision determines whether errors accumulate constructively or destructively.

**Design tradeoffs**: Lower precision reduces memory bandwidth and computational cost but increases numerical errors; higher accumulator precision mitigates error accumulation but increases resource usage. Optimal step size selection requires balancing these factors.

**Failure signatures**: Divergence or stagnation in loss trajectories, particularly when numerical errors dominate the gradient signal. Early signs include increased variance in update steps and failure to improve beyond certain loss thresholds.

## Limitations
- Analysis assumes quasi-convex loss functions, which may not represent complex deep learning landscapes
- Stochastic analysis relies on assumptions about noise moment distributions that remain uncharacterized
- Experimental validation limited to simple logistic regression on MNIST, may not generalize to complex architectures
