# Multipar-T: Multiparty-Transformer for Capturing Contingent Behaviors in Group Conversations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12204
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12204
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in average F-1 scores by 5.2% and individual class F-1 scores by up to 10.0%

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Multipar-T, a transformer model designed to capture contingent behaviors in group conversations for engagement prediction. The core innovation is Crossperson Attention (CPA), which detects temporal alignments between behavioral sequences of different individuals. Evaluated on a video-based group engagement detection benchmark, Multipar-T achieves significant improvements over existing methods, with a 5.2% increase in macro F1 score. The model successfully identifies contingent behaviors through attention weights and provides qualitative insights into group dynamics.

## Method Summary
The Multipar-T model processes video-based group conversations by extracting behavioral features (eye gaze, head location, facial landmarks, action units, and image features) from 64-frame intervals. It uses 1D convolutional layers with positional encoding for individual temporal encoding, Crossperson Attention to capture contingent behaviors between pairs of people, and self-attention for non-contingent behaviors. The model employs multi-layer Crossperson Transformers and a temporal classifier (LSTM + FCN) to predict engagement levels. Training uses focal loss and oversampling to handle class imbalance, with evaluation on accuracy, weighted F1, and macro F1 metrics.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art approaches by 5.2% in average F-1 scores
- Achieves up to 10.0% improvement in individual class F-1 scores
- Demonstrates superior performance in detecting low engagement and disengagement classes
- Qualitative analysis reveals meaningful contingent behaviors through attention visualization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Crossperson Attention captures contingent behavior by using one person's behavioral sequence as query and another's as key/value. The attention weights measure temporal alignment between sequences, producing embeddings that emphasize target behaviors contingent on others' actions. This works under the assumption that contingent behaviors manifest as temporal correlations between individuals' actions.

### Mechanism 2
The direction of CPA (other→self vs self→other) critically affects modeling contingent influences. When attention flows from other→self, the embedding prioritizes target behaviors influenced by others, which empirically works better for engagement prediction. This assumes contingent influence is asymmetric in typical engagement contexts.

### Mechanism 3
Multiple layers of Crossperson Transformer blocks allow contextualization of contingent behaviors with respect to the target's own behavior patterns. The first layer captures raw contingent influences while subsequent layers relate these to different parts of the target's own sequence, enriching the representation. This assumes contingent behaviors interact with non-contingent behaviors in complex ways requiring multi-layer processing.

## Foundational Learning

- **Transformer attention mechanisms**: CPA reuses scaled dot-product attention to align behavioral sequences across individuals. Quick check: What is the difference between self-attention and cross-attention in terms of query/key/value sources?

- **Temporal encoding in sequence models**: The model processes k-frame behavioral sequences and uses positional encoding to preserve temporal order. Quick check: Why does the model add positional encoding to convolutional outputs rather than relying on convolutions alone?

- **Class imbalance handling**: Engagement data is heavily skewed toward high engagement; focal loss and oversampling address this. Quick check: How does focal loss down-weight easy examples compared to standard cross-entropy?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Conv1D → CPA (other→self) → CPT layers → Self-Attention → Concatenate → LSTM → FFN → Prediction

- **Critical path**: Individual Temporal Encoder (Conv1D + PE) → Crossperson Transformer (CPA + FFN + Norm) → Self-Transformer → Concatenate → Temporal Classifier (LSTM + FFN) → Engagement Prediction

- **Design tradeoffs**: CPA captures interpersonal influences but requires pairwise computation vs. self-attention handling individual patterns; attention direction prioritizes target-influenced behaviors; multi-head allows capturing different contingent relationship types but increases parameters

- **Failure signatures**: Uniform attention weights indicate CPA isn't learning contingent relationships; performance drops when ablating CPA suggest contingent behavior is critical; poor macro-F1 with imbalanced classes suggests model isn't learning from rare disengagement examples

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Compare CPAother→self vs CPAself→other to verify attention direction matters; 2) Ablate Crossperson Transformer vs Self-Transformer to isolate contributions; 3) Vary number of CPT layers to find optimal depth for contextualization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. How well does the model generalize to in-person group interactions and diverse cultural contexts, given evaluation on online learning activities? The paper suggests future work should test generalizability across different group settings, relationships, and cultures.

2. Can Crossperson Attention effectively capture contingent behaviors when using multimodal inputs (language and acoustics) in addition to visual features? The paper notes that contingent behavior exists in language and acoustics as well, and multimodal input should be investigated.

3. How does the model perform when the number of participants varies, and can it be adapted to handle variable group sizes? The paper mentions the current model is specific to 5-person settings and future work should focus on developing approaches for variable group sizes.

## Limitations

- The mechanism assumes contingent behavior manifests as temporal alignment between behavioral sequences, but provides limited theoretical justification for why scaled dot-product attention captures this phenomenon
- The superiority of CPAother→self over CPAself→other direction is validated empirically but lacks deeper explanation of the underlying behavioral psychology
- Claims about discovering new contingent behaviors through qualitative analysis rely on subjective interpretation rather than quantitative metrics

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Empirical improvements in engagement detection (5.2% macro F1 gain) supported by ablation studies and baseline comparisons
- **Medium confidence**: Crossperson Attention's ability to capture contingent behaviors is theoretically plausible but lacks rigorous validation against ground truth contingent behavior annotations
- **Low confidence**: Claims about discovering new contingent behaviors through qualitative analysis, as this relies on subjective interpretation

## Next Checks

1. Conduct a controlled experiment where ground truth contingent behavior pairs are synthetically inserted into the data to verify CPA can detect known contingent relationships
2. Perform a human evaluation study where annotators rate whether the top-k attention-weighted behavior pairs correspond to meaningful contingent interactions
3. Test the model's robustness by evaluating performance when the number of participants varies or when behavioral modalities are selectively removed
