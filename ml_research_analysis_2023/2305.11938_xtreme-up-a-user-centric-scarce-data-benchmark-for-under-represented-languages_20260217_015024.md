# XTREME-UP: A User-Centric Scarce-Data Benchmark for Under-Represented Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11938
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11938
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: XTREME-UP introduces a benchmark for under-represented languages with 88 ULs across 9 user-centric tasks, showing byte-based models outperform subword-based models and fine-tuning outperforms in-context learning on limited data.

## Executive Summary
XTREME-UP is a benchmark designed to evaluate language models on under-represented languages (ULs) using user-centric tasks that have broad adoption among high-resource language speakers. The benchmark focuses on scarce-data scenarios rather than zero-shot settings, covering 88 ULs across 9 key technologies including ASR, OCR, MT, and information access tasks. XTREME-UP introduces new datasets for OCR, autocomplete, semantic parsing, and transliteration, while building on existing datasets for other tasks. The benchmark provides methodology for evaluating various modeling scenarios including text-only, multi-modal, supervised parameter tuning, and in-context learning approaches.

## Method Summary
The benchmark evaluates models on 88 under-represented languages across 9 tasks with limited data (maximum 8 hours of annotation per language). Models are fine-tuned on combined training data across ULs using mT5-base and ByT5-base architectures, then evaluated on task-specific metrics averaged over languages. The evaluation includes both fine-tuning and in-context learning (ICL) scenarios using Flan-PaLM-62B. For each task, specific metrics are used including CER for ASR, chrF for MT, Acc@3 for autocomplete, F1 for NER, and MRR/EM for retrieval and QA respectively.

## Key Results
- Byte-based ByT5 models outperform subword-based mT5 models across most tasks, with particularly strong gains on character-level tasks like autocomplete and transliteration
- Fine-tuning approaches consistently outperform in-context learning on limited data scenarios for under-represented languages
- There remains significant headroom for improvement, especially on tasks requiring character-level processing and for the lowest-resource languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Focusing on user-centric tasks improves practical utility for under-represented language speakers by selecting tasks based on their broad adoption by high-resource language speakers, ensuring the benchmark reflects real-world needs rather than academic preferences.

### Mechanism 2
Byte-based models outperform subword-based models on under-represented languages because ByT5 uses raw bytes as input units rather than language-specific subwords, avoiding the need to learn language-specific tokenization patterns that may be suboptimal for morphologically rich or low-resource languages.

### Mechanism 3
Fine-tuning outperforms in-context learning for under-represented languages with limited data because supervised fine-tuning with hundreds of examples provides more stable learning signals than 5-shot in-context learning, which relies heavily on the model's pre-existing cross-lingual capabilities.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Character-level vs. subword-level processing
  - **Why needed here**: Understanding tokenization trade-offs is crucial given the superiority of byte-based models for under-represented languages
  - **Quick check question**: What are the advantages and disadvantages of character-level, subword-level, and byte-level processing for morphologically rich languages?

- **Concept**: Few-shot learning vs. fine-tuning
  - **Why needed here**: XTREME-UP provides results for both few-shot in-context learning and fine-tuning scenarios
  - **Quick check question**: Under what conditions would fine-tuning be expected to outperform few-shot in-context learning, and vice versa?

- **Concept**: Multilingual model evaluation
  - **Why needed here**: Evaluating models across 88 under-represented languages requires understanding how to aggregate performance and interpret results across diverse language families
  - **Quick check question**: How should performance be aggregated across languages with vastly different resource levels and linguistic properties?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Datasets (ASR, OCR, autocomplete, transliteration, MT, QA, retrieval, NER, semantic parsing) → Models (mT5, ByT5, Flan-PaLM) → Evaluation (task-specific metrics) → Analysis
- **Critical path**: Data → Model Training (fine-tuning or in-context learning) → Evaluation → Analysis
- **Design tradeoffs**: Prioritizes user-centric tasks over traditional NLP tasks, focuses on under-represented languages over high-resource languages, emphasizes few-shot scenarios over zero-shot transfer
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on character-level tasks, large performance gaps between in-language and cross-language settings, failure of in-context learning approaches
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Fine-tune ByT5 on the autocomplete task and compare performance to mT5 baseline
  2. Evaluate Flan-PaLM's in-context learning performance on the NER task compared to fine-tuned mT5
  3. Test transliteration performance in both directions for a selected language pair and analyze error patterns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does XTREME-UP ensure that the annotation effort is fairly distributed across languages, especially considering the varying difficulty of annotation tasks and the availability of annotators for different languages?

### Open Question 2
How does XTREME-UP address the potential bias in the data due to the selection of languages and tasks, and how does this impact the generalizability of the results to other under-represented languages not included in the benchmark?

### Open Question 3
How does XTREME-UP plan to update and expand the benchmark as new tasks and languages become relevant, and what mechanisms are in place to ensure that the benchmark remains current and comprehensive?

## Limitations
- The 8-hour annotation limit per language may not be sufficient for capturing the full complexity of many under-represented languages
- The benchmark assumes that tasks valuable to high-resource language speakers will also be valuable to under-represented language speakers without validation
- Cross-lingual transfer capabilities of large language models may improve over time, potentially changing the relative performance of fine-tuning vs in-context learning

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Byte-based models outperform subword-based models on character-level tasks
- Fine-tuning consistently outperforms in-context learning on limited data
- The benchmark successfully covers a diverse set of 88 under-represented languages

**Medium Confidence:**
- User-centric task selection adequately represents real-world needs
- 8-hour annotation limit provides sufficient data for meaningful training
- Averaging across languages provides fair performance comparison

**Low Confidence:**
- Cross-lingual transfer capabilities will not improve significantly for ULs
- Specific task selection will remain relevant as technology evolves
- Methodology can be directly applied to other under-represented language groups

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset Quality Audit**: Conduct systematic evaluation of dataset quality across all 88 languages, measuring annotation consistency, coverage of linguistic phenomena, and potential biases in task selection.

2. **Task Relevance Validation**: Survey speakers of under-represented languages to validate whether selected "user-centric" tasks actually match their technological needs and usage patterns.

3. **Cross-Lingual Transfer Analysis**: Perform detailed error analysis on model failures across different language families to identify specific linguistic features that hinder cross-lingual transfer.
