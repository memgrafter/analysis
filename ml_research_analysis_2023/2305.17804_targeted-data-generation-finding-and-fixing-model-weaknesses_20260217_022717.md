# Targeted Data Generation: Finding and Fixing Model Weaknesses

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17804
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17804
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Improves accuracy on challenging subgroups by 2-13% and overall test accuracy by 0.55-0.16%

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Targeted Data Generation (TDG), a framework to automatically identify and augment challenging subgroups in NLP models. TDG clusters validation data, evaluates subgroup amenability to data augmentation using Generalization in Context (GC) and Interference in Context (IC) metrics, and generates new data with GPT-3 for selected subgroups. Experiments on sentiment analysis and NLI tasks show TDG improves accuracy on challenging subgroups by 2-13% and overall test accuracy by 0.55-0.16%, outperforming baselines like reweighing and paraphrasing.

## Method Summary
TDG uses a pipeline of automatic subgroup discovery (clustering validation data and calculating GC/IC metrics), human-in-the-loop data generation (GPT-3 with user validation), and model fine-tuning with augmented data. The method identifies clusters most amenable to augmentation by finding high GC and low IC scores, then generates and validates challenging examples for these clusters. The framework is tested on SST, MNLI, and QQP tasks using BERT-base and RoBERTa-large models.

## Key Results
- Improves accuracy on challenging subgroups by 2-13% across SST, MNLI, and QQP tasks
- Achieves 0.55-0.16% improvement in overall test accuracy
- Outperforms reweighing and paraphrasing baselines on challenging subgroup performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TDG improves challenging subgroup performance by identifying clusters that benefit from additional data without hurting overall accuracy.
- Mechanism: TDG uses Generalization in Context (GC) to measure how much a cluster benefits from more data, and Interference in Context (IC) to measure potential harm to overall performance. It then generates new data for high-GC/low-IC clusters using GPT-3.
- Core assumption: Clusters with high GC and low IC represent genuine model weaknesses that can be fixed through targeted data augmentation without causing interference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "TDG estimates the expected benefit and potential harm of data augmentation for each subgroup, and selects the ones most likely to improve within group performance without hurting overall performance."
  - [section 2.1]: Formal definitions of GC and IC showing how they quantify the expected benefit and potential harm of data augmentation.
  - [corpus]: The corpus shows related work on subgroup discovery and bias detection, supporting the importance of identifying and addressing model weaknesses in specific subgroups.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Task-based clustering (without label information) identifies the most augmentable clusters.
- Mechanism: Clustering based on the target model's own representation groups examples that the model considers similar or difficult, revealing its weaknesses. This is more effective than agnostic clustering or clustering with label information.
- Core assumption: The target model's representation captures meaningful patterns of difficulty that correlate with opportunities for improvement through data augmentation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2]: Analysis showing task-based clustering without labels yields clusters with positive generalization and near-zero interference scores, while other clustering methods are less effective.
  - [section 3.1]: Experimental results demonstrating task-based clustering identifies clusters most amenable to augmentation.
  - [corpus]: Related work on model-based approaches to improving challenging subgroups supports the effectiveness of using model representations for clustering.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Human-in-the-loop validation with GPT-3 generated examples ensures the augmented data is both challenging for the model and consistent with the original data distribution.
- Mechanism: Users label GPT-3 generated examples that either contain the correct label when the model is wrong, or where the local and global models disagree. This process continues until the cluster's local model mostly agrees with the global model.
- Core assumption: The disagreement between local and global models, combined with human validation, identifies examples that are challenging yet consistent with the original data distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3]: Description of the human-in-the-loop process for generating and validating new examples for identified challenging clusters.
  - [section 9.1]: Details on the user interface and process for adding challenging examples through GPT-3 generation and human validation.
  - [corpus]: Related work on human-in-the-loop data augmentation supports the benefits of this approach over automatic methods.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Generalization in Context (GC) and Interference in Context (IC)
  - Why needed here: These metrics quantify whether a cluster benefits from more data (GC) and whether augmenting that cluster would hurt performance on the original dataset (IC), guiding the selection of clusters for data augmentation.
  - Quick check question: If a cluster has high GC but also high IC, should it be selected for augmentation? (Answer: No, because while it would benefit from more data, it would also hurt overall performance.)

- Concept: Task-based clustering using model representations
  - Why needed here: Clustering based on the target model's representation groups examples the model considers similar or difficult, revealing its weaknesses and identifying clusters amenable to data augmentation.
  - Quick check question: Why might clustering based on label information result in high GC and IC scores? (Answer: Because it creates label-imbalanced clusters that simply shift the model's predictions toward a certain label, rather than addressing genuine model weaknesses.)

- Concept: Human-in-the-loop data validation
  - Why needed here: Human validation ensures that GPT-3 generated examples are both challenging for the model and consistent with the original data distribution, preventing the introduction of noise.
  - Quick check question: What criteria should users use to add GPT-3 generated examples to the augmented dataset? (Answer: Examples where the model's prediction is incorrect and the user provides the correct label, or where the local and global models disagree.)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Validation data clustering -> GC/IC calculation -> GPT-3 data generation -> Human validation -> Model fine-tuning
- Critical path: Automatic subgroup discovery identifies challenging clusters, GPT-3 generates examples for these clusters, humans validate and add challenging examples, and the model is fine-tuned with the augmented data.
- Design tradeoffs: Task-based clustering without labels vs. with labels (captures model weaknesses vs. creates label-imbalanced clusters), simultaneous augmentation of all clusters vs. one at a time (potential interference vs. more targeted improvements)
- Failure signatures: High interference scores across all clustering methods (as seen with QQP), overfitting to augmented clusters, catastrophic forgetting of original data patterns
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run clustering on a held-out validation set and calculate GC and IC scores for each cluster to identify promising candidates for augmentation.
  2. Use GPT-3 to generate examples for the top 2-3 clusters with highest GC and lowest IC, and have humans validate and add challenging examples.
  3. Fine-tune the model with the augmented data and evaluate improvements on the challenging clusters and overall test accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the relationship between clusters impact the effectiveness of TDG when augmenting multiple clusters simultaneously?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that combining and fine-tuning conflicting clusters together may negatively impact the performance of one or both clusters, and that fine-tuning TDG(all) augmented data on individual clusters can lead to improved performance on certain clusters and worse performance on others.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that there may be relationships between clusters, such as mutual benefit or conflict, but does not provide a detailed analysis of how these relationships impact the effectiveness of TDG when augmenting multiple clusters simultaneously.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive analysis of the relationships between clusters and their impact on the effectiveness of TDG when augmenting multiple clusters simultaneously would help resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can TDG be adapted to handle cases where the target model's representation does not align well with the challenging subgroups?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper assumes that the target model's representation is used for clustering, which may not always align well with the challenging subgroups. The effectiveness of TDG may be limited if the target model's representation does not capture the relevant features for identifying challenging subgroups.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not discuss how to handle cases where the target model's representation does not align well with the challenging subgroups, which could limit the effectiveness of TDG.
- What evidence would resolve it: Exploring alternative clustering methods or incorporating additional information beyond the target model's representation could help address this issue and improve the effectiveness of TDG in such cases.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size of the validation dataset impact the effectiveness of TDG in identifying and augmenting challenging subgroups?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the size of the clusters in the MNLI experiment is small compared to the overall devtest set size, which may dilute the impact of improvements in challenging subgroups.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the size of the validation dataset impacts the effectiveness of TDG in identifying and augmenting challenging subgroups, or how to determine an optimal size for the validation dataset.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying sizes of validation datasets and analyzing the impact on the effectiveness of TDG in identifying and augmenting challenging subgroups would help address this question.

## Limitations
- The framework's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of initial clustering to identify truly challenging subgroups.
- Human-in-the-loop validation introduces significant costs and potential biases, limiting scalability.
- The paper doesn't thoroughly address catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning with augmented data.

## Confidence
- High confidence in framework design rationale and theoretical utility of GC/IC metrics
- Medium confidence in experimental results showing 2-13% improvements on challenging subgroups
- Low confidence in scalability due to GPT-3 generation and human validation costs
