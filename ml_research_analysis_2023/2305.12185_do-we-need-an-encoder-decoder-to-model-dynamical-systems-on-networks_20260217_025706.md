# Do We Need an Encoder-Decoder to Model Dynamical Systems on Networks?
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12185
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12185
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Embedding-based models for network dynamics often overfit and fail to capture correct long-term behaviors.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the conventional approach of using encoder-decoder architectures for modeling dynamical systems on networks. Through extensive experiments, it demonstrates that embedding-based models, while fitting training data well, often produce incorrect long-term dynamical behaviors and stability properties. The authors propose an alternative embedding-free approach called DNND that directly models self-dynamics and coupling effects using two separate neural networks. This approach achieves better performance in both short- and long-term predictions while correctly capturing the stability properties of the underlying dynamical systems.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes DNND (Dy-Net Neural Dynamics), an embedding-free approach that models network dynamics using two separate MLPs: one for self-dynamics (Fθ) and one for pairwise coupling (Gθ). The model directly operates in the original state space without embedding, using a vector-field structure that decomposes the dynamics into node-specific and coupling terms. Training involves affine regularization and a temperature-based loss warm-up schedule to address numerical instability. The approach is evaluated on three dynamical systems (heat diffusion, biochemical reactions, and birth-death processes) across various network topologies, with performance measured by prediction accuracy and stability metrics.

## Key Results
- DNND achieves mean absolute percentage errors (MAPE) below 12% across different dynamical systems
- The model successfully captures correct fixed points and dynamical stability, with largest Lyapunov exponents matching ground truth values
- DNND outperforms embedding-based models in both short- and long-term predictions
- The model reliably recovers dynamics on various network topologies including ER, BA, WS, and LFR networks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Encoder-decoder structures overfit network dynamics by introducing spurious latent dimensions that enable fitting without capturing true long-term behavior.
- Mechanism: The encoder maps scalar state variables to higher-dimensional latent vectors, increasing the hypothesis space beyond what the data can constrain. This allows the model to fit training observations while producing flows with incorrect stability properties (e.g., positive Lyapunov exponents for stable systems).
- Core assumption: Embedding dimension d is large relative to the number of available observations n, creating an ill-posed inverse problem.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "embeddings tend to induce a model that fits observations well but simultaneously has incorrect dynamical behaviours"
  - [section]: "If we keep increasing the embedding dimension d, we can always fit a given observation time series"
- Break condition: When embedding dimension is strictly constrained or encoder/decoder are enforced as inverse homeomorphisms satisfying topological conjugacy conditions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Graph Laplacian aggregation is insufficient for modeling nonlinear coupling in network dynamics.
- Mechanism: Graph Laplacian performs linear aggregation that cannot capture nonlinear coupling effects between neighboring nodes, limiting expressiveness even when combined with embedding-based approaches.
- Core assumption: Real network dynamics often involve nonlinear coupling between neighboring nodes that cannot be adequately represented by linear graph operators.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "the graph Laplacian is a linear aggregation operator that cannot handle potentially nonlinear coupling in network dynamics"
- Break condition: When dynamics are strictly linear or when nonlinear coupling can be captured through sufficiently complex linear combinations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Direct modeling in original state space avoids topological conjugacy issues inherent in encoder-decoder approaches.
- Mechanism: By modeling dynamics directly on original state variables using separate networks for self-dynamics and coupling, the model avoids the need for topological conjugacy between latent and original spaces, ensuring the learned flow operates in the correct state space.
- Core assumption: The decomposition into self-dynamics and coupling terms is sufficiently expressive to capture the target dynamics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we show that the difficulties can be avoided by not using embedding"
- Break condition: When topological conjugacy provides necessary dimension reduction that cannot be achieved through other means.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Fθ network (self-dynamics) -> Gθ network (coupling effects) -> Direct state space modeling

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize Fθ and Gθ with affine components
  2. Apply temperature-based loss warm-up to stabilize early training
  3. Optimize both networks simultaneously to minimize prediction error
  4. Evaluate on long-term prediction and stability metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Higher embedding dimensions improve short-term fitting but harm long-term stability
  - More complex coupling functions improve expressiveness but increase training difficulty
  - Direct state space modeling avoids conjugacy issues but requires more careful architectural design

- Failure signatures:
  - Diverging predictions beyond training horizon
  - Positive Lyapunov exponents for stable ground truth systems
  - Inconsistent trajectories under time reparameterization
  - Poor performance on networks with nonlinear coupling

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on heat diffusion dynamics on grid network, evaluate long-term predictions and Lyapunov exponents
  2. Test on biochemical reaction network, compare short vs long-term MAPE
  3. Evaluate on random networks (ER, BA, WS, LFR) with birth-death dynamics, measure prediction accuracy across topologies

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the theoretical conditions under which embedding-based models fail to capture correct dynamical behaviors, and how can these conditions be characterized?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how embeddings induce incorrect dynamical behaviors but does not provide a complete theoretical characterization of when and why this occurs.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies problems with embeddings through empirical examples and analysis, it does not offer a rigorous mathematical framework for determining when embedding-based models will fail.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof showing conditions on the dynamical system, network topology, and embedding dimension that guarantee incorrect behavior, along with counterexamples demonstrating the boundaries of these conditions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can symbolic regression be effectively used to recover analytical forms of the evolution functions in DNND, and what are the limitations of this approach?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that symbolic regression could potentially be applied to recover exact algebraic forms of the two functions (Fθ and Gθ) in DNND, but provides only preliminary results.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the possibility of recovering analytical forms but does not explore the effectiveness, accuracy, or limitations of symbolic regression in this context.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments applying symbolic regression to various dynamical systems, comparing recovered analytical forms with ground truth, and identifying factors that affect success rates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the learnability of dynamical flows, up to topological equivalence, from irregularly spaced observations relate to the concept of suspension flows under a roof function?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion mentions that characterizing learnability of dynamical flows from irregularly spaced observations is an interesting question worth exploring, relating it to finding suspension flows under a roof function.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as an open question but does not provide any theoretical framework or empirical results connecting the two concepts.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical framework linking the mathematical properties of suspension flows to the learnability of dynamical systems from irregularly sampled data, along with empirical validation on various dynamical systems.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks quantitative analysis of overfitting mechanisms in embedding-based models
- The topological conjugacy argument is theoretically sound but lacks empirical validation
- The claim that graph Laplacian aggregation is fundamentally insufficient is not fully substantiated with direct examples of prediction failures

## Confidence
- High confidence: The empirical demonstration that DNND outperforms embedding-based models on long-term predictions and stability metrics
- Medium confidence: The theoretical explanation of why embedding-based models overfit, due to lack of quantitative evidence
- Low confidence: The claim that DNND's architecture is the only viable alternative to embedding-based approaches
