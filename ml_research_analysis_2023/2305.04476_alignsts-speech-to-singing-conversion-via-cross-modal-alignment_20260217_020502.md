# AlignSTS: Speech-to-Singing Conversion via Cross-Modal Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04476
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04476
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: MOS 3.58 overall quality, 3.81 prosody naturalness

## Executive Summary
AlignSTS addresses the challenge of speech-to-singing conversion by explicitly modeling cross-modal alignment between speech content and target singing pitch. The method introduces a rhythm adaptor that predicts quantized rhythm representations to bridge the modality gap, then uses cross-attention to realign content features according to this rhythm. Trained self-supervised on paired speech-singing data, AlignSTS achieves state-of-the-art performance with MOS scores of 3.58 for overall quality and 3.81 for prosody naturalness, outperforming baseline models across objective and subjective metrics.

## Method Summary
AlignSTS converts speech to singing by encoding speech content, target pitch, and rhythm into separate representations, then predicting a quantized rhythm representation conditioned on both content and pitch. This rhythm serves as a temporal scaffold for cross-modal alignment via cross-attention, realigning content features to match the target rhythm. The aligned features are fused with pitch and timbre information and passed through a diffusion decoder for mel-spectrogram synthesis. The model is trained self-supervised on paired speech-singing data using combined losses including commitment loss for rhythm quantization, guided attention loss for alignment, and reconstruction losses for the diffusion decoder.

## Key Results
- Achieved MOS 3.58 for overall quality and 3.81 for prosody naturalness
- Outperformed baseline models on objective metrics: LSD, RCA, and RRD
- Ablation studies show rhythm adaptor and cross-modal aligner are critical components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Explicit rhythm representation bridges the modality gap between content and pitch
- Mechanism: Rhythm adaptor predicts discrete rhythm representation conditioned on content and pitch, cross-modal aligner uses rhythm as temporal scaffold to realign content features
- Core assumption: Rhythm can be disentangled as separate modality capturing temporal dynamics distinct from pitch and content
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "adopts a novel rhythm adaptor to predict the target rhythm representation to bridge the modality gap between content and pitch"
  - [section]: "We leverage the temporal modality, rhythm, to bridge the modality gap between the speech content and the target pitch."
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus does not contain direct evidence about rhythm bridging modality gaps, only mentions related works on voice conversion and speech disentanglement
- Break condition: If rhythm representation cannot be reliably predicted from content and pitch, the bridge collapses and alignment degrades

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Vector quantization of rhythm creates discrete temporal states that model phoneme onsets/offsets
- Mechanism: Continuous rhythm features are quantized into discrete embedding space, forcing model to learn distinct temporal dynamic states (attack, sustain, transition, silence)
- Core assumption: Rhythm contains discrete temporal states that can be modeled with vector quantization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we adopt a Vector Quantization(VQ) module to quantize the continuous rhythm features to model these temporal states"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus mentions voice conversion and speech disentanglement but lacks direct evidence on VQ for rhythm modeling
- Break condition: If rhythm dynamics are too continuous or noisy, VQ may collapse to few embeddings, losing temporal resolution

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Cross-attention with rhythm as query realigns content features according to target rhythm
- Mechanism: Cross-modal aligner uses rhythm embeddings as query and content as key/value in cross-attention, performing soft-selection over content along time axis
- Core assumption: Cross-attention can effectively realign content features when guided by rhythm embeddings
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We simply use a stack of two cross-attention layers... to place the linguistic features along the time axis according to the target rhythm."
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus does not provide direct evidence about cross-attention realignment with rhythm queries
- Break condition: If attention weights become scattered or non-monotonic, content misalignment produces garbled phonemes

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Vector quantization (VQ) and discrete latent spaces
  - Why needed here: VQ forces rhythm into discrete temporal states, creating information bottleneck that improves disentanglement
  - Quick check question: What happens to rhythm representation if you remove VQ commitment loss?

- Concept: Cross-attention mechanism and attention regulation
  - Why needed here: Cross-attention with rhythm query realigns content features temporally; windowing and guided loss ensure monotonic, diagonal attention paths
  - Quick check question: How does guided attention loss weight matrix change if you increase concentration parameter g?

- Concept: Diffusion models for mel-spectrogram synthesis
  - Why needed here: Diffusion decoder provides high-fidelity reconstruction while handling complex distribution of singing audio
  - Quick check question: What reconstruction loss components (MAE, SSIM) are combined in diffusion decoder training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Encoders (pitch, rhythm, content) → Rhythm adaptor (VQ + predictor) → Cross-modal aligner (cross-attention) → Fusion → Diffusion decoder → Output
- Critical path: Encoded content → Cross-modal aligner (rhythm query) → Aligned content → Fusion with pitch/rhythm/timbre → Diffusion decoder
- Design tradeoffs: VQ quantization provides disentanglement but may lose fine-grained rhythm; cross-attention realignment improves alignment but adds computational cost
- Failure signatures: Disordered phonemes (poor alignment), muffled audio (VQ collapse), unnatural rhythm (rhythm adaptor failure)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Remove VQ module - observe if rhythm becomes continuous and alignment degrades
  2. Remove cross-modal aligner - observe if content remains misaligned to pitch
  3. Replace diffusion decoder with GAN - observe changes in audio quality and naturalness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model perform when trained on singing-to-singing tasks and tested on unseen speech inputs, as explored in the zero-shot STS experiment?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions conducting extensional experiments on zero-shot STS by training the model on singing-to-singing tasks and testing it on unseen speech samples.
- Why unresolved: While results show some improvement over baseline models, performance is not as good as model trained on speech-to-singing tasks. Paper does not explore reasons for this difference in performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments comparing performance of model on different types of input data (e.g., singing-to-singing, speech-to-singing, and mixed) and analyzing differences in model's behavior on each type of input.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the rhythm adaptor module affect the model's performance in terms of phoneme intelligibility and overall audio quality?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that rhythm adaptor is designed to predict the target rhythm representation and bridge the modality gap between content and pitch.
- Why unresolved: While ablation study shows removing rhythm adaptor significantly degrades model's performance, paper does not provide detailed analysis of how rhythm adaptor affects model's ability to generate intelligible phonemes and high-quality audio.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments analyzing model's output with and without rhythm adaptor, focusing on phoneme intelligibility and overall audio quality.

## Limitations

- Weak corpus evidence supporting core mechanisms: rhythm bridging modality gaps, VQ for discrete rhythm states, cross-attention for temporal realignment
- Reliance on architectural intuition rather than established precedent for specific STS application
- Performance degradation when trained on singing-to-singing tasks and tested on unseen speech inputs

## Confidence

- High confidence: STS task formulation and evaluation methodology (MOS testing, objective metrics) are well-established; MOS scores of 3.58 (overall quality) and 3.81 (prosody naturalness) are supported by experimental results
- Medium confidence: Architectural components (rhythm adaptor, cross-modal aligner, diffusion decoder) are technically sound with precedent in related domains, but specific combination and claimed effectiveness for STS requires more direct validation
- Low confidence: Claims about rhythm specifically bridging modality gaps and VQ creating discrete temporal states are weakest, as corpus provides minimal evidence for these specific assertions in STS context

## Next Checks

2. **Attention visualization analysis**: Generate and analyze attention weight visualizations during cross-modal alignment to verify that rhythm-guided attention produces monotonic, diagonal patterns as claimed, and that misalignment correlates with audio quality degradation.
