# This Prompt is Measuring <MASK>: Evaluating Bias Evaluation in Language Models

- Key outcome: Systematic analysis of bias evaluation methods in language models reveals frequent mismatches between claimed and actual measurement, with guidance for improving bias test design

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive taxonomy for categorizing bias tests in language models, drawing on measurement modeling theory to reveal systematic issues in how bias is conceptualized and measured. The authors analyze 77 papers proposing bias tests, identifying common problems including unclear conceptualizations, overreliance on English and Western contexts, and unexamined assumptions about desired model behavior. The taxonomy illuminates both the scope of biases currently measured and types that remain under-researched, offering concrete guidance for improving bias test design and documentation.

## Method Summary
The authors employ an inductive-deductive approach to develop a taxonomy for categorizing bias tests, using measurement modeling as the theoretical foundation. They apply this taxonomy to annotate 77 papers proposing bias tests, systematically categorizing them along dimensions including conceptualizations of bias, operationalizations, and desired outcomes. The analysis reveals patterns and validity threats across the annotated corpus, from which the authors derive recommendations for practitioners to improve bias test design and documentation.

## Key Results
- Taxonomy reveals frequent mismatches between what bias tests claim to measure and how they are constructed
- Analysis identifies overreliance on English language, Western contexts, and explicit identity terms as limitations
- The study illuminates under-researched bias types and provides concrete guidance for improving bias test design
- Common validity threats include unclear conceptualizations and unexamined assumptions about desired model behavior

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop bias tests that effectively measure bias across diverse cultural contexts without imposing Western-centric assumptions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the overreliance on English and Western contexts in bias testing, noting that "Most English bias tests assume an American or Western context."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this issue but does not propose specific methodologies for creating culturally inclusive bias tests.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of bias tests that are specifically designed for and tested in multiple cultural contexts, with clear documentation of cultural assumptions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective ways to operationalize bias measurements that capture subtle and implicit forms of bias beyond explicit identity terms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "Identity terms can only reveal descriptions and slurs linked to an explicit target" and that "bias tests are generally unable to address bias against speakers of marginalised language varieties."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies limitations in current proxy terms but does not explore alternative methods for capturing implicit bias.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different proxy term strategies and their effectiveness in capturing implicit bias, including qualitative analyses of model outputs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we establish a reliable and valid framework for measuring bias that accounts for the tradeoffs between ecological validity and construct validity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the tradeoff between ecological validity and construct validity, noting that "many papers that conceptualise bias in terms of toxic content generation use prompt continuation as a prompt task, and operationalise bias as differences in toxicity across generated output."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the tradeoff but does not propose a framework for balancing these competing validity concerns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of a comprehensive validity framework that explicitly addresses the tradeoffs between different types of validity in bias measurement.

## Limitations

- The taxonomy development process relies heavily on subjective interpretation, which may not align with all stakeholders' perspectives
- Analysis is limited to papers proposing bias tests, potentially missing insights from work that critiques or implements these tests
- Focus on English-language papers and Western conceptualizations may limit generalizability to other cultural contexts and languages

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The identification of common issues in bias test design (unclear conceptualizations, overreliance on English, unexamined assumptions about desired behavior)
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that the taxonomy reveals mismatches between claimed and actual measurement, as this depends on the quality of the taxonomy itself
- **Medium confidence**: The identification of under-researched bias types, as this is based on the coverage of papers reviewed rather than systematic exploration of all possible bias types

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-cultural validation**: Apply the taxonomy to bias tests developed for non-English languages and non-Western contexts to assess generalizability and identify culturally-specific bias conceptualizations
2. **Construct validity testing**: Design a study where multiple bias tests targeting the same purported bias are compared for consistency, to empirically validate whether the taxonomy's categorization aligns with actual measurement outcomes
3. **Stakeholder review**: Conduct structured interviews with 10-15 practitioners from diverse backgrounds (researchers, policymakers, industry practitioners) to evaluate whether the taxonomy's dimensions and the identified validity threats resonate with their experiences and needs
