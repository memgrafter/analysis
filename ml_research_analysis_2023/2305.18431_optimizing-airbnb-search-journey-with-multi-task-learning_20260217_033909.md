# Optimizing Airbnb Search Journey with Multi-task Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18431
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18431
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: +0.48% NDCG improvement offline, +0.61% to +9.0% online gains across Airbnb products

## Executive Summary
Journey Ranker introduces a modular multi-task deep learning architecture that models both positive and negative milestones in Airbnb's guest search journey to optimize for uncancelled bookings. The architecture includes a Base Module for positive milestones (clicks, long clicks, payment, reservation, booking), a Twiddler Module for negative milestones (rejections, host/guest cancellations), and a Combination Module that balances these using context-dependent weights. Offline experiments showed a +0.48% NDCG improvement over the baseline with only a +9.2% parameter increase. Online A/B tests across four Airbnb products delivered significant gains: +0.61% uncancelled bookings for stays, +2.0% and +9.0% for experiences, and +0.7% nights booked with -3.7% unsubscribes for email.

## Method Summary
Journey Ranker uses a four-module architecture: Shared Representation processes listing and context features, Base Module models 6 positive milestones using multi-task learning, Twiddler Module handles 3 negative milestones as binary classification tasks, and Combination Module learns context-dependent weights to balance module outputs. The model decomposes the probability of uncancelled booking into a sequential conversion funnel, capturing the full guest journey through intermediate milestones. Training uses an unweighted sum of losses across modules with pairwise loss for the final ranking output.

## Key Results
- +0.48% NDCG improvement over baseline in offline experiments
- +0.61% uncancelled bookings for stays in online A/B testing
- +2.0% and +9.0% improvements for in-real-life and online experiences respectively
- +0.7% nights booked with -3.7% unsubscribes for email marketing

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-task learning with sequential guest actions improves model generalization beyond binary booking labels
- Mechanism: The Base Module decomposes the probability of uncancelled booking into a chain of intermediate milestones (clicks, long clicks, payment page, reservation request, booking acceptance), capturing the full guest journey rather than just the final conversion
- Core assumption: Intermediate guest actions are informative signals that correlate with final booking outcomes and can be modeled as a sequential conversion funnel
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions leveraging intermediate guest actions as milestones; [section 4.1] reformulates the problem into improving the conversion funnel
- Break condition: If intermediate milestones become noisy or irrelevant to the final booking outcome, the decomposition may introduce harmful interference

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Context-dependent weighting of positive and negative milestones enables dynamic balancing throughout the guest journey
- Mechanism: The Combination Module learns scalar coefficients for each module output based on context features (guest state, query), allowing the model to emphasize avoiding negative milestones when guests are further along in their journey
- Core assumption: Guest preferences and risk tolerance change dynamically throughout the search journey, requiring adaptive weighting of conflicting objectives
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions balancing positive and negative milestones across the guest journey; [section 4.3] discusses changing guest needs as they gather information
- Break condition: If context features are not predictive of the appropriate balance, the learned coefficients may not improve ranking performance

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Separate modeling of guest-initiated and host-initiated negative milestones improves accuracy
- Mechanism: The Twiddler Module independently models rejections, host cancellations, and guest cancellations as binary classification tasks, allowing for specialized handling of class imbalance and delayed outcomes
- Core assumption: Negative milestones have distinct characteristics (prevalence, timing, causality) that benefit from dedicated modeling rather than being inferred from positive milestone chains
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.2] explains more accurate modeling by separately modeling cancellations initiated by guest and host
- Break condition: If separation between negative milestones becomes too fine-grained relative to available training data, overfitting may occur

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Chain rule of probability for sequential conversion funnels
  - Why needed here: Enables decomposition of the final booking probability into interpretable intermediate milestones, capturing the full guest journey
  - Quick check question: How does modeling P(uncancelled booking) = P(unc|book) * P(book|req) * P(req|pp) * P(pp|lc) * P(lc|c) * P(c) help the model learn better than a single binary label?

- Concept: Multi-task learning with shared representation layers
  - Why needed here: Allows the model to learn common guest preference patterns while maintaining task-specific parameters for each milestone, avoiding interference between tasks
  - Quick check question: Why does the Base Module use shared embeddings for all six positive milestones rather than separate embeddings for each task?

- Concept: Context-dependent coefficient learning
  - Why needed here: Enables dynamic adjustment of how much weight to give positive vs negative milestones based on guest state and query features, reflecting changing preferences throughout the journey
  - Quick check question: What advantage does learning context-dependent coefficients in the Combination Module provide over using fixed global weights?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Shared Representation -> Base Module -> Combination Module (primary path for positive milestones) plus Shared Representation -> Twiddler Module -> Combination Module (secondary path for negative milestones)

- Critical path: Shared Representation → Base Module → Combination Module (primary path for positive milestones) plus Shared Representation → Twiddler Module → Combination Module (secondary path for negative milestones)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Task granularity: More tasks capture more journey detail but increase parameter count and risk interference
  - Module separation: Independent modules allow specialized modeling but require careful balancing
  - Context dependency: Dynamic coefficients adapt to journey stage but add complexity to interpretation

- Failure signatures:
  - Coefficients in Combination Module becoming flat or extreme indicates context features not predictive
  - Base Module outperforming Combination Module suggests negative milestones not properly balanced
  - Twiddler Module not learning indicates insufficient negative examples or poor feature representation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline comparison: Implement previous single-task model (Section 3.2) and measure offline NDCG improvement
  2. Module ablation: Test Base Module with only uncancelled booking vs full 6-task setup to quantify intermediate milestone value
  3. Coefficient analysis: Plot Normalized Twiddler Coefficients against key context features to verify learned balancing aligns with product intuition

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the Journey Ranker architecture perform when extended to handle more complex negative milestones beyond the current three (rejections, host cancellations, guest cancellations)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that future work could include extending the Twiddler Module with more negative milestones such as customer support tickets and trip quality, but does not provide experimental results
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests three negative milestones and suggests potential for more complex ones, but doesn't explore this experimentally
- What evidence would resolve it: A study testing Journey Ranker with a broader set of negative milestones and comparing performance metrics against the current implementation

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number and selection of intermediate positive milestones in the Base Module for different use cases beyond Airbnb stays ranking?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "other use cases would have different number of positive milestones corresponding to their specific guest journey" but doesn't provide a systematic approach for selecting these milestones
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates successful application to different Airbnb products, it doesn't provide a general framework for determining the optimal set of milestones for arbitrary use cases
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study across multiple use cases testing different configurations of positive milestones and their impact on model performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the context-dependent coefficient learning in the Combination Module perform compared to alternative weighting schemes, such as auto-hyperparameter tuning or Future Incremental Value (FIV) estimations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that future work could modify the Combination Module to directly optimize for business Overall Evaluation Criteria (OEC) and weigh various outcomes using FIV estimations, but doesn't test these alternatives
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a linear combination with context-dependent coefficients but suggests other approaches could be better without empirical comparison
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing the current Combination Module design with alternative weighting schemes in terms of business metrics and model interpretability

## Limitations

- The model's performance in true cold-start scenarios is not explicitly validated, though the paper claims it handles cold-start well due to the multi-task architecture
- The context-dependent coefficients in the Combination Module are learned but their interpretability and stability across different user segments remain unclear
- The exact feature engineering pipeline and hyperparameter tuning process are not fully specified, making faithful reproduction challenging

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The modular architecture design and the use of intermediate milestones to capture the guest journey are well-justified and technically sound
- **Medium Confidence**: The reported offline and online improvements are credible given the methodology, but the variance across products suggests context-dependent effectiveness that requires further investigation
- **Low Confidence**: The claims about cold-start performance and the interpretability of context-dependent coefficients lack sufficient empirical validation

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation on Negative Milestones**: Remove the Twiddler Module entirely and measure the impact on both offline NDCG and online metrics to quantify the value of explicitly modeling rejections and cancellations

2. **Coefficient Stability Analysis**: Track the Normalized Twiddler Coefficients over time and across different user segments to assess whether the learned balancing remains stable or requires periodic retraining

3. **Cold-Start Stress Test**: Simulate cold-start conditions by training on subsets of the data with limited user history and measuring performance degradation compared to the full model
