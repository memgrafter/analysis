# Continual Reasoning: Non-Monotonic Reasoning in Neurosymbolic AI using Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02171
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02171
- Reference count: 36
- Key outcome: A new methodology combining Logic Tensor Networks with continual learning to address non-monotonic reasoning tasks, achieving significant accuracy improvements through task separation and knowledge completion.

## Executive Summary
Continual Reasoning introduces a novel methodology for non-monotonic reasoning in neurosymbolic AI by combining Logic Tensor Networks (LTN) with continual learning techniques. The approach addresses the challenge of belief revision and exception handling by splitting knowledge into a curriculum of stages, allowing the system to learn new information without forgetting prior knowledge. By prioritizing facts before general rules and learning each predicate independently, the method enables effective reasoning that can revise conclusions when exceptions arise. Experiments on multiple datasets demonstrate substantial performance improvements compared to baseline approaches.

## Method Summary
The methodology combines Logic Tensor Networks with continual learning to enable non-monotonic reasoning through a staged curriculum approach. Knowledge bases containing first-order logic rules and facts are divided into sequential learning stages. The system employs task separation (learning rules for individual predicates separately) and knowledge completion (prioritizing facts before general rules), combined with rehearsal techniques to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This allows the model to revise conclusions when new exceptions are introduced while maintaining previously learned knowledge. The approach is evaluated across three datasets including the Penguin Exception Task, Smokers and Friends task, and bAbI dataset.

## Key Results
- Significant accuracy improvements achieved on non-monotonic reasoning tasks compared to baseline and random curricula
- Task separation yields the best results, demonstrating the effectiveness of learning predicates independently
- Knowledge completion improves reasoning by building from facts to abstract rules before learning general rules
- The approach successfully handles belief revision and exception handling in logical reasoning tasks

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Knowledge Base (KB) -> LTN -> Curriculum Designer -> Rehearsal Module -> Neural Network

- Critical path:
  1. Define KB of FOL rules and facts
  2. Design curriculum (task separation or knowledge completion)
  3. Train LTN on first stage of KB
  4. Recall prior knowledge and train on next stage
  5. Repeat until all stages are learned
  6. Evaluate reasoning accuracy on test set

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Task Separation vs. Knowledge Completion: Task separation may be better for independent predicates, while knowledge completion may be better for building from facts to rules.
  - Stage size: Larger stages may lead to forgetting, while smaller stages may be inefficient.
  - Rehearsal frequency: More frequent rehearsal may prevent forgetting but slow down learning.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low satisfiability on queries that should be true based on learned rules
  - Inability to retract conclusions when exceptions are introduced
  - High variance in accuracy across different curriculum designs

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement LTN on the Penguin Exception Task with a baseline curriculum (all rules learned at once)
  2. Implement task separation curriculum on the same task and compare accuracy
  3. Implement knowledge completion curriculum on the same task and compare accuracy to baseline and task separation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does Continual Reasoning perform on lifelong learning scenarios where the knowledge base grows indefinitely over time?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that "rudimentary exploration of extending the PET to learn about a 'super-penguin' which could fly, resulted in the LTN mostly failing to learn the exception to the exception" and suggests that more advanced continual learning techniques could address this.
- Why unresolved: The paper only briefly mentions this scenario and does not provide experimental results on how the system handles increasingly complex exception hierarchies over extended periods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing performance degradation (or lack thereof) when the knowledge base is extended with multiple layers of exceptions over many training stages, compared to baseline methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can Continual Reasoning be effectively applied to other neural-symbolic frameworks beyond Logic Tensor Networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "While LTNs proved to be a straightforward NeSy model to apply Continual Reasoning on, it should be possible to apply our methodology to other NeSy models, such as LNNs."
- Why unresolved: The paper only applies Continual Reasoning to LTN and mentions potential applicability to other models without experimental validation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementation and performance comparison of Continual Reasoning using other neural-symbolic frameworks (e.g., LNN, CILP) on the same reasoning tasks, demonstrating comparable or improved results.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal curriculum design for Continual Reasoning in terms of stage count and knowledge distribution?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper experiments with three-stage curricula but notes that "task separation and knowledge completion curricula are not the only viable option for success" and mentions that "even random curriculum performs better on average than the baseline."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore curricula with different numbers of stages or alternative knowledge distribution strategies beyond the two proposed paradigms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experimentation varying the number of stages and knowledge distribution patterns, identifying optimal configurations for different types of reasoning tasks and measuring performance improvements over baseline methods.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks detailed specification of the LTN framework implementation, particularly how predicates are grounded into vectors and how satisfiability is calculated
- Specific hyperparameters for training are not provided, which could significantly impact reproducibility
- The paper lacks direct citations for the concepts of task separation and knowledge completion as applied to this context

## Confidence
- **High**: The overall methodology of combining LTN with continual learning techniques for non-monotonic reasoning is well-founded and supported by experimental results on multiple datasets
- **Medium**: The effectiveness of task separation and knowledge completion in improving reasoning accuracy is demonstrated, but the specific mechanisms and their relative contributions are not fully elucidated
- **Low**: The claim that this approach represents a significant advancement in neurosymbolic AI for non-monotonic reasoning is difficult to assess without more context on prior work and a more detailed comparison of the proposed methods to existing approaches
