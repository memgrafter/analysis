# Language Models sounds the Death Knell of Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03980
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03980
- Reference count: 20
- This study demonstrates that large language models (LLMs), specifically BioRedditBERT, can replace knowledge graphs (KGs) for synonym extraction in healthcare applications.

## Executive Summary
This paper argues that large language models can effectively replace knowledge graphs for synonym extraction in healthcare applications. The researchers demonstrate that fine-tuned BioRedditBERT can capture and organize medical terminology relationships through embedding clustering, achieving comparable results to manually curated KGs without requiring domain expert intervention. The approach successfully identifies parent nodes for medical concepts through cosine similarity analysis and clustering of LLM-generated embeddings, potentially eliminating the need for labor-intensive KG construction.

## Method Summary
The researchers used BioRedditBERT to generate embeddings for medical terms and their synonyms from the COMETA corpus. They applied UMAP for dimensionality reduction to visualize clustering patterns, then used KMeans clustering to identify parent nodes for each medical concept. Cosine similarity analysis was performed to validate that synonyms of the same medical concept had higher similarity scores compared to terms from different concepts. The approach focused on synonym extraction and parent node identification without requiring manual KG construction or domain expert intervention.

## Key Results
- LLM-generated embeddings successfully clustered semantically similar medical terms together, matching KG classifications
- Cosine similarity scores were significantly higher for synonyms of the same medical concept compared to terms from different concepts
- The approach identified parent nodes for medical concepts including "Oral Contraception," "Crohn's Disease," and "Diabetes Mellitus Type 2"
- Fine-tuned LLMs captured and organized medical terminology relationships effectively without manual KG construction

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can LLMs replace KGs for more complex semantic relationships beyond synonym extraction, such as hierarchical relationships (hypernymy) or causal relationships?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that current algorithms require human intervention for hypernym identification and suggest exploring ways to extract KGs from LLMs to handle such cases.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focused only on synonym extraction and clustering; the methodology does not address more complex semantic relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating LLM performance on hypernym extraction, causal relationship identification, and other complex semantic relationships compared to traditional KGs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the computational and storage efficiency trade-offs between using fine-tuned LLMs versus traditional KGs for large-scale medical terminology management?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper argues LLMs can replace KGs but does not address practical considerations like model size, inference speed, or resource requirements.
- Why unresolved: No comparison of computational costs, storage requirements, or inference time between LLM-based and KG-based approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmark studies comparing model size, inference latency, memory usage, and update efficiency between LLMs and KGs for equivalent medical terminology tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do LLMs handle medical terminology evolution and updates compared to traditional KGs that can be incrementally modified?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper does not discuss how LLMs would adapt to new medical terms or changing terminology over time.
- Why unresolved: No discussion of LLM fine-tuning procedures for terminology updates or comparison with KG update mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Studies comparing the efficiency and accuracy of updating LLMs with new medical terminology versus updating traditional KGs, including time-to-deployment and consistency measures.

## Limitations

- The BioRedditBERT model variant used is not explicitly specified, making exact reproduction challenging
- The COMETA corpus source and preprocessing steps are not detailed, particularly how medical terms were extracted and handled
- Hyperparameter selection for UMAP (n_neighbors, min_dist) and KMeans clustering is not reported, which could significantly impact clustering quality
- The validation relies on visual inspection of UMAP plots and cosine similarity scores without quantitative evaluation metrics or comparison to ground truth KG classifications

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: LLM embeddings can capture semantic relationships between medical terms (supported by clustering patterns)
- **Medium Confidence**: Cosine similarity between embeddings reflects semantic similarity (requires more rigorous validation)
- **Medium Confidence**: UMAP effectively visualizes embedding structure for cluster validation (dependent on proper hyperparameter tuning)
