# From Audio to Symbolic Encoding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13401
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13401
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Modified Onsets and Frames architecture with highway networks, dilated convolutions, and self-attention improves AMT F1 scores but shows limited success on speech recognition

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether a single generic neural network architecture can perform both automatic music transcription (AMT) and speech recognition tasks, which share the common goal of translating audio to symbolic encoding. The authors extend the state-of-the-art Onsets and Frames architecture for AMT by incorporating highway networks, dilated convolutions, and self-attention. While these modifications improve note transcription performance, particularly with highway networks, the same architecture shows less effectiveness when adapted for speech recognition. The study demonstrates architectural transfer potential between domains but highlights task-specific challenges, particularly for speech recognition.

## Method Summary
The authors modify the Onsets and Frames architecture by adding highway networks to ease gradient-based training in deep networks, dilated convolutions to capture long-distance feature relations efficiently, and self-attention to better capture relationships among different frames. The architecture takes log mel-spectrogram inputs and uses a convolutional stack, BiLSTM layers, and output stacks for onset, offset, and velocity predictions. The same architecture is adapted for speech recognition by treating phonemes as notes and omitting onset/offset stacks, using the TIMIT dataset with mel-spectrograms in the human speech frequency range.

## Key Results
- Highway networks significantly improve F1 scores for note transcription compared to the baseline
- Dilated convolutions enhance the model's ability to capture longer distance feature relations
- Self-attention provides minimal benefit for music transcription but is more crucial for speech recognition

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Highway networks improve performance by enabling unimpeded information flow through layers. They introduce gated connections that allow information to bypass certain transformations, mitigating the vanishing gradient problem in deep networks. The core assumption is that the depth of the network creates barriers to effective gradient flow that highway networks can overcome. Evidence shows that highway networks add robustness against network depth. Break condition: If gating mechanism fails to learn appropriate values or network depth is insufficient.

### Mechanism 2
Dilated convolutions capture long-distance feature relations more efficiently than standard convolutions. They skip input values at a regular rate, expanding the receptive field without increasing parameters. The core assumption is that audio features have long-range dependencies that standard convolutions cannot capture efficiently. Evidence indicates that dilation increases the model's ability to capture longer distance feature relations and robustness against scaling. Break condition: If dilation rate is poorly chosen or features don't exhibit expected long-range dependencies.

### Mechanism 3
Self-attention helps capture contextual relationships between frames. It computes weighted combinations of frame representations based on their relevance to each other. The core assumption is that in speech recognition, phonemes depend heavily on surrounding context, while in music, notes are relatively independent. Evidence shows that attention helps capture relationships among different frames, though notes in music are independent from each other. Break condition: If contextual dependencies are weak or non-existent, or if attention mechanism overfits to noise.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input: Log mel-spectrogram → Conv Stack → BiLSTM → (Attention) → Output
- **Critical path**: Conv Stack → BiLSTM → (Attention) → Output
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Depth vs. training stability (highway networks help)
  - Receptive field size vs. computational cost (dilated convolutions help)
  - Contextual modeling vs. overfitting (self-attention helps in speech but less so in music)
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor performance on onset detection suggests issues with Conv Stack or onset-specific layers
  - Poor performance on offset detection suggests issues with offset-specific layers
  - Poor overall performance suggests issues with fundamental architecture or training setup
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train baseline model without any modifications to establish performance baseline
  2. Add highway networks to baseline and compare performance
  3. Add dilated convolutions to highway network model and compare performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of the generic neural network architecture for AMT and speech recognition tasks compare when using different audio datasets with varying complexity and domain specificity? The paper mentions that the architecture was tested on both AMT and speech recognition tasks, but does not provide a comprehensive comparison of performance across different datasets.

### Open Question 2
What are the specific limitations of the current architecture that prevent it from achieving ideal results in speech recognition tasks, and how can these limitations be addressed? The paper mentions that speech recognition was not very successful due to uniform distribution of speech phonemes, changing spectral patterns, and lack of post-processing for duplicated phonetic predictions.

### Open Question 3
How does the inclusion of self-attention in the architecture affect the performance of AMT and speech recognition tasks, and what are the potential benefits and drawbacks of using self-attention in these tasks? The paper mentions that self-attention was added but its impact on performance was not as significant as expected.

## Limitations
- The study lacks ablation studies isolating individual contributions of architectural modifications
- Speech recognition results show substantially higher phoneme error rates compared to specialized models
- Limited comparative analysis between domains makes it difficult to quantify transfer potential

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Highway networks improving gradient flow in deep networks is well-established in literature
- **Medium confidence**: Dilated convolutions capturing long-range dependencies is theoretically sound but lacks direct empirical validation
- **Low confidence**: Self-attention providing minimal benefit for music transcription while being crucial for speech recognition requires further investigation

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study implementation**: Conduct controlled experiments removing highway networks, dilated convolutions, and self-attention individually to quantify their independent contributions to performance improvements

2. **Cross-domain transfer analysis**: Systematically evaluate the architecture's performance when trained on one domain (music) and tested on another (speech), and vice versa, to better understand transfer limitations

3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: Test the model's robustness to changes in dilation rates, highway network gating parameters, and self-attention configurations to identify optimal settings for each task domain
