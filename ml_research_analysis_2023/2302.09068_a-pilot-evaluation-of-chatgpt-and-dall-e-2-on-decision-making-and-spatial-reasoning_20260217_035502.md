# A Pilot Evaluation of ChatGPT and DALL-E 2 on Decision Making and Spatial Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09068
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09068
- Reference count: 2
- Primary result: Generative models show partial capabilities in spatial reasoning and decision-making but resist traditional evaluation methods

## Executive Summary
This pilot study evaluates ChatGPT and DALL-E 2 on cognitive tasks involving spatial reasoning and decision-making through neutral prompts. DALL-E 2 successfully generated at least one correct image per spatial reasoning prompt (80% overall accuracy), though most outputs were incorrect. ChatGPT demonstrated some rational decision-making but frequently violated Von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms, with performance varying unpredictably across problem complexities. The results highlight fundamental challenges in quantitatively benchmarking generative models using traditional closed-set evaluation methods, emphasizing the need for neutral, large-scale evaluations to mitigate confirmation bias.

## Method Summary
The study used browser-based DALL-E 2 to generate four images per spatial reasoning prompt, with qualitative assessment identifying correct outputs. ChatGPT responses were analyzed for rational decision-making through prompts testing Von Neumann-Morgenstern axioms including independence, transitivity, and Sure Thing principle. Both evaluations relied on post-hoc qualitative analysis rather than predefined answer keys, reflecting the generative nature of these models.

## Key Results
- DALL-E 2 generated at least one correct image for each spatial reasoning prompt, achieving 80% overall accuracy
- ChatGPT showed rational decision-making in some contexts but violated VNM axioms including transitivity and independence
- Both models demonstrated sensitivity to prompt phrasing, with explicit preferences improving ChatGPT's decision-making performance
- Traditional accuracy metrics proved insufficient for evaluating generative models' open-ended outputs

## Limitations
- Subjective qualitative assessment introduces potential bias in evaluating DALL-E 2 outputs
- Limited sample size (10 prompt pairs) may not generalize to broader model capabilities
- Unknown sensitivity to prompt phrasing variations that could affect reproducibility
