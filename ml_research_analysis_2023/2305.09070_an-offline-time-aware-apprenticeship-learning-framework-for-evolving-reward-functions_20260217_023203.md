# An Offline Time-aware Apprenticeship Learning Framework for Evolving Reward Functions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09070
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09070
- Reference count: 5
- The paper proposes THEMES, a time-aware apprenticeship learning framework for modeling evolving reward functions in healthcare settings, achieving AUC of 0.905 and Jaccard score of 0.600 on sepsis treatment tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces THEMES (Time-aware Hierarchical EM Energy-based Sub-trajectory Apprenticeship Learning Framework), a novel approach for apprenticeship learning with evolving reward functions in offline settings. The framework addresses the challenge of modeling complex human-centric decision-making tasks where rewards change over time, such as sepsis treatment in healthcare. By partitioning trajectories into sub-trajectories using a reward-regulated time-aware clustering approach and then clustering these into groups with consistent policies, THEMES can effectively capture the evolving nature of decision-making patterns. The method is evaluated on real-world electronic health records for sepsis treatment and demonstrates significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Method Summary
THEMES employs a hierarchical approach to apprenticeship learning with evolving rewards. It first uses Reward-regulated Multi-series Time-aware Toeplitz Inverse Covariance-based Clustering (RMT-TICC) to partition trajectories into sub-trajectories based on time-invariant structural patterns and reward differences. Then, EM-EDM clusters these sub-trajectories and induces policies for each cluster using energy-based distribution matching. A high-level reward regulator is learned from these state-action pairs and fed back to refine the RMT-TICC partitioning iteratively. The framework is designed to work with offline data and does not require online interaction or explicit reward function specification, making it particularly suitable for healthcare applications where rolling out policies is unethical.

## Key Results
- THEMES achieves an AUC of 0.905 and Jaccard score of 0.600 on sepsis treatment tasks, significantly outperforming five state-of-the-art baselines and three ablation models
- The method demonstrates strong performance across multiple evaluation metrics including Accuracy (0.879), Recall (0.892), Precision (0.888), and F1-score (0.890)
- RMT-TICC successfully partitions trajectories into meaningful sub-trajectories that correspond to different stages of disease progression, validated through ablation studies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: THEMES can effectively partition trajectories into sub-trajectories that correspond to different stages of disease progression by incorporating time-aware clustering and reward-regulated consistency.
- Mechanism: The Reward-regulated Multi-series Time-aware Toeplitz Inverse Covariance-based Clustering (RMT-TICC) uses a sliding window to examine local state patterns and assigns each window to a cluster based on time-invariant structural patterns. It incorporates a reward regulator that penalizes assignments that split consecutive states with high reward differences into different clusters, thus aligning partitions with decision-making patterns.
- Core assumption: Consecutive states within the same progressive stage exhibit consistent decision-making patterns, and irregular time intervals between states carry meaningful information about disease progression.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The method "partitions trajectories into sub-trajectories using a reward-regulated multi-series time-aware Toeplitz inverse covariance-based clustering approach"
  - [section 3.1] "To incorporate the decision-making patterns when partitioning the trajectories, we introduce a reward regulator to MT-TICC and propose aReward-regulated MT-TICC (RMT-TICC)"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus papers address the same specific combination of time-awareness, reward-regulation, and Toeplitz-based clustering for apprenticeship learning
- Break condition: If the reward regulator fails to capture meaningful decision-making patterns or if the time intervals do not correlate with disease progression stages, the partitioning will become arbitrary and not reflect actual clinical decision patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The hierarchical structure with high-level states and actions enables THEMES to iteratively refine sub-trajectory partitions by learning reward regulators that capture evolving decision-making patterns.
- Mechanism: THEMES learns high-level states (sub-trajectory clusters) from RMT-TICC, then uses EM-EDM to cluster these into groups with consistent policies (high-level actions). A high-level reward regulator is learned from these state-action pairs and fed back to refine the RMT-TICC, creating an iterative improvement loop.
- Core assumption: Decision-making patterns can be meaningfully represented at multiple hierarchical levels, and refining partitions based on learned policies will converge to more accurate stage identification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "Instead of directly learning the rewards from observed state-action pairs, we employ such hierarchical structure"
  - [section 3.2] "Taking sub-trajectories {ˆξˆn|ˆn = 1 ,..., ˆN} learned by RMT-TICC as input, with ˆN being the number of sub-trajectories, EM-EDM aims to cluster these sub-trajectories and learn the cluster-wise policies {Πg|g = 1,...G}"
  - [corpus] Weak - while hierarchical learning exists in other domains, the specific application to reward evolution in offline apprenticeship learning with this iterative structure is not directly supported
- Break condition: If the iterative refinement fails to converge or if the high-level representation loses critical information about state-action relationships, the learned reward regulator will not meaningfully improve partitioning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The energy-based distribution matching (EDM) component can effectively induce policies for each sub-trajectory cluster without requiring online interaction or reward function specification.
- Mechanism: EM-EDM clusters sub-trajectories and then uses EDM to match the distribution of state-action pairs in each cluster to the learned policy distribution, maximizing log-likelihood without needing to roll out policies or explicitly specify rewards.
- Core assumption: The occupancy measure of state-action pairs in expert demonstrations can be matched to learned policies through energy-based modeling, and this distribution matching is sufficient to capture complex decision-making patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The method uses "an energy-based distribution matching approach" for policy induction
  - [section 3.2] "EM-EDM aims to cluster these sub-trajectories and learn the cluster-wise policies {Πg|g = 1,...G}" and "EM-EDM to cluster the partitioned sub-trajectories and induce policy for each cluster"
  - [corpus] Moderate - EDM has been demonstrated in other contexts, but its application to multiple evolving reward functions in healthcare is not directly evidenced
- Break condition: If the state-action distributions in sub-trajectories are too complex or multimodal to be captured by energy-based distribution matching, the induced policies will not accurately represent expert decision-making.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: RMT-TICC (trajectory partitioning) → EM-EDM (policy induction) → reward regulator → feedback to RMT-TICC
- Critical path: The iterative loop between RMT-TICC and EM-EDM is critical - if either component fails, the entire framework breaks down. The reward regulator must effectively capture decision-making patterns.
- Design tradeoffs: RMT-TICC trades computational complexity for accuracy by using Toeplitz constraints and sparsity terms. EM-EDM trades explicit reward specification for distribution matching. The hierarchical structure adds complexity but enables iterative refinement.
- Failure signatures: Poor clustering (low Jaccard score, low AUC) indicates RMT-TICC issues. Low precision/recall suggests EM-EDM problems. Failure to converge in iterations indicates reward regulator issues.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run RMT-TICC with synthetic data where ground truth partitions are known to verify it can recover the correct sub-trajectory structure
  2. Test EM-EDM on a simpler dataset with known reward functions to verify it can correctly induce policies without online interaction
  3. Run the full THEMES pipeline on a subset of the sepsis data with known intervention outcomes to verify the iterative refinement improves performance

## Limitations

- The framework assumes that time intervals and reward differences can be meaningfully incorporated into the clustering process through the decay function, but this may not hold if the relationship between timing and clinical severity is not monotonic or if measurement intervals are too irregular.
- The energy-based distribution matching approach requires sufficient data density in state-action space to accurately learn policies, which may be challenging in rare clinical scenarios.
- The iterative refinement process may not converge if the high-level representation loses critical information about individual patient trajectories.

## Confidence

- High confidence in the core mechanism of using hierarchical clustering with reward regulation to partition trajectories, as this follows established time-series clustering principles.
- Medium confidence in the specific Toeplitz inverse covariance approach for handling irregular time intervals, as the decay function's effectiveness depends on proper calibration.
- Medium confidence in the energy-based distribution matching for policy induction, as this requires sufficient data density and may struggle with multimodal state-action distributions.
