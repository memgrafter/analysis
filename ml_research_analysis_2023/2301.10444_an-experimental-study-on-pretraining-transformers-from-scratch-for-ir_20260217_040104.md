# An Experimental Study on Pretraining Transformers from Scratch for IR

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.10444
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10444
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Pretraining transformers from scratch on target IR collections achieves equivalent or better effectiveness compared to generic pretraining for first-stage retrieval

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the conventional wisdom that transformers must be pretrained on large generic collections before being used for information retrieval tasks. The authors systematically investigate whether pretraining transformers from scratch directly on target IR collections (MSMARCO, Mr. TyDi, TripClick) can achieve comparable or superior effectiveness to models pretrained on larger generic collections. Through extensive experiments with different architectures (BERT, DeBERTa, SPLADE) and tasks (first-stage retrieval, reranking), the study demonstrates that models pretrained solely on their target collection perform statistically significantly better for first-stage retrieval while showing only slight drops for reranking tasks. The work also highlights practical benefits including better control over efficiency, data bias, and replicability.

## Method Summary
The study pretrains BERT and DeBERTa transformer variants from scratch using Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and MLM+FLOPS objectives on three IR-specific collections: MSMARCO, Mr. TyDi (Arabic, Japanese, Russian), and TripClick. Models are then fine-tuned for different IR architectures including dense bi-encoders, sparse SPLADE models, and cross-encoders using contrastive loss with negative sampling. The pretrained models are evaluated on development sets (MSMARCO dev, TREC DL) and the BEIR zero-shot benchmark, measuring effectiveness using MRR@10, nDCG@10, R@1k, and R-FLOPS for sparse models.

## Key Results
- Models pretrained solely on MSMARCO with MLM+FLOPS achieve statistically significantly better effectiveness than models pretrained on larger generic collections for first-stage retrieval
- SPLADE models pretrained from scratch on Mr. TyDi and TripClick show comparable or better performance than MContriever baselines
- Different transformer architectures like DeBERTa do not bring significant improvements to IR effectiveness despite better NLP benchmark performance
- Reranking models show a slight effectiveness drop when pretrained solely on target collections compared to generic pretraining

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pretraining from scratch on the target IR collection can achieve equivalent or better effectiveness compared to models pretrained on larger generic collections for first-stage retrieval.
- Mechanism: By pretraining solely on the target collection, the model learns co-occurrence statistics and domain-specific patterns that are directly relevant to the retrieval task, potentially leading to better initial representations.
- Core assumption: The target collection contains sufficient diversity and volume to capture the necessary language patterns for effective IR.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "models pretrained solely on their collection have equivalent or better effectiveness compared to more general models"
  - [section] "surprisingly, models trained solely on MSMARCO with MLM+FLOPS actually can perform statistically significantly better than their counterparts pretrained over larger collections"
  - [corpus] Weak - the paper does not provide direct corpus statistics comparing target collections to generic ones
- Break condition: If the target collection is too small or lacks diversity, the model may not learn effective representations and will underperform.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pretraining from scratch enables better control of efficiency, data bias, and replicability.
- Mechanism: By controlling the pretraining process, researchers can optimize for specific efficiency requirements, mitigate data biases inherent in large generic datasets, and ensure experiments are reproducible.
- Core assumption: Pretraining from scratch allows for fine-grained control over the training process and data selection.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "doing so could enable better control of efficiency, data bias and replicability"
  - [section] "by focusing only on 'off-the-shelf models' we could be losing possible performance gains of better initialized models"
  - [corpus] Weak - the paper mentions control benefits but does not provide empirical evidence comparing bias or replicability
- Break condition: If the pretraining process is not carefully controlled, it may introduce new biases or fail to improve efficiency.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different transformer architectures (e.g., DeBERTa) do not necessarily improve IR effectiveness, even when trained from scratch.
- Mechanism: The standard BERT architecture may already be well-suited for IR tasks, and architectural changes that benefit NLP tasks may not translate to IR improvements.
- Core assumption: The effectiveness of transformer architectures in IR depends more on pretraining and fine-tuning strategies than on architectural changes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we did not actually see major improvements using these architectural changes"
  - [section] "Variants of Transformers architectures, such as DeBERTa, alleged to be better in NLP benchmarks do not bring benefits to IR"
  - [corpus] Weak - the paper does not compare DeBERTa to BERT in other IR studies
- Break condition: If future research demonstrates that architectural changes can significantly improve IR effectiveness, this mechanism would be invalidated.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Masked Language Modeling (MLM)
  - Why needed here: MLM is the pretraining task used to train the transformer models from scratch, teaching them to predict masked words in a sentence.
  - Quick check question: What is the purpose of masking words during pretraining, and how does this help the model learn language patterns?

- Concept: Fine-tuning
  - Why needed here: After pretraining, the models must be fine-tuned on the specific IR task (e.g., first-stage retrieval, reranking) to adapt their representations to the retrieval domain.
  - Quick check question: How does fine-tuning differ from pretraining, and why is it necessary for achieving good IR effectiveness?

- Concept: Contrastive loss and negative sampling
  - Why needed here: These techniques are used during fine-tuning to train the model to distinguish between relevant and irrelevant passages for a given query.
  - Quick check question: What is the role of negative sampling in contrastive learning, and how does it help the model learn to rank passages effectively?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pretraining (MLM/MLM+FLOPS) -> Fine-tuning (bi-encoder/cross-encoder) -> Evaluation (IR metrics)
- Critical path: Pretraining (MLM/MLM+FLOPS) → Fine-tuning (bi-encoder/cross-encoder) → Evaluation (IR metrics)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pretraining from scratch vs. using generic pretrained models: trade-off between domain specificity and generalization
  - Dense vs. sparse representations: trade-off between effectiveness and efficiency
  - 6-layer vs. 12-layer models: trade-off between model size and performance
- Failure signatures:
  - Low effectiveness on target collection: pretraining or fine-tuning may not be capturing domain-specific patterns
  - Poor generalization to other collections: model may be overfitting to target collection
  - High computational cost: model may be too large or inefficient for the target task
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Pretrain a 6-layer MLM model on MSMARCO and fine-tune as a bi-encoder for first-stage retrieval, compare to DistilBERT baseline.
  2. Pretrain a 12-layer MLM+FLOPS model on Mr. TyDi Arabic collection and fine-tune as a sparse SPLADE model, compare to MContriever baseline.
  3. Pretrain a 6-layer MLM model on TripClick and fine-tune as a dense bi-encoder, compare to SciBERT and PubMedBERT baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How much does pretraining data diversity actually contribute to downstream IR effectiveness compared to pretraining data quantity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper directly challenges the assumption that large generic pretraining is necessary, showing models trained from scratch on target collections can match or exceed effectiveness of models pretrained on larger generic collections.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates this for specific IR collections but doesn't systematically vary both diversity and quantity to isolate their individual contributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying both the diversity and size of pretraining collections while keeping other factors constant, measuring downstream IR effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can pretraining from scratch be effectively extended to non-English languages with limited training data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows promising results for Arabic, Japanese, and Russian on Mr. TyDi, but acknowledges that these are the three largest languages in the dataset and didn't test on all languages.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments are limited to three non-English languages and don't address languages with extremely limited training data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing pretraining from scratch on languages with progressively smaller training corpora, measuring effectiveness across different IR tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between pretraining from scratch and fine-tuning for different IR architectures (sparse vs dense vs cross-encoders)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows different behaviors for sparse (SPLADE), dense, and cross-encoder architectures, with pretraining from scratch working better for first-stage rankers but showing slight effectiveness drops for rerankers.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides initial evidence but doesn't explore the full space of architectural variations or determine optimal pretraining/fine-tuning strategies for each type.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies varying pretraining duration, fine-tuning strategies, and architectural modifications for each IR architecture type, measuring effectiveness across multiple datasets.

## Limitations

- The paper does not provide direct corpus statistics comparing the target IR collections to generic pretraining datasets, making it difficult to assess whether the target collections contain sufficient diversity and volume.
- Benefits of pretraining from scratch for efficiency, data bias, and replicability are asserted rather than empirically demonstrated through direct comparisons.
- The study focuses primarily on BERT variants and does not extensively explore whether other transformer architectures could provide additional benefits for IR tasks.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The finding that models pretrained solely on MSMARCO achieve equivalent or better effectiveness compared to more general models for first-stage retrieval is well-supported by experimental results across multiple collections and metrics.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that pretraining from scratch enables better control of efficiency, data bias, and replicability is supported by theoretical arguments but lacks direct empirical evidence comparing these factors between scratch pretraining and generic pretraining approaches.

**Medium confidence**: The observation that different transformer architectures (e.g., DeBERTa) do not necessarily improve IR effectiveness is based on experiments with specific variants, but the paper does not conduct comprehensive comparisons across multiple architectural changes or provide extensive literature context.

## Next Checks

1. **Corpus diversity analysis**: Conduct a quantitative comparison of corpus statistics between the target IR collections (MSMARCO, Mr. TyDi, TripClick) and commonly used generic pretraining datasets to validate whether the target collections contain sufficient diversity and volume for effective pretraining from scratch.

2. **Bias and efficiency control study**: Design experiments that directly compare data bias and efficiency characteristics between models pretrained from scratch on target collections versus models using generic pretraining, including measurements of bias propagation, training efficiency, and inference efficiency.

3. **Cross-collection generalization test**: Evaluate models pretrained from scratch on their target collections across multiple different IR tasks and collections beyond BEIR to assess whether these models generalize as well as or better than generic pretrained models when transferred to new domains.
