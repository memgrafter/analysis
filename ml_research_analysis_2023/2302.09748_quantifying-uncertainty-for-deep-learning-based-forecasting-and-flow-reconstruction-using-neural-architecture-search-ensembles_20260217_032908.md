# Quantifying uncertainty for deep learning based forecasting and flow-reconstruction using neural architecture search ensembles

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09748
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09748
- Reference count: 14
- The proposed AutoDEUQ method achieves superior accuracy and uncertainty quantification compared to individual high-performing models and physics-based models on sea surface temperature forecasting and flow reconstruction tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of uncertainty quantification in deep learning models for forecasting and flow reconstruction tasks in computational physics. The authors propose a scalable neural architecture and hyperparameter search approach using genetic algorithms and Bayesian optimization to discover ensembles of high-performing models. A variance decomposition approach is then employed to estimate both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty from the ensemble predictions. The method is validated on two tasks - forecasting and flow reconstruction for sea surface temperature data - demonstrating superior performance compared to individual models and benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three main phases: (1) neural architecture search using aging evolution with asynchronous Bayesian optimization (AgEBO) to discover diverse, high-performing models, (2) top-K ensemble construction from the discovered models based on validation performance, and (3) uncertainty quantification through variance decomposition of ensemble predictions into aleatoric and epistemic components. The approach is applied to sea surface temperature forecasting using stacked LSTM architectures and flow reconstruction using fully-connected networks with skip connections.

## Key Results
- AutoDEUQ outperforms individual high-performing models and physics-based models in terms of accuracy for both forecasting and flow reconstruction tasks
- The ensemble-based approach provides reliable uncertainty estimates that capture both data noise (aleatoric) and model uncertainty (epistemic)
- Computational scalability is achieved through distributed neural architecture search, allowing efficient exploration of the model space

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The ensemble variance decomposition approach correctly separates aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty.
- Mechanism: The total variance of the ensemble prediction is decomposed into the expected individual variances (aleatoric) and the variance of the individual means (epistemic) using the law of total variance.
- Core assumption: The ensemble members are sufficiently diverse and equally high-performing, and the individual models' predictions are Gaussian distributed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "A variance decomposition approach is then employed to estimate the uncertainty of predictions from the ensemble."
  - [section]: "Equation (3) formally provides the decomposition of the overall uncertainty of the ensemble into its individual components such that E[σ²_θ] marginalizes the effect of θ and captures the aleatoric uncertainty and V[µ_θ] captures the spread of the prediction across different models and neglects the noise of the data, therefore capturing the epistemic uncertainty."
- Break condition: If ensemble members are not sufficiently diverse or the Gaussian assumption is violated, the decomposition will be inaccurate.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Neural architecture search (NAS) discovers high-performing and diverse models suitable for ensemble construction.
- Mechanism: The search space is explored using genetic algorithms and Bayesian optimization, which iteratively mutate and evaluate architectures based on validation loss, leading to a catalog of high-performing models.
- Core assumption: The search space is well-defined and the validation loss is a good proxy for model performance on unseen data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Specifically, we use genetic algorithms and Bayesian optimization for sampling the search space of neural network architectures and hyperparameters."
  - [section]: "The AgEBO method follows the manager-worker paradigm, wherein a manager node runs a search method to generate multiple NNs and W workers (compute nodes) train them simultaneously."
- Break condition: If the search space is poorly defined or the validation loss is not representative of true performance, the discovered models will be suboptimal.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Top-K ensemble selection ensures that only the best models are included, improving overall accuracy and uncertainty quantification.
- Mechanism: After the NAS phase, the K models with the lowest validation loss are selected to form the ensemble, ensuring that only high-performing models contribute to the final prediction.
- Core assumption: The validation data is representative of the generalization task and the validation loss is a reliable indicator of model quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Subsequently, a model selection approach is used to identify candidate models for ensemble set construction."
  - [section]: "To build the ensemble E of models from C, we adopt a top-K strategy (lines 27–32). Here, the idea is to select the K highest-performing models for the validation data and construct the ensemble from its individual predictions."
- Break condition: If the validation data is not representative or the validation loss is not reliable, the selected models may not be the best for the task.

## Limitations

- The variance decomposition approach assumes Gaussian prediction distributions, which may not hold for complex flow reconstruction tasks with multi-modal or heavy-tailed distributions.
- The neural architecture search space is underspecified, making it difficult to assess whether the search algorithm has sufficient coverage to discover truly optimal architectures.
- There is limited evidence on how diversity among selected ensemble members is explicitly ensured beyond validation loss minimization, which is crucial for reliable uncertainty quantification.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The general framework of using ensembles for uncertainty quantification, as this is well-established in the literature
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific variance decomposition approach for separating aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, pending validation of Gaussian assumptions
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of AgEBO for discovering diverse, high-performing models, given limited detail on diversity constraints
- **Low Confidence**: The reproducibility of results due to underspecified search space and hyperparameter ranges
