# Learning the Legibility of Visual Text Perturbations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05077
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05077
- Reference count: 15
- This paper introduces LEGIT, a dataset and methodology for understanding and modeling the legibility of visual text perturbations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LEGIT, a dataset and methodology for understanding and modeling the legibility of visual text perturbations. The authors collect human annotations on the legibility of perturbed words, where characters are replaced with visually similar Unicode characters. They develop both text-based (ByT5) and vision-based (TROCR) models that predict legibility and rank perturbations, achieving up to 0.91 F1 score in classification and 0.86 accuracy in ranking. The models are shown to align well with human judgments and successfully identify challenging perturbations.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce the LEGIT dataset of human-annotated pairwise comparisons of perturbed text pairs, generated from top 10,000 English words using visual similarity models. They fine-tune both text-based (ByT5) and vision-based (TROCR) models on classification and ranking tasks using multi-task losses. The TROCR model uses a Siamese architecture with a contrastive ranking loss, while ByT5 uses byte tokenization to preserve character-level information. Both models are evaluated on their ability to predict legibility and rank perturbations, with TROCR-MT achieving the best performance.

## Key Results
- TROCR-MT model achieves up to 0.91 F1 score in predicting legible vs illegible perturbations
- TROCR-MT achieves 0.86 accuracy in pairwise ranking of perturbations
- LEGIT perturbations are more effective at degrading NLP model performance than existing attack strategies
- Multi-task training improves both classification and ranking performance compared to single-task models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Visual character similarity models can effectively rank perturbations by legibility
- **Mechanism**: TROCR encoder produces visual feature embeddings that correlate with human judgments of legibility. The model learns to map visual renderings of perturbed words to scalar scores that reflect how easily humans can read them.
- **Core assumption**: Visual similarity in pixel space translates to perceived legibility by humans
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "We build both text- and vision-based models which achieve up to 0.91 F1 score in predicting whether an input is legible"
  - [section 4.4] "we find that the TROCR-MT model outperforms its single-task counterpart TROCR-R"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus shows work on robot motion legibility but not text-based visual attacks
- **Break condition**: If perturbations involve characters outside the trained Unicode range or use non-visual attacks (invisible characters, homoglyphs from rare scripts)

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Byte-level language models can distinguish legible from illegible perturbations
- **Mechanism**: ByT5's byte tokenization preserves character-level information, allowing it to detect when substitutions maintain readability versus when they break words entirely
- **Core assumption**: Pretraining on web text exposes ByT5 to enough visual character variants to recognize legibility patterns
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.3] "we find that ByT5 models might have encountered some visual perturbations during pretraining"
  - [section 5] "ByT5 is significantly worse than the vision-based models on ranking, suggesting that language model pretraining is effective at separating legible from illegible perturbations"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors discuss robot motion legibility, not NLP text attacks
- **Break condition**: If perturbations use character combinations that never appear in pretraining data, or if context is essential for legibility

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Multi-task training improves legibility prediction accuracy
- **Mechanism**: Joint classification and ranking objectives create a richer training signal that captures both binary legibility and relative preference information
- **Core assumption**: The ranking task provides additional supervision that helps the model calibrate legibility scores
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.4] "training with a multi-task objective improves performance on both ranking and classification tasks when compared to single-objective models"
  - [section 5] "TROCR-MT model, trained in the multi-task setting, outperforms all other models for F1 score on both tasks"
  - [corpus] None directly relevant
- **Break condition**: If tasks conflict (e.g., ranking prefers one ordering but classification says both are illegible)

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Visual similarity in Unicode character space
  - Why needed here: The attack strategy relies on substituting characters with visually similar Unicode equivalents, so understanding how visual similarity is computed is fundamental
  - Quick check question: What distance metric is used to find k-th nearest neighbors in the visual embedding space?

- **Concept**: Pairwise comparison annotation methodology
  - Why needed here: The dataset was collected using pairwise comparisons rather than absolute ratings, which affects how the data can be used for training models
  - Quick check question: How are binary legibility labels inferred from the pairwise comparison labels?

- **Concept**: Multi-task learning with Siamese networks
  - Why needed here: The TROCR model uses a Siamese architecture with both classification and ranking objectives, requiring understanding of how these components interact
  - Quick check question: How does the contrastive loss encourage the model to rank perturbations correctly?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Image rendering → TROCR encoding → Feature extraction → Legibility scoring → Thresholding for classification
- **Critical path**: Visual rendering of perturbed words → TROCR feature extraction → Legibility score prediction → Classification threshold
- **Design tradeoffs**: Vision-based models capture visual similarity but require rendering overhead; text-based models are faster but may miss visual nuances
- **Failure signatures**: Low F1 scores on hard subsets indicate difficulty with subtle perturbations; poor ranking accuracy suggests calibration issues
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train TROCR-C on classification task and evaluate on test set
  2. Train TROCR-R on ranking task and compare with TROCR-C performance
  3. Train TROCR-MT with both objectives and measure improvement over single-task models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How would the performance of TROCR-MT compare to vision-based models if the model architecture were modified to accept multimodal inputs (both pixels and Unicode bytes)?
- **Open Question 2**: How would the results differ if the dataset included words with lengths less than 4 characters?
- **Open Question 3**: How would the performance of the models change if the perturbation process considered character deletions, additions, or swaps in addition to substitutions?
- **Open Question 4**: How would the results differ if the dataset included context around the words, rather than just individual words in isolation?

## Limitations

- Domain transferability is unclear for non-Latin scripts and mixed-language contexts
- The paper doesn't establish whether TROCR-based defenses can withstand more sophisticated attacks combining visual perturbations with contextual manipulation
- Human agreement calibration is uncertain due to lack of inter-annotator agreement rates

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: TROCR model architecture and its superior performance on both classification (0.91 F1) and ranking (0.86 accuracy) tasks
- **Medium Confidence**: Language model pretraining helps ByT5 distinguish legible from illegible perturbations
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that current NLP models are "vulnerable to a broader range of legible perturbations than previously thought"

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Script Generalization Test**: Evaluate TROCR and ByT5 models on LEGIT-style perturbations for non-Latin scripts to quantify performance degradation and identify visual similarity model limitations.

2. **Transfer Attack Evaluation**: Test whether perturbations effective against toxicity classifiers also transfer to other NLP tasks (sentiment analysis, named entity recognition) to assess the breadth of the attack surface.

3. **Annotator Agreement Analysis**: Re-analyze the pairwise comparison data to measure Cohen's kappa between annotators and identify specific perturbation types with high disagreement rates.
