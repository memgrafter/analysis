# Pre-training Multi-party Dialogue Models with Latent Discourse Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15175
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15175
- Reference count: 18
- State-of-the-art results on multi-party dialogue tasks without addressee annotations

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of pre-training models for multi-party dialogues where addressee labels are missing. It introduces a two-stage pre-training strategy that combines Expectation-Maximization (EM) and Variational Inference (VI) to infer latent discourse structures and train a discourse-aware model without explicit annotations. In stage one, EM is used for single-turn addressee inference with context-response matching objectives. In stage two, VI extends inference to multi-turn reply-to relations using the EM-derived prior. Experiments on downstream tasks show the method outperforms strong baselines, achieving state-of-the-art results on discourse parsing, question answering, response generation, and new-entry prediction tasks, validating the effectiveness of leveraging unlabeled multi-party conversational data.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a two-stage pre-training strategy for multi-party dialogue models without addressee annotations. Stage one employs Expectation-Maximization (EM) to infer addressee labels for single-turn dialogues using context-response matching objectives. Stage two extends this to multi-turn dialogues through Variational Inference (VI), which infers reply-to relations while leveraging the addressee prior from stage one. The approach combines these latent discourse inference techniques with standard pre-training objectives to create a discourse-aware model that can be fine-tuned for downstream tasks. The method is evaluated on Ubuntu and Persona-Chat datasets across four tasks: discourse parsing, question answering, response generation, and new-entry prediction.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on four multi-party dialogue downstream tasks
- Outperforms strong baselines that use explicit addressee annotations
- Demonstrates effectiveness of latent discourse inference for pre-training without labeled data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging the structure inherent in multi-party dialogues without requiring explicit annotations. The EM algorithm in stage one identifies likely addressee relationships in single-turn contexts by optimizing a context-response matching objective, effectively learning to recognize patterns that indicate who is responding to whom. The VI framework in stage two extends this to multi-turn contexts by modeling reply-to relations as latent variables with a prior derived from the EM stage, allowing the model to capture longer-range discourse dependencies. This two-stage approach creates a coherent latent discourse structure that can be used to train a discourse-aware model without the need for expensive annotation.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Raw Dialogue Data -> EM Addressee Inference -> VI Reply-to Inference -> Pre-training Objectives -> Downstream Fine-tuning
Critical path: EM inference (Stage 1) -> VI inference (Stage 2) -> Model pre-training -> Task-specific fine-tuning
Design tradeoffs: The two-stage approach adds complexity but enables training without annotations; simpler alternatives might treat all participants as speakers but would miss discourse structure
Failure signatures: Poor addressee inference leading to incorrect discourse structures; VI not properly leveraging EM prior resulting in inconsistent multi-turn predictions
First experiments: 1) Test EM addressee inference accuracy on single-turn dialogues with available labels, 2) Validate VI reply-to inference on multi-turn dialogues with partial annotations, 3) Compare downstream task performance against baselines using ground-truth discourse structures

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on downstream task performance rather than direct validation of inferred discourse structures
- No ablation studies demonstrating the necessity of both EM and VI stages
- Limited comparison to simpler approaches like treating all participants as speakers without addressee prediction
- Does not address scalability or computational overhead compared to standard pre-training approaches

## Confidence
High: The methodology is technically sound and the empirical results are consistent across four different downstream tasks
Medium: The lack of direct evaluation of inferred discourse structures and limited baseline comparisons reduces confidence in the approach's advantages
Low: The paper does not provide sufficient evidence for the necessity of the complex two-stage approach over simpler alternatives

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies comparing EM-only, VI-only, and the full EM+VI pipeline to quantify the contribution of each component
2. Perform human evaluation of the inferred addressee predictions and reply-to relations to directly assess the quality of the latent discourse inference
3. Test the approach on additional multi-party dialogue datasets with varying characteristics (e.g., more participants, different domains) to evaluate generalizability beyond the Ubuntu and Persona-Chat datasets used in the experiments
