# OTW: Optimal Transport Warping for Time Series

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00620
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00620
- Reference count: 0
- This paper proposes OTW (Optimal Transport Warping), a new time series distance metric based on optimal transport theory.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces OTW (Optimal Transport Warping), a time series distance metric that combines the benefits of optimal transport with linear computational complexity. OTW overcomes the quadratic complexity of DTW by using a windowed cumulative sum approach while maintaining differentiability and metric properties. The method achieves moderate sensitivity to time and shape distortions, making it particularly suitable for time series classification and clustering tasks.

## Method Summary
OTW adapts optimal transport theory for time series by treating sequences as probability distributions with appended sink elements to handle mass imbalance. The method uses cumulative sums within a sliding window to compute transport costs, achieving linear time complexity instead of DTW's quadratic complexity. A smooth ℓ₁ loss function replaces absolute values to maintain differentiability, while a waste cost parameter handles mass differences between time series.

## Key Results
- OTW outperforms DTW in 1-NN classification on 6/7 dataset types from UCR archive
- OTW achieves superior hierarchical clustering results on all 7 dataset types tested
- Replacing DTW with OTW in DTW-Net architecture achieves similar accuracy 50-60% faster
- OTW maintains linear time/space complexity while being differentiable and GPU-parallelizable

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input time series vectors -> Cumulative sums with window s -> Smooth ℓ₁ loss application -> Weighted sum for final distance

- Critical path:
  1. Compute cumulative sums with window s
  2. Apply smooth ℓ₁ loss to differences
  3. Sum weighted differences for final distance
  4. Backpropagate through smooth approximation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Window size s: Larger s increases alignment flexibility but reduces local pattern sensitivity
  - Waste cost m: Higher m penalizes mass imbalance more severely
  - Smoothness parameter β: Smaller β approaches true metric but may cause numerical issues

- Failure signatures:
  - If s is too small: Performance degrades to ℓ₁ norm, losing alignment benefits
  - If m is too large: Mass imbalance dominates distance, overshadowing shape/time patterns
  - If β is too small: Numerical instability in gradient computation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of OTW compare to DTW on multivariate time series data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that a limitation is the constraint that time series be one-dimensional and leaves possible extensions to the multivariate case as a promising direction of future research.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates OTW on univariate time series datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing OTW to DTW on multivariate time series benchmarks would show whether OTW's advantages extend to higher dimensions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the waste cost parameter m on OTW's sensitivity to mass differences between time series?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the waste cost m and states that for large values of n, the choice m = n would put too much weight on the first component m|A(n) - B(n)|, which strongly penalizes the total mass difference.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic study of how different values of m affect OTW's performance across datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation study varying m and measuring OTW's classification/clustering accuracy would quantify the impact of the waste cost on performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does OTW's sensitivity to time shifts compare to Soft-DTW's sensitivity, and is there a theoretical explanation for the difference?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that Soft-DTW has a quadratic lower bound on its sensitivity to time-shifts, while OTW has a linear upper bound. It provides a lemma showing OTW's sensitivity increases linearly with time shift magnitude.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides an upper bound for OTW's sensitivity, not a lower bound. It also does not explain why OTW's sensitivity is linear while Soft-DTW's is quadratic.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving a matching lower bound for OTW's sensitivity and deriving the theoretical reason for the difference in sensitivity between OTW and Soft-DTW would fully characterize OTW's behavior with respect to time shifts.
