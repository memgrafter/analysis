# MoMo: A shared encoder Model for text, image and multi-Modal representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05523
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05523
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Compact shared-encoder multimodal model outperforming state-of-the-art with 2/5th parameters and 1/3rd training data

## Executive Summary
MoMo introduces a compact, efficient shared-encoder model for multimodal tasks. It uses a single transformer to process text, images, and multimodal inputs, avoiding separate modality-specific encoders. The model is trained in three stages: (1) unimodal image data, (2) unimodal image and text data jointly, and (3) unimodal text and multimodal image-text data jointly, using simultaneous learning across modalities. MoMo outperforms state-of-the-art multimodal models like FLAVA and CLIP on vision, language, and multimodal benchmarks, achieving competitive performance with 2/5th the parameters and 1/3rd the training data. A larger MoMo-Large variant shows further performance gains. The approach demonstrates strong data efficiency, runtime efficiency, and simplifies deployment by unifying multimodal and unimodal tasks under a single model.

## Method Summary
MoMo is trained through a three-stage pipeline using simultaneous learning with cross-modality gradient accumulation (CMGA). Stage 1 trains on ImageNet-1K using MIM loss with 75% masking. Stage 2 jointly trains on Wikipedia & Books Corpus (MLM, 15% masking) and repeated ImageNet-1K (MIM, 75% masking) for 100 epochs. Stage 3 trains on PMD-Subset (COCO, SBU Captions, Conceptual Captions, Visual Genome, RedCaps) for 100 epochs using MLM for text (15% masking), CMM for multimodal pairs, and global contrastive and image-text matching losses. The shared transformer encoder has 12 layers with 768 hidden dimension, while separate decoders (8 layers, 512 dim) are used per stage and discarded after training.

## Key Results
- Outperforms FLAVA and CLIP on vision, language, and multimodal benchmarks
- Achieves competitive performance with 2/5th the parameters of comparable models
- Requires only 1/3rd the training data of state-of-the-art approaches
- MoMo-Large variant shows further performance improvements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Stage-wise training with simultaneous modality learning prevents catastrophic forgetting and preserves unimodal performance. By training sequentially through stages—first on images, then jointly on text and images, and finally on text and multimodal pairs—the model can first establish strong unimodal representations before learning to align modalities. The simultaneous gradient accumulation (CMGA) ensures each modality's gradients are applied together, preventing dominance by any single modality.

### Mechanism 2
Shared encoder architecture reduces parameters and inference cost while maintaining performance across tasks. By using a single transformer to process all modalities rather than separate encoders, the model shares parameters and computational resources. During inference, the same encoder weights are reused for text-only, image-only, and multimodal tasks, eliminating the need for separate model deployments.

### Mechanism 3
Cross-modal masking loss enhances multimodal representation learning by forcing the model to use information from both modalities. During stage 3, both image and text tokens are masked at high fractions (75%) and the model must reconstruct them using visible tokens from the other modality. This creates strong cross-modal dependencies and forces the model to learn meaningful alignments.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Masked Language Modeling (MLM)
  - Why needed here: MLM is the primary objective for text modality learning in stages 2 and 3, teaching the model to understand and generate text representations
  - Quick check question: If you mask 15% of tokens in a sentence, what is the expected number of masked tokens in a 20-token sequence?

- Concept: Masked Image Modeling (MIM)
  - Why needed here: MIM is the primary objective for image modality learning in stages 1 and 2, teaching the model to understand and reconstruct visual information
  - Quick check question: If you use 75% masking on a 16x16 image patch sequence, how many patches remain visible for reconstruction?

- Concept: Cross-modal contrastive learning
  - Why needed here: Contrastive loss aligns image and text representations in a shared embedding space during stage 3, enabling zero-shot retrieval and multimodal understanding
  - Quick check question: In contrastive learning, what happens to the loss if positive pairs are incorrectly labeled as negatives?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input (text + image patches) -> Token Embedding Layer -> Shared Transformer Encoder (12 layers, 768 dim) -> Stage-specific Decoders -> Loss Heads -> Backpropagation

- Critical path: 1. Tokenize input (text + image patches) 2. Embed tokens into shared latent space 3. Process through shared encoder 4. Apply appropriate decoder (stage-dependent) 5. Compute loss and backpropagate 6. Accumulate gradients from both modalities 7. Apply single weight update

- Design tradeoffs: Shared encoder vs separate encoders (saves parameters but may limit modality-specific optimization), separate decoders vs shared decoder (better performance but more parameters), high masking ratios (stronger cross-modal learning but may reduce stable training signals)

- Failure signatures: Training instability (gradients from different modalities have vastly different magnitudes), performance collapse (unimodal tasks degrade significantly during multimodal training), slow convergence (cross-modal interactions are too weak to learn meaningful alignments)

- First 3 experiments: 1. Train stage 1 only on ImageNet-1K with MIM loss, verify ImageNet linear evaluation performance matches baseline 2. Train stage 2 with simultaneous text and image data, verify unimodal text performance matches BERT-base while maintaining image performance 3. Train full model through all three stages, verify multimodal retrieval performance on COCO and Flickr30K datasets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the simultaneous learning strategy generalize to other modalities beyond text, images, and text-image pairs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that their simultaneous learning strategy is effective for learning from multiple modalities, but does not explore its applicability to other modalities like audio or video.
- Why unresolved: The paper only experiments with text, images, and text-image pairs. It does not investigate whether the simultaneous learning strategy would be effective for other modalities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments using the simultaneous learning strategy on models trained on audio, video, or other modalities would provide evidence for its generalizability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using a shared encoder on the model's ability to learn modality-specific features?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that using a shared encoder can lead to information loss when trained independently on different modalities. However, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how the shared encoder affects the model's ability to learn modality-specific features.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of the shared encoder on the model's ability to learn modality-specific features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that compare the performance of the shared encoder model with modality-specific encoders on tasks that require learning modality-specific features would provide evidence for the impact of the shared encoder.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of MoMo scale with larger datasets and more complex tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that increasing the model size leads to significant performance gains, indicating potential for improvements with larger models. However, it does not explore the impact of larger datasets or more complex tasks on the model's performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the impact of larger datasets or more complex tasks on the model's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments using larger datasets and more complex tasks would provide evidence for the scalability of MoMo's performance.

## Limitations

- Gradient accumulation stability remains untested under different batch sizes, learning rates, and modality data distributions
- Architecture generality to other modalities like audio, video, or 3D data is not addressed
- Dataset composition transparency is limited, making it difficult to assess whether performance gains are due to model architecture or data curation

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Claims about parameter efficiency (2/5th parameters, 1/3rd training data) are directly measurable and well-supported by ablation studies.

**Medium Confidence**: Claims about runtime efficiency improvements and simplified deployment are reasonable given the shared architecture, but depend on specific implementation details not fully disclosed.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about the 75% cross-modal masking being optimal are weakly supported - the paper doesn't explore other masking ratios or provide theoretical justification for this specific value.

## Next Checks

1. **Gradient Norm Analysis**: Measure and compare gradient norms from different modalities during simultaneous learning. If gradients from text and images have significantly different magnitudes, the accumulated update may be unstable or biased toward one modality.

2. **Modality Ablation Test**: Train the model with only text data through all three stages, then only image data through all three stages. Compare performance degradation to traditional separate-encoder approaches to quantify the cost of sharing parameters.

3. **Cross-Modal Masking Sensitivity**: Systematically vary the cross-modal masking ratio (25%, 50%, 75%, 90%) while keeping other parameters constant. Measure the impact on multimodal retrieval performance to determine if 75% is truly optimal or just one of many viable options.
