# Towards Understanding and Improving GFlowNet Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07170
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07170
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GFlowNets undersample high-reward objects during initialization, addressed by prioritized replay, relative edge flow parametrization, and guided trajectory balance, achieving up to 10x faster learning on biochemical tasks

## Executive Summary
GFlowNets aim to match a target distribution proportional to a reward function but face challenges under practical training constraints. The authors identify that GFlowNets tend to undersample high-reward objects, especially at initialization when their policy has high entropy. To address this, they propose three main improvements: prioritized replay training that focuses on high-reward examples, a relative edge flow parametrization that generalizes better by conditioning on child states, and guided trajectory balance, a novel objective that solves substructure credit assignment by guiding flow through important substructures. Experiments on five biochemical design tasks show these methods substantially improve sample efficiency and convergence, with up to 10x faster learning and better final performance compared to baselines.

## Method Summary
The paper addresses GFlowNet training challenges through three complementary improvements. First, prioritized replay training focuses on high-reward examples by sampling transitions based on their reward magnitude, addressing the undersampling of valuable states. Second, the relative edge flow parametrization conditions flow values on child states rather than parent states, enabling better generalization across similar substructures. Third, guided trajectory balance introduces a new objective that solves the substructure credit assignment problem by explicitly guiding flow through important substructures identified during training. These methods work synergistically to improve both sample efficiency and final performance on molecular design tasks.

## Key Results
- Prioritized replay, relative edge flow parametrization, and guided trajectory balance together achieve up to 10x faster learning compared to baseline GFlowNet methods
- The proposed methods show substantially better final performance on five diverse biochemical design tasks including molecular property optimization and protein function prediction
- Ablation studies demonstrate that each component contributes meaningfully, with guided trajectory balance providing the largest individual improvement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The undersampling of high-reward objects during GFlowNet initialization occurs because high-entropy policies explore broadly rather than concentrating on promising regions of the state space. The prioritized replay mechanism directly addresses this by ensuring high-reward transitions are experienced more frequently during training. The relative edge flow parametrization improves generalization by making flow predictions conditional on child states, allowing the model to share knowledge across similar substructures. The guided trajectory balance objective solves the credit assignment problem by explicitly routing flow through high-value substructures, preventing the dilution of gradient signals that occurs when flow is distributed evenly across all possible paths.

## Foundational Learning
- **GFlowNets**: Generative flow networks that learn to sample objects from a distribution proportional to a reward function, needed for tasks where we want to generate high-reward examples
- **Credit assignment in generative models**: The challenge of properly attributing rewards to specific actions in the generation process, critical for learning efficient sampling policies
- **Trajectory balance objective**: The standard GFlowNet training objective that balances flow conservation across trajectories, but struggles with substructure credit assignment
- **Prioritized experience replay**: A technique from RL that samples important transitions more frequently, here adapted to focus on high-reward examples in GFlowNets
- **Relative edge flow**: A parametrization that conditions flow values on child states rather than parent states, enabling better generalization across similar substructures
- **Flow conservation constraints**: The fundamental requirement that flow into a state equals flow out of it, which guides the learning process but creates challenges for credit assignment

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Policy network -> Flow predictor (edge flow) -> Reward predictor -> Prioritized replay buffer -> Guided trajectory balance loss

Critical path:
Input state → Policy network → Action selection → Environment → Next state → Flow prediction → Reward calculation → Loss computation → Parameter update

Design tradeoffs:
- Prioritized replay vs. uniform sampling: balances exploration and exploitation but may introduce bias
- Relative vs. absolute edge flow: generalization vs. simplicity in implementation
- Guided trajectory balance vs. trajectory balance: better credit assignment vs. increased computational complexity

Failure signatures:
- Mode collapse when prioritization is too aggressive
- Unstable training when flow conservation constraints are violated
- Poor generalization when relative edge flow is not properly conditioned

## Limitations
- Empirical evaluation limited to biochemical domains with specific reward landscape properties
- Claims about performance improvements tied to specific problem structures that may not generalize
- Guided trajectory balance requires additional computational overhead compared to standard objectives

## Confidence
