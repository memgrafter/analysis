# Augmenting Autotelic Agents with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12487
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12487
- Reference count: 21
- One-line primary result: LMA3 agents autonomously discover and master ~9000 distinct goals in CookingWorld, including complex abstract goals, by leveraging LLMs for goal generation, relabeling, and reward functions.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LMA3, a language model-augmented autotelic agent that autonomously generates and pursues diverse, abstract, and human-relevant goals in a task-agnostic text-based environment without hand-coded goal representations or reward functions. The key innovation is leveraging a large language model (LLM) as a model of human cultural transmission to implement three components: a relabeler that describes achieved goals in trajectories, a goal generator that suggests new high-level goals decomposed into subgoals, and a reward function to measure goal completion. The agent uses an LLM to generate goals and rewards, and a simple evolutionary algorithm for skill learning. In the CookingWorld environment, LMA3 discovers and masters around 9000 distinct goals, including complex and abstract ones involving conjunctions, categories, and object attributes. The diversity of goals is significantly increased by using chain-of-thought prompting and human-generated advice in the LLM prompts. LMA3 achieves near-perfect performance on a human-defined set of 69 evaluation goals after finetuning with an oracle relabeler, demonstrating its ability to learn human-relevant skills autonomously.

## Method Summary
LMA3 uses a large language model (ChatGPT) to implement three components: a relabeler that describes achieved goals in trajectories, a goal generator that suggests new high-level goals decomposed into subgoals, and a reward function to measure goal completion. The agent learns via a simple evolutionary algorithm that evolves sequences of actions conditioned on the goal. The agent goes through a bootstrapping phase and then uses the LLM Goal Generator to suggest new high-level goals and their decompositions. The LLM relabeler describes what goals the agent achieved in each trajectory, and the reward function provides feedback on goal completion. LMA3 does not require any human or engineer input and can generate and master a much wider diversity of goals thanks to the common-sense knowledge and generalization capabilities of LMs.

## Key Results
- LMA3 discovers and masters around 9000 distinct goals in CookingWorld, including complex and abstract ones involving conjunctions, categories, and object attributes.
- The diversity of discovered goals is significantly increased by using chain-of-thought prompting and human-generated advice in the LLM prompts.
- LMA3 achieves near-perfect performance (97%) on a human-defined set of 69 evaluation goals after finetuning with an oracle relabeler.
- LMA3 demonstrates the ability to learn human-relevant skills autonomously without any hand-coded goal representations or reward functions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Language models act as imperfect models of human cultural transmission, capturing aspects of common-sense, intuitive physics, and overall interests.
- Mechanism: The LM relabels agent trajectories with human-relevant goal descriptions and generates new goals decomposed into subgoals the agent already masters. This mimics how humans learn from others' experiences and recombine existing skills.
- Core assumption: The LM has been pretrained on sufficient human-generated text to capture relevant cultural knowledge about goals and skills.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The LM is used as an imperfect model of human cultural transmission; an attempt to capture aspects of humans' common-sense, intuitive physics and overall interests."
  - [section] "We leverage a pretrained language model (LM) as a (crude) model of human interests, biases and common-sense."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Chain-of-thought prompting and human advice in prompts increase the diversity and abstraction of discovered goals.
- Mechanism: By providing examples of abstract goals involving conjunctions, categories, and creative combinations, the LM is nudged to generate more diverse goal descriptions beyond simple single-action goals.
- Core assumption: The LM can generalize from provided examples to generate novel abstract goals not explicitly shown in examples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Although the conditions with/without human advice use the same number of examples in their prompts, the more abstract examples used in the full LMA3 condition drives a significant increase in both the diversity of discovered goals... and in the abstraction of these goals"
  - [section] "we measure the sensitivity of LMA3 agents to a small amount of human advice... nudging the relabeler to describe more abstract behaviors involving conjunctions of actions"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of relabeler, goal generator, and reward function enables autotelic learning without predefined goal representations.
- Mechanism: The relabeler creates goal representations from trajectories, the goal generator suggests new goals using existing ones, and the reward function provides feedback for learning - together enabling autonomous skill discovery.
- Core assumption: The LM can accurately relabel trajectories and generate meaningful goals when prompted appropriately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "LMA3 agents discover and master around 9000 distinct goals... including complex and abstract ones involving conjunctions, categories, and object attributes."
  - [section] "LMA3 does not require any human or engineer input and can generate and master a much wider diversity of goals thanks to the common-sense knowledge and generalization capabilities of LMs."

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Autotelic learning - agents that are intrinsically motivated to represent, generate, pursue, and master their own goals
  - Why needed here: LMA3 is specifically designed as an autotelic agent that learns skills by generating its own goals rather than following predefined objectives
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes autotelic learning from traditional goal-conditioned RL?

- Concept: Cultural transmission in AI - learning through representations evolved and transmitted across individuals, particularly using language
  - Why needed here: The paper explicitly frames the LM as a model of human cultural transmission, which is central to how LMA3 generates relevant goals
  - Quick check question: How does cultural transmission differ from traditional imitation learning in AI?

- Concept: Hindsight experience replay - the ability to learn from failed attempts by relabeling trajectories with achieved goals
  - Why needed here: LMA3 uses LM relabeling to implement hindsight learning, allowing it to learn from all trajectories regardless of whether the intended goal was achieved
  - Quick check question: Why is hindsight experience replay particularly valuable in open-ended learning environments?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: LM Goal Generator -> Rollout -> LM Relabeler -> LM Reward Function -> Skill Learning -> Dictionary update
- Critical path: Goal Generator → Rollout → Relabeler → Reward Function → Skill Learning → Dictionary update
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simple evolutionary algorithm vs. deep RL for skill learning (reduced sample complexity vs. better generalization)
  - Deterministic environment (simplifies learning) vs. stochastic (more realistic)
  - No explicit goal representation (autonomy) vs. hard-coded goals (efficiency)
- Failure signatures:
  - LM hallucinating goals → agent learns irrelevant skills
  - Poor relabeling accuracy → agent cannot learn from experience
  - Goal generator stuck in local minima → limited diversity of discovered skills
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run LMA3 without human tips to establish baseline diversity and abstraction
  2. Test LMA3 with human tips to measure impact on goal diversity and abstraction
  3. Compare LMA3 against oracle baseline to quantify performance on human-relevant goals

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would LMA3's performance scale in more complex, open-world environments like Minecraft compared to CookingWorld?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions considering larger worlds and Minecraft as a potential future direction, noting that current text-based environments are not open worlds.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates LMA3 in the constrained CookingWorld environment and discusses potential extensions but does not provide empirical evidence of performance in more complex environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing LMA3's goal diversity, learning efficiency, and skill mastery in Minecraft or similar open-world environments versus CookingWorld would provide evidence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using different language models or prompting strategies on LMA3's ability to generate diverse and abstract goals?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses ChatGPT and specific prompting strategies (chain-of-thought, human advice) but acknowledges that future work could explore other models and prompting techniques.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one language model (ChatGPT) and a limited set of prompting strategies, leaving open the question of how other models or prompts might affect performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the language model (e.g., GPT-4, open-source alternatives) and prompting strategies would reveal their impact on goal diversity and quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does LMA3's goal generation and skill learning compare to other unsupervised skill discovery methods in terms of efficiency and quality of learned skills?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper contrasts LMA3 with unsupervised skill discovery methods like DIAYN and Skew-Fit but does not provide direct comparisons or quantitative analysis of their relative performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses theoretical differences, it lacks empirical comparisons to measure efficiency and skill quality relative to other methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head experiments comparing LMA3 with DIAYN, Skew-Fit, and similar methods on the same tasks would provide insights into their relative strengths and weaknesses.

## Limitations

- The paper's claims about LM-as-cultural-transmission rest on the untested assumption that pretraining data sufficiently captures human-relevant goal structures for novel environments.
- The CookingWorld environment is deterministic and relatively constrained, limiting generalization claims to stochastic or continuous domains.
- The evolutionary algorithm for skill learning, while simple, may not scale to more complex action spaces.
- The human evaluation of goal relevance is qualitative and not systematically validated.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The core architecture (LM relabeler + goal generator + reward function + evolutionary skill learning) is well-specified and the empirical results in CookingWorld are reproducible given the environment implementation.
- Medium confidence: Claims about goal diversity improvements from chain-of-thought prompting and human advice, as these depend on specific prompt engineering choices not fully detailed in the paper.
- Low confidence: Generalization claims to more complex environments and the assertion that LMA3 captures "human cultural transmission" in any meaningful sense beyond surface-level goal relabeling.

## Next Checks

1. **Transfer validation**: Test LMA3 in a stochastic variant of CookingWorld or a different domain (e.g., ScienceWorld mentioned in related work) to assess robustness beyond the deterministic environment.

2. **Prompt ablation study**: Systematically vary the complexity and abstraction level of examples in LM prompts to quantify the relationship between prompt design and goal diversity, isolating the contribution of chain-of-thought prompting.

3. **Oracle comparison refinement**: Compare LMA3 not just against an oracle with perfect LM calls, but against a version using a smaller language model or a rule-based relabeler to measure the specific contribution of large LMs versus simpler approaches.
