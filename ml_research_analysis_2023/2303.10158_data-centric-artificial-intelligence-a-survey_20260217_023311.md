# Data-centric Artificial Intelligence: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10158
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10158
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This survey provides a comprehensive overview of data-centric artificial intelligence, organizing existing research into three main goals: training data development, inference data development, and data maintenance.

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of data-centric artificial intelligence, a rapidly growing field that emphasizes the systematic engineering of data for AI systems. The authors organize existing research into three main goals: training data development, inference data development, and data maintenance. For each goal, they discuss representative methods, challenges, and provide a framework for categorizing approaches based on automation levels and human participation. The survey also collects and analyzes data benchmarks across different domains and data modalities, revealing that tabular and image data are the most frequently studied.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically reviews data-centric AI literature by organizing it into three hierarchical goals and categorizing methods based on automation levels and human participation degrees. The authors collected and analyzed 36 benchmarks across various domains and data modalities, using these as a basis for assessing research progress and identifying gaps. The methodology involves a comprehensive literature review, taxonomy development, and meta-analysis of existing benchmarks to provide a global view of the data-centric AI landscape.

## Key Results
- Data-centric AI literature can be organized into three main goals: training data development, inference data development, and data maintenance
- Methods are categorized by automation level (fully manual, semiautomated, fully automated) and human participation degree (fully human-driven, human-in-the-loop, fully automated)
- Tabular and image data are the most frequently studied domains in data-centric AI research, with less attention given to audio, graph, and time-series data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a unified benchmark that comprehensively evaluates data quality and various data-centric AI techniques across different domains and data modalities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss the need for unified data benchmarks and mention that existing benchmarks typically focus on specific tasks, with DataPerf [155] being a notable but limited effort
- Why unresolved: Creating a comprehensive benchmark that spans multiple data-centric AI tasks, domains, and modalities is a complex challenge that requires addressing various technical, methodological, and practical issues
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of a benchmark suite that covers a wide range of data-centric AI tasks, includes diverse datasets from multiple domains and modalities, and demonstrates its effectiveness in evaluating and comparing different techniques and approaches

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective strategies for jointly automating multiple data-centric AI tasks across different goals (training data development, inference data development, and data maintenance)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss pipeline search methods for automating multiple tasks within training data development but note that joint automation across different goals remains largely unexplored
- Why unresolved: Automating multiple tasks across different goals requires addressing complex interactions, dependencies, and trade-offs between various techniques, which is a challenging problem that has not been fully explored
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of methods that can effectively automate multiple data-centric AI tasks across different goals, demonstrating improved performance, efficiency, or other benefits compared to individual task automation or manual approaches

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we effectively debias data to address fairness issues in high-stakes applications while maintaining data utility and model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors identify debiasing data as a potential future direction, noting that biases often originate from imbalanced distributions of sensitive variables in the data
- Why unresolved: Debiasing data is a complex challenge that requires balancing multiple objectives (fairness, utility, and performance) and addressing various types of biases, which is an active area of research with no definitive solutions
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of techniques that can effectively mitigate biases in data while preserving or improving data utility and model performance, demonstrated through rigorous evaluation on diverse datasets and real-world applications

## Limitations

- The three-goal taxonomy may not capture all emerging data-centric AI tasks or methodologies
- Classification of methods by automation and collaboration levels relies on subjective judgments
- Benchmark analysis may not fully represent all relevant domains or emerging areas in data-centric AI research

## Confidence

- High confidence: The general framework of data-centric AI goals (training, inference, maintenance) is well-established in the literature
- Medium confidence: The automation/collaboration categorization is useful but may not align perfectly with all practitioners' mental models
- Medium confidence: The benchmark analysis accurately reflects current research trends but may miss emerging areas
