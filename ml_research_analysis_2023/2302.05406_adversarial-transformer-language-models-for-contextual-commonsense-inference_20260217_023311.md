# Adversarial Transformer Language Models for Contextual Commonsense Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05406
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05406
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces GAN-based approach for contextual commonsense inference from stories using hinting technique, joint inference across knowledge bases, and adversarial training for plausibility scoring

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for generating commonsense assertions relevant to a target sentence within a story context using adversarial transformer language models. The system employs a "hinting" technique with soft and hard prompts for controllable generation, performs joint inference across multiple knowledge bases (ConceptNet, ATOMIC2020, GLUCOSE), and uses adversarial training with a discriminator to evaluate the plausibility of generated assertions. Experiments demonstrate that the hinting technique improves automated metric performance, joint inference achieves comparable results to single knowledge base models when combined with hinting, and the adversarial setup achieves 69% accuracy in validating generated assertions.

## Method Summary
The approach utilizes a GAN-based framework where a generator produces commonsense assertions based on story context and target sentences, while a discriminator evaluates their plausibility. The hinting technique allows controllable generation by incorporating soft and hard prompts into the transformer architecture. The system performs joint inference across multiple commonsense knowledge bases (ConceptNet, ATOMIC2020, GLUCOSE) to generate contextually relevant assertions. The adversarial training setup involves the generator attempting to produce plausible assertions that can fool the discriminator, while the discriminator learns to distinguish between real and generated assertions based on their plausibility.

## Key Results
- Hinting technique using soft/hard prompts improves automated metric performance
- Joint inference across multiple knowledge bases provides comparable results to single knowledge base models when combined with hinting
- Adversarial setup achieves 69% accuracy in validating generated assertions
- System enables controllable, multi-source commonsense inference with plausibility assessment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system works by leveraging the transformer's ability to capture contextual relationships within stories and generate relevant commonsense assertions. The hinting technique provides control over the generation process by incorporating specific prompts that guide the model toward desired types of inferences. The joint inference across multiple knowledge bases enriches the commonsense knowledge available to the model, allowing it to draw from diverse sources of information. The adversarial training creates a feedback loop where the generator learns to produce increasingly plausible assertions while the discriminator becomes more adept at evaluating them.

## Limitations
- The discriminator's training methodology and evaluation features are not detailed, making it difficult to assess robustness
- Automated metrics (BLEU, METEOR, BERTScore) may not fully capture quality and relevance of commonsense inferences in narrative contexts
- Lack of comparison of inference quality or diversity between joint and single knowledge base models

## Confidence
- High confidence: The hinting technique using soft/hard prompts is a valid approach for controllable generation and shows measurable improvement in automated metrics
- Medium confidence: The joint inference across multiple knowledge bases provides comparable results to single knowledge base models, though the paper lacks comparison of inference quality or diversity
- Medium confidence: The 69% accuracy of the adversarial setup in validating generated assertions is reported, but without details on the discriminator training or evaluation methodology, this claim requires further validation
