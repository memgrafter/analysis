# Mastering the ABCDs of Complex Questions: Answer-Based Claim Decomposition for Fine-grained Self-Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14750
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14750
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: GPT-3.5 can distinguish between correct and incorrect answers by checking claim satisfaction, with significant differences across all datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces answer-based claims decomposition (ABCD), a prompting strategy that decomposes complex questions into true/false claims to verify which criteria an LLM answer satisfies. The approach uses GPT-3.5 to first generate claims based on the question, then self-evaluate the LLM's answer against these claims. Experimental results show that GPT-3.5 can distinguish to some extent between correct and incorrect answers by checking how many claims the answer satisfies, with significant differences observed across all datasets. However, the method is not fully reliable, and an error analysis identifies limitations such as self-consistency, tense handling, ambiguity, reasoning complexity, and false assumptions in claims.

## Method Summary
The ABCD method involves three main steps: (1) question decomposition into true/false claims using GPT-3.5 with few-shot examples, (2) initial answer generation with GPT-3.5, and (3) fine-grained self-evaluation where the model checks each claim independently. The approach treats claims as independent assertions that must be true if the answer is correct, allowing the LLM to quantify how close an incorrect answer is to being correct. For multi-hop reasoning questions, the method includes entity linking to maintain consistency across related claims.

## Key Results
- GPT-3.5 shows significant ability to distinguish correct from incorrect answers based on claim satisfaction across TRIVIA QA, HOTPOT QA, and OBSCURE QA datasets
- Error analysis reveals systematic issues including self-consistency problems, tense handling difficulties, and reasoning failures
- False assumptions in claims present a challenge, though recursive decomposition is proposed as a potential solution

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Decomposing complex questions into true/false claims enables LLMs to self-evaluate which criteria an answer satisfies
- Mechanism: The ABCD method transforms a complex question into a checklist of claims that must be true if the answer is correct, allowing the LLM to verify each criterion independently
- Core assumption: LLMs can accurately decompose questions into claims and evaluate each claim independently when given appropriate prompts
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose answer-based claim decomposition (ABCD), a prompting strategy that decomposes questions into a series of true/false claims that can be used to verify which criteria of the input question an answer satisfies"
  - [section] "we propose a three-step prompt for ABCD, illustrated in Figure 2 (left). First, we decompose the question into a list of independent...claims"
- Break condition: The decomposition process fails when claims become too interdependent or when the LLM cannot maintain consistency across related claims

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-grained self-evaluation provides more insight than binary correct/incorrect assessment
- Mechanism: By calculating the proportion of claims an answer satisfies, the LLM can quantify how close an incorrect answer is to being correct and identify specific knowledge gaps
- Core assumption: The distribution of true/false claims for correct vs incorrect answers will be significantly different
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find that GPT-3.5 has some ability to determine to what extent its answer satisfies the criteria of the input question"
  - [section] "Using these claims, we perform fine-grained self-evaluation, illustrated in Figure 2...we use the same model to self-evaluate the proportion of ABCD claims the LLM believes its answer satisfies"
- Break condition: When LLM responses become self-inconsistent or when claim evaluation is ambiguous due to linguistic complexity

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Recursive decomposition can overcome false assumptions in claims
- Mechanism: When claims contain false assumptions, further decomposition into sub-claims can isolate and identify the problematic assumptions
- Core assumption: LLMs can recognize when claims are not fully decomposed and can break them down further
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find that some of the decomposed claims contain false assumptions, which are challenging for LLMs to address"
  - [section] "we believe the issue of false assumptions could be overcome by performing ABCD recursively until each claim is determined to be fully decomposed"
- Break condition: The decomposition process becomes circular or the claims become too granular to be meaningful

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: In-context learning with few-shot examples
  - Why needed here: The ABCD method relies on prompting GPT-3.5 with examples to learn how to decompose questions into claims
  - Quick check question: How many examples are typically needed to prompt GPT-3.5 for the ABCD method?
  - Answer: 5-7 examples per dataset (from section 3.1)

- Concept: True/false claim evaluation
  - Why needed here: The self-evaluation process requires the LLM to independently verify each claim as true or false
  - Quick check question: What temperature setting is used when the LLM evaluates claims?
  - Answer: Temperature of 0 (from section 3.2)

- Concept: Claim linking for multi-hop reasoning
  - Why needed here: Some questions require maintaining consistency across multiple related claims, necessitating entity linking
  - Quick check question: What additional step is performed after generating independent claims?
  - Answer: Identifying which claims discuss the same entities and including extra tags for said entities (from section 3.1)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Question → ABCD decomposition → Initial answer generation → Claim verification → Proportion calculation
- Critical path: The system follows a sequential flow from question input through decomposition, answer generation, and self-evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: The method trades computational efficiency for granular insight, requiring multiple LLM calls (decomposition, answer generation, claim verification) rather than a single evaluation
- Failure signatures: Inconsistent claim evaluations, tense mismatches, ambiguous claims, false assumptions, and reasoning failures
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test ABCD decomposition on a simple single-hop question to verify basic functionality
  2. Evaluate claim consistency by running the same claim verification multiple times
  3. Test the system on questions with obvious false assumptions to see if recursive decomposition is needed

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does ABCD performance vary across different types of complex questions beyond factual trivia, such as hypothetical scenarios or opinion-based queries?
- Basis in paper: Inferred - The paper notes that ABCD was tested on factual trivia questions and acknowledges it did not examine other types like TRUTHFUL QA or BOOL QA.
- Why unresolved: The study's scope was limited to factual trivia questions with short entity answers, leaving open the question of how well ABCD generalizes to questions requiring longer responses or yes/no answers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing ABCD on diverse QA datasets like TRUTHFUL QA (imitative falsehoods, longer responses) and BOOL QA (yes/no answers) would reveal its generalizability and limitations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of incorporating claim importance weighting in ABCD on the accuracy and reliability of fine-grained self-evaluation?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The authors mention that some claims may be more important than others when evaluating an answer and suggest that ranking claims by relevance, specificity, or difficulty could help the LLM reason more effectively.
- Why unresolved: The current implementation treats all claims equally, without considering their relative importance, which could lead to suboptimal self-evaluation performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing fine-grained self-evaluation with and without claim importance weighting would demonstrate the impact on accuracy and reliability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can ABCD be leveraged to improve factuality and truthfulness during the initial generation of answers, rather than just post-hoc self-evaluation?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The authors suggest that given GPT-3.5 recognizes its answers do not satisfy all claims, one promising direction is to optimize question answering on satisfying the ABCD claims themselves, potentially leading to uncertainty responses like "IDK" when all claims cannot be satisfied.
- Why unresolved: The current use of ABCD is limited to self-evaluation after answer generation, leaving open the potential for using it as a guiding mechanism during the generation process itself.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing a method that incorporates ABCD during answer generation, optimizing for claim satisfaction, would demonstrate its potential to improve factuality and truthfulness at the source.

## Limitations
- The method shows significant reliability limitations with systematic error patterns including self-consistency issues, tense handling problems, and reasoning failures
- Requires manual annotation of answer correctness and has not been tested on diverse question types beyond factual trivia
- Limited sample sizes (200-300 questions total across three datasets) may not generalize to broader domains

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core finding that ABCD can provide meaningful insights into LLM answer quality, as evidenced by statistically significant differences in claim satisfaction between correct and incorrect answers across all three datasets
- Medium confidence in the practical utility of the approach given the identified reliability issues and error patterns
- Low confidence in the scalability and generalizability of the method beyond the tested datasets and question types

## Next Checks
1. Implement and test the recursive decomposition approach to verify whether breaking down claims with false assumptions into sub-claims improves evaluation accuracy and consistency

2. Conduct systematic testing of self-consistency by running the same claim verification prompts multiple times on identical inputs to measure variability and identify conditions that trigger inconsistent responses

3. Expand evaluation to additional datasets with different question types and domains, including questions requiring temporal reasoning, counterfactual scenarios, and multi-hop reasoning with complex entity relationships, to assess generalizability
