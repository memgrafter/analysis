# Multi-Aspect Explainable Inductive Relation Prediction by Sentence Transformer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01664
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01664
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: KRST achieves best performance in 4/6 transductive/inductive test cases and 11/12 few-shot cases

## Executive Summary
KRST introduces a sentence transformer-based approach for inductive relation prediction in knowledge graphs that provides multi-aspect explainable predictions. The method filters unreliable paths using coverage and confidence metrics before training, then encodes reliable paths and triplets into embeddings for similarity-based prediction. KRST outperforms state-of-the-art models on WN18RR, FB15k-237, and NELL-995 datasets, particularly excelling in few-shot learning scenarios where it achieves the highest scores in 11 out of 12 cases.

## Method Summary
KRST extracts logical reasoning paths from knowledge graphs, filters them using relation path coverage and confidence metrics, then converts both triplets and paths into semicolon-separated sentences using entity descriptions. These sentences are encoded with a sentence transformer (all-mpnet-base-v2) into embeddings, which are compared using cosine similarity. The model is trained using cosine embedding loss and provides multi-aspect explanations through K-means clustering of path embeddings. KRST is evaluated on transductive and inductive relation prediction tasks using MRR and Hit@1 metrics across three benchmark datasets.

## Key Results
- KRST achieves highest performance in 4 out of 6 transductive and inductive test cases
- KRST obtains the best scores in 11 out of 12 few-shot learning scenarios
- KRST outperforms BERT-based models, demonstrating the effectiveness of sentence transformers for this task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Relation path filtering using coverage and confidence metrics improves model performance by reducing noise from unreliable paths.
- Mechanism: The model calculates two metrics—relation path coverage (ratio of paths with same source/destination and length) and relation path confidence (ratio over all entities satisfying target relation paths)—to filter out unreliable paths before training. Paths with low coverage or confidence are excluded, ensuring only semantically meaningful paths are used for training.
- Core assumption: Unreliable paths negatively impact model training and lead to overfitting on irrelevant patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "introduce the concepts of relation path coverage and relation path conﬁdence to ﬁlter out unreliable paths prior to model training to elevate the model performance."
  - [section] "We propose two novel path extraction metrics named relation path coverage and relation path conﬁdence, and a novel path-based model named KRST."
- Break condition: If the metrics fail to capture what constitutes a reliable path, the filtering could exclude useful paths or retain noisy ones, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using sentence transformer instead of BERT for sequence classification enables more flexible and effective path encoding.
- Mechanism: Sentence transformer encodes paths and triplets separately into embeddings, allowing direct cosine similarity comparison between them. This contrasts with BERT, which requires pairwise input and implicit comparison.
- Core assumption: Separate encoding with cosine similarity is more effective for measuring semantic similarity between paths and triplets than BERT's sequence classification approach.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we adopt sentence transformer in KRST to encode triplets and paths into embeddings, which allows us to explicitly compare between embeddings and cluster paths w.r.t various aspects."
  - [section] "Compared with the commonly applied BERT for sequence classiﬁcation model, the sentence transformer adopting cosine similarity achieves a higher performance in multiple tasks."
- Break condition: If the sentence transformer fails to capture the semantic relationships between paths and triplets effectively, the similarity scores would be unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Clustering path embeddings enables multi-aspect explanations by grouping paths with similar semantics.
- Mechanism: After encoding paths into embeddings, K-means clustering groups paths with similar semantic content. Each cluster represents a different explanatory perspective for the target triplet.
- Core assumption: Paths with similar semantics will have similar embeddings, and these groups will correspond to meaningful explanatory aspects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "KRST is designed to encode the extracted reliable paths in KGs, allowing us to properly cluster paths and provide multi-aspect explanations."
  - [section] "By grouping them into different clusters, we could provide a multi-aspect comprehensive explanation."
- Break condition: If the embeddings don't capture the semantic relationships well, clustering will group unrelated paths together or separate related ones.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Path extraction (with filtering) → Sentence formation → Embedding generation (KRST) → Similarity scoring → Clustering for explanation
- Critical path: Path extraction → Sentence formation → Embedding generation → Similarity scoring (for predictions)
- Design tradeoffs: Sentence transformer vs BERT (flexibility vs. established framework), path filtering (accuracy vs. coverage), clustering (explainability vs. complexity)
- Failure signatures: Poor path filtering leads to noisy training data; inadequate sentence formation loses semantic information; clustering produces meaningless groups
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify path filtering metrics work as expected by testing coverage/confidence calculations on known good/bad paths
  2. Validate sentence formation preserves semantic relationships by checking embedding similarity between related paths
  3. Test clustering quality by manually inspecting whether clusters group semantically similar paths together

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does KRST's performance compare when using different pre-trained language models beyond BERT and sentence transformers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions KRST uses sentence transformers and notes their superior performance compared to BERT in certain tasks, but does not explore other pre-trained models
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates KRST with sentence transformers and does not conduct experiments with other pre-trained language models like GPT or RoBERTa
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing KRST's performance across multiple pre-trained language models on the same datasets

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of paths (M) to extract per triplet for maximizing KRST's performance across different datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper sets M=3 as a fixed hyperparameter but acknowledges this is a parameter that could affect performance
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a fixed value of M=3 without exploring how different values affect performance across various datasets and settings
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying M across a range of values (e.g., 1-10) for each dataset to identify optimal settings

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does KRST's multi-aspect explanation capability scale with increasingly complex knowledge graphs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates multi-aspect explanations on three datasets but does not address scalability to larger, more complex KGs
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to relatively small datasets (WN18RR, FB15k-237, NELL-995), and the paper does not discuss performance on larger KGs
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing KRST on larger, more complex knowledge graphs and evaluating both explanation quality and computational efficiency

## Limitations
- The filtering mechanism's effectiveness depends heavily on chosen thresholds for coverage and confidence metrics, but sensitivity analysis is lacking
- The sentence transformer approach lacks ablation studies comparing it against other embedding methods
- Clustering-based explanations haven't been validated for semantic coherence by human evaluators

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: KRST's superior performance on the six transductive and inductive test cases (4/6 wins) is well-supported by the experimental results presented.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim of best performance across all 12 few-shot cases needs verification, as the paper only reports aggregate statistics without per-case breakdowns.
- **Medium Confidence**: The explanation quality from clustering is theoretically sound but lacks empirical validation of whether clusters actually represent meaningful semantic categories.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies to determine the optimal thresholds for relation path coverage and confidence metrics across different dataset characteristics.
2. Perform human evaluation studies to verify that clustered paths actually represent coherent and meaningful explanatory aspects for the target relations.
3. Test KRST's generalization to entirely new knowledge graphs beyond the three datasets used in the paper to validate its inductive capabilities.
