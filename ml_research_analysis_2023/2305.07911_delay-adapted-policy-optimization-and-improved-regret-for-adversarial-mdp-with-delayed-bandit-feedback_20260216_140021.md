# Delay-Adapted Policy Optimization and Improved Regret for Adversarial MDP with Delayed Bandit Feedback

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07911
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07911
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes Delay-Adapted Policy Optimization (DAPO) for adversarial MDPs with delayed bandit feedback, achieving near-optimal regret of $\tilde{O}(H^2\sqrt{SAK} + H^3\sqrt{D})$ in tabular settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning in adversarial Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with delayed bandit feedback. The proposed Delay-Adapted Policy Optimization (DAPO) algorithm introduces a novel delay-adapted ratio that measures local policy changes during delays, reducing estimator variance while controlling bias. DAPO achieves near-optimal regret bounds in both tabular and linear function approximation settings, and extends naturally to deep reinforcement learning through a modified PPO objective. The method demonstrates that simple local policy updates can effectively handle delays, contrasting with more complex global optimization approaches.

## Method Summary
DAPO works by computing a delay-adapted ratio that cancels out the largest possible mismatch between the policy at time k and the policy at time k+dk when feedback arrives. This ratio is used to modify the standard importance-sampling estimator for Q-function values. The algorithm computes local bonuses from visitation statistics and propagates them through standard Bellman equations to obtain global bonuses. For policy improvement, DAPO uses exponential weights in the tabular case and a PPO-style objective with delay-adapted ratios in the deep RL case. The method handles delayed feedback by buffering observations and properly aligning them with the correct policy versions during estimation.

## Key Results
- DAPO achieves near-optimal regret of $\tilde{O}(H^2\sqrt{SAK} + H^3\sqrt{D})$ for tabular MDPs, matching state-of-the-art when delay is dominant
- First sub-linear regret algorithm for adversarial MDPs with delayed feedback under linear function approximation
- DAPPO outperforms delayed PPO baselines on MuJoCo environments in deep RL settings
- Demonstrates that local policy updates with delay adaptation can effectively handle delays without requiring global optimization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Delay-adapted ratio reduces estimator variance while controlling bias
- Mechanism: Multiplies standard importance-sampling estimator by ratio rk_h(s,a) = πk_h(a|s) / max{πk_h(a|s), πk+dk_h(a|s)}, which cancels out the largest possible mismatch between πk and πk+dk
- Core assumption: The ratio remains bounded and preserves unbiasedness up to a controllable bias term

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Bellman equation computation of global bonus simplifies analysis and enables function approximation
- Mechanism: Instead of computing delayed bonuses through complex dilated Bellman equations, computes local bonuses and uses standard Bellman equations to obtain global bonus
- Core assumption: Local bonuses can be constructed to provide sufficient exploration and stability when propagated through standard Bellman equations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal horizon dependence for policy optimization algorithms under delayed bandit feedback in adversarial MDPs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "In the tabular case, it still remains unclear what is the optimal dependency under delayed feedback in terms of the horizon H."
- Why unresolved: The paper provides near-optimal regret bounds but cannot determine if the H² dependence in their DAPO algorithm is optimal or if it can be improved to match non-delayed bounds.
- What evidence would resolve it: A lower bound proof showing Ω(H²√SAK + H√D) for delayed feedback, or an upper bound algorithm achieving Ω(H√SAK + H√D) would definitively answer this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can DAPO be extended to achieve sub-linear regret in adversarial MDPs with delayed feedback without requiring a simulator?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The Linear-Q DAPO algorithm requires a simulator for the Matrix Geometric Resampling procedure, and the authors note "under the stronger assumption that the MDP is linear... we can replace this procedure with the OLSPE procedure of Sherman et al. (2023) which would make our algorithm fully efficient while achieving the same regret guarantee."
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a solution for the linear MDP case with a simulator, leaving the general case open.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof that DAPO can achieve sub-linear regret in adversarial MDPs with delayed feedback without any simulator access, or a lower bound showing this is impossible.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of delay length on deep reinforcement learning performance, and can delay-adapted methods fully mitigate this degradation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe "delays cause significant drop in performance" in their MuJoCo experiments and note "Our experiment demonstrate the potential that delay-adaptation methods have for deep RL applications. However, a much more thorough empirical study needs to be done in order to fully understand the implications of these methods on deep RL."
- Why unresolved: The paper provides preliminary experiments showing performance degradation with delays, but the relationship between delay length and performance degradation is not fully characterized, nor is it clear if delay-adapted methods can completely eliminate this degradation.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive empirical study varying delay lengths across multiple environments, showing the performance degradation curve and whether DAPPO (or similar methods) can maintain performance close to the non-delayed baseline across all delay lengths.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation in high-dimensional settings beyond basic MuJoCo experiments
- Computational overhead in high-dimensional function approximation settings not fully characterized
- Performance sensitivity to delay length and policy change rates not thoroughly explored

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Delay-adapted ratio): High confidence - rigorous mathematical formulation with strong theoretical grounding
- Mechanism 2 (Bellman bonus computation): Medium confidence - theoretically sound but practical effectiveness in high dimensions uncertain
- Mechanism 3 (Deep RL extension): Low confidence - promising approach but limited experimental details and ablation studies
