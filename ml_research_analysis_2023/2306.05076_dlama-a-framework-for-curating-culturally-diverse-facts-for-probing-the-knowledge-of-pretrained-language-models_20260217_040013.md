# DLAMA: A Framework for Curating Culturally Diverse Facts for Probing the Knowledge of Pretrained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.05076
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05076
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Key outcome**: DLAMA framework addresses cultural bias in multilingual benchmarks by curating culturally diverse facts from Wikidata, enabling balanced evaluation of PLMs across contrasting cultures.

## Executive Summary
DLAMA is a novel framework designed to address the cultural bias present in existing multilingual benchmarks for probing factual knowledge in pretrained language models. The authors identify that benchmarks like mLAMA are heavily skewed toward Western facts, which can unfairly disadvantage non-English models. DLAMA enables the creation of balanced, culturally diverse benchmarks by curating facts from Wikidata across contrasting cultures. The framework was used to create DLAMA-v1, which contains 78,259 triples across 20 relation predicates, representing Arab-Western, Asian-Western, and South American-Western cultural pairs. Experiments with mBERT and monolingual models demonstrate that models perform better on culturally proximate facts, validating the framework's effectiveness in revealing cultural biases.

## Method Summary
The DLAMA framework operates through a systematic approach to curating culturally diverse facts from Wikidata. It begins by identifying contrasting cultures using demographic data, then selects relevant relation predicates that capture culturally important knowledge. For each culture pair, the framework filters candidate triples using Wikidata constraints to ensure factual consistency and relevance. The selection process considers cultural proximity metrics and demographic statistics to create balanced benchmark subsets. Human verification is employed to validate cultural relevance, though the authors acknowledge this may introduce additional biases. The framework generates triples in the format (subject, relation, object) suitable for LAMA-style probing tasks.

## Key Results
- DLAMA-v1 contains 78,259 triples across 20 relation predicates representing Arab-Western, Asian-Western, and South American-Western cultural pairs
- mBERT performs better on Western facts compared to non-Western facts, while monolingual Arabic and Korean models excel on their culturally proximate facts
- Western facts dominate DLAMA-v1 (53.2%), showing significant but reduced Western bias compared to mLAMA (95.7% Western facts)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DLAMA works by systematically identifying and incorporating facts from underrepresented cultures into knowledge probing benchmarks. The framework leverages Wikidata's structured knowledge base and applies filtering constraints to ensure factual consistency across cultures. By using demographic data and cultural proximity metrics, DLAMA can create balanced test sets that reveal model biases toward specific cultural knowledge domains. The approach addresses the fundamental problem that most web data and existing benchmarks are Western-centric, causing models trained on such data to perform poorly on non-Western cultural facts.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Cultural Pair Selection -> Relation Predicate Filtering -> Triple Extraction -> Constraint Validation -> Human Verification -> Benchmark Generation

**Critical Path**: The framework's critical path involves identifying contrasting cultures, selecting appropriate relation predicates, extracting candidate triples from Wikidata, applying consistency constraints, and performing human verification. Each step builds upon the previous one to ensure cultural diversity and factual accuracy.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The framework balances between computational efficiency (using automated filtering) and cultural accuracy (requiring human verification). It also trades off between comprehensive coverage (1,584 possible predicates) and focused representation (20 selected predicates) to maintain manageable benchmark sizes.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Western bias persists if demographic data or cultural proximity metrics are skewed
- Factual inconsistencies arise when Wikidata constraints are too permissive or too restrictive
- Cultural misrepresentation occurs if human annotators lack cultural expertise or introduce their own biases

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper acknowledges that human verification of cultural relevance may introduce biases from the annotators themselves. It also questions whether the selection of 20 relation predicates from 1,584 possible predicates adequately represents the full spectrum of culturally important knowledge domains.

## Limitations
- DLAMA-v1 still shows significant Western dominance (53.2% of triples), limiting truly balanced cross-cultural evaluation
- Selection of only 20 relation predicates from 1,584 possible Wikidata relations may bias which cultural knowledge domains are represented
- Human verification process, while necessary, may introduce cultural biases from the annotators themselves

## Confidence
High confidence: The framework's methodology for identifying contrasting cultures and filtering candidate triples using relation predicates and Wikidata constraints is clearly described and technically sound.

Medium confidence: The experimental results showing performance differences between mBERT and monolingual models on culturally proximate facts are convincing, though the interpretation that these differences directly reflect cultural bias requires some assumptions about model training data and cultural proximity metrics.

Medium confidence: The claim that DLAMA-v1 represents a significant improvement in cultural diversity over mLAMA is supported by the data, but the Western-centric skew remains substantial enough to limit the framework's effectiveness for truly balanced cross-cultural evaluation.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a comprehensive cultural relevance assessment by recruiting native speakers from each represented culture to evaluate whether the facts genuinely reflect culturally important knowledge, not just demographic statistics.

2. Perform an analysis of fact distribution across the 20 relation predicates to identify which domains show the strongest Western bias and develop strategies for expanding representation in underrepresented knowledge areas.

3. Implement a longitudinal study tracking how model performance on culturally diverse facts evolves as models are trained on increasingly diverse web data, to validate whether DLAMA can effectively measure progress in reducing cultural bias.
