# Explaining SAT Solving Using Causal Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06294
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06294
- Reference count: 40
- This work is the first to apply causal reasoning to analyze the behavior of SAT solvers, introducing CausalSAT framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CausalSAT, a novel framework that applies causal reasoning to analyze SAT solver behavior by learning causal graphs from execution data and computing causal effects to answer queries about clause utility. The approach verifies four established hypotheses about clause utility (low LBD clauses have higher utility, small clauses have greater utility, high LBD clauses experience rapid utility decay, and LBD impact exceeds size impact) and answers three novel questions about branching and restart heuristics. The framework demonstrates that causal reasoning achieves comparable prediction error to traditional machine learning models while providing better correlation with clause utility than individual features alone.

## Method Summary
The CausalSAT framework operates through several key steps: first, it generates observational data by tracing SAT solver execution and labeling clause utility using DRAT proofs; second, it learns a causal graph structure using hill-climbing algorithm with prior knowledge constraints; third, it formulates causal queries about solver behavior; fourth, it identifies backdoor sets to deconfound causal effects; and finally, it estimates causal effects using linear regression on the identified backdoor sets. The approach validates results through refutation tests and demonstrates effectiveness across multiple benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Verified four hypotheses about clause utility: low LBD clauses have higher utility, small clauses have greater utility, high LBD clauses experience rapid utility drop over time, and LBD has greater impact than size
- Answered three novel questions: Maple branching heuristic leads to greater clause utility than VSIDS, Luby restart heuristic achieves highest utility, and propagation has greatest impact on utility among non-size/LBD/activity factors
- CausalSAT achieves comparable prediction error to linear models and decision trees while exhibiting higher Pearson correlation with clause utility than any individual variable

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Causal reasoning framework accurately identifies backdoor variables and computes unbiased causal effects.
- Mechanism: Uses causal graph structure learned from observational data combined with prior knowledge constraints to identify minimal backdoor sets. Applies linear regression on these sets to estimate causal effects without introducing confounding bias.
- Core assumption: The causal relationships among SAT solver components can be represented as a directed acyclic graph, and the minimal backdoor sets identified are sufficient to deconfound the causal effect estimates.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "learns a structured graph representing the causal relationships between the components of a SAT solver"
  - [section] "We use the hill-climbing algorithm [44] in our approach to learn a causal graph for its practical performance"
- Break condition: If the true causal graph contains hidden confounders not captured in the observed variables, or if the hill-climbing algorithm converges to a local optimum that misses critical causal edges.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Causal effects computed on SAT solver behavior validate known empirical findings and reveal new insights about solver design choices.
- Mechanism: Translates domain-specific questions about clause utility into formal causal queries (ATE, CATE, ACATE), computes estimates using identified backdoor sets, and validates results through refutation tests.
- Core assumption: The causal model captures sufficient structure to answer queries about solver behavior, and the linear regression assumptions hold for the relationships being measured.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "quantitatively verify hypotheses previously regarded as 'rules of thumb' or empirical findings"
  - [section] "We use CausalSAT to answer the questions in Table 2 by calculating the causal effects for the queries"
- Break condition: If the causal relationships are non-linear or if the assumptions underlying the identification strategies (like backdoor criterion) are violated by the actual solver dynamics.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Causal reasoning provides better correlation with clause utility than individual features alone.
- Mechanism: The causal model incorporates all relevant features and their causal relationships, allowing it to capture the combined effect on clause utility better than any single variable can.
- Core assumption: The joint distribution of features and their causal relationships contains predictive information about clause utility that individual variables miss.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "CausalSAT's prediction has a higher correlation with Utility than any individual variable"
  - [section] "CausalSAT fits the dataset, attaining a comparable loss to both linear models and decision trees"
- Break condition: If the causal graph is misspecified or if the relationships between features and utility are dominated by non-causal correlations that the causal approach doesn't capture.

  - Why needed here: Identifying which variables to control for when estimating causal effects is critical to avoid biased estimates of how solver components affect clause utility.

  - Why needed here: The causal graph must be learned from data since prior knowledge is incomplete, requiring algorithms that can discover causal structure from observational data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data Generation -> Structure Learning -> Query Formulation -> Identification -> Estimation -> Refutation

- Critical path: Data Generation → Structure Learning → Query Formulation → Identification → Estimation → Refutation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Hill-climbing vs. PC Stable: Hill-climbing always returns directed graphs but may get stuck in local optima; PC Stable finds Markov equivalence classes but may leave edges undirected
  - Linear regression vs. other estimators: Linear regression is interpretable and works well for ATE under linearity, but may miss non-linear effects
  - Prior knowledge constraints: Helps guide structure learning but may introduce bias if incorrect

- Failure signatures:
  - Refutation tests fail: Suggests estimation method is unreliable or model is misspecified
  - Low correlation with utility: Indicates causal graph doesn't capture important relationships
  - High MSE compared to baseline models: Suggests poor fit to data

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run on a small subset of instances with known heuristics to verify basic functionality and check if learned graph matches expected structure
  2. Test refutation tests by adding random common causes and verifying estimates change as expected
  3. Compare ATE estimates for LBD on utility with and without controlling for backdoor variables to demonstrate the importance of proper identification

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of branching heuristic affect clause utility beyond the specific comparison between VSIDS and Maple?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares Maple to VSIDS but doesn't explore other branching heuristics like CHB or LRB.
- Why unresolved: The study only examined two branching heuristics, leaving open questions about other popular heuristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Causal analysis of clause utility with additional branching heuristics like CHB (conflict history based) and LRB (learning rate based).

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the precise mechanism by which LBD affects clause utility over time, and at what threshold does the rapid drop occur?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper confirms high-LBD clauses experience rapid utility drop but doesn't quantify the exact threshold or mechanism.
- Why unresolved: The study only establishes the phenomenon exists without determining specific thresholds or causal pathways.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed causal analysis identifying the exact LBD threshold where utility drop accelerates and the intermediate variables in this causal pathway.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does clause utility interact with solver-specific optimizations like phase saving or polarity heuristics?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on clause management features but doesn't examine interactions with phase selection heuristics.
- Why unresolved: The causal graph and analysis excluded phase-related variables and their potential effects on clause utility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extended causal analysis incorporating phase saving and polarity heuristics as potential causes of clause utility.

## Limitations
- The hill-climbing algorithm for structure learning may converge to local optima rather than global structure
- Linear regression assumptions may not capture non-linear relationships in solver dynamics
- Method requires substantial observational data and DRAT proofs for ground truth, limiting applicability to problems with existing proofs

## Confidence

- High confidence: The framework can learn causal graphs from solver execution data and compute causal effects using identified backdoor sets.
- Medium confidence: The four verified hypotheses align with established empirical findings about clause utility.
- Medium confidence: Causal reasoning achieves better correlation with clause utility than individual features alone.
