# Zero-shot performance of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) in 2D medical imaging: A comprehensive evaluation and practical guidelines

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00109
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00109
- Reference count: 0
- SAM's zero-shot performance on 2D medical images is comparable to and sometimes superior to state-of-the-art methods

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the zero-shot performance of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) on 2D medical images across six diverse datasets spanning four imaging modalities. The study tests eight prompting strategies including central points, random points, and bounding boxes. Results demonstrate that SAM achieves impressive segmentation performance, with the bounding box strategy consistently outperforming other methods. The largest model (ViT-H) achieved a 0.973 Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) for femur segmentation and 0.889 DSC for ilios segmentation in hip joint X-rays, while also outperforming state-of-the-art by approximately 5% on ultrasound data.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates SAM's zero-shot performance on six medical imaging datasets (ISIC 2018, HAM10000, Montgomery-Shenzhen CXR, HJXR, CVC-ClinicDB, and BUSI) using eight prompting strategies. The evaluation compares three SAM model sizes (ViT-B, ViT-L, ViT-H) and measures performance using DSC. Datasets were preprocessed with normalization and format conversion, then segmented using central points, random points (with erosion), and bounding boxes with 5-20% variation. The study tested automatic vs. manual physician selection of the best prediction from SAM's three-output strategy.

## Key Results
- Bounding box strategy consistently outperformed point-based methods across all datasets
- SAM achieved 0.973 DSC for femur segmentation and 0.889 DSC for ilios segmentation in hip joint X-rays
- SAM outperformed state-of-the-art by approximately 5% on BUSI ultrasound dataset
- Model size scaling (ViT-B → ViT-H) provided marginal improvements at significantly higher computational cost

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bounding box prompts outperform point-based strategies even with minor annotation errors
- Mechanism: Bounding boxes provide both positive (inside region) and negative (outside region) spatial constraints, giving the model more explicit guidance than isolated points which only indicate inclusion
- Core assumption: SAM's attention mechanisms can effectively utilize the spatial context provided by bounding boxes to improve segmentation accuracy
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] states "The bounding box strategy consistently outperformed other methods, even with minor errors" and [section] shows BBS5 and BBS10 outperforming point methods
- Break Condition: This mechanism breaks down when bounding boxes are drawn far from actual object boundaries (e.g., BBS20 with 20% variation), as negative spatial information becomes misleading

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SAM's zero-shot performance is comparable to and sometimes superior to state-of-the-art models fine-tuned for specific medical imaging tasks
- Mechanism: SAM's massive training on SA-1B dataset (11 million images, 1 billion masks) provides generalizable visual representations that transfer effectively to medical domains despite minimal direct medical training data
- Core assumption: Visual features learned from natural images are sufficiently transferable to medical imaging modalities with similar structural patterns
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] claims "SAM's zero-shot performance is not only comparable to, but in certain cases, surpasses the current state-of-the-art" and [section] demonstrates SAM outperforming SOTA by ~5% on BUSI dataset
- Break Condition: This mechanism fails when medical images have significantly different visual characteristics than natural images, such as ultrasound's low signal-to-noise ratio or specific attenuation patterns in X-rays

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The three-prediction output strategy with physician selection provides a practical balance between automation and expert oversight
- Mechanism: SAM generates multiple interpretations of ambiguous prompts, allowing physicians to choose the best prediction rather than requiring perfect initial annotation
- Core assumption: Physicians can reliably identify the correct segmentation from among the three predictions in practical settings
- Evidence anchors: [section] states "we evaluated the highest DSC per image, regardless of its position among the three predictions" showing ~1% improvement over automatic selection
- Break Condition: This mechanism breaks when the three predictions are too similar or all incorrect, providing no meaningful choice for the physician

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image loading -> Preprocessing (normalization, format conversion) -> Prompt processor (converts CP, RP, BB to SAM format) -> SAM core (ViT-based with three outputs) -> Post-processing (mask merging, format conversion) -> Evaluation module (DSC calculation)

- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess image/mask pairs
  2. Generate prompts based on strategy (CP, RP, BB, etc.)
  3. Run SAM inference with prompts
  4. Select best prediction from three outputs
  5. Calculate DSC against ground truth
  6. Aggregate results across dataset

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. performance: ViT-B offers good performance with lower memory requirements, while ViT-H provides marginal improvements at significantly higher computational cost
  - Prompt precision vs. usability: Bounding boxes are easier to draw but may be less precise than multiple point selections
  - Three predictions vs. single output: Multiple predictions provide flexibility but require additional physician time to select

- Failure signatures:
  - Bounding boxes drawn too loosely (BBS20) causing negative space to include target regions
  - Point prompts near edges causing ambiguous segmentations due to erosion preprocessing
  - Poor ground truth masks leading to misleadingly low DSC scores despite accurate SAM predictions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test ViT-B model with central point (CP) and bounding box (BB) strategies on a small subset of CXR dataset to verify basic functionality
  2. Run all eight prompt strategies on a single representative image from each dataset to understand strategy differences
  3. Compare automatic best-prediction selection vs. manual physician selection on 10 images to validate the ~1% improvement claim

## Limitations
- Study did not evaluate SAM's performance on 3D medical imaging data, representing a significant gap for volumetric imaging in clinical practice
- Computational requirements for larger SAM models may limit practical deployment in resource-constrained clinical settings
- Results are based on curated, high-quality datasets that may not reflect real-world clinical images with artifacts and varying acquisition protocols

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: SAM's bounding box strategy consistently outperforming point-based methods across all datasets
- **Medium Confidence**: SAM's zero-shot performance being "comparable or superior" to state-of-the-art, requiring broader validation
- **Low Confidence**: The practical guideline's effectiveness in actual clinical workflows, as physician selection validation is incomplete
