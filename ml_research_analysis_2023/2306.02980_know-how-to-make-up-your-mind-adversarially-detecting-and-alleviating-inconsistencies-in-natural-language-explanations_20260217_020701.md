# KNOW How to Make Up Your Mind! Adversarially Detecting and Alleviating Inconsistencies in Natural Language Explanations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02980
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02980
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 9
- Key outcome: eKnowIA attack detects more inconsistencies than template-based approaches with significantly less computation time, and KNOW mitigation reduces inconsistencies by 58% on average.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces eKnowIA, an adversarial attack that detects inconsistent natural language explanations (NLEs) by leveraging external knowledge bases like ConceptNet and applying systematic rules such as negation handling, antonym replacement, and unrelated noun replacement. Unlike prior template-based approaches, eKnowIA generates more diverse and realistic inconsistencies. The authors also propose KNOW, a mitigation method that grounds models with relevant background knowledge to reduce inconsistencies. Experiments show eKnowIA detects significantly more inconsistencies with less computation time, and KNOW reduces inconsistencies by 58% while preserving NLE quality.

## Method Summary
The paper presents eKnowIA, an adversarial attack that detects inconsistent NLEs by replacing adjectives/adverbs with antonyms and nouns with unrelated concepts from ConceptNet. The attack systematically generates logically contradictory statements and checks for contradictions using ConceptNet relationships. To mitigate inconsistencies, the authors propose KNOW, which extracts relevant knowledge triplets from ConceptNet and injects them into the input to provide additional context. The method is evaluated on e-SNLI (NLI task) and Cos-E (CQA task) datasets using models like NILE, CAGE, and WT5-base.

## Key Results
- eKnowIA achieves 12.88% success rate on e-SNLI vs 2.19% for previous eIA attack, with 40 minutes vs 10 days computation time
- KNOW reduces inconsistencies by 58% on average while maintaining explanation quality (e-ViL score)
- Higher-quality NLEs (measured by e-ViL) do not guarantee fewer inconsistencies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Using ConceptNet to replace words with antonyms or unrelated nouns creates more natural adversarial examples than template-based approaches. The attack leverages external knowledge to systematically generate logically contradictory statements by replacing adjectives/adverbs with antonyms and nouns with unrelated concepts, producing more diverse and realistic inconsistencies.

### Mechanism 2
Grounding models with relevant background knowledge from ConceptNet reduces the number of inconsistent NLEs. The KNOW method extracts relevant knowledge triplets for each instance and injects them into the input, providing additional context that helps the model generate more consistent explanations.

### Mechanism 3
Higher-quality NLEs do not necessarily imply fewer inconsistencies. The attack reveals that models with better automatic evaluation metrics (e-ViL scores) can still generate many inconsistent explanations, suggesting that plausibility and consistency are separate dimensions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: eKnowIA attack module -> ConceptNet knowledge base integration -> POS tagger -> Reverse explainer training module -> KNOW mitigation module -> Automatic evaluation metrics -> Human evaluation framework

- Critical path: 1) Load test dataset 2) Apply eKnowIA rules to generate inconsistent candidates 3) Check for logical contradictions using ConceptNet 4) Extract relevant knowledge triplets 5) Inject knowledge into input 6) Evaluate consistency reduction and quality preservation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Speed vs. coverage: Template-based approaches are faster but less general; knowledge-based approaches are slower but more comprehensive
  - Knowledge quality vs. noise: More knowledge can help but may introduce irrelevant information
  - Human effort vs. automation: Manual templates require effort but may be more accurate; automated approaches are scalable but may have errors

- Failure signatures:
  - Low success rate on eKnowIA: Attack rules not generating meaningful contradictions
  - High rejection rate in human evaluation: Adversarial examples too unnatural
  - KNOW increases inconsistencies: Wrong knowledge triplets being selected or injected
  - Quality degradation with KNOW: Knowledge injection disrupting the model's generation process

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run eKnowIA on a small subset of e-SNLI with manual verification of generated inconsistencies
  2. Test KNOW mitigation on one model/task pair with before/after consistency comparison
  3. Compare eKnowIA vs eIA on WT5-base using the same evaluation metrics and timing

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does the effectiveness of eKnowIA generalize to languages with complex morphology? The paper only tests eKnowIA on English datasets and acknowledges potential limitations for morphologically complex languages without empirical validation.

### Open Question 2
How does the choice of knowledge base affect the performance of both eKnowIA and the KNOW mitigation method? The paper uses ConceptNet without exploring alternative knowledge bases or quantifying the impact of knowledge base quality on results.

### Open Question 3
What is the relationship between the size of the knowledge base and the effectiveness of the KNOW method in reducing inconsistencies? The paper does not investigate how varying the knowledge base size or coverage affects the consistency improvements achieved by KNOW.

## Limitations

- ConceptNet quality and coverage limitations affect eKnowIA's effectiveness, particularly for domain-specific terminology
- Human evaluation reliability concerns due to low inter-annotator agreement without quality control
- Knowledge grounding scalability challenges due to computational overhead in extracting relevant knowledge triplets

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: eKnowIA detects more inconsistencies than template-based approaches; KNOW reduces inconsistencies by 58% while maintaining quality; WT5-base with knowledge grounding outperforms other models in consistency metrics.

**Medium Confidence**: Higher-quality NLEs don't guarantee fewer inconsistencies; eKnowIA generates more diverse adversarial examples than template-based approaches.

**Low Confidence**: Generalizability of eKnowIA to other knowledge bases; long-term stability of KNOW's improvements across model updates and different datasets.

## Next Checks

1. Knowledge Triplet Filtering Validation: Re-run eKnowIA on a small validation set with different filtering criteria for ConceptNet triplets to determine sensitivity to knowledge base quality.

2. Human Evaluation Quality Control: Conduct controlled human evaluation experiment with multiple annotators rating the same set of adversarial examples, comparing agreement rates with and without trusted example filtering.

3. Cross-Dataset Generalization Test: Apply eKnowIA and KNOW to a third, unseen dataset (e.g., e-SNLI-VE) to test whether improvements transfer beyond original evaluation sets.
