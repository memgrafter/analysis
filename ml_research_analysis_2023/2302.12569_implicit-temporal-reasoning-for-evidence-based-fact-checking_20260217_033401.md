# Implicit Temporal Reasoning for Evidence-Based Fact-Checking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12569
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12569
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Implicit temporal reasoning substantially improves fact-checking model performance by grounding claims and evidence on shared timelines

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a novel approach to improve automated fact-checking by incorporating implicit temporal reasoning. The method grounds claims and evidence on shared timelines using publication dates and in-text time expressions, then integrates this temporal information into fact-checking models. The approach demonstrates substantial performance improvements over baseline models, showing that temporal reasoning is crucial for determining the relevance and supporting or refuting character of evidence documents. The research validates that multi-level temporal reasoning—at both document and content levels—is appropriate for automated fact-checking tasks.

## Method Summary
The method extracts publication dates from claims and evidence documents, then uses HeidelTime to normalize time expressions found in the text. Claims and evidence are grounded on shared timelines by computing distances between publication dates and time expressions, which are then discretized into discrete time buckets. These temporal representations are integrated into fact-checking models through local integration (before text encoding) or global integration (after text encoding). The approach is tested with both RNN-based (BiLSTM) and Transformer-based (DistilRoBERTa) architectures, comparing document-level grounding (based on publication dates) with content-level grounding (based on in-text time expressions).

## Key Results
- Implicit temporal reasoning substantially improves Micro F1 and Macro F1 scores on the MultiFC fact-checking dataset
- Global integration of temporal information outperforms local integration for both document-level and content-level reasoning
- The approach demonstrates that multi-level temporal reasoning is appropriate for automated fact-checking
- Time bucket construction quality significantly influences model performance in determining evidence relevance and support/refutation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Grounding claims and evidence on shared timelines improves fact-checking model performance by enabling temporal reasoning. The model learns implicit temporal relations by mapping claims and evidence to discrete time buckets based on publication dates and in-text time expressions. These time buckets serve as additional features that help the model determine relevance and support/refutation. Core assumption: Time information provides useful signals for distinguishing between supporting and refuting evidence.

### Mechanism 2
Local integration of time embeddings before text encoding improves content-level reasoning. Time embeddings are prepended to word embeddings, with special handling for predicates and time expressions. The model learns which tokens correspond to temporal information and weights them appropriately. Core assumption: The position of time expressions relative to predicates carries meaningful temporal information.

### Mechanism 3
Global integration of time embeddings after text encoding allows for flexible weighting of temporal information. Time embeddings are projected to the same dimension as text representations and combined using weighted sums. The model learns optimal weights through training. Core assumption: The model can learn when to prioritize text versus temporal information for different types of claims.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Time expression normalization using HeidelTime
  - Why needed here: Claims and evidence contain various temporal expressions that need to be standardized to compute distances from publication dates
  - Quick check question: What happens if a time expression cannot be normalized to a specific date?

- Concept: Document-level vs. content-level grounding
  - Why needed here: Document-level grounding captures when evidence was published relative to the claim, while content-level grounding captures when events mentioned in the text occurred
  - Quick check question: How does the model distinguish between evidence published before the claim versus evidence discussing past events?

- Concept: Integrated gradients for attribution analysis
  - Why needed here: To understand how much the model relies on temporal information versus text for final predictions
  - Quick check question: What does it mean if temporal information has higher attribution strength than text features?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Text Encoder (BiLSTM or DistilRoBERTa) → Claim/Evidence Representations
  - Time Embedding Matrix → Time Representations (document and content level)
  - Local/Global Integration Layers → Combined Text-Time Representations
  - Label Scorer and Evidence Scorer → Final Veracity Prediction and Evidence Ranking

- Critical path: Text Encoder → Integration Layer → Scorer → Prediction
  - Time embeddings flow through separate path but converge at integration layer

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Local integration captures position-specific temporal information but requires complex preprocessing
  - Global integration is simpler but may lose positional information
  - BiLSTM is less parameter-heavy but benefits more from temporal reasoning than Transformers

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on claims with no temporal information indicates over-reliance on time features
  - Inconsistent predictions across similar claims suggests time bucket construction issues
  - High variance across runs suggests hyperparameter sensitivity, particularly for integration weights

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare document-level vs. content-level grounding on a small validation set
  2. Test different integration levels (local vs. global) for the best-performing grounding level
  3. Perform ablation study by removing time features entirely to measure their impact

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of implicit temporal reasoning compare to explicit temporal reasoning in fact-checking models?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that their implicit temporal reasoning approach outperforms prior methods that explicitly model temporal relations between evidence.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that implicit temporal reasoning improves model performance, it does not provide a detailed comparison of the two approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed comparison of the performance of implicit and explicit temporal reasoning methods in fact-checking models.

### Open Question 2
How does the quality of extracted and normalized temporal information impact the performance of fact-checking models?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that implementing pretrained extraction and normalization models inevitably introduces noise in the data, and they assess the quality of these models to ensure that the noise is limited.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore how the quality of extracted and normalized temporal information affects the performance of fact-checking models.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis of the impact of the quality of extracted and normalized temporal information on the performance of fact-checking models.

### Open Question 3
How does the integration level of temporal information (local vs. global) affect the performance of fact-checking models?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the integration level of temporal information and shows that global integration substantially surpasses local integration for both document-level and content-level reasoning.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that global integration outperforms local integration, it does not explore the reasons behind this difference or the optimal integration level for different types of temporal information.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis of the reasons behind the difference in performance between local and global integration of temporal information and the optimal integration level for different types of temporal information.

## Limitations

- Reliance on HeidelTime for temporal normalization, which may struggle with informal language or domain-specific time expressions
- Assumption that publication dates are available and reliable for all evidence documents
- Limited exploration of alternative temporal reasoning approaches beyond bucket-based discretization
- Potential overfitting to the MultiFC dataset's temporal patterns

## Confidence

- Performance improvements: High - consistent gains across multiple metrics and baselines
- Generalization to other domains: Medium - temporal grounding approach may not transfer to non-news domains
- Temporal feature importance: Medium - attribution analysis suggests importance but doesn't establish causality

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-domain robustness test**: Evaluate the temporal reasoning approach on fact-checking datasets from different domains (scientific claims, social media misinformation) to assess generalization.
2. **Temporal feature ablation under noise**: Systematically corrupt time expressions and publication dates to measure the model's robustness to temporal uncertainty.
3. **Alternative temporal representations**: Compare bucket-based discretization with continuous temporal embeddings and other time representation methods to determine if the improvements are specific to the chosen approach.
