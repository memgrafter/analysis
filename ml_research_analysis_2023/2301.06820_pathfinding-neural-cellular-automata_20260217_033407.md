# Pathfinding Neural Cellular Automata

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06820
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06820
- Reference count: 40
- The paper presents hand-coded neural implementations of BFS and DFS using Neural Cellular Automata, showing improved generalization through architectural modifications like weight sharing and max-pooling.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the use of Neural Cellular Automata (NCA) for pathfinding tasks, specifically implementing Breadth-First Search and Depth-First Search algorithms in a neural framework. The authors develop hand-coded NCA implementations of these classic algorithms and demonstrate their ability to generate Dijkstra maps, extract shortest paths, and compute graph diameters on grid-based mazes. They then propose architectural modifications inspired by these implementations, showing that techniques like weight sharing, cutting corners, and adding max-pooling layers significantly improve the generalization capabilities of learned NCA models on shortest path and diameter problems.

## Method Summary
The authors implement hand-coded BFS and DFS algorithms using the Neural Cellular Automata framework, which consists of iterative neural networks with equal-size inputs and outputs. They demonstrate these implementations on grid-based mazes, showing their ability to generate Dijkstra maps, extract shortest paths, and compute graph diameters. Based on these hand-coded implementations, the authors propose architectural modifications including weight sharing, cutting corners, and adding max-pooling layers. They then evaluate these modifications on learned NCA models, comparing their performance against Graph Convolutional Networks and Multi-Layer Perceptrons on shortest path and diameter problems.

## Key Results
- Hand-coded NCA implementations successfully generate Dijkstra maps, extract shortest paths, and compute graph diameters on grid-based mazes
- Architectural modifications (weight sharing, cutting corners, max-pooling) improve generalization of learned NCA models on shortest path and diameter problems
- NCAs outperform GCNs and MLPs on tested pathfinding tasks, with proposed modifications further enhancing performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The NCA framework's iterative nature allows it to capture the step-by-step progression inherent in pathfinding algorithms like BFS and DFS. By implementing these algorithms as neural networks, the authors leverage the ability of neural networks to learn patterns and generalize from training data. The proposed architectural modifications are inspired by the hand-coded implementations and aim to incorporate algorithmic principles directly into the network structure, enabling better performance on pathfinding tasks.
