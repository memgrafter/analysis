# Sequential training of GANs against GAN-classifiers reveals correlated "knowledge gaps" present among independently trained GAN instances

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.15533
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15533
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Sequential GAN-classifier training reveals consistent artifacts across GANs and ordered artifact space structure

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether GAN-generated images contain consistent artifacts ("knowledge gaps") across different generator instances and how these artifacts evolve when GANs are trained to fool classifiers designed to detect them. Through iterative experiments alternating between GAN and classifier training, the authors demonstrate that high-capacity classifiers learn similar artifact sets while lower-capacity ones learn different subsets, and that StyleGAN2s can be trained to fool classifiers without quality degradation while revealing an ordered artifact space structure.

## Method Summary
The method involves iteratively training pools of GAN generators and classifier CNNs. First, multiple GAN instances are trained independently on the same dataset. Then classifiers are trained on samples from these generators to distinguish real from generated images. New GANs are then trained with modified loss functions that include terms to fool the classifiers, while maintaining image quality. This process repeats for multiple rounds. The study compares DCGAN on MNIST with StyleGAN2 on FFHQ, using different classifier architectures (basic CNN, ResNet-50, Inception-v3, MobileNetV2) and modified loss functions ("fool-all" vs "memoryless").

## Key Results
- Classifiers trained on multiple generators generalize well to unseen generators, confirming consistent artifacts across GANs
- DCGANs fail to fool classifiers without quality degradation, while StyleGAN2s can fool them without quality loss
- High-capacity classifiers (ResNet-50, Inception-v3) learn similar artifact sets, while lower-capacity ones (MobileNetV2) form artifact clusters
- StyleGAN2s continue to learn new artifacts across iterations without quality degradation, suggesting an ordered artifact space

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
GAN-generated images exhibit consistent "knowledge gaps" (artifacts) across different generator instances trained on the same dataset. When multiple GAN generators are trained independently on the same dataset, they produce images with similar distinguishing artifacts that can be learned by classifiers. This consistency allows classifiers trained on one set of generators to generalize to unseen generators.

### Mechanism 2
High-capacity classifier architectures (ResNet-50, Inception-v3) learn similar artifact sets while lower-capacity ones (MobileNetV2) learn different subsets. Classifier capacity determines how much of the available artifact space can be learned. High-capacity models can learn most available artifacts, leading to high overlap between instances, while low-capacity models learn partial subsets, creating artifact space clusters.

### Mechanism 3
Sequential training creates an "ordering over optima" in generator parameter space, where generators shift consistently between artifact spaces. When generators are trained to fool classifiers that have learned specific artifacts, they move to new regions of parameter space producing different artifacts. This process is repeatable across iterations because the transformation is consistent, not random.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Generator pool (multiple GAN instances) -> Classifier pool (multiple CNN classifiers) -> Training loop (alternating GAN/classifier training) -> Loss functions (modified to include classifier-fooling terms) -> Evaluation pipeline (measuring classifier generalization and generator quality)

- **Critical path**: 1) Train initial generator pool on dataset, 2) Train classifier pool on samples from generator pool, 3) Modify generator loss to include classifier-fooling terms, 4) Train new generator pool to fool classifiers, 5) Train new classifier pool on new generator pool, 6) Repeat for desired number of iterations

- **Design tradeoffs**: Number of generators per pool (more improves classifier generalization but increases computational cost), Classifier architecture choice (higher capacity improves fooling generalization but may learn different artifact sets), Loss function weighting (balances between fooling classifiers and maintaining output quality), Number of iterations (more reveal artifact space structure but may lead to decorrelation)

- **Failure signatures**: DCGANs failing to fool classifiers without quality degradation, Classifiers failing to generalize even with sufficient training generators, MobileNetV2 classifiers forming unpredictable clusters

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Train 5 DCGAN instances on MNIST, train 1 classifier on 3 generators, test on remaining 2 - verify classifier generalization, 2) Train StyleGAN2 with standard loss, then with ϕ=0.001 classifier-fooling term - verify fooling ability and quality maintenance, 3) Train ResNet-50 and MobileNetV2 classifiers on same generator pool, compare their generalization abilities - verify capacity-artifact relationship

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How many distinct artifacts can a single high-capacity classifier like ResNet-50 learn from StyleGAN2 generators, and what is the theoretical maximum capacity of such classifiers to detect GAN-generated artifacts? The paper shows ResNet-50 classifiers achieve strong generalization across generators but does not quantify the exact number or types of artifacts learned, nor establish theoretical capacity limits.

### Open Question 2
What is the mathematical relationship between the number of MobileNetV2 classifiers needed for generalization and their internal clustering behavior? The paper observes that MobileNetV2 classifiers form clusters with mutual-fooling properties and requires multiple instances for generalization, but does not derive a quantitative relationship.

### Open Question 3
Does the ordered artifact space observed in StyleGAN2 generators continue indefinitely across iterations, or does it eventually converge or become chaotic? The paper observes consistent artifact space transformations across multiple iterations but notes potential decorrelation after many iterations without quantifying when or how this occurs.

## Limitations
- Focuses exclusively on image generation tasks (MNIST and FFHQ), leaving unclear whether findings generalize to other data modalities
- Does not investigate the theoretical basis for why artifact space should have ordered structure
- Uses fixed 80/20 train-test split without exploring impact of split ratio on classifier generalization

## Confidence
- High confidence: Classifiers generalizing across different GAN instances, confirming consistent artifacts across generators
- Medium confidence: High-capacity vs low-capacity classifiers learning different artifact subsets
- Medium confidence: Sequential training revealing "ordering over optima" in parameter space

## Next Checks
1. Repeat sequential training experiments on at least two additional datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10 and LSUN bedrooms) to test generalization beyond MNIST and FFHQ
2. Systematically vary classifier architecture capacity while keeping other factors constant to establish clearer relationship between capacity and artifact space partitioning
3. Implement a randomization baseline where classifiers are trained on randomly selected subsets of artifacts to confirm observed clustering patterns are not experimental artifacts
