# Deep Active Learning with Structured Neural Depth Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02808
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02808
- Reference count: 30
- Active-SNDS achieves ~93% accuracy on CIFAR-10 at 20K labeled samples vs 84-92% for alternatives

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Structured Neural Depth Search (SNDS), a novel approach to active learning that integrates neural architecture search with structured variational inference to learn depth uncertainty during the active learning cycle. The method addresses the high computational costs and limited flexibility of existing Active-iNAS methods that require training multiple models per cycle. By formulating depth search as continuous optimization through gradient descent, SNDS achieves higher accuracy with lower label budgets across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 datasets using various querying strategies.

## Method Summary
SNDS proposes a structured variational inference framework that learns depth uncertainty during active learning cycles, replacing the multiple-model training approach of Active-iNAS. The method performs continuous depth search using gradient descent, theoretically avoiding the "rich-get-richer" problem that occurs with mean-field variational inference which favors shallow networks. The approach integrates seamlessly with existing active learning pipelines and three querying strategies (random, uncertainty entropy, coreset) while maintaining computational efficiency by avoiding multiple full model trainings per cycle.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10, Active-SNDS achieves ~93% accuracy at 20K labeled samples, while Active-iNAS and VI-based methods plateau at 84-92%
- Strong performance improvements across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 with all three querying strategies
- Achieves higher accuracy with lower label budgets compared to existing methods
- Maintains computational efficiency through continuous depth search rather than multiple model training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The structured variational inference framework in SNDS addresses the fundamental limitation of mean-field VI in neural architecture search, where shallow networks tend to dominate due to the "rich-get-richer" effect. By learning depth uncertainty in a structured manner and performing continuous depth search through gradient descent, SNDS can effectively explore the architecture space without the computational overhead of training multiple discrete models. This approach allows for more flexible and efficient architecture adaptation during active learning cycles.
