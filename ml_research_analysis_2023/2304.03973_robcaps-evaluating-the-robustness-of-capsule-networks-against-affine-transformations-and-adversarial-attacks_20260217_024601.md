# RobCaps: Evaluating the Robustness of Capsule Networks against Affine Transformations and Adversarial Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03973
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03973
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Capsule networks, especially DeepCaps, are more robust against adversarial attacks and affine transformations than CNNs with similar parameters

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of capsule networks' (CapsNets) robustness against adversarial attacks and affine transformations compared to traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The authors propose a systematic methodology to compare robustness across multiple datasets and attack types. Their findings reveal that CapsNets, particularly DeepCaps, achieve superior robustness on CIFAR10 and GTSRB datasets, while showing mixed results on MNIST. The study identifies that hierarchical feature learning through capsules, rather than dynamic routing, is the primary contributor to this enhanced robustness.

## Method Summary
The authors systematically compare the robustness of capsule networks (ShallowCaps, DeepCaps) against traditional CNNs (ResNet20, baseline CNN) across three datasets (MNIST, GTSRB, CIFAR10) and their affine-transformed versions. Models are trained with identical hyperparameters and data augmentation. Robustness is evaluated through adversarial attacks (PGD, CW) and testing on transformed datasets. The study also investigates the contribution of dynamic routing by training DeepCaps with and without this mechanism. The methodology ensures fair comparison by matching parameter counts and training procedures across architectures.

## Key Results
- Capsule networks achieve better robustness against adversarial examples and affine transformations compared to CNNs with similar parameter counts
- DeepCaps outperforms ResNet20 on CIFAR10 dataset in both accuracy and robustness
- Dynamic routing mechanism contributes minimally to CapsNets' robustness, with hierarchical feature learning through capsules being the primary factor
- CapsNets show mixed performance on MNIST, performing worse than ResNet20 for PGD attacks but better for affine transformations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The hierarchical feature learning through capsules provides greater robustness against adversarial attacks and affine transformations than traditional CNNs.
- Mechanism: Capsules encode spatial relationships and pose information in vector form, preserving equivariance. This hierarchical structure allows the network to learn robust cross-correlations between features, which remain stable under transformations and perturbations.
- Core assumption: The capsule structure inherently learns to represent spatial relationships more effectively than scalar neurons with max pooling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Overall, CapsNets achieve better robustness against adversarial examples and affine transformations, compared to a traditional CNN with a similar number of parameters."
  - [section IV-B] "Compared to the DeepCaps, the ResNet20 is deeper but has fewer parameters. It can generalize better for the affMNIST and affGTSRB but worse for the affCIFAR dataset."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The dynamic routing mechanism does not significantly contribute to the robustness of CapsNets.
- Mechanism: The dynamic routing algorithm is computationally expensive and primarily serves to increase confidence in predictions by propagating only highly agreeing activations. However, it does not fundamentally improve the network's ability to generalize under adversarial attacks or affine transformations.
- Core assumption: The robustness improvements are primarily due to the capsule structure itself, not the routing mechanism.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Moreover, our results unleash a key finding that the dynamic routing does not contribute much to improving the CapsNets' robustness. Indeed, the main generalization contribution is due to the hierarchical feature learning through capsules."
  - [section VI-B] "For the MNIST dataset, the DeepCaps with dynamic routing is slightly more robust than the version without it. On the contrary, for the CIFAR10, the accuracy of the DeepCaps without dynamic routing decreases faster when increasing the perturbation ε."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DeepCaps achieves better robustness than ResNet20 on complex datasets like CIFAR10 due to the combination of capsule architecture and depth.
- Mechanism: The DeepCaps model uses a deeper architecture with 3D convolutions and capsules, allowing it to learn more complex hierarchical features. This structure is more effective at handling the increased complexity of datasets like CIFAR10 compared to traditional deep CNNs like ResNet20.
- Core assumption: The combination of depth and capsule structure provides a unique advantage in learning complex features that are robust to transformations and attacks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section IV-B] "For this kind of algorithm and the MNIST dataset, Figure 6a shows that the DeepCaps behaves worse than the ResNet20. On the contrary, for more complex datasets like CIFAR10 or GTSRB, the results in Figure 6 show that the ResNet20 is not as robust as for the MNIST dataset."
  - [abstract] "Similar conclusions have been derived for deeper versions of CapsNets and CNNs."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input image → Convolutional layer → Primary capsule layer → Dynamic routing (or alternative) → DigitCaps layer → Class predictions

- Critical path:
  1. Input image → Convolutional layer → Primary capsule layer
  2. Primary capsules → Dynamic routing (or alternative) → DigitCaps layer
  3. DigitCaps layer → Class predictions

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using capsules instead of scalar neurons increases the model's ability to preserve spatial relationships but also increases computational complexity
  - Dynamic routing improves prediction confidence but does not significantly enhance robustness and is computationally expensive
  - Deeper CapsNet architectures (like DeepCaps) can achieve better robustness on complex datasets but may not outperform shallower CapsNets on simpler tasks

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on affine-transformed datasets indicates the model is not effectively learning spatial relationships
  - High vulnerability to adversarial attacks suggests the model is learning brittle features
  - Excessive computational cost with minimal robustness gains may indicate inefficient use of the dynamic routing mechanism

- First 3 experiments:
