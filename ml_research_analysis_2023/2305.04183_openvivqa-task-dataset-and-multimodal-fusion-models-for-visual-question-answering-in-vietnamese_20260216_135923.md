# OpenViVQA: Task, Dataset, and Multimodal Fusion Models for Visual Question Answering in Vietnamese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04183
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04183
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: OpenViVQA dataset introduced with 11,199 images and 37,914 Vietnamese QA pairs; three novel generator-based VQA methods (FST, QuMLAG, MLPAG) outperform classification approaches with MLPAG achieving BLEU@4 of 0.1889, METEOR of 0.2230, and CIDEr of 1.6104.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces OpenViVQA, the first large-scale Vietnamese visual question answering dataset featuring open-ended answers. The dataset contains 11,199 images and 37,914 manually annotated Vietnamese question-answer pairs. To address the challenges posed by this dataset, the authors propose three novel answer-generation methods that fuse image and question information using attention mechanisms. These methods generate answers iteratively rather than selecting from fixed answer sets, demonstrating superior performance compared to classification-based approaches.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three generator-based VQA methods: FST (Stacked Attention with LSTM and Transformer decoder), QuMLAG (Question-guided Attention with Transformer decoder), and MLPAG (Context + Spatial + Self Attention with Dynamic Pointer Network). All methods use multimodal fusion to combine image features (ResNeXt152++ or FasterRCNN) with question embeddings (LSTM or FastText + LSTM) and scene text embeddings (SwinTextSpotter + FastText). The models are trained with early stopping on CIDEr metric using Adam optimizer, batch size 64, and learning rate schedule from Vaswani et al. (2017).

## Key Results
- MLPAG achieves BLEU@4 of 0.1889, METEOR of 0.2230, and CIDEr of 1.6104 on OpenViVQA
- Proposed generator methods outperform state-of-the-art classification-based approaches
- Answers in OpenViVQA have diverse linguistic levels and lengths, making classification approaches inadequate
- Scene text integration through Dynamic Pointer Network improves Text QA performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Open-ended answer generation is necessary because Vietnamese VQA datasets with fixed answer sets cannot capture the linguistic complexity and variability of real-world questions and answers.
- Mechanism: The paper defines "open-ended" in terms of both question and answer freedom, avoiding predetermined answer lists and enabling natural language generation. This approach better reflects human-like answering behavior.
- Core assumption: A fixed set of answers (classification task) inherently limits expressiveness and fails to capture the open-ended nature of Vietnamese questions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we introduce the OpenViVQA dataset... with open-ended answers in Vietnamese"
  - [section]: "We define a different form of the VQA task, the open-ended VQA, which has open-ended questions and open-ended answers"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; related works focus on dataset construction but do not directly compare fixed vs. open-ended answer performance in Vietnamese.
- Break condition: If Vietnamese questions can be adequately answered using a small, fixed vocabulary (e.g., very constrained domains), classification could suffice.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Scene text in Vietnamese images must be read and flexibly integrated into generated answers; copying text verbatim is insufficient.
- Mechanism: The MLPAG method adds a Dynamic Pointer Network to fuse scene text features with generated tokens, enabling context-aware integration of scene text into answers.
- Core assumption: Scene text provides additional contextual information beyond object recognition, and effective integration improves answer quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Moreover, we proposed FST, QuMLAG, and MLPAG which fuse information from images and answers"
  - [section]: "MLPAG was designed carefully to keep the spirit of LoRRA... it uses the Dynamic Pointer Network that fuses the features of embedded features of all tokens in vocab with the embedded features of scene texts"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; related works mention reading scene text but do not show superior performance of flexible integration vs. simple copying in Vietnamese.
- Break condition: If scene text is redundant or irrelevant to most questions, or if simple copying yields comparable results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Answer generation models can outperform classification models on open-ended VQA tasks when the dataset has long, complex answers.
- Mechanism: Generator-based methods (FST, QuMLAG, MLPAG) iteratively construct answers using transformer decoders, allowing longer, more natural responses than fixed answer sets.
- Core assumption: Longer answers correlate with higher linguistic complexity and require generative models for adequate representation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our proposed methods achieve results that are competitive with SOTA models such as SAAA, MCAN, LORA, and M4C"
  - [section]: "answers in the OpenViVQA dataset have diverse linguistic levels... this indicates the classification approach... is not adaptable"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no direct comparison of generator vs. classifier performance on Vietnamese datasets with similar complexity.
- Break condition: If Vietnamese answers are typically short and simple, classification could be equally effective.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Difference between classification and generation in VQA
  - Why needed here: The paper's core argument is that classification approaches fail on open-ended datasets, while generation succeeds.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain why fixed answer sets limit expressiveness in natural language tasks?

- Concept: Attention mechanisms in multimodal fusion
  - Why needed here: Proposed methods use various attention mechanisms (Stacked, Question-guided, Dynamic Pointer) to fuse image and text features.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between single-hop and multi-hop attention in VQA models?

- Concept: Scene text recognition and integration
  - Why needed here: MLPAG's Dynamic Pointer Network must read and integrate scene text from images into answers.
  - Quick check question: How does a pointer network differ from standard sequence generation in copying tokens?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image features (ResNeXt152++ or FasterRCNN) -> Question embeddings (LSTM or FastText + LSTM) -> Scene text embeddings (SwinTextSpotter + FastText) -> Multimodal fusion (Stacked/Question-guided/Dynamic Pointer Attention) -> Answer generation (Transformer decoder)
- Critical path: Image/text features → Multimodal fusion → Answer generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed answer set (classification) vs. open-ended generation
  - Grid vs. region features for images
  - LSTM vs. transformer-based question embedding
  - Simple copying vs. context-aware scene text integration
- Failure signatures:
  - Classification models fail to generate long/complex answers
  - Generator models repeat question tokens excessively
  - Scene text integration fails when text is irrelevant or noisy
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare classification vs. generation performance on a subset with only short answers
  2. Ablate scene text reading capability to measure its impact on Text QA performance
  3. Vary attention mechanism complexity to identify performance bottlenecks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific architectural modifications to the Dynamic Pointer Network would be needed to enable effective copying of scene text from Vietnamese images to open-ended answers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that M4C's Dynamic Pointer Network struggles with open-ended answers in Vietnamese, frequently misusing scene text or failing to copy it appropriately, unlike in English datasets where answers are often just scene text.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but doesn't propose concrete architectural changes to address it, instead suggesting "we need a better solution" without specifying what that would be.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study showing performance improvements when modifying the pointer network's attention mechanisms, copying strategy, or integration with Vietnamese language processing specifically for scene text.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the linguistic complexity of Vietnamese answers (as measured by dependency tree height and token count) correlate with the performance of generator-based VQA models across different answer length groups?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows Vietnamese answers have higher dependency tree heights and more complex linguistic structures than English VQA datasets, and notes generator models struggle more with longer answers.
- Why unresolved: While correlations are observed between length groups and performance, the paper doesn't establish a direct causal relationship between linguistic complexity metrics and model performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Regression analysis showing how dependency tree metrics predict performance degradation, potentially identifying complexity thresholds where model performance drops significantly.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would a multilingual VQA dataset combining OpenViVQA with similar Vietnamese datasets significantly improve the performance of vision-language models compared to training on OpenViVQA alone?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose creating a multilingual VQA dataset and note that OpenViVQA's size is relatively small compared to English datasets, suggesting this limits model performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the dataset size limitation but doesn't empirically test whether combining datasets would help, nor does it explore the optimal composition of such a multilingual dataset.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments training models on OpenViVQA alone versus OpenViVQA combined with other Vietnamese VQA datasets, measuring performance improvements and identifying diminishing returns points.

## Limitations
- No direct empirical comparison between classification and generation approaches on OpenViVQA
- Weak evidence for superiority of flexible scene text integration over simple copying in Vietnamese contexts
- Dataset representativeness limited to Vietnam-specific imagery and questions

## Confidence
- High confidence: Dataset construction methodology and technical implementation of generator models are well-specified and reproducible
- Medium confidence: Claim that open-ended answers are necessary for Vietnamese VQA lacks direct empirical comparison with classification approaches
- Low confidence: Assertion that flexible scene text integration significantly outperforms simple copying in Vietnamese contexts is weakly supported

## Next Checks

2. Implement a variant of MLPAG that only copies scene text verbatim without contextual integration, then compare performance on Text QA subsets to isolate the benefit of flexible text handling
