# A Study of Generative Large Language Model for Medical Research and Healthcare

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13523
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13523
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 0

## Executive Summary
This study develops GatorTronGPT, a 20-billion parameter clinical generative LLM trained on 277 billion words of mixed clinical and English text. The model demonstrates significant improvements in biomedical NLP tasks compared to existing models, achieving F1-scores of 0.500, 0.494, and 0.419 on relation extraction tasks. Notably, synthetic clinical text generated by GatorTronGPT enables training of NLP models (GatorTronS) that outperform or match those trained on real-world clinical text. Physician evaluation shows that generated clinical notes are linguistically and clinically comparable to human-written text, with no significant differences in readability (p = 0.22) or clinical relevance (p = 0.91).

## Method Summary
The study trains GatorTronGPT from scratch using GPT-3 architecture with 20 billion parameters on a corpus of 277 billion words, combining 82 billion words of de-identified UF Health clinical text with 195 billion words from the Pile dataset. The model is trained using 560 A100 GPUs with batch size 560 and learning rate 1e-5. For task adaptation, prompt-tuning (p-tuning) is applied to biomedical NLP tasks including relation extraction and question answering. The study generates 20 billion words of synthetic clinical text using top-p sampling and trains GatorTronS models on this synthetic data. A Turing test evaluation with 7 physicians assesses the quality of generated clinical text against human-written notes using 1-9 scales for linguistic readability and clinical relevance.

## Key Results
- GatorTronGPT outperforms existing biomedical transformer models on three benchmark datasets with performance gains of 3%-10%
- Synthetic clinical text generated by GatorTronGPT enables training of GatorTronS models that outperform NLP models trained on real-world clinical text
- Physician evaluation shows no significant difference between GatorTronGPT-generated and human-written clinical notes in linguistic readability (p = 0.22) or clinical relevance (p = 0.91)

## Limitations

- The study relies on a single institutional dataset (UF Health) for clinical text, which may limit generalizability to other healthcare systems with different patient populations and documentation styles.
- While physician evaluations show no significant difference in linguistic readability and clinical relevance, the study does not assess whether synthetic text contains subtle factual errors or logical inconsistencies that human evaluators might miss.
- Training a 20B parameter model requires substantial computational resources (560 A100 GPUs), making this approach inaccessible to many research groups and limiting reproducibility.
