# Quantifying Character Similarity with Vision Transformers

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a novel approach to measuring character similarity for OCR error correction by training vision transformers with contrastive learning on augmented digital fonts. The method learns a metric space where homoglyphic characters - those with similar visual appearance - are represented nearby, enabling more accurate record linkage through improved edit distance calculations. The approach demonstrates significant improvements over traditional string matching methods, particularly for CJK scripts where OCR errors tend to be visually similar.

The research extends to ancient Chinese characters, revealing fascinating insights about how abstract concepts were conceptualized in historical societies. By capturing visual relationships between characters that have been noted in archaeological literature, the method bridges modern machine learning techniques with historical linguistics, suggesting broader applications for understanding ancient writing systems and cultural evolution.

## Method Summary
The approach employs large-scale self-supervised training of vision transformers on augmented digital fonts from CJK scripts. Using contrastive learning, the model learns to place different augmentations of the same character nearby in the embedding space while pushing dissimilar characters apart. The cosine distance between character embeddings serves as the substitution cost in edit distance algorithms, replacing traditional character-specific costs. This visual similarity metric is particularly effective for OCR error correction since errors often involve homoglyphic substitutions.

## Key Results
- Cosine distance between ViT embeddings significantly improves record linkage compared to traditional string matching methods
- Method successfully captures homoglyphic relationships across CJK scripts
- Application to 3,000-year-old ancient Chinese characters reveals culturally significant conceptual relationships

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method leverages the inherent visual similarity of OCR errors, which frequently involve characters that look alike but have different meanings. By training on augmented fonts, the ViT learns robust visual features that generalize across font styles and minor variations. The contrastive learning framework ensures that the model develops a meaningful metric space where visually similar characters cluster together, making the cosine distance a natural choice for measuring substitution costs.

## Limitations
- Generalizability across diverse font styles and document degradation patterns remains uncertain
- Assumption that homoglyphic similarity captures all relevant OCR error patterns may not hold for all languages
- Validation on ancient Chinese characters represents a small sample size that may not generalize to other historical scripts

## Confidence
- High confidence in the technical approach of using ViT with contrastive learning for character similarity learning
- Medium confidence in the improvement of record linkage performance compared to traditional methods
- Medium confidence in the method's applicability to low-resource and historical scripts
- Low confidence in the broader implications about ancient societies' conceptual relationships
