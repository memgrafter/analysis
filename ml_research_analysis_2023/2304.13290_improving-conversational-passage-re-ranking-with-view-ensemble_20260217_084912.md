# Improving Conversational Passage Re-ranking with View Ensemble

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13290
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13290
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ConvRerank achieves better top-ranking effectiveness (nDCG@3) and lower latency compared to existing multi-stage pipelines and baselines on TREC CAsT datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ConvRerank, a conversational passage re-ranker that leverages a view-ensemble pseudo-labeling approach to enhance re-ranking quality. The method synthesizes training data by combining ranked lists from both human-rewritten queries and answers, filtering and reordering passages to improve relevance labeling. Experiments demonstrate that ConvRerank achieves superior top-ranking effectiveness (nDCG@3) while maintaining lower latency compared to traditional multi-stage pipelines. The model shows robustness across different first-stage retrievers and scales effectively with larger model sizes.

## Method Summary
ConvRerank employs a view-ensemble pseudo-labeling technique that synthesizes training data from multiple sources to improve re-ranking performance. The method combines ranked lists generated from human-rewritten queries and answers, filtering and reordering passages to create more accurate relevance labels. This approach addresses the challenge of limited training data in conversational search by generating synthetic yet high-quality training examples. The re-ranker integrates these pseudo-labeled examples into its training pipeline, enabling better capture of conversational context and relevance patterns compared to traditional re-ranking approaches.

## Key Results
- Achieves better top-ranking effectiveness with nDCG@3 on TREC CAsT datasets compared to existing multi-stage pipelines and baselines
- Demonstrates lower latency compared to traditional re-ranking approaches
- Shows robustness across different first-stage retrievers and scales well with larger model sizes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The view-ensemble pseudo-labeling approach works by synthesizing training data from multiple perspectives - human-rewritten queries and answers - which provides complementary views of relevance. This multi-view approach captures different aspects of conversational context and user intent that single-view labeling might miss. By filtering and reordering passages from these different views, the method creates more reliable and comprehensive relevance labels. This enriched training data enables the re-ranker to better understand the nuanced relationships between conversational queries and relevant passages, leading to improved ranking performance.

**View ensemble learning** - Why needed: Different query transformations (rewritten queries vs. answers) provide complementary information about relevance. Quick check: Confirm that combining multiple views produces better labels than any single view alone.

**Multi-stage retrieval pipelines** - Why needed: Conversational search typically requires separate retrieval and re-ranking stages. Quick check: Ensure the re-ranker effectively improves upon first-stage retriever outputs.

**Latency optimization in ranking systems** - Why needed: Real-time conversational systems require fast response times. Quick check: Measure end-to-end latency and compare against baseline systems.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Query Input -> First-stage Retriever -> ConvRerank -> Final Ranked List

**Critical path**: The critical path involves generating ranked lists from multiple views (rewritten queries and answers), synthesizing pseudo-labels through ensemble filtering, training the re-ranker on these labels, and applying the trained model to re-rank first-stage retriever outputs.

**Design tradeoffs**: The method trades off some computational overhead for pseudo-label generation against improved ranking quality. The view-ensemble approach requires multiple retrieval passes but produces more reliable training data. The design prioritizes top-ranking effectiveness (nDCG@3) over other metrics.

**Failure signatures**: Potential failures include: (1) poor quality pseudo-labels if view synthesis is ineffective, (2) computational bottlenecks during multi-view retrieval, (3) overfitting to synthetic data patterns, (4) failure to generalize across different conversational domains.

## Limitations
- Reliance on human-rewritten queries raises scalability concerns for real-world deployment
- Computational requirements and cost implications for production environments are not fully explored
- Evaluation focuses primarily on nDCG@3 with limited analysis of other metrics or cutoff points
- Claims of robustness across different first-stage retrievers need more extensive validation
