# Efficient Sensitivity Analysis for Parametric Robust Markov Chains

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an efficient method for sensitivity analysis of parametric robust Markov chains (prMCs), enabling the computation of partial derivatives of the solution function with respect to uncertain transition probabilities. The approach leverages linear programming and differentiation of optimization problems to efficiently identify parameters with the highest impact on model behavior. By computing derivatives through a novel combination of LP solutions and linearized equation systems, the method achieves significant speedups compared to explicit computation of all derivatives. The authors demonstrate the approach's scalability on models with over a million states and thousands of parameters, and show its effectiveness in guiding exploration within iterative learning schemes.

## Method Summary
The method computes partial derivatives of the robust solution function by first solving a linear program to obtain the robust solution and dual variables, then solving a linearized equation system derived from the active constraints. For selecting the k parameters with highest derivatives, an LP relaxation of the original MILP is solved, which yields binary solutions that directly identify the optimal parameters. The approach handles parametric uncertainty by treating transition probabilities as convex combinations of vertices, and uses the nonsingularity of a constraint matrix to ensure differentiability. This framework enables efficient sensitivity analysis even for large-scale prMCs.

## Key Results
- Computing k highest derivatives is orders of magnitude faster than computing all derivatives explicitly
- The LP relaxation consistently yields binary solutions that correctly identify the k parameters with maximal derivatives
- The sensitivity analysis effectively guides exploration in iterative learning frameworks, leading to faster convergence

## Limitations

- The method relies on two critical assumptions: the matrix C is nonsingular and the LP relaxation yields binary solutions, which may not hold for all prMC instances.
- Numerical stability for solving large-scale LPs and linear systems for models with millions of states remains an open concern.
- The assumption that higher derivatives directly correlate with optimal sampling decisions may not hold due to potential nonlinearities or unmodeled dependencies.

## Confidence
