# A Match Made in Heaven: A Multi-task Framework for Hyperbole and Metaphor Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17480
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17480
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: Multi-task learning framework achieves SOTA hyperbole detection (F1=0.881) and establishes new metaphor detection benchmark (F1=0.805)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a multi-task deep learning framework that jointly detects hyperbole and metaphor in text. The key insight is that these two figurative language phenomena are linguistically related, so training a model to detect both simultaneously can improve performance over single-task approaches. To enable this, the authors annotate existing hyperbole datasets with metaphor labels and vice versa, creating new combined datasets. Experiments show that their multi-task learning approach achieves state-of-the-art hyperbole detection results and establishes a new benchmark for sentence-level metaphor detection. The multi-task model outperforms single-task learning by up to 17%, demonstrating the benefits of leveraging the linguistic relationship between hyperbole and metaphor.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a multi-task deep learning framework that jointly detects hyperbole and metaphor in text. They create new combined datasets by annotating existing hyperbole datasets with metaphor labels and vice versa. The framework uses a BERT encoder with task-specific attention mechanisms to capture the linguistic relationships between hyperbole and metaphor. The model is trained on the combined datasets using multi-task learning, allowing it to leverage shared features between the two figurative language phenomena. Experiments demonstrate that this approach achieves superior performance compared to single-task learning baselines.

## Key Results
- Multi-task learning approach achieves state-of-the-art hyperbole detection with F1=0.881
- Establishes new benchmark for sentence-level metaphor detection with F1=0.805
- Outperforms single-task learning by up to 17%, demonstrating the benefits of leveraging linguistic relationships between hyperbole and metaphor

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's approach works because hyperbole and metaphor share underlying linguistic features and cognitive processes. By training a single model to detect both phenomena simultaneously, the network can learn shared representations that capture common patterns in figurative language. The multi-task learning framework allows the model to transfer knowledge between tasks, improving generalization and performance on both hyperbole and metaphor detection. The use of a pre-trained BERT encoder provides strong language understanding capabilities, while task-specific attention mechanisms allow the model to focus on relevant features for each figurative language type.

## Limitations
- The evaluation relies on annotated datasets created by manually labeling existing corpora, which may introduce annotation inconsistencies and not capture full complexity of real-world figurative language usage.
- The paper does not report confidence intervals or statistical significance testing for performance differences, making it difficult to assess whether improvements over single-task approaches are robust.
- The metaphor detection benchmark claim lacks detailed validation of the metaphor annotation process and does not compare against alternative metaphor detection approaches.

## Confidence
High: The multi-task learning architecture is sound and technically well-implemented. The architectural choices (BERT encoder, task-specific attention mechanisms) are appropriate for the problem domain.

Medium: The quantitative performance improvements reported for hyperbole detection are likely real but may be somewhat overstated without statistical significance testing. The qualitative claims about linguistic relationships between hyperbole and metaphor are reasonable but not empirically validated.

Low: The metaphor detection benchmark claim is the least substantiated, as the paper lacks detailed validation of the metaphor annotation process and does not compare against alternative metaphor detection approaches.
