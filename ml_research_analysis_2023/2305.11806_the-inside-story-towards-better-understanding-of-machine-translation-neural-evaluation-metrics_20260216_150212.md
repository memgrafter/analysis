# The Inside Story: Towards Better Understanding of Machine Translation Neural Evaluation Metrics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11806
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11806
- Reference count: 19
- Reference-based neural metrics for MT evaluation can be analyzed using attribution methods to understand their decision-making process

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the explainability of neural machine translation evaluation metrics, specifically focusing on COMET and UNITE architectures. Using attribution methods like attention weights and gradient scores, the authors analyze how these metrics process translation quality and whether their explanations can identify weaknesses in their evaluation capabilities. The research employs both human-annotated translation errors from MQM data and synthetically-generated critical errors to validate the explanations. The findings reveal that explanations are architecture-dependent, with reference information improving explanation quality, and that critical errors like hallucinations are better explained than non-critical errors.

## Method Summary
The authors employ attribution methods including attention weights and gradient-based scores to generate explanations for neural MT evaluation metrics. They analyze COMET and UNITE models using both real human-annotated errors from MQM datasets and synthetically generated critical errors. The study compares explanation effectiveness across different architectures and error types, examining how reference information influences the quality of explanations. Correlation analyses are conducted to validate whether the attribution-based explanations align with human judgments of translation quality.

## Key Results
- Explanations are architecture-dependent, with attention-based methods performing better for UNITE models
- Reference information consistently improves explanation quality across both architectures
- Explanations for critical errors like hallucinations are more effective than for non-critical errors
- COMET explanations struggle with localized errors, likely due to architectural differences

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of attribution methods in explaining neural MT evaluation metrics stems from their ability to trace back the model's decision-making process to specific input features. Attention weights reveal which parts of the input the model focuses on when making quality judgments, while gradient-based methods show how changes in input would affect the output score. This mechanistic insight helps identify whether the metrics are focusing on the right aspects of translation quality and can reveal architectural biases that affect their evaluation capabilities.

**Component Map:** Input text -> Embedding layer -> Encoder/Attention mechanism -> Scoring layer -> Output quality score
**Critical Path:** Input processing → Reference integration → Attention computation → Quality assessment
**Design Tradeoffs:** Attention-based vs gradient-based attribution methods; reference inclusion vs standalone evaluation; computational cost vs explanation quality
**Failure Signatures:** Poor localization of errors, inability to detect hallucinations, architecture-specific blind spots
**First Experiments:** 1) Generate attention heatmaps for known good/bad translations, 2) Compare gradient scores for critical vs non-critical errors, 3) Test explanation consistency across different reference translations

## Limitations
- Analysis limited to COMET and UNITE architectures, limiting generalizability
- Synthetic error generation may not capture real-world translation complexity
- MQM dataset may contain biases affecting validation of explanations
- Attribution methods themselves have known limitations in providing faithful explanations

## Confidence
Major claims about architecture-specific differences (High confidence): The findings that COMET and UNITE produce different explanation patterns are well-supported by the methodology, though limited to these specific architectures.

Claims about reference information improving explanations (Medium confidence): While supported by experimental results, the mechanism and extent of this improvement could benefit from further investigation across different datasets and error types.

Claims about explanation effectiveness for critical errors (Medium confidence): The results are promising but based on synthetic error generation, which may not fully represent real translation scenarios.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the analysis with additional neural MT evaluation metrics beyond COMET and UNITE to test architecture-generalizability of the findings.

2. Conduct human evaluation studies to validate whether the attribution-based explanations align with human understanding of metric behavior, particularly for localized errors where COMET showed weaknesses.

3. Test the explanation methods on real-world translation errors from diverse domains and languages to assess robustness beyond the controlled synthetic error generation and MQM dataset.
