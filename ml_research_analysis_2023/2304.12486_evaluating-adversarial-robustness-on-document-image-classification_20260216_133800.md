# Evaluating Adversarial Robustness on Document Image Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12486
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12486
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: Adversarial training significantly improves robustness against all attacks, while JPEG compression and grayscale transformation have inconsistent effects

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the adversarial robustness of document image classification models using the RVL-CDIP dataset. The authors test two model architectures (ResNet50 and EfficientNetB0) against various gradient-based, transfer-based, and score-based attacks. They examine the impact of JPEG compression, grayscale transformation, and adversarial training on model robustness. The main finding is that adversarial training consistently improves robustness across all attacks, reducing accuracy drop to around 2-3% compared to 50-60% without training.

## Method Summary
The study uses the RVL-CDIP dataset containing 400,000 black-and-white document images in 16 categories, downsampled to 240x240 pixels with 3 RGB channels. Two model architectures (ResNet50 and EfficientNetB0) are trained with different defense strategies: no defense, grayscale transformation, JPEG compression, and adversarial training. Model robustness is evaluated against gradient-based attacks (FGM, BIM, MIM), transfer-based attacks, and score-based attacks (SPSA) using a test set of 1000 randomly selected images.

## Key Results
- Adversarial training significantly improves robustness against all evaluated attacks, reducing accuracy drop to 2-3%
- Without adversarial training, models show 50-60% accuracy drop under white-box attacks
- JPEG compression and grayscale transformation have inconsistent effects on robustness across different attacks and models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial training significantly improves model robustness against gradient-based attacks
- Mechanism: Including adversarial examples in training helps models recognize and classify inputs near decision boundaries
- Core assumption: BIM-generated adversarial examples are representative of real-world attacks
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The main finding is that adversarial training significantly improves robustness against all attacks"; [section] "The adversarial training, on the other hand, improves consistently for all model backbones and under all evaluated attacks the robustness of targeted models"
- Break Condition: If threat model changes to include attacks outside L-infinity and L2 norms used during training

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: JPEG compression preprocessing can improve model robustness against adversarial attacks
- Mechanism: JPEG compression reduces effectiveness of small perturbations by removing high-frequency noise
- Core assumption: Compression process removes high-frequency noise often used in adversarial perturbations
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "while JPEG compression and grayscale transformation have inconsistent effects"; [section] "The grey-scale transformation is an other low-cost preprocessing step"
- Break Condition: If attack method is designed to be robust to JPEG compression

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transfer-based attacks are less effective on adversarially trained models
- Mechanism: Adversarially trained models learn features invariant to perturbations used in transfer attacks
- Core assumption: Adversarially trained model has learned perturbation-invariant features
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "while adversarial training significantly improves robustness against all attacks..."; [section] "The adversarial training, on the other hand, improves consistently for all model backbones and under all evaluated attacks the robustness of targeted models"
- Break Condition: If substitute model is significantly different from target model

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do adversarial examples generated for document image classification models transfer to multimodal models like DocFormer or LayoutLMv2?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "it would be interesting to evaluate the transferability of the generated examples to a multimodal model such as DocFormer or LayoutLMv2"
- Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates robustness on visual-only models and suggests testing transferability to multimodal models as future work
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments measuring accuracy drop on multimodal models when attacked with adversarial examples generated for visual-only models

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can document-specific adversarial attacks (e.g., changing font, size, bold/italic characters) be more effective than standard image-based attacks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "attacks specific to document data could be thought of, for example changing the style of the text by changing the font, the size, using bold or italic characters"
- Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates standard image-based attacks and mentions document-specific attacks as future work
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of document-specific attack methods showing improved effectiveness compared to standard attacks

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal trade-off between adversarial training strength and model accuracy on clean data for document image classification?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper shows adversarial training improves robustness but doesn't explore trade-off with clean data accuracy
- Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates one adversarial training method and doesn't explore different training strengths or their impact on clean accuracy
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying adversarial training parameters and measuring both clean accuracy and adversarial robustness to find optimal trade-offs

## Limitations

- Limited attack diversity: Only four attack types evaluated, all within L-infinity and L2 threat models
- Inconsistent preprocessing effects: JPEG compression and grayscale transformation show dataset-specific or implementation-dependent properties not fully characterized
- Underspecified implementation: Adversarial training implementation details remain underspecified, making exact reproduction challenging

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Claims about adversarial training consistently improving robustness across all evaluated attacks
- Medium Confidence: Claims about preprocessing methods having inconsistent effects
- Medium Confidence: Claims about transfer attacks being less effective on adversarially trained models

## Next Checks

1. Replicate experiments with additional attack methods beyond L-infinity and L2 norms to test adversarial training's broader effectiveness

3. Conduct ablation studies comparing adversarial training with different attack methods during training to identify optimal training strategies
