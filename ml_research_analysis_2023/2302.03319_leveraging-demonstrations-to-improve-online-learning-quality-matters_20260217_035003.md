# Leveraging Demonstrations to Improve Online Learning: Quality Matters

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03319
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03319
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Introduces informed Thompson sampling that leverages demonstration data through an informative prior, achieving regret reduction that increases with expert competence level.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of improving online learning performance by leveraging offline demonstration data from an expert. The key insight is that the quality of demonstrations matters significantly, and this quality can be quantified through knowledgeability (λ) and deliberateness (β) parameters. The authors propose an informed Thompson sampling algorithm that constructs an informative prior using Bayes' rule with demonstration data, leading to substantial regret reduction compared to standard Thompson sampling. A practical approximation method using Bayesian bootstrapping is also introduced to handle the computational challenges of exact posterior sampling.

## Method Summary
The method involves constructing an informative prior distribution for Thompson sampling by combining the initial prior with offline demonstration data through Bayes' rule. The demonstration data is weighted by the expert's competence level, parameterized by knowledgeability (λ) and deliberateness (β). At each time step, the algorithm either samples from the exact posterior (computationally intensive) or uses a Bayesian bootstrapping approximation that perturbs the loss function and computes MAP estimates. This approach allows the algorithm to start with a more accurate belief about the environment, reducing the exploration phase required in standard Thompson sampling.

## Key Results
- Proposed informed Thompson sampling algorithm that leverages demonstration data through an informative prior
- Novel information-theoretic regret decomposition showing regret reduction depends on expert competence level
- Practical Bayesian bootstrapping approximation achieving substantial empirical regret reduction
- Theoretical regret bounds that explicitly depend on the expert's competence parameters

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The informed Thompson sampling algorithm reduces regret by leveraging an informative prior that encodes the expert's competence level through Bayes' rule.
- Mechanism: The algorithm constructs an informative prior by combining the initial prior with the offline demonstration data, where the action likelihood term P(¯An|θ) captures the expert's competence through the knowledgeability (λ) and deliberateness (β) parameters. This prior is then used to initialize the Thompson sampling process, allowing the algorithm to start with a more accurate belief about the environment.
- Core assumption: The expert's competence level (λ and β) can be accurately estimated or known, and the expert policy follows the softmax form with knowledgeability and deliberateness parameters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose an informed TS algorithm that utilizes the demonstration data in a coherent way through Bayes' rule and derive a prior-dependent Bayesian regret bound that offers insight into how conditioning on demonstration data can greatly improve online performance and how the degree of improvement increases with the expert's competence level."
  - [section 3]: "It constructs an informative prior by the use of offline dataset D0 and Bayes' rule, and which satisfies P(θ ∈ ·|D0) ∝ P(D0|θ ∈ ·)ν0(·)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers discuss demonstration quality but don't specifically address competence-aware priors in Thompson sampling.

- Break condition: If the expert's competence parameters are misspecified or cannot be accurately estimated, the informative prior may not reflect the true environment, potentially leading to worse performance than uninformed Thompson sampling.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The information-theoretic regret decomposition captures how an informative prior reduces both the information ratio and entropy of the optimal arm distribution.
- Mechanism: By introducing an (1-ε)-informative set U that contains the optimal arm with high probability, the regret can be decomposed into two terms: one bounded by the expected cardinality of U and the other by a small additive term. This decomposition shows how the informative prior effectively reduces the search space for the optimal arm.
- Core assumption: There exists a set U that can be constructed from the offline data which contains the optimal arm with high probability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "We now introduce a novel information-theoretic regret decomposition such that the Bayesian regret bound can reflect the effect of the prior distribution."
  - [section 4.3]: "The set UA is (1 − f1)-informative and E[|UA|] ≤ f2" where f1 and f2 depend on the competence parameters.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence - related work focuses on demonstration quality but not information-theoretic decomposition.

- Break condition: If the offline data is insufficient or the expert's competence is too low, the informative set U may not contain the optimal arm with high enough probability, making the decomposition ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Bayesian bootstrapping provides a practical approximation to the exact posterior sampling required by informed Thompson sampling.
- Mechanism: By perturbing the loss function used to compute the MAP estimate with random weights and noise, the algorithm can approximate samples from the posterior distribution without the computational complexity of exact Bayesian inference.
- Core assumption: The perturbed MAP estimates serve as good approximations to posterior samples for Thompson sampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1]: "We adapt the existing approximate-TS approach based on Bayesian bootstrapping... The key idea is to perturb the loss function for the maximum a posterior (MAP) estimate and use the point estimate as a surrogate for the exact posterior sample."
  - [section 6.4]: "We consider the d = 2 case where we can compute a nearly exact posterior distribution by a brute-force method... We see that the regret of iTS is very close to that of Grid-TS"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers discuss demonstration quality but not specifically Bayesian bootstrapping for Thompson sampling.

- Break condition: If the perturbations are not sufficient to capture the posterior uncertainty, or if the optimization landscape is too complex for the MAP estimate to be representative, the approximation quality may degrade significantly.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the regret reduction achieved by informed Thompson Sampling scale with the number of arms K in the multi-armed bandit setting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives a prior-dependent Bayesian regret bound that shows regret reduction depends on the expert's competence level, but the exact scaling with K is not fully characterized in the main theoretical results.
- Why unresolved: The analysis focuses on the effect of the expert's competence parameters (β and λ) but does not provide a clear characterization of how the regret bound scales with the number of arms K.
- What evidence would resolve it: A more detailed analysis of the regret bound showing its explicit dependence on K, possibly through additional experiments varying the number of arms while keeping other parameters fixed.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the Bayesian bootstrapping approximation method be extended to handle more complex environments beyond linear bandits, such as contextual bandits or Markov Decision Processes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the Bayesian bootstrapping approach is computationally challenging for exact posterior sampling but does not explore its applicability to more complex environments.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on linear bandits and does not investigate whether the Bayesian bootstrapping technique can be generalized to other settings where posterior sampling is more challenging.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness of Bayesian bootstrapping in more complex environments, or theoretical analysis showing how the approximation quality might degrade in these settings.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is the informed Thompson Sampling algorithm to misspecification of the expert's competence parameters when the true expert policy is not exactly a softmax distribution?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows robustness to model misspecification in experiments but does not provide a theoretical analysis of how sensitive the algorithm is to incorrect estimates of β and λ.
- Why unresolved: While empirical results suggest some robustness, the theoretical guarantees for the algorithm's performance under misspecified competence parameters are not established.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis characterizing the degradation in performance as a function of the degree of misspecification in the competence parameters, possibly extending the regret bound analysis to account for this uncertainty.

### Open Question 4

- What evidence would resolve it: A complexity analysis showing how the time and space requirements of the algorithm scale with N, possibly accompanied by experiments demonstrating the trade-off between approximation quality and computational cost.

## Limitations
- The algorithm's performance heavily depends on accurate estimation of the expert's competence parameters, and misspecification can lead to worse performance than uninformed methods.
- The Bayesian bootstrapping approximation introduces additional uncertainty about posterior sampling quality, particularly in high-dimensional settings where exact computation is infeasible.
- The information-theoretic regret decomposition assumes the existence of an (1-ε)-informative set that may be difficult to construct when offline data is limited or the expert's competence is low.

## Confidence

- **High**: The core mechanism of using Bayes' rule to construct an informative prior from demonstration data is theoretically sound and well-established in Bayesian statistics.
- **Medium**: The information-theoretic regret decomposition provides theoretical insight, but practical implementation challenges may limit its effectiveness.
- **Medium**: The Bayesian bootstrapping approximation method is practical but the approximation quality depends on problem-specific factors.
