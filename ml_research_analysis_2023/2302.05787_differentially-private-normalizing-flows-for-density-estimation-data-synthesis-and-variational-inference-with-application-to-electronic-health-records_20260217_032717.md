# Differentially Private Normalizing Flows for Density Estimation, Data Synthesis, and Variational Inference with Application to Electronic Health Records

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05787
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05787
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: DP-NF can generate privacy-preserving synthetic data with reasonable utility for downstream learning tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for generating privacy-preserving synthetic data and performing privacy-preserving variational inference using normalizing flows with differential privacy guarantees. The authors apply their approach to electronic health records containing sensitive patient information, demonstrating that their method can produce useful synthetic data for downstream learning tasks while maintaining reasonable privacy guarantees. The work also reveals that privacy-preserving VI via DP-NF using standard free energy bounds can significantly distort correlation structures in variational approximations.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a framework that combines normalizing flows with differential privacy through gradient noise injection and clipping. The approach uses Masked Autoregressive Flows (MAF) for tractable density estimation and implements Gaussian differential privacy for rigorous privacy accounting. The method is applied to both synthetic data generation and variational inference tasks, with experiments conducted on EHR datasets containing mixed attribute types and missing values.

## Key Results
- DP-NF generates synthetic data that maintains reasonable utility for downstream classification and regression tasks
- Privacy-preserving VI via DP-NF using free energy bounds produces variational approximations with significantly altered correlation structures
- Alternative loss functions based on Wasserstein distance may better preserve correlation structure than KL divergence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Privacy-preserving synthetic data generation via DP-NF preserves utility when the privacy loss parameter μ is reasonably large. The algorithm injects Gaussian noise into gradients during training, balancing privacy guarantees with utility preservation. Larger μ allows more noise tolerance while maintaining reasonable density estimation accuracy. Core assumption: EHR dataset structure allows effective density estimation even with missing data and mixed attribute types.

### Mechanism 2
Privacy-preserving VI via DP-NF using free energy bound loss produces variational approximations with significantly altered correlation structures. Noise injection during gradient optimization increases posterior variance estimates. The KL divergence minimization drives the variational distribution toward high-variance regions to maintain similarity despite privacy constraints. Core assumption: Free energy bound as loss function is sensitive to variance inflation from DP noise.

### Mechanism 3
Alternative dissimilarity metrics like Wasserstein distance can preserve correlation structure better than KL divergence in privacy-preserving VI. Wasserstein distance is more robust to variance inflation because it measures distributional differences in a geometric manner rather than through density ratios. Core assumption: The geometry of Wasserstein distance provides stability against noise-induced variance changes.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data preprocessing -> NF layers -> DP noise injection -> gradient clipping -> optimizer -> privacy accounting -> downstream tasks
- **Critical path**: Data preprocessing -> DP-NF training -> synthetic data generation -> downstream learning/VI
- **Design tradeoffs**: Privacy vs. utility (μ parameter), computational cost vs. accuracy (iterations, batch size), model complexity vs. privacy preservation
- **Failure signatures**: Training divergence (too much noise), poor downstream accuracy (utility-privacy tradeoff), unstable correlations (loss function sensitivity)
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train DP-NF on synthetic data with varying μ to observe utility-privacy tradeoff
  2. Compare KL divergence vs. Wasserstein distance loss functions in DP-NF VI
  3. Test DP-NF VI on simple synthetic datasets with known correlation structures

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the correlation structure in variational distributions change when using different dissimilarity metrics (e.g., Wasserstein distance vs. KL divergence) in DP-NF-VI, and can this be mathematically characterized? The paper suggests alternative loss functions may improve results but does not provide mathematical characterization of how different dissimilarity metrics affect correlation structure.

### Open Question 2
How does the choice of privacy loss parameter (μ) affect the trade-off between privacy and utility in downstream learning tasks based on privacy-preserving synthetic data generated via DP-NF? The paper provides empirical results for a specific dataset but lacks a general theoretical framework for choosing optimal μ across different tasks.

### Open Question 3
How can the performance of DP-NF-VI be improved for small datasets with a large number of attributes and missing values, such as the EHR dataset used in this study? The authors encountered difficulties with their EHR dataset but do not provide solutions for improving performance on such challenging data.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation on diverse EHR datasets with varying missingness patterns
- Lack of rigorous theoretical justification for Wasserstein distance superiority
- No systematic evaluation across a broader range of privacy budgets

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Basic DP-NF mechanism for synthetic data generation, fundamental relationship between μ and privacy-utility tradeoff
- **Medium Confidence**: Correlation structure preservation claims, Wasserstein distance recommendations
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about alternative loss functions without empirical validation, generalization to other EHR datasets

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate DP-NF performance across multiple EHR datasets with varying missingness patterns and attribute distributions to assess generalizability

3. Verify the claimed privacy guarantees using composition theorems and track-and-check frameworks to ensure μ-GDP guarantees translate to meaningful (ε,δ)-DP bounds for practical applications
