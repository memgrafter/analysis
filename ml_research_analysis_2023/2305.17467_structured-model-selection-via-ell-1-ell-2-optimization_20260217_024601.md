# Structured model selection via $\ell_1-\ell_2$ optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17467
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17467
- Reference count: 8
- This work addresses automated model selection for dynamical systems from undersampled and noisy spatiotemporal data using nonconvex $\ell_1-\ell_2$ sparse optimization.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method for automated model selection of dynamical systems using nonconvex $\ell_1-\ell_2$ sparse optimization. The approach identifies structured governing equations by fitting candidate functions to undersampled and noisy spatiotemporal data. Theoretical analysis establishes recovery guarantees using a Bernstein-like inequality and coherence condition, while numerical experiments on viscous Burgers' equation and reaction-diffusion systems demonstrate superior performance compared to $\ell_1$ optimization.

## Method Summary
The method uses ADMM to solve a constrained $\ell_1-\ell_2$ optimization problem for model selection. A dictionary matrix is constructed using Legendre polynomials with cyclic structure assumptions to reduce dimensionality. The optimization promotes sparsity in the coefficient vector, which is then used to identify governing equations from spatiotemporal data. Theoretical guarantees rely on the dictionary forming a random sampling matrix of a bounded orthonormal system under uniform initial conditions.

## Key Results
- ℓ1 − ℓ2 optimization outperforms ℓ1 in numerical experiments for coefficient recovery
- Stable recovery with bounded error is theoretically guaranteed under coherence conditions
- The method successfully identifies governing equations for viscous Burgers' equation and two reaction-diffusion systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ℓ1 − ℓ2 optimization promotes sparsity more effectively than ℓ1 alone by penalizing both the ℓ1 and ℓ2 norms of the coefficient vector.
- Mechanism: The level curves of ℓ1 − ℓ2 are closer to the ℓ0 level curves (which directly count nonzeros) than those of ℓ1 alone. This geometric property makes ℓ1 − ℓ2 better at identifying truly sparse solutions.
- Core assumption: The underlying coefficient vector is sparse, and the dictionary matrix satisfies coherence conditions that allow stable recovery.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ℓ1 − ℓ2 optimization outperforms the ℓ1 counterpart in numerical experiments."
  - [section] "It is shown in Lou et al (2015) that the level curves of ℓ1 − ℓ2 are closer to ℓ0 than those of ℓ1, which implies that minimizing (P 1−2,σ) is theoretically better than minimizing (P1,σ) to promote sparsity."
- Break condition: If the coherence condition is violated (e.g., if the dictionary is too coherent), the ℓ1 − ℓ2 recovery guarantees no longer hold.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The cyclic structure assumption reduces the dimensionality of the problem by allowing recovery from a subset of measurements.
- Mechanism: If the governing equation satisfies the cyclic condition fj(u1,...,un) = fi(uj−i+1,...,un,u1,...,uj−i+n), then the problem reduces to finding just the first column of the coefficient matrix C, instead of the full matrix.
- Core assumption: The dynamical system has cyclic structure (e.g., Lorenz 96 system or discretized PDEs with periodic boundaries).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The physical meaning behind the cyclic structure relates to the invariance of a dynamics to (spatial) location."
  - [section] "The standard discretization of the one-way wave equation: ˙uj = 1/2h (uj+1 − uj−1), j = 1, 2, ..., n, with periodic conditions... Each component of f has the same structure, and is invariant to the index j."
- Break condition: If the dynamics lack cyclic structure, this dimensionality reduction fails and the full coefficient matrix must be recovered.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The Legendre basis provides a bounded orthonormal system (BOS) that enables probabilistic recovery guarantees.
- Mechanism: By sampling initial conditions from the uniform distribution on [−1,1]^n, the Legendre polynomials form a BOS. This allows the use of Bernstein-like inequalities to establish high-probability recovery bounds.
- Core assumption: Initial data is i.i.d. sampled from uniform distribution on [−1,1]^n, and the dictionary is constructed from Legendre polynomials.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "An important example of BOSs is the Legendre polynomials... Upon normalization, the Legendre polynomials Li, i = 0, 1, 2, ..., form a system of orthonormal polynomials with respect to the Lebesgue measure dx/2 on [−1, 1]."
  - [section] "To build a random sampling matrix associated to the Legendre polynomials, assume that the initial data u(t0) of the dynamics (5) are drawn i.i.d. from the uniform distribution [−1, 1]n."
- Break condition: If the initial data distribution differs significantly from uniform, the BOS property may not hold and the theoretical guarantees fail.

- Critical path: Data generation → Dictionary construction → ℓ1 − ℓ2 optimization → Evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Localization and restriction reduce dictionary size but may miss nonlocal interactions
  - Higher-order Legendre polynomials increase expressive power but also increase dimensionality
  - ADMM convergence depends on parameter tuning (λ, τ)

- Failure signatures:
  - High coherence in dictionary → recovery fails
  - Insufficient number of bursts or samples → recovery fails
  - Non-cyclic dynamics → wrong structure assumed
  - Poor choice of basis → BOS property violated

## Limitations

- The cyclic structure assumption is restrictive and limits applicability to specific classes of dynamical systems.
- The theoretical guarantees depend on uniform sampling of initial conditions, which may not be practical for real-world applications.
