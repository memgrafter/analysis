# Evaluating Temporal Observation-Based Causal Discovery Techniques Applied to Road Driver Behaviour

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00064
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00064

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates 10 contemporary observational temporal causal discovery techniques on real-world and synthetic autonomous driving data to assess their ability to detect causal relationships between agents. The methods are tested on a simple convoy scenario where one vehicle's behavior causes another's, with a third independent vehicle as a control. The results show that while some methods perform well on synthetic data, they struggle significantly on real-world data, with the best method (DYNOTEARS) achieving only 0.565 F1 score. The paper highlights the challenges of causal non-stationarity and sparsity in real-world driving scenarios, suggesting that either significant improvements to existing methods or new approaches are needed for effective causal discovery in autonomous driving.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates 10 temporal observational causal discovery methods (including Granger causality variants, DYNOTEARS, PCMCI, TCDF, VarLiNGAM) on real-world Lyft Level 5 Prediction and High-D datasets, plus synthetic convoy scenarios. Methods are applied with varying significance thresholds (alpha 0.001-0.1) and maximum time lags (2.5s, 3.6s, 4.9s) to detect the ground truth causal relationship (c0→c1) in scenes where one vehicle follows another in a convoy formation. Performance is measured using F1 score, precision, and recall, comparing discovered graphs against ground truth across 50 Lyft scenes, 3395 High-D scenes, and synthetic data.

## Key Results
- DYNOTEARS achieves the highest real-world performance with 0.565 F1 score, while other methods struggle significantly
- All methods perform near-perfectly (F1 approaching 1.0) on synthetic data with consistent reaction times
- Granger causality-based methods show consistent limitations on real-world driving data due to their assumption of linear relationships
- Real-world driving data exhibits causal sparsity and non-stationarity that severely impacts method performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DYNOTEARS outperforms other methods because it uses differentiable acyclicity constraints in the score function, enabling end-to-end optimization of causal graph structure.
- Mechanism: DYNOTEARS defines a loss function that combines data fit with a differentiable acyclicity penalty, allowing gradient-based optimization to search for a DAG structure that maximizes likelihood while avoiding cycles.
- Core assumption: The causal structure can be represented as a weighted adjacency matrix A, and optimizing A via gradient descent yields a valid DAG.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The best method (DYNOTEARS) achieving only 0.565 F1 score"
  - [section] "DYNOTEARS defines a new metric for this purpose... after optimising A we can then construct a causal graph by thresholding each element ai,j t′ within A"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence about DYNOTEARS mechanisms in corpus
- Break condition: If the differentiable acyclicity constraint is not tight enough, the optimization may produce graphs with cycles or poor structure.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Granger causality-based methods fail because they assume linear relationships and ignore non-linear behavioral interactions between agents.
- Mechanism: Granger causality methods test whether past values of one variable help predict another variable beyond what the past of the target variable alone can explain, but this linear prediction fails when human driving behavior involves non-linear decision processes.
- Core assumption: The underlying causal relationships are linear and stationary over time.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Granger causality based approaches assume a linear relationship between variables, which should indeed be true of the variables we consider"
  - [section] "While some methods such as MVGC and TiMINO are able to get close to an F1 score of 1.0, the greatest performance we see on real world data is from DYNOTEARS"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus does not discuss Granger causality failure modes in detail
- Break condition: If the causal relationships are non-linear or non-stationary, Granger causality methods will produce spurious results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Real-world driving data suffers from causal sparsity and non-stationarity, making causal discovery difficult for all methods.
- Mechanism: In real driving scenarios, causal interactions between agents are infrequent and the relationships change over time due to varying human reaction times, making it hard for methods to find consistent patterns.
- Core assumption: Causal relationships remain stable within the observation window.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The results show that while some methods perform well on synthetic data, they struggle significantly on real-world data"
  - [section] "the synthetic dataset effectively has stationarity as a result of the agent reaction time always being 0.5s, meanwhile real world drivers feature inconsistent reaction times"
  - [section] "By causal sparsity we refer to the lack of causal interactions exhibited within scenes"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors discuss causality in general but not specifically driving scenarios
- Break condition: If the observation window is too short or contains too few causal interactions, no method can reliably discover the underlying structure.

## Limitations

- The synthetic data used as a baseline represents an idealized convoy scenario with perfectly consistent reaction times, making the performance gap with real data difficult to interpret
- The ground truth evaluation focuses only on single c0→c1 relationships, not capturing the complexity of real multi-agent interactions
- The High-D dataset is limited to 3395 scenes from a single geographic location, potentially not representing diverse driving conditions

## Confidence

- High confidence: DYNOTEARS outperforms other methods on real data, and Granger causality methods show consistent limitations on real-world driving data
- Medium confidence: Causal sparsity and non-stationarity are the primary challenges for real-world performance, though alternative explanations exist
- Low confidence: The magnitude of the performance gap between synthetic and real data accurately reflects algorithmic limitations versus data difficulty

## Next Checks

1. **Ground truth expansion**: Test methods on scenarios with known multi-edge ground truth graphs (not just single c0→c1 relationships) to verify that methods can detect more complex causal structures in real data.

2. **Parameter sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the maximum time lag and significance thresholds across a wider range to determine if current parameter choices are suboptimal, particularly for methods that performed poorly on real data.

3. **Data diversity validation**: Evaluate the same methods on driving datasets from multiple geographic locations and conditions (urban vs highway, different countries) to determine if the observed limitations are specific to the Dresden dataset or represent universal challenges in real-world driving causal discovery.
