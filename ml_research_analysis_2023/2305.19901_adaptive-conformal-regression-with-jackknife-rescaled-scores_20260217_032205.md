# Adaptive Conformal Regression with Jackknife+ Rescaled Scores

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19901
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19901
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Rescaled conformal scores with Jackknife+ methodology achieve better local coverage than existing approaches, particularly in low-data regimes

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of achieving local coverage in conformal regression, where global coverage guarantees often fail to capture local error distributions. The authors propose a method that rescales conformal scores using a locally estimated mean of the score distribution, combining split-conformal and Jackknife+ methodologies. This enables the use of calibration data in score estimation without violating exchangeability requirements, while maintaining global coverage guarantees and providing new bounds on local coverage based on mutual information between input data and nonconformity scores.

## Method Summary
The method combines split-conformal prediction with Jackknife+ methodology to create locally adaptive prediction intervals. It rescales absolute errors by an estimate of the local mean error using a Nadaraya-Watson estimator with KNN or RBF kernels. The Jackknife+ approach preserves exchangeability by evaluating each calibration point's score using estimators trained on all other calibration points. Kernel parameters are optimized to minimize mutual information between normalized scores and input features. This framework enables calibration data usage while maintaining theoretical coverage guarantees and improving local adaptivity.

## Key Results
- Global coverage guarantees are preserved while achieving improved local coverage through score rescaling
- Local coverage bounds based on mutual information provide theoretical insight into adaptivity performance
- Method outperforms existing approaches (MADSPLIT, LVD) in low-data regimes and achieves comparable results with more data
- Particularly effective for applications requiring accurate uncertainty quantification with limited samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Rescaling conformal scores by local mean estimates improves adaptivity by reducing dependence on global score distribution
- Mechanism: The method divides each absolute error by an estimate of the local mean error (ˆs̄(X)), creating a normalized score that captures local error scale. This normalization transforms the score into a scale family where the conditional distribution depends primarily on a multiplicative factor rather than absolute magnitude
- Core assumption: The conditional score distribution p(s(X,y)|X) is approximately a scale family - meaning the shape of the distribution is stable across different X values, with only the scale parameter varying
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "rescales conformal scores with an estimate of local score distribution"
  - [section 3.1]: "we can achieve ISCC by rescaling score function s: σqα(X,y) = s(X,y)/q(1-α)(s|X)"
  - [corpus]: Weak - related work focuses on different adaptive approaches but doesn't directly validate this specific scaling mechanism
- Break condition: When the score distribution shape varies significantly with X (non-scale family), the normalized scores will still show strong X-dependence, breaking adaptivity

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using Jackknife+ framework enables use of calibration data for local mean estimation without breaking exchangeability
- Mechanism: By evaluating each calibration point's score using estimators trained on all other calibration points (excluding both the point itself and the test point), the method maintains exchangeability between the test point and calibration set. This allows incorporation of calibration statistics while preserving coverage guarantees
- Core assumption: The Jackknife+ modification of the mean estimator preserves the exchangeability properties needed for conformal coverage while providing sufficient accuracy in mean estimation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "inspired by the Jackknife+ method, which enables the use of calibration data in conformal scores without breaking calibration-test exchangeability"
  - [section 3.2]: "This allows predictions with a single ML model, local error scale measures on the calibration data, while preserving coverage guarantees"
  - [corpus]: Strong - Jackknife+ is well-established for this type of exchangeability preservation
- Break condition: If the mean estimator's variance becomes too large due to limited calibration data, the resulting intervals become unreliable despite theoretical guarantees

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Minimizing mutual information between scores and inputs improves local coverage by reducing score-input dependence
- Mechanism: The method uses mutual information as a tuning criterion for kernel parameters, selecting parameters that minimize the statistical dependence between the normalized scores and input features. This creates scores that are more likely to satisfy the independence condition required for strong local coverage
- Core assumption: There exists a kernel parameterization that can effectively decorrelate the normalized scores from inputs, and that mutual information minimization provides a practical proxy for this optimization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.1]: "Score-input independence is never realized in practice... mutual information can be used instead to place a bound on local coverage"
  - [section 3.2]: "All Kij can be taken from the same family of kernels and have their parameters optimized to minimize the test-score mutual information"
  - [corpus]: Moderate - mutual information as a dependence measure is well-established, but its effectiveness for this specific application needs empirical validation
- Break condition: When the true score-input dependence cannot be reduced through kernel parameterization (e.g., in high-dimensional spaces where relevant features are difficult to capture)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base model trainer -> Score calculator -> Kernel estimator -> Jackknife+ processor -> Quantile finder -> Interval generator
- Critical path: Training → Calibration score computation → Kernel mean estimation → Exchangeable score matrix creation → Quantile determination → Prediction interval generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Calibration data usage: More data improves local estimates but increases computational cost
  - Kernel complexity: Complex kernels may better capture local structure but risk overfitting
  - Score normalization: Mean rescaling assumes scale-family distributions; other normalization may be needed for different score distributions
- Failure signatures:
  - Infinite or extremely large intervals: Indicates poor kernel parameter selection or insufficient local structure capture
  - Systematic undercoverage: Suggests the score normalization doesn't adequately capture local scale
  - Computational inefficiency: Results from poor kernel choice in high-dimensional spaces
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Simple 1D regression test with known heteroscedastic noise to verify local adaptivity
  2. Comparison with MADS PLIT on a small dataset to validate data efficiency claims
  3. High-dimensional synthetic test to evaluate kernel selection and mutual information minimization effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what conditions can we derive PAC bounds for Jackknife+ rescaled conformal scores, given the no-go theorem by Bian and Barber?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions hope to extend the method to a CV+ approach to circumvent the no-go theorem of Barber et al.
- Why unresolved: The paper states that while there's hope for PAC bounds, they haven't been established yet, and extending to CV+ approach remains to be explored
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof showing that PAC bounds can be established for this method, either through CV+ extension or alternative approaches

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we characterize the relationship between score-input mutual information and local coverage guarantees in a way that's both tight and practically useful?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proves a new bound on local coverage based on mutual information between input data and nonconformity scores, but notes this bound is vacuous for low-probability sets
- Why unresolved: While the mutual information bound provides theoretical insight, its practical utility is limited by its vacuousness on low-probability sets and lack of a PAC-style bound
- What evidence would resolve it: A refined bound that remains meaningful across all probability sets, or empirical evidence showing how to use the mutual information metric effectively in practice

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal way to balance kernel tuning complexity against performance gains for Jackknife+ rescaled conformal scores?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions using KNN kernels as a simple approach, but also describes a more complex kernel tuning algorithm (Algorithm S.2) that could provide better performance
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that tuned RBF kernels yield better results with more data, but doesn't provide a comprehensive analysis of the tradeoff between tuning complexity and performance
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing various kernel tuning strategies across different data regimes and dimensionalities, including computational costs and performance metrics

## Limitations
- The scale-family assumption for score distributions may not hold in complex real-world datasets
- Kernel bandwidth selection using mutual information minimization may be computationally prohibitive in high dimensions
- Performance in very low-data regimes (<100 calibration points) has not been extensively tested

## Confidence
- **High**: Theoretical coverage guarantees for global coverage, validity of Jackknife+ exchangeability preservation
- **Medium**: Local coverage bounds based on mutual information, empirical performance claims on real-world datasets
- **Low**: Claims about computational efficiency relative to existing methods in high-dimensional settings
