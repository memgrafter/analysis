# Solving Cosine Similarity Underestimation between High Frequency Words by L2 Norm Discounting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10610
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10610
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: Addresses cosine similarity underestimation between high-frequency words in contextualised embeddings by discounting L2 norms based on word frequency

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical limitation in contextualised token embeddings from masked language models like BERT, where high-frequency words exhibit systematically underestimated cosine similarities due to their inflated L2 norms. The authors propose a frequency-based L2 norm discounting method that learns discounting parameters through Monte-Carlo Bayesian Optimisation on the WiC dataset. Their approach successfully reduces the underestimation bias, improving binary classification accuracy on WiC dev data from 66.67% to 71.00%, with a 12% improvement in recall for retrieving same-meaning instances.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a method to correct cosine similarity underestimation between high-frequency words by discounting the L2 norm of contextualised word embeddings based on word frequency. The discounting parameters are learned using Monte-Carlo Bayesian Optimisation on the WiC dataset. The method adjusts cosine similarity calculations by applying frequency-specific scaling factors to the L2 norms, thereby reducing the bias that causes high-frequency words to appear less similar than they actually are in semantic contexts.

## Key Results
- Improves binary classification accuracy on WiC dev data from 66.67% to 71.00%
- Achieves 12% improvement in recall (from 74.7% to 83.7%) for retrieving same-meaning instances
- Demonstrates reduction in cosine similarity underestimation for high-frequency words through frequency-based L2 norm discounting

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by recognizing that contextualised embeddings from models like BERT exhibit a correlation between word frequency and L2 norm magnitude. High-frequency words tend to have inflated L2 norms, which artificially reduces their cosine similarity scores when compared to other words. By discounting the L2 norm based on frequency, the method normalizes these embeddings, allowing cosine similarities to more accurately reflect semantic relationships rather than being biased by frequency effects.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Contextualised Embeddings**: Word representations that capture semantic meaning based on surrounding context; needed because static embeddings fail to capture polysemy and context-dependent meanings.
2. **Cosine Similarity**: Measures angular distance between vectors; quick check - values range from -1 to 1, with higher values indicating greater similarity.
3. **L2 Norm**: Euclidean length of a vector; needed as a measure of vector magnitude that correlates with frequency in contextualised embeddings.
4. **Monte-Carlo Bayesian Optimisation**: Probabilistic optimization technique for hyperparameter tuning; needed to efficiently search the parameter space for optimal discounting factors.
5. **WiC Dataset**: Word-in-Context dataset for evaluating word sense disambiguation; provides binary classification of whether words have the same meaning in different contexts.
6. **Masked Language Models**: Models like BERT that predict masked tokens based on context; the source of contextualised embeddings used in this study.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input embeddings -> L2 norm calculation -> Frequency lookup -> Discounting parameters -> Discounted L2 norms -> Cosine similarity calculation -> Output similarity scores

**Critical Path**: The most critical path is the frequency-based L2 norm discounting, as this directly addresses the core problem of underestimation. The Monte-Carlo Bayesian Optimisation for learning discounting parameters is also crucial for achieving optimal performance.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades computational complexity (additional frequency lookups and discounting calculations) for improved semantic similarity accuracy. The choice of Monte-Carlo Bayesian Optimisation over simpler optimization methods balances exploration of parameter space against computational efficiency.

**Failure Signatures**: The method may fail when the linear relationship between L2 norm and frequency breaks down, particularly for domain-specific vocabularies or non-English languages. It may also underperform when frequency distributions differ significantly from the training data used for learning discounting parameters.

**First Experiments**: 1) Verify the correlation between L2 norm and frequency across different frequency ranges, 2) Test discounting parameters on held-out data to ensure generalization, 3) Compare performance against baseline cosine similarity without discounting across multiple frequency bins.

## Limitations
- The linear relationship between L2 norm and word frequency may not hold across all contexts or model architectures
- Monte-Carlo Bayesian Optimisation is computationally intensive and may not generalise well to datasets with different frequency distributions
- Evaluation focuses solely on the WiC dataset, limiting generalisability to other semantic similarity tasks

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- The empirical observation that high-frequency words exhibit reduced cosine similarity scores in contextualised embeddings is well-supported by the data
- The improvement in binary classification accuracy on WiC dev data (66.67% to 71.00%) is statistically significant and reproducible

**Medium Confidence:**
- The linear relationship between L2 norm and word frequency may not be universal across different embedding models or domains
- The discounting method's effectiveness on other semantic similarity tasks beyond WiC remains uncertain without further validation

**Low Confidence:**
- The long-term impact of L2 norm discounting on downstream tasks that rely on contextualised embeddings is unknown
- The method's performance on low-resource languages or specialised vocabularies is not evaluated
