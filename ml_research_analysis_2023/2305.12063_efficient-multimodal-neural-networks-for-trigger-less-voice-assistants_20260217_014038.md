# Efficient Multimodal Neural Networks for Trigger-less Voice Assistants

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12063
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12063
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: 90% relative reduction in both False Reject Rate and False Accept Rate

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a neural network-based audio-gesture multimodal fusion system for trigger-less voice assistants on smartwatches. By combining gesture and audio data, the proposed solution outperforms traditional Finite State Machines in understanding the temporal correlation between modalities, generalizing to various environments, and maintaining low power consumption for on-device deployment. The key innovation is a late fusion approach that uses logits from unimodal detectors, processed by a lightweight GRU-based neural policy, achieving significant accuracy improvements while simplifying the development process.

## Method Summary
The system employs a sequential training pipeline where unimodal speech and gesture detectors are trained separately on modality-specific datasets, then their outputs are fused using a GRU-based neural policy. Gesture features are extracted from 3-axis accelerometer data, while audio features use 40-dimensional mel filter banks. Two fusion approaches are explored: softmax neural fusion and logit neural fusion, with the latter incorporating raw model outputs directly to preserve dynamic range. The approach addresses the challenge of triggering voice assistants without explicit commands by leveraging the natural correlation between user gestures and speech initiation.

## Key Results
- 90% relative reduction in both False Reject Rate and False Accept Rate compared to traditional methods
- Outperforms FSM-based systems in accuracy, generalization, and resource efficiency
- Achieves real-time performance suitable for low-power smartwatch deployment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Late fusion of gesture and audio modalities outperforms early fusion due to loose coupling between the signals. The unimodal detectors process gesture and audio independently, generating per-frame probability or logit outputs that are then merged and fed into a lightweight GRU-based neural policy that learns temporal patterns without being constrained by cross-modal dependencies in early layers.

### Mechanism 2
Using logits instead of softmax probabilities in the fusion network improves accuracy by preserving dynamic range. The unimodal detectors output logits (raw scores) rather than softmax probabilities, which are passed directly to the GRU-based neural policy, avoiding the compression and saturation effects of softmax and enhancing the model's ability to distinguish subtle activation patterns.

### Mechanism 3
The sequential training pipeline (unimodal → fusion) maximizes data utilization and reduces overfitting risk. Each unimodal detector is trained on its full modality-specific dataset, even when the other modality is missing, and the fusion model is then trained on the aligned outputs of these trained unimodal models, enabling full use of the data without requiring paired multimodal labels during early stages.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Audio featurizer (40-dim mel filter banks) → Speech detector (1DCNN, 2-class streaming) → Logits/probabilities
  - Gesture featurizer (31-dim statistical features) → Gesture detector (1DCNN, 4-class streaming) → Logits/probabilities
  - Multimodal fusion (GRU-based neural policy, 2-class) → Trigger decision

- Critical path:
  - Raw audio/accelerometer → featurizers → unimodal detectors → aligned logits/probs → neural policy → trigger decision
  - Latency dominated by unimodal inference + GRU forward pass (~1.4ms worst case)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Late fusion vs. early fusion: Late fusion chosen for loose coupling and device constraints; early fusion could improve cross-modal interaction learning but increases complexity and memory use.
  - Logits vs. softmax: Logits preserve dynamic range but are noisier; softmax stabilizes but may compress useful signal.
  - GRU vs. LSTM: GRU is lighter and faster, suitable for on-device; LSTM may capture longer dependencies but at higher computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - High FAR in noisy environments → unimodal detectors over-triggering; consider stricter thresholds or more negative training data.
  - High FRR in complex gestures → gesture detector missing subtle raise patterns; augment training with more gesture variability.
  - Degraded accuracy after quantization → logit-based fusion sensitive to FP16 rounding; validate with quantization-aware training.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train unimodal speech and gesture detectors separately on their respective datasets, evaluate per-frame accuracy.
  2. Implement and train the GRU-based neural policy using softmax fusion; measure EER and on-device latency.
  3. Switch to logit fusion, retrain the neural policy, and compare accuracy, latency, and memory usage to softmax version.
