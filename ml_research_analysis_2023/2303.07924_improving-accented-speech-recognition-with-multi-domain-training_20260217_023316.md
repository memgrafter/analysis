# Improving Accented Speech Recognition with Multi-Domain Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.07924
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07924
- **Authors:** Not specified
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Multi-domain training on French accents reduces error rates by up to 25% on African and Belgian accents

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of automatic speech recognition (ASR) robustness to accented speech by proposing a multi-domain training approach. The method involves training ASR models on datasets containing different French accents, including the use of data augmentation to increase accented training data. The experiments demonstrate that mixing data from various accents during training leads to significant improvements in recognition accuracy, both for in-domain accents and previously unseen out-of-domain accents.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves creating training sets that mix data from different French accents, leveraging data augmentation techniques to expand the amount of accented training data available. The approach uses multi-domain fine-tuning where the model is trained simultaneously on multiple accent-specific datasets rather than being optimized for a single accent. This training strategy aims to improve the model's ability to generalize across different accent variations while maintaining performance on familiar accents.

## Key Results
- Multi-domain training achieves up to 25% relative reduction in error rates on African and Belgian French accents
- Improvements observed for both in-domain and out-of-domain accent recognition
- The approach demonstrates better robustness compared to single-domain training methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Multi-domain training improves accented speech recognition by exposing the model to diverse phonetic variations and pronunciation patterns during training. By mixing data from different accents, the model learns to identify underlying speech patterns that transcend specific accent characteristics, making it more robust to variations in pronunciation and prosody.

## Limitations
- Focus exclusively on French accents limits generalizability to other languages and accent types
- Data augmentation methods are used but their specific contribution to performance improvements is not isolated or quantified
- Evaluation limited to Word Error Rate without deeper analysis of error types or linguistic patterns

## Confidence
**Multi-domain training effectiveness (High):** Well-supported by 25% relative error reduction with clear baseline comparisons
**Data augmentation contribution (Medium):** Used in methodology but specific impact not quantified or isolated
**Generalizability across accents (Low):** Based on only two additional French accents; limited scope for assessing broader applicability
