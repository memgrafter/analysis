# Explainable AI for Time Series via Virtual Inspection Layers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06365
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06365
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Virtual inspection layers enable interpretable explanations for time series models by propagating relevance attributions to frequency/time-frequency domains

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces virtual inspection layers to enhance explainability for time series models by transforming signals to interpretable representations like frequency domain via DFT or time-frequency via STFT. The approach uses closed-form expressions to propagate Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) attributions through these transformations, enabling human-interpretable explanations without retraining models. Experiments demonstrate the method's ability to reveal model strategies, detect Clever Hans behavior, and achieve near-perfect relevance localization scores on synthetic data with ground truth.

## Method Summary
The method transforms time series data to interpretable representations using linear invertible transformations (DFT or STFT), then propagates LRP relevance scores to these domains through a virtual inspection layer. This layer consists of linear T and T⁻¹ transformations attached to the input, allowing relevance computation in interpretable domains while the model operates on original time series. The approach preserves total relevance through conservation properties and can reveal differences between models trained on different input domains.

## Key Results
- DFT-LRP achieves near-perfect relevance localization scores on synthetic data with ground truth
- For real audio data, DFT-LRP produces most faithful explanations compared to other methods
- Virtual inspection layer reveals differences between models trained on time vs. frequency domains
- Method successfully detects spurious correlations and Clever Hans behavior in models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transforming relevance scores from time domain to frequency domain preserves total relevance
- Mechanism: DFT-LRP uses closed-form expressions for relevance propagation through linear transformations, ensuring relevance conservation at each layer
- Core assumption: The DFT and inverse DFT are linear invertible transformations, allowing relevance to be propagated through them while maintaining the total sum of relevance scores
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We present a closed-form expression for relevance propagation through DFT and STDFT"
  - [section]: "The total relevance in frequency domain equals the total relevance in time domain, i.e. ∑kRk = ∑nRn"
- Break condition: If the transformation T is not invertible or the signal contains components that break the linearity assumption (e.g., non-linear effects), relevance conservation may fail

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Virtual inspection layer enables interpretable explanations for time series without retraining
- Mechanism: By attaching linear layers that perform T and T⁻¹ transformations to the input, relevance scores can be computed in the interpretable domain while the model operates on the original time series
- Core assumption: The transformation T makes the time series interpretable (e.g., frequency domain) and the inverse transformation T⁻¹ reconstructs the original signal without loss of information
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We put forward a virtual inspection layer, that transforms the time series to an interpretable representation and allows to propagate relevance attributions to this representation via local XAI methods like layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP)"
  - [section]: "Without the need to retrain the model on{˜x}, we can now quantify the relevance of˜xi by attaching the inverse transform to the network"
- Break condition: If the transformation T is not sufficiently interpretable or if the model relies heavily on non-linear interactions that are not preserved by the transformation, explanations may remain unclear

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DFT-LRP can reveal differences in model strategies between time and frequency domain
- Mechanism: By comparing relevance scores in time and frequency domain, one can identify which features (time points or frequencies) the model considers important for its decisions
- Core assumption: Models trained on different input domains (time vs. frequency) learn different classification strategies that can be revealed through DFT-LRP
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We showcase how DFT-LRP reveals differences in the classification strategies of models trained in different domains (e.g., time vs. frequency domain)"
  - [section]: "Here, we compare the 1d CNN sex audio classifier operating on the raw waveforms of the AudioMNIST dataset (time model), to a model of the architecture, but trained on absolute values of the signal in frequency domain (frequency model)"
- Break condition: If the models trained on different domains learn similar strategies or if the frequency domain representation does not capture the relevant information for the task, the differences may not be apparent

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Time series -> Virtual inspection layer (T, T⁻¹) -> LRP -> Relevance scores in interpretable domain
- Critical path:
  1. Transform time series to interpretable representation using T
  2. Compute relevance scores using LRP
  3. Propagate relevance scores through T⁻¹ to interpretable domain
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Choice of transformation T: Different transformations may provide different levels of interpretability for different types of time series
  - Window size and overlap for STFT: Trade-off between time and frequency resolution
  - Choice of LRP rule: Different rules may emphasize different aspects of the model's decision-making process
- Failure signatures:
  - Relevance scores are not conserved between time and interpretable domain
  - Explanations are not interpretable despite using a transformation T
  - Model strategies are not revealed when comparing time and frequency domain explanations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply DFT-LRP to a simple synthetic dataset with known ground truth and verify relevance conservation
  2. Compare DFT-LRP explanations to standard LRP explanations for a time series classifier and assess interpretability
  3. Train models on the same dataset in time and frequency domain and use DFT-LRP to reveal differences in their strategies

## Limitations
- The approach relies on linear transformations (DFT/STFT) which may not capture non-linear relationships the model actually uses
- Quality of explanations depends heavily on choice of LRP rules and hyperparameters requiring domain-specific tuning
- Effectiveness for complex non-stationary signals remains to be fully validated

## Confidence
- High confidence: The mathematical framework for relevance propagation through DFT and STFT is well-established and the conservation properties are theoretically sound
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of explanations for real-world time series data, particularly for complex non-stationary signals
- Medium confidence: The ability to detect Clever Hans behavior, as this depends on the specific nature of spurious correlations in the data
