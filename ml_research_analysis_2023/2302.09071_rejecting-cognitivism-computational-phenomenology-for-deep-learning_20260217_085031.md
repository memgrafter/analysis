# Rejecting Cognitivism: Computational Phenomenology for Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.09071
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09071
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Proposes non-representationalist framework interpreting deep learning models through computational phenomenology

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for interpreting deep learning models through computational phenomenology, rejecting the dominant neuro-representationalist view that neural networks encode symbolic representations of external entities. Instead, it argues that deep learning models should be understood as capturing lived experience and habits rather than mapping to an objective external world. The framework establishes a dialogue between first-person phenomenological descriptions and computational model mechanisms, providing a conceptual toolkit for understanding non-decomposable cognitive processes in deep learning.

## Method Summary
The paper develops a non-representationalist framework that establishes dialogue between phenomenological descriptions and computational model mechanisms. It systematically analyzes perception, imagination, and language through the lens of sedimentation and re-activation of experiences, arguing that deep learning models capture habits rather than symbolic representations. The approach provides a conceptual bridge between phenomenological insights about consciousness and computational models, offering an alternative interpretation framework that moves beyond traditional representational approaches.

## Key Results
- Proposes a non-representationalist framework for interpreting deep learning models
- Demonstrates how models can be understood as capturing lived experience rather than symbolic representations
- Provides conceptual tools for understanding non-decomposable cognitive processes in deep learning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by establishing a conceptual bridge between phenomenological insights about lived experience and computational mechanisms in deep learning models. Rather than viewing neural networks as encoding symbolic representations of external entities, it interprets them as sedimenting experiences into habits that can be re-activated. This approach aligns with phenomenological observations about consciousness and world-intertwining, providing a more nuanced understanding of how deep learning models process and represent information.

## Foundational Learning

**Phenomenological Description**: Understanding lived experience from first-person perspective
- Why needed: Provides conceptual foundation for non-representationalist interpretation
- Quick check: Can describe everyday experiences without referring to external objects

**Sedimentation**: Process of experiences becoming habits over time
- Why needed: Explains how models develop stable patterns without symbolic encoding
- Quick check: Can identify examples of learned behaviors becoming automatic

**Re-activation**: Ability to bring past experiences back into consciousness
- Why needed: Provides mechanism for understanding model recall and generation
- Quick check: Can distinguish between fresh processing and memory retrieval

**World-Intertwining**: Concept that consciousness and world are inseparable
- Why needed: Challenges traditional representationalist assumptions
- Quick check: Can explain experiences without separating subject from object

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Model Mechanisms -> Phenomenological Concepts -> Interpretation Framework
**Critical Path**: Experience -> Sedimentation -> Habit Formation -> Re-activation
**Design Tradeoffs**: Phenomenological accuracy vs computational tractability
**Failure Signatures**: Over-reliance on symbolic interpretation, ignoring habit formation
**First Experiments**:
1. Analyze model attention patterns through sedimentation lens
2. Compare habit formation across different architectures
3. Test re-activation patterns in sequence generation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper acknowledges significant uncertainties in bridging phenomenological insights with computational models. Key questions include: how to empirically validate the phenomenological framework, how to develop quantitative metrics for concepts like sedimentation and re-activation, and how to apply the framework across different deep learning architectures. The paper also questions whether the proposed framework can provide more actionable insights for model debugging compared to existing interpretability methods.

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation of the phenomenological framework
- Difficulty in operationalizing concepts like sedimentation and re-activation
- Unclear applicability across different deep learning architectures
- Primarily conceptual rather than computational approach
