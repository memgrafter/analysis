# Towards Improving the Explainability of Text-based Information Retrieval with Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06974
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06974
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: With ideal entity matcher, KG integration improves sentence retrieval accuracy by 6.96% and base retrieval performance by 0.45% on WIKIQA and Robust04 datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the explainability challenge in information retrieval systems by integrating knowledge graphs to provide structured semantic relationships between entities. The authors propose a general architecture that incorporates KGs into various phases of retrieval, using entity/relationship matching to enhance explanations. Two specific implementations are developed: one for highlighting the most important sentence in retrieved documents, and another for re-ranking documents using explainable features derived from KG connectivity. The approach shows promising improvements in retrieval accuracy when entity linking is ideal, though results depend heavily on entity matching quality.

## Method Summary
The method integrates knowledge graphs into a vector-based information retrieval system by adding entity/relationship matching components. The general architecture takes queries and documents as input, processes them through query preprocessing, candidate generation, and re-ranking stages, with KG integration at each step. Two specific instances are implemented: (1) query expansion for identifying the most important sentence by expanding queries with entity information from KGs, and (2) re-ranking documents using entity relatedness scores derived from KG connectivity. The system uses embedding models (MPNet-base), indexes documents with FAISS, and calculates pair-to-pair relatedness between query and document entities using incoming link overlap in Wikidata.

## Key Results
- With ideal entity matcher, sentence retrieval accuracy improved by 6.96% compared to baseline
- Document ranking performance improved by 0.45% (MAP@20) with KG-based re-ranking
- Results are highly dependent on entity linking quality, with erroneous entity matchers potentially decreasing performance below baseline
- KG integration provides interpretable ranking features and highlights for explainability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge graph integration improves explainability by providing structured semantic relationships between entities mentioned in queries and documents.
- Mechanism: The system uses entity linking and relationship extraction to connect text to KG nodes, then leverages this structured information to generate explanations (e.g., highlighting most important sentences or re-ranking documents).
- Core assumption: The semantic relationships in KGs accurately reflect the relevance relationships between queries and documents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Knowledge graphs record structured information about entities and inherently explainable relationships."
  - [section 2.1] "KGs provide explicit semantic relations between entities, which can serve as explanation, or help clarify the intent of the query."
- Break condition: If entity linking is inaccurate or the KG lacks relevant relationships, the explanations become misleading or unhelpful.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Query expansion using KG entities improves the identification of the most important sentence by reducing semantic ambiguity.
- Mechanism: The query is expanded with entity descriptions, aliases, or related entities from the KG, which provides additional context that helps the sentence retriever identify more relevant sentences.
- Core assumption: The expanded query with KG information better captures the user's intent than the original query.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2] "To avoid semantic ambiguity in the query, we expand the query using entity linking and relationship extraction with a reference KG."
- Break condition: If the entity linker introduces incorrect or irrelevant entities, the expanded query may mislead the retriever and decrease performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Re-ranking documents using query-document entity relatedness scores derived from KG improves explainability by providing interpretable ranking features.
- Mechanism: The system calculates pair-to-pair relatedness between query and document entities using their connectivity in the KG, then uses these scores as explainable features for re-ranking.
- Core assumption: The entity relatedness scores calculated from KG connectivity accurately reflect document relevance to the query.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3] "Entity matching finds how two text passages are related to each other by (1) extracting entities from two passages, and (2) calculating the pair-to-pair relatedness between two types of entities."
- Break condition: If the entity linker fails to identify relevant entities or the KG lacks sufficient connectivity information, the relatedness scores become unreliable for ranking.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the quality of entity linking impact the performance of knowledge graph-enhanced information retrieval systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "the performance of our approach largely depends on the quality of entity matching" and that with an erroneous entity linker, performance can decrease compared to baselines.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that ideal entity matching improves performance, it doesn't investigate the relationship between entity linking quality and retrieval performance across different entity matching methods or quality levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing retrieval performance across a spectrum of entity matching quality levels, from poor to ideal, would establish the relationship between entity linking accuracy and retrieval effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can incorporating structure information from knowledge graphs (such as entity relationships and path information) improve the explainability of information retrieval systems beyond using only entity matching?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note "we leave exploring structure information of the sentence as future work" and suggest investigating how KG structure could be integrated with embedding models.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses entity matching and relatedness scores from KGs but doesn't explore how structural information like entity relationships, path patterns, or graph connectivity could enhance explainability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing retrieval and explanation performance with and without structural KG features would demonstrate the value of incorporating KG structure beyond entity matching.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do knowledge graph-enhanced explainable information retrieval approaches perform in human evaluations compared to traditional methods?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention future work to "evaluate how our approaches influence the explainability of IR system in human's perspective with user study," indicating this hasn't been done yet.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses standard IR metrics (accuracy, MAP, NDCG) but doesn't measure how well users understand or trust the explanations generated by KG-enhanced systems.
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies measuring comprehension, trust, and satisfaction with KG-enhanced explanations versus traditional highlighting or feature-importance methods would provide human-centered evaluation of these approaches.

## Limitations
- The approach's effectiveness is heavily dependent on entity linking quality, which was idealized in experiments but may perform poorly in practice.
- Knowledge graph coverage may be insufficient for specialized domains, limiting the applicability of KG-based explanations.
- The method requires substantial computational overhead for entity linking and KG navigation, potentially making it impractical for real-time systems.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The general architecture for integrating knowledge graphs into IR systems is sound and the theoretical benefits of structured semantic relationships are well-established.
- **Medium confidence**: The specific implementation details for query expansion and re-ranking using entity relatedness scores are reasonable but lack empirical validation with realistic entity linking performance.
- **Low confidence**: The 6.96% improvement in sentence retrieval accuracy assumes "ideal entity matcher" performance, which may not reflect real-world conditions where entity linking errors are common.

## Next Checks

1. Benchmark entity linking accuracy on a sample of queries from WIKIQA and Robust04 datasets using the actual entity linking tool (TagMe or similar) rather than assuming ideal performance.
2. Conduct ablation studies to quantify the contribution of each KG-based feature (query expansion, entity relatedness scores) to overall performance improvements.
3. Test the system's explainability with human evaluators to determine if the generated explanations are actually perceived as helpful and interpretable in practice.
