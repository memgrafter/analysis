# Imitating Graph-Based Planning with Goal-Conditioned Policies

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of sample-efficient goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) for long-horizon tasks. The authors propose PIG (Planning-guided self-Imitation learning for Goal-conditioned policies), which combines graph-based planning with self-imitation learning to distill subgoal-conditioned policies into goal-conditioned policies. The method significantly improves sample efficiency, particularly in complex environments like the Large U-shaped AntMaze where it achieves a 57.41% success rate compared to 19.08% for the baseline. Notably, the trained policy can perform competitively even without a planner at test time, making it practical for scenarios where planning cost is expensive.

## Method Summary
PIG combines graph-based planning with self-imitation learning to improve sample efficiency in long-horizon goal-conditioned reinforcement learning. The core mechanism involves distilling subgoal-conditioned policies into the target-goal-conditioned policy using a novel self-imitation loss term. During execution, the method introduces subgoal skipping to further enhance efficiency. The approach is evaluated on various MuJoCo environments including 2DReach, Reacher, Pusher, and AntMaze tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in sample efficiency and success rates compared to baseline methods.

## Key Results
- PIG achieves 57.41% success rate in Large U-shaped AntMaze environment
- Baseline method achieves only 19.08% success rate in the same environment
- Policy trained with PIG performs competitively even without a planner at test time
- Significant improvement in sample efficiency across all tested MuJoCo environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging graph-based planning to generate high-quality trajectories, then using self-imitation learning to distill these trajectories into a goal-conditioned policy. The self-imitation loss term encourages the policy to mimic successful subgoal-conditioned behaviors, effectively transferring knowledge from the planner to the policy. Subgoal skipping during execution allows the policy to bypass intermediate subgoals when possible, improving efficiency. This combination enables the policy to learn from both successful demonstrations and planner-generated trajectories, resulting in improved sample efficiency and performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Graph-based planner -> Subgoal-conditioned policy -> Goal-conditioned policy (via self-imitation) -> Execution with subgoal skipping

**Critical Path**: Planning phase generates trajectories -> Self-imitation loss trains policy -> Policy executes with subgoal skipping -> Performance evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Higher planning cost during training vs. lower inference cost
- Complexity of graph-based planning vs. policy simplicity
- Trade-off between following subgoals strictly vs. skipping them

**Failure Signatures**:
- Policy fails to generalize when planner assumptions break
- Subgoal skipping causes instability in certain environments
- Self-imitation loss leads to overfitting on planner trajectories
