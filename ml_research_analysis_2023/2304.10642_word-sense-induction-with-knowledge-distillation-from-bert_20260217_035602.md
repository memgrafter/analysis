# Word Sense Induction with Knowledge Distillation from BERT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10642
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10642
- Reference count: 8
- The paper proposes a two-stage method to distill multiple word senses from BERT using knowledge distillation to improve static multi-sense embeddings

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for extracting and modeling multiple word senses from BERT using knowledge distillation. The method operates in two stages: first extracting sense embeddings through linear decomposition of BERT's contextual embeddings, then training a static sense embedding model to mimic BERT's sense distribution via attention-based sense disambiguation. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on word sense induction tasks, with substantial improvements over previous multi-sense embedding methods.

## Method Summary
The method consists of a two-stage process for creating multi-sense embeddings. First, sense embeddings are extracted from BERT by performing linear decomposition on its contextual embeddings, separating them into a sense-agnostic base component and sense-specific components. Second, a sense embedding model is trained to mimic BERT's sense distribution using knowledge distillation, where an attention mechanism is employed to perform sense disambiguation during training. This approach leverages BERT's contextual understanding while producing static embeddings that capture multiple word senses.

## Key Results
- Achieved 1.25x-2.25x improvement in ARI scores over prior multi-sense embeddings when using knowledge distillation
- Demonstrated competitive performance on contextual word similarity tasks
- Showed improved downstream topic modeling performance compared to single-sense embeddings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging BERT's rich contextual understanding while addressing its limitation of not explicitly modeling multiple senses. By extracting sense-specific components through linear decomposition and then training a model to reproduce BERT's sense distributions, the method effectively transfers BERT's nuanced sense understanding into static embeddings that can be used without requiring contextual information at inference time.

## Foundational Learning

**Linear Decomposition**: Breaking down contextual embeddings into base and sense-specific components. Why needed: To separate general word meaning from sense-specific information. Quick check: Verify that decomposed components capture distinct semantic aspects.

**Knowledge Distillation**: Transferring knowledge from a larger model (BERT) to a smaller, more efficient model. Why needed: To capture BERT's nuanced sense understanding in a practical embedding format. Quick check: Ensure student model approximates teacher's output distribution.

**Attention-based Sense Disambiguation**: Using attention mechanisms to identify relevant word senses during training. Why needed: To guide the model in distinguishing between different word senses. Quick check: Verify attention weights correlate with sense appropriateness.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: BERT Contextual Embeddings -> Linear Decomposition -> Sense Embeddings -> Attention-based Disambiguation -> Trained Multi-sense Model

**Critical Path**: BERT extraction → sense decomposition → knowledge distillation training → evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs**: Static embeddings vs contextual computation trade-off; number of senses per word parameter choice; decomposition method selection

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance on words with highly overlapping senses; suboptimal results when sense number parameter is poorly chosen; degraded performance on specialized domains

## Limitations
- Generalizability across different languages and domains remains untested beyond English Wikipedia
- Single linear decomposition method may not capture all semantic nuances for words with highly overlapping senses
- Choice of 128 senses per word appears somewhat arbitrary and may not be optimal for all vocabulary items

## Confidence
- **High** for WSI evaluation benchmarks: Substantial quantitative improvements reported
- **Medium** for downstream task improvements: Topic modeling results are positive but limited in scope
- **Low** for low-resource language claims: Not directly tested in experiments
