## Executive Summary
This comprehensive survey systematically categorizes and analyzes the state-of-the-art adversarial attacks and defenses in machine learning-powered networks, with a particular focus on deep neural networks. The survey classifies attack methods into four main categories: gradient-based, constrained optimization-based, gradient-free (heuristic), and adversarial patch attacks. Defense techniques are categorized into detection/preprocessing and robustness enhancement methods. The survey highlights emerging attack avenues such as search-based, decision-based, and drop-based methods, as well as the development of physical-world adversarial patches. It also identifies key challenges in the field, including balancing defense effectiveness with computational overhead, addressing the root cause of robustness loss, ensuring scalability and generalizability, and reducing dependence on data. The survey concludes by suggesting future research directions to develop more effective and efficient adversarial defenses.

## Method Summary
The survey provides a comprehensive overview of adversarial attack and defense methods in deep learning networks, focusing on deep neural network-based classification models. It describes various attack methods including gradient-based attacks (e.g., FGSM, PGD), constrained optimization-based attacks (e.g., C&W), gradient-free attacks, and adversarial patches. Defense techniques covered include adversarial attack detection, data preprocessing, and robustness enhancement methods. The survey evaluates these methods using metrics such as attack success rate (ASR) and robustness against attacks. ## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Gradient-based attacks like LAFEAT exploit latent features in hidden layers to generate more transferable and computationally efficient adversarial examples.
- Mechanism: By using latent features from intermediate layers (via mappings $h^{(l)}$ and weights $\lambda^{(l)}$) in gradient descent steps, the attack finds adversarial examples that are more aligned with the model's internal representations, bypassing direct pixel-level perturbations.
- Core assumption: The model's latent features contain more semantically meaningful information that, when manipulated, can produce adversarial examples with higher transferability across different models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "LAFEAT, a unified $\ell_\infty$-norm white-box attack algorithm that uses latent features throughout gradient descent steps for computationally efficient attacks"
  - [section]: "Yu et al. [106] discover that certain "robust" models have hidden features that are unexpectedly susceptible to adversarial attacks. They propose latent feature attack (LAFEAT), a unified $\ell_\infty$-norm white-box attack algorithm that uses latent features throughout gradient descent steps for computationally efficient attacks"
  - [corpus]: Weak/no direct evidence; LAFEAT is a recent contribution not covered in the related corpus.
- Break condition: If the latent feature mappings are not well-aligned with the model's decision boundaries or if the model uses architectures where latent features are less semantically meaningful (e.g., very shallow networks).

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Constrained optimization-based attacks like ATTA improve transferability by learning adversarial transformations that capture the most destructive deformations, making the perturbations more robust to distortions.
- Mechanism: ATTA trains a CNN as an adversarial transformation network that learns to capture and transform the most damaging deformations into adversarial noises. This transformation makes the adversarial samples more resistant to distortions and thus more transferable.
- Core assumption: Adversarial examples that are robust to transformations (like rotations, scaling) will maintain their adversarial properties across different models and datasets, improving transferability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Adversarial Transformation-enhanced Transfer Attack (ATTA) [124] is a technique that uses adversarial learning to train a CNN as an adversarial transformation network. This network is capable of capturing the most destructive deformations and transforming them into adversarial noises."
  - [section]: "ATTA’s performance may be enhanced by combining it with other transfer-based attacks... The adversarial samples created are more robust and transferable; Combine well with other transfer-based attacks to boost effectiveness."
  - [corpus]: Weak/no direct evidence; ATTA is a recent contribution not covered in the related corpus.
- Break condition: If the transformation network overfits to the specific architecture of the attack algorithm or if the transformations are too simplistic to capture the complexity of real-world distortions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adversarial detection methods like ROSA improve robustness by introducing universal noise to disrupt adversarial perturbation patterns while preserving boundaries, making it harder for adversarial examples to deceive the model.
- Mechanism: ROSA adds universal noise to the input image and uses a two-part system (piecewise-masked component and context-aware refinement component) to predict the saliency map. This disrupts adversarial noise patterns while preserving the essential image boundaries.
- Core assumption: Adversarial perturbations often target specific, localized features that can be disrupted by adding noise that preserves the overall structure of the image.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "ROSA, presented by Li et al. [181], is an innovative technique for enhancing the robustness of FCN-based salient object recognition models against adversarial attacks. It works by adding universal noise to the input image, then using a two-part system to predict the saliency map of the image: A piecewise-masked component that disrupts adversarial noise patterns while preserving boundaries, and a context-aware refinement component that adjusts the saliency mapping by using contrast modeling."
  - [section]: "ROSA enhances network robustness to attacks and performs comparably or better on natural images than current methods, which only focus on non-target attacks."
  - [corpus]: Weak/no direct evidence; ROSA is a recent contribution not covered in the related corpus.
- Break condition: If the universal noise disrupts too much of the original image content, leading to a significant drop in clean accuracy, or if the adversarial attacks adapt to the noise patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (data preprocessing and feature extraction) -> Middle layer (model architecture and regularization) -> Output layer (loss functions and distillation) -> Detection layer (adversarial sample detection and verification)

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess input data to remove or mitigate adversarial perturbations.
  2. Apply regularization techniques to improve model robustness.
  3. Use adversarial training to expose the model to adversarial examples during training.
  4. Implement detection mechanisms to identify and reject adversarial samples.
  5. Use ensemble methods to combine multiple models for improved robustness.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Defense effectiveness vs. computational overhead: More complex defense methods may be more effective but also more computationally expensive.
  - Model accuracy vs. robustness: Increasing robustness against adversarial attacks may sometimes come at the cost of reduced accuracy on clean data.
  - Generalizability vs. specificity: Defense methods that are highly effective on one dataset or model may not generalize well to others.

- Failure signatures:
  - High false positive rate in adversarial detection.
  - Significant drop in clean accuracy after applying defense methods.
  - Model vulnerability to adaptive white-box attacks.
  - Poor transferability of defense methods across different datasets or models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the transferability of adversarial attacks be further improved across different datasets and models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The survey discusses the transferability of adversarial attacks and mentions the need for future research to improve transferability across different datasets and models.
- Why unresolved: Despite some progress, achieving high transferability across diverse datasets and models remains challenging due to differences in model architectures, training data, and optimization landscapes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating significantly improved transferability rates across a wide range of datasets and model architectures using novel attack methods or optimization techniques.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the root cause of robustness loss in deep learning models against adversarial attacks, and how can it be effectively addressed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The survey mentions the unanswered root cause of robustness loss and discusses methods like Lipschitz continuity and gradient masking as potential approaches.
- Why unresolved: The exact mechanisms leading to robustness loss are not fully understood, and existing methods like gradient masking may not address the fundamental issue.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis and empirical studies that identify the key factors contributing to robustness loss and propose effective mitigation strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can adversarial defenses be made more scalable and generalizable to handle complex models and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The survey highlights the challenges of scalability and generalizability in adversarial defenses, noting that methods effective on one model or dataset may not work on others.
- Why unresolved: Existing defense methods often struggle to maintain effectiveness when applied to larger, more complex models or datasets due to increased computational costs and the need for extensive training data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of defense methods that demonstrate consistent performance across various model architectures and datasets, with manageable computational overhead.

## Limitations
- Many newer attack and defense methods (LAFEAT, ATTA, ROSA) lack extensive empirical validation and peer review
- Survey focuses primarily on image classification, limiting generalizability to other domains like NLP or graph neural networks
- Defense effectiveness often evaluated under specific attack scenarios with limited discussion of adaptive attacks
- Computational overhead and practical implementation challenges of proposed defense methods not extensively addressed
