# Neural World Models for Computer Vision

## Executive Summary
This thesis investigates autonomous driving through learning world models using deep neural networks trained on camera observations and expert demonstrations. The central insight is that world models allow agents to anticipate future events, resolve uncertainty, and improve decision-making. By integrating computer vision concepts including geometry, semantics, and motion, the approach scales world models to complex urban driving scenarios. The research demonstrates that neural world models can predict future depth, segmentation, optical flow, and trajectories, achieving state-of-the-art performance in closed-loop driving simulation while enabling agents to imagine diverse futures and drive from imagined plans.

## Method Summary
The approach develops a general framework for inferring world models and policies from data, leveraging computer vision techniques to process surround camera inputs. The method integrates geometric reasoning, semantic understanding, and motion analysis to predict future states in both camera view and bird's-eye view representations. The models are trained on expert demonstrations and can generate diverse future trajectories, enabling planning and decision-making in simulation environments. The framework emphasizes scalability to urban driving complexity while maintaining the ability to reason about static scenes, dynamic objects, and ego-vehicle behavior simultaneously.

## Key Results
- First model to predict future trajectories in bird's-eye view from surround cameras
- State-of-the-art results in closed-loop driving simulation with joint prediction of static scene, dynamic scene, and ego-behavior
- World models demonstrate ability to imagine diverse futures and drive from imagined plans
- Novel models successfully predict future depth, segmentation, and optical flow

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging the predictive power of neural networks to learn representations of the driving environment that capture both spatial and temporal relationships. By incorporating computer vision concepts like geometry, semantics, and motion, the models can reason about the 3D structure of scenes, understand object categories and their meanings, and track how elements evolve over time. This multi-faceted understanding enables the system to anticipate future states more accurately than approaches relying on single modalities. The ability to generate diverse futures stems from the model's capacity to capture uncertainty in predictions, while the closed-loop driving success comes from integrating perception, prediction, and planning into a unified framework that can operate from imagined scenarios.

## Foundational Learning
- **Camera Geometry**: Understanding how 3D scenes project onto 2D image planes is essential for interpreting visual inputs and reconstructing spatial relationships. Quick check: Verify that the model correctly handles perspective projection and camera calibration parameters.
- **Semantic Segmentation**: Classifying each pixel into meaningful categories provides context about the driving environment, distinguishing roads, vehicles, pedestrians, and obstacles. Quick check: Confirm that segmentation predictions align with ground truth labels across diverse urban scenes.
- **Optical Flow**: Estimating pixel-level motion between consecutive frames enables tracking of moving objects and understanding scene dynamics. Quick check: Validate flow predictions against known camera motion and object velocities.
- **Bird's-eye View Transformation**: Converting from camera perspective to top-down view is crucial for planning and navigation, providing a more intuitive representation for driving decisions. Quick check: Ensure BEV projections maintain spatial consistency and correct scale.
- **Trajectory Prediction**: Forecasting future paths of dynamic agents (vehicles, pedestrians) is fundamental for safe decision-making in autonomous driving. Quick check: Compare predicted trajectories against actual paths in held-out test sequences.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Surround Cameras -> Feature Extraction -> Multi-task Prediction Network -> Future Depth/Segmentation/Flow/BEV Trajectories
- **Critical Path**: Camera input → Geometric transformation → Semantic understanding → Motion estimation → Future prediction → Planning decision
- **Design Tradeoffs**: The architecture balances between model complexity and real-time performance, choosing multi-task learning to share representations while maintaining specialized heads for different prediction tasks. The tradeoff between accuracy and speed favors sufficient complexity for urban driving while remaining computationally feasible.
- **Failure Signatures**: Common failure modes include incorrect semantic segmentation in ambiguous lighting, flow estimation breakdown during rapid motion or occlusion, and BEV projection errors when camera calibration drifts. The model may also generate unrealistic trajectories when faced with rare or adversarial scenarios.
- **3 First Experiments**: 1) Validate single-frame depth and segmentation predictions against ground truth, 2) Test optical flow estimation on sequences with known motion patterns, 3) Evaluate BEV trajectory predictions on simple driving scenarios before scaling to complex urban environments.

## Limitations
- Performance in highly dynamic, adversarial, or rare edge-case scenarios (extreme weather, construction zones, unpredictable pedestrian behavior) remains inadequately evaluated
- Computational efficiency for real-time deployment on resource-constrained hardware is not explicitly addressed
- Claims about "human-like reasoning" and "diverse future imagining" are primarily supported by simulation results and qualitative assessments rather than rigorous real-world validation
