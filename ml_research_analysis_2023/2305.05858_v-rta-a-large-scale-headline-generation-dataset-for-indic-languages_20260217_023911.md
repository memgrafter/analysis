# Vārta: A Large-Scale Headline-Generation Dataset for Indic Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05858
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05858
- Authors: [Not provided]
- Reference count: 26

## Executive Summary
This work introduces Vārta, a large-scale multilingual headline generation dataset for 14 Indic languages, collected from DailyHunt, a popular news aggregator. The dataset contains 41.8 million high-quality news articles and is designed to enable strong transfer learning for both NLU and NLG tasks in Indic languages. Experiments show that the dataset is challenging even for state-of-the-art abstractive models, with models performing only slightly better than extractive baselines. Additionally, the dataset can be used to pretrain strong language models that outperform competitive baselines on both NLU and NLG benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The Vārta dataset is collected from DailyHunt, a popular news aggregator, and contains 41.8 million high-quality news articles in 14 Indic languages. The dataset is used to pretrain language models (Vārta-BERT and Vārta-T5) that are then fine-tuned for headline generation and evaluated on NLU and NLG benchmarks. The paper explores the impact of different settings, such as using English vs. Hindi as a bridge language and using original scripts vs. transliterated scripts, on the performance of the models.

## Key Results
- The Vārta dataset is challenging even for state-of-the-art abstractive models, with models performing only slightly better than extractive baselines.
- The dataset can be used to pretrain strong language models that outperform competitive baselines on both NLU and NLG benchmarks.
- Hindi is the most effective bridge language for the Vārta dataset, and multilingual training with transliterated scripts enables positive transfer among related languages.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The dataset's size and diversity enable strong transfer learning for both NLU and NLG tasks in Indic languages.
- **Mechanism:** Pretraining on a large, multilingual corpus with high-quality articles and headlines from diverse sources allows models to learn robust language representations that generalize across related languages.
- **Core assumption:** The quality and diversity of the data directly correlate with the effectiveness of transfer learning to downstream tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Owing to its size, we also show that the dataset can be used to pretrain strong language models that outperform competitive baselines in both NLU and NLG benchmarks."
  - [section 5.2]: "We find that Vārta-T5 generalizes well on other, similar tasks like abstractive summarization. Vārta-BERT and Vārta-T5 generally perform on par or better than strong baselines on both NLU and NLG benchmarks for Indic languages."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The paper claims diversity and quality but does not quantify coverage or provide detailed analysis of linguistic phenomena across languages.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Using Hindi as a bridge language is more effective than English for transfer learning in the Vārta dataset.
- **Mechanism:** Hindi's typological similarity to other Indic languages and shared script (Devanagari) with 4 of the 14 languages allows for better knowledge transfer during fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** Linguistic similarity and shared script are more important for effective transfer learning than the size of the pretraining data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1.3]: "We find that the largest pretraining language typically acts as the best bridge language. It helps if the language is typologically similar to other languages and shares a common script with them. In the case of dataset Vārta, Hindi is the ideal bridge language as it has all of the above properties."
  - [section 5.1.2]: "Finetuning on Original Scripts Vārta-T5 fine-tuned in this setting has the highest performance among all models... This supports our previous hypothesis that the language with the largest pretraining data is the most effective bridge language."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The paper claims typological similarity and shared script but does not provide quantitative measures or linguistic analysis.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Multilingual training, even with transliterated scripts, enables positive transfer among related languages.
- **Mechanism:** Training on a unified script (Latin or Devanagari) allows the model to learn shared linguistic patterns across languages, improving performance compared to monolingual training.
- **Core assumption:** Related languages share enough linguistic structure that training on a unified script can capture these patterns effectively.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.1.2]: "Uniﬁed Script On average, the Devanagari uniﬁed model demonstrates better performance, with an improvement of 0.67 ROUGE -L, when compared to the Latin uniﬁed model."
  - [section 5.1.3]: "Uniﬁed Script On average, the Devanagari uniﬁed model is 2.06 ROUGE -L points better than the Latin uniﬁed model. The Devanagari model performs much better on Gujarati, Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, and Urdu..."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. The paper reports performance improvements but does not analyze the specific linguistic patterns learned or the impact of script unification on model behavior.

## Limitations
- The paper claims high quality and diversity of the Vārta dataset but provides limited quantitative evidence to support these claims.
- The assumption that Hindi's typological similarity and shared script with 4 of the 14 languages make it the most effective bridge language is not rigorously tested.
- The paper relies on ROUGE scores to evaluate headline generation quality, but these metrics may not fully capture the abstract nature of headlines or the nuances of different languages.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The claim that the Vārta dataset can be used to pretrain strong language models that outperform competitive baselines on NLU and NLG benchmarks.
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that Hindi is the most effective bridge language for the Vārta dataset.
- **Low Confidence:** The claim that multilingual training with transliterated scripts enables positive transfer among related languages.
