# Explaining a machine learning decision to physicians via counterfactuals

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06325
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06325
- Reference count: 30
- This paper proposes CF-VAE, a novel method for generating counterfactual explanations for machine learning models in healthcare settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CF-VAE, a variational autoencoder-based method for generating counterfactual explanations of machine learning predictions in healthcare settings. The key innovation is a modified loss function that incorporates counterfactual objectives, encouraging generated explanations to be plausible, relevant to clinical tasks, and sparsely perturbed. The method is evaluated on predicting ICU interventions like vasopressors and ventilators, where it outperforms prior approaches in generating explanations that physicians judge as more plausible and relevant. Importantly, CF-VAE achieves this while being 100x faster than previous optimization-based methods, addressing a critical bottleneck in deploying counterfactual explanations in clinical practice.

## Method Summary
CF-VAE extends the standard variational autoencoder architecture by incorporating a modified loss function that includes reconstruction loss, KL divergence, counterfactual cross-entropy, and sparsity terms. The model is trained on 48-hour time-series data from ICU patients and generates counterfactual explanations by sampling from the learned latent space. A pre-trained classifier evaluates whether the generated counterfactuals successfully flip the prediction, while physician evaluation assesses plausibility and relevance. The method is designed to produce explanations that are both computationally efficient and clinically meaningful.

## Key Results
- CF-VAE generates counterfactuals that physicians rate as more plausible and relevant than prior methods
- The method is 100x faster than optimization-based approaches for generating explanations
- CF-VAE successfully generates sparse perturbations that flip classifier predictions for ICU interventions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: CF-VAE generates counterfactuals that are physiologically plausible because the latent space is learned from real patient data distributions.
- **Mechanism**: The VAE encoder maps real patient time-series data into a continuous latent space. The decoder then reconstructs plausible counterfactuals by sampling from this learned distribution. The VAE reconstruction loss ensures outputs lie on the true data manifold, avoiding unrealistic values like negative SpO2.
- **Core assumption**: The training data sufficiently captures the space of physiologically plausible time-series trajectories.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "more plausible CFs as evaluated by their likelihood w.r.t original data distribution"
  - [section] "our CFs are usually physiologically plausible... we sample from the latent space of a VAE trained on real patients"
- **Break condition**: If the training data contains errors, biases, or missing rare but plausible trajectories, the VAE may still generate implausible CFs despite being trained on real data.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: CF-VAE produces counterfactuals relevant to the prediction task by incorporating a task-specific loss during training.
- **Mechanism**: The loss function includes a cross-entropy term between the predicted probability of the CF and the desired opposite class. This term "teaches the VAE to encode the classifier boundary in its latent space, and to map a given X to a latent point of the opposite class." The CF-VAE thus learns a representation that captures task-relevant features.
- **Core assumption**: The target classifier is a reasonable proxy for the task-relevant features.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "Our CF-VAE incorporates a loss which captures relevance to the prediction task"
  - [section] "CF-VAE can be trained in the same way as a Vanilla VAE, using stochastic gradient descent"
- **Break condition**: If the target classifier is poorly trained or overfits to irrelevant features, CF-VAE will encode those irrelevant features and generate CFs that are not task-relevant.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: CF-VAE generates sparsely perturbed counterfactuals through an ℓ1 regularization term on the perturbation.
- **Mechanism**: The loss function includes a λS||X − XCF||1 term, which penalizes the total magnitude of changes across all features. This encourages the model to change as few features as possible while still flipping the classifier output. The sparsity term directly shapes the optimization landscape.
- **Core assumption**: The target classifier's decision boundary can be crossed with minimal feature changes.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "We achieve sparse perturbations through a ℓ1 regularization on the perturbation"
  - [section] "Figures 6 and 16 illustrate two examples with and without sparsity in the CF-VAE loss function"
- **Break condition**: If the decision boundary requires changing many features simultaneously, the ℓ1 penalty may prevent CF-VAE from finding any valid counterfactual, resulting in either no output or large, dense perturbations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  - Input: 48-hour time-series of patient vitals and interventions
  - Encoder: Multihead transformer or MLP mapping input to latent distribution (μ, Σ)
  - Latent space: Continuous representation learned via VAE objective
  - Decoder: Reconstructs counterfactual from sampled latent vector
  - Classifier: Pre-trained binary model (fixed) evaluating CF validity
  - Loss: Sum of reconstruction loss, KL divergence, cross-entropy (task), and ℓ1 sparsity

- **Critical path**:
  1. Encode original patient → latent space
  2. Sample latent vector (implicitly guided by CF loss)
  3. Decode to counterfactual time-series
  4. Evaluate with classifier, update via backprop

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using a fixed classifier vs. training jointly: Faster inference but less adaptive
  - Sparsity vs. validity: Strong ℓ1 may prevent finding valid CFs
  - VAE capacity vs. overfitting: Larger models may memorize training data

- **Failure signatures**:
  - CFs outside physiological ranges → training data distribution issues
  - CFs irrelevant to intervention → classifier focused on wrong features
  - No valid CF found → sparsity term too strong or classifier too confident

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train CF-VAE with λS=0, λcf=0 (vanilla VAE) and verify reconstructions match inputs
  2. Add λcf>0 term, verify CFs flip classifier output but may be implausible
  3. Add λS>0 term, verify CFs become sparser and more plausible

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on subjective physician judgments, which introduces variability
- Comparison with optimization methods limited to speed rather than systematic quality comparison
- Sparsity regularization may fail when decision boundaries require coordinated feature changes
- Generalization to other clinical tasks beyond the two studied interventions is not demonstrated

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using VAE with counterfactual loss for generating plausible CFs, and the computational efficiency improvement over optimization methods
- **Medium confidence**: The clinical relevance and utility of generated CFs for physician decision-making, based on physician evaluations
- **Medium confidence**: The sparsity regularization effectively generates minimal perturbations, though this may vary by task complexity

## Next Checks

2. **Cross-task generalization**: Apply CF-VAE to a different clinical prediction task (e.g., sepsis prediction or mortality prediction) using the same MIMIC-III data to validate whether the method generalizes beyond intervention prediction

3. **Physician validation scale-up**: Conduct a larger-scale physician study with blinded presentation of CFs generated by CF-VAE versus baselines, measuring not just plausibility but also whether the CFs would change clinical decision-making in realistic scenarios
