# Language models can generate molecules, materials, and protein binding sites directly in three dimensions as XYZ, CIF, and PDB files

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05708
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05708
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Language models trained on 3D chemical file formats can directly generate valid molecules, materials, and protein binding sites without architectural modifications

## Executive Summary
Language models, when trained on sequences derived from chemical file formats (XYZ, CIF, PDB), can directly generate novel and valid molecules, materials, and protein binding sites in three dimensions without architectural modifications. The approach uses tokenization strategies (character-level and atom+coordinate-level) to convert 3D structures into token sequences for next-token prediction training. On the ZINC benchmark, the model achieves validity rates of ~98%, uniqueness of 100%, and outperforms many graph and string-based generative models in property-based metrics (e.g., WADMW ≈ 1.8, WA QED ≈ 0.004). For crystals, it reaches validity of 95–100% on PEROV5 and MP20 datasets with strong coverage and property statistics. For protein binding sites, it generates valid pockets with ~99% residue-level correctness and produces novel amino acid orderings. This demonstrates that language models can serve as powerful, general-purpose generative models for complex 3D chemical structures.

## Method Summary
The approach trains transformer-based language models on sequential representations of 3D chemical structures from XYZ, CIF, and PDB file formats. Two tokenization strategies are used: character-level tokenization (where every character including spaces and newlines is a token) and atom+coordinate-level tokenization (where atomic elements and coordinate values are grouped into single tokens). The models are trained using next-token prediction on datasets including ZINC (250K molecules), PEROV5 (18,928 perovskite crystals), MP20 (45,231 crystals), and protein binding site pairs (~180K pairs). Data augmentation through random rotations is applied during training to help the model learn rotation-invariant features. Generated structures are evaluated for validity using specialized tools (xyz2mol for molecules, residue validation for protein pockets) and assessed on property statistics, coverage metrics, and geometric accuracy.

## Key Results
- Achieves validity rates of ~98% on ZINC benchmark for molecule generation, with uniqueness of 100% and superior property statistics compared to state-of-the-art models
- Reaches validity of 95–100% on PEROV5 and MP20 crystal datasets with strong coverage and property statistics matching training distributions
- Generates protein binding sites with ~99% residue-level correctness and novel amino acid orderings, demonstrating the approach works across diverse 3D chemical structure domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Language models can learn to generate valid 3D chemical structures directly from sequential representations of XYZ, CIF, and PDB files without requiring architectural modifications.
- Mechanism: By tokenizing atomic elements and coordinates (or entire coordinate strings) from 3D chemical file formats, the language model learns to predict the next token in the sequence, effectively learning the complex spatial and compositional relationships between atoms. The transformer architecture's ability to process entire sequences at once allows it to capture long-range dependencies necessary for 3D structure generation.
- Core assumption: The sequence representation of 3D chemical files contains sufficient information for the language model to learn valid structural patterns without requiring explicit geometric transformations or equivariances.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "language models trained directly on sequences derived directly from chemical file formats like XYZ files, Crystallographic Information files (CIFs), or Protein Data Bank files (PDBs) can directly generate molecules, crystals, and protein binding sites in three dimensions"
  - [section] "we frame language modeling as unsupervised distribution estimation from a set of examples (x1, x2,..., xn) each composed of variable length sequences of tokens ti such that x = (t1, t2,..., tn)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus contains related papers on 3D molecule generation but none directly address the specific claim of using language models on XYZ/CIF/PDB sequences without architectural modifications
- Break condition: If the tokenized sequences lose critical spatial information or if the language model cannot learn the complex constraints required for valid 3D structures, the approach would fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The tokenization strategy (character-level vs atom+coordinate-level) significantly impacts the model's ability to generate valid 3D structures.
- Mechanism: Character-level tokenization requires the model to predict every individual character including spaces and newlines, forcing it to learn the entire file structure. Atom+coordinate-level tokenization simplifies this by grouping atomic elements and coordinate values into single tokens, reducing sequence length and focusing the model on the essential chemical information.
- Core assumption: The choice of tokenization granularity affects the model's learning efficiency and ability to capture valid structural patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We use two different strategies: the first is character-level tokenization (LM-CH), where the model must generate every necessary individual element of the file including spaces between coordinates... Next, in atom+coordinate-level tokenization (LM-AC), the model only generates atom tokens like 'C' for carbon and coordinates tokens like '-1.98'"
  - [section] "Both character and coordinate level language models show strong performance in all evaluation metrics from validity, property statistics, and diversity"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus does not provide specific information about tokenization strategies for 3D structure generation
- Break condition: If one tokenization strategy consistently produces invalid structures or fails to capture essential chemical information, that strategy would break down.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Data augmentation through random rotations of training structures improves the language model's ability to generate valid 3D structures.
- Mechanism: Since the language model lacks built-in rotation and translation invariance, randomly rotating training structures during each epoch helps the model learn rotation-invariant features and prevents overfitting to specific orientations.
- Core assumption: The model can learn to generalize across different orientations of the same structure through data augmentation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Since the model is not invariant to rotations or translations, to improve performance at every epoch we can randomly rotate any training structure about its center to expand the training data"
  - [section] "Models trained without data augmentation can still achieve performance close to SOTA"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - the corpus does not mention data augmentation for 3D structure generation
- Break condition: If random rotations introduce noise that prevents the model from learning valid structural patterns, or if the model becomes too dependent on orientation-specific features, the augmentation would break the learning process.

## Limitations
- Tokenization strategy implementation details are not fully specified, particularly for atom+coordinate-level tokenization where coordinate tokens are defined and whether they're learned versus fixed vocabulary entries
- Generated structures may not fully explore chemical space beyond training distribution, potentially limiting novelty in real-world applications
- Protein binding site generation may not capture full complexity of protein dynamics and conformational flexibility in biological systems

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core claim that language models can generate valid 3D chemical structures directly from sequential file representations is strongly supported by the experimental results (validity rates of ~98% on ZINC, 95-100% on crystals, ~99% for protein pockets).

- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that this approach works without architectural modifications is supported, but the role of data augmentation through random rotations is less clear (models trained without augmentation can still achieve performance close to SOTA).

- **Medium Confidence**: The assertion that language models can serve as general-purpose generative models for complex 3D structures is promising but requires further validation (works across domains but needs testing for truly novel, synthetically accessible compounds and functionally relevant protein pockets).

## Next Checks

1. **Tokenization robustness test**: Systematically evaluate how different tokenization granularities (character vs atom+coordinate) affect validity rates and property statistics across all three domains (molecules, crystals, protein pockets) using controlled experiments with identical model architectures and hyperparameters.

2. **Generalization beyond training distribution**: Generate structures that systematically explore chemical space outside the training data bounds (e.g., larger molecules, different crystal compositions, novel protein binding pocket geometries) and evaluate whether the model can produce valid structures in these extended regions while maintaining reasonable property statistics.

3. **Functional validation of protein binding sites**: Beyond residue-level correctness, test whether generated protein binding pockets can actually accommodate drug-like molecules through docking experiments or whether they maintain the structural features necessary for specific protein-ligand interactions observed in the training data.
