# Semantic Segmentation using Vision Transformers: A survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03273
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03273
- Reference count: 40
- This survey comprehensively reviews Vision Transformer applications in semantic segmentation across multiple domains including remote sensing, medical imaging, and video processing.

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of Vision Transformers (ViTs) applied to semantic segmentation tasks. The authors examine how transformer architectures, originally designed for natural language processing, have been adapted for dense prediction tasks in computer vision. The paper systematically reviews various ViT-based segmentation models, analyzes their performance on standard benchmarks, and identifies key challenges including computational efficiency and data limitations. The survey also explores practical approaches for overcoming data scarcity and discusses various loss functions used in semantic segmentation.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a comprehensive survey methodology that synthesizes recent literature on Vision Transformers for semantic segmentation. The authors systematically categorize and analyze different ViT architectures designed for segmentation tasks, examining their design principles, advantages, and limitations. The survey covers a wide range of models including SETR, Swin Transformer, Segmenter, SegFormer, PVT, Twins, DPT, HRFormer, and Mask2Former. The methodology involves collecting and analyzing quantitative results from these models across benchmark datasets (ADE20K, Cityscapes, PASCAL-Context) and discussing their performance characteristics, computational requirements, and practical deployment considerations.

## Key Results
- ViT-based semantic segmentation models show competitive performance on standard benchmarks but face challenges with computational efficiency and real-time deployment
- Various architectural modifications and hybrid approaches have been proposed to adapt transformers for dense prediction tasks
- Self-supervised learning approaches show promise in addressing data limitations for ViT-based segmentation models
- Performance varies significantly across different datasets and model variants, with no clear consensus on optimal configurations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Vision Transformers work for semantic segmentation by leveraging the self-attention mechanism to capture long-range dependencies and contextual information across the entire image. Unlike CNNs that process images through local receptive fields, ViTs divide images into patches and use self-attention to model relationships between these patches globally. This allows the model to capture both local and global context simultaneously, which is crucial for accurate semantic segmentation. The multi-head attention mechanism enables the model to focus on different aspects of the image features, while the positional encoding preserves spatial information lost during patch embedding.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Image -> Patch Embedding -> Transformer Encoder -> Feature Refinement -> Decoder -> Segmentation Output
**Critical Path**: The transformer encoder with self-attention mechanism is the critical path, as it determines the model's ability to capture contextual information
**Design Tradeoffs**: Large patch sizes reduce computational cost but may lose fine details; more transformer layers increase context modeling but add computational overhead
**Failure Signatures**: Poor boundary delineation indicates insufficient local feature processing; class confusion suggests inadequate context modeling

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal patch size and number of transformer layers for ViT-based semantic segmentation across different datasets and domains?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that "ViT cannot be directly applied to dense prediction tasks such as image segmentation" and discusses various ViT architectures designed for segmentation, but doesn't provide a definitive answer on optimal configurations.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents a survey comparing different ViT architectures and their variants, but the results show that performance varies depending on the specific dataset and model variant used. There is no clear consensus on the best patch size or number of layers across all scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing the performance of different ViT architectures with varying patch sizes and number of layers on a wide range of semantic segmentation datasets and domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can ViT-based models be made more efficient for real-time semantic segmentation applications?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that ViTs are "still in the early stage of development" and discusses their high computational demand as a limitation.
- Why unresolved: While the paper surveys various ViT architectures for semantic segmentation, it doesn't provide specific solutions for improving their efficiency. The high computational cost remains a significant barrier to real-time applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Research demonstrating successful optimization techniques or architectural modifications that significantly reduce the computational complexity of ViT-based semantic segmentation models while maintaining or improving accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the most effective self-supervised learning approach for pre-training ViT models for semantic segmentation?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses "practical approaches to overcome the data limitation" and mentions that "self-supervised learning plays a great role as a remedy for data-hungry problems in deep learning."
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions the potential of self-supervised learning, it doesn't provide a definitive answer on which approach is most effective for pre-training ViT models for semantic segmentation. Different pretext tasks and SSL categories are mentioned, but their relative effectiveness is not compared.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies evaluating the performance of different self-supervised learning approaches (generative, contrastive, and adversarial) for pre-training ViT models on various semantic segmentation tasks and datasets.

## Limitations
- The survey focuses primarily on recent literature without systematic analysis of older transformer-based approaches or hybrid CNN-transformer models
- Quantitative comparisons are limited to a few benchmark datasets, which may not represent diverse real-world scenarios
- Discussion of data limitations and loss functions lacks specific empirical validation or ablation studies
- The survey does not address potential biases in transformer-based approaches or detailed computational efficiency comparisons with established CNN methods
