# Controlling the Extraction of Memorized Data from Large Language Models via Prompt-Tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11759
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11759
- Reference count: 20
- This work presents a method to control memorization in LLMs using prompt-tuning, achieving up to 9.3 percentage points improvement in extraction rates and 97.7% reduction in defense settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to control the extraction of memorized data from large language models (LLMs) using prompt-tuning. The authors propose two strategies: an attack method to increase memorization extraction rates and a defense method to decrease them. By optimizing soft-prompts through gradient-based learning, the attack can improve exact extraction rates by up to 9.3 percentage points, while the defense can reduce extraction rates by up to 97.7% with a modest 16.9% increase in perplexity. The approach offers tunable privacy-utility trade-offs through a user-specified threshold parameter.

## Method Summary
The method employs prompt-tuning to control memorization extraction from LLMs. For the attack, soft-prompts are optimized using aligned causal language modeling (CLM) to increase the likelihood of generating memorized suffixes. For the defense, a threshold θ is used to apply gradient ascent when loss is below θ, destabilizing confident generation of memorized content. The approach works by prepending learned continuous prompts to input embeddings without modifying model weights, allowing for efficient and targeted control over memorization extraction.

## Key Results
- Attack achieves 9.3 percentage point increase in exact extraction rates compared to baseline
- Defense reduces extraction rates by up to 97.7% relative to baseline
- Defense incurs 16.9% increase in perplexity for general text generation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The trained prompt functions as a directional signal that steers the model's generation toward memorized content.
- Mechanism: By prepending a learned continuous prompt to the prefix embedding, the prompt effectively biases the attention and internal representations of the LLM toward specific training sequences. This bias amplifies the likelihood that the model will generate memorized suffixes when prompted with corresponding prefixes.
- Core assumption: The prompt-tuning process can learn a compact representation that captures discriminative features of memorized data, and these features are transferable to the test prefixes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We present a novel approach which uses prompt-tuning to control the extraction rates of memorized content in LLMs."
  - [section]: "During prompt training... we minimize the loss objective... while keeping the parameters of the model frozen."
- Break condition: If the prompt becomes too overfit to training prefixes, it may fail to generalize to test prefixes, reducing extraction rates.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The defense mechanism increases the model's perplexity around memorized sequences by adding controlled noise through gradient ascent.
- Mechanism: By tuning the prompt with a learning threshold θ, the defense introduces perturbations that destabilize the model's confidence in generating memorized content. When the loss is below θ, gradient ascent is applied to push the model away from low-loss (high-confidence) memorized sequences.
- Core assumption: The model's generation confidence (measured via loss) correlates with the likelihood of extracting memorized content, and manipulating this loss through prompt tuning can effectively reduce memorization leakage.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our defense can be tuned to achieve different privacy-utility trade-offs by a user-specified hyperparameter."
  - [section]: "When loss is less than θ we do gradient ascent to penalize the prompt. If the loss is greater than θ, we perform gradient descent with respect to the prompt as usual."
- Break condition: If the gradient ascent introduces too much noise, the model's utility (measured by perplexity on general text) degrades significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The aligned CLM training objective improves extraction rates by focusing the prompt's learning on suffix generation rather than full sequence prediction.
- Mechanism: By minimizing cross-entropy loss only on suffixes given prefixes, the prompt-tuning process aligns the training objective with the inference task. This alignment ensures that the prompt learns to facilitate correct suffix generation without being distracted by prefix prediction accuracy.
- Core assumption: The model's ability to generate memorized suffixes is more dependent on the prompt's influence on suffix prediction than on overall sequence prediction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our attack yields a 9.3 percentage point increase in extraction rate compared to our baseline."
  - [section]: "In the second, the prompt is optimized by minimizing the cross entropy loss of only the suffixes, given the prefixes."
- Break condition: If the aligned CLM objective causes the prompt to overfit to training suffixes, it may not generalize to test suffixes, reducing extraction rates.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input data -> Continuous prompt initialization -> Frozen GPT-Neo model -> Cross-entropy loss (CLM or aligned CLM) -> Gradient descent/ascent on prompt -> Trained prompt for extraction control

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize prompt and split data into training and test sets
  2. Train prompt using CLM or aligned CLM objective
  3. Apply trained prompt to test prefixes and generate suffixes
  4. Measure extraction rates and perplexity
  5. Tune hyperparameters (prompt length, θ) for desired trade-offs

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Prompt length vs. extraction rate: Longer prompts may improve extraction but increase computational cost and risk of overfitting.
  - θ value vs. privacy-utility trade-off: Lower θ values may reduce extraction rates more but increase perplexity (reduce utility).
  - CLM vs. aligned CLM: Aligned CLM may improve extraction rates but requires more careful training to avoid overfitting.

- Failure signatures:
  - Attack fails: Extraction rates do not improve over baseline, or extraction rates degrade with longer prompts.
  - Defense fails: Extraction rates remain high despite tuning θ, or perplexity increases significantly without sufficient reduction in extraction rates.
  - Training instability: Loss does not converge or oscillates during prompt training.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a prompt using basic CLM on a small subset of the data and measure extraction rates to establish a baseline.
  2. Train a prompt using aligned CLM and compare extraction rates to the basic CLM baseline to assess the impact of alignment.
  3. Tune the defense with different θ values and measure the trade-off between extraction rates and perplexity to find an optimal setting.

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies on a fixed prompt length (30 tokens) without systematic analysis of how prompt length affects extraction rates across different datasets or model sizes.
- The defense mechanism's effectiveness depends heavily on the user-specified threshold θ, but lacks systematic sensitivity analysis across various memorization scenarios.
- The attack and defense are evaluated primarily on exact extraction rates and perplexity, but do not address other potential memorization extraction methods that could bypass the prompt-based controls.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The basic mechanism of using prompt-tuning to control LLM behavior is well-established in the literature, and the experimental setup follows standard practices for measuring memorization extraction.
- **Medium confidence**: The specific claims about 9.3 percentage point improvement in extraction rate and 97.7% relative reduction in defense are supported by the reported experiments, but lack comprehensive ablation studies.
- **Low confidence**: The claim that this approach provides "tunable" privacy-utility trade-offs is supported by a single hyperparameter (θ) without demonstrating the full Pareto frontier or showing robustness across different types of memorized content.

## Next Checks

1. **Prompt length sensitivity analysis**: Systematically evaluate extraction rates and perplexity across a range of prompt lengths (10-100 tokens) to determine the optimal trade-off and identify any overfitting patterns.

2. **Cross-dataset generalization**: Test the trained prompts on multiple memorization benchmarks beyond the LAMBADA dataset to verify that the attack and defense mechanisms generalize to different types of memorized content and model architectures.

3. **Defense robustness testing**: Evaluate the defense mechanism against alternative extraction methods (temperature scaling, beam search with larger beams, nucleus sampling with different p values) to determine if the prompt-based defense can be bypassed by changing generation strategies.
