# Dimension-reduced KRnet maps for high-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00573
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00573
- Reference count: 34
- One-line primary result: DR-KRnet achieves lower relative error (0.39 vs 0.40) with half the computational cost compared to VAE-prior MCMC for inferring log-permeability fields from Darcy flow observations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DR-KRnet, a dimension-reduced approach for high-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems that combines variational autoencoder (VAE) priors with Knothe-Rosenblatt network (KRnet) maps. The method learns a low-dimensional latent representation of the prior data via VAE, then approximates the posterior distribution in the latent space using an invertible KRnet transport map. To improve efficiency, a physics-constrained surrogate model is trained to replace expensive forward/adjoint PDE solves. Experiments on inferring log-permeability fields from Darcy flow observations show DR-KRnet achieves lower relative error with significantly reduced computational cost compared to VAE-prior MCMC.

## Method Summary
The DR-KRnet approach consists of a two-stage procedure. First, a VAE is trained on historical data to learn a low-dimensional latent representation that captures the essential features of the high-dimensional parameter space. Second, a physics-constrained surrogate model is constructed to approximate the forward model without requiring labeled data by enforcing PDE constraints during training. The KRnet map is then trained in the latent space to approximate the posterior distribution by pushing the prior to the posterior. This reformulation in a lower-dimensional space enables efficient Bayesian inference while maintaining accuracy.

## Key Results
- DR-KRnet achieves relative error of 0.39 compared to 0.40 for VAE-prior MCMC
- Computational cost reduced by approximately 50% compared to baseline
- Surrogate model achieves relative errors of 0.05694 and 0.08260 for two test problems
- Method successfully handles high-dimensional posteriors with 1089-dimensional observations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - VAE prior (encoder and decoder) -> Physics-constrained surrogate model -> KRnet map (transport map between prior and posterior) -> Forward model (original PDE-based model)

- Critical path:
  1. Train VAE prior on historical data
  2. Train physics-constrained surrogate model
  3. Construct KRnet map in latent space
  4. Generate posterior samples and compute statistics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Dimension of latent space: Higher dimensions may capture more information but increase computational cost
  - Complexity of KRnet: More complex maps may better approximate the posterior but require more training data
  - Accuracy of surrogate model: More accurate surrogates reduce error but may require more training data and computational resources

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor reconstruction quality in VAE prior indicates insufficient latent space dimensionality
  - Large differences between surrogate and true forward model outputs suggest inadequate surrogate training
  - Inconsistent posterior samples or high relative error indicate problems with KRnet approximation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train VAE prior on synthetic data and evaluate reconstruction quality
  2. Train physics-constrained surrogate model on synthetic data and compare with true forward model
  3. Implement KRnet map on a low-dimensional test problem and verify invertibility and transport accuracy

## Limitations
- Method's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of VAE prior representation
- Physics-constrained surrogate model introduces approximation errors that may propagate through inference
- Computational savings come at the cost of additional modeling layers and potential accuracy trade-offs

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The mathematical framework for combining VAE priors with KRnet transport maps is well-established and the experimental setup is clearly described
- **Medium confidence**: The relative performance improvements (0.39 vs 0.40 error, 50% cost reduction) are reported but would benefit from additional test cases to verify generalizability
- **Medium confidence**: The physics-constrained surrogate model approach is novel, but the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency requires further investigation
