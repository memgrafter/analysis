# A Novel Scheme to classify Read and Spontaneous Speech

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08012
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08012
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Achieves 88.12% accuracy in distinguishing read from spontaneous speech using explainable features derived from DeepSpeech output

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel method to classify read and spontaneous speech using features extracted from DeepSpeech's alphabet output. The approach leverages the observation that spontaneous speech contains more pauses and thinking time, resulting in different patterns of inactive alphabets compared to read speech. By converting audio to alphabet sequences and analyzing statistical properties like word length and speaking rate, the method achieves high accuracy without requiring linguistic content analysis.

## Method Summary
The method processes audio through a pre-trained DeepSpeech model to generate alphabet sequences, then extracts five measured features (duration, word count, alphabet count, active alphabet count, inactive alphabet count) and derives three features (active average word length, inactive alphabets per second, word rate). These features are combined using a logistic scoring function with fixed thresholds to classify speech as read or spontaneous. The approach claims language independence since it relies on DeepSpeech's output rather than the underlying linguistic content.

## Key Results
- 88.12% classification accuracy on the allsstar-db dataset
- Active average word length is higher in spontaneous speech (6.33 vs 4.55 alphabets)
- Word rate is lower for spontaneous speech (1.45 vs 2.43 words per second)
- Method uses only three derived features to achieve effective classification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Read speech has fewer inactive alphabets per word compared to spontaneous speech due to shorter pauses.
- Mechanism: DeepSpeech generates a sequence of alphabets including active letters and inactive tokens (⋄). Spontaneous speech contains more thinking time and pauses, resulting in more inactive alphabets between words. Read speech is faster with fewer pauses, hence fewer inactive alphabets.
- Core assumption: The number of inactive alphabets per word is a reliable proxy for speaking pace and cognitive load during speech production.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The main advantage of the proposed scheme is that the features are explainable and are derived by processing the alphabet string output of DeepSpeech, making the features independent of the linguistic content of the utterance."
  - [section 2.2] "We hypothesize that ds(x(t)), as a representation of speechx(t), contains sufficient information that can help distinguish between read and spontaneous speech...for the same sentence, the output of DeepSpeech should having more number of InActive alphabets compared to read speech."
  - [corpus] Weak - No corpus-level statistics on inactive alphabet distributions, only general comparison in the air-db dataset.
- Break condition: If the DeepSpeech model is retrained on spontaneous speech with modified tokenization rules, the distribution of inactive alphabets may no longer reliably indicate speaking style.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Active average word length is higher in spontaneous speech because spontaneous speakers use more varied and longer words.
- Mechanism: Spontaneous speech tends to include more diverse vocabulary and sentence structures, leading to longer average word lengths. Read speech from scripts is often more controlled and repetitive, resulting in shorter average word lengths.
- Core assumption: Word length variability is primarily driven by speaking style rather than the content being read or spoken.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3] "there are more lengthy words in spontaneous speech...there are words of length90 alphabets in spontaneous speech compared to< 60 alphabets per word in read speech."
  - [section 2.2] "there are more words (with same number of alphabets) in spontaneously spoken passage compared to the read passage...there are more lengthy words in spontaneous speech."
  - [corpus] Weak - No corpus-level statistical comparison of word length distributions, only anecdotal evidence from the example paragraph.
- Break condition: If the read speech corpus consists of complex technical texts or literature, active average word length may be higher than spontaneous speech.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Word rate (words per second) is lower for spontaneous speech due to increased cognitive processing time.
- Mechanism: Spontaneous speech requires real-time cognitive processing, leading to slower word production rates. Read speech can be delivered at a faster pace since the cognitive load is reduced.
- Core assumption: Speaking rate differences are primarily due to cognitive processing requirements rather than other factors like speaker familiarity with the topic.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3] "Word Rate (wps) [f3] 1.45 2.43" showing spontaneous speech has lower word rate than read speech.
  - [section 2.2] "spontaneous speech requires thinking time between words, between sentences...the number of InActive alphabets must be more in spontaneous speech compared to read speech."
  - [corpus] Moderate - The air-db dataset shows consistent differences in word rate between read and spontaneous speech segments.
- Break condition: If speakers are highly familiar with the spontaneous topic or reading unfamiliar material, word rate differences may diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Audio input → DeepSpeech processing → Alphabet sequence generation → Feature extraction (f1, f2, f3) → Logistic classifier → Read/spontaneous classification
- **Critical path**: The slowest component is DeepSpeech processing; all other steps are lightweight string operations.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using pre-trained DeepSpeech provides language-independent features but limits control over tokenization; custom feature engineering provides interpretability but may miss subtle acoustic cues.
- **Failure signatures**: High false positive rates for spontaneous speech classified as read may indicate over-reliance on word length features; high false negative rates may indicate insufficient discrimination from inactive alphabet counts.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate feature distributions: Compute and visualize f1, f2, f3 distributions for both read and spontaneous speech in a held-out validation set.
  2. Threshold sensitivity analysis: Vary τR and observe precision-recall tradeoffs to find optimal operating point.
  3. Cross-speaker generalization: Test classification performance when training on one speaker and testing on another to assess speaker independence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal threshold value (τR) for classifying read and spontaneous speech?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions an empirical choice of τR = 1.75 but does not provide a systematic method for determining this threshold.
- Why unresolved: The threshold value significantly impacts classification performance and may vary across different datasets or languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic analysis of how classification accuracy varies with different threshold values across multiple datasets would help determine the optimal threshold.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How well does the proposed method generalize to languages other than English?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments were conducted on English speech data, but the method's language independence is claimed without empirical validation.
- Why unresolved: The DeepSpeech model is trained on English data, and the effectiveness of derived features may vary across languages with different phonetic and prosodic characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the proposed method on multiple languages with different linguistic properties would validate its generalizability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of varying audio quality and recording conditions on classification performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses relatively clean broadcast data and does not discuss the method's robustness to noise or varying recording conditions.
- Why unresolved: Real-world applications may involve noisy environments or varying recording qualities, which could affect the DeepSpeech output and subsequent feature extraction.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the method on datasets with varying noise levels and recording conditions would quantify its robustness to audio quality variations.

## Limitations

- The method relies heavily on DeepSpeech's specific tokenization behavior, which may vary across different model versions or implementations.
- Datasets used have inherent biases and may not represent typical spontaneous speech contexts or conversational scenarios with multiple speakers.
- Fixed thresholds and coefficients in the logistic scoring function were not optimized through cross-validation, potentially limiting performance on unseen data.

## Confidence

**High Confidence (80-100%)**: The claim that DeepSpeech can effectively convert speech to alphabet sequences is well-supported by the experimental results. The observation that word rate differs between read and spontaneous speech (1.45 vs 2.43 wps) is statistically robust and directly observable from the data.

**Medium Confidence (50-80%)**: The claim that the proposed features are explainable and language-independent has moderate support but relies on the assumption that DeepSpeech's tokenization behavior is consistent across languages. The effectiveness of using only three derived features (active average word length, inactive alphabets per second, and word rate) for classification is supported by results but may not generalize to more diverse speech conditions.

**Low Confidence (0-50%)**: The claim that the method can distinguish read from spontaneous speech "very effectively" with only a "small set of self-explanatory features" is overstated given the limited dataset sizes and lack of comparison with more sophisticated feature sets or deep learning approaches. The assertion that features are completely independent of linguistic content is questionable since word length distributions are inherently language-dependent.
