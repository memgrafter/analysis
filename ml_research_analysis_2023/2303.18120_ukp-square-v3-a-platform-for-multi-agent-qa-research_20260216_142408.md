# UKP-SQuARE v3: A Platform for Multi-Agent QA Research

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.18120
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18120
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: MetaQA achieves performance close to the best model, is compatible with any QA format, and has fast inference time when using parallel processing.

## Executive Summary
UKP-SQuARE v3 extends the existing QA research platform to support multi-agent systems, enabling researchers to combine expert QA agents using three main approaches: Skill selection, early-fusion, and late-fusion. The platform deploys three specific methods—TWEAC (router-based), MADE (adapter-based), and MetaQA (late-fusion)—to demonstrate the potential of multi-agent systems in QA. Experiments on six MRQA datasets show that while multi-agent systems can match or exceed the performance of multi-dataset models like UnifiedQA, they come with distinct trade-offs in training cost, inference speed, and format flexibility. The choice of system depends on specific use case requirements, with MetaQA offering strong performance and speed, TWEAC providing flexibility at lower cost, and MADE delivering highest performance with higher resource demands.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates and compares three multi-agent QA systems (TWEAC, MADE, MetaQA) against a multi-dataset model (UnifiedQA) using six datasets from the MRQA 2019 Shared Task. The evaluation focuses on three metrics: F1 score for performance, inference time in seconds, and training cost classification (cheap/expensive). TWEAC employs a router model trained on 16 datasets to select the most suitable QA model, MADE combines adapter weights from multiple models, and MetaQA uses late fusion to combine predictions from 18 expert agents. UnifiedQA serves as a baseline multi-dataset model trained on multiple datasets. The comparison uses 20 random questions per dataset to measure inference timing and performance across different system configurations.

## Key Results
- MetaQA achieves performance close to the best model, is compatible with any QA format, and has fast inference time when using parallel processing.
- TWEAC is cheap to train and flexible but underperforms on extractive QA.
- MADE offers the highest performance and fast inference but is expensive to train and less flexible.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Datastores -> Models -> Skills -> Explainability/Adversarial Attacks

- Critical path:
  1. User selects or creates a Skill.
  2. Skill is configured with the desired QA pipeline.
  3. Skill leverages Datastores and Models to perform QA.
  4. Explainability and Adversarial Attacks can be applied to analyze the Skill.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Flexibility vs. complexity: The platform offers a high degree of flexibility in combining agents, but this can lead to increased complexity for users.
  - Performance vs. speed: Some methods of combining agents may offer higher performance but at the cost of slower inference times.

- Failure signatures:
  - Skills not working as expected: Check the configuration of the Skill and ensure that the Datastores and Models are properly integrated.
  - Poor performance: Verify that the expert agents are diverse and that the combination method is appropriate for the task.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Deploy a simple Skill using a single pre-trained model and test its performance on a standard QA dataset.
  2. Combine two expert agents using the Skill selection method and compare its performance to the single model Skill.
  3. Experiment with different methods of combining agents (early-fusion, late-fusion) and analyze the trade-offs between performance, training cost, and inference speed.

## Limitations
- Dataset and Task Coverage: The evaluation is limited to six datasets from the MRQA 2019 Shared Task, which may not represent the full diversity of real-world QA scenarios.
- Model Configuration Transparency: Critical details about model architectures, hyperparameters, and training procedures for TWEAC, MADE, and MetaQA are not fully specified.
- Hardware Dependency: Inference speed measurements are hardware-dependent and may vary significantly across different computational environments.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The general observation that different multi-agent systems offer distinct trade-offs between performance, training cost, and inference speed is well-supported by the experimental results.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that MetaQA achieves performance close to the best model is supported, but the extent of this advantage across diverse real-world scenarios is uncertain.
- Low Confidence: The assertion that TWEAC underperforms on extractive QA is based on limited experimental evidence and may not hold across all extractive QA datasets.

## Next Checks
