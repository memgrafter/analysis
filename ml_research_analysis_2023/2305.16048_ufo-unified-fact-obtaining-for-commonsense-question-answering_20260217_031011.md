# UFO: Unified Fact Obtaining for Commonsense Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16048
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16048
- Reference count: 35
- Key result: Unified fact obtaining approach improves multiple commonsense QA benchmarks by 1.4-10.4%

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UFO, a unified fact obtaining approach that transforms large-scale language models into knowledge sources for commonsense question answering. The method uses few-shot prompting to generate diverse, question-related facts across different commonsense domains, followed by dense retrieval to select the most relevant facts. UFO significantly outperforms existing approaches on multiple benchmarks, achieving improvements of 6.5% on CommonsenseQA 2.0, 7.0% on OpenBookQA, 10.4% on QASC, and 1.4% on Social IQA.

## Method Summary
UFO employs few-shot prompting to generate diverse facts related to commonsense questions, then applies dense retrieval to identify the most relevant facts for each question. The approach leverages large language models as knowledge sources without requiring manual knowledge base construction. The generated facts are integrated with downstream QA models to enhance their commonsense reasoning capabilities across multiple domains.

## Key Results
- 6.5% improvement on CommonsenseQA 2.0 benchmark
- 7.0% improvement on OpenBookQA benchmark  
- 10.4% improvement on QASC benchmark
- 1.4% improvement on Social IQA benchmark
- Outperforms models using manually constructed knowledge sources

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
UFO works by leveraging the vast knowledge encoded in large language models through few-shot prompting, generating diverse facts that capture different aspects of commonsense knowledge. The dense retrieval component then efficiently selects the most relevant facts for each specific question, providing targeted knowledge augmentation that enhances the QA model's reasoning capabilities.

## Foundational Learning

**Few-shot prompting** - Teaching language models new tasks with minimal examples
*Why needed:* Enables fact generation without extensive training data
*Quick check:* Test with varying numbers of demonstration examples

**Dense retrieval** - Vector-based similarity matching for information retrieval
*Why needed:* Efficiently selects most relevant facts from generated pool
*Quick check:* Verify retrieval accuracy on known question-fact pairs

**Commonsense reasoning** - Understanding implicit knowledge humans use daily
*Why needed:* Core challenge in making AI systems more human-like
*Quick check:* Test on questions requiring real-world knowledge

**Knowledge augmentation** - Enhancing models with external information
*Why needed:* Supplements model's inherent knowledge limitations
*Quick check:* Compare performance with and without retrieved facts

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Few-shot prompt generation -> Fact generation -> Dense retrieval -> Fact selection -> QA model integration

**Critical Path:** Question → Few-shot prompt → Generated facts → Dense retrieval → Selected facts → Enhanced QA prediction

**Design Tradeoffs:** Few-shot prompting offers flexibility but depends heavily on prompt quality; dense retrieval provides efficiency but may miss context-dependent relevance; automated fact generation avoids manual curation but introduces quality variability.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor prompt design leads to irrelevant or redundant facts; dense retrieval may miss semantically relevant but lexically different facts; generated facts may contain hallucinations or factual errors.

**First 3 Experiments:**
1. Test few-shot prompting with varying numbers of demonstration examples (1, 3, 5, 10)
2. Evaluate dense retrieval performance using different similarity thresholds
3. Compare generated fact diversity against manually constructed knowledge sources

## Limitations
- Few-shot prompting quality heavily depends on prompt design and demonstration examples
- Dense retrieval may struggle with abstract or context-dependent commonsense knowledge
- No comprehensive analysis of fact diversity, redundancy, or coverage completeness

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Benchmark improvements are statistically significant and methodology is reproducible
- **Medium confidence:** Claims about unified fact obtaining are supported but lack deeper diversity analysis
- **Low confidence:** Optimal few-shot prompting method assertion without comparison to alternatives

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies varying demonstration examples in few-shot prompts to quantify impact on fact generation quality and QA performance
2. Implement fact redundancy analysis to measure actual diversity of generated facts across domains and identify coverage gaps
3. Test approach on out-of-distribution questions and adversarial examples to evaluate dense retrieval robustness with ambiguous commonsense scenarios
