# DEDGAT: Dual Embedding of Directed Graph Attention Networks for Detecting Financial Risk

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03933
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03933
- Reference count: 16
- This paper proposes DEDGAT, which achieves 96.18% AUC on Elliptic, 98.16% AUC on Ant, and 81.37% AUC on DGraphFin, outperforming GAT and other baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses directional bias in graph attention networks (GAT) when applied to directed graphs for financial risk control. The authors propose DGAT, a directed graph attention network that explicitly calculates in-degree and out-degree attentions separately, and DEDGAT, which further introduces dual embedding to distinguish in-degree and out-degree representations. Experiments on three financial risk control datasets demonstrate that DEDGAT achieves superior performance, with visualizations confirming its ability to balance in-degree and out-degree contributions.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes DEDGAT, which addresses directional bias in GAT by using dual embeddings for each node - one for in-degree and one for out-degree representations. The method computes separate attention mechanisms for incoming and outgoing edges, allowing the model to better capture directional semantics in financial graphs. DEDGAT consists of two DEDGAT layers followed by a linear layer for node classification. The approach is evaluated on three directed graph datasets for financial risk detection, measuring performance using AUC.

## Key Results
- DEDGAT achieves 96.18% AUC on Elliptic dataset
- DEDGAT achieves 98.16% AUC on Ant dataset
- DEDGAT achieves 81.37% AUC on DGraphFin dataset
- GNNExplainer visualizations show DEDGAT effectively balances in-degree and out-degree edge contributions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DGAT reduces directional bias by computing in-degree and out-degree attentions separately.
- Mechanism: DGAT maintains two distinct linear transformations and two attention functions for in-degree and out-degree, ensuring incoming and outgoing edges are treated distinctly.
- Core assumption: In financial graphs, incoming and outgoing relationships have different semantic meanings.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states directional bias exists in GAT; section 2.1 describes DGAT's explicit treatment of out-degree; corpus shows weak evidence from nearest neighbor papers.
- Break condition: If dataset structure is nearly symmetric, separation yields little gain and may overfit.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DEDGAT improves discriminative power by assigning separate embeddings for in-degree and out-degree representations.
- Mechanism: Each node maintains two independent embeddings and applies distinct linear transforms before computing attentions, allowing richer directional semantics.
- Core assumption: A single embedding cannot simultaneously capture the differing roles a node plays as receiver vs sender.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract describes dual embedding approach; section 2.2 explains separate representations; corpus shows weak evidence from nearest neighbor papers.
- Break condition: If neighbors per node are very low, benefit of separate embeddings diminishes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Visualization with GNNExplainer demonstrates DEDGAT's ability to balance in-degree and out-degree contributions.
- Mechanism: GNNExplainer outputs importance scores showing DEDGAT's balanced edge importance distribution versus GAT's in-degree bias.
- Core assumption: Edge importance scores accurately reflect model's attention mechanism and meaningfulness of directional contributions.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions visualization results; section 3.5 describes edge importance analysis; corpus shows weak evidence from nearest neighbor papers.
- Break condition: If dataset contains noise edges that distort importance scores, visual evidence may mislead.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer -> Linear transforms (W^(l), U^(l) for DGAT; w^(l), u^(l) for DEDGAT) -> Attention heads (separate for in-degree/out-degree) -> Aggregation (sum of attended features) -> Output layer (linear classifier)

- Critical path: 1) Load directed graph and initialize dual embeddings (DEDGAT) or shared embedding (DGAT) 2) Apply directional linear transforms per layer 3) Compute separate attention scores for in-degree and out-degree neighbors 4) Aggregate attended features per direction 5) Combine directional embeddings and pass to next layer 6) Final classification via linear layer

- Design tradeoffs: Memory doubles with DEDGAT's dual embeddings; training time increases with separate attention computations; DEDGAT allows richer directional modeling but may overfit on small datasets

- Failure signatures: Model collapse if both embeddings converge to similar values; overfitting with excessive attention heads or high dimensions; degenerate attention if softmax yields uniform weights

- First 3 experiments: 1) Train DGAT on small subgraph and compare edge importance distribution with GAT 2) Replace DEDGAT with DGAT on Elliptic dataset; observe AUC drop 3) Vary number of attention heads in DEDGAT; measure impact on bias balance and performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the dual embedding approach affect computational complexity and scalability on very large-scale financial graphs?
- Basis in paper: Paper demonstrates effectiveness but lacks computational complexity analysis or scalability benchmarks
- Why unresolved: Focuses on classification performance and visualization, not computational analysis
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical measurements of training/inference time and memory usage for DEDGAT versus GAT on increasingly large graphs, with Big-O complexity analysis

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is DEDGAT to noise and adversarial attacks where edge directions might be obfuscated?
- Basis in paper: Demonstrates effectiveness on clean datasets but doesn't evaluate robustness to noisy or adversarial data
- Why unresolved: No experiments with injected noise or adversarial perturbations
- What evidence would resolve it: Adversarial attack experiments (edge flipping, direction obfuscation) and noise injection studies showing classification accuracy degradation rates

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can DEDGAT be effectively extended to heterogeneous graphs with multiple edge types and node features beyond binary classification?
- Basis in paper: Focuses on homogeneous directed graphs for binary classification but mentions potential heterogeneity in financial relationships
- Why unresolved: Only evaluated on homogeneous graphs with binary labels
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying DEDGAT to heterogeneous financial graphs with multiple edge types and multi-class/multi-label node classification

## Limitations

- Limited computational analysis: Paper doesn't provide explicit complexity analysis or scalability benchmarks for very large graphs
- No robustness evaluation: Model's resilience to noise and adversarial attacks remains untested
- No heterogeneity extension: Method only validated on homogeneous graphs, leaving multi-relational scenarios unexplored

## Confidence

- Performance improvement claim: Medium confidence - strong AUC values but lack statistical significance tests
- Reproducibility: Low confidence - missing hyperparameter details and preprocessing specifications
