# A Unified Analysis of Nonstochastic Delayed Feedback for Combinatorial Semi-Bandits, Linear Bandits, and MDPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08629
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08629
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Develops a unified analysis of FTRL for delayed bandit feedback, achieving optimal or near-optimal regret bounds for combinatorial semi-bandits, adversarial MDPs, and linear bandits.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel analysis of Follow The Regularized Leader (FTRL) for online learning with delayed bandit feedback. The key insight is a regret decomposition that cleanly separates the standard pseudo-distance term from the costs of delayed feedback and bandit feedback. This separation enables the derivation of new, optimal or near-optimal regret bounds in three important settings: combinatorial semi-bandits, adversarial Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), and linear bandits. The analysis relies on the stability of FTRL under mild assumptions on the Hessian of the regularizer, allowing for repeated application of stability lemmas across multiple rounds.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes FTRL for online learning with delayed bandit feedback by decomposing regret into three components: standard pseudo-distance, delayed feedback cost, and bandit feedback cost. For each setting (combinatorial semi-bandits, adversarial MDPs, linear bandits), the method implements FTRL with appropriate regularizers, constructs importance-weighted loss estimators, and applies the regret decomposition to bound each component separately. The analysis leverages the stability properties of FTRL under mild Hessian conditions to ensure the decomposition remains valid across multiple rounds. The approach allows for the first optimal regret bounds in delayed feedback settings for combinatorial semi-bandits and adversarial MDPs.

## Key Results
- First optimal regret bounds for combinatorial semi-bandits with delay: O(√B(KT+BD) log(K/B))
- First optimal regret bounds for adversarial MDPs with delay: O(H√SAT + H√D)
- Computationally efficient algorithm for linear bandits with near-optimal regret: O(BK√νT log(T) + B√D)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The regret decomposition cleanly separates three sources of regret: standard pseudo-distance, delay cost, and bandit feedback cost.
- Mechanism: By writing the instantaneous regret as a sum of terms H1, H2, H3 (and bias/cheating regret), the algorithm isolates the effect of delayed feedback from bandit feedback.
- Core assumption: The estimator's bias term `bt` can be controlled separately from the bandit noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "By separating the cost of delayed feedback from that of bandit feedback, our analysis allows us to obtain new results in three important settings."
  - [section]: Equation (2) in the analysis section shows the decomposition into standard pseudo-distance, delayed feedback cost, and bandit feedback cost.
  - [corpus]: "Banker Online Mirror Descent: A Universal Approach for Delayed Online Bandit Learning" shows similar separation ideas in delayed bandit literature.
- Break condition: If the delay distribution is adversarial or unknown, the decomposition may fail because the independence assumptions across rounds no longer hold.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: FTRL remains stable across multiple rounds under mild assumptions on the Hessian of the regularizer.
- Mechanism: Stability is ensured by bounding the local norm of loss estimates and using log-barrier regularization, allowing repeated application of stability lemmas.
- Core assumption: The regularizer satisfies 4∇²R(w) ⪰ ∇²R(w′) ⪰ ¼∇²R(w) for all w and w′ in the Dikin ellipsoid.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our novel regret decomposition shows that FTRL remains stable across multiple rounds under mild assumptions on the Hessian of the regularizer."
  - [section]: Lemma 10 proves multi-round stability of FTRL iterates under the stated Hessian conditions.
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus support for this specific stability condition, but similar Hessian assumptions appear in bandit optimization literature.
- Break condition: If the Hessian bounds fail (e.g., due to aggressive tuning of η or γ), iterates may leave the stability region, breaking the analysis.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Independence of loss estimates across rounds with the same delay allows clean separation of delay and bandit costs.
- Mechanism: For τ, τ′ ∈ mt with τ ≠ τ′, the estimators ˆℓτ and ˆℓτ′ are independent, enabling Jensen's inequality to separate variance terms.
- Core assumption: Delayed feedback arrives after exactly dt rounds, and estimators for different τ are conditionally independent given Ft.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: Corollary 3 uses E[(ˆℓτ - ℓτ)ᵀ(∇²R(w(ˆLt)))⁻¹(ˆℓτ′ - ℓτ′) | Ft] = 0 for τ ≠ τ′.
  - [corpus]: "Stochastic Submodular Bandits with Delayed Composite Anonymous Bandit Feedback" uses similar independence assumptions for delayed feedback analysis.
- Break condition: If feedback is aggregated or anonymous (multiple rounds' feedback arrive together without labels), the independence assumption fails.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: FTRL optimizer -> Estimator generator -> Stability monitor -> Regret decomposer
- Critical path:
  1. Compute FTRL iterate wt
  2. Generate action distribution pt from wt
  3. Play action at ~ pt and observe delayed feedback
  4. Update loss estimates ˆℓτ for τ + dτ = t
  5. Maintain stability via Hessian checks
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Stability vs. adaptivity: stronger Hessian bounds ensure stability but may reduce adaptivity to problem structure
  - Computational cost: exact FTRL may be expensive; approximations (e.g., for linear bandits) trade regret for speed
  - Delay handling: separate delay and bandit costs work best with independent feedback; aggregated feedback breaks this
- Failure signatures:
  - Instability: large changes in wt across rounds indicate Hessian bounds violated
  - Regret blowup: if |mt| grows too large relative to T, the delay cost term dominates
  - Estimator bias: if E[ˆℓt|Ft] ≠ ℓt, the decomposition fails
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test FTRL stability on a small combinatorial semi-bandit instance with known delays; verify Hessian bounds hold
  2. Implement the importance-weighted estimator; check unbiasedness empirically on synthetic data
  3. Run the full algorithm on a delayed linear bandit with varying delay distributions; measure separation of delay vs bandit regret components

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: Can the dmax^2 log(T) term in the regret bounds be improved to dmax log(T) or removed entirely?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as a key open problem in the discussion section, stating "the main downside of our approach is the additive d^2_max log(T) term in our bounds."
  - Why unresolved: The current analysis relies on stability properties of FTRL that lead to this dmax^2 log(T) term. Removing it would require a fundamentally different analysis technique.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A new regret analysis technique that can separate delayed feedback and bandit feedback costs without introducing the dmax^2 log(T) term, demonstrated on at least one of the three settings (combinatorial semi-bandits, MDPs, or linear bandits).

- **Open Question 2**: Can the FTRL framework be extended to adversarial MDPs with unknown transition functions?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "in the MDPs with unknown transitions. Extending our ideas to that setting is not straightforward due the fact that standard analyses for this problem use a changing domain W."
  - Why unresolved: Standard analyses for MDPs with unknown transitions require a changing decision set W, while the current FTRL framework assumes a fixed W. This creates technical challenges in applying the current analysis.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A new algorithm and regret analysis for adversarial MDPs with unknown transitions that either adapts the FTRL framework to handle changing W or develops an entirely new analysis technique that can handle both delayed feedback and unknown transitions.

- **Open Question 3**: Can the results for non-delayed linear bandits with specific choice of W (as in Bubeck et al. 2012) be transferred to the delayed feedback setting?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as a direction for future work: "Finally, in the linear bandits setting it would be interesting to see whether the results for non-delayed feedback and the specific choice of W used by Bubeck et al. (2012) can be transferred to the delayed feedback setting."
  - Why unresolved: The specific choice of W in Bubeck et al. (2012) provides better regret bounds for non-delayed linear bandits, but it's unclear whether the analysis techniques used there can be adapted to handle delayed feedback.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A new algorithm for delayed linear bandits that uses the same W as Bubeck et al. (2012) and achieves better regret bounds than the current algorithm, along with a proof that the analysis can be adapted to handle delayed feedback.

## Limitations
- The analysis heavily depends on the independence assumption between loss estimates across rounds with the same delay, which may not hold in practical settings with aggregated or anonymous feedback.
- The Hessian stability conditions, while mild, require careful parameter tuning and may be violated in aggressive optimization scenarios.
- The decomposition approach may break down when delay distributions are adversarial or unknown, as the independence assumptions no longer hold.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The regret decomposition framework and its application to linear bandits; the FTRL stability analysis under Hessian conditions
- Medium Confidence: The extension to combinatorial semi-bandits with delay; the MDP analysis relies on results from other works and has less detailed exposition
- Low Confidence: The doubling trick implementation for unknown parameters and its impact on the final regret bounds
