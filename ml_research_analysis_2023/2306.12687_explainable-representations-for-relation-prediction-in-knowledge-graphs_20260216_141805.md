# Explainable Representations for Relation Prediction in Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12687
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12687
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: SEEK generates explainable KG embeddings by identifying shared semantic aspects between entities, achieving significantly better performance than standard methods while providing both necessary and sufficient explanations

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical trade-off between performance and explainability in knowledge graph embedding methods for relation prediction. The authors propose SEEK, a novel approach that generates explainable embeddings by representing entity pairs based on their shared semantic aspects (disjoint common ancestors in the ontology). SEEK identifies these aspects, learns representations for each subgraph, and aggregates them to create multi-faceted pair representations that are both informative and explainable. The method is evaluated on two real-world biomedical tasks - protein-protein interaction prediction and gene-disease association prediction - demonstrating significantly improved performance while providing interpretable explanations.

## Method Summary
SEEK generates explainable KG embeddings by first converting the KG to an RDF graph and learning embeddings for ontology classes using methods like RDF2Vec or OWL2Vec*. For each entity pair, it computes disjoint common ancestors of their associated ontology classes to identify shared semantic aspects. These aspects form subgraphs that capture relevant relationship dimensions. SEEK then aggregates the embeddings of these aspects to create pair representations. A supervised ML model (RF, XGB, or MLP) is trained on these representations for relation prediction. Explanations are generated by perturbing the pair representation - removing one aspect at a time to identify necessary aspects and using single aspects to identify sufficient ones.

## Key Results
- SEEK achieves significantly better F1-scores than standard KG embedding methods on both PPI and GDA prediction tasks
- Explanations generated by SEEK are concise (around 30% of shared semantic aspects) while maintaining predictive power
- The approach successfully identifies both necessary and sufficient semantic aspects for predictions without requiring model retraining

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SEEK generates explainable embeddings by representing entity pairs based on their shared semantic aspects (disjoint common ancestors in the ontology)
- Mechanism: For each entity pair, SEEK computes the disjoint common ancestors of their associated ontology classes. These shared semantic aspects form subgraphs that capture the relevant dimensions of the relationship. SEEK then aggregates the embeddings of these aspects to create a pair representation that is both informative and explainable
- Core assumption: Shared semantic aspects between entities contain the most relevant information for predicting their relationship, and these aspects can be effectively identified through disjoint common ancestor computation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It is based on identifying relevant shared semantic aspects (i.e., subgraphs) between entities and learning representations for each subgraph"
  - [section] "To compute the set of disjoint shared aspects, we first compute the disjoint common ancestors of C1 and C2"
- Break condition: If the ontology structure is too flat or if entities share too many ancestors (making disjoint common ancestors uninformative), the method loses its explanatory power

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SEEK explanations identify both necessary and sufficient semantic aspects for a prediction through perturbation of the pair representation
- Mechanism: After training a relation prediction model on the aggregated pair representations, SEEK generates explanations by creating perturbed versions of the representation - removing one aspect at a time for necessary explanations and using single aspects for sufficient explanations. This identifies which aspects are critical for the prediction
- Core assumption: The supervised model's behavior when aspects are added or removed reliably indicates their importance for the prediction
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "identifying both sufficient and necessary explanations based on shared semantic aspects"
  - [section] "A semantic aspect is considered necessary for a prediction if the predicted class changes when it is removed"
- Break condition: If the supervised model is highly unstable or non-differentiable in how it responds to aspect perturbations, the necessary/sufficient determination becomes unreliable

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SEEK avoids the computational burden of retraining that plagues other KG explanation methods
- Mechanism: Unlike methods that identify explanations by modifying the KG and retraining (e.g., Kelpie, CRIAGE), SEEK works with composite representations of ontology classes. Since these class embeddings are fixed and easy to modify by adding/removing aspects, explanations can be generated without retraining the model
- Core assumption: The ontology structure remains fixed during explanation generation, making class embeddings stable reference points
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "SEEK avoids this hurdle since it is based on composite representations of ontology classes, which are easy to modify and do not require retraining"
- Break condition: If the ontology itself changes (new classes added, hierarchies modified), the precomputed embeddings become invalid and explanations would require retraining

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Disjoint common ancestors in ontologies
  - Why needed here: This is the core mechanism for identifying shared semantic aspects between entities. Understanding how to compute disjoint common ancestors is essential for implementing SEEK's aspect identification
  - Quick check question: Given two sets of ontology classes C1={A,B} and C2={B,C}, where A ⊑ B and B ⊑ C, what would be the disjoint common ancestors of C1 and C2?

- Concept: Knowledge graph embedding methods (TransE, TransH, distMult, RDF2Vec, OWL2Vec*)
  - Why needed here: SEEK is agnostic to the specific KG embedding method used. A new engineer needs to understand these methods to implement SEEK extensions and to interpret results
  - Quick check question: What is the fundamental difference between translational methods (like TransE) and semantic matching methods (like distMult) in how they represent relations?

- Concept: Local post-hoc explanation methods
  - Why needed here: SEEK falls under this category, meaning it explains individual predictions rather than the global model. Understanding this distinction is important for correctly interpreting SEEK's explanations
  - Quick check question: How does a local post-hoc explanation differ from a global explanation in terms of what it reveals about the model?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: RDF graph converter -> KG embedding learner -> Aspect identifier -> Pair representation generator -> Relation prediction model -> Explanation generator
- Critical path: RDF conversion -> KG embedding learning -> Aspect identification -> Pair representation -> Prediction -> Explanation generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Aggregation method: Average vs. Hadamard vs. sum affects representation quality and computational efficiency
  - Embedding method choice: Different KG embedding methods capture different structural properties
  - Supervised model selection: Ensemble methods vs. neural networks affect performance and explanation quality
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor prediction performance: Likely issues with aspect identification or embedding quality
  - Overly long explanations: May indicate too many shared ancestors or need for better filtering
  - Inconsistent explanations: Could signal instability in the supervised model
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify disjoint common ancestor computation on a small ontology with known structure
  2. Test pair representation generation with different aggregation methods on simple entity pairs
  3. Validate explanation generation by manually checking if removed aspects actually affect predictions on a small dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper mentions that user studies with biomedical domain experts are planned for future work to evaluate SEEK explanations, but does not provide results from such studies. This leaves open the question of how well SEEK's explanations align with domain expert knowledge and whether they provide meaningful insights for biomedical applications.

## Limitations
- Dependency on high-quality ontologies with meaningful hierarchical structures
- Performance may degrade when entities share many common ancestors
- Explanation quality depends heavily on the chosen KG embedding method and aggregation operator

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: SEEK's core mechanism of using disjoint common ancestors to identify shared semantic aspects is well-founded and clearly specified
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that SEEK achieves "significantly better performance" than standard methods needs verification across diverse datasets and embedding configurations
- **Medium Confidence**: The explanation generation through perturbation is theoretically sound but may be sensitive to model choice and aspect representation quality

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset validation**: Test SEEK on additional biomedical KGs beyond PPI and GDA to verify generalizability of performance improvements
2. **Ablation study**: Systematically evaluate the impact of different aggregation methods (average, sum, Hadamard) and KG embedding approaches on both prediction accuracy and explanation quality
3. **Stability analysis**: Assess how sensitive the necessary/sufficient explanations are to minor perturbations in the input data and model parameters
