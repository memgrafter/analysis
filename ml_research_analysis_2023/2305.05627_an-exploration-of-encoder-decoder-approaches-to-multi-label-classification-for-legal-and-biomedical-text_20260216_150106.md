# An Exploration of Encoder-Decoder Approaches to Multi-Label Classification for Legal and Biomedical Text

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05627
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05627
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: T5 encoder-decoder models outperform encoder-only models for multi-label classification, with non-autoregressive approaches yielding best results.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores four multi-label classification methods using T5 encoder-decoder models on legal and biomedical text datasets. The study compares two encoder-only approaches (Encoder+Head, LWAN) with two encoder-decoder methods (Seq2Seq, T5Enc) across four datasets with varying label granularity. Results demonstrate that encoder-decoder methods consistently outperform encoder-only approaches, with the non-autoregressive T5Enc method showing the best overall performance. The advantage is particularly pronounced for rare labels and finer granularity label sets, suggesting decoder components effectively capture label dependencies and semantic nuances.

## Method Summary
The study compares four multi-label classification methods using T5 models on four datasets (UKLEX, EURLEX, BIOASQ, MIMIC-III) with two levels of label granularity each. Two methods use only the encoder (Encoder+Head and LWAN), while two utilize both encoder and decoder (Seq2Seq and T5Enc). T5Enc generates label representations in parallel using fixed label descriptors as inputs to the decoder, constraining the output space to valid labels. All methods are evaluated using micro-F1 and macro-F1 scores, with T5 models of different sizes fine-tuned using Adafactor optimizer with fixed learning rate 1e-4.

## Key Results
- Encoder-decoder methods consistently outperform encoder-only methods across all datasets and metrics
- T5Enc (non-autoregressive) achieves the best overall performance, particularly for rare labels and finer granularity
- Decoder self-attention contributes significantly to performance, with ablation showing 0.4-2.2% absolute drops
- Label descriptor form (original, simplified, numerical) shows mixed performance differences without clear superiority

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: T5 encoder-decoder models outperform encoder-only models in multi-label classification due to their ability to generate label-aware representations through decoder self-attention.
- Mechanism: The decoder in T5Enc uses fixed label descriptors as inputs and applies self-attention across multiple layers to build contextualized representations for each label, conditioned on the input document. This allows the model to capture label dependencies and semantic nuances better than encoder-only approaches.
- Core assumption: Label dependencies exist in the data and can be effectively modeled through bidirectional self-attention in the decoder.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "encoder-decoder methods outperform encoder-only methods, with a growing advantage on more complex datasets and labeling schemes of finer granularity"
  - [section 5.5]: "We measure the contribution of this system component by ablating it, i.e. training T5Enc models with no self-attention. In Table 9, we report results on Level 2 datasets under No attention... We observe that without self-attention, performance suffers considerably for all datasets"
  - [corpus]: Weak - related papers focus on encoder-only or other architectures, not directly on decoder self-attention contribution.
- Break condition: If label dependencies are minimal or the decoder cannot effectively condition on input context, the performance advantage disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Non-autoregressive use of the decoder (T5Enc) is more effective than autoregressive generation (Seq2Seq) for multi-label classification.
- Mechanism: T5Enc generates all label representations in parallel using fixed label descriptors, constraining the output space to valid labels. Seq2Seq must learn this constraint during training and can generate invalid labels, leading to errors.
- Core assumption: The output space in multi-label classification is discrete and known, making parallel generation more efficient and accurate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Using encoder-decoder models in a non-autoregressive fashion, in particular, yields the best performance overall"
  - [section 5.2]: "Models trained on UKLEX and EURLEX therefore seem to interpret the output space as open-ended and on occasion generate novel labels"
  - [section 5.6]: "the total number of novel labels generated is negligible, so this could not explain the lower performance of this approach compared to T5Enc"
  - [corpus]: Moderate - related work on encoder-only models doesn't directly compare autoregressive vs non-autoregressive decoder use.
- Break condition: If the output space is truly open-ended or label generation order matters, autoregressive approaches may become superior.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Label descriptors provide semantic information that improves classification performance compared to arbitrary numerical labels.
- Mechanism: Natural language label descriptors are represented in T5's embedding space and benefit from pre-training, whereas numerical labels are randomly initialized and must be learned from scratch.
- Core assumption: Semantic information in label descriptors transfers from pre-training and provides inductive bias for classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2]: "We observe mixed rankings between the three forms of label descriptors across different metrics and datasets, with slight advantage for a lexical form over the arbitrary numerical one"
  - [introduction]: "Label verbalizers are words in natural language which verbalize the underlying semantics of a given class... This can be more optimal especially for few- and zero-shot labels"
  - [corpus]: Weak - related papers don't specifically address label descriptor form or semantic transfer.
- Break condition: If label descriptors are too complex or domain-specific to transfer well from pre-training, numerical labels may perform equally or better.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input document → T5 encoder → decoder (for encoder-decoder methods) → label-wise representations → classification heads → output labels
- Critical path: Input → T5 encoder → decoder (for encoder-decoder methods) → label-wise representations → classification heads → output
- Design tradeoffs: Encoder-only methods use fewer parameters but lose decoder's ability to model label dependencies; Seq2Seq can generate invalid labels but models label order; T5Enc constrains output space but requires fixed label descriptors
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on rare labels suggests insufficient decoder capacity; generation of invalid labels indicates Seq2Seq struggles with output space; comparable encoder-only performance suggests decoder contribution is minimal
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare Encoder+Head vs T5Enc on a small legal dataset to verify encoder-decoder advantage
  2. Ablate decoder self-attention in T5Enc to measure its contribution to performance
  3. Test different label descriptor forms (original, simplified, numerical) to verify semantic transfer hypothesis

## Limitations

- Performance gains of encoder-decoder methods vary considerably across datasets, suggesting context-dependent rather than universal advantages
- Experiments focus exclusively on T5 architecture, limiting generalizability to other encoder-decoder models
- Label descriptor form experiments show no clear superiority for semantic forms, contradicting theoretical advantages of pre-trained knowledge transfer

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core finding that encoder-decoder methods outperform encoder-only methods is well-supported by consistent results across four datasets and two evaluation metrics.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific claim that non-autoregressive decoder use (T5Enc) is superior to autoregressive generation (Seq2Seq) is supported by experimental results but has limitations in explaining the exact mechanism.

**Low Confidence**: The hypothesis that label descriptors provide semantic advantage through pre-training transfer is weakly supported by mixed empirical results.
