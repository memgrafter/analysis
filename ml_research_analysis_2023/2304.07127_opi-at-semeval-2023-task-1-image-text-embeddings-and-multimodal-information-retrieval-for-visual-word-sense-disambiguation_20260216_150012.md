# OPI at SemEval 2023 Task 1: Image-Text Embeddings and Multimodal Information Retrieval for Visual Word Sense Disambiguation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07127
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07127
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: 70.49% accuracy on multilingual task; 84.02% on English subtask

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the OPI system for the SemEval 2023 Task 1 on multilingual visual word sense disambiguation. The system leverages CLIP-based multimodal embeddings, learning-to-rank methods, and knowledge-based retrieval from Wikipedia and lexical databases to disambiguate word senses by matching images to text contexts. The approach ranked third overall and first in the Persian subtask. The system's strength lies in combining deep multimodal representations with supplementary knowledge to improve sense disambiguation accuracy.

## Method Summary
The OPI system uses a CLIP-based classifier as its backbone, augmented with knowledge retrieved from Wikipedia and lexical databases to enrich the input representation. The approach combines multimodal embeddings with learning-to-rank techniques to select the most appropriate image for a given word sense in context. The system processes both image and text inputs, aligning them in a shared embedding space to determine the correct sense. The knowledge-based enrichment aims to provide additional context and disambiguating cues for polysemous words.

## Key Results
- 70.49% accuracy on the multilingual visual word sense disambiguation task
- 84.02% accuracy on the English subtask, the highest among all submissions
- Third place overall in SemEval 2023 Task 1; first in the Persian subtask

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system's effectiveness stems from leveraging CLIP's strong multimodal alignment capabilities to bridge visual and textual information. By enriching this with knowledge from Wikipedia and lexical databases, the model gains additional context for disambiguating word senses. The learning-to-rank framework allows the system to compare multiple candidate images against the context, selecting the best match. The combination of deep multimodal embeddings and supplementary knowledge provides robustness across languages and senses.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
CLIP model -> Learning-to-rank module -> Wikipedia/lexical knowledge retrieval -> Image-text sense matching

**Critical Path**
Input context and candidate images -> CLIP embeddings -> Knowledge retrieval and augmentation -> Learning-to-rank scoring -> Best image selection

**Design Tradeoffs**
- CLIP backbone offers strong multimodal alignment but may introduce domain biases.
- Knowledge retrieval enriches context but adds latency and dependency on external sources.
- Learning-to-rank enables fine-grained comparison but requires careful tuning of ranking criteria.

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor performance on culturally specific or out-of-domain images.
- Reduced accuracy for languages with limited Wikipedia coverage.
- Over-reliance on retrieved knowledge may dilute multimodal signal.

**First Experiments**
1. Ablation study: Remove knowledge retrieval to measure its impact on accuracy.
2. Cross-validation: Assess robustness and statistical significance of results across languages.
3. Domain shift test: Evaluate system on out-of-domain or culturally diverse image-text pairs.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation beyond accuracy; robustness and error analysis not fully explored.
- Reliance on CLIP may introduce domain-specific biases, especially for non-English languages.
- Contribution of knowledge-based enrichment not rigorously validated.

## Confidence

- **Medium**: Contribution of knowledge-based enrichment is plausible but not rigorously validated.
- **Low**: Robustness across languages and impact of domain bias are not fully addressed.
