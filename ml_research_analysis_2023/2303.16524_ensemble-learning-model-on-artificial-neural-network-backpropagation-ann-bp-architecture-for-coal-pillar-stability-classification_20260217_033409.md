# Ensemble Learning Model on Artificial Neural Network-Backpropagation (ANN-BP) Architecture for Coal Pillar Stability Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16524
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16524
- Reference count: 19
- This study proposes an ANN-BP ensemble model with ReLU, ELU, and GELU activations to classify coal pillar stability into four categories, achieving high accuracy and F2-score for critical cases.

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the critical challenge of accurately classifying coal pillar stability in underground mines to ensure worker safety. The authors propose a novel ensemble learning approach using Artificial Neural Networks with Backpropagation (ANN-BP), incorporating three different activation functions (ReLU, ELU, and GELU) to mitigate the vanishing gradient problem. By expanding the pillar stability classification from two to four categories based on Safety Factor (SF) values, the model better discriminates between cases where SF theory matches reality and where it does not. The ensemble approach significantly improves classification accuracy compared to single ANN-BP models, achieving an average accuracy of 87.98% and an F2-score of 99.27% for the most critical class (failed with suitable SF).

## Method Summary
The study uses 423 case histories from South African coal mines with five features (pillar width, mining height, bord width, depth to floor, and width/height ratio). Pillar stability is classified into four categories (F0, F1, I0, I1) based on SF values. Three ANN-BP models with different activation functions (ReLU, ELU, GELU) are trained with ensemble learning using bagging and SMOTE oversampling. The ensemble combines predictions via majority vote, with F0 as default in ties. Models are evaluated using accuracy, F1-score, and F2-score across 10 trials with various data splits (80:20, 70:30 training:testing; 80:10:10, 70:15:15 training:validation:testing).

## Key Results
- The ensemble learning model significantly improves performance over single ANN-BP models, achieving average accuracy of 87.98%.
- GELU activation function consistently outperformed ReLU and ELU across all evaluation metrics.
- The model achieved an F2-score of 99.27% for the most critical class (F1 - failed with suitable SF), demonstrating exceptional sensitivity for detecting dangerous conditions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deep ensemble learning reduces prediction variance and improves classification accuracy for coal pillar stability.
- Mechanism: By training three ANN-BP models with different activation functions (ReLU, ELU, GELU) on bootstrapped samples and combining their predictions via majority vote, the ensemble smooths out individual model errors and captures diverse decision boundaries.
- Core assumption: Each base model has similar bias but different variance; ensemble voting will favor the most reliable class (F0) when disagreements occur.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "the ensemble learning model significantly improves the performance of single ANN-BP models" [section] "The ensemble learning process begins with bootstrapping... classification of pillar stability using ensemble learning with the ANN-BP basic model was also carried out"
- Break condition: If base models are highly correlated (similar errors), ensemble gains will be minimal; if F0 is not truly the lowest-risk class, majority vote may misclassify.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GELU activation function mitigates vanishing gradients and yields better performance than ReLU and ELU.
- Mechanism: GELU's smooth, probabilistic gating allows stable gradient flow even in deep networks, enabling more effective weight updates and better generalization on pillar stability classification.
- Core assumption: The vanishing gradient problem is present in the used ANN-BP architecture and significantly harms performance.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "overcome the vanishing gradient problem" and "GELU activation function consistently outperformed" [section] "Several activation functions that have been proposed in recent years to overcome the vanishing gradient problem include ReLU, ELU, and GELU"
- Break condition: If the network depth or training regime does not cause vanishing gradients, switching activation may have negligible impact.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Expanding pillar stability labels into four categories (F0, F1, I0, I1) based on SF suitability improves model discrimination and safety prioritization.
- Mechanism: Additional granularity allows the model to distinguish between cases where SF theory matches reality (F0, I0) and cases where it does not (F1, I1), focusing risk mitigation on the most dangerous class (F1).
- Core assumption: The four-way labeling captures meaningful distinctions in pillar behavior that are relevant to mining safety.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "expand the pillar stability classification into four categories based on the Safety Factor (SF) value" [section] "The initial labels in the dataset will be expanded from 2 categories (intact and failed) to 4 categories... because F1 is the most dangerous class"
- Break condition: If SF thresholds are poorly chosen or the data distribution does not support four classes, labeling may add noise rather than signal.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input features (5) -> Hidden layers (512, 256, 256, 128 neurons) -> Output (4 classes) -> Base models (ReLU, ELU, GELU) -> Ensemble (bootstrap + majority vote)
- Critical path: Preprocessing → Bootstrapped training of 3 ANN-BPs → Ensemble voting → Evaluation (accuracy, F1, F2)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More hidden neurons → higher capacity but risk overfitting; fewer → underfit
  - Bootstrap percentage → 70% (more diversity) vs 90% (more stability)
  - Activation choice → ReLU (fast, but may die) vs ELU (smooth negative) vs GELU (probabilistic gating)
- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy with high variance → models not learning common patterns
  - Majority vote always choosing F0 → class imbalance or over-prioritization
  - Vanishing gradients → slow convergence or stuck weights
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train single ANN-BP with ReLU on 80/20 split; record accuracy and F1 per class
  2. Repeat with ELU and GELU activations; compare per-class F1 scores
  3. Build ensemble with 70% bootstrap; compare average accuracy and F2-score for F1 against best single model

## Limitations

- The study relies on a single proprietary dataset from South African coal mines (423 samples), limiting generalizability to other geological conditions.
- The ensemble method's effectiveness is demonstrated through 10 trials but lacks statistical significance testing to confirm improvements are not due to chance.
- The vanishing gradient problem's impact is assumed rather than empirically measured, and the choice of four categories based on SF values is not validated against alternative labeling schemes.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The architectural design (ANN-BP with specified layers/neurons) and ensemble methodology (bagging with majority vote) are clearly described and reproducible.
- **Medium confidence**: The superiority of GELU activation is demonstrated but not statistically validated against alternatives.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that F1 is the most dangerous class is logical but relies on the chosen SF thresholds without sensitivity analysis.

## Next Checks
