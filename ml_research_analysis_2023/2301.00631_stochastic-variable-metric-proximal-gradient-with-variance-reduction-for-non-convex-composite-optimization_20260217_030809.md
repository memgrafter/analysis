# Stochastic Variable Metric Proximal Gradient with variance reduction for non-convex composite optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00631
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00631
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: 3P-SPIDER algorithm for non-convex composite optimization with finite sum structure

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces 3P-SPIDER, a novel algorithm designed to solve non-convex composite optimization problems with finite sum structure. The method combines a preconditioned forward operator, a variable metric proximity operator, and a mini-batch strategy with variance reduction. By addressing three key constraints - using possibly preconditioned operators, allowing deterministic or random approximations of the forward operator, and reducing the variability induced by the finite sum setting - 3P-SPIDER extends existing algorithms to composite optimization scenarios where the forward operator cannot be computed in closed form. The paper provides a non-asymptotic convergence analysis in expectation and studies the algorithm's complexity.

## Method Summary
3P-SPIDER is an algorithm that tackles non-convex composite optimization problems by integrating three key components: a preconditioned forward operator, a variable metric proximity operator, and a mini-batch strategy with variance reduction. The preconditioning allows for potentially ill-conditioned problems to be handled more effectively, while the variable metric proximity operator provides flexibility in the optimization process. The mini-batch strategy with variance reduction addresses the challenges posed by the finite sum structure of the problem, improving the algorithm's stability and convergence properties. The method is specifically designed to handle cases where the forward operator cannot be computed in closed form, making it applicable to a broader range of optimization problems.

## Key Results
- 3P-SPIDER provides non-asymptotic convergence guarantees in expectation for non-convex composite optimization with finite sum structure
- The algorithm successfully extends existing methods to handle cases where the forward operator cannot be computed in closed form
- Numerical experiments on a logistic regression model with random effects demonstrate the effectiveness of 3P-SPIDER

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of 3P-SPIDER stems from its ability to address three key challenges in non-convex composite optimization simultaneously. The preconditioning strategy allows the algorithm to handle ill-conditioned problems more effectively by improving the conditioning of the optimization landscape. The variable metric proximity operator provides flexibility in the optimization process, allowing for better adaptation to the problem structure. The mini-batch strategy with variance reduction addresses the inherent variability in finite sum problems, leading to more stable and efficient convergence. By combining these elements, 3P-SPIDER can navigate complex non-convex landscapes more effectively than traditional methods.

## Foundational Learning

**Non-convex composite optimization**: Optimization problems where the objective function is the sum of a non-convex smooth function and a non-smooth convex function. Why needed: Most real-world optimization problems are non-convex and often involve composite structures. Quick check: Can you identify the smooth and non-smooth components in a given problem?

**Variance reduction techniques**: Methods that reduce the variance in stochastic gradient estimates to improve convergence. Why needed: Finite sum problems inherently have high variance in gradient estimates, which can slow down convergence. Quick check: How does the mini-batch size affect the variance in gradient estimates?

**Preconditioning**: The process of transforming a problem to improve its conditioning. Why needed: Ill-conditioned problems can lead to slow convergence or numerical instability in optimization algorithms. Quick check: What is the effect of preconditioning on the condition number of a problem?

**Variable metric methods**: Optimization techniques that use a changing metric (like a changing Hessian approximation) to guide the search direction. Why needed: Fixed metrics may not be optimal for all parts of the optimization landscape. Quick check: How does a variable metric differ from a fixed metric in terms of convergence behavior?

**Non-asymptotic convergence analysis**: Analysis that provides convergence guarantees for a finite number of iterations, rather than just in the limit. Why needed: Provides more practical insights into algorithm performance and complexity. Quick check: What is the difference between asymptotic and non-asymptotic convergence guarantees?

**Component map**: Preconditioning (A) -> Forward operator approximation (B) -> Variable metric proximity operator (C) -> Mini-batch variance reduction (D)

**Critical path**: The algorithm iteratively applies the preconditioned forward operator, followed by the variable metric proximity operator, with variance reduction applied at each step. **Design tradeoffs**: The choice of preconditioning strategy balances between improving conditioning and computational overhead. The variable metric approach trades off between adaptability and stability. The mini-batch size affects the variance reduction versus computational cost tradeoff.
