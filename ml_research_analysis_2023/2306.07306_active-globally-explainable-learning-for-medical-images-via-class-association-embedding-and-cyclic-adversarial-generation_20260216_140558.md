# Active Globally Explainable Learning for Medical Images via Class Association Embedding and Cyclic Adversarial Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07306
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07306
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: CAE generates synthetic images preserving individual characteristics while changing class assignments, enabling visualization of global classification rules and producing more precise saliency maps for medical image classification.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of explainable AI (XAI) in medical image classification by proposing a method called class association embedding (CAE). The core idea is to encode medical images into two subspaces: one for class-related features (style codes) and another for individual-specific features (individual-style codes). By combining these codes from different samples, the method generates synthetic images that preserve individual characteristics while changing class assignments. This enables the visualization of global classification rules and provides more precise saliency maps compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
CAE uses an encoder-decoder architecture with separate encoders for class-style (Ec) and individual-style (Es) codes. The method employs random shuffling paired training, where samples from different classes are paired and their class-style codes are swapped during training. This enables learning of class-separated style spaces without paired supervision. The decoder generates synthetic images from combined class-style and individual-style codes. Multi-class adversarial training with reconstruction and cyclic losses ensures the encoded space preserves meaningful relationships. Active manipulation of class-style vectors enables precise saliency map generation through guided counterfactual examples.

## Key Results
- Class-swapping consistency rates of over 92% on retinal OCT and fundus image datasets
- Saliency maps with clearer contours and more accurate localization of regions of interest compared to state-of-the-art XAI methods (LIME, Occlusion, Grad-CAM)
- Effective generation of synthetic images that preserve individual characteristics while changing class assignments
- Demonstrated visualization of disease pathology through traversal in class-style space

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Class association embedding (CAE) enables global explanation by learning separate subspaces for class-related and individual-specific features. The encoder splits image features into class-style (CS) and individual-style (IS) codes. By recombining IS from one sample with CS from another, synthetic images can be generated that preserve individual characteristics while changing class assignments. This enables visualization of global classification rules through synthetic transitions. The core assumption is that medical images contain separable class-related and individual-specific features that can be encoded into distinct subspaces.

### Mechanism 2
Random shuffling paired training enables learning of class-separated style spaces without paired supervision. By randomly pairing samples from different classes and swapping their class-style codes during training, the model learns to separate class-related features from individual features. The cyclic adversarial training with reconstruction losses ensures the encoded space preserves meaningful relationships. The core assumption is that random pairing of samples from different classes provides sufficient supervision to learn class separation without explicit paired data.

### Mechanism 3
Active manipulation of class-style vectors enables precise saliency map generation through guided counterfactual examples. By creating paths in the class-style space between different classes and generating synthetic samples along these paths, the model can identify regions of interest through frame-to-frame differences weighted by classification probability changes. The core assumption is that small movements in the class-style space correspond to meaningful pathological changes that can be visualized through synthetic transitions.

  - Quick check question: How does the discriminator loss encourage both realism and correct class assignment in synthetic samples?

  - Why needed here: Understanding how to separate style/content features is crucial for grasping the class-style vs individual-style separation
  - Quick check question: What's the difference between the style transfer in CAE versus traditional style-GAN approaches?

- Concept: Counterfactual explanations and perturbation analysis
  - Why needed here: The active XAI framework generates counterfactuals by traversing class-style space, which requires understanding counterfactual reasoning
  - Quick check question: How does generating counterfactuals through style-space traversal differ from pixel-level perturbations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image → Ec+Es → class-style + individual-style codes → G → synthetic image → D → classification → loss computation → parameter updates

- Critical path: Image → Ec+Es → class-style + individual-style codes → G → synthetic image → D → classification → loss computation → parameter updates

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Separate encoders vs single encoder with feature separation: Separate encoders provide cleaner separation but may lose some feature correlation information
  - Random pairing vs curriculum learning: Random pairing is simpler but may be less efficient than adaptive pairing strategies
  - Linear paths vs learned paths: Linear interpolation is simple but may not capture non-linear relationships in the style space

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor class transitions: If synthetic images don't change class assignment when class-style codes are swapped
  - Identity loss: If synthetic images don't preserve individual characteristics when individual-style codes are reused
  - Non-separated space: If class-style codes from different classes overlap significantly in PCA visualization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify code separation: Train on simple dataset (e.g., MNIST with different writing styles), visualize PCA of class-style codes to check separation
  2. Test class transition: Generate synthetic samples by swapping class-style codes between different classes, verify with external classifier
  3. Validate saliency generation: Compare saliency maps generated through CAE with ground truth pathology regions on medical dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can CAE fully represent all behaviors of the black-box model it explains? The paper acknowledges this as a limitation and future research direction.
- How does the performance of CAE scale with increasing dataset size and class complexity? The paper demonstrates effectiveness on two medical image datasets but does not explore scalability.
- What is the optimal architecture and hyperparameter configuration for the encoder, decoder, and discriminator components of CAE? The paper presents one configuration but does not explore the design space.
- How robust is CAE to adversarial attacks and out-of-distribution samples? The paper does not address robustness to adversarial examples or performance on OOD samples.
- How can the computational efficiency of CAE be further improved for real-time medical applications? While the paper shows CAE is faster than perturbation-based methods, it does not explore advanced optimization techniques.

## Limitations
- The method requires paired samples for training through random shuffling, but the effectiveness of this approach versus unsupervised disentanglement methods is not established
- Class-style and individual-style separation relies on architectural priors rather than learned disentanglement, which may limit generalization to other medical imaging domains
- The paper claims superior saliency map quality but only compares against baseline XAI methods without ablation studies on the CAE architecture components

## Confidence
- High: The core mechanism of encoding medical images into class-related and individual-specific subspaces, and using synthetic transitions for explanation
- Medium: The effectiveness of random shuffling paired training and the superiority of generated saliency maps over existing methods
- Low: Claims about cross-domain generalization and the necessity of paired training approach

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation Study**: Remove the separate encoders and train with a single encoder that learns feature separation through adversarial losses. Compare saliency map quality and class transition accuracy.
2. **Cross-Domain Test**: Apply the CAE framework to a different medical imaging modality (e.g., chest X-rays or histopathology slides) and evaluate if the class-individual separation still produces meaningful explanations.
3. **Unsupervised Alternative**: Replace random shuffling paired training with an unsupervised disentanglement approach (e.g., contrastive learning) and compare the quality of learned subspaces and generated explanations.
