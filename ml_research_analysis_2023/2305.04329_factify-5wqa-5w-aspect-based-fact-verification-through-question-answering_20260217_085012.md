# FACTIFY-5WQA: 5W Aspect-based Fact Verification through Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04329
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04329
- Reference count: 26
- This paper proposes a novel approach to fact verification by breaking down claims into 5W aspects (who, what, when, where, why) and generating question-answer pairs for each aspect

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to fact verification by decomposing claims into 5W aspects (who, what, when, where, why) and generating question-answer pairs for each aspect. The authors created a semi-automatically generated dataset called FACTIFY-5WQA containing 395,019 claims with relevant 5W QAs. The system leverages semantic role labeling and masked language models to generate QA pairs, then uses a baseline QA system to automatically locate answers from evidence documents. The proposed method demonstrates high accuracy in identifying false claims and providing explanations for why they are false.

## Method Summary
The FACTIFY-5WQA system breaks down fact verification into a multi-step process. First, claims are decomposed into 5W aspects using semantic role labeling to identify the relevant entities and relationships. For each aspect, a masked language model generates specific question-answer pairs that capture the semantic meaning. These QA pairs serve as probes to verify the claim against evidence documents. A baseline QA system then automatically locates answers within the evidence documents, with discrepancies between the generated QA pairs and retrieved answers indicating potential false claims. The system achieves high accuracy in identifying false claims and provides explanations by highlighting which 5W aspects failed verification.

## Key Results
- FACTIFY-5WQA dataset contains 395,019 claims with relevant 5W QAs
- Proposed method achieves high accuracy in identifying false claims
- System provides explanations for why claims are false by highlighting failed 5W aspects

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The 5W decomposition approach works by creating multiple orthogonal verification paths for each claim. By breaking claims into who, what, when, where, and why aspects, the system can isolate specific portions of a claim that may be false rather than treating the entire claim as a monolithic unit. The QA generation process creates natural language probes that can be matched against evidence documents, leveraging existing QA technology for fact verification. This approach is particularly effective because it transforms the complex task of claim verification into a series of simpler, more focused verification tasks that can be handled by specialized models.

## Foundational Learning
**Semantic Role Labeling**: Needed to identify the predicate-argument structure of claims and extract 5W aspects. Quick check: Verify SRL correctly identifies agents, patients, locations, and temporal expressions in diverse claim types.

**Masked Language Modeling**: Used to generate question-answer pairs by predicting masked tokens in claim templates. Quick check: Ensure generated QAs maintain semantic coherence with original claims and cover all 5W aspects.

**Question Answering Systems**: The baseline QA system must accurately retrieve answers from evidence documents. Quick check: Validate QA system performance on both extractive and abstractive answer types across different domains.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Claims -> SRL -> 5W Aspects -> Masked LM -> QA Pairs -> Evidence Documents -> QA System -> Verification Results

Critical Path: The core verification pipeline follows: Claims → Semantic Role Labeling → 5W Aspect Extraction → QA Pair Generation → Evidence Retrieval → Answer Extraction → Claim Verification

Design Tradeoffs: The system trades computational complexity for verification granularity. While 5W decomposition increases processing steps, it provides more interpretable results and targeted false claim identification. The semi-automatic dataset generation balances scalability with quality control.

Failure Signatures: System failures typically manifest as: (1) SRL misidentifying aspect boundaries, (2) QA pairs that don't align with claim semantics, (3) evidence documents lacking relevant information, or (4) QA system failing to extract correct answers from evidence.

## Limitations
- Semi-automatic dataset generation may introduce systematic biases through SRL and masked LM artifacts
- Limited evaluation of true claim verification performance, focusing primarily on false claim detection
- Explanation quality assessment lacks detailed human evaluation metrics or interpretability measures
