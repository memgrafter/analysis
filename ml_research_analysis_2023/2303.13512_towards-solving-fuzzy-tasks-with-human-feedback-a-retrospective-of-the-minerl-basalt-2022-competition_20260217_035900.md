# Towards Solving Fuzzy Tasks with Human Feedback: A Retrospective of the MineRL BASALT 2022 Competition

## Executive Summary
The MineRL BASALT 2022 competition aimed to advance algorithms for fine-tuning foundation models from human feedback in Minecraft environments. Participants tackled four open-world tasks: FindCave, MakeWaterfall, CreateVillageAnimalPen, and BuildVillageHouse. The competition demonstrated significant progress compared to the previous year, with winning approaches combining machine learning techniques and human knowledge. The evaluation methodology using Amazon Mechanical Turk for human judgments proved effective, while highlighting challenges in consistency and generalizability of results.

## Method Summary
The competition employed a human feedback-based evaluation approach where participants developed algorithms to solve Minecraft tasks without explicit reward functions. The top solutions utilized a combination of object detection, classifiers, and scripted actions. The winning approach, GoUp, decomposed tasks into components solvable by code and those requiring machine learning. Evaluation was conducted through Amazon Mechanical Turk, where human judges assessed task completion based on video demonstrations of agent behavior.

## Key Results
- The winning approach GoUp achieved an overall score of 2.09 by decomposing tasks into code-solvable and ML-solvable components
- Participants demonstrated increased performance and interest compared to the previous year's competition
- The evaluation methodology using human judgments through Amazon Mechanical Turk proved viable for assessing task completion

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging human feedback to guide foundation model fine-tuning in environments without explicit reward functions. By decomposing tasks into manageable components and combining scripted actions with machine learning, participants could effectively navigate the open-world Minecraft environment. The human evaluation methodology provided nuanced assessment of task completion that traditional reward functions cannot capture.

**Design Tradeoffs**:

- Tradeoff between evaluation consistency and task complexity
- Choice between model complexity and computational efficiency

**Failure Signatures**:
- Inconsistent human evaluation scores
- Inability to generalize across task variations
- Failure in object detection leading to incorrect actions
- Scripted actions not handling edge cases

## Limitations
- Human evaluation methodology introduces potential variability in scoring consistency
- Results may not generalize beyond the Minecraft domain
- Specific tasks chosen may not represent the full spectrum of fuzzy tasks

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Competition successfully attracted increased participation and demonstrated viability of human feedback for task evaluation
- **Medium Confidence**: Effectiveness of combining scripted actions with machine learning approaches for fuzzy task completion
- **Low Confidence**: Scalability of approaches to tasks beyond Minecraft domain and more complex open-world scenarios
