# Pretrained Embeddings for E-commerce Machine Learning: When it Fails and Why?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04330
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04330
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Authors investigate why pretrained embeddings fail in e-commerce ML systems, identifying encoding/decoding mismatch as the cause of instability and developing kernel-based metrics to predict performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical issues of instability and unpredictability in pretrained embeddings for e-commerce machine learning systems. Through systematic experiments across multiple datasets and tasks, the authors demonstrate that downstream performance can vary significantly even with identical pretraining configurations, primarily due to mismatched encoding/decoding structures between pretraining and downstream models. They propose kernel-based metrics that can predict embedding performance interactively and scalably, validated through both offline benchmarks and online A/B testing on a major e-commerce platform.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct experiments using Item2vec and BERT models for pretraining on e-commerce datasets (Instacart, Amazon Electronics, MovieLens-1M). They evaluate pretrained embeddings using standard downstream tasks (entity classification, sequential recommendation) and propose kernel-based metrics to predict performance. The method involves training embeddings with varying hyperparameters, computing kernel alignment between embedding and task kernels, and correlating these metrics with actual downstream performance. Online validation is performed through A/B/C testing on the ECOM platform.

## Key Results
- Pretrained embeddings show significant instability in downstream performance when encoding/decoding structures don't match between pretraining and downstream models
- Kernel-based metrics strongly correlate with actual downstream performance, providing a scalable solution for evaluating pretrained embeddings
- Online A/B testing validates that matching pretraining-downstream configurations leads to more stable and predictable performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pretrained embeddings encode information in a specific way that downstream models must decode using matching structures to maintain stability.
- Mechanism: When pretraining uses inner product encoding (like CL-based methods), the embeddings are optimized for retrieval via inner product. If downstream models use incompatible decoding (like linear regression), additional noise is introduced, causing instability.
- Core assumption: The pretraining algorithm's optimization objective implicitly defines how information should be accessed and decoded downstream.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the design of the pretraining and downstream models, particularly how they encode and decode information via embedding vectors, can have a profound impact"
  - [section] "using a matching pretraining-downstream design is crucial for the stability of downstream performance"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly address encoding/decoding mismatch
- Break condition: If pretraining and downstream use the same encoding/decoding structure, or if pretraining uses feed-forward encoding compatible with downstream linear models.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Kernel-based metrics can predict downstream performance by measuring alignment between pretrained embedding kernels and task-specific kernels.
- Mechanism: The inner product of pretrained embeddings defines a kernel 𝐾𝜙. The downstream task's labeling mechanism defines another kernel 𝐾𝑌. The alignment E[𝐾𝑌(𝑦,𝑦′)𝐾𝜙(𝑥,𝑥′)] predicts performance independent of downstream model structure.
- Core assumption: The kernel perspective captures the essential information content of embeddings relevant to the task, regardless of downstream architecture.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we establish a principled perspective of pre-trained embeddings via the lens of kernel analysis, which can be used to evaluate their predictability, interactively and scalably"
  - [section] "the quantity of E[𝐾𝑌(𝑦,𝑦′)𝐾𝜙(𝑥,𝑥′)] can reasonably predict the downstream performance of 𝜙(𝑥)"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus focuses on related embedding topics but not kernel-based predictability metrics
- Break condition: If the kernel alignment metric doesn't capture the true task-relevant information, or if the task kernel is too complex for simple alignment measurement.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory explains how pretrained embeddings influence downstream generalization through kernel alignment.
- Mechanism: For wide neural networks, the NTK determines training dynamics and generalization. Since NTK is a function of the embedding kernel 𝐾𝜙, well-aligned 𝐾𝜙 leads to better NTK properties and downstream performance.
- Core assumption: Wide neural networks with scaled initialization behave like kernel methods during training, with NTK determining their learning trajectory.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "As NTK(𝜙(𝑥),𝜙(𝑥′)) = 𝑔(𝐾𝜙(𝑥,𝑥′)), finding a good 𝑔(·) becomes easier in downstream task"
  - [section] "NTK theory sheds light on how pretrained embeddings influence the performance of downstream task"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus mentions transformers but not NTK theory specifically
- Break condition: If networks aren't wide enough for NTK approximation to hold, or if initialization doesn't follow proper scaling.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Pretraining service: Generates embeddings using specified algorithms and data
  - Feature store: Holds pretrained embeddings for offline model development
  - Memcache: Provides embeddings for online inference
  - Downstream models: Various architectures consuming pretrained embeddings
  - Evaluation pipeline: Both standard offline evaluation and kernel-based metrics

- Critical path: Pretraining → Feature store → Downstream model training → Evaluation → Online deployment
  - Key dependencies: Pretraining algorithm choice affects downstream compatibility
  - Failure points: Mismatched encoding/decoding structures cause instability

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Homogeneous vs heterogeneous configurations: Homogeneous provides stability but may limit model flexibility
  - Kernel-based vs standard evaluation: Kernel metrics are faster but may miss some model-specific interactions
  - Window size and negative samples: Larger values capture more context but increase training time

- Failure signatures:
  - High variance across repeated pretraining runs with same configuration
  - Poor correlation between offline evaluation and online performance
  - Downstream models fail to converge or show unstable training

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test stability: Run pretraining 10 times with same config, measure embedding variance and downstream performance variance
  2. Test alignment: Compare homogeneous vs heterogeneous configurations on same task, measure performance gap
  3. Test predictability: Compute kernel-based metrics for different pretrained embeddings, correlate with actual downstream performance across multiple models

## Limitations

- The kernel-based predictability metrics may not capture all task-relevant information for complex downstream architectures
- The analysis focuses primarily on CL-based pretraining methods with inner product encoding, leaving uncertainty about generalizability to other pretraining approaches
- Online A/B testing results are validated but implementation details and traffic allocation are not fully specified

## Confidence

- **High**: The mechanism of encoding/decoding mismatch causing downstream instability is well-supported by controlled experiments across multiple datasets and tasks.
- **Medium**: The kernel-based predictability metrics show strong correlations with actual performance, but the theoretical foundation (NTK theory) is more established for wide networks than practical deep learning applications.
- **Medium**: The online A/B testing results provide real-world validation, though the exact implementation details and traffic allocation are not fully specified.
