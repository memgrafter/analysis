# Büyük dil modellerinin Türkçe verisetleri ile eğitilmesi ve ince ayarlanması

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03978
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03978
- Reference count: 0
- This work explores training and fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) with Turkish language data to address the lack of satisfactory coverage in existing open-access models.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates training and fine-tuning large language models using Turkish datasets to address the lack of satisfactory Turkish language coverage in existing open-access models. The researchers created a Turkish dataset from Wikipedia articles and trained a GPT-2 model with 124 million parameters, achieving training and test loss values of 1.3784 and 1.6127 respectively after 8000 iterations. The study also attempted to fine-tune the LLaMa model with Turkish inputs, which resulted in unsuccessful outcomes due to the model's lack of prior Turkish language exposure. The research concludes that successful Turkish language model development requires both large Turkish datasets and models with Turkish training support.

## Method Summary
The researchers created a Turkish dataset from Wikipedia articles (731 MB, 818,454 text samples, 296.1 million tokens) and trained a GPT-2 model with 124 million parameters using the nanoGPT implementation for 8000 iterations with a learning rate of 0.0006 and cosine learning rate decay. They also attempted to fine-tune the LLaMa model with Turkish inputs without prior Turkish training, which resulted in unsuccessful outcomes. The study employed tokenization using the tiktoken module with the r50k_base tokenizer, and all experiments were run on a single NVIDIA A100-40GB GPU.

## Key Results
- GPT-2 model trained from scratch on Turkish Wikipedia data achieved training and test loss values of 1.3784 and 1.6127 respectively after 8000 iterations
- Fine-tuning LLaMa model with Turkish inputs without prior Turkish training resulted in unsuccessful outcomes
- The Turkish dataset consisted of 296.1 million tokens created from 818,454 text samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuning pre-trained models on Turkish text fails without prior Turkish language exposure.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained models lack learned representations for Turkish morphology and vocabulary, leading to poor adaptation when fine-tuning on Turkish-only data.
- Core assumption: Language models rely on pre-existing lexical and syntactic representations to effectively learn from new fine-tuning data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "fine-tuning the LLaMa model with Turkish inputs without prior Turkish training resulted in unsuccessful outcomes, as the model had not encountered Turkish words before."
  - [section] "LlaMa modelinin Türkçe girdilerle eğitilmemesi neticesinde ilk yapılan çalışmanın sonuçları başarısız olmuştur. Zaten Türkçe kelimeleri görmemiş ve tanımamış bir modelin bu şekilde yanıt dönebilmesini beklemek mantıksız olacaktır."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training a language model from scratch with Turkish data yields better performance than fine-tuning non-Turkish models.
- Mechanism: Training from scratch allows the model to build appropriate Turkish-specific representations from the ground up, avoiding the mismatch between pre-existing representations and Turkish linguistic features.
- Core assumption: Building representations specifically for Turkish is more effective than adapting non-Turkish representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "using a small Turkish dataset derived from Wikipedia articles, a GPT-2 model with 124 million parameters achieved training and test loss values of 1.3784 and 1.6127, respectively, after 8000 iterations."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Turkish-specific tokenization and preprocessing significantly impact model performance.
- Mechanism: Proper tokenization that handles Turkish morphology and character set enables the model to learn meaningful patterns rather than treating Turkish text as noise.
- Core assumption: Tokenization method must be compatible with the target language's linguistic properties.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Bu metinler, tiktoken modülü ile belirtekleştirilince 296.1 milyon adet belirteke oluşmuştur. Bu sonuç GPT-2 modelinin de belitekleştirildiği 50 bin ögeden oluşan r50k_base dil kodlaması kullanıldığında elde edilen sayıdır."

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific factors caused the unexpectedly low loss values (1.3784 training, 1.6127 test) in the GPT-2 model trained on Turkish Wikipedia data after 8000 iterations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes these loss values are "quite lower" than expected and suggests examining the dataset size and composition as potential factors.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the need for further examination but does not provide specific analysis of why the loss values were lower than expected.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of the relationship between dataset characteristics (size, diversity, quality) and loss values, including comparisons with similar models trained on different datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would using a larger Turkish dataset or a model with native Turkish training capabilities produce significantly better results in fine-tuning for Turkish language tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that fine-tuning LLaMa with Turkish inputs failed because "the model had not encountered Turkish words before" and proposes using a large Turkish dataset with a model that has Turkish training support.
- Why unresolved: The proposed solution was not implemented in the current study.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing fine-tuning performance using larger Turkish datasets versus the current small Wikipedia-based dataset, and comparing results with models that have native Turkish training capabilities.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the minimum computational requirements (GPU count, memory) needed to effectively train large language models on Turkish datasets of different sizes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that training large models requires "more number of hardware and GPU cards" and notes that all experiments were run on a single NVIDIA A100-40GB GPU.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide systematic analysis of computational scaling requirements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical data showing training performance and requirements across different GPU configurations and dataset sizes, including time-to-completion and memory usage metrics.

## Limitations

- The Turkish dataset used (731 MB) is relatively small and may not capture the full complexity of the Turkish language
- The study lacks comparative benchmarks against other established Turkish language models
- Limited technical details about training hyperparameters and evaluation methodology prevent precise replication

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The fundamental claim that Turkish language models require Turkish-specific training data is well-supported by the basic principles of language modeling and the observed failure of non-Turkish models to adapt to Turkish text.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific loss values achieved by the GPT-2 model on the Turkish dataset are reported but lack sufficient context for meaningful interpretation. The failure of LLaMa fine-tuning is demonstrated but not thoroughly investigated.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about the necessity of large Turkish datasets for successful model training are proposed but not empirically validated through systematic experimentation with varying dataset sizes.

## Next Checks

1. **Benchmark Comparison**: Compare the GPT-2 model's performance on Turkish text against other established Turkish language models using standardized evaluation metrics such as perplexity, downstream task performance, and human evaluation of generated text quality.

2. **Fine-tuning Methodology Exploration**: Systematically test multiple fine-tuning approaches including gradual unfreezing, learning rate scheduling, and use of multilingual pre-trained models to determine if the LLaMa fine-tuning failure was due to methodology rather than the fundamental claim about Turkish training requirements.

3. **Dataset Size Sensitivity Analysis**: Conduct controlled experiments training models with progressively larger Turkish datasets (e.g., 1GB, 5GB, 10GB+) to empirically establish the relationship between dataset size and model performance, validating the claim about the necessity of large Turkish datasets.
