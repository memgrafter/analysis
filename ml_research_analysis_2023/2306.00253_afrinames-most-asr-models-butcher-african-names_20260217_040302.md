# AfriNames: Most ASR models "butcher" African Names

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00253
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00253
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: 81.5% relative WER improvement on samples with African-named entities

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of automatic speech recognition (ASR) models performing poorly on African-named entities, a bias that can propagate errors to downstream tasks. The authors model this as a distribution shift and propose a method to mitigate the bias through multilingual pre-training, intelligent data augmentation strategies to increase the representation of African-named entities, and fine-tuning multilingual ASR models on multiple African accents. The primary result is an 81.5% relative WER improvement compared to the baseline on samples with African-named entities.

## Method Summary
The method involves three key components: (1) intelligent data augmentation that increases the representation of African-named entities by replacing Western-named entities in existing transcripts, (2) fine-tuning multilingual pre-trained ASR models (Whisper, Wav2vec2, XLSR) on an augmented dataset rich with African-accented speech and named entities, and (3) evaluation using named entity recognition to specifically assess performance on named entities rather than general speech. The AfriSpeech-200 dataset was curated specifically for this purpose, containing 200.91 hours of accented English speech from 13 Anglophone African countries with a high density of African-named entities.

## Key Results
- 81.5% relative WER improvement on samples with African-named entities compared to baseline
- Fine-tuned models show robust performance across both Western and African-named entities
- Named entity recognition reveals significant model bias against African-named entities that general WER metrics obscure

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuning multilingual ASR models on accented speech with African-named entities improves recognition accuracy by shifting the model's distribution to match the target domain.
- Mechanism: The model starts with a multilingual pre-trained ASR (e.g., Whisper, XLSR) that has learned general phonetic and linguistic patterns. Fine-tuning on an augmented dataset containing accented African speech paired with transcripts containing African-named entities allows the model to adapt its learned representations to better handle the specific phonetic and linguistic characteristics of African names and accents.
- Core assumption: The distribution shift between the pre-training data (dominantly Western English) and the target domain (African-accented English with African-named entities) is the primary cause of poor performance. Fine-tuning can effectively bridge this gap.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We model this problem as a distribution shift and demonstrate that such model bias can be mitigated through multilingual pre-training, intelligent data augmentation strategies to increase the representation of African-named entities, and fine-tuning multilingual ASR models on multiple African accents."
  - [section 4.2]: "We select two best-performing open-source models from section 4.1 and fine-tune them on an accented speech corpus dense with African and western-named entities to achieve robustness to western and African-named entities."
  - [corpus]: The AfriSpeech-200 dataset was specifically curated to contain accented African English speech with a high density of African-named entities, providing the necessary data for this distribution shift.
- Break condition: If the augmented dataset does not adequately represent the diversity of African accents and named entities, or if the model overfits to the augmented data, the fine-tuning process may not generalize well to unseen African speech.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Named entity recognition (NER) can be used to identify and evaluate ASR performance specifically on named entities, revealing model bias that general WER metrics might obscure.
- Mechanism: A specialized NER model is applied to both the ground truth and predicted transcripts to extract named entities. By computing CER on these extracted entities, the evaluation focuses on the model's ability to recognize named entities specifically, rather than general speech. This highlights the model's bias against African-named entities.
- Core assumption: Named entities are a critical component of ASR performance for downstream tasks, and their accurate transcription is a key measure of model utility. General WER metrics can mask poor performance on named entities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3.1]: "To evaluate ASR performance on named entities (NEs), we need a reliable way to identify samples inY2 with NEs... We denote this subset Y3 (Afri-NER). For each model, we compute WER on corresponding model predictions Z3."
  - [section 5.4]: "When named entities are isolated as described in Section 4.3.2, we observe that our fine-tuned whisper-medium model worsens by 1.9% (relative) in comparison to the pre-trained whisper-large model (column 8, char-AfriNER)."
  - [corpus]: The AfriSpeech-200 dataset contains a rich variety of named entities, both Western and African, allowing for meaningful NER-based evaluation.
- Break condition: If the NER model used for evaluation is not accurate or if it fails to identify African-named entities reliably, the evaluation results will be misleading.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Intelligent data augmentation that increases the representation of African-named entities in the training data can improve ASR model performance on these entities.
- Mechanism: The paper employs a data augmentation strategy that involves masking Western-named entities in existing transcripts and replacing them with African-named entities. This creates a new augmented dataset with a higher proportion of African-named entities, which is then used to fine-tune the ASR model. This process increases the model's exposure to and familiarity with African-named entities.
- Core assumption: The underrepresentation of African-named entities in the original training data is a significant factor contributing to the model's poor performance on these entities. By increasing their representation, the model can learn to recognize them more effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "In this study, we primarily explore the AfriSpeech-200 dataset, a 200.91 hours novel accented English speech corpus rich with African-named entities, curated for clinical and general domain ASR using the augmentation process described above."
  - [section 3.3]: "To increase the representation of African named entities, we start with a corpus D1 using large open-source predominantly western corpora... We augment D′ 1 in three key steps: 1. Named-Entity Extraction with NER Models... 2. Template Selection... 3. Named Entity Replacement..."
  - [corpus]: The AfriSpeech-200 dataset was specifically created using this augmentation process, resulting in a dataset with a significantly higher proportion of African-named entities compared to standard ASR training data.
- Break condition: If the augmented dataset contains errors or inconsistencies in the placement of African-named entities, or if the augmentation process introduces biases that do not reflect real-world usage, the model's performance may not generalize well.

## Limitations

- The evaluation methodology relies heavily on NER model accuracy, which may not be consistent across different African-named entities
- The data augmentation process assumes that replacing Western-named entities with African-named entities preserves linguistic context and pronunciation patterns
- The results may not generalize to all African accents and named entities, as the dataset covers only 13 Anglophone African countries

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The fundamental problem of ASR models performing poorly on African-named entities is well-established and the distribution shift framing is sound. The general approach of fine-tuning multilingual models on accented speech with African-named entities is theoretically valid.
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific 81.5% relative WER improvement figure, while based on rigorous methodology, depends heavily on the quality of the NER-based evaluation and the specific data augmentation choices. The improvement might vary significantly with different evaluation datasets or augmentation strategies.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of results to all African accents and named entities, given the dataset covers only 13 Anglophone African countries. The long-term robustness of the approach without continued exposure to diverse African speech patterns during training.
