# Topics in the Haystack: Extracting and Evaluating Topics beyond Coherence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17324
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17324
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Context Based Topic Model (CBTM) outperforms state-of-the-art models on benchmark datasets, achieving near-human level results on word-intrusion tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel topic modeling approach that leverages sentence and document embeddings to identify topics beyond simple word frequencies. The Context Based Topic Model (CBTM) expands the reference corpus with additional text and focuses on nouns and noun phrases to improve topic quality. The authors propose new evaluation metrics based on intruder words and semantic similarity, demonstrating that CBTM achieves near-human performance on benchmark datasets and outperforms existing state-of-the-art models in coherence scores and topic interpretability.

## Method Summary
The paper presents the Context Based Topic Model (CBTM), a novel topic modeling approach that uses sentence and document embeddings instead of traditional word frequency methods. The model incorporates reference corpus expansion by adding relevant external text, focuses specifically on nouns and noun phrases to enhance topic quality, and introduces new evaluation metrics based on semantic similarity and intruder words. CBTM leverages large language models to generate embeddings, which are then used to identify coherent topics through a combination of statistical and semantic analysis.

## Key Results
- CBTM outperforms state-of-the-art models on benchmark datasets
- Achieves near-human level results on the word-intrusion task
- Demonstrates superior performance in both traditional coherence scores and novel evaluation metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's approach works by leveraging semantic embeddings rather than relying solely on word co-occurrence patterns. By using sentence and document embeddings from large language models, the model captures deeper contextual relationships between words and documents. The expansion of the reference corpus with additional relevant text provides broader context for topic identification, while the focus on nouns and noun phrases helps isolate the most semantically meaningful content. The new evaluation metrics based on intruder words and semantic similarity provide more robust assessment of topic quality than traditional coherence measures alone.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
CBTM consists of these main components: Document Processing -> Embedding Generation -> Topic Identification -> Evaluation Metrics

**Critical Path**
1. Input documents are preprocessed and cleaned
2. Sentence and document embeddings are generated using large language models
3. Topics are identified through statistical and semantic analysis
4. Topics are evaluated using both traditional and novel metrics

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Computational complexity vs. semantic richness: Using large language models for embeddings provides better semantic understanding but increases computational requirements
- Reference corpus expansion: Adding external text improves context but may introduce noise
- Focus on nouns/noun phrases: Improves topic quality but may miss important contextual information

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor topic coherence when reference corpus is too narrow or too broad
- Overfitting to specific document types when evaluation metrics are not diverse enough
- Computational bottlenecks when processing very large corpora

**3 First Experiments**
1. Run CBTM on a small, well-understood corpus to verify basic functionality
2. Compare topic quality with and without reference corpus expansion
3. Test the impact of focusing on different parts of speech (nouns vs. all words)
