# A Survey on Fairness-aware Recommender Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00403
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00403
- Reference count: 40
- This paper provides a comprehensive survey of fairness-aware recommender systems, categorizing methods into pre-processing, in-processing, and post-processing approaches

## Executive Summary
This comprehensive survey examines the growing field of fairness-aware recommender systems, addressing how bias can propagate through recommendation pipelines and methods to mitigate these issues. The paper systematically categorizes fairness interventions into three main approaches: pre-processing methods that modify training data, in-processing methods that incorporate fairness constraints during model training, and post-processing methods that rearrange recommendation outputs. The survey also discusses evaluation metrics, industrial applications, and connections to other trustworthy AI dimensions like explainability and privacy.

## Method Summary
The survey conducts a systematic literature review of fairness-aware recommender systems, analyzing 40+ references to identify common patterns and approaches. It organizes methods into three categories based on when they intervene in the recommendation pipeline: before model training (pre-processing), during model training (in-processing), and after model training (post-processing). The analysis examines specific techniques within each category, including data re-labeling, adversarial learning, and re-ranking algorithms, while also discussing evaluation metrics and practical implementation challenges.

## Key Results
- Fairness-aware recommender systems can be categorized into pre-processing, in-processing, and post-processing approaches
- Each intervention category addresses different sources of bias: data bias, model bias, and feedback bias respectively
- The survey identifies standardization of fairness concepts and balancing fairness with recommendation performance as key future research directions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-processing methods address data bias by modifying the training data distribution before model training
- Mechanism: Data re-labeling changes noisy labels to correct user preference signals; data re-sampling balances imbalanced user-item interactions; data modification handles missing or incomplete data
- Core assumption: Bias in recommender systems originates primarily from the training data distribution
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Unbalanced data distribution may lead to a decrease in the training effect of recommender systems" (pre-processing section)
  - [abstract] "Pre-processing methods include data re-labeling, re-sampling, and modification to remove bias from training data"
- Break condition: If data bias is not the dominant source of unfairness, pre-processing alone will be insufficient

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: In-processing methods address model bias by modifying the training objective to incorporate fairness constraints
- Mechanism: Regularization terms penalize unfair predictions; causal inference methods remove confounding variables; adversarial learning removes sensitive attribute information; reinforcement learning optimizes for dynamic fairness
- Core assumption: Model training amplifies data bias through flawed loss functions and representation learning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Models with design flaws (e.g., ranking bias) may further magnify biases in the input data" (in-processing section)
  - [abstract] "In-processing methods use regularization, causal inference, adversarial learning, reinforcement learning, and ranking optimization to learn fair models"
- Break condition: If the model architecture fundamentally cannot represent fair solutions, in-processing modifications will be ineffective

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Post-processing methods address feedback bias by rearranging recommendation outputs without modifying the underlying model
- Mechanism: Non-parametric re-ranking uses heuristic search to maximize fairness under constraints; parametric re-ranking learns re-ranking parameters to maintain recommendation quality while improving fairness
- Core assumption: Recommendation results from trained models can be improved through output rearrangement without retraining
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Post-processing methods rearrange recommendation results via re-ranking" (abstract)
  - [section] "Post-processing methods aim to rearrange the recommendation results provided by target models" (post-processing section)
- Break condition: If the underlying model's ranking is too strongly biased, re-ranking cannot sufficiently improve fairness

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing pipeline → Model training module → Post-processing re-ranking → Evaluation metrics
  - Fairness methods can be inserted at each stage: preprocessing (data cleaning), in-processing (loss modification), post-processing (output re-ranking)
  - Evaluation requires both accuracy metrics (AUC, Precision@k) and fairness metrics (Gini coefficient, KL-divergence)

- Critical path:
  - Identify bias type → Select appropriate fairness method category (pre/in/post-processing) → Implement method → Evaluate using relevant metrics → Iterate
  - Most critical decision: choosing the right intervention point based on where bias originates in the lifecycle

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pre-processing: Faster to implement but may not address all bias sources; may require data augmentation which increases storage/compute
  - In-processing: More comprehensive but requires model modification and retraining; may impact recommendation accuracy
  - Post-processing: Least invasive but limited by model's inherent bias; may require significant re-ranking computation

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy degradation beyond acceptable thresholds
  - Fairness metrics show minimal improvement despite method implementation
  - Computational overhead becomes prohibitive for production deployment
  - User satisfaction decreases despite improved fairness scores

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement data re-sampling on imbalanced dataset and measure impact on popularity bias using Gini coefficient
  2. Add regularization term to existing recommendation model and compare accuracy-fairness tradeoff using NDCG@k vs KL-divergence
  3. Apply post-processing re-ranking to baseline model and evaluate improvement in group fairness metrics without retraining

## Limitations
- The survey's categorization of fairness methods, while useful, may have fuzzy boundaries in practice as some methods combine elements from multiple categories
- Effectiveness of each approach depends heavily on specific fairness definitions and recommendation contexts
- Fairness evaluation metrics remain inconsistent across studies, complicating comparative analysis of different methods

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The survey accurately captures the three main methodological approaches to fairness-aware recommendation (pre-processing, in-processing, post-processing) and their general mechanisms
- **Medium confidence**: The categorization of specific methods within each approach is comprehensive, though some emerging techniques may not be fully represented
- **Medium confidence**: The identification of future research directions is reasonable, but the relative importance and feasibility of different directions may vary based on evolving industry needs
