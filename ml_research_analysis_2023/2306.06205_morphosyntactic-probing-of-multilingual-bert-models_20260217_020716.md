# Morphosyntactic probing of multilingual BERT models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06205
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06205
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 35
- Pre-trained Transformer models (mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa) learn morphological features that outperform LSTM baselines across 247 tasks in 42 languages

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a comprehensive dataset for evaluating how well multilingual BERT models capture morphological information across 42 languages and 10 language families. The authors find that pre-trained Transformers significantly outperform character-level LSTM baselines on morphosyntactic probing tasks. Through perturbation analysis and Shapley value attribution, they discover a surprising asymmetry where preceding context provides more disambiguating information than following context for most languages, a pattern that persists even when controlling for model architecture.

## Method Summary
The study extracts probing tasks from Universal Dependencies treebanks, creating 247 tasks across 42 languages where each task consists of a sentence with a target word and its morphological tag. Two MLP classifiers are trained on top of frozen mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa embeddings using early stopping. The authors then apply perturbations (masking target/context words, shuffling word order) and compute Shapley values to identify information sources. Results are compared against character-level LSTM and fastText baselines, as well as a Stanza parser trained on each language.

## Key Results
- mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa significantly outperform character LSTM and fastText baselines on morphosyntactic probing tasks
- Left context consistently provides more morphosyntactic information than right context across most languages and POS-tag combinations
- Target word masking causes the most severe performance degradation, followed by context word masking
- XLM-RoBERTa generally outperforms mBERT, with the gap being largest for morphological features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-trained MLMs capture morphological knowledge that surpasses task-specific LSTM baselines
- Mechanism: MLMs are trained on large multilingual corpora, allowing them to learn general morphological patterns across languages
- Core assumption: The morphological patterns learned during pre-training are transferable to specific morphosyntactic tagging tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that pre-trained Transformer models (mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa) learn features that attain strong performance across these tasks"
  - [section 5.1] "Both are clearly superior to chLSTM and fastText. Out of the 14 ⟨POS, tag⟩ combinations, mBERT is only better than XLM-RoBERTa in⟨ADJ, Gender⟩"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for pre-training effectiveness
- Break condition: If the morphological patterns are too language-specific or the pre-training data lacks coverage of the target languages

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The left context provides more morphosyntactic information than the right context
- Mechanism: Morphological features spread progressively through the sentence, with earlier words influencing later ones more strongly
- Core assumption: The directional bias in feature spreading is due to linguistic properties rather than model architecture
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The most intriguing finding that emerges is a strong tendency for the preceding context to hold more information relevant to the prediction than the following context"
  - [section 7.2] "What is more, not even our experiments with random weighted MLMs... show such asymmetry"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for progressive feature spreading
- Break condition: If the model architecture introduces directional bias or if the linguistic data shows symmetric context effects

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Perturbations and Shapley values can effectively identify the source of morphosyntactic information
- Mechanism: By systematically removing information sources and measuring the impact on prediction accuracy, we can attribute responsibility to specific context positions
- Core assumption: The impact of information removal is proportional to the importance of that information source
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.1] "Target masking or TARG is by far the most destructive perturbation with an average effect of 16.1% for mBERT"
  - [section 7] "The Shapley values suggest for morphology is that progressive assimilation... is more relevant than regressive"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for perturbation effectiveness
- Break condition: If the model relies on non-local or distributed representations that cannot be isolated by simple perturbations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Dataset: 247 probing tasks across 42 languages and 10 language families
  - Models: mBERT, XLM-RoBERTa, chLSTM, fastText, Stanza
  - Methods: Perturbations (masking, permutation), Shapley values
  - Evaluation: Probing accuracy, perturbation effects, Shapley distributions

- Critical path:
  1. Sample probing tasks from UD treebanks
  2. Train MLP classifiers on top of frozen MLM layers
  3. Apply perturbations to test context importance
  4. Compute Shapley values to attribute feature importance
  5. Analyze results across languages and POS tags

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using frozen MLMs vs. fine-tuning: Frozen models are faster to train but may miss task-specific adaptations
  - Number of probing tasks: More tasks provide better coverage but increase computational cost
  - Perturbation methods: More granular perturbations give finer-grained insights but are more expensive to compute

- Failure signatures:
  - Low probing accuracy: The MLM may not have learned the relevant morphological features
  - Asymmetric perturbation effects: The model may have directional bias or the data may have imbalanced context
  - Unusual Shapley distributions: The morphological feature may have atypical context dependencies

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a simple MLP classifier on top of mBERT embeddings for a basic morphosyntactic task (e.g., English NOUN Number)
  2. Apply target word masking to measure the importance of the word form itself
  3. Compute Shapley values for a few representative tasks to see the context contribution pattern

## Limitations

- The study uses frozen MLMs without fine-tuning, potentially underestimating the models' true morphological learning capacity
- The probing tasks are derived from Universal Dependencies treebanks, which may not represent all morphosyntactic phenomena or language varieties
- The perturbation analysis may not fully capture the complexity of context interactions in transformer models
