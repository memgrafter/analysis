# Beyond CO2 Emissions: The Overlooked Impact of Water Consumption of Information Retrieval Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16668
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16668
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents methodology for quantifying water consumption of IR models, showing neural models consume significantly more water than traditional approaches.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical gap in environmental impact assessment of information retrieval models by introducing a methodology to quantify water consumption alongside the traditionally measured CO2 emissions and energy usage. The authors develop a framework that accounts for both on-site water usage (data center cooling) and off-site consumption (power plant cooling), demonstrating that while traditional IR models have minimal water footprint, neural models based on large language models can consume substantial amounts of water. The study provides practical tools including a web-based calculator and W&B plugin to help the IR community monitor and reduce their environmental impact.

## Method Summary
The methodology builds on existing energy consumption data from Scells et al. [32] for various IR models (BM25, LambdaMART, DPR, monoBERT, TILDEv2, uniCOIL) and applies water consumption calculations using equations from Li et al. [19]. The approach separates water usage into on-site components (cooling towers at data centers) and off-site components (power plant cooling for electricity generation). On-site calculations use power consumption values combined with local wet-bulb temperature, cycles of concentration, and power usage effectiveness. The total water consumption is derived by combining both components, with the analysis considering temporal factors like time of day and season through adjustments to the wet-bulb temperature parameter.

## Key Results
- Neural IR models (monoBERT, DPR, TILDEv2) consume significantly more water than traditional keyword-matching and learning-to-rank models
- Water consumption varies by time of day and season due to changes in wet-bulb temperature
- The methodology provides a framework for calculating both on-site and off-site water usage
- Practical tools are provided to help the IR community monitor water consumption

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The methodology works by establishing a direct relationship between energy consumption (already measured for IR models) and water usage through established thermodynamic equations. The separation into on-site and off-site components allows for more accurate modeling of the complete water footprint, recognizing that data centers require cooling while the power plants generating their electricity also consume water for cooling. The temporal adjustments account for the fact that water usage effectiveness varies with environmental conditions, making the calculations more realistic than static estimates.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cycles of concentration (CoC)**: The ratio of dissolved solids in recirculating water to dissolved solids in make-up water; why needed because it determines blow-down frequency and thus water consumption; quick check: higher CoC means less blow-down and lower water usage
- **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)**: Ratio of total facility energy to IT equipment energy; why needed because it captures cooling overhead in data centers; quick check: typical PUE values range from 1.2 to 2.0, with lower being better
- **Wet-bulb temperature**: The lowest temperature achievable by evaporative cooling; why needed because it determines cooling tower efficiency and water consumption; quick check: higher wet-bulb temperatures require more water for cooling
- **Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE)**: Water consumption per unit of energy used; why needed as the primary metric for comparing water efficiency across different setups; quick check: includes both on-site and off-site components
- **Blow-down**: The process of removing concentrated cooling tower water to maintain water quality; why needed because it represents a major component of on-site water consumption; quick check: frequency depends on cycles of concentration and water quality

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Energy Consumption Data -> Power Usage Effectiveness -> On-site Water Usage -> Off-site Water Usage -> Total Water Consumption

Critical path: The calculation flow moves from measured energy consumption through PUE adjustment to determine total facility power, then splits into on-site (cooling tower) and off-site (power generation) water usage calculations, finally combining both for total consumption.

Design tradeoffs: The methodology trades precision for accessibility by using fixed parameters (PUE, CoC) rather than facility-specific measurements, making it widely applicable but potentially less accurate for specific locations.

Failure signatures: Overestimation occurs when assuming poor water quality (low CoC), while underestimation happens with overly optimistic CoC values. Mismatched wet-bulb temperature data leads to incorrect temporal adjustments.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does water quality in different geographic locations affect on-site water consumption of data centers running IR models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses water quality's influence on water consumption, noting that high-quality water requires fewer blow downs
- Why unresolved: The paper provides only a Brisbane, Australia example without exploring geographic variations
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative study of water consumption across data centers in different regions with varying water quality

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the long-term environmental impact of using chemicals to improve water quality in cooling towers for data centers?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions chemicals could improve water quality but notes potential environmental harm if released
- Why unresolved: No information provided on long-term environmental impacts of these chemicals
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive environmental impact assessment of cooling tower chemicals over extended periods

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the energy mix (nuclear, coal, solar) used to power data centers affect the off-site water consumption associated with IR models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses off-site water consumption and notes different energy sources have different water usage effectiveness
- Why unresolved: No detailed analysis of how different energy mixes impact off-site water consumption
- What evidence would resolve it: Study comparing off-site water consumption of data centers powered by different energy mixes

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on power consumption data from a single prior study, creating uncertainty about underlying energy measurements
- Uses fixed parameters (PUE=1.894, CoC=2.25) that may not reflect actual data center conditions across different facilities
- Time-interval analysis is limited by cumulative rather than interval-specific energy consumption data

## Confidence
High: The overall framework for calculating water consumption is methodologically sound and builds appropriately on existing environmental impact research.
Medium: The comparative analysis showing neural IR models consume significantly more water than traditional methods appears reasonable given the energy intensity of these models.
Low: The time-of-day and seasonal variation analyses are limited by the availability of granular energy consumption data, making temporal pattern conclusions less reliable.
