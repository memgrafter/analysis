# Correlation-Driven Multi-Level Multimodal Learning for Anomaly Detection on Multiple Energy Sources

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02323
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02323
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: F1 score improvements of 0.56%-26.85% on three-source datasets and 6.41%-25.32% on four-source datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a correlation-driven multi-level multimodal learning approach for anomaly detection across multiple energy sources. The method defines anomalies not only by individual energy sources but also by considering correlations between them using Cook's distance. The proposed model fuses energy sources at multiple levels based on correlation strength, employing early fusion for weakly correlated sources and late fusion for strongly correlated ones. A novel three-level architecture is designed to handle non-correlated energy sources by differentiating their weight contributions. Experiments on real-world datasets (AMPds2, 3-ECK-2021, 3-ECK-2022, 4-ECK-2022, 5-ECK-2022) show significant improvements over existing methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed correlation-driven multi-level multimodal learning approach defines anomalies by considering both individual energy sources and correlations between them. The method uses Cook's distance to identify influential observations in regression models. Energy sources are fused at multiple levels based on their correlation strength: early fusion for weakly correlated sources (Pearson correlation < 0.2) and late fusion for strongly correlated sources. A three-level architecture is introduced to handle non-correlated sources by processing them separately and concatenating at the third level with supervised learning to differentiate weight contributions. The model employs USAD or LSTM-AE as feature extractors and LightGBM as the classifier.

## Key Results
- F1 score improvements of 0.56%-26.85% on three-source datasets compared to baseline methods
- F1 score improvements of 6.41%-25.32% on four-source datasets
- Significant performance gains as more energy sources are added, demonstrating model scalability
- Superior performance on real-world datasets including AMPds2 and 3-ECK series datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-level fusion based on correlation strength improves anomaly detection by aligning fusion strategy with source interdependencies.
- Mechanism: The model uses early fusion for weakly correlated sources to capture joint patterns and late fusion for strongly correlated sources to preserve distinct feature representations, thereby leveraging both shared and unique information.
- Core assumption: The correlation strength between energy sources is stable enough to guide the fusion strategy effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The distinguishing property of the model incorporates multiple energy sources in multi-levels based on the strengths of the correlations between them.
  - [section] We integrate two energy sources that have weaker correlations than the other by early fusion... and then integrate the fused result with a remaining energy source by late fusion.
- Break condition: If energy source correlations fluctuate significantly over time, the fixed fusion strategy may become suboptimal.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Correlation-driven anomaly definition captures anomalies missed by individual source analysis by identifying influential instances via Cook's distance.
- Mechanism: Anomalies are defined not only by individual source deviations but also by unusual relationships between correlated sources, using Cook's distance to measure influence on regression models.
- Core assumption: Anomalies can manifest as abnormal correlations even when individual sources appear normal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] We first propose a method for defining anomalies considering not only individual energy sources but also correlations between them.
  - [section] We present an anomaly definition method that augments the anomalies by the correlations between energy sources.
- Break condition: If correlations between energy sources are spurious or non-linear, Cook's distance may not capture true anomalies.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Three-level architecture with correlation-based weight differentiation effectively integrates non-correlated sources without diluting the model's focus on correlated anomalies.
- Mechanism: Non-correlated sources are processed separately and concatenated at the third level, with supervised learning differentiating their weight contributions from correlated sources based on anomaly annotations.
- Core assumption: The distinction between correlated and non-correlated sources can be learned and maintained through supervised training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The proposed model is scalable in integrating more arbitrary energy sources... considering not only correlated but also non-correlated sources.
  - [section] We devise a model architecture to integrate non-correlated energy sources... by training the third-level fusion layer with the datasets where anomalies are defined by considering the correlation between energy sources.
- Break condition: If the proportion of non-correlated sources is too high, the model may struggle to maintain clear differentiation in weight contributions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Correlation analysis → Feature extraction (USAD/LSTM-AE) → Early fusion (weakly correlated) → Late fusion (strongly correlated) → Third-level fusion (non-correlated) → Classifier (LightGBM) → Anomaly output
- Critical path: Correlation analysis → Feature extraction → Multi-level fusion → Classifier training → Anomaly detection
- Design tradeoffs: Early fusion captures joint patterns but may lose individual source nuances; late fusion preserves distinct features but may miss cross-source interactions
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on datasets with high non-correlated source proportions; degradation when source correlations are unstable over time

## Limitations
- The method assumes relatively stable correlations between energy sources, which may not hold in dynamic environments
- Effectiveness of Cook's distance for anomaly detection in non-linear relationships between energy sources is not fully validated
- The three-level architecture may struggle to maintain clear differentiation between correlated and non-correlated sources when non-correlated sources dominate the dataset

## Confidence
- High confidence: The F1 score improvements on the tested datasets are well-documented and reproducible
- Medium confidence: The correlation-driven fusion strategy works as described for stable correlation scenarios
- Medium confidence: The three-level architecture can integrate non-correlated sources effectively in balanced datasets
