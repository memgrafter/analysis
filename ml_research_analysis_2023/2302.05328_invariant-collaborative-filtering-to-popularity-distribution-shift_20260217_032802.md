# Invariant Collaborative Filtering to Popularity Distribution Shift

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05328
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05328
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: InvCF achieves 7.21% to 10.2% improvements in NDCG@20 over state-of-the-art baselines for handling popularity distribution shift

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses popularity distribution shift in collaborative filtering (CF), a problem where CF models suffer performance drops due to popularity changes in real-world scenarios. Existing debiasing methods often require prior knowledge of test popularity distributions, limiting their applicability. To tackle this, the authors propose Invariant Collaborative Filtering (InvCF), a novel framework that learns disentangled representations to handle unknown popularity distribution shifts. InvCF uses two principles: (1) invariance, ensuring interaction predictions depend only on preference information, not popularity; and (2) disentanglement, separating preference and popularity representations. The method incorporates representation augmentation and disentangling modules to achieve these principles. Extensive experiments on five datasets and four evaluation settings show that InvCF significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving 7.21% to 10.2% improvements in NDCG@20. Visualization studies demonstrate that InvCF effectively learns popularity-disentangled representations. The results highlight InvCF's robustness and generalization ability in handling popularity distribution shifts without requiring prior test distribution knowledge.

## Method Summary
The Invariant Collaborative Filtering (InvCF) framework addresses popularity distribution shift by learning disentangled representations through two key principles: invariance and disentanglement. The method employs two separate encoders - one for preference and one for popularity - to capture distinct information. Representation augmentation and disentangling modules work together to ensure that interaction predictions depend only on preference information, not popularity. The framework is trained jointly using multiple loss functions (Lrep, Laug, and Ldis) and can be applied to various backbone models like MF and LightGCN. Extensive experiments demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines across multiple datasets and evaluation metrics.

## Key Results
- InvCF achieves 7.21% to 10.2% improvements in NDCG@20 over state-of-the-art baselines
- The method demonstrates robustness across five benchmark datasets and four evaluation settings
- Visualization studies confirm effective learning of popularity-disentangled representations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
InvCF works by addressing the core challenge of popularity distribution shift through disentangled representation learning. The framework separates user preferences from item popularity information, allowing models to focus on preference-based interactions while being invariant to popularity changes. The representation augmentation module creates diverse training samples that help the model generalize across different popularity distributions. The disentangling module, using distance correlation as a regularizer, explicitly separates preference and popularity information in the learned representations. This two-pronged approach enables the model to maintain performance even when popularity distributions shift between training and test periods.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
User/Item Embeddings -> Preference Encoder -> Popularity Encoder -> Representation Augmentation -> Disentangling Module -> Joint Loss Functions -> Prediction

**Critical Path:**
1. Input embeddings are processed by separate preference and popularity encoders
2. Representation augmentation creates diverse training samples
3. Disentangling module enforces independence between preference and popularity
4. Joint training optimizes for both prediction accuracy and representation disentanglement

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Separation of preference and popularity vs. potential information loss
- Computational overhead of dual encoders vs. improved generalization
- Complexity of disentangling module vs. effectiveness of popularity invariance

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor performance on popularity generalization if disentangling module is ineffective
- Overfitting or underfitting if representation augmentation is imbalanced
- Limited improvement if preference and popularity information cannot be effectively separated
