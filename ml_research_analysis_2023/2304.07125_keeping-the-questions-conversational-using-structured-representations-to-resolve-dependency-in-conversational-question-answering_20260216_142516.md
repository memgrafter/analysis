# Keeping the Questions Conversational: Using Structured Representations to Resolve Dependency in Conversational Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07125
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07125
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: CONVSR framework achieves better F1 scores than standard question rewriting models by using structured representations to resolve implicit dependencies in conversational questions

## Executive Summary
CONVSR introduces a novel approach to conversational question answering that avoids verbose question rewriting by generating structured representations (SRs) of conversational context. The framework uses dynamic history selection to identify relevant turns and extracts SRs containing context and question entities using BART. These SRs are then integrated with the current question to enhance answer prediction. Experiments on QuAC and CANARD datasets demonstrate that CONVSR outperforms standard question rewriting approaches, achieving superior F1 scores while maintaining interpretability through its structured approach.

## Method Summary
CONVSR addresses conversational question answering by generating structured representations rather than rewriting questions. The method employs dynamic history selection using soft cosine similarity to filter relevant turns above a 0.75 threshold. For each selected turn, BART generates two slots: context entity (CE) and question entity (QE). These SRs, along with the current question and history, are prepended to the input of a ConvQA model (BERT, BERT-HAE, or RoBERTa) to guide answer prediction. The approach is trained end-to-end with the ConvQA model, allowing the structured representations to directly enhance answer span prediction.

## Key Results
- CONVSR achieves better F1 scores than standard question rewriting models on QuAC and CANARD datasets
- Dynamic history selection with 0.75 threshold effectively filters irrelevant turns while preserving context
- Structured representations (CE + QE slots) outperform verbose question rewriting in both accuracy and interpretability
- The framework works across multiple ConvQA models (BERT, BERT-HAE, RoBERTa)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dynamic history selection with soft cosine similarity improves accuracy by filtering out irrelevant turns before generating structured representations
- Mechanism: The model computes soft cosine similarity between the current question and each history turn, only retaining turns above a 0.75 threshold
- Core assumption: Follow-up questions in conversational QA only require a small, recent subset of history turns to be interpreted correctly
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a dynamic history selection policy based on 'hard history selection' to only select the relevant subset of conversational turns"
  - [section] "We perform different experiments and conclude that the turns surpassing the threshold value of 0.75 contribute more in predicting the current answer span"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: no explicit FMR/citations for history selection effectiveness
- Break condition: If the conversational flow requires long-range dependencies or topic shifts not captured by recent turns, the hard threshold will miss critical context

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Structured representations (SRs) with separate context and question entity slots enable targeted entity resolution without verbose rewriting
- Mechanism: For each selected history turn, BART generates two slots: a context entity (CE) and a question entity (QE)
- Core assumption: Most conversational dependencies can be resolved by identifying the relevant entity in context and the entity being referred to in the question
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our framework presents an alternative of question rewriting task to complete the ambiguous questions by generating intermediate structured representations"
  - [section] "These SRs serve as an aid to an incomplete question by filling in the gap by adding more context using context entity and/or by resolving co-references using question entity"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: no explicit FMR/citations for SR slot effectiveness
- Break condition: If the question requires complex anaphora resolution or multi-hop reasoning beyond simple entity references, the two-slot SR may be insufficient

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using BART for both history selection and SR generation leverages its sequence-to-sequence strength for this task
- Mechanism: BART encodes the concatenated history and current question, then decodes CE and QE pairs for each selected turn
- Core assumption: A single model trained on both tasks will learn better representations than separate models for history selection and entity extraction
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We utilize a pre-trained seq2seq language model, BART to perform history selection and generate SR entities"
  - [section] "It consists of both an encoder and a decoder. The model is best utilized when the information is duplicated from the input but manipulated to produce the result autoregressively"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: no explicit FMR/citations for BART dual-task effectiveness
- Break condition: If BART's pre-training is not well-aligned with the conversational QA domain, the SR quality may degrade

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Soft cosine similarity vs regular cosine similarity
  - Why needed here: Regular cosine similarity only considers exact term overlap, which is too strict for conversational questions with co-references and paraphrases
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between soft and regular cosine similarity when comparing "What was she obsessed about?" with "Who played Monica Geller in FRIENDS?"
- Concept: Sequence-to-sequence modeling with BART
  - Why needed here: BART's encoder-decoder architecture is ideal for transforming conversational turns into structured entity representations
  - Quick check question: How does BART's denoising pre-training help in generating structured representations from conversational history?
- Concept: Threshold-based filtering in NLP pipelines
  - Why needed here: The 0.75 threshold determines which history turns are kept, directly affecting model performance and noise level
  - Quick check question: What happens to F1 score if the history selection threshold is lowered to 0.5 vs raised to 0.9?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → History selection → SR generation → ConvQA → Output
- Critical path: Conversational history turns + current question → Dynamic history selection (soft cosine similarity + threshold filter) → SR generation (BART producing CE/QE pairs) → ConvQA model (RoBERTa/BERT) with prepended SRs and history → Answer span
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Hard threshold (0.75) vs soft weighting of history turns
  - Two-slot SR vs single-slot or more complex entity representation
  - Joint BART training vs separate models for history selection and entity extraction
- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 with high threshold: missing critical context
  - Low F1 with low threshold: too much noise
  - Stable F1 but poor HEQ: model memorizing rather than reasoning
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary history selection threshold (0.5, 0.75, 0.9) and measure F1/HEQ
  2. Remove one SR slot at a time (CE-only, QE-only) to measure impact
  3. Compare joint BART vs separate models for history selection and SR generation

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation of core mechanisms: Minimal ablation studies and the 0.75 threshold appears selected without systematic analysis
- Dataset and domain constraints: Evaluation only on QuAC and CANARD academic datasets, may not generalize to other domains
- Computational overhead concerns: No runtime comparisons or discussion of computational cost of BART-based dual-task approach

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core claim that structured representations can improve conversational QA performance is well-supported by experimental results
- Medium confidence: The mechanism of dynamic history selection using soft cosine similarity is plausible but lacks rigorous analysis of threshold sensitivity
- Low confidence: The claim that BART's dual-task training provides significant benefits over separate models is not empirically validated
