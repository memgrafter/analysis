# Multi-Start Team Orienteering Problem for UAS Mission Re-Planning with Data-Efficient Deep Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01963
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01963
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a data-efficient training methodology for deep reinforcement learning-based combinatorial optimization solvers, validated on MSTOP and two classical CO problems.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the Multi-Start Team Orienteering Problem (MSTOP) for UAS mission re-planning by proposing a data-efficient training methodology for deep reinforcement learning (DRL)-based combinatorial optimization solvers. The approach introduces a local mini-batch baseline that leverages multiple sample rollouts from coordinate-transformed instances, replacing the computationally expensive greedy rollout baseline. This enables faster learning and a more stable policy gradient estimator while maintaining competitive solution quality. The method is validated across MSTOP and two classical CO problems (TSP and CVRP), demonstrating improved sample efficiency and stability compared to existing approaches.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes replacing the greedy rollout baseline, commonly used in DRL-based combinatorial optimization, with a local mini-batch baseline derived from multiple sample rollouts generated through coordinate transformations of a single problem instance. This transformation exploits the translational invariance of routing problems to create diverse training samples without requiring new problem instances. The method is integrated with a DRL actor-critic framework using the PoWER algorithm and is evaluated on MSTOP, TSP, and CVRP. The local mini-batch baseline improves sample efficiency by reducing the number of expensive environment rollouts needed during training while also stabilizing the policy gradient estimator through reduced variance.

## Key Results
- The local mini-batch baseline achieves improved sample efficiency over the greedy rollout baseline on MSTOP, TSP, and CVRP.
- The method outperforms the greedy rollout baseline even when combined with the maximum entropy objective, and achieves competitive performance with the state-of-the-art POMO method while using significantly less data.
- Experimental results show reduced variance in policy gradients, leading to more stable learning across five random seeds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by exploiting the translational invariance property of routing problems. Instead of generating entirely new problem instances for each rollout, the approach applies coordinate transformations to a single instance to create multiple diverse samples. This allows the model to learn from a richer set of trajectories without the computational cost of generating new environments. The local mini-batch baseline provides a more accurate estimate of the expected return by averaging over these transformed samples, which reduces the variance of the policy gradient estimator and leads to more stable and efficient learning.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
DRL Agent -> Local Mini-Batch Baseline Generator -> Environment (MSTOP/TSP/CVRP) -> Reward Signal -> Policy Update

**Critical Path**
1. Generate coordinate-transformed samples from a single instance
2. Execute rollouts in the environment using the current policy
3. Compute rewards and estimate policy gradients using the local mini-batch baseline
4. Update policy parameters via gradient ascent

**Design Tradeoffs**
- **Computational Efficiency vs. Sample Diversity**: The local mini-batch baseline trades the diversity of entirely new instances for computational efficiency through transformations, which may limit exploration in some problem variants.
- **Variance Reduction vs. Bias Introduction**: While the local mini-batch reduces variance, the use of transformed samples could introduce bias if the transformations don't fully preserve problem structure.

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor convergence or unstable learning curves could indicate that the coordinate transformations are not generating sufficiently diverse samples.
- Degradation in solution quality on problems that lack translational invariance would suggest the method is not universally applicable.

## Limitations
- The performance gains from the local mini-batch baseline may not generalize to other CO problems or problem scales, as the improvements are primarily demonstrated on routing problems.
- The stability claims are based on variance reduction across five random seeds, which is suggestive but not conclusive without wider testing.
- The study does not explore whether the baseline method transfers to other RL frameworks or optimization architectures, limiting its applicability.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The local mini-batch baseline provides a computational advantage over the greedy rollout baseline, as evidenced by consistent sample-efficiency gains and variance reduction across experiments.

**Medium confidence**: The overall solution quality improvements are promising but not uniformly reflected across all problems or error measures, suggesting the method may be problem-dependent.

**Low confidence**: The broad generalizability of the method to other CO problems, RL algorithms, or larger problem sizes remains uncertain without further testing.
