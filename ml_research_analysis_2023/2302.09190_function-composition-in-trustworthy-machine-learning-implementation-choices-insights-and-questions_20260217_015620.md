# Function Composition in Trustworthy Machine Learning: Implementation Choices, Insights, and Questions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09190
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09190
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Composing fairness interventions often reduces post-hoc explanation quality (faithfulness), with impacts varying by dataset and model.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a composer tool for modular composition of fairness and explainability functions from different trustworthy ML pillars. The tool supports bias mitigation algorithms (pre-, in-, post-processing) and post-hoc explainability (LIME), allowing composition via YAML configuration with metrics after each stage. Experiments on 7 datasets with 9 pipelines show that composing fairness interventions often reduces post-hoc explanation quality (faithfulness), with impacts varying by dataset and model. No single composition consistently improves fairness without degrading performance or explainability.

## Method Summary
The composer tool implements modular fairness interventions through a configurable pipeline: data → pre-processing → in-processing → post-processing → LIME → metrics. It supports bias mitigation algorithms (Reweighing, LFR, ROC, CEOdds, GridSearch, ExpGrad) and measures fairness (disparate impact, statistical parity, equal opportunity, average odds difference), performance (accuracy, balanced accuracy, ROC AUC), and explainability (LIME faithfulness). The system uses YAML configuration to define pipeline sequences and evaluates each stage independently.

## Key Results
- Composing fairness interventions often reduces post-hoc explanation quality (faithfulness)
- Overcorrection occurs when base models are already relatively fair, pushing DI > 1
- Post-processing interventions produce inconsistent results, sometimes worsening fairness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Composing fairness interventions can lead to overcorrection when the base model is already relatively fair.
- Mechanism: When the base model has moderate fairness (e.g., DI > 0.6), applying strong fairness corrections like LFR can push the model into reverse bias (DI > 1). Subsequent interventions compound this effect.
- Core assumption: Fairness metrics like DI are sensitive to sequential transformations and interventions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments on 7 datasets with 9 pipelines show that composing fairness interventions often reduces post-hoc explanation quality (faithfulness), with impacts varying by dataset and model."
  - [section] "Fig. 5 shows datasets for which the base classifier was relatively less unfair (DI > 0.6). In such cases, pre-processing typically worked well in making the model fairer. However, LFR had a much stronger corrective effect compared to Reweighing that resulted in an over-correction leading to models that were biased in the other direction (i.e., DI > 1)."
  - [corpus] Weak - no directly related neighbor discusses overcorrection dynamics.
- Break condition: If fairness metrics are recalibrated or bounded after each intervention, overcorrection can be mitigated.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adding post-processing fairness interventions after in-processing can either improve or degrade fairness, depending on the dataset.
- Mechanism: Post-processing methods like ROC or CEOdds adjust decision thresholds based on the output of an already trained in-processing model. Their effect is conditional on the distribution and base fairness of the dataset.
- Core assumption: Post-processing methods assume a well-calibrated in-processing base.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "No single composition consistently improves fairness without degrading performance or explainability."
  - [section] "Fig. 8–11 show the results of similar experiments involving pipelines that do not include a pre-processing intervention... post-processing interventions alone produced fairly inconsistent results; as in the case of pipelines involving pre-processing, CEOdds and ThreshOptim often performed worse than in-processing."
  - [corpus] Weak - no directly related neighbor discusses post-processing effects in multi-stage pipelines.
- Break condition: If the in-processing model is poorly calibrated or the dataset has complex group interactions, post-processing may degrade fairness.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Composing fairness interventions degrades the faithfulness of post-hoc explanations (LIME).
- Mechanism: Each fairness intervention modifies the model's decision surface and feature importance distributions. This alters the local linear approximation that LIME relies on, reducing its faithfulness metric.
- Core assumption: Post-hoc explanation methods assume the model's decision boundary is stable and interpretable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "...composing fairness interventions often reduces post-hoc explanation quality (faithfulness)..."
  - [section] "The quality of the post-hoc local explanations generated using LIME for the model after various interventions is shown by measuring the faithfulness... Fairness interventions result in a deterioration in the quality of the local explanations generated."
  - [corpus] Weak - no directly related neighbor discusses explainability degradation due to fairness interventions.
- Break condition: If explanations are regenerated after each intervention or if a more robust explanation method is used, the degradation can be detected or mitigated.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: YAML parser → Dataset loader → Pre-processing → In-processing → Post-processing → LIME explainer → Metric collector
- Critical path: Data → Pre-processing → Model training → In-processing → Post-processing → LIME → Metrics
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Modularity vs. performance: Each intervention is modular, but chaining them increases runtime and complexity.
  - Fairness vs. explainability: Interventions improve fairness but degrade explanation faithfulness.
  - Dataset generality vs. pipeline specificity: Pipelines are dataset-dependent; no universal best combination.
- Failure signatures:
  - Overcorrection: DI > 1 after fairness intervention (model biased against privileged group).
  - Explanation collapse: Faithfulness metric drops sharply after multiple interventions.
  - Inconsistent post-processing: CEOdds or ThreshOptim fail or worsen fairness.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run a single pre-processing intervention (Reweighing) on a moderately unfair dataset; check DI improvement and faithfulness drop.
  2. Chain pre-processing (LFR) + in-processing (GridSearch) on a highly unfair dataset; observe overcorrection risk.
  3. Apply post-processing (ROC) after in-processing (ExpGrad) on a dataset with already fair base model; evaluate fairness and explanation changes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does composing multiple fairness interventions lead to fairer models than a single intervention across all dataset types and model architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly asks "Does the composition of multiple fairness interventions result in a fairer model compared to a single intervention?"
- Why unresolved: The results show that fairness outcomes depend heavily on the dataset, model type, and specific pipeline configuration. No single composition consistently improves fairness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying dataset characteristics (base fairness levels, protected attributes), model architectures, and composition orders to identify generalizable patterns.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does composing fairness interventions affect the faithfulness of post-hoc explanations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates "How do bias mitigation algorithms for fairness affect local post-hoc explanations?"
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows explanations deteriorate with more interventions, it doesn't characterize the relationship or identify conditions where faithfulness can be preserved.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments measuring faithfulness across different intervention combinations and identifying which stages (pre-, in-, post-processing) have the largest impact.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are there optimal sequences for composing fairness interventions (e.g., pre-processing before in-processing) that consistently improve fairness without degrading performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper's discussion of pipelines with different intervention orders implies this question.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that outcomes are dataset-dependent and that certain orders can overcompensate, but doesn't establish optimal sequencing strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of all possible intervention orderings across multiple datasets to identify sequences that balance fairness gains with performance preservation.

## Limitations
- Limited generalizability due to use of 7 datasets and 9 pipeline configurations
- No single fairness metric dominates; different metrics can give conflicting signals
- Composer tool implementation not publicly available, limiting reproducibility

## Confidence
- High confidence in observation that composing fairness interventions degrades post-hoc explanation quality
- Medium confidence in overcorrection mechanism and inconsistent post-processing effects
- Medium confidence in findings due to limited dataset diversity and unpublished tool implementation
