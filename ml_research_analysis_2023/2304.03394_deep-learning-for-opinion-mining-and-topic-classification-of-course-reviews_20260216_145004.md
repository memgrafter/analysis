# Deep Learning for Opinion Mining and Topic Classification of Course Reviews

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03394
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03394
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: RoBERTa achieved 95.5% accuracy and 84.7% F1-macro on sentiment polarity; SVM achieved 79.8% accuracy and 80.6% F1-macro on topic classification

## Executive Summary
This study applies deep learning and transformer-based models to analyze course reviews for sentiment polarity and topic classification. The authors collected over 10,000 online course reviews and compared traditional classifiers (k-NN, Naïve Bayes, SVM) with deep learning approaches (CNNs, LSTMs) and transformer models (BERT, RoBERTa, XLNet). RoBERTa outperformed all other models on sentiment analysis, while SVM proved most effective for topic classification. The research demonstrates the effectiveness of modern NLP techniques for educational feedback analysis and explores the tradeoff between model accuracy and computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The authors collected 10,610 course reviews from CourseReport.com, preprocessing them for sentiment and topic classification tasks. For sentiment, they mapped 5-point ratings to binary labels (4-5 positive, 1-3 negative). For topic classification, they labeled reviews by course title keywords across four categories. They employed a comprehensive evaluation framework using traditional classifiers with TF-IDF features, deep learning models with word embeddings, and transformer models with pre-trained contextual embeddings. Performance was assessed using accuracy and F1-macro metrics with stratified cross-validation (10-fold for sentiment, 5-fold for topic).

## Key Results
- RoBERTa achieved the highest sentiment polarity accuracy at 95.5% and F1-macro of 84.7%
- SVM outperformed all DL models on topic classification with 79.8% accuracy and 80.6% F1-macro
- Increasing maxlen improved DL model accuracy for topic classification but reduced runtime efficiency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BERT-based models outperform traditional and DL models on sentiment polarity due to their contextualized embeddings.
- Mechanism: BERT uses Masked Language Modeling and bidirectional context, capturing nuanced sentiment in course reviews better than bag-of-words or word2vec embeddings.
- Core assumption: Pre-training on large text corpora provides a strong semantic foundation that generalizes to course review sentiment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "For sentiment polarity, the top model was RoBERTa with 95.5% accuracy and 84.7% F1-macro"
  - [section]: "BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) is considered state-of-the-art in several NLP tasks"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus does not mention BERT directly, but shows use of advanced models
- Break condition: If course reviews contain domain-specific jargon not covered in pre-training, contextualized embeddings may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SVM outperforms DL models on topic classification when using limited input text (low maxlen).
- Mechanism: Linear SVM with TF-IDF features can effectively separate topics based on keyword frequency, especially when topics are distinct (e.g., Web Development vs. Data Science).
- Core assumption: Course reviews contain sufficient discriminative keywords to enable SVM classification without deep contextual understanding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "for topic classification, an SVM was the top classifier with 79.8% accuracy and 80.6% F1-macro"
  - [section]: "SVM algorithm finds a hyperplane that separates the classes by their features"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus shows SVM used but does not explain why it outperformed DL models
- Break condition: If topics overlap significantly in vocabulary or reviews are too short, SVM performance degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Increasing maxlen improves DL model accuracy for topic classification by providing more context.
- Mechanism: Longer input sequences allow CNNs and transformers to capture more topical cues spread across the review, improving classification accuracy.
- Core assumption: Relevant topic indicators are distributed throughout the review rather than concentrated at the beginning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we also experimented with increasing maxlen for the CNN. Fig. 5b shows the effect of different maxlen values on the classification performance"
  - [abstract]: "we also explored how to improve the accuracy of our DL models, while looking at the relation of the improved accuracy on runtime efficiency"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus does not provide specific examples of topic indicators
- Break condition: If reviews are consistently short or topics are well-defined by early keywords, increasing maxlen offers diminishing returns.

  - Why needed here: Balances term frequency with corpus-wide rarity to emphasize discriminative words in course reviews.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data collection → Preprocessing → TF-IDF/Word Embeddings → Model Training (SVM, CNN, LSTM, BERT variants) → Evaluation (Accuracy, F1-macro) → Hyperparameter Tuning (maxlen, epochs)
- **Critical path**: Corpus preparation → Feature extraction → Model selection → Cross-validation → Performance evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**: BERT/RoBERTa/XLNet offer high accuracy but long training times; SVM is fast but less effective on complex patterns; increasing maxlen improves accuracy but increases computation.
- **Failure signatures**: Low F1-macro on minority class (sentiment), confusion between similar topics, overfitting with too many epochs.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run SVM with TF-IDF on sentiment analysis to establish baseline.
  2. Train CNN with word2vec embeddings on topic classification with maxlen=50.
  3. Fine-tune BERT on sentiment analysis with maxlen=100 and 3 epochs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would aspect-based sentiment analysis perform compared to document-level sentiment analysis for course feedback, and what specific aspects (e.g., instructor quality, course content, workload) would be most informative?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention this as a future research direction: "Future research goals are to further explore our data towards aspect-based sentiment analysis."
- Why unresolved: The current study only performs document-level sentiment analysis, not aspect-level analysis. No experiments or comparisons were conducted.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and comparison of aspect-based sentiment analysis models (e.g., using aspect-aware LSTMs or BERT-based models) on the same dataset, with performance metrics and insights on which aspects are most predictive of overall sentiment.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would using domain-specific pre-trained models like EduBERT significantly improve performance on course feedback classification tasks compared to general-purpose models like BERT or RoBERTa?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state: "We also plan to explore other features in the data, such as helpfulness of the reviews, and explore the use of additional pre-trained models such as EduBERT."
- Why unresolved: The study only used general-purpose transformer models (BERT, RoBERTa, XLNet). No experiments were conducted with education-specific pre-trained models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and fine-tuning EduBERT (or similar education-domain models) on the course feedback dataset, comparing performance metrics (accuracy, F1-score) against the general-purpose models used in this study.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would incorporating helpfulness ratings of reviews as additional features impact the performance of sentiment and topic classification models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention: "We also plan to explore other features in the data, such as helpfulness of the reviews."
- Why unresolved: The study did not use helpfulness ratings as features. The dataset contained this information but it was not utilized in the experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Creating new feature vectors that combine text embeddings with helpfulness scores, retraining the classification models, and comparing performance against the original models that only used text features.

## Limitations
- Corpus collected from single source (CourseReport.com), potentially introducing platform-specific biases
- Binary sentiment mapping (4-5 positive, 1-3 negative) discards potentially useful neutral sentiment information
- Topic classification relies on keyword matching of course titles, which may not capture all relevant topics accurately

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: RoBERTa's superior performance on sentiment analysis (95.5% accuracy) - well-supported by results and aligns with established literature on transformer models
- **Medium Confidence**: SVM's top performance on topic classification (79.8% accuracy) - results are clear but mechanism is less well-explained
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about runtime efficiency tradeoffs - while runtime was measured, the analysis of efficiency-accuracy tradeoffs is relatively superficial
