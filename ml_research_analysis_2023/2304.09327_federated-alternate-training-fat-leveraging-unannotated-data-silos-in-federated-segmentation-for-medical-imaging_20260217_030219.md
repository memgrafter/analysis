# Federated Alternate Training (FAT): Leveraging Unannotated Data Silos in Federated Segmentation for Medical Imaging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09327
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09327
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Alternate training between supervised and unsupervised silos improves segmentation performance compared to training only supervised silos

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a federated learning framework, Federated Alternate Training (FAT), to leverage unannotated data silos for medical image segmentation. The key idea is to alternate training between supervised and unsupervised silos, where supervised silos train on ground truth labels while unsupervised silos use a target model to generate pseudo labels for self-supervised learning. The proposed method outperforms the state-of-the-art on two medical image segmentation tasks: kidney and tumor segmentation (KiTS19) and brain tumor segmentation (FeTS2021).

## Method Summary
The FAT framework alternates training between supervised silos (with ground truth labels) and unsupervised silos (without labels) in a federated learning setting. Supervised silos train the global model using supervised learning objectives, while unsupervised silos use a target model to generate pseudo labels for self-supervised learning. The framework employs bootstrapping with online and target models, where the target model is updated via exponential moving average (EMA) of the online model to stabilize training. Mixup augmentation is applied to both supervised and unsupervised training to improve generalization. The method is evaluated on KiTS19 and FeTS2021 datasets, achieving Dice scores of 73% and 66% for tumor and enhancing tumor respectively, compared to 61.5% and 63.5% for the state-of-the-art.

## Key Results
- FAT achieves Dice scores of 73% and 66% for tumor and enhancing tumor segmentation on KiTS19 and FeTS2021 datasets
- The method outperforms the state-of-the-art (61.5% and 63.5% Dice scores) by leveraging unannotated data silos
- FAT also shows improvements over fully supervised federated learning, demonstrating the usefulness of unannotated data silos

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Alternate training between supervised and unsupervised silos improves global model performance compared to training only supervised silos.
- Mechanism: By alternating training rounds between annotated data silos (which train on ground truth labels) and unannotated data silos (which generate pseudo labels using the current global model), the framework leverages unannotated data to improve the model without requiring annotations in all silos.
- Core assumption: The global model learned from supervised silos can generate useful pseudo labels for unsupervised silos, and the model does not forget what it learned from supervised data during unsupervised training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose an alternate training-based framework, Federated Alternate Training (FAT), that alters training between annotated data silos and unannotated data silos."
  - [section] "We proposed alternate training between the supervised and unsupervised silos to solve the objective in eq. (2)."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific alternating mechanism.
- Break condition: If the pseudo labels generated by the global model are too noisy or inaccurate, unsupervised training may degrade rather than improve performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Bootstrapping with online and target models improves pseudo label quality in unsupervised silos.
- Mechanism: Unsupervised silos maintain two models - an online model and a target model. The online model generates predictions for augmented inputs, while the target model generates pseudo labels for unperturbed inputs. The target model is updated via exponential moving average (EMA) of the online model, which stabilizes training and improves pseudo label quality.
- Core assumption: The EMA-updated target model provides more stable and accurate pseudo labels than using the online model alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Instead of maintaining one neural architecture, as is used in the previous work [2], we maintain two models referred to as the online model with parameters ξ and the target model with parameters θ."
  - [section] "After each training step of the online model, the target model is updated by the exponential moving average θ = τθ + (1−τ )ξ, where τ ∈ (0, 1) is a decay rate of the target model."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific bootstrapping mechanism.
- Break condition: If the EMA decay rate τ is too high or too low, the target model may become too slow to adapt or too unstable, degrading pseudo label quality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mixup augmentation improves model generalization in both supervised and unsupervised training.
- Mechanism: The framework uses mixup augmentation, where input data is linearly interpolated between two samples, and the corresponding labels are mixed proportionally. This augmentation is applied to both supervised and unsupervised silos, helping the model generalize better.
- Core assumption: Mixup augmentation helps the model learn more robust features and improves generalization, especially in the unsupervised setting where labels are noisy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "For self-supervised training, we use the mixup approach [6] to augment the input data and feed the perturbed version x′ = λx1 + (1−λ)x2 of two randomly selected input data points x1 and x2, where λ ∈ (0, 1) is a hyperparameter."
  - [section] "In parallel, we feed the unperturbed data points to x1 and x2 to the target modelfθ and perturbed their corresponding prediction probabilities p1 and p2 via mixup logic p′ = λp1 + (1−λ)p2."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for mixup in this specific federated learning context.
- Break condition: If the mixup parameter λ is not properly tuned, the augmented samples may be too far from the original distribution, hurting model performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Server -> Supervised silos -> Unsupervised silos -> Online model -> Target model -> Mixup augmentation

- Critical path: The critical path involves the server initializing the global model, sending it to supervised silos for training, aggregating the updates, sending the updated model to unsupervised silos for self-supervised training, and repeating this process for a fixed number of rounds.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a pre-trained model for initialization vs. training from scratch: Pretraining helps achieve better performance but may introduce bias if the pretraining dataset is not representative.
  - Number of supervised vs. unsupervised silos: Having more unsupervised silos allows leveraging more unannotated data but may lead to noisier pseudo labels.
  - EMA decay rate τ: A higher τ leads to a more stable target model but may slow down adaptation to new data.

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation in unsupervised silos: If the pseudo labels are too noisy, unsupervised training may degrade the global model's performance.
  - Divergence between supervised and unsupervised silos: If the global model's performance on supervised data significantly differs from its performance on unsupervised data, it may indicate a problem with the alternating training approach.
  - Overfitting to pseudo labels: If the model starts overfitting to the pseudo labels generated by the target model, its performance on real data may degrade.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the model using only supervised silos (no unsupervised silos) to establish a baseline performance.
  2. Train the model using supervised and unsupervised silos but without alternate training (i.e., train all silos simultaneously) to assess the importance of the alternating approach.
  3. Train the model using supervised and unsupervised silos with alternate training but without the bootstrapping mechanism (i.e., use only one model instead of online and target models) to evaluate the impact of the bootstrapping approach.

## Limitations
- The framework requires careful hyperparameter tuning for EMA decay rate and mixup augmentation, which may not generalize across different datasets or model architectures
- The method assumes the global model can generate sufficiently accurate pseudo labels for unannotated data, which may not hold for highly heterogeneous or complex medical imaging tasks
- The evaluation focuses on specific medical segmentation tasks (kidney and brain tumor) with limited data partitioning schemes, raising questions about generalizability
