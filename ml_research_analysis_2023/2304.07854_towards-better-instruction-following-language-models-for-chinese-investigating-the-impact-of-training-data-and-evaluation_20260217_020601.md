# Towards Better Instruction Following Language Models for Chinese: Investigating the Impact of Training Data and Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07854
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07854
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- The best model achieves 0.762 macro-averaged score on Chinese instruction following tasks

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how training data factors—quantity, quality, and linguistic distribution—affect Chinese instruction-following language model performance. The researchers extend LLaMA's vocabulary for Chinese and conduct secondary pre-training on 3.4B Chinese words, achieving a 60% reduction in training and inference time without performance loss. They evaluate models using a 1,000-sample test set across nine real-world scenarios, finding that high-quality Chinese instruction data and vocabulary extension significantly improve performance. The best model, combining Chinese Alpaca, ShareGPT, and Belle datasets, outperforms base LLaMA but still shows substantial gaps compared to ChatGPT.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes LLaMA 7B on various Chinese instruction datasets including zh(alpaca-3.5&4), zh(alpaca-3.5), zh(alpaca-4), sharegpt, and belle-3.5. Models are trained for 3 epochs with batch size 32 and learning rate 5e-6. The researchers extend LLaMA's vocabulary to 79,458 tokens using BPE on 12M lines of Chinese text, reducing average tokens per line from 733 to 291. Performance is evaluated on a 1,000-sample Chinese instruction set across nine scenarios using ChatGPT as the automatic judge.

## Key Results
- Extended LLaMA vocabulary reduces tokens per line from 733 to 291, achieving 60% reduction in training/inference time
- GPT-4 generated data (alpaca-4-zh) outperforms GPT-3.5 generated data (alpaca-3.5-zh) for instruction following
- Best model (combination of Chinese Alpaca, ShareGPT, and Belle) achieves 0.762 macro-averaged score
- Chinese instruction data outperforms English instruction data for Chinese tasks, though transfer learning is effective
- Performance gap remains between best Chinese model and ChatGPT

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Extending LLaMA's vocabulary with Chinese-specific tokens improves model efficiency and reduces inference time without performance loss.
- Mechanism: Chinese characters are often split into multiple subword tokens by standard tokenizers, increasing token count and computational overhead. Extending the vocabulary to 79,458 tokens, specifically tuned for Chinese using BPE on 12M lines of text, allows each character to be represented as a single token. This reduces average tokens per line from 733 to 291, directly lowering the computational load.
- Core assumption: A larger, Chinese-optimized vocabulary does not introduce ambiguity or loss of semantic meaning during tokenization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "extend the vocabulary of LLaMA ... and conduct secondary pre-training on 3.4B Chinese words. We test the extended tokenizer and the original tokenizer on 5,000 lines of Chinese text, and the average tokens of a line reduces from 733 to 291."
  - [section] "Chinese character may be split into 2 to 3 byte tokens, which severely affects the model's fine-tuning and inference speed on Chinese data."
- Break condition: If the extended vocabulary causes increased tokenization ambiguity or if the pre-training step overfits to specific Chinese text patterns, performance could degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Higher quality training data (GPT-4 generated vs GPT-3.5) leads to better instruction-following performance.
- Mechanism: GPT-4 produces more accurate, coherent, and contextually appropriate responses compared to GPT-3.5. When used to generate instruction-following samples, these higher-quality responses serve as better supervision signals during fine-tuning, improving the model's ability to understand and execute complex instructions.
- Core assumption: The quality gap between GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 responses is significant enough to measurably impact downstream model performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The performance of LLaMA-EXT, when trained with alpaca-4-zh, surpasses that of the model trained with alpaca-3.5-zh, thus highlighting the critical impact of high-quality training data on enhancing model performance."
- Break condition: If the evaluation set is not sensitive enough to detect quality differences, or if the model overfits to the style of GPT-4 responses without improving generalization.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multilingual instruction-following data improves performance on the target language (Chinese) even when training data is primarily in another language (English).
- Mechanism: Pre-trained models have inherent multilingual capabilities. Training on English instruction data transfers linguistic and reasoning patterns that are applicable across languages. Adding even a small amount of Chinese data (e.g., ShareGPT) significantly boosts Chinese-specific performance by aligning the model's responses with the target language's syntax and semantics.
- Core assumption: The multilingual transfer is effective enough that minimal target-language data can correct for linguistic mismatches.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Comparing the performance of LLaMA-EXT trained on zh(alpaca-3.5&4) with the performance of LLaMA-EXT trained on en(alpaca-3.5&4), it is observed that utilizing Chinese instruction-following data results in superior performance within Chinese testing scenarios. Interestingly, the disparity in performance between these two models is not substantial, which suggests that the model's capacity to comprehend instructions in one language can be effectively transferred to another language."
  - [section] "The scores of zh(alpaca-3.5&4)+sharegpt and en(alpaca-3.5&4)+sharegpt are nearly identical. This signifies that the addition of a modest quantity of Chinese data (which comes from sharegpt) to the English training data can dramatically enhance performance within the Chinese context."
- Break condition: If the evaluation set does not adequately capture cross-linguistic performance differences, or if the model's multilingual transfer is weaker than assumed.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Tokenizer (extended BPE) -> LLaMA 7B backbone -> Fine-tuning pipeline (LoRA/full-parameter) -> ChatGPT API evaluation
- Critical path:
  1. Tokenize Chinese instruction data using extended tokenizer
  2. Fine-tune LLaMA on instruction-following datasets
  3. Generate responses on evaluation set
  4. Score responses using ChatGPT API
  5. Aggregate scores across task categories
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Extending vocabulary increases tokenizer complexity but reduces inference time
  - Using ChatGPT for evaluation provides scalability but introduces potential bias and inconsistency
  - Balancing dataset size and quality affects both training efficiency and final performance
- Failure signatures:
  - Vocabulary extension leads to ambiguous tokenization (multiple valid segmentations)
  - Fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting of general language capabilities
  - Evaluation scores are inconsistent across different ChatGPT invocations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare token counts and inference times using original vs extended tokenizer on a fixed Chinese text corpus
  2. Fine-tune two models (one with Alpaca-3.5, one with Alpaca-4) and compare performance on a shared evaluation set
  3. Train models with increasing amounts of Chinese vs English data and measure performance differences

## Limitations

- The evaluation methodology relies entirely on ChatGPT as an automatic judge, introducing potential bias and inconsistency
- The study doesn't thoroughly examine potential trade-offs of vocabulary extension such as tokenization ambiguity
- Performance gap remains substantial between best Chinese model (0.762) and ChatGPT, suggesting fundamental limitations

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Extended vocabulary reduces token count and inference time; higher-quality training data improves performance
- Medium Confidence: Multilingual transfer enables effective instruction following; modest Chinese data addition improves performance
- Low Confidence: 60% time reduction achieves "no compromise in performance" claim; comprehensive validation of vocabulary extension benefits

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Evaluator Validation**: Run the same evaluation set through multiple independent judges (human annotators and other LLM judges) to assess the reliability and potential bias of ChatGPT-based scoring. This would validate whether the reported performance differences are consistent across evaluation methods.

2. **Tokenization Ambiguity Analysis**: Systematically test the extended vocabulary on diverse Chinese text samples to identify cases where tokenization becomes ambiguous or produces suboptimal segmentations. This would validate the assumption that vocabulary extension doesn't introduce semantic ambiguity.

3. **Transfer Learning Boundary Test**: Conduct controlled experiments varying the ratio of Chinese to English training data to precisely quantify the point at which additional Chinese data stops providing marginal improvements. This would validate the claimed effectiveness of multilingual transfer and help optimize data efficiency.
