# SensePOLAR: Word sense aware interpretability for pre-trained contextual word embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04704
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04704
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: SensePOLAR achieves 87% human agreement for top-1 dimensions in word sense interpretability tasks

## Executive Summary
SensePOLAR addresses the critical challenge of interpretability in contextual word embeddings by extending the POLAR framework to handle word sense disambiguation. The method transforms contextual embeddings into an interpretable space using polar sense dimensions rather than word pairs, enabling differentiation between multiple senses of the same word. By leveraging WordNet as an oracle to generate polar sense embeddings, SensePOLAR constructs a polar sense space through linear algebra operations that maintains performance comparable to original BERT embeddings across various NLP tasks while providing semantically meaningful and human-aligned dimensions.

## Method Summary
SensePOLAR extends the POLAR framework by replacing word pairs with word sense pairs to create interpretable polar dimensions. The method uses WordNet to identify antonymous sense pairs (e.g., "right direction" ↔ "wrong direction") and generates polar sense embeddings through linear transformations of original BERT embeddings. A polar sense space is constructed by performing orthogonalization and projection operations on these embeddings. When applied to downstream tasks, SensePOLAR transforms input embeddings into this interpretable space before classification, achieving performance parity with BERT while providing interpretable dimensions that align well with human semantic judgments.

## Key Results
- Achieves 87% human agreement for top-1 polar sense dimensions in survey experiments
- Maintains performance comparable to BERT on GLUE benchmark (within 0.9% difference)
- Maintains performance comparable to BERT on SQuAD (within 0.5% difference)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SensePOLAR works by leveraging the inherent structure of antonymous word sense pairs to create interpretable dimensions. The method exploits the fact that opposing senses naturally form a basis for semantic space, where each dimension represents a clear semantic opposition. By constructing a polar sense space through orthogonalization of these basis vectors, SensePOLAR creates an interpretable transformation of the original embedding space that preserves semantic relationships while making them explicit and human-understandable.

## Foundational Learning
- **Polar embeddings**: The concept of representing semantic dimensions as pairs of opposing concepts rather than single vectors, needed to create interpretable semantic axes
- **WordNet sense inventory**: A lexical database that provides sense distinctions and relationships, needed as an oracle for identifying valid sense pairs
- **Orthogonalization in embedding spaces**: The mathematical process of creating perpendicular basis vectors, needed to construct a well-defined polar sense space
- **Contextual word embeddings**: Dynamic word representations that vary based on context, needed as the starting point for transformation
- **Linear transformation for interpretability**: Using matrix operations to project embeddings into interpretable spaces, needed to maintain performance while adding interpretability

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Original BERT embeddings -> WordNet sense extraction -> Polar sense embedding generation -> Orthogonalization -> Polar sense space construction -> Transformed embeddings -> Downstream task

**Critical Path**
The critical path flows from original BERT embeddings through WordNet-based sense extraction, polar sense embedding generation, orthogonalization, and space construction to produce transformed embeddings for downstream tasks. Each step depends on the previous one, with the orthogonalization step being computationally intensive but necessary for creating a valid interpretable space.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- **Performance vs interpretability**: The method maintains near-BERT performance but at computational cost of transformation
- **Coverage vs precision**: WordNet provides broad coverage but may miss domain-specific or novel senses
- **Dimensionality vs granularity**: More polar dimensions increase interpretability but may dilute semantic distinctions

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor performance on domain-specific tasks where WordNet coverage is limited
- Reduced interpretability when sense distinctions are subtle or context-dependent
- Computational overhead may limit real-time applications

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on WordNet's sense inventory, which may not capture all semantic nuances
- Survey-based human evaluation with limited sample size (25 participants, 50 dimensions)
- Performance improvements over BERT are marginal (0.9% and 0.5% differences)
