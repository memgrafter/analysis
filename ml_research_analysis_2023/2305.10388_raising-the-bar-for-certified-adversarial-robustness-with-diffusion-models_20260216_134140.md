# Raising the Bar for Certified Adversarial Robustness with Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10388
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10388
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: Certified adversarial robustness improved by up to 5.28 percentage points using synthetic data from diffusion models

## Executive Summary
This work demonstrates that deterministic certified defenses against adversarial attacks can be significantly improved by augmenting training with synthetic data generated from diffusion models. The authors find that training with a mixture of real and generated data (30% real, 70% generated) improves certified accuracy for ℓ∞ and ℓ₂ threat models on CIFAR-10 by up to 5.28 percentage points. Specifically, they achieve new state-of-the-art certified accuracies of 41.78% for ℓ∞ (ε = 8/255) and 69.05% for ℓ₂ (ε = 36/255). The improvement correlates with the model's generalization gap on clean data, and increasing model capacity and training epochs further enhances robustness. The method is also effective on CIFAR-100, improving certified accuracy by up to 8.08 percentage points.

## Method Summary
The authors train deterministic certified defense models (SortNet, ℓ∞-dist Net, LOT, GloroNet) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets augmented with synthetic data generated by diffusion models. They use a 30% real to 70% generated data ratio and conduct ablation studies varying model capacity, training epochs, regularization, and data ratios to identify optimal configurations for certified robustness.

## Key Results
- Achieved new state-of-the-art certified accuracies: 41.78% for ℓ∞ (ε = 8/255) and 69.05% for ℓ₂ (ε = 36/255) on CIFAR-10
- Certified accuracy improved by up to 5.28 percentage points compared to training with real data only
- Effectiveness confirmed on CIFAR-100 with up to 8.08 percentage point improvement
- Generalization gap correlates with robustness improvement when using generated data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training with generated data from diffusion models improves certified robustness by closing the generalization gap.
- Mechanism: Generated data provides additional, high-quality training samples that help the model generalize better to unseen data, which is crucial for achieving strong certified robustness guarantees.
- Core assumption: The quality of generated data is high enough to effectively augment the original training data without introducing harmful biases or artifacts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach achieves state-of-the-art deterministic robustness certificates on CIFAR-10... improving upon the previous best results... by +3.95% and +1.39%, respectively."
  - [section] "One of our main insights is that the generalization gap, i.e., the difference between the training and test accuracy of the original model, is a good predictor of the magnitude of the robustness improvement when using additional generated data."
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct mention of diffusion models or generalization gap in corpus.
- Break condition: If generated data quality is poor or introduces significant biases, it could harm rather than help the model's robustness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Increasing model capacity and training epochs further enhances robustness when using generated data.
- Mechanism: Larger models have more capacity to learn from the increased diversity of training data, and longer training allows them to better fit this data without overfitting.
- Core assumption: The increased model capacity and training time are utilized effectively and do not lead to overfitting on the generated data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "increasing model capacity and training epochs further enhances robustness."
  - [section] "Scaling the Model Size... we find that scaling the model size can increase the certified robustness... for both LOT and GloroNet."
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct mention of model capacity or training epochs in corpus.
- Break condition: If model capacity is increased too much or training is prolonged excessively, it could lead to overfitting and decreased robustness.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Generated data acts as a regularizer, improving robustness even when it slightly decreases clean accuracy.
- Mechanism: Generated data introduces additional variability and constraints during training, forcing the model to learn more robust features that generalize better to adversarial examples.
- Core assumption: The benefits of regularization from generated data outweigh any potential decrease in clean accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In most cases, the increase in certified accuracy is accompanied by an increase in accuracy on clean data..."
  - [section] "Here, the certified robustness of the SortNet model trained without auxiliary data... decreases when using less regularization by removing dropout... However, once additional synthetic data is used, removing dropout actually improves the certified robustness..."
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct mention of regularization or clean accuracy in corpus.
- Break condition: If the regularization effect of generated data is too strong, it could significantly harm clean accuracy and overall model performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data loader -> Model architecture -> Training loop -> Certification module

- Critical path:
  1. Load and mix real and generated data
  2. Forward pass through the model
  3. Compute loss and perform backpropagation
  4. Update model parameters
  5. Periodically compute certified robustness on a validation set
  6. Save the best model based on certified robustness

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Generated data ratio: Balancing the amount of real vs. generated data to maximize robustness without harming clean accuracy
  - Model capacity: Choosing the right model size to effectively learn from the increased data diversity
  - Training duration: Determining the optimal number of epochs to train without overfitting

- Failure signatures:
  - Decreased clean accuracy: Indicates that the generated data may be introducing harmful biases or the model is overfitting
  - No improvement in certified robustness: Suggests that the generated data is not providing sufficient benefit or the model capacity/training duration is not optimized
  - Increased training time without benefits: Implies that the model is overfitting to the generated data or the training duration is too long

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a baseline model without any generated data to establish a reference point for clean and certified accuracy.
  2. Train the same model with a small amount of generated data (e.g., 1M images) to assess the initial impact on robustness.
  3. Gradually increase the amount of generated data (e.g., 5M, 10M images) and monitor the changes in both clean and certified accuracy to find the optimal ratio.

## Limitations
- The exact procedure for generating the synthetic datasets is not fully specified, making independent reproduction difficult
- The benefits of generated data are highly dependent on model capacity and training duration, but the paper does not provide a systematic analysis of how to optimally tune these factors for new datasets or architectures
- The mechanism by which generated data improves robustness is only loosely connected to empirical observations (e.g., generalization gap) and lacks a formal theoretical justification

## Confidence
- Certified accuracy improvements: High
- Mechanism relating to generalization gap: Medium
- Effectiveness of scaling (model capacity, epochs): Medium
