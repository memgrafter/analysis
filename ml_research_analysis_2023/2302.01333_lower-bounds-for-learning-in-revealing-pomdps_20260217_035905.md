# Lower Bounds for Learning in Revealing POMDPs
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01333
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01333
- Reference count: 40
- Multi-step revealing POMDPs require Ω(pS^1.5) sample complexity and Ω(T^2/3) regret

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes fundamental limits for reinforcement learning in revealing POMDPs, a subclass of partially observable environments where observations gradually reveal information about the true state. While previous work showed polynomial sample complexities are achievable in revealing POMDPs, the fundamental limits remained poorly understood. The authors prove that learning in multi-step revealing POMDPs is substantially harder than in fully observable MDPs, with PAC sample complexity scaling as Ω(pS^1.5) rather than Θ(S), and regret bounds of Ω(T^2/3) rather than sublinear. These results significantly reduce the gap between existing upper and lower bounds and provide a solid foundation for understanding the complexity of partially observable RL.

## Method Summary
The authors employ techniques from distribution testing to establish lower bounds for learning in revealing POMDPs. The core approach involves embedding uniformity testing problems into revealing POMDPs, creating hard instances that require substantial exploration to distinguish between different underlying state distributions. By carefully constructing observation structures that reveal information gradually across multiple steps, the authors demonstrate that standard RL algorithms cannot efficiently learn optimal policies without encountering exponential sample complexity or regret. The proofs leverage information-theoretic arguments about the distinguishability of observation sequences and the computational hardness of identifying optimal actions in partially observable settings.

## Key Results
- PAC sample complexity in multi-step revealing POMDPs has at least Ω(pS^1.5) dependence on state space size
- Polynomial sublinear regret requires at least Ω(T^2/3) regret, fundamentally harder than O(√T) achievable in single-step revealing POMDPs
- Hard instance constructions use distribution testing techniques, embedding uniformity testing into revealing POMDPs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The fundamental difficulty arises from the partial observability in revealing POMDPs. Unlike fully observable MDPs where each state can be directly observed, revealing POMDPs require agents to maintain and update belief states based on observation sequences. The authors show that even in revealing POMDPs where observations eventually reveal state information, the multi-step nature creates exponential blowup in the number of possible observation histories. This forces any algorithm to either maintain exponentially large belief states or risk making suboptimal decisions due to uncertainty. The distribution testing embedding creates hard instances where distinguishing between optimal and near-optimal policies requires testing whether observation distributions are uniform, a problem known to have strong information-theoretic lower bounds.

## Limitations
- Results apply specifically to revealing POMDPs, which may not capture all partially observable environments
- The Ω(pS^1.5) bound assumes certain conditions on the revealing rate p, with unclear tightness across different regimes
- The Ω(T^2/3) regret bound may not extend to all revealing POMDP structures with different observation patterns
