# SeeGULL: A Stereotype Benchmark with Broad Geo-Cultural Coverage Leveraging Generative Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.11840
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11840
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Novel stereotype benchmark covering 178 countries with 7,750 stereotypes, enabling comprehensive evaluation of stereotyping harms in NLI models

## Executive Summary
SeeGULL is a large-scale stereotype benchmark dataset created using generative large language models and human validation to evaluate stereotyping harms in natural language inference systems. The dataset covers stereotypes about identity groups across 178 countries spanning 8 geopolitical regions, including state-level identities within the US and India. It contains 7,750 stereotypes with fine-grained offensiveness scores and demonstrates significant global disparities in stereotype prevalence across regions. The benchmark reveals that existing stereotype evaluation methods miss substantial stereotyping in regions like Latin America and Sub-Saharan Africa, and shows that stereotypes about the same groups vary substantially across different geographic contexts.

## Method Summary
The SeeGULL benchmark was created through a multi-stage process combining generative AI with human validation. The authors used generative large language models to produce stereotypes about various identity groups across different geographic regions, then validated and scored these stereotypes through human annotators. The dataset covers 178 countries organized into 8 geopolitical regions, with particular attention to state-level identities within the US and India. Each stereotype was assigned fine-grained offensiveness scores, and the resulting dataset was used to evaluate natural language inference models for stereotyping harms. The approach enabled systematic collection of stereotypes at a scale and geographic breadth that would be difficult to achieve through traditional human annotation alone.

## Key Results
- Dataset covers 7,750 stereotypes across 178 countries spanning 8 geopolitical regions
- Demonstrates significant variation in stereotypes about the same groups across different geographic contexts
- Reveals substantial stereotyping in Latin America and Sub-Saharan Africa that existing benchmarks miss
- Shows global disparities in stereotype prevalence, with different regions exhibiting distinct stereotyping patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark works by leveraging generative models to systematically explore stereotype space across diverse geographic and cultural contexts, then validating these outputs with human judgment. This approach captures the socially situated nature of stereotypes by explicitly modeling how the same identity groups are stereotyped differently in different regions. The fine-grained offensiveness scoring enables nuanced evaluation of stereotyping harms beyond simple binary classifications, while the broad geographic coverage ensures that evaluation is not limited to the typically studied Western contexts.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** LLM Generation -> Human Validation -> Offensiveness Scoring -> NLI Model Evaluation -> Geographic Analysis

**Critical Path:** The critical path flows from generative stereotype creation through human validation to model evaluation. Each stage depends on the previous: generation provides raw material, validation ensures quality, scoring enables nuanced analysis, and evaluation tests real-world impact.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The authors traded potential generation biases for scalability and geographic breadth. While human-only collection would be more culturally grounded, the generative approach enabled coverage of 178 countries that would be impractical with manual annotation alone. The tradeoff between breadth and depth required accepting some uncertainty about whether generated stereotypes accurately reflect local cultural perspectives.

**Failure Signatures:** The system may fail when generative models produce stereotypes that don't reflect actual cultural attitudes, or when human validators impose their own cultural biases on scoring. Geographic analysis may be misleading if region definitions don't align with actual cultural boundaries, and NLI evaluation may not capture all forms of stereotyping harm.

## Limitations
- Generalizability uncertainty due to potential systematic biases in generative model stereotype patterns
- Ethical concerns about documenting and analyzing offensive stereotypes potentially reinforcing harmful biases
- Reliance on NLI models may not capture all forms of stereotyping harm, limiting evaluation comprehensiveness
- Human validation process may introduce cultural biases in what is considered offensive or stereotypical

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** Dataset creation methodology is clearly described and reproducible; scale (7,750 stereotypes across 178 countries) is verifiable; geographic coverage claims are well-supported.

**Medium Confidence:** Finding that stereotypes vary across geographic contexts is supported by data; claim about missing stereotypes in Latin America and Sub-Saharan Africa is plausible but requires independent validation.

**Low Confidence:** Assertion that SeeGULL provides more comprehensive evaluation framework than existing benchmarks; while dataset is broader in scope, superior performance in detecting stereotyping harms is not conclusively demonstrated.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct cross-cultural validation studies where stereotypes identified in one region are evaluated for relevance and offensiveness by human annotators from different regions, to verify the claimed geographic variation in stereotype patterns.

2. Compare stereotype detection performance using SeeGULL versus existing benchmarks across multiple model types (not just NLI models) to establish whether the claimed comprehensiveness advantage holds across different evaluation approaches.

3. Perform an audit of the generative model's output patterns to identify any systematic biases in stereotype generation, such as overrepresentation of certain stereotype types or underrepresentation of others based on the model's training data.
