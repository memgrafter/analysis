# MultiModal Bias: Introducing a Framework for Stereotypical Bias Assessment beyond Gender and Race in Vision Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12734
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12734
- Reference count: 12
- Models exhibit meaningful stereotypical biases favoring certain groups, with up to 93% bias reduction achievable through post-processing debiasing.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the lack of benchmarks for assessing stereotypical bias in multimodal vision–language models beyond gender and race. The authors compile a new dataset called MMBias with 3,500 images and 350 phrases covering 14 subgroups, and use it to evaluate bias in CLIP, ALBEF, and ViLT using metrics derived from the Caliskan test. They find that these models exhibit meaningful biases favoring certain groups (e.g., associating Islam and Judaism with negative attributes more than Christianity and Buddhism). To mitigate this, they propose a post-processing debiasing method that removes redundant embedding dimensions, achieving up to 93% bias reduction with minimal impact on classification accuracy.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce the MMBias dataset containing 3,500 images and 350 phrases across 14 subgroups (religious, national, disability, and sexual orientation minorities). They evaluate bias using the Caliskan test, measuring effect sizes between target groups and attribute sets through cosine similarity. For fusion-based models (ALBEF, ViLT), they adapt the evaluation using image-text matching probabilities. The debiasing method iteratively removes embedding dimensions with low mutual information to classification labels, reducing bias while preserving accuracy. They validate the approach through zero-shot classification experiments and ablation studies.

## Key Results
- CLIP, ALBEF, and ViLT exhibit significant stereotypical biases across religious, national, disability, and sexual orientation groups
- The models show stronger associations between Islam/Judaism and negative attributes compared to Christianity/Buddhism
- The proposed debiasing method achieves up to 93% bias reduction while maintaining classification accuracy on CIFAR-100 and MMBias

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The bias mitigation algorithm removes redundant embedding dimensions without harming classification accuracy.
- Mechanism: By iteratively pruning embedding dimensions with low mutual information to classification labels, the algorithm removes features that contribute little to task performance but amplify bias.
- Core assumption: Some embedding dimensions are highly correlated and can be removed without affecting the model's core representational capacity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "introduce a debiasing method designed specifically for such large pre-trained models that can be applied as a post-processing step to mitigate bias, while preserving the remaining accuracy of the model."
  - [section] "we can remove some without affecting performance, while simultaneously reducing bias."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus lacks specific mention of redundancy-based debiasing.
- Break condition: If dimensions removed are not truly redundant (high MI with labels), classification accuracy will drop.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Caliskan test effectively quantifies bias by measuring differences in cosine similarity between target groups and attribute sets.
- Mechanism: The effect size formula computes the standardized difference in mean cosine distances, revealing how strongly a model associates certain groups with positive/negative attributes.
- Core assumption: Word embeddings or image embeddings capture meaningful semantic relationships that reflect bias patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Caliskan et al. (2017) introduced the Caliskan test shown below in Eq. 1, with φ capturing the difference of the mean of cosine distances between targets and attributes."
  - [section] "The effect size represented by d(X, Y, A, B) is a measure of the magnitude of the bias."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors discuss bias measurement but not Caliskan test specifically.
- Break condition: If embeddings don't capture semantic relationships well, the test will not reflect true bias.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fusion-based models can be evaluated for bias using image-text matching probabilities instead of separate embeddings.
- Mechanism: Since fusion models combine modalities internally, bias is measured by comparing ITM probabilities for different target-attribute pairings.
- Core assumption: A fair model will assign similar matching probabilities to positive attributes across different target groups.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "However, interestingly one of the objectives these models optimize for is the image–text matching (ITM) objective. ITM is the problem of estimating the probability that a given image–text pair is a match."
  - [section] "we can argue that a model is fair if the probability of assigning pleasantness scores is similar across different concepts."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors don't discuss fusion models or ITM-based bias measurement.
- Break condition: If ITM probability differences don't correlate with actual bias, this metric won't be reliable.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MMBias dataset collection -> Bias measurement (Caliskan test/ITM) -> Bias mitigation (dimensionality reduction) -> Validation (accuracy/bias metrics)
- Critical path: Data collection → Bias measurement → Mitigation → Validation (accuracy/bias reduction metrics)
- Design tradeoffs: The choice between cross-modal (separate embeddings) and intra-modal (fusion-based) evaluation methods reflects a tradeoff between precision and applicability across different model architectures.
- Failure signatures: If bias reduction is accompanied by significant accuracy drop, the mitigation is too aggressive. If bias remains high after mitigation, the algorithm may not be removing the right features.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run bias measurement on CLIP using the Caliskan test to establish baseline bias scores.
  2. Apply the debiasing algorithm with varying numbers of dimensions removed to find the optimal trade-off point.
  3. Validate the debiased model on both the MMbias dataset and an external dataset (like CIFAR-100) to ensure minimal accuracy degradation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does bias manifest in vision-language models for religious groups beyond the ones studied (e.g., Sikhism, Buddhism variations)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on five major religions (Islam, Christianity, Judaism, Buddhism, Hinduism) but acknowledges the need to extend the dataset to a larger pool of classes.
- Why unresolved: The current dataset only covers a limited set of religious groups, and there is a lack of standardized benchmark datasets for assessing bias against other religious minorities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Expanding the MMBias dataset to include images and phrases related to additional religious groups and evaluating bias in vision-language models using the expanded dataset.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How effective are bias mitigation techniques in reducing intersectional biases that compound across multiple protected attributes (e.g., religion and nationality)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses debiasing methods that can be applied as a post-processing step, but it does not explore the effectiveness of these techniques on intersectional biases.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on bias mitigation for individual protected attributes but does not address the complexity of biases that arise from the intersection of multiple attributes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to evaluate the effectiveness of bias mitigation techniques on intersectional biases by creating and analyzing datasets that capture the intersection of multiple protected attributes.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do cultural and regional variations within nationality groups affect bias in vision-language models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the "Arab" category collectively refers to a number of Arab countries, each having other cultural groups, and suggests that more specific nationalities and cultural groups can be added in the future.
- Why unresolved: The current dataset treats nationality as a monolithic category without considering the cultural and regional variations within each nationality group.
- What evidence would resolve it: Expanding the dataset to include more specific nationalities and cultural groups within each nationality category and evaluating how these variations affect bias in vision-language models.

## Limitations

- The mutual information-based pruning mechanism assumes that low-MI dimensions are truly redundant, which may not hold uniformly across different model architectures or datasets.
- The effectiveness of the Caliskan test adaptation for multimodal embeddings depends on the quality of cross-modal semantic alignment, which varies significantly between CLIP and fusion-based models.
- The debiasing approach may not effectively address intersectional biases that compound across multiple protected attributes.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The observation that CLIP, ALBEF, and ViLT exhibit measurable stereotypical biases across religious, national, disability, and sexual orientation groups. The Caliskan test application for quantifying these biases is well-established and the results showing bias favoring Christianity/Buddhism over Islam/Judaism are consistent with known societal patterns.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that removing N dimensions with low mutual information to classification labels reduces bias without harming accuracy. While the methodology is sound, the optimal value of N and its relationship to bias reduction efficiency requires more systematic exploration across different models and subgroups.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that fusion-based models can be evaluated using ITM probabilities as a proxy for bias. The paper acknowledges this is a weaker signal than cross-modal embeddings, and the reliability of ITM-based bias measurement needs more rigorous validation.

## Next Checks

1. **Dimensionality-Accuracy Tradeoff Analysis**: Systematically vary N (number of dimensions removed) and plot both bias reduction and accuracy degradation curves to identify the optimal pruning threshold. This would validate whether the assumed relationship between redundancy and bias holds across the full range of pruning levels.

2. **Cross-Modal Semantic Alignment Test**: Evaluate how well the Caliskan test captures bias in fusion-based models by comparing ITM-based bias scores with human judgments or alternative bias metrics. This would validate whether the proposed workaround for fusion models provides meaningful bias quantification.

3. **Generalization to Unseen Groups**: Apply the debiasing algorithm to groups not in the MMBias dataset (e.g., other religions, nationalities) and measure whether bias reduction generalizes beyond the training set. This would test whether the pruning mechanism removes bias-specific features rather than just dataset-specific patterns.
