# When does the student surpass the teacher? Federated Semi-supervised Learning with Teacher-Student EMA

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of integrating semi-supervised learning with federated learning, where data remains distributed across multiple devices with privacy constraints. The authors propose FedSwitch, a novel framework that dynamically switches between teacher and student models for pseudo-label generation based on KL-divergence, improving upon existing federated semi-supervised approaches. The method incorporates local teacher adaptation through EMA updates and demonstrates superior performance on benchmark datasets while maintaining privacy and statelessness.

## Method Summary
FedSwitch introduces a dynamic teacher-student switching mechanism that adapts to the quality of pseudo-labels generated during federated learning. The framework alternates between using teacher and student models for pseudo-label generation based on KL-divergence between their outputs, allowing the system to leverage the most reliable predictions at each step. Local teacher models are updated through exponential moving average (EMA) updates, ensuring that knowledge is progressively refined across clients. The approach maintains privacy by design, as it requires no raw data exchange between clients and the central server, and operates in a stateless manner suitable for edge computing environments.

## Key Results
- FedSwitch achieves state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10 and Fashion MNIST datasets
- Outperforms FedMatch and FedRGD baselines in classification accuracy
- Demonstrates robustness to increasing levels of client non-IIDness
- Maintains minimal communication overhead while improving generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of FedSwitch stems from its adaptive approach to pseudo-label quality assessment. By monitoring the KL-divergence between teacher and student model outputs, the system can dynamically determine which model is more reliable for generating pseudo-labels at any given training step. This prevents the propagation of poor-quality pseudo-labels that could degrade model performance. The EMA-based teacher updates ensure smooth knowledge transfer across clients while preventing abrupt model shifts. The stateless design and minimal communication requirements make the approach practical for real-world federated learning scenarios with bandwidth and energy constraints.

## Foundational Learning
**Federated Learning**: Distributed training where multiple clients collaborate without sharing raw data - needed to understand the privacy-preserving context and communication constraints
**Semi-supervised Learning**: Learning from both labeled and unlabeled data - crucial for leveraging the unlabeled data available on client devices
**Exponential Moving Average (EMA)**: Technique for smoothing model updates over time - provides stability in teacher model updates across heterogeneous clients
**KL-divergence**: Measure of difference between probability distributions - enables quality assessment of pseudo-labels to guide teacher-student switching
**Non-IID Data**: Data distribution heterogeneity across clients - important for understanding the robustness requirements in federated settings

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Client devices (data + local models) -> Server (aggregation + coordination) -> Client devices (model update)

**Critical Path**: Data → Local model training → KL-divergence computation → Teacher-student switching decision → EMA teacher update → Aggregation → Next round

**Design Tradeoffs**: Dynamic switching versus static teacher selection; EMA smoothing versus immediate adaptation; privacy preservation versus model accuracy

**Failure Signatures**: Poor pseudo-label quality leading to model degradation; communication bottlenecks slowing convergence; non-IID data causing client drift

**First Experiments**: 1) Ablation study on teacher-student switching strategy, 2) Communication efficiency comparison under varying network conditions, 3) Performance analysis across different levels of data heterogeneity

## Limitations
- Performance generalization beyond CIFAR-10 and Fashion MNIST datasets is uncertain
- Limited comparison with newer federated semi-supervised methods reduces confidence in "state-of-the-art" claims
- Insufficient evaluation of communication costs and energy consumption on edge devices
- Mechanisms of dynamic switching adaptation to extreme data heterogeneity are not fully explained

## Confidence
- Performance improvements on tested datasets: High
- Generalization to other domains: Low
- Robustness to client non-IIDness: Medium
- Communication efficiency claims: Low
