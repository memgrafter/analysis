# GPT-4 to GPT-3.5: 'Hold My Scalpel' -- A Look at the Competency of OpenAI's GPT on the Plastic Surgery In-Service Training Exam

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01503
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01503
- Reference count: 0
- GPT-4 achieved 75-78% accuracy on PSITE exams, placing it in the 88th-99th percentile among plastic surgery residents

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates OpenAI's GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 models on the Plastic Surgery In-Service Training Exam (PSITE), a standardized assessment for plastic surgery residents. Using authentic PSITE questions from 2021 and 2022 exams, the authors demonstrate that GPT-4 significantly outperforms GPT-3.5, improving scores from 54% to 75% (2022) and from 51% to 78% (2021). These gains translate to percentile improvements from 8th to 88th percentile (2022) and 3rd to 99th percentile (2021). The results suggest GPT-4 has achieved superhuman performance on plastic surgery knowledge assessment, particularly with its multimodal capabilities.

## Method Summary
The evaluation used authentic PSITE exam questions (250 per year) with a few-shot learning approach using 5 example questions per prompt. GPT-3.5 was evaluated via OpenAI API with specific temperature and sampling parameters, while GPT-4 was evaluated using ChatGPT interface due to API access limitations. Answers were extracted using secondary prompts and compared to exam keys. The study used MELD methodology to check for potential training data leakage but found no questions exceeded the 0.95 threshold. Percentile scores were calculated using ASPS norm tables.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieved 75% accuracy on 2022 PSITE vs 54% for GPT-3.5
- GPT-4 achieved 78% accuracy on 2021 PSITE vs 51% for GPT-3.5
- GPT-4 performance placed it in the 88th-99th percentile among plastic surgery residents
- GPT-4 demonstrated passing scores on both exams (75-78% correct)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
GPT-4's dramatic performance improvement over GPT-3.5 reflects genuine knowledge acquisition rather than memorization. The 21-27 percentage point improvement (from 3rd-8th to 88th-99th percentile) suggests substantive capability gains. Break condition: If MELD analysis shows training data leakage or performance drops on novel exams.

### Mechanism 2
GPT-4's multimodal processing contributes to superior performance on clinical vignettes. While evaluated text-only, the exam contains visual elements (14-16%), and multimodal capabilities would provide additional gains. Break condition: If multimodal performance doesn't exceed text-only on visual questions.

### Mechanism 3
High scores reflect genuine understanding of plastic surgery concepts rather than pattern matching. GPT-4 achieves passing scores on comprehensive exams that correlate with board certification success. Break condition: If GPT-4 fails on novel clinical scenarios or cannot generalize beyond memorized patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Would multimodal GPT-4 achieve superhuman performance on the 2023 PSITE exam, surpassing the 99th percentile threshold? The paper explicitly states multimodal input "may achieve superhuman performance" but lacked API access for evaluation. Evidence needed: Complete evaluation of GPT-4 with visual input on 2023 exam.

### Open Question 2
Does MELD effectively detect training data leakage for PSITE questions, and what's the optimal threshold for medical content? The study found no questions exceeded 0.95 threshold but notes uncertainty about threshold applicability. Evidence needed: Systematic testing of MELD across different thresholds with medical questions.

### Open Question 3
How does GPT-4 performance compare to actual residents when accounting for multimodal visual questions? Current evaluation only includes text-only questions. Evidence needed: Head-to-head evaluation of GPT-4 (with visual input) versus actual residents on complete PSITE exams.

## Limitations

- Proprietary exam materials limit independent verification and introduce potential sampling bias
- GPT-4 evaluated via ChatGPT interface rather than API, creating parameter control inconsistencies
- Performance near ceiling makes it difficult to assess true knowledge boundaries
- Visual questions (13-16%) not evaluated despite multimodal claims

## Confidence

- High Confidence: GPT-4 significantly outperforms GPT-3.5 on PSITE questions (75% vs 54% correct)
- Medium Confidence: GPT-4's percentile ranking among residents is accurate relative to norm tables
- Low Confidence: Claims about "superhuman" performance and multimodal potential are speculative
