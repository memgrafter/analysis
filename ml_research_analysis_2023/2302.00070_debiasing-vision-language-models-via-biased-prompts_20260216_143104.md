# Debiasing Vision-Language Models via Biased Prompts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.00070
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00070
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: Effective debiasing of vision-language models without additional data or training

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to debiasing vision-language models by projecting out biased directions in text embeddings using calibrated prompts. The method constructs a projection matrix to remove biased directions defined by specific prompts, then calibrates this matrix using a loss function that ensures semantic consistency between paired prompts. The approach demonstrates effectiveness in reducing social bias and spurious correlations in both discriminative models (like zero-shot classifiers) and generative models (like text-to-image systems) without requiring retraining or additional data.

## Method Summary
The method involves defining biased directions using prompts (e.g., gender-related terms), constructing a projection matrix to remove these directions from text embeddings, and calibrating the matrix using a loss function that minimizes discrepancies between semantically equivalent prompt pairs. The calibration ensures that the debiasing process doesn't distort the underlying semantic meaning. The approach leverages a closed-form solution for the calibration matrix, making it computationally efficient and easily integrable into existing pipelines. The method is applied to both discriminative vision-language models (like CLIP) and generative models (like Stable Diffusion) to improve group robustness and reduce biased image generation.

## Key Results
- Effective reduction of social bias and spurious correlation in both discriminative and generative vision-language models
- Improved group robustness in zero-shot classifiers without requiring additional data or training
- Demonstrated reduction of biases in text-to-image generation, particularly for gender and profession associations

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Debiasing via projection matrices removes biased directions from text embeddings without requiring retraining or additional data.
- Mechanism: The approach defines biased directions using prompts (e.g., "a photo of a male/female"), constructs a projection matrix to remove these directions, and calibrates the matrix using a loss function that minimizes discrepancies between embeddings of paired prompts (e.g., "a photo of a male doctor" vs "a photo of a female doctor").
- Core assumption: The projection matrix can effectively remove the biased directions without significantly affecting the useful semantic information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a general approach for debiasing vision-language foundation models by projecting out biased directions in the text embedding."
  - [section] "To improve the estimation of the projection matrix, we leverage positive pairs of prompts that are expected to have the same semantic meaning after projection."
- Break condition: If the biased directions are not well-defined or the prompts do not accurately capture the biases, the projection matrix may not effectively remove the biases.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Calibration using positive pairs of prompts ensures that the debiased embeddings maintain semantic consistency.
- Mechanism: The calibration loss encourages the projected embeddings of paired prompts (e.g., "a photo of a male doctor" and "a photo of a female doctor") to be similar, ensuring that the debiasing process does not distort the semantic meaning of the text.
- Core assumption: The positive pairs accurately represent the same semantic meaning, and the calibration loss can effectively enforce this consistency.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "To address this issue, we propose a calibration loss that minimizes the discrepancy of a pair of prompt embeddings."
  - [section] "The loss encourages the linear projection P to be invariant to the difference between (i,j), i.e., the spurious attributes."
- Break condition: If the positive pairs are not well-chosen or do not accurately represent the same semantic meaning, the calibration process may not be effective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The closed-form solution for the calibration matrix enables efficient integration into large-scale pipelines.
- Mechanism: The optimization problem for the calibration matrix has a closed-form solution, which can be computed without requiring iterative training or access to downstream datasets.
- Core assumption: The closed-form solution is computationally efficient and can be easily integrated into existing pipelines.
