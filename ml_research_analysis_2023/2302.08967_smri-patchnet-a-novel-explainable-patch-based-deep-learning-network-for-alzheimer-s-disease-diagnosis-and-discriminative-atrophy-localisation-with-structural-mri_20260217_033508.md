# sMRI-PatchNet: A novel explainable patch-based deep learning network for Alzheimer's disease diagnosis and discriminative atrophy localisation with Structural MRI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08967
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08967
- Reference count: 40
- Achieves 0.920 accuracy for AD vs NC classification and 0.819 for MCI conversion prediction using patch-based deep learning with SHAP-based explainability

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces sMRI-PatchNet, a novel patch-based deep learning architecture for Alzheimer's disease diagnosis using structural MRI. The method combines a fast recursive partition perturbation approach for SHAP coefficient computation with position embeddings to identify and classify disease-relevant brain regions while maintaining spatial information. The approach demonstrates superior performance in diagnostic accuracy, generalizability, and computational efficiency compared to existing methods, while providing interpretable localization of pathological brain regions.

## Method Summary
The method employs a patch-based deep learning framework that uses a fast recursive partition perturbation technique to compute SHAP coefficients for identifying the most discriminative AD-related patches from structural MRI scans. These selected patches are then processed through a network that extracts features and classifies AD status while retaining spatial information through position embeddings. The recursive approach significantly reduces computational complexity compared to traditional SHAP computation methods, making the explainability aspect more practical for large-scale neuroimaging studies.

## Key Results
- Achieved 0.920 accuracy for Alzheimer's disease vs normal control classification
- Attained 0.819 accuracy for MCI conversion prediction
- Successfully identified discriminative pathological locations that align with known AD-related brain regions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method's effectiveness stems from its hierarchical approach: recursive partition perturbation efficiently identifies the most informative patches for classification, while position embeddings preserve spatial relationships critical for understanding disease progression patterns. This combination allows the network to focus computational resources on relevant regions while maintaining anatomical context, leading to improved diagnostic performance and meaningful localization of atrophy patterns.

## Foundational Learning
- SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) coefficients: Explainable AI method for quantifying feature importance; needed for interpretable patch selection; quick check: verify feature attribution consistency across samples
- Recursive partition perturbation: Efficient approximation method for SHAP computation; reduces computational burden from O(2^n) to manageable complexity; quick check: compare runtime with baseline SHAP implementation
- Position embeddings: Technique to encode spatial coordinates in neural networks; preserves anatomical relationships between patches; quick check: verify spatial consistency of predictions

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Raw MRI -> Recursive SHAP computation -> Patch selection -> Feature extraction -> Position embedding -> Classification

**Critical Path:** The sequence from recursive SHAP computation through patch selection to the final classification module represents the critical path, as each step directly impacts the discriminative power and explainability of the final predictions.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The patch-based approach trades global context for computational efficiency and interpretability, while the recursive SHAP method sacrifices some approximation accuracy for significant runtime improvements. Position embeddings add minimal overhead while providing crucial spatial information.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor patch selection quality would manifest as low SHAP coefficient consistency across similar samples; incorrect position embeddings would show as anatomically implausible localization patterns; classification failures would appear as degraded accuracy on known pathology regions.
