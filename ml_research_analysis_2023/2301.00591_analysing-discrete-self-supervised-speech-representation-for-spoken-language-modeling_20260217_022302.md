# Analysing Discrete Self Supervised Speech Representation for Spoken Language Modeling

## Executive Summary
This work presents a comprehensive analysis of discrete self-supervised speech representations through the lens of Generative Spoken Language Modeling (GSLM). The authors quantitatively and visually analyze HuBERT and CPC-based representations with respect to phoneme classes, gender, and speaker identity. They introduce an unsupervised metric to measure redundancies in k-means clustering and propose three methods to improve clustering quality. The proposed methods - double k-means, k-means with hierarchical clustering, and k-means with weighted hierarchical clustering - show significant improvements in zero-resource speech metrics such as ABX and speaker information, with up to 18% relative improvement in ABX across score.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze discrete speech representations obtained by clustering outputs from self-supervised models (HuBERT and CPC) using k-means. They evaluate these representations using V-Measure to quantify correlation with phonemes, speaker, and gender attributes. To address redundancies in the discrete representations, they introduce a novel Circular Resynthesis metric and propose three clustering improvement methods: double k-means, k-means with hierarchical clustering, and k-means with weighted hierarchical clustering. The methods are evaluated using ABX within/across speaker metrics, speaker probing, and resynthesis quality measures.

## Key Results
- High correlation between speech units and phonemes, with weaker correlation to speaker or gender information
- Units' context contributes to redundancies in the discrete representation
- Proposed clustering improvements achieve up to 18% relative improvement in ABX across score
- Double k-means, hierarchical clustering, and weighted hierarchical clustering methods significantly improve both ABX and speaker probing results

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: High correlation between speech units and phonemes, with weaker correlation to speaker or gender information
- Mechanism: The self-supervised speech representations (HuBERT/CPC) capture phonetic content effectively, but do not encode speaker-specific or gender-specific information as strongly
- Core assumption: The clustering process in GSLM preserves phonetic information while suppressing speaker/gender identity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our analysis finds a high correlation between the speech units to phonemes and phoneme families, while their correlation with speaker or gender is weaker"
  - [section 4.2]: "The V-Measure for the speaker and gender scores is lower than the score of the phonemes- which indicates of high correlation to the phonemes and a low correlation to the speaker or gender"

- Break condition: If clustering is modified to preserve speaker/gender information, the phonetic correlation may decrease

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Units' context contributes to redundancies in the discrete representation
- Mechanism: The same phoneme in different contexts is represented by different units, leading to unnecessary duplication in the discrete space
- Core assumption: Contextual information in speech (e.g., surrounding phonemes) affects the continuous representation before quantization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Following this analysis, we propose a new, unsupervised metric to measure unit redundancies"
  - [section 4.3]: "We can see that the context of the units critically affects the results, while the unit's length has a lower effect"

- Break condition: If the continuous representation is made context-independent before quantization, redundancies may reduce

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The proposed clustering improvements (double k-means, hierarchical clustering, weighted hierarchical clustering) effectively reduce redundancies and improve downstream metrics
- Mechanism: By iteratively merging clusters or weighting by a redundancy metric, the methods create more robust and compact discrete representations
- Core assumption: The initial k-means clustering creates redundant units that can be merged without significant loss of phonetic information
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Finally, we use this metric to develop new methods that improve the robustness of units clustering and show significant improvement considering zero-resource speech metrics such as ABX"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: SSL Model (HuBERT/CPC) -> k-means clustering -> Discrete Units -> ULM -> UTS
- Critical path: STU → ULM → UTS (for generation), with analysis pipeline running in parallel for evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of clusters (k): Higher k may capture more phonetic detail but increases redundancy
  - Context window size: Larger context improves phonetic accuracy but increases computational cost
  - Clustering method: Simple k-means is fast but may have redundancies; hierarchical methods are more robust but slower
- Failure signatures:
  - High ABX scores across speakers: Indicates poor phonetic representation
  - High V-Measure for speaker/gender: Indicates speaker information leakage
  - Poor Circular Resynthesis score: Indicates high redundancy in discrete units

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the exact relationship between unit context and the observed redundancies in discrete speech representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "we found redundancies in the units, which the units' context can explain" and shows that context significantly affects resynthesis results
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that context affects unit representation through resynthesis experiments, it doesn't provide a mechanistic explanation for why context creates redundancies or quantify the specific contribution of context versus other factors
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed ablation studies varying context length and structure, combined with quantitative analysis showing the exact contribution of context to redundancy compared to other factors like phonetic similarity or speaker variation

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the proposed clustering improvement methods affect downstream language modeling performance compared to standard k-means?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates clustering improvements using ABX and speaker information metrics but doesn't test actual language model performance
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on clustering quality metrics but doesn't demonstrate whether these improvements translate to better language modeling capabilities like coherence, diversity, or task-specific performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Language modeling benchmarks comparing unit representations obtained through standard k-means versus the proposed methods on tasks like text generation, speech synthesis quality, or downstream speech processing tasks

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are there optimal numbers of discrete units for different speech processing tasks, or does task specificity require different unit granularities?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper varies unit numbers (50, 100, 200, 2000) and shows different V-Measure scores for phonemes, speakers, and gender, suggesting task-dependent optimal configurations
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that unit number affects correlation with different attributes, it doesn't systematically explore whether there are optimal unit counts for specific tasks or if a single configuration can serve multiple purposes effectively
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments mapping unit numbers to performance across multiple speech processing tasks, identifying whether Pareto-optimal configurations exist or if task-specific tuning is necessary

## Limitations
- The correlation analysis relies on V-Measure which may not fully capture nuanced relationships between units and attributes
- The study uses a fixed number of clusters (2000) without systematic exploration of optimal cluster counts
- The Circular Resynthesis metric for measuring redundancy is novel and needs validation against alternative measures

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: The findings that HuBERT and CPC representations show high correlation with phonemes but weaker correlation with speaker/gender information are well-supported by the V-Measure analysis and visualization results. The proposed clustering improvement methods demonstrate consistent improvements across multiple metrics (ABX, speaker probing) with statistically significant results.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that unit context contributes to redundancies is supported by the Circular Resynthesis metric, but the mechanism by which context creates redundancies is not fully explored. The effectiveness of the clustering improvement methods, while demonstrated, may depend on specific implementation details and hyperparameters that are not fully specified.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that the Circular Resynthesis metric accurately measures redundancy in discrete representations is based on a novel method that has not been validated against alternative redundancy measures or through ablation studies.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Model Validation**: Repeat the correlation analysis (V-Measure) and clustering improvement experiments with additional SSL models (e.g., Wav2Vec 2.0, APC) to verify that the findings are not model-specific.

2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically vary the number of clusters (k) and context window sizes to determine the optimal configuration for each clustering improvement method and SSL model combination.

3. **Alternative Redundancy Metrics**: Compare the Circular Resynthesis metric with alternative measures of redundancy (e.g., entropy-based metrics, mutual information between adjacent units) to validate its effectiveness in capturing unit-level redundancies.
