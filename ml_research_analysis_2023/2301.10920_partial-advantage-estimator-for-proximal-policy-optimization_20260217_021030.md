# Partial advantage estimator for proximal policy optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10920
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10920
- Reference count: 5
- The paper introduces Partial GAE to reduce bias in truncated advantage estimates while maintaining computational efficiency in PPO training

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in policy gradient methods: the bias-variance trade-off in value estimation when using truncated Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) with incomplete trajectories. The authors propose Partial GAE, which selectively discards the latter portion of advantage estimates where bias is highest, using only the first ϵ steps from each trajectory during training updates. The method aims to reduce estimation bias while preserving computational efficiency and was evaluated across MuJoCo continuous control tasks and the µRTS game environment, showing consistent performance improvements over standard PPO with truncated GAE.

## Method Summary
The Partial GAE method modifies the standard truncated GAE calculation by discarding the latter portion of advantage estimates from each trajectory during training. Specifically, for each truncated trajectory, only the first ϵ steps of the advantage estimates are used in the policy update, while the remaining steps are ignored. This selective truncation reduces the bias introduced by incomplete future information while maintaining the computational efficiency of truncated GAE. The approach is integrated into the standard PPO framework with minimal modifications to the existing training pipeline, requiring only changes to how advantage estimates are computed and applied during the policy update phase.

## Key Results
- Partial GAE consistently outperformed standard PPO with truncated GAE across MuJoCo environments
- Optimal performance achieved at intermediate sampling length T ∈ [64,128] and partial coefficient ϵ ∈ [384,512] for Ant-v3
- Method demonstrated effectiveness in both dense reward (MuJoCo) and sparse reward (µRTS) environments
- Performance improvements were robust across different task complexities and reward structures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Partial GAE works by reducing the bias inherent in truncated advantage estimates while maintaining computational efficiency. When trajectories are truncated, standard GAE suffers from bias because future rewards beyond the truncation point are unknown. By discarding the latter portion of advantage estimates where this bias is most pronounced (typically the last steps of each truncated trajectory), Partial GAE focuses training on more reliable estimates. This selective truncation preserves the low-variance properties of GAE while mitigating the bias that accumulates in incomplete trajectories, leading to more stable and effective policy updates during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
PPO Trainer -> Advantage Calculator -> Partial GAE Truncation -> Policy Update

**Critical Path**
1. Environment interaction generates trajectories
2. Value function estimates computed for each timestep
3. Standard GAE advantage calculation performed
4. Partial GAE truncation applied (first ϵ steps retained)
5. Policy update uses truncated advantages
6. Repeat training loop

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Computation: Additional overhead from partial truncation vs. improved sample efficiency
- Implementation complexity: Minimal changes to existing PPO codebase
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Requires tuning of T and ϵ parameters

**Failure Signatures**
- Over-truncation (small ϵ) leads to high variance and unstable learning
- Under-truncation (large ϵ) fails to reduce bias effectively
- Poor choice of T affects both bias and variance characteristics

**First 3 Experiments**
1. Compare learning curves of Partial GAE vs standard GAE with varying ϵ values
2. Measure bias and variance of advantage estimates at different truncation points
3. Test sensitivity to sampling length T across multiple environments

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions regarding the scalability and generalizability of Partial GAE. Key questions include how the method performs in truly sparse reward environments beyond the tested µRTS domain, whether the hyperparameter sensitivity to T and ϵ values can be reduced through adaptive mechanisms, and how Partial GAE might be extended to other policy gradient algorithms beyond PPO. The authors also suggest investigating the impact on exploration behavior when discarding portion of advantage estimates, and whether the method could benefit from combining with other variance reduction techniques.

## Limitations
- Performance highly sensitive to hyperparameter tuning of T and ϵ values
- Computational efficiency gains not quantified relative to standard GAE
- Limited evaluation scope focused on MuJoCo and one complex game environment
- No analysis of impact on exploration or learning stability when discarding advantage estimates

## Confidence
