# Implicit Regularization for Group Sparsity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12540
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12540
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a novel neural reparameterization for inducing group sparsity via gradient descent without explicit regularization.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a neural reparameterization approach called diagonally grouped linear neural network (DGLNN) that induces group sparsity through implicit regularization. By reparameterizing a linear predictor into magnitude-direction form within each group, the authors show that gradient descent dynamics naturally promote group sparsity without explicit regularization terms. The method provides theoretical guarantees under orthogonal design and demonstrates improved sample complexity compared to existing diagonal linear networks.

## Method Summary
The proposed DGLNN reparameterizes a linear predictor into magnitude-direction form within each group, creating a structure where gradient descent implicitly regularizes towards group sparsity. Unlike explicit regularization approaches, this method modifies the parameterization itself to encode the desired inductive bias. The authors prove that gradient flow on DGLNN cannot be simulated by mirror descent, requiring new analysis techniques. They analyze gradient dynamics with early stopping and weight normalization, showing implicit regularization towards group sparsity. Under orthogonal design with noisy observations, the method achieves minimax-optimal error rates.

## Key Results
- DGLNN achieves implicit group sparsity regularization without explicit regularization terms
- Under orthogonal design, the method achieves minimax-optimal error rates
- DGLNN shows improved sample complexity compared to diagonal linear networks
- In the degenerate case of size-one groups, the approach provides a new algorithm for sparse linear regression

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on the specific neural reparameterization that creates an implicit bias towards group sparsity through gradient dynamics. By structuring the parameterization into magnitude-direction components within groups, the optimization landscape naturally encourages sparsity at the group level without explicit penalties.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions, including extending the theoretical analysis beyond orthogonal design, understanding the interaction between early stopping and implicit regularization, and exploring the method's effectiveness on real-world datasets with complex feature correlations.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis relies on gradient flow and early stopping assumptions that may not hold in practical SGD implementations
- Orthogonal design assumption is restrictive and may not generalize to correlated feature settings
- Proof that DGLNN cannot be simulated by mirror descent has limited practical implications
- Lack of extensive empirical validation on real-world datasets
