# Making Models Shallow Again: Jointly Learning to Reduce Non-Linearity and Depth for Latency-Efficient Private Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13274
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13274
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Achieves up to 1.73x reduction in ReLU operations and 1.6x reduction in layer depth with minimal accuracy loss for private inference

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of reducing ReLU operations and model depth in deep neural networks to improve latency and compute efficiency for private inference. The authors propose a novel method called gated branching (GB) that leverages ReLU sensitivity to systematically remove ReLU layers and merge adjacent convolution layers, creating shallower blocks. Additionally, they introduce an auxiliary knowledge distillation (AKD) technique to further reduce depth at later layers by placing an auxiliary classifier at a shallower depth. The proposed method is evaluated on ResNet18 and WRN-22-8 models using CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, demonstrating significant reductions in both ReLUs and depth with minimal accuracy loss.

## Method Summary
The method jointly reduces ReLU operations and model depth through two complementary techniques: gated branching and auxiliary knowledge distillation. Gated branching systematically replaces convolutional blocks with high ReLU counts with shallower blocks by gradually introducing them via a gating hyperparameter γg. The auxiliary knowledge distillation technique places an auxiliary classifier at a shallower depth and distills knowledge from a baseline all-ReLU model to reduce overall depth while maintaining accuracy. The approach is evaluated on ResNet18 and WRN-22-8 models trained on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 1.73× reduction in ReLU operations compared to state-of-the-art methods
- Achieves up to 1.6× reduction in layer depth while maintaining accuracy
- Demonstrates 1.47× reduction in MAC operations compared to existing ReLU reduction methods
- Shows minimal accuracy loss (within 0.5%) across different ReLU budgets (25k, 49.5k, 100k)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Gated Branching for Depth Reduction
- Claim: Shallow blocks can replace deeper ReLU-containing blocks without significant accuracy loss when introduced gradually via a gating hyperparameter
- Mechanism: The gated branching method assigns a gating hyperparameter γg to shallow blocks that replaces deeper blocks with ReLU layers. By gradually increasing γg from 0 to 1 during training, the model can transition from the original deep structure to the shallower structure while maintaining performance
- Core assumption: Earlier layers in neural networks have lower ReLU sensitivity and thus can be replaced with shallower blocks without major impact on classification performance
- Evidence anchors: [abstract]: "we systematically replace every convolutional block...having a low ReLU budget...with a shallower block having no non-linearity module"; [section]: "Unlike existing ReLU reduction methods, our joint reduction method can yield models with improved reduction of both ReLUs and linear operations by up to 1.73× and 1.47×, respectively"

### Mechanism 2: Auxiliary Knowledge Distillation (AKD) for Later Layer Reduction
- Claim: An auxiliary classifier placed at a shallower depth can reduce overall model depth while maintaining accuracy through knowledge distillation from a baseline model
- Mechanism: The AKD technique places an auxiliary classifier before the final convolutional layer and distills knowledge from an all-ReLU baseline model to this auxiliary classifier. This allows the model to make predictions at multiple depths while maintaining accuracy
- Core assumption: Later non-linearity layers play a critical role in retaining classification performance, but their depth can be reduced by introducing an auxiliary classifier that learns from a baseline model
- Evidence anchors: [abstract]: "we introduce an auxiliary knowledge distillation (AKD) technique to further reduce depth at later layers by placing an auxiliary classifier at a shallower depth"; [section]: "To reduce depth at the later layers, we present an auxiliary classifier (AC) branch located after a shallow depth of the DNN"

### Mechanism 3: ReLU Sensitivity Thresholding
- Claim: ReLU layers with sensitivity below a threshold can be removed without significant accuracy loss, allowing for joint reduction of both ReLUs and MAC operations
- Mechanism: The method computes ReLU sensitivity for each layer and removes those below a threshold (dth), then merges the preceding and succeeding convolution layers into a single layer, reducing both ReLU count and linear operations
- Core assumption: High ReLU sensitivity correlates with non-linearity importance, so low-sensitivity layers can be removed safely
- Evidence anchors: [abstract]: "we systematically replace every convolutional block...having a low ReLU budget...with a shallower block"; [section]: "we present a model architecture optimization framework that simultaneously learns to reduce both the ReLUs and the MACs of a model"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Main model (ΦS) with gated branching capability -> Auxiliary classifier branch (AC) for depth reduction -> All-ReLU baseline model (ΦT) for knowledge distillation -> Gating hyperparameter γg for gradual shallow block introduction -> ReLU sensitivity threshold (dth) for block replacement decisions

- Critical path: Model initialization -> Stage 1 (ReLU sensitivity computation) -> Stage 2 (mask evaluation) -> Stage 3 (fine-tuning with GB and AKD) -> Model evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - ReLU reduction vs. accuracy: More aggressive ReLU removal yields better latency but may sacrifice accuracy
  - Depth reduction vs. model complexity: Shallower models are more efficient but may have reduced capacity
  - Gradual vs. immediate shallow block introduction: Gradual introduction (GB) maintains accuracy better but requires more training epochs

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy drops significantly when ReLU sensitivity thresholding is too aggressive
  - Training instability when γg increases too rapidly
  - Knowledge distillation fails when baseline model accuracy is much lower than target model

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate ReLU sensitivity distribution across layers for ResNet18 on CIFAR-100 with different ReLU budgets (25k, 49.5k, 100k) to validate the core assumption about earlier layers being less important
  2. Test gated branching with different γg increment functions (cosine vs. linear) to determine optimal gradual introduction strategy
  3. Compare models with only GB, only AKD, and joint GB+AKD to quantify the contribution of each component to overall performance improvement

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation of ReLU sensitivity distribution assumptions across different architectures
- Unclear generalizability to larger models beyond ResNet18 and WRN-22-8
- No specified quality threshold for acceptable baseline models in knowledge distillation

## Confidence
- Gated Branching Effectiveness (Medium): The claim that systematic ReLU removal via gated branching can achieve 1.73x reduction with minimal accuracy loss is supported by experimental results but relies on assumptions about ReLU sensitivity distribution
- Auxiliary Knowledge Distillation (Low-Medium): The AKD technique's contribution to depth reduction is demonstrated but lacks ablation studies showing its isolated impact versus GB alone
- Joint Reduction Benefits (High): The combined approach's superiority over individual ReLU reduction or depth reduction methods is well-supported by comparative results
