# Plan To Predict: Learning an Uncertainty-Foreseeing Model for Model-Based Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08502
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08502
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: P2P outperforms state-of-the-art MBRL methods on Hopper, Humanoid, and Ant environments

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental challenge of model bias in model-based reinforcement learning, where inaccurate models can lead to poor policy learning. The authors propose Plan To Predict (P2P), a novel framework that treats the model rollout process as a sequential decision making problem. By allowing the model to actively adapt to the current policy and minimize multi-step prediction errors, P2P enables the model to foresee and avoid regions of high uncertainty. The approach provides theoretical guarantees showing that P2P approximately optimizes a lower bound of the true environment return, offering tighter error bounds compared to prior work.

## Method Summary
The P2P framework reformulates model learning as a sequential decision problem where the model predicts the next state conditioned on the current state and the current policy. Instead of training the model to minimize single-step prediction errors, P2P considers the long-term effects of immediate predictions across multiple steps. The model is trained using policy-aware rollouts where it can "choose" intermediate predictions that minimize cumulative prediction error along trajectories induced by the current policy. This active prediction approach allows the model to identify and avoid uncertain regions early in the prediction process, effectively reducing the propagation of errors through longer trajectories.

## Key Results
- P2P consistently outperforms state-of-the-art MBRL methods on challenging benchmark tasks including Hopper, Humanoid, and Ant environments
- Accumulated model error analysis demonstrates P2P's effectiveness in reducing prediction errors along policy-induced trajectories
- Theoretical analysis shows P2P provides a tighter error bound compared to prior approaches by approximately optimizing a lower bound of true environment return

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
P2P works by fundamentally changing how the model learns from a static prediction problem to an active sequential decision problem. By considering the policy context during model training, P2P enables the model to identify regions where uncertainty will compound over multiple steps and adjust its predictions accordingly. The framework effectively creates a feedback loop where the model learns to "see ahead" and avoid making predictions that would lead to high uncertainty down the trajectory, similar to how a good planner would avoid uncertain paths when making long-term plans.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Policy -> P2P Model -> Environment Simulator -> Return Calculator -> Policy Update

**Critical path**: During training, the policy generates trajectories which are used by P2P to train the model through sequential decision making on model predictions. The updated model then generates more accurate rollouts for policy improvement.

**Design tradeoffs**: P2P trades increased computational complexity for better long-term prediction accuracy and tighter theoretical bounds. The sequential decision making approach requires more computation per prediction but results in better policy learning outcomes.

**Failure signatures**: The model may fail to identify high-uncertainty regions if the policy changes dramatically, or if the state space has regions of inherent unpredictability that cannot be avoided.

## Limitations
- P2P's effectiveness relies on having access to a reasonable policy during training, limiting applicability in truly exploratory settings
- The sequential decision-making approach may introduce additional computational overhead compared to simpler model learning methods
- Empirical evaluation is limited to continuous control tasks, leaving uncertainty about performance in discrete or high-dimensional state spaces

## Confidence
**High confidence**: P2P's performance gains on standard benchmarks are well-supported by empirical results
**Medium confidence**: Theoretical guarantees show approximate rather than exact optimization of return bounds
**Medium confidence**: Empirical evidence supports uncertainty-foreseeing claims but doesn't conclusively demonstrate predictive uncertainty awareness across all scenarios
