# Strategies for improving low resource speech to text translation relying on pre-trained ASR models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00208
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00208
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Proposed model achieves 7.3 BLEU on Tamasheq-French low-resource ST, outperforming IWSLT 2022 by 1.6 points using multilingual ASR initialization and CTC auxiliary objectives

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates techniques for improving low-resource speech-to-text translation (ST) by leveraging pre-trained automatic speech recognition (ASR) models. The authors explore multilingual ASR initialization and the use of Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) as an auxiliary objective during training and decoding. Through experiments on both simulated and real low-resource setups using English-Portuguese and Tamasheq-French language pairs, they demonstrate that multilingual ASR models provide superior initialization for ST systems compared to monolingual models, even when trained on less total data. The addition of CTC as an auxiliary objective further improves translation quality by helping to reorder internal representations, achieving state-of-the-art results on the challenging Tamasheq-French dataset with only 300 hours of pre-training data.

## Method Summary
The approach involves training multilingual ASR models with joint CTC-attention objectives on 300 hours of speech data spanning six languages, then initializing ST encoder-decoder models from these pre-trained ASR weights. The ST models are fine-tuned on low-resource paired speech-translation data with CTC as an auxiliary objective, using weighted combinations of CTC and attention losses during both training and beam search decoding. The architecture employs transformer encoder-decoder components with 12-layer encoder and 6-layer decoder, 256-dimensional embeddings, 4 attention heads, and dropout rates of 0.1-0.3. Evaluation uses BLEU and CHR F2 metrics on low-resource datasets including the real Tamasheq-French corpus from IWSLT 2022.

## Key Results
- Multilingual ASR model (6L 300h) outperforms most French monolingual models trained on much larger data
- Using CTC as auxiliary objective during training and decoding improves translation quality
- Proposed model achieves 7.3 BLEU on Tamasheq-French, outperforming prior IWSLT 2022 works by 1.6 points
- Joint CTC-attention decoding provides additional benefits over pure attention decoding

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multilingual ASR models trained on multiple languages provide strong initialization for low-resource ST systems
- Mechanism: Parameter sharing across languages enables learning robust speech representations that transfer effectively to unseen target languages through cross-lingual patterns
- Core assumption: Representation space learned from multiple languages contains useful abstractions that generalize to new language pairs
- Evidence anchors: Model achieves 7.3 BLEU on Tamasheq-French, multilingual model outperforms monolingual models despite less target language data
- Break condition: Insufficient model capacity or languages too dissimilar for cross-lingual transfer

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: CTC as auxiliary objective during training and decoding helps reorder internal representations
- Mechanism: CTC enforces monotonic alignment as regularization during training; joint decoding combines monotonic and non-monotonic alignment patterns
- Core assumption: Combining CTC's monotonic constraint with attention's flexible alignment captures both local and global reordering patterns
- Evidence anchors: CTC auxiliary objective improves final translation quality
- Break condition: CTC objective overwhelms attention-based translation objective

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Initializing both encoder and decoder from same pre-trained ASR eliminates misalignment
- Mechanism: Shared pre-training ensures representations exist in same semantic space, avoiding cross-modal alignment learning during fine-tuning
- Core assumption: Representation misalignment between encoder and decoder is significant barrier in low-resource settings
- Evidence anchors: Misalignment problem doesn't arise with same pre-trained ASR initialization
- Break condition: ASR and translation task vocabulary requirements differ significantly

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Speech features → Conv2d layer → 12-layer transformer encoder → CTC layer (optional) → 6-layer transformer decoder → target text
- Critical path: Input speech features flow through convolutional layers for downsampling, then transformer encoder layers for contextual representation, followed by either CTC layer (ASR) or attention-based decoding (ST)
- Design tradeoffs: Multilingual vs monolingual ASR trades model capacity for cross-lingual generalization; CTC objective adds computational overhead but improves reordering; separate vocabularies increase model size but allow language-specific processing
- Failure signatures: Poor performance despite pre-training suggests distribution mismatch; high WER indicates feature extraction or capacity issues; BLEU plateauing suggests insufficient capacity or inappropriate hyperparameters
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train monolingual ASR on target language with varying data amounts to establish baseline
  2. Train multilingual ASR on 6 languages and compare against monolingual baselines
  3. Initialize ST model from pre-trained ASR with CTC as auxiliary objective, varying CTC weight α

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does effectiveness of multilingual ASR initialization vary across different low-resource language pairs and data sizes?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - multilingual ASR initialization performed better than monolingual in most settings, but performance varies by language pair and data size
- Why unresolved: Only tested two language pairs and simulated low-resource scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on wider range of low-resource language pairs with varying pre-training data amounts

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is optimal balance between CTC and attention objectives during training and decoding?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - found CTC auxiliary objective helpful but didn't determine optimal weights
- Why unresolved: Only tested few values for CTC weight α, didn't explore joint decoding weight β extensively
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic exploration of different CTC and attention objective weights for various low-resource scenarios

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does use of pre-trained multilingual ASR and MT models affect high-resource ST systems?
- Basis in paper: Inferred - focused on low-resource scenarios but mentioned benefit diminishes with very low fine-tuning data
- Why unresolved: Didn't conduct experiments on high-resource ST systems
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on high-resource ST systems comparing models with and without pre-trained model initialization

## Limitations

- Multilingual ASR initialization benefit not tested across diverse language families and data distributions
- CTC mechanism for improving reordering not definitively proven through ablation studies
- Representation alignment claims lack direct empirical evidence measuring alignment quality

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Empirical results showing multilingual ASR initialization improves low-resource ST performance are well-supported by quantitative comparisons and sound methodology.

**Medium confidence**: CTC auxiliary objective mechanism is plausible but requires additional investigation through controlled ablations to prove specific reordering effects.

**Low confidence**: Claims about shared initialization eliminating representation misalignment lack direct evidence measuring representation alignment.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct ablation study varying CTC weight α during training and β during decoding to quantify precise contribution of CTC regularization versus pure attention-based translation

2. Test cross-lingual generalization by training multilingual ASR on different language combinations and measuring ST performance on held-out target languages

3. Analyze representation alignment by computing similarity between encoder and decoder representations across random initialization, pre-trained encoder with random decoder, and full pre-trained initialization conditions
