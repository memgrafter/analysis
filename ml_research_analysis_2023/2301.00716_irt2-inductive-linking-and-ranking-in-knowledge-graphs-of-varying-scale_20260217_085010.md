# IRT2: Inductive Linking and Ranking in Knowledge Graphs of Varying Scale
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00716
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00716
- Reference count: 0
- This paper presents a benchmark for open-world knowledge graph completion that addresses the challenge of linking entities to a knowledge graph using noisy text data, particularly in scenarios with small or medium-sized graphs.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces IRT2, a benchmark for open-world knowledge graph completion, focusing on the task of linking entities from noisy text to a knowledge graph. The benchmark addresses the challenge of entity linking and ranking in scenarios where the knowledge graph is small or medium-sized. The authors propose two neural models—a joint training approach (JOINT) and a two-step approach (OWE)—and evaluate their performance on two tasks: ranking (discovering text contexts containing mentions of entities) and linking (predicting triples for discovered entities).

## Method Summary
The IRT2 benchmark is designed to address the challenge of linking entities from noisy text to a knowledge graph, especially in small and medium-scale settings. Two neural models are proposed: a joint training approach (JOINT) and a two-step approach (OWE) that first trains a knowledge graph completion model and then aligns text and graph representations. The models are evaluated on two tasks: ranking (discovering text contexts containing mentions of entities) and linking (predicting triples for discovered entities). The benchmark includes datasets of varying sizes (small, medium, large) to assess model performance across different scales of knowledge graphs.

## Key Results
- Both neural models significantly outperform a bag-of-words baseline on small and medium-sized graphs.
- The OWE approach generally performs better than the JOINT approach.
- For the linking task, the models achieve high precision, with hits@10 scores ranging from 53.82% to 75.39% depending on the dataset size and model configuration.
- For the ranking task, the neural models outperform the baseline but overall performance is not yet at a practical level.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The IRT2 benchmark works by providing a structured framework for evaluating entity linking and ranking in knowledge graphs of varying scales. The two neural models, JOINT and OWE, are designed to handle the challenges of noisy text data and small to medium-sized knowledge graphs. The OWE approach, which involves first training a knowledge graph completion model and then aligning text and graph representations, is particularly effective because it leverages pre-trained KG embeddings and focuses on alignment, which may be more robust in small-data scenarios. The benchmark's focus on small and medium-sized graphs fills a gap in existing open-world KG completion benchmarks, which typically focus on large-scale graphs.

## Foundational Learning
- **Knowledge Graph Completion (KGC)**: The task of predicting missing triples in a knowledge graph. *Why needed:* KGC is fundamental for expanding and maintaining knowledge graphs, especially in open-world scenarios. *Quick check:* Ensure the model can predict plausible missing links in a KG.
- **Entity Linking**: The process of mapping mentions in text to entities in a knowledge graph. *Why needed:* Entity linking is crucial for integrating unstructured text data into structured knowledge graphs. *Quick check:* Verify the model can correctly link entity mentions to KG entities.
- **Text-Graph Alignment**: The task of aligning representations of text and graph data. *Why needed:* Alignment enables the integration of heterogeneous data sources (text and graphs) for improved entity linking and ranking. *Quick check:* Assess the quality of aligned text and graph embeddings.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Text corpus -> Text encoder -> Aligned embeddings -> KG embeddings -> Joint model (JOINT) or OWE model -> Entity linking/ranking predictions
- **Critical Path**: Text encoding and alignment -> Entity linking/ranking
- **Design Tradeoffs**: JOINT vs. OWE: JOINT trains jointly on text and graph data, while OWE first trains on KG data and then aligns text and graph representations. OWE may be more robust in small-data scenarios.
- **Failure Signatures**: Poor performance on small graphs, inability to handle noisy text, or failure to align text and graph embeddings effectively.
- **First Experiments**: 1) Evaluate model performance on small graphs with clean text. 2) Test model robustness to noisy text data. 3) Compare JOINT and OWE approaches on medium-sized graphs.

## Limitations
- The evaluation focuses exclusively on small and medium-scale knowledge graphs (up to 300k triples), leaving the performance on large-scale graphs (billions of triples) largely unknown.
- The reported metrics are based on manually curated development and test sets; the robustness of the models to noisy, real-world text corpora remains uncertain.
- The OWE approach is consistently better than JOINT, but the reasons are not fully explored—whether this is due to the alignment step or simply better initial KG embeddings is unclear.

## Confidence
- High: Neural models significantly outperform bag-of-words baseline on small/medium graphs
- Medium: OWE approach generally outperforms JOINT; high precision in linking task
- Medium: IRT2 fills a gap in open-world KG completion benchmarks
