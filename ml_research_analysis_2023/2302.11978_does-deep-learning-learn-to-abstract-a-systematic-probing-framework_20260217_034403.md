# Does Deep Learning Learn to Abstract? A Systematic Probing Framework

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11978
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11978
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Framework shows deep learning models can learn abstract concepts through transferability experiments, with abstraction concentrated in middle-layer attention heads

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a systematic probing framework to investigate whether deep learning models can learn abstract concepts from concrete instances rather than just memorizing surface patterns. The framework evaluates abstraction capability through transferability experiments across controlled tasks with shared abstract concepts but different surface patterns. Experiments on pre-trained language models (T5 and GPT2) show strong evidence of abstraction capability, revealing a "memorize-then-abstract" two-stage learning process and concentration of abstract concepts in middle-layer attention heads.

## Method Summary
The method uses a transferability-based probing framework where models are trained on task A, then tested on task B that shares abstract grammar concepts but different surface patterns. Three experimental conditions are compared: control (fine-tune only on B), main (pre-train on A then fine-tune on B), and contrast (pre-train on mutated A then fine-tune on B). Performance gains indicate abstraction capability. The framework uses synthetic formal grammar tasks derived from COGS, with T5-Base and GPT2-Medium models fine-tuned for 100k steps with early stopping. Pruning experiments identify which attention heads store abstract concepts.

## Key Results
- Strong transferability performance gains demonstrate abstraction capability in PLMs
- Learning exhibits a "memorize-then-abstract" two-stage process with clear phase separation
- Abstract concepts are concentrated in a small number of middle-layer attention heads rather than distributed evenly
- Models show greater robustness to low-level/source-side mutations compared to high-level/target-side ones
- Pre-training and larger model sizes significantly enhance abstraction capability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The transferability perspective decouples abstract concepts from surface patterns by design.
- Mechanism: Tasks are constructed so that abstract concepts (grammar rules) are shared across tasks while surface patterns (terminals) differ completely. This allows transfer performance gains to directly indicate abstraction learning.
- Core assumption: Abstract concepts are inherently transferable across contexts while surface patterns are not.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our key idea is to probe the abstraction capability from a transferability perspective, since surface patterns are always bounded with task-specific characteristics while abstract concepts can be more generally reused."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A "memorize-then-abstract" two-stage learning process emerges during training.
- Mechanism: Early in training, the model memorizes task-specific surface patterns to achieve in-task performance. Later, it extracts generalizable abstract concepts that enable cross-task transfer.
- Core assumption: Deep learning models initially rely on memorization before discovering underlying abstractions.
- Evidence anchors: [section 6.1] "There is an obvious phase difference (48k training steps) between two time points that two learning processes achieve their 90% relative performance, respectively."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Abstract concepts are concentrated in middle-layer attention heads rather than distributed evenly.
- Mechanism: Pruning experiments show that only a small subset of middle-layer attention heads significantly impact abstract concept performance, indicating modular concentration.
- Core assumption: Abstract concepts can be modularized in specific network components rather than requiring distributed representation.
- Evidence anchors: [section 6.2] "Among hundreds of heads, only a dozen of them are highly correlated with storing abstract concepts... the abstract attention heads are mainly located in the middle layers of T5-Base model"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Transfer learning paradigm and its relationship to abstraction
  - Why needed here: The entire framework relies on understanding how knowledge transfers between tasks to indicate abstraction
  - Quick check question: If a model learns abstract concepts from task A, why would this help with task B that shares the same abstract concepts but different surface patterns?

- Concept: Catastrophic forgetting and its mitigation
  - Why needed here: The framework depends on the model retaining learned abstract concepts when fine-tuning on new tasks
  - Quick check question: What training strategy is mentioned to prevent the model from forgetting abstract concepts learned during pre-training?

- Concept: Modular representation in neural networks
  - Why needed here: Understanding whether abstract concepts are stored in specific network components affects how we interpret pruning experiments
  - Quick check question: What experimental evidence suggests that abstract concepts are not evenly distributed throughout the model?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: T5-Base and GPT2-Medium architectures with attention sub-layers as the critical components for abstraction
- Critical path: Pre-training → Continue pre-training on task A → Fine-tuning on transfer set B → Testing on test set B → Analysis of attention heads
- Design tradeoffs: Larger model sizes improve abstraction capability but increase computational cost; modular concentration vs. distributed representation
- Failure signatures: Poor transfer performance despite good in-task performance indicates memorization without abstraction; uniform performance degradation across all heads suggests distributed representation rather than modular concentration
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run the main probing experiment (A⇒B) to establish baseline abstraction capability
  2. Conduct pruning experiments on attention heads to identify which store abstract concepts
  3. Test robustness by introducing mutations to source and target grammars and measuring performance degradation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise relationship between abstraction capability and other cognitive abilities like reasoning and systematic generalizability?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that previous work on neural models' inability to learn abstract concepts may have been caused by the lack of other abilities rather than abstraction itself, suggesting a complex relationship between abstraction and other cognitive skills.
- Why unresolved: The paper only speculates about this relationship without providing concrete evidence or analysis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental studies that systematically vary both abstraction tasks and reasoning/generalization tasks to determine how they interact and contribute to overall performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the abstraction capability of PLMs scale with model size and data scale in a quantitative manner?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that larger models and more data improve abstraction, but does not provide a quantitative relationship or scaling laws for abstraction capability specifically.
- Why unresolved: The experiments show trends but don't establish precise scaling relationships for abstraction as opposed to general performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies measuring abstraction capability (using metrics like MoA) across a wide range of model sizes and data scales to derive scaling laws.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the exact mechanism by which pre-training enables abstraction capability in PLMs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that pre-training is critical for abstraction, but the specific mechanisms remain unknown.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows correlation between pre-training and abstraction but doesn't explain the causal mechanisms at the architectural or representational level.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of how pre-training changes the internal representations and attention patterns to enable abstraction.

## Limitations
- The framework relies exclusively on synthetic formal grammar tasks, limiting ecological validity
- The "memorize-then-abstract" learning process observation is based on single time point comparison rather than continuous monitoring
- Pruning experiments may not capture distributed representations effectively

## Confidence
- High: Transferability methodology and observation that pre-training/larger models enhance abstraction
- Medium: "Memorize-then-abstract" two-stage process due to reliance on specific timing observations
- Low: Concentration of abstract concepts in middle-layer attention heads due to potential limitations of pruning experiments

## Next Checks
1. Test the probing framework on non-grammar tasks (e.g., visual reasoning or mathematical problem-solving) to verify whether transferability-based abstraction detection generalizes beyond formal languages.

2. Implement fine-grained monitoring of model parameters throughout training (not just at discrete checkpoints) to map the exact progression from memorization to abstraction and identify potential intermediate stages.

3. Apply different interpretability methods (e.g., linear probing, activation atlases, or causal interventions) to the same models to verify whether middle-layer attention concentration remains consistent across analysis techniques.
