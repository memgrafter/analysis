# When Gradient Descent Meets Derivative-Free Optimization: A Match Made in Black-Box Scenario

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10013
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10013
- Reference count: 11
- Key outcome: GDFO achieves 81.85% average accuracy across seven datasets, outperforming state-of-the-art black-box tuning methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GDFO, a novel method that integrates gradient descent with derivative-free optimization to improve black-box tuning of continuous prompts. Unlike existing black-box tuning methods that rely solely on derivative-free optimization, GDFO incorporates gradient descent through knowledge distillation. It trains a student model to emulate a black-box teacher model, then jointly optimizes a prompt generator via gradient descent and continuous prompts via CMA-ES. Experimental results show GDFO significantly outperforms state-of-the-art gradient-free and gradient-based methods, achieving an average accuracy of 81.85% across seven benchmark datasets in a 16-shot setting.

## Method Summary
GDFO addresses black-box prompt tuning where gradients and model parameters are unavailable. The method uses knowledge distillation to train a student model that mimics a black-box teacher's outputs, enabling gradient-based optimization. A prompt generator is optimized via gradient descent while continuous prompts are optimized via CMA-ES. The final continuous prompt combines both approaches through a weighted sum. The system requires 8,000 API calls for optimization and demonstrates superior performance across seven benchmark datasets.

## Key Results
- GDFO achieves 81.85% average accuracy across seven benchmark datasets in 16-shot setting
- Outperforms state-of-the-art black-box tuning methods (BBT, BBTv2) by significant margins
- Ablation studies show removing either gradient descent or derivative-free optimization reduces performance
- Successfully evaluated on multiple black-box PLM architectures including RoBERTa, GPT-2, BART, and T5

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Knowledge Distillation for Gradient Access
Knowledge distillation enables gradient-based training in black-box settings by providing a surrogate model with accessible gradients. The student model learns to mimic the black-box teacher's outputs through KL divergence and cross-entropy loss, creating a differentiable proxy that allows gradient descent to optimize the prompt generator. This assumes the student can sufficiently approximate the teacher's behavior while maintaining computational efficiency.

### Mechanism 2: Complementary Optimization Strengths
Combining gradient descent and derivative-free optimization creates complementary optimization strengths. Gradient descent through the student model optimizes the prompt generator for sample-specific continuous prompts, while CMA-ES optimizes a shared continuous prompt vector. Both are combined via weighted sum, assuming the two approaches capture different aspects of the optimization landscape and their combination yields better results than either alone.

### Mechanism 3: Sample-Specific Prompt Adaptation
Sample-specific continuous prompts outperform fixed continuous prompts in black-box tuning. The prompt generator creates unique continuous prompts for each training sample, allowing the model to adapt more precisely to individual instances rather than using a single shared prompt. This assumes different samples benefit from different prompt representations, and the generator can learn meaningful sample-specific patterns.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Knowledge distillation and teacher-student training frameworks
  - Why needed here: Understanding how to transfer knowledge from an inaccessible black-box model to a trainable student model is fundamental to enabling gradient-based optimization in black-box scenarios
  - Quick check question: What loss functions are typically used in knowledge distillation, and how do they differ from standard supervised learning?

- Concept: Derivative-free optimization (DFO) and evolutionary algorithms
  - Why needed here: CMA-ES and other DFO methods provide the gradient-free optimization component that works directly with the black-box model, complementing the gradient-based approach through the student
  - Quick check question: How does CMA-ES balance exploration and exploitation in high-dimensional continuous spaces?

- Concept: Continuous prompt tuning and prompt generators
  - Why needed here: The prompt generator architecture and its integration with both gradient and DFO optimization is central to GDFO's design and requires understanding of prompt-based learning techniques
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between fixed continuous prompts and learned prompt generators in terms of parameter efficiency and performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Black-box teacher (inaccessible) → Student model (trainable via KD) ← Prompt generator (gradient-based) + CMA-ES (DFO) → Combined continuous prompts → Black-box teacher
- Critical path: Knowledge distillation training → Joint optimization of prompt generator and CMA-ES → Inference with combined prompts
- Design tradeoffs: Higher computational cost (student model + prompt generator) vs. improved performance; complexity of balancing gradient and DFO contributions
- Failure signatures: Student model underperforms on distillation (check KL divergence), CMA-ES converges slowly or to poor solutions, prompt generator fails to generate diverse prompts
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify knowledge distillation works by comparing student vs. teacher performance on a small validation set
  2. Test prompt generator alone with student model to establish baseline gradient-based performance
  3. Test CMA-ES alone with student model to establish baseline DFO performance before combining approaches

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does GDFO perform on sequence labeling and generation tasks compared to its performance on language understanding tasks? The paper mentions that the effectiveness of GDFO on tasks such as sequence labeling and generation tasks has yet to be fully examined, as it only focuses on language understanding tasks.

### Open Question 2
How does the performance of GDFO change when using different black-box models with varying architectures, such as encoder-only, decoder-only, and encoder-decoder models? While the paper shows GDFO performs well across different black-box models, it doesn't provide detailed analysis of how performance changes with different architectures.

### Open Question 3
How does the choice of student model impact the performance of GDFO, and does using a student model with a similar architecture to the black-box model lead to better results? The paper provides some insights into the impact of student models on GDFO's performance but doesn't offer comprehensive analysis of the relationship between student model architecture and GDFO's performance.

## Limitations
- Knowledge distillation fidelity is not thoroughly validated, raising questions about whether the student adequately captures the teacher's behavior
- Computational cost of training both student model and prompt generator is not discussed, leaving scalability concerns unaddressed
- Sample-specific prompts' generalization benefits are claimed but lack strong comparative evidence against fixed prompt baselines

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** Experimental results demonstrating GDFO's superior performance over existing black-box tuning methods are well-supported with concrete accuracy metrics across seven datasets. The ablation study showing performance degradation when removing either optimization component is convincing.

**Medium Confidence:** The knowledge distillation mechanism for enabling gradient-based optimization in black-box settings is plausible and supported by standard KD theory, but the paper doesn't provide sufficient evidence that the student model adequately captures the teacher's behavior for effective optimization.

**Low Confidence:** The claim that sample-specific continuous prompts significantly outperform fixed prompts is novel but lacks strong supporting evidence - the paper doesn't compare against fixed prompt baselines on the same experimental setup.

## Next Checks

1. **Knowledge Distillation Fidelity Analysis:** Evaluate the student model's performance against the black-box teacher on a held-out validation set to quantify how well distillation captures the teacher's behavior. Compute both KL divergence and accuracy metrics to ensure the student provides meaningful gradient signals.

2. **Optimization Component Contribution Analysis:** Perform controlled ablation studies where each optimization component is disabled individually, and measure not just final accuracy but also convergence speed and optimization stability. Include statistical significance testing across multiple runs.

3. **Sample-Specific Prompt Generalization Test:** Design experiments comparing GDFO's sample-specific prompts against fixed continuous prompts under varying dataset sizes and domain shifts to determine whether the added complexity of sample-specific generation provides consistent benefits.
