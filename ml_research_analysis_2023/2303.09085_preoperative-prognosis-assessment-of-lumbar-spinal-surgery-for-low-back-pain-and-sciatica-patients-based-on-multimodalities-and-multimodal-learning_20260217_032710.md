# Preoperative Prognosis Assessment of Lumbar Spinal Surgery for Low Back Pain and Sciatica Patients based on Multimodalities and Multimodal Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09085
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09085
- Reference count: 40
- Best performance achieved: 0.81 accuracy using standard operative assessments, body constitution assessments, and planned surgical approach

## Executive Summary
This study developed a preoperative assessment tool to predict surgical outcomes for lumbar spinal surgery patients using multimodal learning. The researchers combined standard operative assessments, Traditional Chinese Medicine body constitution evaluations, planned surgical approach descriptions, and vowel pronunciation recordings. By implementing various fusion strategies across neural networks and tree-based algorithms, they demonstrated that integrating Eastern medicine assessments with machine learning effectively predicts surgical outcomes. The approach shows potential for wide clinical application in preoperative risk assessment.

## Method Summary
The study collected multimodal data from 176 patients undergoing lumbar spinal surgery, including clinical metrics (VAS, EQ-5D, ODI, ASA scores), TCM body constitution assessments (Yang-Xu, Yin-Xu, Stasis, Gentleness), planned surgical approach descriptions, and vowel pronunciation audio recordings. Data was preprocessed with scaling, PubMedBERT encoding for text, and STFT spectrogram processing for audio. The researchers implemented unimodal models (CatBoost for tabular, neural networks for text, LSTM for audio) and tested five fusion strategies (early, joint, late, and hybrid approaches) using five-fold cross-validation with L2 regularization.

## Key Results
- Best performance achieved: 0.81 accuracy using standard operative assessments, body constitution assessments, and planned surgical approach
- Joint fusion (JF) strategy performed best for triple modalities, while early fusion (EF) was optimal for dual modalities
- Model showed superior performance over single-modality approaches, demonstrating the value of multimodal integration

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Preprocessor → Encoder → Classifier pipeline for each modality, with fusion layers combining encoded representations
- Critical path: Data preprocessing → modality-specific encoding → fusion strategy application → classification output
- Design tradeoffs: Early fusion offers joint feature learning but requires compatible feature spaces; joint fusion enables complex feature extraction but needs careful architecture design; late fusion is simpler but may miss interactions
- Failure signatures: Low specificity despite high accuracy suggests model bias toward majority class; high variance in bootstrap intervals indicates poor generalization; modality contributions not matching domain knowledge suggests feature extraction issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare unimodal performance: train separate models for tabular, text, and audio modalities to establish baseline capabilities
  2. Test fusion strategy impact: implement early, joint, and late fusion for best unimodal combinations to identify optimal fusion approach
  3. Validate generalizability: perform bootstrap confidence interval analysis on best-performing multimodal model to assess stability across different data subsets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do specific vowel pronunciation features correlate with individual TCM body constitution types and surgical outcomes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study mentions that vowel pronunciation symbolizes inner energy imbalance in TCM, and Stasis score showed correlation with audio canonical variate weights, but specific acoustic features are not detailed
- Why unresolved: The study used general audio features without isolating which acoustic parameters specifically relate to constitution types or prognosis
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed acoustic feature analysis linking specific formant frequencies, pitch variability, or energy distribution to individual constitution types and surgical outcomes

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal multimodal fusion strategy for preoperative assessment across different surgical procedures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study tested various fusion strategies and found JF performed best for triple modalities, but only for lumbar surgery
- Why unresolved: Research focused on lumbar spinal surgery; effectiveness across other procedures remains unknown
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies testing fusion strategies across multiple surgical procedures with outcome prediction

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the preoperative assessment model generalize across different healthcare systems and patient populations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study recruited 105 patients from a single Taiwanese hospital with specific TCM integration
- Why unresolved: Limited sample size and single-center design restricts generalizability
- What evidence would resolve it: Multi-center validation studies across diverse populations and healthcare systems with larger sample sizes

## Limitations
- Small sample size (176 patients) despite promising results, lacking detailed statistical power analysis
- TCM classification thresholds not explicitly defined, affecting reproducibility
- Audio preprocessing pipeline lacks specific technical details (STFT parameters, framing size)

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Unimodal performance metrics and general superiority of multimodal approaches over single-modality predictions
- Medium Confidence: Comparative performance between fusion strategies demonstrated, but architectural details and hyperparameters could influence outcomes
- Low Confidence: Interpretation of TCM constitution assessments' contribution to surgical outcomes lacks strong clinical validation in Western medical literature
