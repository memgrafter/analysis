# NoisywikiHow: A Benchmark for Learning with Real-world Noisy Labels in Natural Language Processing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.10709
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10709
- **Reference count**: 33
- **Key outcome**: Introduces NoisywikiHow, a large-scale NLP benchmark for learning with real-world noisy labels, revealing that widely accepted LNL approaches are not always effective on instance-dependent, heterogeneous noise

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces NoisywikiHow, a large-scale NLP benchmark designed to evaluate learning with real-world noisy labels. Unlike existing benchmarks that focus on synthetic noise in computer vision, NoisywikiHow addresses the gap in NLP by providing heterogeneous, instance-dependent label noise that closely mimics human annotation errors. The dataset contains 89,143 instances across 158 classes with long-tailed distribution, constructed from the wikiHow website with controlled noise injection inspired by human cognition. Through extensive experiments with various pre-trained models and label noise learning methods, the study demonstrates that instance-dependent and heterogeneous noise sources pose significant challenges that many existing approaches cannot effectively handle.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed NoisywikiHow by crawling procedural text data from wikiHow, cleaning it through filtering and human annotation, then injecting controlled instance-dependent label noise across three sources: sub-categories (SC), categories beyond commonsense (BCC), and tail categories (TC). The dataset exhibits a long-tailed class distribution. Experiments involved fine-tuning pre-trained language models (BERT, XLNet, RoBERTa, GPT2, ALBERT, T5, BART) with cross-entropy loss for 10 epochs, and evaluating label noise learning methods (Mixup, Data Parameter, SR, Co-teaching, CNLCU, SEAL) on noisy training data while measuring performance on clean validation sets using Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- Instance-dependent label noise is significantly harder to mitigate than instance-independent noise, with overlapping loss distributions between correct and incorrect samples
- Heterogeneous noise sources (ambiguity, expertise gaps, attention slips) require different mitigation strategies than single-source noise
- Long-tailed distribution amplifies noise impact on minority classes, with tail class performance degrading faster under noise conditions
- Widely accepted LNL methods show limited effectiveness on real-world noise compared to their performance on synthetic noise benchmarks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Instance-dependent label noise is harder to mitigate than instance-independent noise
- Mechanism: Instance-dependent noise correlates label corruption probability with both the original label and instance features, making it impossible to separate clean and noisy samples by loss alone
- Core assumption: Real-world annotation errors are context-dependent, not random across all samples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "synthetic noise is not instance-dependent, making this approximation not always effective in practice"
  - [section 3.3] "instance-dependent label noise exists in each noise source"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on class-conditional or symmetric noise, not instance-dependent
- Break condition: If noise source becomes purely random across all instances regardless of content

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Heterogeneous noise sources require different mitigation strategies than single-source noise
- Mechanism: Different error types (ambiguity, expertise gaps, attention slips) create overlapping but distinct loss distributions that single-method approaches cannot handle
- Core assumption: Human annotation errors stem from multiple cognitive factors rather than one dominant source
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "noise sources within may be single or fuzzy"
  - [section 3.3] "we simulate various mistakes committed by annotators to produce real-world noise containing heterogeneous noise sources"
  - [corpus] Weak - most benchmark datasets use single synthetic noise types
- Break condition: If all noise sources produce identical statistical properties

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Long-tailed distribution amplifies noise impact on minority classes
- Mechanism: Limited samples in tail classes mean each noisy label disproportionately affects the learned representation, creating feedback loops of poor generalization
- Core assumption: Real-world datasets exhibit power-law class distributions rather than uniform
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "training samples in our benchmark exhibit a long-tailed class distribution"
  - [section 3.2] "we observe that the labels have two types...and we select the category with the finest granularity as the label"
  - [corpus] Weak - related work focuses on balanced or moderately imbalanced datasets
- Break condition: If class distribution becomes balanced or noise injection is independent of class frequency

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Crawling → Cleaning → Noise injection → Train/Val/Test split
- Model layer: Pre-trained language models (BERT, RoBERTa, BART, etc.)
- LNL methods: Mixup, Data Parameter, SR, Co-teaching, CNLCU, SEAL
- Evaluation: Top-1/Top-5 accuracy on clean validation set

- Critical path:
  1. Crawl wikiHow articles and extract procedural events + categories
  2. Clean data through filtering and human annotation
  3. Inject controlled instance-dependent noise across three sources
  4. Train base models and LNL methods on noisy training set
  5. Evaluate on clean validation set

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Noise realism vs controllability: Real-world noise is more challenging but harder to quantify
  - Human supervision vs automation: Minimal supervision saves effort but may miss edge cases
  - Class granularity vs sample size: Finer categories provide more realistic task but reduce samples per class

- Failure signatures:
  - Loss distributions of correct/incorrect samples overlapping completely
  - Performance degradation proportional to noise level across all methods
  - Tail class performance dropping faster than head classes

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train base BART model on clean data (0% noise) to establish baseline
  2. Inject 10% instance-dependent noise from SC source only and train SEAL
  3. Compare performance of Mixup vs Co-teaching on 40% synthetic vs real-world noise

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the performance characteristics of learning with real-world noisy labels differ between the NLP domain and the computer vision/ audio signal processing domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "Since most LNL method evaluation datasets focus on the CV and ASP, our NLP benchmark facilitates the modal integrity of the existing datasets."
- Why unresolved: The paper introduces NoisywikiHow as the first large-scale NLP benchmark for learning with real-world noisy labels, but does not provide direct comparisons between NLP and other domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct empirical comparisons of LNL methods on NoisywikiHow vs. established CV/ASP benchmarks like Food-101N, AudioSet, or NoisyNER.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the specific challenges and limitations of applying long-tailed learning methods to noisy label scenarios in NLP tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the long-tailed distribution of NoisywikiHow and evaluates several long-tailed learning methods, finding limited effectiveness compared to their performance on CV datasets.
- Why unresolved: While the paper presents some results on long-tailed learning methods, it does not deeply explore the specific challenges of combining long-tailed distributions with label noise in NLP.
- What evidence would resolve it: In-depth analysis of why long-tailed learning methods underperform on NoisywikiHow, possibly through error analysis or ablation studies isolating the effects of noise vs. class imbalance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of noise injection strategy impact the effectiveness of learning with noisy labels methods in real-world scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper describes a specific strategy for injecting instance-dependent label noise inspired by human cognition, but notes that "few LNL methods can effectively combat real-world noise and synthetic noise at the same time."
- Why unresolved: The paper presents one approach to creating realistic label noise but does not explore alternative strategies or their impact on LNL method performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of LNL method performance on NoisywikiHow vs. benchmarks created with different noise injection strategies, or an ablation study varying the noise injection parameters.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of instance-dependent noise modeling may be overestimated since synthetic noise injection cannot perfectly replicate true human annotation errors
- The long-tailed class distribution impact might be confounded with sample size effects rather than noise vulnerability per se
- Limited evaluation to only English-language procedural text may restrict generalizability to other NLP domains

## Confidence

- **High**: The dataset construction methodology and noise injection pipeline are well-documented and reproducible
- **Medium**: The claim that heterogeneous noise requires different mitigation strategies is supported but needs more ablation studies
- **Low**: The assertion that instance-dependent noise is fundamentally harder to mitigate than instance-independent noise requires more theoretical justification
