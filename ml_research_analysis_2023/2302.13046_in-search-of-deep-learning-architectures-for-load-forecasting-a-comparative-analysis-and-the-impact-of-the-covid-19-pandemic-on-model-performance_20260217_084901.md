# In Search of Deep Learning Architectures for Load Forecasting: A Comparative Analysis and the Impact of the Covid-19 Pandemic on Model Performance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13046
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13046
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: N-BEATS achieves highest accuracy (MAPE 2.001) for short-term load forecasting while TCN shows better robustness to COVID-19-induced distribution shifts

## Executive Summary
This study conducts a comprehensive comparison of three deep learning architectures—N-BEATS, LSTM, and TCN—for short-term load forecasting using Portuguese national 15-minute resolution net load data from 2009-2020. The research evaluates both accuracy and sustainability (training time) while examining how these architectures handle the distribution shifts caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. N-BEATS emerges as the most accurate model, achieving a MAPE of 2.001, while TCN demonstrates superior robustness to pandemic-induced load pattern changes despite lower overall accuracy.

The study provides practical insights for smart grid applications, suggesting that TCN is more suitable for scenarios requiring robustness to unexpected events without frequent retraining, whereas N-BEATS is preferred when maximum accuracy and sustainability are priorities in stable conditions. The authors also propose a high-level deployment framework for integrating these architectures into operational smart grid systems, highlighting the importance of considering both technical performance and operational requirements in model selection.

## Method Summary
The study compares N-BEATS, LSTM, and TCN architectures for short-term load forecasting using Portuguese national net load data at 15-minute resolution. Temporal covariates (year, month, day of year, day of week, week of year, holiday) are generated from timestamps, except for N-BEATS which uses only the raw time series. Models are trained on data from 2009-2017, validated on 2018 data, and tested on both pre-COVID (2019) and COVID period (2020) data using a backtesting approach. The PSF ensemble models serve as baseline. Model performance is evaluated using Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), while training sustainability is measured by GPU training time. The study also examines how each architecture handles the distribution shifts caused by the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Key Results
- N-BEATS achieved the best accuracy with MAPE of 2.001, outperforming LSTM and TCN on Portuguese net load forecasting
- TCN demonstrated better robustness to COVID-19-induced load distribution shifts despite having lower overall accuracy than N-BEATS
- Training sustainability showed that larger, more complex neural networks require fewer epochs to converge, reducing GPU training time
- The study confirms that model selection should balance accuracy requirements with robustness needs, particularly in scenarios prone to unexpected events

## Foundational Learning
1. **Temporal Covariates**: Calendar-based features (year, month, day of week, holidays) capture cyclical patterns in load demand
   - Why needed: Load consumption exhibits strong temporal dependencies and seasonal patterns
   - Quick check: Verify temporal features align with known consumption patterns (higher loads in summer/winter)

2. **Distribution Shifts**: Changes in data distribution that affect model performance, such as those caused by COVID-19 lockdowns
   - Why needed: Models trained on historical data may fail when future conditions significantly differ
   - Quick check: Compare training vs. test period statistics to identify distribution changes

3. **MAPE as Evaluation Metric**: Mean Absolute Percentage Error measures forecasting accuracy as percentage deviation
   - Why needed: Provides interpretable error measurement that scales with load magnitude
   - Quick check: Ensure no zero values in ground truth to avoid division errors

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of N-BEATS, LSTM, and TCN architectures change when incorporating weather variables and forecasts into the models?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention that future work includes "the integration of weather variables—and specifically weather forecasts—in the models."
- Why unresolved: The current study focuses on temporal covariates but does not include weather data, which is a significant exogenous variable affecting load forecasting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing model performance with and without weather variables, demonstrating the impact on accuracy and robustness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal trade-off between model accuracy and GPU training time for sustainable load forecasting in smart grid applications?
- Basis in paper: The authors discuss the importance of sustainability and mention that "larger and more complex neural network architectures on average result to fewer epochs as they can fit the datasets faster, denoting lower GPU times and thus higher sustainability."
- Why unresolved: The study provides insights into the trade-off but does not offer a comprehensive analysis of the optimal balance for different operational scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed study quantifying the trade-off between accuracy and GPU time across various model architectures and operational requirements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different deep learning ensemble methods perform in terms of out-of-distribution generalization during crisis events like the COVID-19 pandemic?
- Basis in paper: The authors suggest future work on "the extensive search of rigorous setups—including DL ensembles—that will allow for out-of-distribution generalization without the compromise of underfitting during normal periods."
- Why unresolved: The current study does not explore ensemble methods, which could potentially improve robustness during distribution shifts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments evaluating various ensemble methods on their ability to handle distribution shifts while maintaining accuracy during stable periods.

## Limitations
- Single national dataset (Portugal) limits generalizability to other grid characteristics and load patterns
- Evaluation restricted to 15-minute resolution data, limiting applicability to other temporal granularities
- COVID-19 impact assessment based on only one year (2020), potentially missing longer-term pandemic effects
