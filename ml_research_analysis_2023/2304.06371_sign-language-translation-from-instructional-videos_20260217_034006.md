# Sign Language Translation from Instructional Videos
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06371
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06371
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First baseline results for SLT on How2Sign dataset with BLEU score of 8.03

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the first baseline results for sign language translation (SLT) on the How2Sign dataset, presenting a Transformer model trained over I3D video features. The authors introduce reduced BLEU (rBLEU) as an evaluation metric, arguing it's more appropriate than standard BLEU for low-resource datasets with repetitive patterns. The implementation achieves a BLEU score of 8.03 on the test set, providing a foundation for future research in this challenging domain.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a Transformer-based approach for sign language translation, using I3D video features as input representations. The model architecture follows a standard encoder-decoder structure, where the encoder processes video features and the decoder generates translated text sequences. A key methodological contribution is the adoption of reduced BLEU (rBLEU) as the primary evaluation metric, which the authors argue better captures translation quality in low-resource settings where traditional BLEU scores may be misleading due to repetitive patterns in instructional content.

## Key Results
- Achieved BLEU score of 8.03 on How2Sign test set
- Established first baseline results for SLT on How2Sign dataset
- Demonstrated effectiveness of reduced BLEU (rBLEU) metric for low-resource SLT evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging transformer architectures' ability to capture long-range dependencies in sign language video sequences. The I3D features provide rich spatiotemporal representations that encode both the spatial configuration of signs and their temporal progression. The transformer model can then learn to map these visual representations to corresponding textual translations, handling the complex relationship between sign language and spoken language that often involves different grammatical structures and conceptual mappings.

- Reduced BLEU (rBLEU): Modified evaluation metric accounting for repetitive patterns - needed for fair assessment in low-resource settings; quick check: compare rBLEU vs BLEU on synthetic repetitive data
- Sign Language Grammar: Understanding of non-linear, spatial grammar structures - needed to interpret translation quality; quick check: analyze translation outputs for grammatical correctness

Component Map: I3D Feature Extractor -> Transformer Encoder -> Transformer Decoder -> Text Output
Critical Path: Video Input → I3D Features → Transformer Encoder → Transformer Decoder → Translation Output
Design Tradeoffs: Used pre-trained I3D features rather than training from scratch to save computation, but this limits adaptability to specific sign language variations
Failure Signatures: Low BLEU scores when videos contain complex sign sequences, model struggles with rare signs, and rBLEU may over-penalize creative translations

## Limitations
- Use of rBLEU may not fully capture translation quality compared to standard BLEU
- Dataset size of 69.6 hours remains limited for training complex transformer models
- No detailed ablation studies or error analysis provided to understand performance drivers
