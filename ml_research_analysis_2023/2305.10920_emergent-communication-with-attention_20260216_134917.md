# Emergent Communication with Attention

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.10920
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10920
- **Reference count**: 5
- **Primary result**: Attention mechanisms improve compositionality and interpretability of emergent languages in referential games

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how attention mechanisms affect the emergence of compositional languages in multi-agent communication systems. By implementing cross-modal attention in Speaker and Listener agents playing a referential game with Fashion-MNIST images, the authors demonstrate that attention leads to more interpretable and compositional emergent languages. The attention weights reveal clear associations between symbols and object attributes, showing patterns like monosemy, polysemy, and gibberish symbols. Crucially, the alignment between Speaker and Listener attention weights correlates with communication success, suggesting that attention helps agents develop shared understanding of symbols.

## Method Summary
The authors implement a referential game using Fashion-MNIST images with two agents: a Speaker that generates messages from objects and a Listener that infers objects from messages. Objects are represented as feature vectors extracted from a pretrained ConvNeXt Tiny model. Both agents use either LSTM or Transformer architectures with optional attention mechanisms. Speaker agents are trained with REINFORCE algorithm and entropy/KL regularization, while Listener agents use multi-class classification loss. The study compares attention vs non-attention agents across multiple configurations, evaluating performance using training accuracy, generalization accuracy, and topographic similarity metrics.

## Key Results
- Attention agents achieve higher training and generalization accuracy compared to non-attention baselines
- Attention leads to more compositional emergent languages with clearer symbol-object associations
- Alignment between Speaker and Listener attention weights correlates with communication success
- Attention weights reveal interpretable patterns including monosemy, polysemy, and gibberish symbols

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Attention enables agents to associate symbols with specific object attributes, leading to more compositional language.
- Mechanism: The attention mechanism allows agents to dynamically focus on relevant parts of input stimuli when generating or interpreting symbols. This creates a direct mapping between symbols and specific object attributes rather than encoding information into a single vector.
- Core assumption: The input representation is spatially disentangled enough that attention can reliably associate symbols with distinct object attributes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We implement this intuition as cross-modal attention mechanisms in Speaker and Listener agents in a referential game and show attention leads to more compositional and interpretable emergent language."
  - [section] "Our hypothesis is that attention can help agents learn clear associations between subparts of input stimuli and language symbols, resulting in more compositional language."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on different aspects of emergent communication, not specifically on attention mechanisms.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Attention alignment between Speaker and Listener predicts communication success.
- Mechanism: When Speaker and Listener agents develop similar attention patterns for the same symbols and objects, they establish a shared understanding of what each symbol represents, leading to more successful communication.
- Core assumption: Both agents are optimizing for the same communication goal and their attention patterns will converge toward similar interpretations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The alignment between Speaker and Listener attention weights correlates with communication success, suggesting that attention agents develop shared understanding of symbols."
  - [section] "An important prerequisite of successful communication is the participants engaging in joint attention and establishing a shared understanding of each word."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in related papers about attention alignment correlation with communication success.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Attention provides interpretability of emergent languages by revealing what each symbol refers to.
- Mechanism: The attention weights associated with each symbol generation step provide a window into what the agent is focusing on when producing that symbol, making the meaning of each symbol interpretable.
- Core assumption: The attention weights genuinely reflect the agent's reasoning process rather than being arbitrary or post-hoc rationalizations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The attention weights provide insights into symbol-object associations and reveal patterns such as monosemy, polysemy, and gibberish symbols."
  - [section] "Attention weights, on the other hand, have been shown to provide insights into the basis of the network's prediction."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in related papers about attention providing interpretability of emergent languages.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different attention mechanisms (e.g., multi-head vs single-head) affect the compositionality and interpretability of emergent languages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "The original Transformer also has the multi-head attention mechanism, but in the main experiments, we set the number of the attention heads to one for interpretability and ease of analysis."
- Why unresolved: The study only explored single-head attention, leaving the impact of multi-head attention on emergent language compositionality and interpretability unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the compositionality metrics (e.g., TopSim) and interpretability of emergent languages using single-head vs multi-head attention mechanisms in similar experimental setups.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can attention mechanisms be effectively applied to emergent languages with more complex inputs, such as abstract concepts or multi-modal data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors suggest that "While learning to attend to abstract attributes such as color and shape in images can be more challenging, we anticipate that it is achievable with the use of high-quality feature extractors and learning configurations."
- Why unresolved: The study used spatially disentangled images with clear object boundaries, which may not generalize to more complex input types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing attention mechanisms on emergent languages with abstract concepts or multi-modal data (e.g., text, audio, and video) and evaluating their compositionality and interpretability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the alignment between Speaker and Listener attention weights influence the emergence of more human-like communication protocols?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors found that "The degree of the alignment between Speaker's and Listener's attention weights can be regarded as a proxy of their mutual understanding and predictive of communication success."
- Why unresolved: The study only explored the correlation between attention alignment and communication success, without investigating the causal relationship or the impact on communication protocol development.
- What evidence would resolve it: Manipulating the attention alignment between Speaker and Listener agents and measuring the resulting changes in communication success, compositionality, and interpretability of emergent languages.

## Limitations

- The analysis of attention weights is largely qualitative rather than quantitative, making it difficult to verify claims about monosemy, polysemy, and gibberish patterns
- The correlation between attention alignment and communication success lacks statistical significance testing
- The choice of ConvNeXt Tiny for feature extraction and the specific configuration used is not detailed, which could affect reproducibility

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Claims about attention agents achieving better training and generalization accuracy compared to non-attention baselines. The experimental setup and metrics (TrainAcc, GenAcc, TopSim) are well-defined and the results are presented with clear baselines.

**Medium Confidence**: Claims about attention leading to more interpretable emergent languages. While the qualitative analysis of attention weights provides suggestive evidence, the interpretation of monosemy/polysemy patterns could benefit from more rigorous statistical validation.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about attention alignment predicting communication success. The correlation presented is intriguing but lacks the statistical rigor needed to establish causation. Alternative explanations (e.g., shared optimization objectives) could account for the observed correlation.

## Next Checks

1. **Statistical Validation of Attention Patterns**: Implement quantitative metrics to measure monosemy, polysemy, and gibberish patterns in attention weights, then statistically test whether these distributions differ significantly between attention and non-attention agents.

2. **Ablation Study on Feature Extraction**: Compare results using different feature extraction methods (e.g., ConvNeXt vs ResNet vs raw pixels) to determine whether the compositional benefits of attention depend on the quality of input representations.

3. **Controlled Attention Alignment Experiment**: Design an experiment where Speaker and Listener attention weights are deliberately misaligned (e.g., through regularization) to test whether the correlation between attention alignment and communication success is causal or merely correlational.
