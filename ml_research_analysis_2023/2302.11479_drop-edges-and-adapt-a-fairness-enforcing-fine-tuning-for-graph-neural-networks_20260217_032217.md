# Drop Edges and Adapt: a Fairness Enforcing Fine-tuning for Graph Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.11479
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11479
- **Authors**: 
- **Reference count**: 14
- **Primary result**: Improves both accuracy and fairness in GNN link prediction by learning to drop unfair edges while adapting model parameters

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses fairness issues in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for link prediction, where existing algorithms can increase social network segregation by disfavoring links between different demographic groups. The authors propose DEA (Drop Edges and Adapt), a fine-tuning strategy that simultaneously learns to drop unfair edges and adapt the model's parameters to these modifications. The core innovation is a novel covariance-based constraint that enforces fairness by minimizing the correlation between edge predictions and sensitive attributes, implemented through a learnable adjacency matrix.

## Method Summary
The DEA method introduces a two-stage optimization process for fairness-aware GNN fine-tuning. It employs a covariance-based constraint that minimizes the correlation between predicted links and sensitive attributes, implemented through a learnable adjacency matrix. The approach uses a Gumbel-sigmoid trick to maintain discrete edges during training, enabling gradient-based optimization while preserving the discrete nature of edge dropping. The method operates by simultaneously optimizing edge weights (to drop unfair edges) and model parameters (to adapt to the modified graph structure).

## Key Results
- Improves accuracy from 82.4% to 83.6% on DBLP dataset
- Achieves better fairness metrics across mixed and group dyadic definitions
- Removes only 10% of edges compared to competitors' 20-30% while maintaining superior performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by directly addressing the correlation between link predictions and sensitive attributes through a novel covariance-based constraint. By learning which edges to drop (rather than just reweighting), the approach can eliminate the most problematic connections that drive unfair predictions. The simultaneous optimization of edge dropping and parameter adaptation allows the model to maintain predictive performance while improving fairness.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input graph -> DEA optimizer -> Modified adjacency matrix -> GNN model -> Predictions -> Fairness/accuracy metrics

**Critical Path**: The optimization loop where edge weights are updated to minimize unfairness while maintaining accuracy, feeding back into the GNN parameter updates.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades computational complexity (iterative optimization) for improved fairness and accuracy. The 10% edge removal rate represents a balance between maintaining graph structure and achieving fairness goals.

**Failure Signatures**: The method might fail when the correlation between edges and sensitive attributes is not the primary source of unfairness, or when edge dropping significantly disrupts the graph's community structure.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses on binary link prediction tasks; performance on multi-class or weighted link prediction is unknown
- The 10% edge removal rate, while efficient, may still significantly impact network structure
- Computational overhead of iterative optimization could limit scalability to larger graphs

## Confidence

- **Low**: Claims about broader social impact and real-world deployment implications
