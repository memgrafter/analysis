# Noise Is Not the Main Factor Behind the Gap Between SGD and Adam on Transformers, but Sign Descent Might Be

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13960
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13960
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Noise is not the main factor behind SGD-Adam performance gap on transformers

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why Adam consistently outperforms SGD on transformer models by systematically varying batch sizes to control for stochasticity. Through comprehensive experiments, the authors demonstrate that noise reduction alone cannot explain the performance gap - in fact, Adam's advantage increases with larger batch sizes. The study reveals that the gap is largely deterministic in nature, and proposes that Adam's success can be attributed to its similarity to sign descent with momentum, a simpler optimization variant that can close most of the performance gap in full-batch settings.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct extensive empirical experiments on transformer models, systematically varying batch sizes from small (noisy) to full-batch (deterministic) settings. They compare standard SGD against Adam across multiple batch sizes, measuring performance to isolate the contribution of noise to the observed performance gap. Additionally, they implement and test sign descent - a variant that uses only the sign of gradients - with momentum to evaluate its effectiveness relative to both SGD and Adam.

## Key Results
- Adam outperforms SGD even in full-batch settings, indicating the gap is deterministic rather than noise-driven
- As batch size increases, Adam's performance advantage over SGD grows rather than diminishes
- Sign descent with momentum can close most of the SGD-Adam performance gap in full-batch training
- The improvement of Adam over SGD is not primarily due to noise reduction but rather to other algorithmic properties

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper suggests that Adam's effectiveness stems from its implicit bias toward sign descent behavior combined with momentum. While traditional understanding attributed Adam's success to noise handling through adaptive learning rates, the empirical evidence shows this explanation is incomplete. Instead, Adam's update rule appears to approximate sign descent updates with momentum, which may be better suited to the geometry of the loss landscape in transformer models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sign descent**: An optimization method using only the sign of gradients rather than their magnitude; needed to understand Adam's implicit behavior
- **Gradient noise scale**: The variance in stochastic gradients that decreases with larger batch sizes; key for noise-based explanations
- **Momentum in optimization**: The technique of accumulating past gradients to accelerate convergence; critical for both Adam and sign descent variants
- **Transformer optimization landscape**: The specific geometry and smoothness properties of transformer loss surfaces; context for why certain optimizers work better
- **Full-batch vs. stochastic training**: The distinction between deterministic and noisy gradient updates; essential for isolating noise effects
- **Adaptive learning rates**: The mechanism by which optimizers like Adam scale updates per-parameter; commonly cited but here shown insufficient to explain performance

Design tradeoffs: Noise reduction vs. computational efficiency; adaptive learning rates vs. simplicity; momentum vs. responsiveness

Failure signatures: Adam underperforms when gradients are homogeneous; SGD struggles with ill-conditioned loss landscapes

## Limitations
- Analysis is primarily empirical without theoretical justification for the sign descent connection
- Findings may be specific to transformer architectures and not generalize to other model types
- Sign descent variant tested is simplified and may not capture all aspects of Adam's behavior

## Confidence
- The noise independence finding: High confidence
- The sign descent connection: Medium confidence
- The deterministic nature of Adam's advantage: Medium confidence
