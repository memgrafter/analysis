# BiasAsker: Measuring the Bias in Conversational AI System

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12434
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12434
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: 32.83% of generated questions trigger biased responses with 0.93 accuracy in bias detection

## Executive Summary
BiasAsker is an automated framework designed to identify and measure social bias in conversational AI systems. The framework addresses the challenge of comprehensively evaluating bias by generating questions that trigger potential bias and employing novel methods to identify two types of biases: absolute bias and related bias. It constructs a comprehensive social bias dataset containing 841 social groups and 8,110 biased properties, using NLP techniques to generate diverse questions. The framework then measures bias through sentence similarity and existence measurements.

## Method Summary
BiasAsker operates through a multi-stage process: first constructing a comprehensive social bias dataset, then generating diverse questions targeting potential bias triggers, and finally measuring bias responses through two distinct approaches. The framework uses sentence similarity measurements and existence measurements to identify absolute and related bias types. Extensive experiments were conducted on eight commercial systems and two research models, including ChatGPT and GPT-3, to evaluate the framework's effectiveness in detecting biased behaviors.

## Key Results
- 32.83% of generated questions successfully triggered biased behaviors in tested conversational AI systems
- Framework achieved 0.93 accuracy in detecting bias
- Tested across eight commercial systems and two research models, demonstrating broad applicability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BiasAsker works by systematically generating questions that are likely to trigger biased responses based on known social group-property associations. The framework's dual measurement approach (sentence similarity and existence measurements) allows for comprehensive detection of both explicit and implicit biases. By constructing a large dataset of social groups and properties, BiasAsker can target a wide range of potential bias scenarios.

## Foundational Learning
- Social group-property mapping: Why needed - to identify potential bias triggers; Quick check - verify dataset coverage of diverse social groups
- NLP question generation: Why needed - to create varied scenarios for bias testing; Quick check - ensure generated questions are contextually appropriate
- Sentence similarity measurement: Why needed - to quantify degree of bias in responses; Quick check - validate against known biased/unbiased examples

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Dataset Construction -> Question Generation -> Bias Detection -> Results Analysis

Critical Path:
Social group dataset -> NLP question generation -> Conversational system interaction -> Response analysis -> Bias classification

Design Tradeoffs:
- Dataset comprehensiveness vs. computational efficiency
- Question diversity vs. focused bias targeting
- Detection accuracy vs. false positive rates

Failure Signatures:
- High false positive rates may indicate overly sensitive bias detection
- Low bias trigger rates could suggest insufficient question diversity
- Inconsistent results across similar systems may indicate framework limitations

First Experiments:
1. Test framework on controlled conversational AI with known biases
2. Validate question generation against human-annotated bias scenarios
3. Compare framework performance across different commercial systems

## Limitations
- Framework primarily focuses on English-language systems, limiting cross-cultural applicability
- Reliance on Wikipedia-derived data may introduce sampling biases
- Binary bias detection may oversimplify complex bias manifestations

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Question generation methodology is well-documented and reproducible
- Bias detection framework shows consistent results across tested systems
- Dataset construction approach is robust and systematic

**Medium Confidence:**
- Accuracy metrics would benefit from independent validation
- Bias trigger rate may vary with different question generation parameters
- Framework effectiveness across different conversational AI architectures

**Low Confidence:**
- Generalizability to non-English language systems
- Framework's ability to detect subtle or context-dependent biases
- Long-term effectiveness against evolving conversational AI systems
