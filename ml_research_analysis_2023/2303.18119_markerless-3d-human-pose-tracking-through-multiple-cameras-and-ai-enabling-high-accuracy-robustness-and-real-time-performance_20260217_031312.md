# Markerless 3D human pose tracking through multiple cameras and AI: Enabling high accuracy, robustness, and real-time performance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.18119
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18119
- Reference count: 40
- The framework achieves markerless 3D human pose tracking with average errors below 35 mm and real-time performance at 30 FPS using multi-camera views and AI-based 2D pose estimation.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a markerless 3D human pose tracking framework that leverages multi-camera views and 2D AI-based pose estimation to reconstruct human motion in real-time. The system integrates a Weighted Least Squares (WLS) algorithm to combine 2D pose estimations from multiple cameras into accurate 3D skeletons, addressing limitations of traditional marker-based systems. The approach achieves high accuracy with average errors below 35 mm and demonstrates robust performance under occlusions while maintaining real-time operation at 30 FPS.

## Method Summary
The framework uses multiple RGB cameras to capture human motion, with 2D pose estimation performed by AI models like OpenPose on each camera view. A Weighted Least Squares (WLS) algorithm then triangulates 3D joint positions by combining the 2D detections across all views, with weights based on confidence scores, visibility metrics, and geometric factors. The system employs simple ArUco marker-based calibration for extrinsic camera parameters and integrates within the Open-VICO framework for flexible simulation and real-world applications. Real-time performance is achieved through GPU acceleration for 2D pose estimation and ROS-based asynchronous processing for triangulation.

## Key Results
- Average joint position error below 35 mm across walking sequences
- Real-time operation at 30 FPS with 2D pose estimation latency of 25-125 ms
- Robust performance under occlusions with error growth remaining within acceptable bounds
- Successful integration with Open-VICO framework for simulation and teleoperation applications

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Cameras (RealSense D435 RGB-D) -> ROS nodes for capture -> GPU nodes for OpenPose 2D estimation -> ROS node for WLS triangulation -> RViz/Gazebo visualization -> downstream control/teleoperation
- Critical path: Camera capture -> 2D pose estimation -> WLS triangulation -> 3D skeleton output
- Design tradeoffs: Multi-GPU setup maximizes 2D estimation speed but increases hardware cost; RGB-D cameras provide depth redundancy but are not required for the method.
- Failure signatures: Dropped frames in RViz, increasing MPJPE over time, latency spikes correlated with GPU load.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Static pose with no occlusions: Verify WLS accuracy against known joint positions.
  2. Dynamic walking with full visibility: Measure real-time FPS and MPJPE over 60s.
  3. Occluded pose scenario: Test robustness by introducing artificial occlusions and measuring error growth.

## Limitations

- Hardware requirements: Multi-camera setup (minimum 3 cameras) and powerful GPU hardware create deployment barriers for resource-constrained applications.
- Performance degradation: WLS triangulation accuracy degrades under severe occlusions or insufficient camera overlap, with joint visibility being critical for reliable 3D reconstruction.
- Accuracy ceiling: 35 mm average error falls short of marker-based optical systems (typically 5-10 mm) and may not meet requirements for precision-demanding tasks like clinical gait analysis.
