# Incomplete Multi-view Clustering via Diffusion Completion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.11489
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11489
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: IMVCDC outperforms state-of-the-art methods on four datasets using diffusion models for missing view completion

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Incomplete Multi-view Clustering via Diffusion Completion (IMVCDC), a novel approach that leverages diffusion models to recover missing views in multi-view clustering tasks. The method operates by first reconstructing views using autoencoders, then completing missing views in the latent space through diffusion models conditioned on observable views, and finally applying contrastive learning to cluster the multi-view data. Experimental results demonstrate superior performance compared to existing methods across four datasets, achieving higher accuracy, normalized mutual information, and adjusted rand index scores.

## Method Summary
IMVCDC addresses the incomplete multi-view clustering problem through a three-module framework: view reconstruction, diffusion completion, and contrastive clustering. The method first uses autoencoders to learn latent representations of each view, then employs diffusion models to complete missing views in the latent space by conditioning on available views. Finally, contrastive learning with spectral contrastive loss is applied to learn consistent representations across all views for clustering. The approach handles missing data by treating it as a reconstruction problem in latent space rather than working directly with incomplete observations.

## Key Results
- Achieves superior clustering performance on Multi-Fashion, Multi-Coil20, NoisyMNIST, and NoisyDigit-Product datasets
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods in terms of Accuracy, NMI, and ARI metrics
- Demonstrates effectiveness of diffusion models for view completion in multi-view clustering

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging diffusion models' ability to learn complex data distributions for view completion. By completing missing views in the latent space rather than raw data space, the approach captures more abstract and consistent representations across views. The contrastive learning component then enforces consistency among the completed multi-view representations, leading to better clustering performance. The three-module framework allows each component to specialize in its task while maintaining end-to-end optimization.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Autoencoders (view reconstruction) -> Diffusion Models (view completion) -> Contrastive Learning (clustering)

**Critical Path**: View reconstruction provides latent representations → Diffusion models complete missing views in latent space → Contrastive learning clusters completed views

**Design Tradeoffs**: The three-module approach allows specialized optimization but requires careful coordination. Alternative: end-to-end training vs. staged optimization. The choice of diffusion models for completion vs. simpler imputation methods balances reconstruction quality with computational complexity.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor reconstruction quality indicates autoencoder issues. Incomplete or noisy view completion suggests diffusion model problems. Suboptimal clustering points to contrastive learning difficulties.
