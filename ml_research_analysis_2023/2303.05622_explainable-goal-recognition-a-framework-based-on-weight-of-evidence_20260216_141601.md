# Explainable Goal Recognition: A Framework Based on Weight of Evidence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05622
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05622
- Reference count: 10
- This paper presents a framework for generating human-like explanations for goal recognition (GR) systems based on the Weight of Evidence (WoE) concept.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the XGR model, a framework that generates human-like explanations for goal recognition systems using the Weight of Evidence (WoE) concept from information theory. The model provides contrastive explanations answering 'why?' and 'why not?' questions by ranking observations according to their WoE scores. The framework was evaluated computationally on eight GR benchmark domains, showing minimal overhead (0.2%-45% increase) with most time spent on counterfactual planning. A human study comparing XGR explanations to human-generated ground truth showed agreement in 73%-93% of scenarios, while a second study found that XGR explanations improved task prediction and trust in the GR agent compared to no explanations.

## Method Summary
The XGR model computes Weight of Evidence scores for each observation in a sequence, ranking them to identify the most and least supportive observations for goal hypotheses. The framework uses a GR algorithm to generate predicted goals and counterfactual goals, then calculates WoE scores based on posterior probabilities. Observational markers (highest WoE) and counterfactual markers (lowest WoE) are selected to form explanations. For "why not" explanations, the system generates counterfactual plans showing alternative actions that would have been taken for the counterfactual goal. The model was evaluated on benchmark domains and through human studies comparing generated explanations to human annotations.

## Key Results
- Computational overhead of 0.2%-45% increase, with most time spent on counterfactual planning
- Human agreement of 73%-93% between XGR explanations and human-generated ground truth
- XGR explanations improved task prediction and trust compared to no explanations in user studies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Weight of Evidence (WoE) framework effectively explains goal recognition decisions by quantifying the strength of evidence for one goal hypothesis versus another.
- Mechanism: The framework calculates log-odds ratios that measure how much each observation supports one goal over a counterfactual goal, allowing the system to rank observations by their explanatory power.
- Core assumption: Observations can be meaningfully compared across different goal hypotheses through probabilistic reasoning, and human users can interpret these log-odds based comparisons.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer -> WoE calculation engine -> Explanation selection module -> Counterfactual planning component -> Output formatter
- Critical path: GR output → WoE computation → Explanation selection → Counterfactual planning → User presentation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost vs. explanation quality: Using planners for counterfactuals adds overhead but improves explanation quality
  - Simplicity vs. completeness: Selecting single markers is simpler but may miss multi-observation explanations
  - Domain independence vs. optimization: Generic WoE approach works across domains but misses domain-specific optimizations
- Failure signatures:
  - WoE values clustering around zero indicates observations don't discriminate between goals
  - High computational overhead suggests planner inefficiency or domain complexity issues
  - Low human agreement suggests mismatch between automated and human explanation preferences
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test WoE calculation on a simple deterministic domain with clear goal distinctions to verify basic functionality
  2. Evaluate explanation selection by comparing top-ranked observations against human judgments on simple scenarios
  3. Measure computational overhead by timing explanation generation across domains of increasing complexity

## Limitations
- The framework assumes reliable GR probability estimates, which may not hold in noisy or uncertain environments
- The single-observation explanation selection may miss complex multi-observation explanations that humans naturally provide
- The counterfactual planning approach may be computationally prohibitive in domains with large state spaces or complex action effects

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the core WoE-based explanation generation mechanism, as the mathematical framework is well-established
- **Medium confidence** in human-centered evaluation, due to limited corpus support and sparse methodology details
- **High confidence** in computational efficiency claims based on reported overhead percentages
