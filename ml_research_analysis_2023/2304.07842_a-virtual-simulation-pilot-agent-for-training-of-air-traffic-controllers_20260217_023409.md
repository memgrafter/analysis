# A Virtual Simulation-Pilot Agent for Training of Air Traffic Controllers

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive virtual simulation-pilot system designed to enhance air traffic controller training. The system integrates automatic speech recognition (ASR), natural language understanding (NLU), and text-to-speech (TTS) modules using state-of-the-art AI tools. The modular design allows for easy adaptation to different training scenarios and airports, addressing the need for more effective and flexible ATCo training methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed system employs a modular architecture with three main components: ASR using Wav2Vec 2.0, NLU using BERT, and TTS using FastSpeech2. The ASR module achieves low word error rates (WER) on ATCo audio data, while the NLU module demonstrates high accuracy in extracting callsigns, commands, and values from controller communications. The system also incorporates contextual data like surveillance information to improve callsign recognition accuracy.

## Key Results
- ASR achieves word error rates (WER) as low as 5.5% on high-quality ATCo audio and 15.9% on low-quality audio
- NLU module achieves F1-scores of 97.1%, 82.0%, and 87.2% for callsign, command, and value extraction, respectively
- System incorporates contextual data like surveillance information to improve callsign recognition accuracy to over 96%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system's effectiveness stems from its integration of state-of-the-art AI modules and its ability to leverage contextual information. The use of advanced ASR (Wav2Vec 2.0) allows for accurate transcription of ATCo speech, while the NLU module (BERT) excels at understanding the structure and meaning of controller communications. The incorporation of surveillance data enhances the system's ability to correctly identify callsigns, a critical component of ATCo-pilot communications.

## Foundational Learning
1. Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)
   - Why needed: To accurately transcribe spoken commands from air traffic controllers
   - Quick check: Low WER on ATCo audio data

2. Natural Language Understanding (NLU)
   - Why needed: To extract and interpret key information from controller communications
   - Quick check: High F1-scores for callsign, command, and value extraction

3. Text-to-Speech (TTS)
   - Why needed: To generate realistic pilot responses for training scenarios
   - Quick check: Natural-sounding audio output

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
ASR -> NLU -> TTS

Critical Path:
1. ASR transcribes ATCo speech
2. NLU interprets transcribed text and extracts key information
3. TTS generates appropriate pilot response

Design Tradeoffs:
- Modular design allows for easy adaptation but may introduce integration challenges
- High ASR performance on high-quality audio, but significant degradation on low-quality audio

Failure Signatures:
- Increased WER in noisy or low-quality audio conditions
- Varying NLU performance across different command types

First 3 Experiments:
1. Test ASR performance on audio with varying quality and background noise
2. Evaluate NLU accuracy across a wider range of command types and scenarios
3. Assess system performance in a simulated multi-aircraft training scenario

## Limitations
- ASR performance highly dependent on audio quality, with WER increasing significantly on low-quality recordings
- NLU module shows varying performance across different command types
- Limited evaluation on small dataset may not capture full variability of real-world ATCo-pilot communications

## Confidence
- ASR performance: High confidence
- NLU accuracy: Medium confidence
- System integration and adaptability: Low confidence
