# Improved Stock Price Movement Classification Using News Articles Based on Embeddings and Label Smoothing

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a significant advancement in stock price movement classification using news articles, achieving a 10% improvement over existing models with an 80.7% test accuracy. The methodology is well-grounded in established deep learning techniques, and the experimental results are compelling. However, several uncertainties and limitations should be considered when evaluating these findings.

## Method Summary
The authors improve stock price movement classification using news articles by applying deep learning regularization and optimization techniques to a bidirectional GRU-based model. They incorporate weight decay, batch normalization, dropout, and label smoothing, and introduce dual-phase training to handle validation accuracy fluctuations from batch normalization. Experiments on the R&B dataset show an average test accuracy of 80.7%, a 10% improvement over existing models. Ablation studies indicate batch normalization and label smoothing are most effective, improving accuracy by 6.0% and 3.4% respectively.

## Key Results
- Achieved 80.7% test accuracy, representing a 10% improvement over existing models
- Batch normalization and label smoothing are most effective regularization techniques, improving accuracy by 6.0% and 3.4% respectively
- Dual-phase training successfully addresses validation accuracy fluctuations from batch normalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed approach leverages deep learning regularization techniques to improve the robustness and generalization of the bidirectional GRU model. By incorporating weight decay, batch normalization, dropout, and label smoothing, the model becomes less prone to overfitting and better at capturing the complex relationships between news articles and stock price movements. The dual-phase training strategy specifically addresses the challenge of validation accuracy fluctuations caused by batch normalization, leading to more stable and reliable model performance.
