# Large language models predict human sensory judgments across six modalities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01308
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01308
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: LLM-generated similarity judgments strongly correlate with human psychophysical data across six perceptual domains

## Executive Summary
This work presents a novel approach for distilling psychophysical knowledge from large language models (LLMs) by eliciting similarity judgments over perceptual stimuli. The authors use GPT-3 to generate pairwise similarity scores across six perceptual domains (color, pitch, vocal consonants, loudness, timbre, taste) through carefully crafted prompts. These similarity judgments are analyzed using multidimensional scaling to uncover the underlying psychological organization of the stimuli. The results show that the LLM-based judgments strongly correlate with human data and successfully recover well-known psychophysical structures like the color wheel and pitch spiral.

## Method Summary
The paper uses prompt engineering to elicit similarity judgments from GPT-3 and GPT-4 for pairs of stimuli across six perceptual domains. Prompts provide physical or semantic specifications of stimulus pairs and constrain the model's response to be a numerical similarity score. The LLM-generated similarity matrices are then analyzed using multidimensional scaling (MDS) to uncover underlying psychological organization, and Pearson correlation coefficients are computed between LLM and human similarity matrices.

## Key Results
- LLM-generated similarity judgments strongly correlate with human data across six perceptual domains
- MDS analysis of LLM similarity matrices successfully recovers well-known psychophysical structures (color wheel, pitch spiral)
- The approach demonstrates how combining machine models with classic cognitive paradigms can provide insights into perception and language

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- GPT3's next-word prediction training on massive text corpora captures enough statistical regularities to infer perceptual similarity across domains
- The model learns distributional patterns linking words describing perceptual stimuli to underlying perceptual structures
- Core assumption: Text corpus encodes sufficient perceptual grounding for learned representations to reflect real-world perceptual similarity

### Mechanism 2
- Carefully engineered prompts constrain GPT3's output to produce numerical similarity ratings
- Prompt template includes explicit instructions and examples that guide the model toward generating similarity scores on a specified scale
- Core assumption: Model's in-context learning capabilities allow it to adapt to task format when provided with appropriate examples

### Mechanism 3
- Multidimensional scaling (MDS) applied to GPT3 similarity matrices reveals underlying perceptual structures
- Similarity judgments preserve relational structure necessary for MDS to uncover meaningful perceptual geometry
- Core assumption: Similarity judgments produced by the model reflect real perceptual relationships despite being machine-generated

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Prompt generation module -> LLM interface -> Data processing pipeline -> Analysis module -> Visualization tools

- Critical path:
  1. Design domain-specific prompt templates with examples
  2. Iterate through all stimulus pairs, collecting 10 ratings per pair from GPT3
  3. Aggregate ratings into similarity matrices
  4. Apply MDS to extract geometric representations
  5. Compute correlation with human similarity matrices
  6. Visualize results and interpret divergences

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Same few-shot examples across all pairs ensures consistency but may limit adaptation to domain-specific nuances
  - Collecting 10 ratings per pair increases reliability but also computational cost and API usage
  - Choice between metric and non-metric MDS affects interpretability of resulting embeddings

- Failure signatures:
  - Low correlation with human data across all domains suggests prompt engineering approach is not effectively eliciting perceptual judgments
  - High variance in similarity ratings for same pair across repetitions indicates instability in model's outputs
  - MDS solutions lacking clear geometric organization suggest similarity matrix lacks meaningful structure

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate prompt template by testing on small subset of color pairs and comparing model outputs to known perceptual similarities
  2. Test effect of varying number of in-context examples on consistency and quality of similarity ratings
  3. Compare similarity matrices generated with different GPT3 model versions to assess model sensitivity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does extracted psychophysical knowledge from LLMs represent genuine understanding or just learned patterns to generate plausible responses?
- Basis: The paper states "the extent to which the derived signal from the language model can be called 'knowledge' should be used with caution and remains to be seen"
- Why unresolved: Unclear whether model truly understands perceptual concepts or is simply mimicking human-like responses based on patterns in training data
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic probing of model's representations from different angles, such as using different psychophysical metrics or asking for explicit explanations of similarity judgments

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do LLMs capture and represent secondary features of similarity judgments beyond primary symmetric and aggregate component?
- Basis: Paper mentions this as limitation, stating "one could also explore whether the model also captures secondary features such as response variability, asymmetric relations and respects"
- Why unresolved: Study focused on primary symmetric and aggregate component, but unclear if model also captures more nuanced aspects of similarity judgments
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing model's responses for variability, asymmetry, and other secondary features of similarity judgments

### Open Question 3
- Question: To what extent can LLM-based approach be applied to non-perceptual domains like number representations and physical understanding?
- Basis: Paper suggests this as direction for future research, stating "our approach could be equally applied to other non-perceptual domains such as number representations and physical understanding"
- Why unresolved: While study focused on perceptual domains, unclear how well approach would generalize to other cognitive domains
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying LLM-based approach to non-perceptual domains and comparing results with human data

## Limitations

- Results may not generalize to perceptual concepts that are less frequently discussed in text or have inconsistent linguistic representations
- Approach assumes similarity in language use translates to similarity in perception, which may not hold for all perceptual domains or cultural contexts
- Paper acknowledges uncertainty about whether extracted knowledge represents genuine understanding versus learned response patterns

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Correlation between LLM-generated similarity judgments and human data (Pearson r values reported)
- **Medium confidence**: Recovery of known psychophysical structures (color wheel, pitch spiral) from MDS solutions
- **Medium confidence**: Prompt engineering methodology effectively constraining model outputs to numerical similarity ratings

## Next Checks

1. Test approach on perceptual domains with sparse linguistic descriptions to assess limits of corpus-based grounding assumption
2. Compare similarity judgments across different language models with varying training corpus sizes to establish relationship between corpus coverage and judgment quality
3. Validate approach with human perceptual experts to assess whether recovered structures align with established psychophysical theories beyond specific examples tested
