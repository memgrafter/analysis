# DSE-TTS: Dual Speaker Embedding for Cross-Lingual Text-to-Speech

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14145
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14145
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: DSE-TTS achieves over 0.3 higher nativeness MOS scores and lower word error rates across all language combinations compared to SANE-TTS in cross-lingual synthesis

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DSE-TTS, a dual speaker embedding framework for cross-lingual text-to-speech synthesis that separates linguistic speaking style from speaker timbre. The approach uses vector-quantized acoustic features with reduced speaker information and two distinct speaker embeddings - one for the acoustic model to learn language-specific speaking styles and another for the vocoder to maintain target speaker identity. Experiments across Mandarin, English, German, and Spanish demonstrate significant improvements in nativeness while preserving speaker similarity, outperforming the state-of-the-art SANE-TTS system.

## Method Summary
DSE-TTS employs a dual speaker embedding architecture where vector-quantized acoustic features from wav2vec 2.0 serve as input to the acoustic model. The framework uses two separate speaker embeddings: one fed to the acoustic model (txt2vec) to learn linguistic speaking style conditioned on both speaker and language IDs, and another integrated into the vocoder (vec2wav) to mimic target speaker timbre. Pitch shifting is applied to align native speaker pitch distributions with target speaker characteristics. The model is trained on 4 languages (Mandarin, English, Spanish, German) with IPA phoneme normalization, using 200 epochs for txt2vec and 100 epochs for vec2wav.

## Key Results
- DSE-TTS achieves over 0.3 higher nativeness MOS scores compared to SANE-TTS across all language combinations
- Lower word error rates are observed for cross-lingual synthesis tasks
- Speaker similarity is maintained while significantly improving nativeness in cross-lingual speech

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: VQ acoustic features contain less speaker identity than mel-spectrograms
- Mechanism: Vector-quantized speech representations have coarser granularity than mel-spectrograms, reducing the amount of speaker-dependent information that can be extracted
- Core assumption: VQ features capture linguistic information while discarding fine-grained speaker characteristics
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "VQ acoustic feature contains less speaker information than mel-spectrogram"
  - [section 3.3]: "VQ features have significantly less speaker information, leading to a lower accuracy rate than the mel-spectrogram"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct comparisons found in related papers
- Break condition: If VQ features still retain enough speaker information for high-accuracy classification, the disentanglement benefit disappears

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Dual speaker embedding enables separate modeling of linguistic style and speaker timbre
- Mechanism: One embedding controls linguistic characteristics in the acoustic model, while another controls speaker timbre in the vocoder, preventing cross-contamination
- Core assumption: The acoustic model can focus purely on linguistic features when speaker information is removed from the input representation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "one embedding is fed to the acoustic model to learn the linguistic speaking style, while the other one is integrated into the vocoder to mimic the target speaker's timbre"
  - [section 2.3]: "VQ-based TTS model naturally learns how to speak different languages in a native way with the timbre of a non-native speaker"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related work focuses on single embedding approaches
- Break condition: If the two embeddings become correlated during training, the separation benefit is lost

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pitch shifting compensates for native speaker characteristics when synthesizing cross-lingual speech
- Mechanism: The pitch distribution of the native speaker is shifted to match the target speaker's pitch characteristics before vocoder processing
- Core assumption: Pitch is a separable acoustic feature that can be modified independently without affecting other speaker characteristics
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3]: "to bring the timbre closer to the target speaker while doing cross-lingual synthesis, we shift the distribution of the native speaker's pitches predicted by txt2vec to match the pitch of the target speaker"

  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in related work
- Break condition: If pitch shifting introduces artifacts or unnatural prosody, the quality benefit disappears

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text encoder → IPA phoneme sequence → Text encoder embedding → Acoustic model (txt2vec) with speaker and language IDs → VQ feature predictor → Auxiliary controller → Length regulator → Vocoder (vec2wav) with X-vector embedding → Pitch-shifted auxiliary features → Speech output

- Critical path: Text input → IPA conversion → Text encoder → Speaker/language embeddings → VQ feature prediction → Duration modeling → Auxiliary feature prediction → Pitch shifting → Vocoder → Speech output

- Design tradeoffs: Using VQ features instead of mel-spectrograms reduces speaker information but may also reduce overall acoustic detail; dual embeddings increase model complexity but enable better disentanglement

- Failure signatures: Low speaker similarity scores indicate embedding separation issues; high WER suggests poor linguistic modeling; unnatural prosody indicates pitch shifting problems

## Limitations

- Limited architectural details for txt2vec and vec2wav components make exact reproduction challenging
- Only 4 languages tested, limiting generalizability to other language families
- No comparison to other disentanglement methods that could achieve similar results

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core hypothesis that dual speaker embeddings can separately model linguistic style and speaker timbre is supported by the significant performance improvements (0.3+ NMOS increase) over SANE-TTS across all language combinations.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that VQ features contain less speaker information than mel-spectrograms is supported by the reported speaker classification accuracy rates but lacks direct comparative evidence within this work.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of pitch shifting for cross-lingual synthesis is demonstrated empirically but the underlying mechanism could benefit from more detailed analysis of pitch distribution matching quality.
