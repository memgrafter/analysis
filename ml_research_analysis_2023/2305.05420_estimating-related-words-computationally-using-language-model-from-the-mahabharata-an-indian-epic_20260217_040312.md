# Estimating related words computationally using language model from the Mahabharata -- an Indian epic

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.05420
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05420
- **Reference count:** 21
- **Primary result:** Pipeline built to extract 25,794-word vocabulary and compute word2vec-based semantic relationships for sentiment-free literary analysis and personality profiling.

## Executive Summary
This study presents an NLP pipeline for computationally extracting semantically related words from the Mahabharata, a large Sanskrit epic, enabling sentiment-free literary analysis. The approach preprocesses the text, applies word2vec, and generates similarity scores between target words and their top related terms. The work aims to support downstream applications in personality profiling and management insights by providing scalable, language-model-based word relationship extraction.

## Method Summary
The authors developed an NLP pipeline that preprocesses the Mahabharata text through cleaning, tokenization, and normalization. They then apply word2vec to generate 100-dimensional word vectors for a vocabulary of 25,794 words, computing cosine similarity scores between target words and their top five related terms. The pipeline is designed for sentiment-free analysis and is intended to support downstream applications such as personality profiling and management insights.

## Key Results
- Built a vocabulary of 25,794 words from the Mahabharata corpus.
- Generated 100-dimensional word vectors using word2vec.
- Produced similarity scores (e.g., “Arjuna” vs. “Partha” with cosine similarity 0.8606) between target words and top five related terms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The pipeline leverages word2vec’s ability to capture semantic relationships in large text corpora by learning distributed representations of words. Preprocessing steps ensure that the input text is clean and normalized, which improves the quality of the learned embeddings. The cosine similarity metric then quantifies semantic relatedness between words, enabling scalable, sentiment-free analysis of literary content.

## Foundational Learning
- **Text Preprocessing:** Cleaning, tokenizing, and normalizing text is essential to remove noise and ensure consistent input for embedding models.
- **Word Embeddings:** word2vec learns dense vector representations that capture semantic relationships between words based on their co-occurrence patterns.
- **Cosine Similarity:** Measures the angle between word vectors, providing a normalized metric for semantic relatedness.
- **Sentiment-Free Analysis:** Focusing on semantic relationships without sentiment allows for broader applications in personality and narrative analysis.
- **Scalability:** Large vocabularies and vector dimensions enable the pipeline to handle extensive literary corpora.
- **Downstream Profiling:** Extracted semantic relationships can inform personality profiling and management insights by mapping narrative roles and associations.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map:** Text Preprocessing -> Word2Vec Training -> Cosine Similarity Computation -> Related Word Extraction
- **Critical Path:** Clean text → Tokenize → Normalize → Train word2vec → Compute similarity → Extract top-5 related words
- **Design Tradeoffs:** Larger vocabulary and higher vector dimensions increase semantic coverage but require more computational resources; sentiment-free focus broadens applicability but may miss nuanced emotional context.
- **Failure Signatures:** Low vocabulary coverage may indicate preprocessing errors or domain-specific terminology; poor similarity scores may result from insufficient training data or inappropriate vector dimensions.
- **First Experiments:**
  1. Verify preprocessing output: check token count and vocabulary size.
  2. Validate word2vec model: confirm vector dimensionality and training convergence.
  3. Test similarity computation: compute and inspect cosine similarity for known related word pairs.
