# Examining the Causal Effect of First Names on Language Models: The Case of Social Commonsense Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01117
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01117
- Reference count: 12
- Key outcome: Name frequency has a direct effect on prediction agreement in language models, with less frequent names producing more divergent outputs that persist even after fine-tuning.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how first names influence language model predictions in social commonsense reasoning tasks. The authors propose a causal experimental framework to measure both direct and indirect effects of name characteristics (frequency and gender) on model outputs. Through analysis of fine-tuned models including GPT2, BERT, and RoBERTa, they demonstrate that name frequency significantly affects prediction agreement, with less frequent names yielding more divergent results. The research reveals that this effect persists even after fine-tuning, suggesting fundamental embedding and activation pattern differences rather than simple accuracy issues.

## Method Summary
The study employs a causal experimental framework to analyze how name characteristics affect language model predictions. Using the SocialIQA dataset, the authors create name-controlled templates where names are systematically varied based on frequency (MOST vs LEAST frequent) and gender. They apply causal inference techniques to estimate direct effects on accuracy and agreement, while also conducting internal analysis of embeddings and neuron activations. The models are evaluated both in their original and fine-tuned states to assess the impact of training on name-related effects.

## Key Results
- Name frequency has a statistically significant direct effect on prediction agreement, with less frequent names producing more divergent outputs
- This frequency effect persists even after fine-tuning, while accuracy effects become non-significant post-fine-tuning
- Less frequent names show lower embedding similarity across model layers and distinct neuron activation patterns compared to frequent names
- The effect is consistent across multiple model architectures including GPT2, BERT, and RoBERTa

## Limitations
- The causal framework relies on name frequency and gender as primary confounders, which may not capture all relevant name-related effects
- Analysis focuses on limited model architectures (GPT2, BERT, RoBERTa) and commonsense reasoning tasks, potentially limiting generalizability
- Template-based input generation may not fully represent natural name usage patterns in real-world scenarios

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The direct effect of name frequency on prediction agreement is well-supported by consistent statistical significance across multiple model configurations and validation methods.
- **Medium confidence**: The embedding similarity findings are compelling but rely on specific similarity metrics that may not capture all aspects of embedding quality.
- **Medium confidence**: The neuron activation analysis provides interesting insights but the clustering patterns could be influenced by factors beyond name frequency.
