# Spatial Functa: Scaling Functa to ImageNet Classification and Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03130
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03130
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Spatial functa achieve 90.3% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 80.7% on ImageNet-1k 256x256, with FID of 8.5 for conditional generation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitation of vector-valued functa in scaling to complex datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1k. While vector functa achieve high reconstruction quality, they struggle on downstream tasks due to their global latent representations that don't capture local spatial information. The authors propose spatial functa, which use spatially arranged latent representations of neural fields, enabling features at each spatial index to capture local signal information. This approach overcomes the limitations of vector-valued functa and scales to ImageNet-1k at 256x256 resolution.

## Method Summary
Spatial functa use a grid of smaller feature vectors instead of a global latent vector, where each dimension captures information relevant to a specific spatial region. The approach uses meta-learning to learn shared parameters and per-field latent representations, with nearest neighbor or linear interpolation to extract position-dependent modulation vectors. The framework is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1k for both classification (using Transformer models) and unconditional/conditional generation (using UNet-based diffusion models).

## Key Results
- CIFAR-10: Spatial functa achieve 90.3% top-1 accuracy (vs 68.3% for vector functa) and FID of 16.5 for unconditional generation (vs 134.8 for vector functa)
- ImageNet-1k 256x256: Spatial functa achieve 80.7% top-1 accuracy (competitive with ViT-B/16) and FID of 8.5 for conditional generation (close to Latent Diffusion's 7.8)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Spatial functa overcome the limitations of vector-valued functa by encoding local spatial information in each latent dimension.
- Mechanism: Instead of using a global latent vector that encodes features spanning the entire image, spatial functa use a grid of smaller feature vectors where each dimension captures information relevant to a specific spatial region. This locality allows architectures like Transformers and UNets to extract useful features for downstream tasks.
- Core assumption: The information needed for downstream tasks is spatially localized rather than global.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We then propose spatial functa, which overcome these limitations by using spatially arranged latent representations of neural fields"
  - [section 3] "We hypothesize that the stark performance differences between functa on CelebA / ShapeNet and CIFAR-10 arise due to differing complexities of the tasks at hand."
- Break condition: If downstream tasks require global context that cannot be adequately captured by local spatial features, or if the spatial arrangement doesn't match the task's inductive biases.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The nearest neighbor interpolation scheme performs better than linear interpolation for spatial functa.
- Mechanism: Nearest neighbor interpolation creates independent c-dimensional latent features per patch, allowing each patch to have its own modulation vector. This independence may help preserve local information without mixing features across patch boundaries.
- Core assumption: Local independence of patches is beneficial for downstream task performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1] "In general, nearest neighbor interpolation tends to perform better than linear interpolation for downstream tasks."
- Break condition: If the task requires smooth interpolation between patches or if the patch boundaries need to be mixed for proper feature extraction.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Higher spatial dimensionality of latent representations (while keeping total dimensionality fixed) improves downstream task performance.
- Mechanism: More spatial dimensions means each individual latent feature vector captures information about a smaller region, increasing the locality and specificity of the features extracted for downstream tasks.
- Core assumption: Increasing spatial resolution of latent features improves the quality of extracted features for downstream tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1] "Moreover, the higher the spatial latent dimensionality (while keeping total number of latent dimensions fixed), the better the performance on both downstream tasks."
- Break condition: If computational/memory constraints prevent using higher spatial dimensions, or if diminishing returns occur beyond a certain spatial resolution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Meta-learning loop (outer: shared parameters, inner: per-field latents) -> Spatial latent representation (grid of feature vectors) -> Interpolation mechanism (nearest neighbor or linear) -> Neural field (SIREN MLP with positional encodings) -> Downstream task architectures (Transformer/UNet)

- Critical path: Meta-learning → Spatial latent extraction → Downstream task training

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Spatial dimension vs. computational cost
  - Nearest neighbor vs. linear interpolation for feature locality vs. smoothness
  - Fixed vs. learned coordinate representations in SIREN

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor reconstruction quality despite high PSNR (memorization in diffusion models)
  - Inconsistent patterns across patches (interpolation issues)
  - Suboptimal performance compared to vector functa (incorrect spatial arrangement)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replicate CIFAR-10 classification results with spatial functa vs vector functa to verify performance improvement
  2. Test different interpolation schemes (nearest neighbor vs linear) on CIFAR-10 to confirm which performs better
  3. Vary spatial dimensions of latent representations while keeping total dimensionality fixed to observe impact on downstream task performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Why do the original vector-valued functa representations struggle to extract useful features for downstream tasks on complex datasets like CIFAR-10, despite achieving high reconstruction quality?
2. How does the performance of spatial functa scale with increasing dataset complexity beyond ImageNet-1k, particularly for video and 3D scene datasets?
3. What is the optimal spatial arrangement of latent features for different types of downstream tasks and dataset characteristics?

## Limitations
- The paper lacks detailed architectural specifications for both the SIREN models used in meta-learning and the downstream task architectures
- Performance scaling to higher resolutions beyond 256x256 and more complex datasets remains untested
- The fundamental reason why global latent representations fail on complex tasks is not fully explained

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Spatial functa significantly outperform vector-valued functa on downstream tasks (90.3% vs 68.3% on CIFAR-10 classification, FID 16.5 vs 134.8 for unconditional generation)
- **Medium Confidence**: Nearest neighbor interpolation performs better than linear interpolation for downstream tasks
- **Low Confidence**: Optimal spatial dimensionality scaling with higher spatial dimensions improving performance

## Next Checks
1. Systematically test nearest neighbor vs linear interpolation across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-32, ImageNet-64) with varying spatial resolutions to quantify when and why nearest neighbor performs better.
2. Evaluate spatial functa performance across a broader range of spatial dimensions (e.g., s=2,4,8,16) on CIFAR-10 while monitoring both downstream task performance and computational costs to identify optimal scaling tradeoffs.
3. Extend the ImageNet-1k experiments to 512x512 resolution to test whether spatial functa maintain their performance advantages on higher-resolution imagery, and whether new architectural modifications become necessary.
