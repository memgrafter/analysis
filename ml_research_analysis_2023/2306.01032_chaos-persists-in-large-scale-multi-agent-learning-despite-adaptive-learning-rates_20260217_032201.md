# Chaos persists in large-scale multi-agent learning despite adaptive learning rates

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01032
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01032
- Reference count: 40
- This work proves that chaos persists even with adaptive learning rates in large population games with only two strategies.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether adaptive learning rate schemes can prevent chaotic behavior in large-scale multi-agent learning systems. The authors analyze the Multiplicative Weights Update (MWU) algorithm with a regret-driven adaptive learning rate that increases when agents' actions appear similar on average and decreases when one action is clearly better. Despite adaptive rates being known to improve convergence in small games, the paper proves that chaos persists even with adaptive rates in large population games with only two strategies. The key insight is that the non-autonomous dynamical system still exhibits volume expansion and turbulent sets, leading to Li-Yorke chaos.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze MWU dynamics with adaptive learning rates based on pseudo-regret in non-atomic congestion games with two strategies. They establish uniform convergence properties for initialization sets confined to the interior of the strategy space and connect the dynamics to symbolic dynamics to prove Li-Yorke chaos. The analysis overcomes the challenge of non-autonomous dynamics by showing that iterates converge uniformly to a fixed chaotic map, preserving the chaotic properties of the limit system. The method involves proving absorption into a common set, establishing volume expansion, and constructing turbulent sets through symbolic dynamics reduction.

## Key Results
- Adaptive learning rates based on regret-like measures do not prevent chaotic dynamics in large congestion games
- Uniform convergence of varying-step MWU maps to a fixed chaotic map ensures chaos persists despite non-autonomous dynamics
- Volume expansion in neighborhoods of the mixed equilibrium ensures chaotic behavior for sufficiently high adaptive learning rates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Adaptive learning rates based on regret-like measures do not prevent chaotic dynamics in large congestion games.
- **Mechanism:** When agents adjust their learning rates based on the average cost difference between two strategies, the resulting non-autonomous dynamical system still exhibits volume expansion and turbulent sets, leading to Li-Yorke chaos.
- **Core assumption:** The adaptive learning rate function \( g(r_n) \) is continuous, bounded, and peaks at zero pseudo-regret.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "the paper proves that chaos persists even with adaptive rates in large population games with only two strategies."
  - [section 4] "Thanks to F(a), while learning rate may vary over iterations and initializations, there exists a set \( \Delta \) –the closure of all perpetual sets F(an) –to which our dynamics eventually will be absorbed."
  - [corpus] "Weak evidence: No direct corpus citations about adaptive rates preventing chaos; related work focuses on fixed rates or reinforcement learning."
- **Break condition:** If the learning rate adaptation scheme converges to a constant value less than or equal to the threshold for chaotic behavior in fixed-rate systems, chaos would be avoided.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Uniform convergence of the sequence of varying-step MWU maps to a fixed chaotic map ensures chaos persists.
- **Mechanism:** Despite non-autonomous dynamics, the iterates converge uniformly to the fixed learning rate map \( f(x, a^*, b) \), preserving the chaotic properties of the limit system.
- **Core assumption:** The learning rate sequence \( a_n(x_0) \) converges uniformly to \( a^*(x_0) = g(0) \) for all initializations in the interior of the strategy space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4] "We establish uniform convergence guarantees for initialization sets that are confined to the interior of the strategy space."
  - [section 5] "Our proof strategy entails assigning every binary sequence to a different initialization such that scrambled binary strings correspond to scrambled initializations."
  - [corpus] "Weak evidence: No direct corpus citations about uniform convergence in non-autonomous MWU systems; related work on fixed rates only."
- **Break condition:** If the learning rate adaptation causes non-uniform convergence or collapses to a low constant rate, the uniform convergence property fails and chaos may not persist.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Volume expansion in neighborhoods of the mixed equilibrium ensures chaotic behavior.
- **Mechanism:** For sufficiently high adaptive learning rates, any neighborhood of the mixed equilibrium expands to cover the perpetual set, leading to complex attractors and chaotic trajectories.
- **Core assumption:** The adaptive learning rate can reach values high enough to enable the volume expansion property proven for fixed-rate systems.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4] "Built upon the full range of the developed machinery of this section, we will strengthen the above volume expansion result by showing that amin could be actually substituted by any \( \tilde{a} = a^* - \epsilon \) for any sufficient small \( \epsilon > 0 \) such that \( a^* - \epsilon > a_{min} > z_b \)."
  - [section 3] "We prove that any interval around b is sufficient to eventually cover F(a)."
  - [corpus] "No direct corpus evidence; volume expansion is a standard technique in chaos theory but not explicitly applied to adaptive MWU."
- **Break condition:** If the adaptive learning rate scheme never allows the rate to reach the threshold needed for volume expansion, the system may converge to stable equilibria instead of exhibiting chaos.

  - System converges to stable equilibria (learning rate never high enough)
  - Learning rate adaptation causes uniform convergence to a non-chaotic fixed map
