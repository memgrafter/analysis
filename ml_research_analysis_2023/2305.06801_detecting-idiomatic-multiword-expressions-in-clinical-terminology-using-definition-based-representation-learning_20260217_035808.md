# Detecting Idiomatic Multiword Expressions in Clinical Terminology using Definition-Based Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06801
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06801
- Reference count: 4
- BioLORD achieves 93% AUC in detecting idiomatic multiword expressions in clinical terminology

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces BioLORD, a definition-based semantic model designed to identify idiomatic multiword expressions (MWEs) in clinical terminology. The core innovation lies in computing a self-explainability score by measuring the semantic similarity between an MWE and a weighted average of its constituent word representations. BioLORD employs contrastive learning to align entity names with their definitions, enabling corpus-free detection of idiomatic expressions. When evaluated on the UMLS ontology, BioLORD significantly outperforms existing biomedical models like SapBERT and CODER, demonstrating the effectiveness of definition-based representation learning for clinical terminology analysis.

## Method Summary
BioLORD is a definition-based semantic model that identifies idiomatic multiword expressions (MWEs) in clinical terminology. The model computes a self-explainability score by measuring the similarity between the semantic representation of an MWE and a weighted average of its constituent word representations. BioLORD is trained using contrastive learning to align entity names with their definitions, enabling the model to learn meaningful semantic representations without requiring large external corpora. This approach allows ontology translators to prioritize challenging MWEs based on their self-explainability scores, with idiomatic expressions typically scoring lower due to their non-compositional nature.

## Key Results
- BioLORD achieves 93% AUC in detecting idiomatic multiword expressions, significantly outperforming SapBERT (84% AUC) and CODER (65% AUC)
- Approximately half of idiomatic MWEs are contained in the top 5% of self-explainability score outliers
- The corpus-free approach enables effective prioritization of challenging MWEs for ontology translators

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of BioLORD stems from its definition-based representation learning approach, which leverages the semantic information contained in entity definitions to create meaningful representations. By training with contrastive learning to align entity names with their definitions, the model captures the inherent meaning of clinical terms without relying on external corpora. The self-explainability score works because idiomatic expressions, by definition, cannot be fully explained by their constituent words, resulting in lower similarity scores compared to compositional expressions. This mechanism effectively identifies expressions where the whole meaning differs from the sum of its parts, which is precisely the challenge in clinical terminology translation and standardization.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Entity Name -> Definition Encoder -> Contrastive Loss -> Semantic Embeddings -> Self-Explainability Score

Critical Path: The model's effectiveness depends on the quality of definition encoding and the contrastive learning objective. The self-explainability score computation is straightforward once embeddings are learned, making the contrastive learning stage the most critical component for overall performance.

Design Tradeoffs: The definition-based approach sacrifices potential contextual information from large corpora in exchange for corpus-free operation and direct alignment with clinical terminology semantics. This tradeoff makes the model more practical for specialized domains where definitions are available but large annotated corpora may not exist.

Failure Signatures: Poor performance may manifest when definitions are missing, incomplete, or ambiguous. The model may also struggle with domain-specific jargon that appears in definitions but differs from common usage. Additionally, highly compositional expressions with unusual semantic relationships might be incorrectly classified as idiomatic.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies entirely on a single dataset (UMLS ontology), limiting generalizability to other biomedical vocabularies
- The contrastive learning approach requires paired entity names and definitions, limiting applicability to ontologies with incomplete definitions
- The self-explainability metric may not capture all types of idiomatic expressions across different linguistic constructions

## Confidence
High: BioLORD outperforms existing biomedical models (SapBERT, CODER) on the UMLS dataset for idiomatic MWE detection.
Medium: The definition-based approach generalizes well to other biomedical ontologies and clinical text beyond UMLS.
Low: The self-explainability score reliably captures all types of idiomatic expressions across different languages and cultural contexts.
