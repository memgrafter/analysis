# Variational Inference for Neyman-Scott Processes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03701
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03701
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: First VI algorithm for NSPs that outperforms MCMC in prediction performance when computational time is limited

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first variational inference (VI) algorithm for Neyman-Scott processes (NSPs), hierarchical point process models typically sampled using MCMC. The authors develop upward Neyman-Scott processes (UNSPs) and upward self-attention processes (USAPs) as variational posterior approximations, minimizing inclusive KL divergence. The key innovation is direct estimation of approximate posterior point processes without MCMC steps or gradient descent, enabling much faster sampling. Experiments on synthetic and real-world data demonstrate that the VI algorithm achieves better prediction performance than MCMC when computational time is constrained.

## Method Summary
The method constructs variational posterior point processes that mirror the generative structure of NSPs but propagate information from observations upward through the hierarchy. UNSPs and USAPs are built bottom-up from observations, with each layer's intensity depending on events from the layer below. The algorithm minimizes inclusive KL divergence between the true and approximate posteriors to obtain variational parameters. Prediction involves sampling from these approximate posterior point processes using inversion sampling, avoiding the mixing time required by MCMC. Both model parameters and variational parameters are updated iteratively using an MCEM framework with Adam optimizer.

## Key Results
- VI algorithm generates samples from approximate posterior point processes much faster than MCMC (no mixing time required)
- USAPs outperform UNSPs by capturing bidirectional temporal dependencies through self-attention
- The method achieves better prediction performance than MCMC when computational time is limited
- Both synthetic and real-world experiments validate the effectiveness of the approach

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The VI algorithm directly estimates approximate posterior point processes without MCMC steps or gradient descent, enabling faster sampling.
- Mechanism: By constructing upward variational posterior point processes that mirror the generative structure but propagate information from observations upward, the algorithm avoids MCMC mixing time.
- Core assumption: Variational approximations can capture posterior distributions well enough for accurate prediction while being computationally cheaper to sample from.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We generate samples from the approximate posterior point processes much faster than MCMC, as we can directly estimate the approximate posterior point processes without any MCMC steps or gradient descent." [section 3] "We can sample from our approximation much faster than from MCMC because mixing is not needed for the approximation."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Upward construction of variational posterior point processes allows direct inference from observations without needing to sample through the full hierarchical generative process.
- Mechanism: Approximate point processes are built bottom-up from the observation, with each layer's intensity depending on events from the layer below.
- Core assumption: Observations contain sufficient information to approximate posterior distributions of hidden layers when propagated upward.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3] "The approximate point processes are generative models...The upward approximation mechanism allows us to infer the approximate posterior distributions of the point processes directly from the observation, which can help accelerate the sampling process." [section 4] "We see that Eq. 5 has the same functional form as Eq. 2."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: USAPs outperform UNSPs because they can capture bidirectional temporal dependencies.
- Mechanism: USAPs use self-attention to encode information from both past and future events at each time point, while UNSPs only propagate information from future to past.
- Core assumption: Both past and future events influence the posterior distribution at any given time point.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4] "Moreover, for the CIFs of the approximate posterior point processes at time t, USAPs can capture the information from both the events happening before t and the events happening after t, while UNSPs can only propagate the information from the future to the past." [section 4] "Both the events happening before t and the events happening after t have an influence on the posterior point processes at time t."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Observation sequence of events -> DNSP with parameters Θ -> UNSP/USAP with parameters ΘI -> Predicted future events with uncertainty

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize model parameters and variational parameters
  2. Iteratively update parameters using MCEM framework
  3. Generate samples from variational posterior for prediction
  4. Estimate prediction metrics (RMSE for time, accuracy for type)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - UNSP vs USAP: Simpler architecture vs. better approximation quality
  - Inclusive vs exclusive KL divergence: Better uncertainty estimation vs. computational complexity
  - Fixed vs. learnable kernel parameters: Faster training vs. more expressive model

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor prediction performance despite fast sampling
  - Unstable training with rapidly diverging parameters
  - Convergence to degenerate solutions (e.g., all events clustered at single times)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the convergence rate of the variational parameters for unbounded-dimensional MCMC chains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Naesseth et al. (2020) prove the convergence of the variational parameters under some regularity conditions for MCMC with a fixed number of dimensions. However, our Markov chains have an unbounded number of dimensions. We leave the research for the convergence of our variational parameters for future work."
- Why unresolved: The convergence analysis for variational parameters in unbounded-dimensional MCMC chains is complex and not yet established in the literature.
- What evidence would resolve it: A rigorous mathematical proof or empirical study demonstrating the convergence rate of the variational parameters in unbounded-dimensional MCMC chains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of kernel function (e.g., Weibull vs. gamma) impact the performance of the VI algorithm?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "We choose the kernel function to be a Weibull kernel" and compares it with gamma kernel, stating "the Weibull kernel has the advantage over the gamma kernel that the gradients of the Weibull kernel function itself and the integral of the Weibull kernel function are both analytically available."
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides a comparison between Weibull and gamma kernels, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how different kernel choices impact the performance of the VI algorithm.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic comparison of the VI algorithm's performance using different kernel functions on various datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the USAP approach be extended to spatial point processes (SPPs) with multiple dimensions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that applying USAPs to SPPs is not straightforward, as it requires identifying "bounding" events for any point in space.
- Why unresolved: The current USAP approach relies on temporal ordering of events, which is not directly applicable to spatial point processes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A successful extension of the USAP approach to SPPs, along with empirical results demonstrating its effectiveness.

## Limitations

- The approximation quality may degrade for more complex NSP structures or different kernel functions
- Self-attention mechanism in USAPs introduces additional complexity that may not scale well to very long event sequences
- Exclusive use of Weibull kernels limits the model's ability to capture different temporal dynamics

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The VI algorithm generates samples faster than MCMC without requiring mixing time. This claim is directly supported by the mechanism described in Section 3 and the computational comparison results.

**Medium Confidence:** USAPs outperform UNSPs due to bidirectional temporal dependency capture. While the paper provides theoretical justification and experimental support, the performance gain may be context-dependent and less pronounced for certain data patterns.

**Medium Confidence:** The inclusive KL divergence minimization leads to better prediction performance than MCMC when computational time is limited. The experimental results support this claim, but the comparison is based on a specific computational budget and may vary with different constraints.
