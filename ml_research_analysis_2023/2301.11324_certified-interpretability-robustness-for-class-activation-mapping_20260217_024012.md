# Certified Interpretability Robustness for Class Activation Mapping

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CORGI, a novel algorithm that provides certifiable robustness guarantees for Class Activation Mapping (CAM) interpretability methods in deep neural networks. The method computes a lower bound on the minimum adversarial perturbation radius required to change the top-k most important pixels in the CAM map. By leveraging the CNN-Cert framework to bound the last convolutional layer outputs under perturbations, CORGI can guarantee that the top-k pixels in the CAM map remain unchanged for a given perturbation radius, offering a new level of trust in neural network interpretability.

## Method Summary
CORGI operates by first using CNN-Cert to efficiently bound the outputs of the last convolutional layer under input perturbations. These bounds are then combined with the network's weights to derive bounds on the CAM map itself. The key insight is that if the lower bound of the k-th largest CAM pixel exceeds the upper bound of the (k+1)-th largest pixel, the top-k pixels are guaranteed to remain unchanged under the specified perturbation radius. This approach provides a mathematically rigorous way to certify the stability of interpretability maps against adversarial attacks, focusing specifically on preserving the most important features identified by CAM.

## Key Results
- CORGI provides lower bounds on minimum adversarial perturbation radii needed to change top-k CAM pixels
- On GTSRB dataset, median ratio of attack bounds to CORGI bounds is only 4.7-5.0, indicating reasonably tight guarantees
- Larger CAM gaps between k-th and (k+1)-th largest pixels correlate with better robustness
- CORGI efficiently certifies interpretability robustness using CNN-Cert framework

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CORGI leverages the mathematical properties of linear bounds in convolutional neural networks to provide certified robustness guarantees. By bounding the last convolutional layer outputs under perturbations and propagating these bounds through the CAM computation, the method can establish rigorous lower bounds on perturbation radii that preserve the top-k most important features. The mechanism relies on the observation that if the k-th most important feature has a sufficiently large gap from the (k+1)-th feature, small perturbations cannot change the ranking of these features.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Input -> CNN Layers -> Last Convolutional Layer (bounded by CNN-Cert) -> CAM Computation -> Top-k Pixels

Critical Path: The certification depends on accurate bounding of the last convolutional layer, as errors here propagate through to the CAM bounds. The CAM computation itself is straightforward once layer bounds are established.

Design Tradeoffs: CORGI trades completeness (certifying all pixels) for tractability by focusing on top-k pixels only. This makes the problem computationally feasible while still providing meaningful guarantees for the most important interpretability features.

Failure Signatures: Large bound gaps indicate potential failure of the certification, particularly for pixels outside the top-k. If the k-th and (k+1)-th pixel bounds overlap significantly, the guarantee becomes weak or meaningless.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper notes that CORGI only provides guarantees for the top-k most important pixels rather than the entire CAM map, potentially limiting its practical usefulness for comprehensive interpretability analysis. The certification is specific to pixel-wise ℓ∞ perturbations and may not generalize to other perturbation types commonly used in adversarial attacks.

## Limitations
- Only certifies top-k pixels rather than entire CAM map, limiting comprehensive interpretability analysis
- Certification specific to pixel-wise ℓ∞ perturbations, may not generalize to other attack types
- Evaluation based on relatively small dataset and limited model architectures
- Computational efficiency claims based on theoretical analysis rather than extensive empirical studies
