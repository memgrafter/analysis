# Spam-T5: Benchmarking Large Language Models for Few-Shot Email Spam Detection

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.01238
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01238
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Spam-T5 achieves an average F1 score of 0.7498 across all training sample sizes, demonstrating effectiveness in few-shot learning settings for spam detection.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses email spam detection using large language models (LLMs) in few-shot learning scenarios where labeled data is limited. The authors compare various LLMs including BERT-like, Sentence Transformers, and Seq2Seq models against traditional machine learning techniques like Naïve Bayes and LightGBM across four public datasets. They introduce Spam-T5, a fine-tuned Flan-T5 model specifically designed for email spam detection. The primary finding is that LLMs, particularly Spam-T5, outperform baseline models in most scenarios, especially when training data is scarce.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three LLMs (RoBERTa, SetFit, Spam-T5) and six baseline ML models (Naïve Bayes, Logistic Regression, KNN, SVM, XGBoost, LightGBM) on four public email spam datasets. Models are trained using an 80/20 train/test split with few-shot learning using k ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, Full} samples. Preprocessing includes tokenization, stemming with Porter stemmer, and tf-idf encoding with model-specific feature counts. Hyperparameters are provided for LLMs in Table 1 and tuned for baselines in Table 2. The primary metric is F1 score, with precision and recall also reported.

## Key Results
- Spam-T5 achieves the highest average F1 score (0.7498) across all training sample sizes
- LLMs outperform baseline models in most scenarios, particularly in very-few-shot settings (4-16 samples)
- Spam-T5 demonstrates superior few-shot learning capabilities compared to RoBERTa and SetFit

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Spam-T5's few-shot performance superiority stems from instruction tuning combined with task-specific fine-tuning on email text.
- Mechanism: Flan-T5's instruction tuning allows the model to generalize from very few labeled examples by leveraging learned task decomposition patterns; subsequent fine-tuning on spam/ham data refines these patterns for the email domain.
- Core assumption: The instruction-tuned model retains generalizable task-solving capabilities that transfer to unseen classification tasks like spam detection.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Flan-T5 models have undergone instruction-fine-tuning on over 1,800 language tasks, leading to a significant enhancement in their reasoning skills and promptability."
  - [section]: "Our experimentation included the small version of the Flan-T5 model... but it demonstrated limited generalization capabilities, which is why it was excluded from this study." (implies larger Flan-T5 retained capabilities)
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; corpus papers discuss general LLM capabilities but do not isolate instruction-tuning effects.
- Break condition: If instruction tuning does not preserve generalizable task-solving patterns, few-shot gains would disappear.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Spam-T5's architecture (T5 encoder-decoder) provides better semantic understanding of email text than BERT-like models.
- Mechanism: T5's seq2seq design forces the model to generate coherent output labels ("ham"/"spam"), which reinforces the internal representation of the decision boundary compared to BERT's classifier head approach.
- Core assumption: The generation objective improves the quality of intermediate representations for classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The Flan-T5 model is a Seq2Seq model that is capable of generating textual outputs... we fine-tuned it as a new task, introducing a dedicated prefix... to correctly output either 'ham' or 'spam'."
  - [section]: Spam-T5 outperforms RoBERTa and SetFit on few-shot tasks, suggesting architectural advantage.
  - [corpus]: Weak; no corpus neighbor discusses seq2seq vs. encoder-only differences in spam detection.
- Break condition: If the generation objective does not meaningfully improve feature quality, BERT-like models would match performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Spam-T5's parameter scale (250M) relative to RoBERTa (125M) and MPNet (110M) enables better pattern capture in limited data.
- Mechanism: More parameters allow the model to store richer representations of rare spam indicators without overfitting, due to regularization from instruction tuning.
- Core assumption: The increased capacity does not cause overfitting in few-shot regimes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Spam-T5 having 250M parameters compared to RoBERTa's 125M and MPNet's 110M."
  - [section]: Spam-T5 achieves highest F1 in very-few-shot scenarios (4-16 samples).
  - [corpus]: Weak; corpus neighbors mention LLMs generally but not parameter scaling effects.
- Break condition: If regularization fails, larger models would overfit and underperform smaller ones in few-shot settings.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Email text → Tokenizer (SentencePiece) → Flan-T5 embedding → Task-specific head → Generated label ("ham"/"spam") → Mapping to 0/1
- Critical path: Tokenizer → Flan-T5 forward pass → Label generation → Mapping to 0/1
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Seq2seq (Spam-T5) vs. encoder-only (RoBERTa): Generation objective vs. simpler classification head
  - Parameter count: 250M (Spam-T5) vs. 125M (RoBERTa) → more capacity but higher compute
  - Tokenizer choice: SentencePiece (Spam-T5) vs. BPE (RoBERTa) → vocabulary coverage differences
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor few-shot performance: Tokenization mismatch, inadequate instruction tuning, insufficient parameter capacity
  - Slow inference: Seq2seq decoding overhead vs. classifier head
  - Overfitting: Large parameter count without proper regularization in small datasets
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare tokenization impact: Run Spam-T5 on SMS dataset with SentencePiece vs. BPE tokenizer.
  2. Test instruction tuning ablation: Fine-tune base T5 vs. Flan-T5 on 4-sample spam task.
  3. Measure capacity scaling: Train Spam-T5-small (80M) vs. Spam-T5-base (250M) on 16 samples and compare F1.

## Limitations

- **Dataset Representativeness**: The four public datasets may not capture the full diversity of real-world email spam, potentially limiting generalizability to production environments.
- **Implementation Specificity**: Critical implementation details such as exact tokenization method, stemming implementation, and tf-idf feature counts remain underspecified.
- **Computational Resource Bias**: The study assumes access to sufficient computational resources for fine-tuning 250M parameter models, creating practical barriers for smaller organizations.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Comparative performance results showing Spam-T5 outperforming baselines across multiple datasets and sample sizes. The empirical F1 scores (0.7498 average) are well-documented and reproducible given access to the same datasets and hyperparameters.
- **Medium Confidence**: The few-shot learning superiority claim, which depends on the assumption that limited training samples are representative of typical deployment scenarios.
- **Low Confidence**: The theoretical mechanisms explaining why Spam-T5 performs better. These are inferred from general LLM literature rather than validated through controlled experiments within this study.
