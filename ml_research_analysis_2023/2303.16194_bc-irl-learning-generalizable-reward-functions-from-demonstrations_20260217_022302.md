# BC-IRL: Learning Generalizable Reward Functions from Demonstrations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16194
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16194
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: BC-IRL learns reward functions that generalize better to new task settings than maximum entropy IRL methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces BC-IRL, a new inverse reinforcement learning framework designed to learn reward functions that generalize better to new task settings than existing IRL methods. Unlike maximum entropy IRL approaches, which learn rewards that maximize around demonstrations, BC-IRL updates reward parameters such that the policy trained with the new reward matches the expert demonstrations better. This is achieved by using gradient-based bi-level optimization to minimize a behavior cloning loss, differentiating through the reinforcement learning policy optimization. BC-IRL-PPO, a specific instantiation using PPO, is shown to learn more generalizable rewards compared to state-of-the-art IRL baselines. On a 2D point mass navigation task and two continuous robotic control tasks (Fetch Reach and TriFinger Reach), BC-IRL-PPO achieves over twice the success rate of baselines in challenging generalization settings, demonstrating the effectiveness of the approach in learning rewards that provide meaningful learning signals in new states.

## Method Summary
BC-IRL is a bi-level optimization framework that learns reward functions from demonstrations. The outer loop updates reward parameters by minimizing a behavior cloning loss between the policy trained with the reward and expert demonstrations. The inner loop optimizes the policy using reinforcement learning with the current reward function. The behavior cloning loss is differentiated through the policy optimization to provide gradients for reward parameter updates. BC-IRL-PPO is a specific implementation using PPO as the inner loop optimizer. The method is evaluated on 2D point mass navigation, Fetch Reach, and TriFinger Reach tasks, with comparisons to AIRL, MaxEnt IRL, GCL, f-IRL, BC, and GAIL baselines.

## Key Results
- BC-IRL-PPO achieves over twice the success rate of baselines in challenging generalization settings on Fetch Reach and TriFinger Reach tasks
- The method learns reward functions that provide meaningful learning signals in new states not covered by demonstrations
- BC-IRL outperforms maximum entropy IRL methods in terms of reward generalization across different start and goal distributions

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Reward function Rψ(s) -> Policy πθ(a|s) -> Behavior Cloning Loss -> Reward parameter update
- Critical path:
  1. Initialize reward and policy parameters.
  2. Optimize policy with current reward (inner loop).
  3. Compute behavior cloning loss between policy and expert demonstrations.
  4. Update reward parameters using gradient descent on the behavior cloning loss (outer loop).
  5. Repeat steps 2-4 until convergence.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a state-only reward function vs. a state-action reward function.
  - Differentiable policy optimization algorithms (e.g., PPO vs. model-based methods).
  - Behavior cloning loss vs. other outer objectives (e.g., maximum entropy).
- Failure signatures:
  - Reward function does not provide meaningful signals for states not covered by demonstrations.
  - Policy does not match expert behavior even with learned reward.
  - Reward function overfits to demonstrations and does not generalize.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test BC-IRL on a simple 2D point mass navigation task with demonstrations and evaluate reward generalization to new start states.
  2. Compare BC-IRL to maximum entropy IRL methods on a continuous control task and evaluate policy performance in new task settings.
  3. Analyze the effect of different policy optimization algorithms (e.g., PPO vs. model-based methods) on BC-IRL's ability to learn generalizable rewards.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of outer loop objective in BC-IRL beyond action matching (e.g., state distribution matching, value function matching) affect the generalizability of learned rewards?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses BC-IRL as a general framework that could optimize alternative outer-level objectives beyond the BC loss, but does not explore these alternatives.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates BC-IRL with action matching as the outer loop objective, leaving open whether other objectives might lead to better generalization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments evaluating BC-IRL with different outer loop objectives (state distribution matching, value function matching, etc.) across various tasks and generalization scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using model-based RL methods in the inner loop of BC-IRL on the sample efficiency and generalization performance compared to model-free methods like PPO?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that model-based methods could be used in the inner loop and are "especially appealing because a single dynamics model could be shared between tasks" but does not implement or evaluate this.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates BC-IRL with PPO as the inner loop optimizer, not exploring potential benefits of model-based methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing BC-IRL with various inner loop optimizers (PPO, SAC, model-based methods) on the same tasks, measuring both sample efficiency and generalization performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does BC-IRL's performance scale with increasing task complexity, particularly for tasks with high-dimensional state and action spaces or long time horizons?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates BC-IRL on relatively simple tasks (2D point navigation, Fetch and TriFinger reaching) but does not explore performance on more complex domains.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to controlled environments with moderate complexity, and it's unclear how the method would perform on more challenging real-world tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluations of BC-IRL on progressively more complex tasks, including high-dimensional continuous control problems, multi-task settings, or tasks with partial observability.

## Limitations

- The claim that BC-IRL learns "more generalizable" rewards is primarily supported by empirical results on three specific tasks, with limited theoretical justification for why the behavior cloning loss leads to better generalization
- The paper does not address potential reward function overfitting to demonstrations, particularly when the number of demonstrations is small (e.g., 1 demonstration for TriFinger Reach)
- The complexity of bi-level optimization with PPO inner loop may limit scalability to high-dimensional tasks or longer time horizons

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: BC-IRL framework is correctly described and implemented with proper bi-level optimization structure
- **Medium Confidence**: Empirical results show improved success rates on tested tasks, but generalizability to other domains is uncertain
- **Low Confidence**: Theoretical claims about why behavior cloning loss leads to better generalization compared to maximum entropy objectives are not well-supported
