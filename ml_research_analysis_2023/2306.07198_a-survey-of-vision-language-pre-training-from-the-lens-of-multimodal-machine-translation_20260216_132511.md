# A Survey of Vision-Language Pre-training from the Lens of Multimodal Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07198
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07198
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Vision-language models show varying effectiveness for multimodal machine translation depending on dataset grounding strength

## Executive Summary
This survey explores how vision-language (V-L) pre-training can enhance multimodal machine translation (MMT). While large V-L models like CLIP excel at tasks requiring strong visual-text alignment (e.g., image captioning), their application to MMT remains underexplored. The paper categorizes pre-training objectives and model architectures, then introduces a method to measure dataset grounding using V-L models for re-ranking text based on visual alignment. Experiments reveal that datasets with stronger grounding (like CoCo) show better visual-text alignment than weakly-grounded datasets (like How2), suggesting V-L models may be more effective for MMT tasks with high grounding.

## Method Summary
The paper measures dataset grounding by using pre-trained CLIP models to re-rank text candidates based on their alignment with visual inputs. For each visual-text pair, CLIP scores are computed for four candidate texts: reference, neighbor, in-domain distractors, and out-of-domain distractors. Grounding strength is quantified by the percentage of times the reference text achieves the highest CLIP score. This method is applied to datasets like CoCo, How2, and VaTeX to reveal varying degrees of grounding.

## Key Results
- V-L models like CLIP show strong performance on image captioning but underexplored potential for MMT
- Grounding strength varies significantly across datasets, with captioning datasets showing stronger visual-text alignment
- Zero-shot classification using V-L models can effectively measure dataset grounding levels
- V-L models may be more effective for MMT tasks with high grounding rather than weakly-grounded datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Grounding strength is a key predictor of MMT performance when using vision-language models.
- Mechanism: V-L models are typically trained on strongly-grounded datasets where text accurately describes visual content. When applied to MMT, the model's effectiveness depends on how well the translation text relates to the visual input.
- Core assumption: The degree of semantic alignment between text and image/video in training data affects how well the V-L model can leverage visual information for translation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The paper notes that V-L models like CLIP have shown success in tasks like image captioning and visual question answering, but their potential for MMT has been underexplored.
  - [section 2] The paper explicitly distinguishes between strong vs weak grounding in MMT datasets, arguing that the effectiveness of visual signals depends on how well the text is grounded in the visual content.
- Break condition: If the text and visual content are loosely related (weak grounding), the V-L model's visual attention mechanisms may not align with the translation-relevant features.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Zero-shot classification using V-L models can measure dataset grounding strength.
- Mechanism: By treating visual inputs as queries and text segments as candidate labels, we can measure how well the model aligns text with visual content. Higher alignment with reference text indicates stronger grounding.
- Core assumption: CLIP's zero-shot classification capability can serve as a proxy measure for semantic alignment between text and visual content.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.1] The paper introduces a method to measure dataset grounding by using V-L models to re-rank sentences based on their alignment with visual inputs.
  - [section 6.1] Experiments on datasets like CoCo, How2, and VaTeX reveal varying degrees of grounding, with captioning datasets showing stronger visual-text alignment.
- Break condition: If the V-L model's visual-text alignment doesn't correlate with translation relevance, this measure may not predict MMT performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different pre-training objectives (Masking, Matching, Dataset-specific) create different capabilities for multimodal tasks.
- Mechanism: Masking objectives help models learn contextual representations, Matching objectives help with alignment between modalities, and Dataset-specific objectives optimize for particular downstream tasks.
- Core assumption: The choice of pre-training objective affects what features the model learns and how well it can transfer to MMT.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] The paper categorizes pre-training objectives into Masking, Matching, and Dataset-specific approaches and reviews various model architectures.
  - [section 5] Different models use different combinations of these objectives, suggesting they create different capabilities.
- Break condition: If the downstream MMT task requires capabilities not emphasized by the pre-training objective, the model may not transfer effectively.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Vision encoder → Fusion layer → Text encoder → Output generation
- Critical path: Vision encoder → Fusion layer → Text encoder → Output generation. For MMT, this would be extended to include a translation component.
- Design tradeoffs: Vision encoder choice (CNN vs ViT vs Perceiver) affects computational efficiency and feature representation. Fusion method affects how well modalities are integrated. Freezing vs fine-tuning encoder layers affects transfer learning effectiveness.
- Failure signatures: Poor grounding in dataset leads to ineffective visual signal usage. Mismatch between pre-training objective and MMT task leads to poor transfer. Inadequate fusion of modalities leads to one modality dominating.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can large pre-trained vision-language models effectively improve multimodal machine translation for weakly-grounded datasets like How2?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenge of applying V-L models to MMT tasks, particularly for datasets with low grounding like How2, where translations are loosely related to the video content.
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights that V-L models are generally trained on strongly-grounded datasets, making it unclear how they would perform on weakly-grounded tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance of V-L models on weakly-grounded MMT datasets (like How2) versus strongly-grounded datasets (like COCO and VaTeX).

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the strength of grounding in a dataset influence the effectiveness of vision-language models in multimodal machine translation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a method to measure dataset grounding by using V-L models to re-rank sentences based on their alignment with visual inputs, revealing varying degrees of grounding across different datasets.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides insights into grounding differences, it does not conclusively determine how grounding strength affects V-L model performance in MMT.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing V-L model performance across datasets with varying levels of grounding, from low (e.g., How2) to high (e.g., COCO and VaTeX).

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the key architectural and training strategies needed to adapt large vision-language models for multimodal machine translation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper reviews various V-L model architectures and pre-training objectives but notes that integrating these models into MMT systems remains challenging, particularly due to data availability and model adaptation issues.
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses potential approaches, it does not provide a definitive solution for adapting V-L models to MMT tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of novel architectures or training strategies specifically designed to bridge the gap between V-L models and MMT tasks, potentially including hybrid approaches or new pre-training objectives.

## Limitations

- The correlation between CLIP-based grounding scores and actual translation performance remains empirically unverified
- Sampling strategy for distractors (neighbors, in-domain, out-of-domain texts) lacks precise specification
- Treatment of video data through single frame sampling versus temporal aggregation is not clearly defined

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The categorization of pre-training objectives (Masking, Matching, Dataset-specific) and their general impact on multimodal capabilities is well-established in the literature.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that grounding strength predicts MMT performance has theoretical support but lacks direct empirical validation.

**Low Confidence**: The grounding measurement method using CLIP zero-shot classification as a proxy for semantic alignment needs more validation.
