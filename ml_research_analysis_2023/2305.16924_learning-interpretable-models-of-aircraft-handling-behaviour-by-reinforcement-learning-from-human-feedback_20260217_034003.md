# Learning Interpretable Models of Aircraft Handling Behaviour by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16924
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16924
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Reward trees can achieve performance close to neural networks while providing intrinsic interpretability for safety-critical aviation applications.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method to capture the handling abilities of fast jet pilots in a software model via reinforcement learning from human preference feedback. The approach uses pairwise preferences over simulated flight trajectories to learn an interpretable rule-based model called a reward tree, which enables automated scoring of trajectories alongside an explanatory rationale. Experiments with synthetic preferences show reward trees to be competitive with uninterpretable neural network reward models on quantitative and qualitative evaluations. The results indicate that reward trees can achieve performance close to neural networks while providing intrinsic interpretability, which is crucial for safety-critical aviation applications.

## Method Summary
The method involves learning interpretable reward trees from human preference feedback for aircraft handling tasks. The process starts with trajectory generation using a model-based RL agent (PETS), followed by preference collection from human evaluators or oracles. A reward tree is then learned using a multi-stage algorithm with growth and pruning, employing a 0-1 loss criterion. The learned reward tree defines the reward function for training the RL agent, and the process iterates online to convergence. The approach is evaluated on three aircraft handling tasks (Follow, Chase, and Land) using synthetic oracle preferences, with performance measured by Oracle Regret Ratio, rank correlation, 0-1 loss, reward correlation, and qualitative trajectory inspection.

## Key Results
- Reward trees achieve competitive performance with neural networks on synthetic preference data for aircraft handling tasks.
- The iterative online learning approach successfully converges, with policy and reward model improving each other.
- ℓ0-1-based trees outperform variance-based ones, with NNs sometimes improving again by a smaller margin.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Preference-based reward learning enables capture of expert handling skills without explicit reward specification
- Mechanism: Pairwise preferences over trajectories are collected from human evaluators, and a reward tree is learned to reconstruct these preferences, which then serves as the reward function for training an RL agent
- Core assumption: Expert preferences can be elicited effectively through pairwise comparisons, and these preferences encode the desired handling behavior
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We use pairwise preferences over simulated flight trajectories to learn an interpretable rule-based model called a reward tree"
  - [section IV]: "we adopt the preference-based approach, in which a human (or in our case, oracle) evaluator is presented with pairs of agent trajectories"
- Break condition: Preference data becomes too sparse or noisy to learn a meaningful reward function, or experts cannot articulate preferences even in pairwise format

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Reward trees provide interpretability while maintaining competitive performance with neural networks
- Mechanism: The tree structure partitions the feature space into interpretable rules, allowing both automated scoring and human-readable explanations of behavior
- Core assumption: A rule-based tree can approximate complex reward functions while remaining interpretable
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "learns an interpretable rule-based model called a reward tree, which enables the automated scoring of trajectories alongside an explanatory rationale"
  - [section V]: "our recent prior work proposes reward trees... an intermediate option between the limited expressiveness of linear models and the uninterpretable complexity of NNs"
- Break condition: Tree becomes too large to remain interpretable, or performance degrades significantly compared to neural network baselines

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Online iterative learning enables refinement of both reward model and agent behavior
- Mechanism: The current reward tree is used to train an RL agent, which generates new trajectories for preference collection, and the process iterates to convergence
- Core assumption: Policy and reward learning can be bootstrapped iteratively, with each improving the other
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section VI.A]: "we resolve both questions with an iterative bootstrapped approach, in which the current reward tree defines the RL agent's reward function at each point in learning"
- Break condition: Iterative process fails to converge, or reward model becomes unstable with new preference data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well do reward trees scale to more complex aircraft handling tasks with larger state and action spaces?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions the current experiments use a simplified aircraft model with 30 features, but acknowledges more complex tasks may require larger models
- Why unresolved: The experiments were limited to three relatively simple aircraft handling tasks to demonstrate proof of concept
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with reward trees on aircraft tasks with significantly larger state spaces and more complex dynamics, comparing performance to neural networks

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust are reward trees to different types of human preference noise and bias compared to neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses experiments with oracle error probability and myopia, but notes these are artificial constructs
- Why unresolved: The experiments used synthetic oracle preferences rather than real human data with various noise patterns
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using real human preference data with different noise patterns and biases

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between tree interpretability and performance for safety-critical applications?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows reward trees can achieve performance close to neural networks while maintaining interpretability, but doesn't explore the trade-off space
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on proving reward trees can match neural networks, but doesn't investigate where the optimal point lies
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic studies varying tree size and complexity while measuring both performance and interpretability metrics for different safety-critical scenarios

### Open Question 4
- Question: How can reward trees be extended to handle multi-expert preferences with conflicting preferences?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as a future direction in the conclusions
- Why unresolved: The experiments used single oracle preferences rather than multiple human experts
- What evidence would resolve it: Methods for aggregating preferences from multiple experts and experiments showing how reward trees handle conflicting preferences

## Limitations
- The paper relies on synthetic oracle preferences rather than real human feedback, which may not capture the full complexity and variability of human decision-making in aircraft handling tasks.
- The aircraft simulator details and exact hyperparameters for PETS agents are not fully specified, which could affect reproducibility.
- The evaluation focuses primarily on synthetic data and three specific tasks, limiting generalizability to other handling scenarios or real-world conditions.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The mechanism of using pairwise preferences to learn reward trees is well-supported by the experimental results showing competitive performance with neural networks on synthetic data.
- **Medium Confidence**: The interpretability of reward trees is demonstrated qualitatively, but a more rigorous quantitative assessment of interpretability would strengthen this claim.
- **Medium Confidence**: The online iterative learning approach is described and implemented, but convergence properties and sensitivity to initial conditions are not thoroughly explored.

## Next Checks

1. **Real Human Feedback Validation**: Test the reward tree learning approach with real human evaluators on the Follow, Chase, and Land tasks to assess performance with actual preference data and identify potential issues with human-in-the-loop learning.
2. **Generalization and Transfer**: Evaluate the learned reward trees on novel aircraft handling tasks or perturbed versions of the existing tasks to measure generalization beyond the training scenarios.
3. **Interpretability Assessment**: Conduct a user study with aviation experts to quantify the interpretability of the reward trees, measuring factors such as explanation satisfaction, trust, and ease of use in safety-critical contexts.
