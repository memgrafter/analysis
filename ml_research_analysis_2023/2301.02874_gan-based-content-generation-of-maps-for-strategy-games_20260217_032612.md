# GAN-Based Content Generation of Maps for Strategy Games

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02874
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02874
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: ProgGAN generates realistic strategy game heightmaps with high efficiency (170 hours training vs. 217 for WGAN)

## Executive Summary
This work explores Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for procedural generation of realistic heightmaps for strategy games. The authors train several GAN variants (DCGAN, WGAN, ProgGAN, and VAE+WGAN) on a dataset of real-world elevation data, preprocessed and augmented to 12,640 images at 128×128 resolution. DCGAN and VAE+WGAN produced poor-quality outputs with artifacts, while WGAN and ProgGAN yielded the best results—generating high-quality heightmaps with natural features such as peninsulas, mountain ranges, and islands. ProgGAN achieved similar visual quality with significantly lower training time (170 vs. 217 hours). The findings demonstrate that GAN-based approaches can effectively generate realistic, appealing maps for strategy games, with ProgGAN offering the best efficiency.

## Method Summary
The authors train four GAN variants on 12,640 heightmap images (128×128 resolution) derived from NASA SRTM elevation data, augmented via rotation and flipping. DCGAN uses binary cross-entropy loss and Adam optimizer; WGAN employs Wasserstein loss with RMSProp and weight clipping; ProgGAN incrementally increases resolution from 64×64 to 128×128; and VAE+WGAN uses a pretrained VAE decoder as the generator. Training progresses through 5,000 epochs for DCGAN and WGAN, with ProgGAN trained for 170 hours total. Generated images are evaluated visually and through 3D rendering to assess natural feature presence.

## Key Results
- WGAN and ProgGAN generated high-quality heightmaps with natural features (peninsulas, mountain ranges, islands)
- DCGAN suffered from vanishing gradients and mode collapse, producing poor outputs
- ProgGAN achieved similar quality to WGAN with 22% less training time (170 vs. 217 hours)
- VAE+WGAN experiments produced artifacts and unstable training, failing to generate quality maps

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DCGAN alone fails due to vanishing gradient problem where the discriminator becomes too strong, providing no useful gradient to the generator.
- Mechanism: The discriminator's binary cross-entropy loss rapidly converges to zero, indicating perfect discrimination. At this point, the generator receives zero or near-zero gradients, halting its learning.
- Core assumption: Standard GAN training without modifications leads to an optimal discriminator before the generator has learned meaningful features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] discusses GANs in the context of generating realistic maps.
  - [section] explicitly states: "As we can observe, D's loss rapidly converges to 0, while G's loss keeps growing unsteadily. Early during the training phase, D reaches its optimal state, which is perfectly discriminating between the real and fake images. Thus, G is unable to improve, leading to a loss growth."
  - [corpus] does not directly address this specific mechanism; no evidence here.
- Break condition: If the discriminator is weakened through techniques like label smoothing, noise addition, or dropout, it provides non-zero gradients, allowing generator training to proceed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: WGAN improves training stability by using Wasserstein distance instead of Jensen-Shannon divergence, providing meaningful gradients even when the discriminator is optimal.
- Mechanism: The critic (WGAN's discriminator) outputs a score rather than a probability. This score estimates the Wasserstein distance, which remains differentiable and informative across all generator states, avoiding the vanishing gradient problem.
- Core assumption: Wasserstein distance provides a smoother, more continuous measure of distributional difference than binary cross-entropy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] introduces GAN-based approaches for map generation.
  - [section] explains: "WGAN proved to be a good option since throughout the whole training phase the generator always had enough information provided by the critic to improve itself, thus ending up generating heightmaps with good quality."
  - [corpus] does not contain direct evidence for this mechanism.
- Break condition: If weight clipping is too restrictive or the critic is over-trained relative to the generator, training can become unstable or slow.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Progressive Growing GAN (ProgGAN) enables high-resolution generation by incrementally adding layers, allowing the model to learn coarse-to-fine detail without overwhelming computational resources.
- Mechanism: Training starts at low resolution (e.g., 64×64), and layers are gradually added to double resolution. This phased approach stabilizes training by focusing on global structure first, then local detail.
- Core assumption: Learning complex, high-resolution patterns is more stable when decomposed into incremental, lower-resolution stages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] discusses generating realistic and appealing maps.
  - [section] states: "With the ProgGAN we saw that we could achieve the same results as in the WGAN with less training time... the quality improved, since there is more variety of complex structures present in the end of the training phase such as bays, peninsulas, mountain ranges near the shore and islands."
  - [corpus] does not provide direct evidence for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If layer growth is too rapid or the fade-in parameter (alpha) is not smoothly adjusted, the model may become unstable or lose previously learned features.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Raw DEM files -> Global Mapper export -> Brightness adjustment -> Sliding window crop (1024×1024) -> Augmentation (rotation, flip) -> Filtering -> Downscale (128×128) -> Normalization
  - Models: DCGAN (baseline), WGAN (stable loss), ProgGAN (progressive resolution), VAE+WGAN (pretrained decoder)
  - Training Loop: For each batch, generate fake images, compute loss, update discriminator/critic, then update generator, repeat

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess and augment dataset correctly (especially filtering out low-content images)
  2. Choose GAN variant based on desired trade-off (quality vs. speed)
  3. Configure optimizer and learning rates (Adam for DCGAN, RMSProp for WGAN/ProgGAN)
  4. Monitor loss curves for signs of mode collapse or vanishing gradients
  5. Validate generated images visually and via 3D rendering

- Design tradeoffs:
  - DCGAN: Simpler, faster to prototype but prone to instability and mode collapse
  - WGAN: More stable, better gradients, but requires careful weight clipping and slower training
  - ProgGAN: High-quality, efficient for large images, but complex implementation and longer total training time
  - VAE+WGAN: Potentially faster convergence with pretrained decoder, but risk of artifacts if VAE is under-trained

- Failure signatures:
  - DCGAN: Discriminator loss near zero, generator loss increasing, repetitive or blurry outputs
  - WGAN: Wasserstein estimate plateaus or oscillates, weight clipping too aggressive
  - ProgGAN: Sudden quality drop during layer growth, alpha fade-in too fast
  - VAE+WGAN: Strange artifacts in generated images, loss stagnation in VAE phase

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train DCGAN with and without discriminator hindering methods (noise, label smoothing, dropout) to observe effects on stability and output quality
  2. Train WGAN with different weight clipping thresholds to find optimal balance between stability and expressiveness
  3. Train ProgGAN with 64×64, 128×128 stages to confirm progressive growth improves quality and efficiency

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the optimal noise injection schedules for preventing mode collapse in GANs trained on terrain data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper experiments with three noise factor equations (Equations 4, 5, and 6) to prevent discriminator over-optimization, showing varying results.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that noise injection helps but doesn't systematically compare different schedules or determine which provides the best trade-off between stability and image quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive ablation study testing various noise injection schedules with quantitative metrics for image quality and mode diversity.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does Progressive GAN training scale to higher resolution heightmaps (e.g., 1024×1024) for strategy game maps?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note they were limited to 128×128 due to memory constraints and state "the ideal scenario would be to work with an higher resolution such as 1024×1024."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests ProgGAN up to 128×128 and cannot determine if quality improvements scale with resolution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training ProgGAN on higher resolution datasets with appropriate hardware to compare quality metrics and training efficiency at different resolutions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the VAE+WGAN architecture be modified to consistently produce high-quality terrain maps without artifacts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The VAE+WGAN experiments produced images with artifacts and unstable training, though the concept was considered promising.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows the approach is problematic but doesn't explore architectural modifications or training adjustments that might fix these issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing modified VAE+WGAN architectures with different latent space configurations, loss functions, or training schedules to achieve artifact-free, high-quality outputs.

## Limitations

- Limited quantitative evaluation, relying primarily on visual inspection rather than standardized metrics for heightmap quality
- Preprocessing pipeline details (brightness adjustment formula, filtering thresholds) not fully specified, limiting reproducibility
- Memory constraints limited experiments to 128×128 resolution, preventing assessment of higher-resolution performance

## Confidence

- High Confidence: WGAN and ProgGAN outperform DCGAN and VAE+WGAN in generating realistic heightmaps; ProgGAN achieves similar quality with less training time
- Medium Confidence: The specific preprocessing pipeline (brightness adjustment, filtering) and noise injection in DCGAN are critical but not fully specified
- Low Confidence: The claim that VAE+WGAN fails due to VAE under-training is plausible but not directly tested or validated
