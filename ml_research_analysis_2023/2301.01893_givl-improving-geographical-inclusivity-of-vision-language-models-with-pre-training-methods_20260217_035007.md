# GIVL: Improving Geographical Inclusivity of Vision-Language Models with Pre-Training Methods

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01893
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01893
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: GIVL achieves state-of-the-art performance on geo-diverse V&L tasks while reducing geographical bias between Western and non-Western images

## Executive Summary
GIVL introduces two novel pre-training objectives, Image-Knowledge Matching (IKM) and Image Edit Checking (IEC), to improve geographical inclusivity in vision-language models. IKM aligns images with culturally relevant textual knowledge from Wikipedia to learn geo-diverse semantic connections, while IEC discriminates between visually similar but culturally distinct concepts through adversarial replacement training. The model achieves state-of-the-art results on geo-diverse benchmarks including MaRVL, GD-VCR, and WIT image-text retrieval, while maintaining competitive performance on standard V&L tasks. Notably, GIVL demonstrates a 26% improvement over VinVL on Dollar Street zero-shot classification, indicating successful mitigation of geographical bias.

## Method Summary
GIVL is a vision-language model that extends BERT architecture with object-level visual features from a ResNeXt-152 C4 detection model. The model is pre-trained using four objectives: masked language modeling (MLM), image-text matching (ITM), image-knowledge matching (IKM), and image edit checking (IEC). IKM learns alignment between images and corresponding Wikipedia knowledge to capture cultural context, while IEC identifies when visual concepts are replaced with similar but irrelevant ones to improve visual discrimination. The pre-training corpus combines WIT dataset (2.95M images), COCO/Flicker30k/GQA images (0.22M), and Wikipedia sections for knowledge alignment. The model uses a [CLS] c [SEP] k [SEP] t [SEP] v input format where c is caption/question, k is knowledge, t is tags/answers, and v is visual embeddings.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on geo-diverse V&L benchmarks: MaRVL, GD-VCR, and WIT image-text retrieval
- Reduces performance gap between Western and non-Western images compared to similar-size models pre-trained on similar data
- Outperforms VinVL by 26% on Dollar Street zero-shot classification task
- Maintains competitive performance on common V&L benchmarks while improving geographical inclusivity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: IKM objective improves geo-diverse knowledge alignment by forcing the model to match images with culturally relevant textual knowledge from Wikipedia.
- Mechanism: The model learns to associate visual concepts with their regional cultural context through textual knowledge alignment, enabling better understanding of geo-diverse imagery.
- Core assumption: Wikipedia sections contain sufficient cultural knowledge to disambiguate similar visual concepts from different regions.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "IKM is used to learn the alignment between images and corresponding textual knowledge in Wikipedia"; [section 3.2.2] "With the help of IKM, we encourage GIVL to learn the corresponding knowledge of the visual concepts and discover connections between geo-diverse visual concepts"
- Break condition: If Wikipedia knowledge is incomplete or biased toward certain regions, the IKM objective may reinforce existing geographical bias rather than mitigate it.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: IEC objective improves visual concept discrimination by training the model to detect when visually similar concepts from different categories are swapped.
- Mechanism: The model learns fine-grained visual distinctions between concepts that share similar visual features but belong to different categories through adversarial concept replacement training.
- Core assumption: The model can learn to distinguish concepts based on subtle visual differences when trained with adversarial replacements.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "IEC is proposed to identify whether a visual concept in input image is replaced by another concept that is visually similar but lies in an irrelevant category"; [section 3.2.3] "IEC is designed to identify whether a specific visual concept pv in input image I is replaced by another visually similar one in an irrelevant category"
- Break condition: If the visual similarity between replaced concepts is too high, the model may fail to learn meaningful distinctions and performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of category similarity learning and visual distinction training creates a balanced approach to geographical inclusivity.
- Mechanism: IKM provides semantic connections between culturally similar concepts while IEC provides fine-grained visual discrimination, creating complementary learning signals.
- Core assumption: Category similarity and visual distinction are complementary rather than competing learning objectives.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "concepts under similar categories have unique knowledge and visual characteristics" and "concepts with similar visual features may fall in completely different categories"; [section 3.2.2] "Learning to identify category similarity can connect diverse visual concepts under similar categories"; [section 3.2.3] "it is highly likely that visual concepts share similar visual characteristics but fall into completely different categories"
- Break condition: If category similarity and visual distinction create conflicting gradients during training, the model may struggle to balance both objectives effectively.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multi-modal representation learning
  - Why needed here: GIVL must learn to represent and reason across both visual and textual modalities to understand geo-diverse concepts
  - Quick check question: How does the model represent the relationship between an image of Chinese paper cuttings and the textual knowledge about its cultural significance?

- Concept: Contrastive learning
  - Why needed here: Both IKM and IEC objectives rely on contrastive learning to distinguish between matching and non-matching image-knowledge pairs
  - Quick check question: What loss function does GIVL use to measure the alignment between image representations and textual knowledge embeddings?

- Concept: Adversarial training
  - Why needed here: IEC objective uses adversarial concept replacement to improve visual discrimination between similar concepts
  - Quick check question: How does the model determine which visual concepts to use for adversarial replacement in the IEC objective?

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of Wikipedia-based knowledge alignment is questionable given Wikipedia's documented geographical bias, which may reinforce rather than mitigate existing biases
- The IEC objective lacks sufficient detail on implementation thresholds and similarity metrics, making it difficult to assess robustness and reproducibility
- Limited analysis of how geographical inclusivity improvements translate to real-world applications beyond curated benchmark datasets

## Confidence

- High confidence: GIVL achieves state-of-the-art performance on geo-diverse V&L benchmarks (MaRVL, GD-VCR, WIT) and significantly outperforms baseline models on Dollar Street zero-shot classification (26% improvement over VinVL)
- Medium confidence: The two novel pre-training objectives (IKM and IEC) effectively improve geographical inclusivity through category similarity learning and visual concept discrimination mechanisms
- Low confidence: Wikipedia knowledge effectively provides sufficient cultural context for geo-diverse visual concept understanding without introducing geographical bias

## Next Checks

Compare performance when using alternative knowledge sources (e.g., culturally diverse encyclopedias or local knowledge databases) to determine if improvements stem from knowledge quality or the IKM methodology itself.

2. Stress-test IEC visual similarity thresholds: Implement ablation studies varying the visual similarity thresholds and concept replacement strategies in the IEC objective. Evaluate model performance across different similarity ranges to identify optimal parameters and assess robustness to adversarial concept choices.

3.
