# Why Can't Discourse Parsing Generalize? A Thorough Investigation of the Impact of Data Diversity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06488
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06488
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Training on diverse genres significantly improves out-of-domain performance for RST discourse parsing compared to single-genre training.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates why RST discourse parsing fails to generalize well across domains despite recent performance improvements. Using RST-DT (news-only) and GUM (multi-genre) corpora, the authors systematically evaluate how training data diversity affects cross-genre parsing stability. They find that heterogeneous training regimes significantly improve generalization across parser architectures, with more genre variety (smaller portions each) outperforming fewer genres with more data per genre. However, substantial degradation remains for document central units and relations requiring pragmatic reasoning, suggesting that robust discourse parsing requires both multi-genre benchmarks and more diverse training data.

## Method Summary
The authors compare RST discourse parsers trained on different genre combinations using two architectures (BOTTOM-UP and TOP-DOWN). They evaluate cross-corpus generalization by training on RST-DT and testing on GUM, and vice versa, then test three joint training strategies: concatenation, model stacking, and fine-tuning SpanBERT on GUM before training on RST-DT. Experiments systematically vary genre composition and quantity, testing whether more genres with smaller portions each improves out-of-domain performance compared to fewer genres with more data. Error analysis focuses on document central units (CDUs) and relations requiring pragmatic reasoning.

## Key Results
- Training on diverse genres significantly improves cross-domain performance compared to news-only training across multiple parser architectures
- When training data is fixed in size, more genre variety (smaller portions each) leads to better generalization than fewer genres with more data each
- Even with diverse training, substantial degradation remains on unseen genres, particularly for CDUs and pragmatic relations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Genre diversity in training data improves cross-domain generalization of RST discourse parsers.
- Mechanism: Training on heterogeneous text types exposes the parser to a broader distribution of discourse structures and relation patterns, reducing overfitting to a single domain's stylistic conventions.
- Core assumption: Discourse relation patterns are partially domain-dependent, and exposure to multiple genres during training enables the model to learn more robust, generalizable representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our results show that a heterogeneous training regime is critical for stable and generalizable models, across parser architectures."
  - [section] "We interpret this result to mean that unlike in EDU segmentation, where results are close in both directions, genre composition of the train and test data plays a crucial role in the generalizability of RST constituent parsing, regardless of parser architecture."
  - [corpus] The comparison between RST-DT (single-genre news) and GUM (multi-genre) provides direct empirical support.
- Break condition: If the target domain is very different from all training genres, or if relation distributions are fundamentally incompatible, diversity alone may not suffice.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Model fine-tuning on a diverse corpus (GUM) followed by training on the target domain (RST-DT) can improve performance.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning first on diverse data helps the underlying language model (e.g., SpanBERT) learn more general discourse-aware representations before specializing to the target domain.
- Core assumption: The language model's pre-training captures some generalizable linguistic patterns that can be adapted to discourse parsing, and diverse fine-tuning enhances this adaptability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our results show that a heterogeneous training regime is critical for stable and generalizable models, across parser architectures."
  - [section] "For model stacking, we test three variants... Finally, we test a simple pretraining approach, in which we fine-tune the underlying SpanBERT model... then load the fine-tuned SpanBERT, and train again on RST-DT (SR-FT)."
  - [corpus] The SR-FT approach achieved the best joint training performance in Table 5.
- Break condition: If the diverse data is too small or too dissimilar, fine-tuning may not provide meaningful generalization benefits.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: More genres with smaller portions each lead to better OOD performance than fewer genres with more data each, given a fixed training set size.
- Mechanism: Having more genres in the training set forces the model to learn more varied discourse patterns, which improves its ability to handle unseen genres at test time.
- Core assumption: Each genre provides unique but partially overlapping discourse phenomena, and having more genres increases the chance that a target domain shares similarities with at least one training genre.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our results show that a heterogeneous training regime is critical for stable and generalizable models, across parser architectures."
  - [section] "Although all scores are rather low due to the small corpus sizes... they suggest that more training genres with smaller portions each promotes OOD generalization."
  - [corpus] The experiment comparing 3-genre cohorts C1/C2 vs. 6-genre cohort C3 in Table 8.
- Break condition: If the genres are too dissimilar or if the training data per genre is too sparse, the model may fail to learn effective representations for any genre.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: EDU segmenter -> shift-reduce parser (bottom-up or top-down) -> relation label mapping -> evaluation metrics
- Critical path: For joint training: map labels → fine-tune SpanBERT on diverse data → train parser on target domain. For cross-domain evaluation: train on source domain → test on target domain.
- Design tradeoffs: Using more genres improves generalization but may reduce per-genre performance; fine-tuning vs. joint training affects model capacity and training stability.
- Failure signatures: Severe OOD degradation, especially on document central units (CDUs) and relations requiring pragmatic reasoning, indicates the model has not learned generalizable discourse patterns.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on RST-DT only, test on GUM (cross-corpus generalization)
  2. Train on GUM only, test on RST-DT (reverse direction)
  3. Fine-tune SpanBERT on GUM, then train parser on RST-DT (joint training via fine-tuning)

## Limitations
- The study focuses only on English RST parsing and two specific parser architectures
- Cannot separate the effects of genre diversity from other confounding factors like annotation scheme differences
- Error analysis lacks fine-grained quantitative breakdowns of specific error types

## Confidence
- High confidence: Core finding that heterogeneous training regimes improve cross-domain performance
- Medium confidence: Specific recommendation that more genres with smaller portions each yields better generalization
- Low confidence: Error analysis findings regarding CDUs and pragmatic relations

## Next Checks
