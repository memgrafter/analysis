# From Word Models to World Models: Translating from Natural Language to the Probabilistic Language of Thought

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12672
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12672
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Introduces a framework integrating LLMs with probabilistic programming to enable language-informed commonsense reasoning across multiple domains.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a framework for language-informed thinking that combines large language models (LLMs) with probabilistic programming to model how humans reason about and learn from language. The key innovation is translating natural language into probabilistic programs, which serve as a general-purpose symbolic substrate for generative world modeling. This allows language to drive coherent updates to beliefs and answer questions about uncertain situations across domains including probabilistic reasoning, relational reasoning, visual and physical reasoning, and social reasoning.

## Method Summary
The method uses LLMs (Codex) fine-tuned on language and code pairs to translate natural language utterances into expressions in a probabilistic programming language (Church). These expressions condition and query generative world models, enabling probabilistic inference to support reasoning. The approach integrates cognitive science theories of probabilistic reasoning with modern language models, creating a unified framework where linguistic meaning construction drives world model updates and inference.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates coherent reasoning across multiple domains including Bayesian tug-of-war, kinship relations, visual scenes, and social reasoning
- Shows language can drive the construction and extension of world models through program translation
- Establishes LLMs as effective learned meaning functions for translating natural language to probabilistic programs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: LLMs can serve as learned meaning functions that translate natural language into probabilistic program expressions.
- **Mechanism**: The LLM leverages training on code and natural language pairs to map linguistic utterances to executable program statements in a probabilistic language of thought. This translation is context-sensitive and resource-rational.
- **Core assumption**: Linguistic meaning can be modeled as a distribution over program expressions conditioned on natural language utterances, and this distribution is learnable from data.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states LLMs support "broad-coverage translation from natural language utterances to code expressions in a probabilistic programming language."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Probabilistic programs provide a unifying substrate for world modeling and rational inference that interfaces with language.
- **Mechanism**: The probabilistic language of thought (PLoT) expresses generative models of domains and supports conditioning on observations and querying for inferences. This allows language to drive coherent updates to beliefs.
- **Core assumption**: Human reasoning about uncertainty can be formalized as Bayesian inference in probabilistic programs, and these programs can express the core dynamics of diverse domains.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract frames "linguistic meaning as a context-sensitive mapping from natural language into a probabilistic language of thought (PLoT)—a general-purpose symbolic substrate for generative world modeling."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Language can drive the construction and extension of world models, enabling learning new concepts and domains from linguistic instruction.
- **Mechanism**: Sentences that introduce new concepts or define new domains are translated into program expressions that extend the existing PLoT. This allows language to grow the knowledge base that supports reasoning.
- **Core assumption**: New concepts can be defined in terms of existing concepts in the PLoT, and entire new domain models can be constructed from linguistic descriptions of their dynamics.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract explores "how language can drive the construction of world models themselves."

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Probabilistic programming**
  - Why needed here: Probabilistic programs are the substrate for world modeling and inference in this framework. Understanding how they express generative models and support conditioning and querying is crucial.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between a condition statement and a query statement in a probabilistic program?

- **Concept: Bayesian inference**
  - Why needed here: Bayesian inference is the formal mechanism for updating beliefs and answering questions in the PLoT. Understanding how priors, likelihoods, and posteriors relate is essential.
  - Quick check question: How does conditioning on an observation in a probabilistic program relate to Bayesian belief updating?

- **Concept: Language-to-code translation**
  - Why needed here: LLMs serve as learned meaning functions by translating natural language to code. Understanding how few-shot prompting and context-sensitivity enable this is important.
  - Quick check question: How does providing a few translation examples help the LLM generalize to new utterances?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Natural language utterances -> LLM meaning function -> Probabilistic language of thought -> Graphics/physics/planning engines (optional) -> Inference engine -> Results

- **Critical path**: 1. Receive natural language utterance 2. LLM translates utterance to PLoT code 3. PLoT code updates or queries world model 4. Inference engine computes results 5. Results are returned (as distributions, samples, etc.)

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - LLM vs symbolic semantic parsing: LLMs offer broader coverage and context-sensitivity but less interpretability and verifiability than hand-engineered parsers
  - Rejection sampling vs more advanced inference: Rejection sampling is simple but inefficient; more advanced methods like SMC or variational inference could scale better but are more complex
  - Integrating external engines vs self-contained PLoT: External engines offer specialized capabilities but add complexity; self-contained PLoT is more modular but may be less expressive

- **Failure signatures**:
  - LLM generates incorrect or nonsensical PLoT code
  - PLoT code does not capture intended meaning or domain structure
  - Inference in PLoT is too slow or produces degenerate results
  - External engines fail to integrate properly or produce incorrect outputs

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement a simple Bayesian tug-of-war model and test LLM translation of a few example utterances to condition and query statements
  2. Extend the kinship domain with a new concept like "uncle" and test translation of definitions and queries involving the new concept
  3. Integrate a simple graphics engine to render scenes from the visual reasoning domain and test translation of visual descriptions to conditions and queries

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we scale probabilistic inference in dynamically synthesized world models to achieve human-like reasoning speed and robustness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses limitations of rejection sampling and need for more efficient inference methods like dynamic programming over blocks of variables or neural network-based proposals
- Why unresolved: Current inference methods struggle with complex world models and require exponential computation time
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstrations of efficient inference algorithms that can handle large, complex world models and produce results comparable to human reasoning

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we model the resource-rational amortization of meaning construction and problem-solving in a unified framework?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses need to balance between translation and inference, and potential for neural models to amortize certain pragmatic inferences
- Why unresolved: Optimal balance between explicit inference and amortized computation is unclear
- What evidence would resolve it: A unified framework integrating symbolic and neural approaches to language understanding and reasoning

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we build world models on the fly that are both relevant to the problem at hand and consistent with our broader knowledge of the world?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights challenge of dynamically constructing world models balancing fidelity to prior knowledge, relevance to task, and computational efficiency
- Why unresolved: Current methods are either too rigid or too computationally expensive
- What evidence would resolve it: A system that can dynamically construct and revise world models based on linguistic input

## Limitations
- Reliance on LLMs introduces uncertainty about faithfulness and verifiability of language-to-program translations
- Approach lacks rigorous evaluation of translation quality and inference robustness
- Use of rejection sampling may limit efficiency and scalability for complex world models

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Overall framework of integrating LLMs with probabilistic programming for language-informed thinking is well-motivated and technically sound
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about LLMs serving as effective learned meaning functions for translating natural language to probabilistic programs are supported by examples but lack rigorous evaluation
- **Low confidence**: Claims about language driving construction and extension of world models are only briefly demonstrated and require further investigation

## Next Checks

1. Conduct systematic evaluation of LLM translations across diverse natural language utterances and domains, comparing generated probabilistic programs to ground truth translations and measuring impact on downstream inference quality

2. Investigate scalability to more complex world models and inference tasks, exploring alternative inference algorithms (sequential Monte Carlo, variational inference) and their trade-offs with rejection sampling

3. Experiment with integrating more sophisticated external engines (advanced physics simulators, planning algorithms) and evaluate their impact on expressiveness and performance of language-informed reasoning system
