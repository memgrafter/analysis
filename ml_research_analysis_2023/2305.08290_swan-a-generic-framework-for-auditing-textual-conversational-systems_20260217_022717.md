# SWAN: A Generic Framework for Auditing Textual Conversational Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08290
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08290
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces SWAN framework for auditing textual conversational systems with Schematised Weighted Average Nugget score

## Executive Summary
SWAN provides a generic framework for auditing textual conversational systems by evaluating conversation sessions through a weighted scoring system. The framework addresses the need for timely and versatile evaluation of LLM-based conversational systems, considering various criteria such as correctness, harmlessness, and fairness. SWAN aims to provide a transparent, neutral, and agile approach to assessing potential negative impacts on users and society.

## Method Summary
The SWAN framework extracts nuggets (claims or dialogue acts) from conversation sessions, scores each nugget against multiple criteria, and computes a weighted average score that incorporates nugget positions and user models. The method involves three main phases: nugget extraction from conversation turns, scoring each nugget for 20+ criteria, and calculating the final SWAN score using weighted averaging based on nugget positions.

## Key Results
- Proposes a Schematised Weighted Average Nugget (SWAN) score for conversation evaluation
- Introduces a schema of twenty (+1) criteria for comprehensive conversational system assessment
- Framework supports both task-oriented and non-task-oriented conversation evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SWAN uses nugget positioning to weight evaluation scores, improving sensitivity to conversational dynamics
- Mechanism: Nuggets extracted from conversation sessions are weighted based on their position within the dialogue, allowing the framework to capture the importance of early versus late contributions
- Core assumption: Nugget relevance and impact vary systematically with position in a conversation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Following the approaches of S-measure and U-measure, SWAN utilises nugget positions within the conversations to weight the nuggets based on a user model."
  - [section] "The idea of utilising nugget positions originates from the S-measure [33] and U-measure [31] evaluation frameworks."
- Break condition: If nugget position does not correlate with relevance or user perception, the weighting will not improve evaluation accuracy

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SWAN enables multi-criteria evaluation by scoring individual nuggets across different dimensions
- Mechanism: Each extracted nugget is scored against a schema of twenty criteria (e.g., Correctness, Harmlessness, Coherence), allowing granular and flexible assessment
- Core assumption: Different aspects of conversational quality can be independently evaluated and combined
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also present a schema of twenty (+1) criteria that may be worth incorporating in the SWAN framework."
  - [section] "The second phase in our framework scores each nugget for each of our criteria such as Correctness, Harmlessness, etc."
- Break condition: If criteria are not independent or if scoring is inconsistent across assessors, the combined score will lose meaning

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SWAN supports both task-oriented and non-task-oriented conversation evaluation seamlessly
- Mechanism: The framework treats all conversational turns uniformly as nuggets, allowing evaluation across different conversation types without structural changes
- Core assumption: Nugget-based evaluation is agnostic to conversation intent
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The frameworks should be able to handle task-oriented and non-task-oriented conversations seamlessly."
  - [section] "Firstly, they should be able to handle task-oriented and non-task-oriented conversations seamlessly."
- Break condition: If task-oriented and non-task-oriented conversations require fundamentally different evaluation approaches, the unified framework will be inadequate

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Nugget extraction from dialogue
  - Why needed here: SWAN depends on atomic claims or dialogue acts being identified before scoring
  - Quick check question: Can you extract "the paper is 15 pages long" as a factual nugget from "I cannot provide a one-page summary as it is 15 pages long"?

- Concept: Weighted averaging in evaluation metrics
  - Why needed here: SWAN computes scores as weighted averages over nuggets, requiring understanding of position-based weighting
  - Quick check question: If a conversation has 5 nuggets with weights [0.5, 0.3, 0.1, 0.05, 0.05], what is the weighted average score if individual scores are [1, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4, 0.3]?

- Concept: Schema-based multi-criteria evaluation
  - Why needed here: SWAN applies 20+ criteria independently to each nugget, requiring schema design and scoring integration
  - Quick check question: How would you define a schema entry for "Correctness" that differentiates factual claims from opinions?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Nugget extractor → Scorer (per criterion) → Weighting engine → Aggregator (SWAN score)
- Critical path: Sample conversations → Extract nuggets → Score each nugget per criterion → Apply nugget weights → Aggregate to SWAN score
- Design tradeoffs: Position weighting improves sensitivity but adds complexity; multi-criteria scoring increases granularity but requires more human effort
- Failure signatures: Inconsistent nugget scoring across criteria, position weights not reflecting actual user value, high variance in SWAN scores across similar conversations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test nugget extraction accuracy on a small set of annotated conversations
  2. Validate position weighting by comparing SWAN scores with and without weighting on known-good conversations
  3. Evaluate inter-annotator agreement for each criterion in the schema on sample nuggets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we effectively sample conversation sessions to ensure alertness, versatility, agility, and neutrality in auditing LLM-based conversational systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that this is "outside the scope of this paper" and mentions it is "of utmost importance".
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the importance of appropriate conversation sampling but does not provide specific methods, leaving this as a critical open research area.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of robust sampling strategies that balance human-in-the-loop experiments with user simulation, while avoiding exploitation and ensuring diverse, representative conversation samples.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we design (semi)automatic scoring mechanisms for the Harmlessness criterion that avoid worker exploitation and capture microaggressions across sequences of system turns?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "manually scoring system turns from the Harmlessness viewpoint can lead to worker exploitation, and therefore (semi)automatic scoring is necessary" and that "some facets of Harmlessness such as microaggressions...is necessary to observe a sequence of system turns rather than to focus on a single nugget or a single turn."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the need for (semi)automatic scoring of Harmlessness but does not provide specific solutions for this complex task.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of (semi)automatic scoring models that can detect various forms of harm across turn sequences without requiring extensive human labor.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we construct common seed user turns for comparing multiple conversational systems using the SWAN framework, given that different system responses will lead to branching conversation paths?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "for comparing multiple systems using the SWAN framework, a set of common seed user turns that initialise a conversation can be utilised" but notes that "this means that (S)W AN scores from different systems are not directly comparable."
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the need for common seed user turns for system comparison but does not provide a solution for the resulting lack of direct comparability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of a methodology for constructing seed user turns and a framework for comparing SWAN scores across different conversation paths.

## Limitations

- Automatic nugget extraction (especially Type O nuggets representing dialogue acts) remains challenging and unreliable
- Position-based weighting mechanism lacks empirical validation to prove it captures nugget importance
- 20+ criterion schema may suffer from overlapping definitions and inconsistent scoring across human annotators

## Confidence

- Nugget-based evaluation approach: Medium
- Multi-criteria evaluation mechanism: High for structured factual nuggets, Low for nuanced dialogue acts and subjective criteria
- Position-weighting mechanism: Low due to lack of empirical validation

## Next Checks

1. Conduct inter-annotator agreement studies on the 20+ criteria schema using sample nuggets to measure consistency and identify criteria requiring clearer definitions.

2. Compare SWAN scores with and without position weighting on a set of known-good and known-bad conversations to validate whether position weighting meaningfully improves discrimination.

3. Test automatic nugget extraction accuracy on a diverse conversation corpus, measuring precision and recall for both Type F (factual) and Type O (dialogue act) nuggets.
