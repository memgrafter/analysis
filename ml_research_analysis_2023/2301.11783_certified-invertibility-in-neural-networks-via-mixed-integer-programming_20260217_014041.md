# Certified Invertibility in Neural Networks via Mixed-Integer Programming

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11783
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11783
- Reference count: 40
- The paper addresses invertibility of neural networks by formulating it as a mixed-integer programming (MIP) problem

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a mixed-integer programming approach to certify invertibility in ReLU neural networks. The method formulates invertibility as an optimization problem that seeks to minimize the distance between two points with identical network outputs, using binary variables to represent ReLU constraints. The approach is particularly relevant for two contexts: certifying local invertibility in neural network approximations of dynamical systems, and determining transformations between different neural networks. The authors demonstrate that this MIP formulation can effectively certify invertibility regions and compute output uniqueness conditions.

## Method Summary
The core methodology encodes the invertibility problem as a mixed-integer linear program that searches for pairs of distinct inputs producing the same output. The formulation incorporates pre-activation bounds and uses binary variables to represent the activation status of ReLU neurons. By minimizing the distance between two input points while constraining their outputs to be identical, the approach can either find counterexamples to invertibility or prove that no such counterexamples exist within a specified region. The method leverages the tight relationship between MIP formulations and the piecewise linear structure of ReLU networks to provide certified guarantees about invertibility properties.

## Key Results
- MIP formulation can certify local invertibility of ReLU networks for dynamical system approximations
- Small time horizons in dynamical systems lead to larger regions of guaranteed invertibility
- The approach enables computing regions where outputs of one network are uniquely determined by another network's output

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The MIP formulation works by leveraging the fact that ReLU networks are piecewise linear functions with a finite number of linear regions. By formulating invertibility as a constraint satisfaction problem with binary variables representing ReLU activation patterns, the method can exhaustively search through possible linear regions to either find non-invertible points or prove invertibility within a bounded region. The key insight is that finding two distinct inputs with identical outputs is equivalent to solving a feasibility problem, which the MIP solver can address by exploring the combinatorial space of activation patterns systematically.

## Limitations
- Scalability challenges when applying to deep networks with many neurons due to combinatorial explosion in binary variables
- Heavy dependence on quality of pre-activation bounds, which may be conservative and limit practical applicability
- Experimental validation limited to small networks and specific use cases, raising questions about broader applicability
