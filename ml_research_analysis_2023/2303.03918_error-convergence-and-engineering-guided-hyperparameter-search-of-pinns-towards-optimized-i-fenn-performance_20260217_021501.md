# Error convergence and engineering-guided hyperparameter search of PINNs: towards optimized I-FENN performance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03918
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03918
- Reference count: 40
- This paper investigates the error convergence and hyperparameter-performance relationship of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) within the context of the Integrated Finite Element Neural Network (I-FENN) framework.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the error convergence and hyperparameter-performance relationship of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) within the context of the Integrated Finite Element Neural Network (I-FENN) framework. The authors conduct a systematic numerical analysis to validate PINN convergence against network complexity and training sample size, and to identify optimal network architectures for predictive accuracy, computational efficiency, and training robustness. Their findings demonstrate consistent convergence of PINN training and global errors, establish that higher network complexity is more impactful than larger datasets, and identify optimal aspect ratios and architectures that minimize computational costs while avoiding training failures. The study fills a gap in PINN literature by providing comprehensive convergence analysis and engineering-guided hyperparameter selection, with implications for broader ML applications in science and engineering.

## Method Summary
The study employs a systematic numerical analysis of PINNs within the I-FENN framework, using FEM models including single-notch, double-notch, and L-shaped specimens with varying mesh discretizations. PINNs are trained using Adam optimizer followed by L-BFGS, with 10 independent runs per configuration. Networks are initialized with Xavier method and use tanh activation. The analysis varies network sizes (8x8, 12x12, 20x20, 32x32) and aspect ratios (0.083 to 12) while fixing total neuron count. Performance metrics include training error, global error (L2-norm of non-local strain relative prediction error), computational efficiency metrics (AdamRT100, IterLBFGS, IterIFENN), and parameter change during training (Δθ, sΔθ).

## Key Results
- PINN error convergence follows the same trends as classical neural networks when network complexity and training dataset size increase
- Higher network complexity is more impactful than larger datasets for reducing PINN errors
- Shallow-and-wide PINNs (high aspect ratio) overfit high-frequency components and are computationally expensive in L-BFGS stage
- Deep-and-narrow PINNs (low aspect ratio) are prone to vanishing gradients, leading to trivial constant solutions
- Optimal aspect ratios and architectures minimize computational costs while avoiding training failures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PINN error convergence follows the same trends as classical neural networks when network complexity and training dataset size increase.
- Mechanism: As PINN depth and width grow, or as more collocation points are added, the approximation error EA decreases due to universal approximation properties; estimation error EE shrinks with more training samples; optimization error EO reduces as the optimizer converges to a local minimum.
- Core assumption: The PINN architecture satisfies universal approximation and the training process behaves like supervised learning with continuous loss minimization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "...consistent converging behavior of the two error types for any investigated combination of network complexity, dataset size and choice of hyperparameters..."
  - [section 3.2] "...both of the training error ET (represented by J) and the global error E (represented by ¯εL2RSE) are minimized as the network complexity and training dataset increase."
  - [corpus] No direct citations, but nearby papers on PINN convergence provide weak indirect support.
- Break condition: If the loss landscape becomes too non-convex or gradients vanish, optimization may stall before reaching a low EO, breaking the assumed convergence trend.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Shallow-and-wide PINNs (high aspect ratio) overfit high-frequency components of the strain field and are computationally expensive in the L-BFGS stage.
- Mechanism: Large width increases parameter count, leading to high-frequency sensitivity; fewer layers mean less regularization, causing overprediction of peak strain; wide architectures require more L-BFGS iterations to converge.
- Core assumption: The target PDE solution has moderate frequency content, and the loss function does not penalize high-frequency deviations strongly.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 7.1] "...shallow-and-wide networks tend to overestimate high frequencies of the strain field..."
  - [section 7.1] "...L-BFGS tends to converge faster, but it has only a minor impact on their predictive accuracy."
  - [corpus] Weak; no direct neighboring papers discussing frequency behavior in PINNs.
- Break condition: If the PDE solution is inherently high-frequency or if regularization terms are added to suppress high-frequency modes, the overprediction issue may disappear.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Deep-and-narrow PINNs (low aspect ratio) are prone to vanishing gradients, leading to trivial constant solutions.
- Mechanism: Many layers with tanh activation cause gradients to shrink exponentially in backprop; weights update minimally, so the network outputs a constant approximating the mean of local strain; the PDE residual collapses to zero.
- Core assumption: Activation function gradients decay multiplicatively through depth; the optimizer does not switch to a different activation or use residual connections.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 7.2] "...very deep-and-narrow networks are prone to the well-known issue of vanishing gradients..."
  - [section 7.2] "...the partial derivatives of the non-local strain with respect to the input variables vanish to zero, and this results in the Laplacian term in Equation 16 vanishing to zero as well."
  - [corpus] Weak; no direct citations on vanishing gradients in PINNs in the neighbor set.
- Break condition: If ReLU or residual connections are used, or if depth is limited, the vanishing gradient failure mode will not occur.

  - Why needed here: Determines balance between depth and width; directly impacts convergence speed, computational cost, and risk of vanishing gradients.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (x, y, g, εeq) -> hidden layers (tanh activation) -> output layer (¯εeq and its derivatives) -> loss function (PDE residual + BC) -> optimizers (Adam → L-BFGS)
- Critical path: Generate collocation points → Forward pass → Compute PDE residuals → Backprop → Update weights → Check convergence → Output predictions
- Design tradeoffs: Depth vs width (aspect ratio) affects accuracy, training time, and risk of vanishing gradients; total neurons trade off expressivity vs computational cost; learning rate and epochs balance speed vs stability
- Failure signatures: Training stalls at trivial constant solution (deep-narrow); Adam training time explodes (deep networks); L-BFGS iterations spike (wide networks); optimizer oscillates near local minima (premature low Δθ)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do PINN error convergence trends extend to time-dependent PDEs and spatio-temporal problems?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study focuses on static PDEs and notes that generalizability to time and space domains remains an open question.
- Why unresolved: The current analysis is limited to steady-state problems, and time-dependent problems introduce additional complexity in terms of temporal discretization and stability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating PINN convergence on time-dependent PDEs (e.g., heat equation, wave equation) with varying temporal discretizations and network architectures.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between PINN network depth and width for problems with different characteristic length scales and solution smoothness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study identifies optimal aspect ratios but doesn't fully explore how these change with problem characteristics.
- Why unresolved: The analysis focuses on a single problem type, and different physical phenomena may require different network architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic studies varying problem length scales and solution smoothness across multiple physical domains, comparing PINN performance for different depth-width combinations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of activation function affect PINN convergence and performance compared to the hyperbolic tangent used in this study?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study uses tanh activation function but doesn't explore alternatives, despite their known impact on gradient flow.
- Why unresolved: Different activation functions can significantly affect optimization dynamics and representational capacity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using various activation functions (ReLU, leaky ReLU, swish, etc.) on the same problem set, measuring convergence rates, final accuracy, and computational efficiency.

## Limitations
- The analysis is restricted to specific PDE formulations and FEM meshes, with limited exploration of activation functions beyond tanh or alternative optimizers
- The claim that shallow-and-wide networks "overfit high-frequency components" lacks direct frequency analysis and supporting citations from neighboring literature
- The study doesn't fully explore how optimal aspect ratios and network sizes change with problem characteristics like length scales and solution smoothness

## Confidence
- High: General convergence trends - consistent numerical results across multiple configurations
- Medium: Aspect ratio tradeoffs - observed performance patterns but weak theoretical grounding
- Low: Claims about frequency behavior and vanishing gradients - absence of direct evidence in the corpus
