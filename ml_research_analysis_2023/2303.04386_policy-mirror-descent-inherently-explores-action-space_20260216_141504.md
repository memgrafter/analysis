# Policy Mirror Descent Inherently Explores Action Space

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.04386
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04386
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: SPMD with TOMC attains O(HD/ε²) sample complexity, inherently exploring action space without explicit exploration strategies

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a sample complexity analysis of on-policy stochastic policy gradient methods for finite MDPs, focusing on policy mirror descent (SPMD). The key insight is that SPMD with a truncated on-policy Monte Carlo (TOMC) evaluation operator inherently explores the action space, eliminating the need for explicit exploration strategies. The paper introduces two on-policy evaluation operators and analyzes their sample complexity, showing that TOMC achieves stronger convergence properties with high-probability bounds. The analysis reveals that the choice of Bregman divergence significantly impacts sample complexity, with Tsallis divergence providing polynomial scaling compared to exponential scaling with KL divergence.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes stochastic policy mirror descent (SPMD) for finite MDPs, introducing two on-policy evaluation operators: value-based estimation (VBE) and truncated on-policy Monte Carlo (TOMC). SPMD uses these operators to construct stochastic first-order information for policy improvement. The method operates under the assumption that Markov chains induced by generated policies are uniformly mixing with non-diminishing minimal visitation measure. Two implementations of VBE are presented, both using a two-step process to avoid direct Q-function estimation bias. TOMC truncates Q-function estimates for actions with low policy probability, ensuring inherent exploration. The paper provides sample complexity bounds for both operators and demonstrates that Tsallis divergence yields polynomial dependence on action space and horizon.

## Key Results
- SPMD with TOMC attains O(HD/ε²) sample complexity with polynomial dependence on action space and horizon
- VBE operator achieves O(1/ε²) sample complexity with linear dependence on action space size
- Tsallis divergence with p=1/2 provides polynomial scaling, while KL divergence leads to exponential scaling
- SPMD inherently explores action space without explicit exploration strategies through TOMC truncation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SPMD with TOMC inherently explores action space without explicit exploration strategies
- Mechanism: TOMC truncates the Q-function estimate to a constant (1/(1-γ)) for actions with policy probability below threshold τ, ensuring these actions are still sampled with non-zero probability during policy improvement
- Core assumption: The Markov chains induced by generated policies are uniformly mixing with non-diminishing minimal visitation measure
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SPMD with the second evaluation operator, namely truncated on-policy Monte Carlo (TOMC), attains an O(HD/ε²) sample complexity, where HD mildly depends on the effective horizon and the size of the action space with properly chosen Bregman divergence"
  - [section 5.1] "TOMC sets up a threshold τ>0, and for any action with its policy value falling below τ, TOMC completely truncates the process of estimating Qπt(s,a) using the collected samples, by simply discarding the samples and directly assigning an upper bound 1/(1-γ) as a trivial estimate"
- Break condition: If the threshold τ is set too high or the mixing assumption fails, the inherent exploration property may break down

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: VBE operator avoids direct Q-function estimation bias by using two-step process
- Mechanism: VBE-I uses two independent trajectories - one to estimate value function, another to construct Q-function using estimated values; VBE-II uses single trajectory with future discounted cost
- Core assumption: The bias of value estimator Vπ,ξV decays linearly with trajectory length n
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "VBE-I maintains two independent trajectories, using one trajectory to construct an estimator of the value function, which is then used in conjunction with the other trajectory, to construct an estimator of the Q-function"
  - [section 4.1] "The bias for each state-action pair in the VBE estimator diminishes with a rate that is linear in the trajectory length"
- Break condition: If the bias of value estimator doesn't decay linearly or if the trajectories are not independent, the VBE approach may fail

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Tsallis divergence provides polynomial dependence on action space and horizon, while KL divergence gives exponential dependence
- Mechanism: Different Bregman divergences lead to drastically different sample complexity bounds; Tsallis divergence with index p=1/2 yields polynomial scaling
- Core assumption: The distance-generating function for Tsallis divergence is strongly-convex with respect to ℓ1-norm
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2] "Tsallis divergence yields a polynomial dependence in both the effective horizon and the action space"
  - [section 5.2] "HD is a divergence-dependent function of the effective horizon and the size of the action space, which leads to an exponential dependence of the latter two quantities for the KL divergence, and a polynomial dependence for the divergence induced by negative Tsallis entropy"
- Break condition: If the strong-convexity assumption for Tsallis divergence doesn't hold or if the index p is not properly chosen, the polynomial scaling may not be achieved

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MDP -> SPMD Algorithm -> TOMC/VBE Operator -> Policy Update -> Next Policy
- Critical path:
  1. Initialize policy π0
  2. For each iteration t:
     - Generate trajectory using current policy πt
     - Evaluate policy using TOMC or VBE operator
     - Update policy using mirror descent with Bregman divergence
  3. Return best policy found

- Design tradeoffs:
  - TOMC vs VBE: TOMC provides high-probability bounds but requires careful parameter tuning; VBE is simpler but only provides expectation bounds
  - KL divergence vs Tsallis divergence: KL divergence is more standard but leads to exponential sample complexity; Tsallis divergence provides polynomial scaling
  - Multiple trajectories vs single trajectory: Multiple trajectories provide better noise control but increase sample complexity; single trajectory is more efficient but requires careful analysis

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor mixing: If the Markov chains don't mix well, the on-policy estimators will have high bias
  - Incorrect threshold τ: If τ is set too high, the inherent exploration may not work; if too low, the truncation may not help
  - Non-optimal Bregman divergence: Using KL divergence when Tsallis would be better could lead to exponential sample complexity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement SPMD with TOMC operator on a simple MDP with known optimal policy, verify that all actions are sampled with non-zero probability throughout optimization
  2. Compare sample complexity of SPMD with KL divergence vs Tsallis divergence on an MDP with large action space, verify polynomial vs exponential scaling
  3. Test VBE operator on an MDP where some actions have very low probability under the optimal policy, verify that the two-step estimation avoids bias issues

## Limitations

- The inherent exploration property relies on uniform mixing assumptions that may not hold for all MDPs
- Performance is sensitive to hyperparameter choices, particularly the truncation threshold τ
- Extension to continuous state and action spaces remains unclear

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Sample complexity bounds for SPMD with VBE and TOMC are mathematically derived with clear assumptions
- **Medium confidence**: Inherent exploration property is demonstrated through theoretical analysis but may be affected by practical implementation details
- **Low confidence**: Generalization to continuous spaces and impact of poor Markov chain mixing are not thoroughly explored
