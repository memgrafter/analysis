# PURR: Efficiently Editing Language Model Hallucinations by Denoising Language Model Corruptions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14908
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14908
- Reference count: 21
- Large language models frequently generate false claims ("hallucinations"), but existing methods for correcting these are either too slow (prompt-based) or too limited in scope (fine-tuned).

## Executive Summary
Large language models frequently generate false claims ("hallucinations"), but existing methods for correcting these are either too slow (prompt-based) or too limited in scope (fine-tuned). This work introduces a novel data generation approach that leverages large language models to create synthetic "hallucinations" by corrupting factual text, then trains compact editors to denoise these corruptions using retrieved evidence. The resulting model, PURR, outperforms much larger prompted editors on multiple benchmarks for improving attribution and factuality, while being orders of magnitude faster and more efficient. PURR sets a new state-of-the-art on the unified F1AP metric across three diverse tasks, demonstrating that compact models can rival the performance of trillion-parameter models when trained on well-constructed synthetic data.

## Method Summary
PURR addresses language model hallucinations through a two-stage approach: synthetic data generation and denoising fine-tuning. First, the method generates synthetic hallucinations by corrupting factual text through three strategies: dropping facts, replacing facts with retrieved alternatives, or inserting irrelevant facts. Large language models are prompted to apply these corruptions to create training pairs. Second, a compact language model is fine-tuned to denoise these corruptions by retrieving evidence and reconstructing factual accuracy. The denoising editor is trained on this synthetic data to produce factual corrections while preserving the original context and style. This approach enables efficient hallucination correction without requiring computationally expensive prompting or extensive manual annotation.

## Key Results
- PURR outperforms much larger prompted editors on multiple benchmarks for improving attribution and factuality
- Sets new state-of-the-art on unified F1AP metric across three diverse tasks
- Achieves orders of magnitude faster and more efficient performance than existing methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PURR works by training compact editors on synthetic hallucinations created through controlled corruption of factual text. The method leverages large language models to generate diverse training data by systematically introducing factual errors into clean text, then uses retrieved evidence to guide the denoising process. This approach allows the compact editor to learn effective correction patterns while maintaining efficiency. By focusing on denoising rather than comprehensive rewriting, PURR preserves original context and style while targeting specific factual inaccuracies.

- **Synthetic data generation**: Why needed - Creating training data without expensive manual annotation. Quick check - Does the corruption strategy produce diverse and realistic hallucinations?

- **Evidence retrieval integration**: Why needed - Grounding corrections in verifiable sources. Quick check - Is retrieved evidence relevant and correctly applied to the corrections?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Corruption generator -> Evidence retriever -> Denoising editor -> Factual accuracy checker
**Critical path:** Corruption generation → Synthetic dataset creation → Evidence-augmented fine-tuning → Evaluation
**Design tradeoffs:** The method trades some precision in real-world generalization for efficiency gains through synthetic data, and prioritizes factual accuracy over full-text fluency.
**Failure signatures:** Potential issues include over-reliance on synthetic patterns that don't match real hallucinations, incorrect evidence retrieval leading to wrong corrections, and degradation of text style or coherence.
**First experiments:** 1) Test corruption strategies on sample factual text to verify diversity of generated hallucinations. 2) Evaluate evidence retrieval accuracy on sample corrections. 3) Compare denoised outputs against ground truth for precision and recall.

## Limitations
- Quality and diversity of synthetic hallucinations may not fully capture real-world factual errors
- Reliance on retrieved evidence assumes availability of reliable supporting documents
- Limited assessment of broader language quality impacts beyond attribution and factuality

## Confidence
**High confidence** in efficiency gains and computational advantages
**Medium confidence** in general applicability of synthetic data generation approach
**Medium confidence** in relative performance improvements on benchmark tasks
**Low confidence** in method's effectiveness across diverse real-world applications

## Next Checks
