# SamToNe: Improving Contrastive Loss for Dual Encoder Retrieval Models with Same Tower Negatives

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.02516
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02516
- **Reference count:** 5
- **Primary result:** SamToNe improves dual encoder retrieval performance by adding same-tower negatives to contrastive loss

## Executive Summary
SamToNe (Same Tower Negatives) is a novel contrastive loss objective designed to improve the training of dual encoder retrieval models. By incorporating queries or documents from the same encoder tower as negatives alongside standard in-batch negatives, SamToNe encourages better alignment between the two embedding spaces. Evaluated across question-answering retrieval tasks and zero-shot information retrieval benchmarks, SamToNe consistently enhances retrieval performance, particularly when applied to the query tower. The method acts as an effective regularizer, pushing similarity ratios between same-tower and cross-tower pairs toward 1.0, resulting in more aligned embedding spaces and improved retrieval quality.

## Method Summary
SamToNe enhances the standard contrastive loss for dual encoder retrieval models by adding same-tower negatives. During training, the loss function includes not only cross-tower negative pairs (query-document) but also same-tower negatives (query-query or document-document) in the denominator. This encourages the model to treat queries or documents within the same tower as mutually exclusive while maintaining alignment between the two towers. The method requires no architectural changes and can be applied to any dual encoder setup, making it broadly applicable to tasks like question-answering retrieval and zero-shot information retrieval.

## Key Results
- SamToNe consistently improves retrieval performance across QA tasks and zero-shot IR benchmarks
- Best performance achieved when SamToNe is applied to the query tower
- SamToNe acts as an effective regularizer, pushing similarity ratios between same-tower and cross-tower pairs toward 1.0

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SamToNe regularizes the embedding space by pushing query-query similarity distributions toward 1.0.
- Mechanism: Adding same-tower negatives in the contrastive loss denominator forces the model to treat queries as mutually exclusive, pulling them apart while keeping cross-tower alignments tight.
- Core assumption: Query embeddings are not uniformly distributed and need additional contrastive pressure to achieve alignment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SamToNe acts as an effective regularizer, pushing similarity ratios between same-tower and cross-tower pairs towards 1.0."
  - [section]: "By placing the regularizing query-query similarity terms ... together in the denominator with same weight, SamToNe pushes the similarity ratio ... to be centered around 1.0."
  - [corpus]: Weak—no direct corpus comparison; only inferred from paper's internal analysis.
- Break condition: If query-query similarities are already uniformly low, the regularizer may have negligible effect or cause over-separation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SamToNe encourages embedding space alignment without requiring architectural changes.
- Mechanism: Same-tower negatives implicitly enforce alignment between query and document encoders by penalizing high similarity within the same tower.
- Core assumption: Alignment can be improved through loss regularization rather than explicit architectural sharing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "By directly probing the embedding spaces ... via the t-SNE algorithm ... we observe that SamToNe ensures the alignment between the embedding spaces."
  - [section]: "SamToNe is proposed as the 'force' drawing the embeddings of each ground truth training pair together."
  - [corpus]: Weak—no corpus study, only internal t-SNE visualizations.
- Break condition: If both towers are already perfectly aligned, adding same-tower negatives may degrade performance by introducing unnecessary noise.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SamToNe improves generalization in zero-shot settings by producing more coherent embedding neighborhoods.
- Mechanism: Better alignment and separation in training embeddings translate to more stable nearest-neighbor rankings in unseen domains.
- Core assumption: Improved local structure in embedding space generalizes to new distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "By evaluating on ... heterogenous zero-shot information retrieval benchmarks (BEIR) ... we demonstrate that SamToNe can effectively improve the retrieval quality."
  - [section]: "Evaluated with the same setting as GTR ... SamToNe demonstrates strong performance on BEIR."
  - [corpus]: Weak—no explicit corpus evidence; relies on benchmark results.
- Break condition: If zero-shot domains differ drastically in semantic distribution, regularization benefits may not transfer.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Two encoder towers (query and document) -> Shared projection layer (for ADE-SPL) -> SamToNe loss wrapper

- Critical path:
  1. Encode queries and documents
  2. Compute cosine similarities
  3. Apply standard in-batch softmax
  4. Add same-tower similarity terms to denominator
  5. Backpropagate combined loss

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pro: No extra hyperparameters, single-stage training, improves alignment
  - Con: May hurt performance on datasets with many duplicate documents if applied bidirectionally

- Failure signatures:
  - Query embeddings collapse to a single point
  - Retrieval quality degrades on datasets with low query diversity
  - Over-regularization causes loss of discriminative power

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare P@1/MRR with and without SamToNe on a small QA dataset
  2. Visualize t-SNE embeddings to confirm alignment improvement
  3. Test zero-shot performance on BEIR after MS MARCO fine-tuning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SamToNe's performance vary with different query distributions, especially when a large fraction of similar queries are present in the training set?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that SamToNe's efficacy may depend on the diversity of the queries used as inputs, and that techniques like masking might be needed for datasets with many similar queries.
- Why unresolved: The paper only briefly mentions this limitation and suggests it as future work, without providing empirical evidence or specific guidelines on handling diverse query distributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying query diversity in the training set, measuring SamToNe's performance degradation with increasing query similarity, and demonstrating the effectiveness of proposed masking techniques.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can SamToNe be effectively applied to other contrastive loss functions beyond in-batch sampled softmax, such as triplet loss?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that applying SamToNe to other contrastive losses is left as future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses solely on demonstrating SamToNe's effectiveness with in-batch sampled softmax loss, without exploring its applicability to other contrastive loss formulations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of SamToNe with alternative contrastive losses like triplet loss, comparing performance gains across different loss functions on standard retrieval benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for applying SamToNe to both query and document towers when datasets contain a large fraction of duplicated documents?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that applying SamToNe to the document tower can hinder performance on datasets with low document uniqueness, but doesn't provide concrete solutions for this scenario.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but leaves the exploration of optimal strategies for such cases as future work, without proposing specific methodologies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different strategies (e.g., selective application, modified loss formulations, document filtering) on datasets with varying levels of document duplication, measuring the trade-offs between alignment quality and performance degradation.

## Limitations

- Performance may degrade on datasets with many duplicate documents when SamToNe is applied bidirectionally
- The paper relies heavily on internal analysis rather than extensive ablation studies
- The asymmetric dual encoder (ADE-SPL) implementation may limit generalizability to other dual encoder architectures
