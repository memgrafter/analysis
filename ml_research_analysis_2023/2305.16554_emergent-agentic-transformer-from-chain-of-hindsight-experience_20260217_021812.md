# Emergent Agentic Transformer from Chain of Hindsight Experience

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16554
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16554
- Reference count: 25
- State-of-the-art performance on D4RL and ExoRL offline RL benchmarks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Agentic Transformer (AT), a transformer-based decision-making model that learns by combining information from multiple sub-optimal trials. The key innovation is using chain of hindsight experience, where trajectories are relabeled and sorted by returns, allowing the model to improve upon itself both during training and test time. AT achieves state-of-the-art results on D4RL and ExoRL benchmarks, outperforming both temporal-difference and imitation-learning-based approaches. The model shows promising scaling trends, with larger models consistently improving performance. Notably, AT can achieve desired target returns through multiple trajectories at test time, demonstrating its ability to learn from sub-optimal data and improve upon its own experience.

## Method Summary
Agentic Transformer uses a GPT-style decoder-only transformer to predict actions autoregressively from sequences of trajectories. The key innovation is chain of hindsight experience, where trajectories are sorted by total return and relabeled to use the maximum return in the sequence as the target. The model trains on these relabeled sequences, learning to generate higher-return behaviors by "stitching" together patterns from sub-optimal experiences. At test time, multiple trajectories are rolled out sequentially, with the model improving its performance based on the accumulated hindsight experience. The architecture includes modality-specific linear embeddings for returns-to-go, states, actions, rewards, and completion tokens, combined with positional encoding and causal attention masking.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on D4RL and ExoRL offline RL benchmarks
- Demonstrates consistent performance improvement with larger model sizes (Small → Base → Large → XLarge)
- Successfully learns from sub-optimal data by improving performance through multiple trajectories at test time
- Outperforms both temporal-difference methods (DT, TD3+BC) and imitation-learning approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Agentic Transformer learns to improve from multiple sub-optimal trials by relabeling trajectories with hindsight experience.
- Mechanism: The model trains on sequences of trajectories sorted by ascending total rewards, where each trajectory's target return is relabeled to the maximum return in the sequence. This allows the model to "stitch" sub-optimal experiences into higher-return behaviors through autoregressive action prediction.
- Core assumption: The transformer architecture can effectively extract useful patterns from sequences of sub-optimal trajectories when conditioned on relabeled returns-to-go and task completion tokens.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our method consists of relabelling target return of each trajectory to the maximum total reward among in sequence of trajectories"
  - [section] "Equation 7 sets the hindsight target: for all n trajectories, initial target equals to trajectoryn's total reward"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers discuss hindsight experience replay but don't provide direct evidence for transformer-based relabeling effectiveness
- Break condition: If the transformer cannot learn meaningful patterns from sub-optimal sequences, or if the relabeling strategy doesn't create useful supervision signals.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The task completion token provides critical feedback that helps the model learn when it has achieved the desired return target.
- Mechanism: The completion token d indicates whether the cumulative reward in a trajectory meets or exceeds the desired return-to-go. This binary signal helps the model understand which trajectories successfully achieved their targets and adjust behavior accordingly.
- Core assumption: The completion token provides sufficient information for the transformer to distinguish successful from unsuccessful attempts and learn appropriate action sequences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We feed the model with a completion token d that indicates whether the achieved cumulative rewards in a trajectory are larger than or equal to desired returns-to-go"
  - [section] "Equation 2 states the ordering requirement, meaning that trajectories are ascending sorted according to their total reward"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers don't discuss completion tokens in transformer architectures
- Break condition: If the completion token doesn't provide useful gradient signals, or if the model ignores this signal during training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Larger transformer models consistently improve performance due to better pattern extraction from diverse trajectory data.
- Mechanism: Scaling the transformer model size (Small → Base → Large → XLarge) increases the model's capacity to learn complex relationships between states, actions, rewards, and returns-to-go across multiple trajectories.
- Core assumption: Model capacity scales beneficially with the complexity of the chain of hindsight experience patterns, and larger models can extract more useful information from the same data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our Agentic Transformer also shows a promising scaling trend that bigger models consistently improve results"
  - [section] "Figure 4 shows the results. Agentic Transformer improves with larger model size, showing promising scaling behavior"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers don't provide direct evidence about transformer scaling in this specific context
- Break condition: If increasing model size leads to diminishing returns or if the model becomes overparameterized for the task complexity.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Chain of hindsight experience
  - Why needed here: This technique allows learning from sub-optimal data by relabeling trajectories with higher return targets, enabling the model to learn improvement strategies rather than just imitation.
  - Quick check question: If you have trajectories with returns [10, 15, 20], what target return would you assign to the first two trajectories using chain of hindsight?

- Concept: Autoregressive sequence modeling
  - Why needed here: The transformer predicts actions sequentially based on previous states, actions, and returns, which is essential for generating coherent behavior policies.
  - Quick check question: In autoregressive modeling, what is the key architectural constraint that prevents the model from "cheating" by looking at future tokens?

- Concept: Hindsight experience replay
  - Why needed here: This concept is adapted from goal-conditioned RL where failed attempts are relabeled as successful attempts for different goals, allowing learning from sparse rewards.
  - Quick check question: How does hindsight experience replay differ from standard experience replay in terms of the data it generates?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: States, actions, rewards, returns-to-go, and completion tokens → Modality-specific linear embeddings → Positional episodic timestep encoding → GPT-style decoder-only transformer with causal self-attention masking → Action prediction

- Critical path: Data → Trajectory sorting and relabeling → Token embedding → Transformer layers → Action prediction → Environment interaction. The most critical components are the relabeling step and the causal attention mask.

- Design tradeoffs: The model uses a fixed number of trajectories (4 by default) in each training sequence, which balances computational cost against the richness of hindsight experience. Using more trajectories increases memory usage quadratically with sequence length.

- Failure signatures: Poor performance on diverse datasets despite good results on expert data suggests the relabeling strategy isn't working. Lack of improvement with more trajectories at test time indicates the completion token feedback isn't being utilized effectively.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on D4RL Medium dataset with n=2 trajectories vs n=4 trajectories to verify the impact of chain length
  2. Remove the completion token 'd' to confirm its importance for learning from hindsight
  3. Test with varying target returns (1st, 2nd, 3rd trajectory total rewards) to verify the hindsight relabeling effectiveness

## Limitations
- Weak empirical evidence for the effectiveness of chain of hindsight experience and completion token mechanisms
- Limited analysis of scaling behavior beyond tested model sizes
- High computational cost due to quadratic memory complexity of self-attention with long sequences
- Limited generalization testing beyond D4RL and ExoRL benchmarks

## Confidence

- **Medium confidence**: The overall performance claims on D4RL and ExoRL benchmarks, as these are empirically measured results
- **Low confidence**: The specific mechanisms by which chain of hindsight experience and completion tokens enable learning from sub-optimal data
- **Medium confidence**: The scaling behavior claims, though the underlying reasons remain unclear

## Next Checks

1. **Mechanism ablation study**: Remove the completion token 'd' and the hindsight relabeling strategy separately to quantify their individual contributions to performance improvements. This would clarify whether the claimed mechanisms are actually driving the results.

2. **Scaling analysis beyond tested sizes**: Evaluate the model's performance with transformer sizes larger than XLarge and analyze whether the scaling continues linearly or shows diminishing returns. Include computational cost analysis to assess practical feasibility.

3. **Generalization to diverse environments**: Test the Agentic Transformer on a broader range of control tasks beyond the D4RL and ExoRL benchmarks, including environments with different state/action dimensionalities and reward structures, to validate the robustness of the chain of hindsight experience approach.
