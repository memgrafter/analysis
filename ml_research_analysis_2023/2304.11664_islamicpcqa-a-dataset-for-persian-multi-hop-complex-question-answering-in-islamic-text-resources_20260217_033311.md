# IslamicPCQA: A Dataset for Persian Multi-hop Complex Question Answering in Islamic Text Resources

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11664
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11664
- Reference count: 40
- First Persian dataset for complex multi-hop question answering from Islamic texts with 12,282 QA pairs

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces IslamicPCQA, the first Persian dataset designed for answering complex, multi-hop questions from non-structured Islamic text sources. The dataset comprises 12,282 question-answer pairs extracted from 9 Islamic encyclopedias, supporting various reasoning types including intermediary entity, comparison, and multi-step reasoning. Four pre-trained models (XLM-RoBERTa, ParsBERT, BERT multilingual, mT5) were fine-tuned on the dataset, demonstrating the resource's viability for training and evaluating Persian question answering systems in specialized domains.

## Method Summary
The dataset was constructed by extracting question-answer pairs from 9 Persian Islamic encyclopedias, focusing on complex questions requiring multi-hop reasoning. The questions were designed to test various reasoning types, including intermediary entity identification, comparison operations, and sequential logical steps. Four pre-trained transformer models were fine-tuned on the dataset: XLM-RoBERTa, ParsBERT, BERT multilingual, and mT5. Models were evaluated using F1 scores in distractor settings, where questions include both relevant and irrelevant answer candidates to assess discrimination ability.

## Key Results
- XLM-RoBERTa achieved the highest performance with F1 score of 80.44
- ParsBERT achieved F1 score of 75.84
- BERT multilingual achieved F1 score of 72.37
- mT5 achieved the lowest performance with F1 score of 61.28

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dataset succeeds by addressing a critical gap in Persian language resources for complex question answering. The multi-hop nature of questions requires models to perform sequential reasoning across multiple text passages, making it suitable for training sophisticated QA systems. The domain-specific focus on Islamic texts provides rich, structured knowledge while maintaining linguistic consistency. The distractor setting evaluation ensures models learn to distinguish relevant from irrelevant information, improving practical applicability.

## Limitations
- Performance variance between models (61.28-80.44 F1) suggests potential dataset-level inconsistencies
- Reliance on Islamic encyclopedias may limit representation of broader Persian language patterns
- Distractor setting evaluation may not fully capture real-world performance without explicit negative examples

## Confidence
- Dataset novelty and significance: **High** - well-documented gap in Persian QA resources
- Model performance claims: **Medium** - strong performance for some models but significant variance suggests potential dataset-level factors
- Domain applicability: **Medium** - specialized but potentially limited generalizability
