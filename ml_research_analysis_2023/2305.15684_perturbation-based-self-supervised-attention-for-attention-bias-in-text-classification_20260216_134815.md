# Perturbation-based Self-supervised Attention for Attention Classification

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.15684
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15684
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Perturbation-based self-supervised attention improves text classification by refining attention distributions without additional annotation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses attention bias in text classification, where models focus excessively on frequent words while ignoring domain-specific terms. The proposed perturbation-based self-supervised attention (PBSA) approach adds noise to word embeddings while preserving semantics and predictions, using perturbation amplitude to infer word importance. The method refines attention distributions without requiring additional annotation, addressing limitations of existing self-supervised approaches that rely on masking schemes and suffer from out-of-distribution problems.

## Method Summary
The method involves pre-training a baseline model (BERT, BiLSTM, or MemoryNet), generating attention supervision through perturbation-based word importance scoring using the WBCP mechanism, and retraining the model with attention supervision via KL divergence. The perturbation amplitude for each word is optimized to add as much noise as possible while maintaining semantic meaning and classification accuracy. This amplitude is then inverted to create an importance distribution that serves as supervision for the attention weights. The process is applied iteratively T times to capture the important word distribution more accurately.

## Key Results
- PBSA significantly improves performance of attention-based models on three text classification tasks
- The approach outperforms existing self-supervised methods that rely on masking schemes
- Improvements are particularly notable on smaller models like Att-BiLSTM compared to larger pre-trained models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Perturbation amplitude indicates word importance in text classification
- **Mechanism**: Adding noise to word embeddings while preserving semantics and predictions, with the hypothesis that words tolerating more noise are less significant
- **Core assumption**: Semantic meaning and classification outcome remain unchanged when noise is added below a certain threshold
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "we add as much noise as possible to all the words in the sentence without changing their semantics and predictions"; [section] "under the premise of trying not to change the semantics of the input text, unimportant words can withstand more changes than more significant ones"
- **Break condition**: If semantic drift occurs before classification change, or if the model's decision boundary is highly sensitive to small perturbations

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Sample-specific attention supervision improves attention distribution
- **Mechanism**: The perturbation amplitude is converted to an importance distribution using the inverse relationship (smaller amplitude = higher importance), then used as supervision for attention weights
- **Core assumption**: The inverse of perturbation tolerance correlates with true importance for classification
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "We hypothesize that words that tolerate more noise are less significant, and we can use this information to refine the attention distribution"; [section] "We can therefore use the perturbation magnitude to compute a word-level importance distribution as attention supervision information"
- **Break condition**: If the model learns to ignore the supervision signal or if the perturbation-based distribution doesn't align with actual importance

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Self-supervised perturbation avoids OOD problems of masking schemes
- **Mechanism**: Instead of masking tokens and creating out-of-distribution samples, noise is added in the embedding space which maintains distributional consistency
- **Core assumption**: Embedding space perturbations preserve the data distribution better than token replacement
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "it incorporates Perturbation-based Self-supervised Attention (PBSA) to avoid the OOD problem caused by the masking scheme"; [section] "the quantification occurs in the embedding space rather than replacing the token with a predefined value, thus avoiding the OOD problem caused by masking schemes"
- **Break condition**: If embedding perturbations still create OOD samples or if the model's representation layer is highly sensitive to noise

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How can the perturbation-based self-supervised attention method be effectively combined with transformer models that use multiple-layer and multiple-head attention?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper mentions exploring two simple strategies to combine the approach with Transformers, but does not provide a definitive solution
  - **Why unresolved**: The paper acknowledges the difficulty in selecting a suitable head as the supervised target and the challenge in effectively combining multiple-layer and multiple-head attention with the proposed method
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Developing a more sophisticated method to combine the perturbation-based approach with transformer models, and demonstrating its effectiveness through experiments

- **Open Question 2**: How does the performance of the perturbation-based self-supervised attention method vary with different sample ratios in low-resource scenarios?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper conducts experiments on different sample ratios and observes that the improvement is more prominent when the ratio is in the middle range
  - **Why unresolved**: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the performance variation across a wider range of sample ratios or explain the underlying reasons for the observed trends
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Conducting extensive experiments with various sample ratios and providing a theoretical explanation for the observed performance trends

- **Open Question 3**: How does the size of the pre-trained model affect the performance of the perturbation-based self-supervised attention method?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper observes that the improvements on pre-trained models are relatively marginal compared to smaller models like Att-BiLSTM and suggests that the pre-training on large corpora relieves the attention bias to some extent
  - **Why unresolved**: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the relationship between model size and the effectiveness of the proposed method, nor does it explore the underlying reasons for the observed differences
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Conducting experiments with pre-trained models of varying sizes and analyzing the impact on the performance of the perturbation-based method, along with a theoretical explanation for the observed trends

## Limitations

- The perturbation-importance relationship lacks rigorous empirical validation across diverse datasets
- Claims about OOD problem avoidance compared to masking schemes are not quantitatively supported
- The effectiveness of perturbation-based attention supervision needs direct validation against ground truth importance

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core methodology of using perturbation-based self-supervision is clearly specified and follows established patterns in self-supervised learning. The experimental setup and evaluation metrics are standard.
- **Medium confidence**: The improvement claims over baseline models are supported by experimental results, but the ablation studies could be more comprehensive to isolate the contribution of each component.
- **Low confidence**: The claims about OOD problem avoidance and the perturbation-importance relationship lack direct empirical support within the paper.

## Next Checks

1. **Perturbation validation study**: Conduct controlled experiments on synthetic data where ground truth word importance is known to verify if perturbation amplitude correlates with actual importance for classification.

2. **OOD characterization analysis**: Compare embedding perturbations versus token masking using OOD detection metrics (e.g., density estimation, reconstruction error) to empirically validate the OOD claims.

3. **Attention supervision ablation**: Perform an ablation study removing the attention supervision component to quantify its exact contribution to performance improvements, and analyze whether the model learns to ignore the supervision signal over training iterations.
