# Compression of GPS Trajectories using Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07420
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07420
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: LSTM-based autoencoders outperform Douglas-Peucker line simplification in trajectory compression, reconstructing all points with superior Fréchet and DTW distances at higher compression ratios.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes an LSTM-based autoencoder for compressing GPS trajectories, addressing the challenge of managing large volumes of location data from mobile devices. The method encodes trajectories into a lower-dimensional latent representation and reconstructs them, with adjustable compression ratios via latent space size. Evaluated on both synthetic (Quake III) and real-world (T-Drive) datasets using Fréchet distance, dynamic time warping, and haversine distance, the autoencoder significantly outperforms traditional Douglas-Peucker line simplification, especially at higher compression ratios. The approach consistently reconstructs every point rather than selecting a subset, showing promise for practical GPS trajectory compression applications.

## Method Summary
The proposed method employs an LSTM-based autoencoder architecture to compress GPS trajectories. Trajectories are encoded into a lower-dimensional latent representation, which is then decoded to reconstruct the original path. The compression ratio is controlled by adjusting the size of the latent space. The autoencoder is trained end-to-end to minimize reconstruction error, enabling it to learn compact representations that preserve trajectory shape and spatial relationships. Unlike traditional line simplification methods, the autoencoder reconstructs every point rather than selecting a subset, providing smoother and more continuous outputs.

## Key Results
- The autoencoder significantly outperforms Douglas-Peucker line simplification in Fréchet and DTW distance metrics, especially at higher compression ratios.
- The method reconstructs every point in the trajectory, rather than selecting a subset as in traditional methods.
- Performance is consistent across different trajectory lengths and datasets, though DTW and time-synchronized comparisons sometimes favor Douglas-Peucker.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: The LSTM-based autoencoder likely works by learning temporal dependencies and spatial patterns in GPS trajectories during training, allowing it to compress these patterns into a compact latent representation. The decoder then uses this compressed representation to reconstruct the trajectory with minimal loss of shape and spatial information. This learned compression likely captures both local path details and global trajectory structure more effectively than rule-based methods like Douglas-Peucker, which rely on fixed distance thresholds and may oversimplify complex paths.
