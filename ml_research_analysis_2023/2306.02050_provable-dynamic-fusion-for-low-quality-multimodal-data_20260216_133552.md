# Provable Dynamic Fusion for Low-Quality Multimodal Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02050
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02050
- Reference count: 32
- QMF achieves 68.81% accuracy on NYU Depth V2 with 50% Gaussian noise vs 61.04% for best baseline

## Executive Summary
This paper provides theoretical justifications for robust multimodal fusion under low-quality data by using uncertainty estimation to dynamically weight modalities during fusion. The key insight is that dynamic fusion achieves better generalization than static fusion when fusion weights are negatively correlated with unimodal generalization errors. Based on this theoretical foundation, the authors propose Quality-aware Multimodal Fusion (QMF) that uses energy-based uncertainty to characterize modality quality. Extensive experiments demonstrate QMF outperforms state-of-the-art methods on NYU Depth V2, SUN RGB-D, UPMC FOOD101, and MVSA datasets.

## Method Summary
QMF uses energy-based uncertainty estimation to characterize the quality of each modality and dynamically weight their contributions during fusion. The framework computes uncertainty scores for each modality, then generates fusion weights that are inversely proportional to uncertainty. A regularization term based on historical training trajectory further improves the correlation between uncertainty estimates and actual losses. The method is evaluated on four datasets with various noise levels, showing consistent improvements over static fusion and other dynamic fusion baselines.

## Key Results
- QMF achieves 68.81% accuracy on NYU Depth V2 with 50% Gaussian noise compared to 61.04% for best baseline
- Dynamic fusion provides better worst-case accuracy than static fusion under high noise levels
- Energy-based uncertainty estimation shows better correlation with actual losses than predictive confidence methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Dynamic fusion achieves better generalization when fusion weights are negatively correlated with unimodal generalization errors
- **Mechanism:** The theoretical framework proves that the generalization error bound of multimodal fusion is composed of three terms: average empirical loss, average complexity, and covariance between fusion weight and loss. When dynamic fusion weights satisfy E(wm_dynamic) = wm_static and r(wm_dynamic, ℓ(f m)) ≤ 0, the covariance term becomes non-positive, reducing the overall error bound below static fusion
- **Core assumption:** The correlation between uncertainty estimates and actual losses is positive (Assumption 1: r(um, ℓm(x)) ≥ 0)
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "the authors prove that dynamic fusion achieves better generalization than static fusion when the fusion weights are negatively correlated with unimodal generalization errors"
  - [section]: Theorem 2 explicitly states conditions under which dynamic fusion outperforms static fusion
  - [corpus]: Weak - related works focus on fusion frameworks but don't provide theoretical correlation proofs
- **Break condition:** If uncertainty estimates are not positively correlated with actual losses, the covariance term may not be non-positive, breaking the theoretical advantage

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Uncertainty estimation provides a principled way to design dynamic fusion weights
- **Mechanism:** Energy-based uncertainty measures (e.g., energy score) capture the quality of each modality. Lower energy indicates higher confidence. By setting fusion weights proportional to uncertainty with negative coefficients (wm(x) = αm um(x) + βm where αm < 0), unreliable modalities receive lower weights
- **Core assumption:** Energy score effectively captures out-of-distribution uncertainty
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we leverage energy-based uncertainty to characterize the quality of each modality"
  - [section]: "energy-based uncertainty to characterize the quality of each modality" and "Energy score emerges as a promising way to capture Out-of-Distribution (OOD) uncertainty"
  - [corpus]: Weak - while related works mention uncertainty, none specifically validate energy score for multimodal fusion quality estimation
- **Break condition:** If energy score fails to capture modality quality (e.g., poor correlation with actual performance), the weighting mechanism becomes ineffective

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Regularization based on historical training trajectory enhances the correlation between uncertainty and loss
- **Mechanism:** The proposed Lreg term uses sample-wise training loss history to create supervision: κm_i ≥ κm_j ⇐ ⇒ wm_i ≤ wm_j. This encourages weights to reflect the relative difficulty of samples, improving the alignment between estimated uncertainty and actual performance
- **Core assumption:** Easy-to-classify samples are learned earlier during training compared to hard-to-classify samples
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "Inspired by recent works in Bayesian learning (Maddox et al., 2019) and uncertainty estimation (Moon et al., 2020; Han et al., 2022a), we propose to leverage the information from historical training trajectory to regularize the fusion weights"
  - [section]: "we propose to leverage the information from historical training trajectory to regularize the fusion weights"
  - [corpus]: Weak - related works focus on fusion but don't validate this specific regularization approach
- **Break condition:** If the assumption about training dynamics doesn't hold (e.g., hard samples learned early), the regularization may introduce noise rather than improve correlation

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input → Unimodal classifiers → Uncertainty estimators → Weight generator → Fusion layer → Output
- **Critical path:** Input → Unimodal classifiers → Uncertainty estimators → Weight generator → Fusion layer → Output
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Simple uncertainty estimation (faster, less accurate) vs complex methods (slower, potentially more reliable)
  - Strong regularization (better correlation, possible overfitting) vs weak regularization (faster training, weaker correlation)
  - Temperature parameters (affect uncertainty calibration) vs fixed weights (simpler but less adaptive)
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor correlation between uncertainty and actual losses (weights don't reflect modality quality)
  - Degraded performance under low noise (over-regularization)
  - Instability in weight computation (temperature parameters poorly tuned)
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Correlation validation:** Test Pearson correlation between energy-based uncertainty and actual losses on clean validation data
  2. **Weight behavior analysis:** Visualize fusion weights across noise levels to verify they decrease for degraded modalities
  3. **Ablation study:** Compare performance with and without Lreg to quantify its contribution to robustness

## Limitations

- The theoretical framework assumes perfect correlation between uncertainty estimates and actual losses, which may not hold in practice
- The effectiveness of energy-based uncertainty for multimodal fusion quality estimation is weakly supported by related works
- The regularization approach based on training trajectory assumes a specific learning dynamic that may not generalize across tasks

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mathematical framework for proving dynamic fusion advantages is sound and well-defined
- **Medium confidence**: The experimental results show consistent improvements across multiple datasets and noise levels
- **Low confidence**: The practical implementation details for energy-based uncertainty estimation and regularization are underspecified
