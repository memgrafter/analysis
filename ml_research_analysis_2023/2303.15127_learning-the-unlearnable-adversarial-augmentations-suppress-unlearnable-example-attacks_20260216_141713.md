# Learning the Unlearnable: Adversarial Augmentations Suppress Unlearnable Example Attacks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.15127
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15127
- **Reference count**: 37
- **Key outcome**: UEraser defends against unlearnable example attacks by using error-maximizing data augmentations that disrupt "shortcuts" formed by unlearnable perturbations, achieving high accuracy on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and ImageNet-subset datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UEraser, a method to defend against unlearnable example attacks, which add perturbations to images to make them unlearnable and safeguard data privacy. UEraser counters these attacks by using error-maximizing data augmentation policies that extend beyond the ℓp perturbation bounds assumed by current unlearnable attacks. The approach disrupts the "shortcuts" formed by unlearnable perturbations, enabling models to learn from the original features and achieve high clean test accuracy. UEraser outperforms existing defenses, including ISS and adversarial training, and shows resilience against adaptive attacks and transfer learning scenarios.

## Method Summary
UEraser defends against unlearnable example attacks by using error-maximizing data augmentation policies. It applies transformations like PlasmaTransform, TrivialAugment, and ChannelShuffle that extend beyond the ℓp perturbation bounds assumed by unlearnable attacks. By maximizing the adversarial loss through these augmentations, UEraser disrupts the "shortcuts" formed by unlearnable perturbations, allowing models to learn from the original features. The method involves two stages: adversarial augmentation (K=5) and UEraser-Lite (K=1). UEraser-Lite, a faster variant without loss maximization, is also highly effective in preserving clean accuracies.

## Key Results
- UEraser achieves high clean test accuracy on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and ImageNet-subset datasets with unlearnable perturbations.
- UEraser outperforms existing defenses like ISS and adversarial training, particularly on CIFAR-10 with EM unlearnable examples.
- UEraser shows resilience against adaptive attacks and transfer learning scenarios, maintaining high accuracy even when the model is fine-tuned on different datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial augmentations disrupt the "shortcuts" formed by unlearnable perturbations.
- Mechanism: UEraser applies transformations (e.g., PlasmaTransform, TrivialAugment, ChannelShuffle) that extend beyond the ℓp perturbation bounds assumed by unlearnable attacks. These aggressive augmentations break the correlation between the added perturbations and model predictions.
- Core assumption: Unlearnable attacks rely on constrained perturbations (within small ℓp balls) that form predictable "shortcuts" for the model. Expanding the perturbation space beyond these bounds disrupts these shortcuts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "UEraser uses adversarial augmentations, which extends beyond the confines of ℓp perturbation budget assumed by current unlearning attacks and defenses."
  - [section] "Intuitively, UEraser finds the most 'adversarial' augmentation policies for the current images, and use that to train the model in order to prevent unlearnable 'shortcuts' from emerging during model training."
  - [corpus] Weak. Related papers discuss unlearnable examples but do not specifically analyze shortcut disruption via augmentation.
- Break condition: If unlearnable attacks adapt to include broader transformations or if augmentations are constrained back to ℓp bounds.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Error-maximizing data augmentation policies improve model generalization.
- Mechanism: By selecting augmentations that maximize the loss (error-maximizing), UEraser forces the model to learn robust features rather than relying on the unlearnable perturbations. This is akin to adversarial training but within the augmentation space.
- Core assumption: Maximizing loss during augmentation encourages the model to focus on invariant features, improving generalization to clean data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "UEraser wipes out the unlearning effect with error-maximizing data augmentations, thus restoring trained model accuracies."
  - [section] "By maximizing the adversarial loss in this manner, the model can thus avoid training from the unlearning 'shortcuts' and instead learn from the original features."
  - [corpus] Weak. Related papers focus on defenses but do not explore error-maximizing augmentation policies.
- Break condition: If the loss maximization leads to overfitting to the augmentation pipeline rather than the underlying data distribution.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: UEraser-Lite achieves effectiveness without adversarial augmentations by using aggressive natural transformations.
- Mechanism: The combination of PlasmaTransform, TrivialAugment, and ChannelShuffle inherently disrupts the unlearnable perturbations without the need for loss maximization, providing a faster but slightly less effective variant.
- Core assumption: Natural data augmentations, when applied aggressively and in combination, can sufficiently disrupt unlearnable perturbations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Interestingly, UEraser-Lite, a fast variant without adversarial augmentations, is also highly effective in preserving clean accuracies."
  - [section] "UEraser-Lite contains a series of augmentations that effectively break through unlearned perturbation while also slightly affecting the clean accuracy."
  - [corpus] Weak. Related papers do not discuss fast variants or the specific combination of augmentations used in UEraser-Lite.
- Break condition: If the aggressive natural transformations start degrading the semantic content of the images, leading to poor generalization.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data pipeline: Loads unlearnable examples
  - Augmentation suite: PlasmaTransform, TrivialAugment, ChannelShuffle
  - Loss maximization module: Selects augmentations that maximize loss (K samples per image)
  - Training loop: Combines augmentation selection with model parameter updates
  - UEraser-Lite variant: Uses the same augmentations but without loss maximization (K=1)

- Critical path:
  1. Sample mini-batch from unlearnable dataset
  2. Apply K different augmentations to each image
  3. Compute loss for each augmented image
  4. Select the augmentation with maximum loss per image
  5. Average the max-losses across the mini-batch
  6. Perform gradient descent on model parameters using this averaged loss
  7. Repeat for specified number of epochs, switching to UEraser-Lite after W epochs

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost vs. effectiveness: Higher K increases effectiveness but also computational load
  - Aggressiveness of augmentations vs. semantic preservation: More aggressive augmentations disrupt unlearnable perturbations better but may affect image semantics
  - Number of epochs: More epochs improve performance but increase training time

- Failure signatures:
  - Degraded clean accuracy: Indicates augmentations are too aggressive or loss maximization is leading to overfitting
  - High computational cost: Suggests K is set too high or augmentations are too complex
  - Ineffective against unlearnable examples: Implies the augmentation suite is not sufficiently disruptive or the loss maximization is not working as intended

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement UEraser-Lite on CIFAR-10 with EM unlearnable examples; compare clean test accuracy to standard training
  2. Vary K (number of repeated augmentation samples) and observe the effect on clean test accuracy and computational cost
  3. Test UEraser against adaptive unlearnable attacks that incorporate UEraser's augmentations into their poisoning process

## Limitations

- Limited theoretical analysis: The paper provides experimental results but lacks a comprehensive theoretical framework to explain why error-maximizing augmentation policies are effective against unlearnable examples.
- Scalability concerns: While UEraser is evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and a subset of ImageNet, its performance on larger-scale datasets and more complex model architectures is not explored.
- Adaptive attack robustness: The paper tests UEraser against adaptive attacks that incorporate its augmentations, but it is unclear if UEraser can defend against future, more sophisticated adaptive attacks that might combine multiple augmentation strategies or exploit weaknesses in the error-maximizing process.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: UEraser's effectiveness on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with EM unlearnable examples. The results are consistent and significant across multiple runs.
- **Medium Confidence**: Generalization to SVHN, ImageNet-subset, and other attack types (HYPO, LSP, AR). While results are promising, they are based on fewer experiments and may not capture all failure modes.
- **Low Confidence**: Robustness against adaptive attacks and transfer learning scenarios. The paper provides limited analysis of these critical aspects, and the results may not hold under more sophisticated attacks.
