# Rethinking the Role of Pre-ranking in Large-scale E-Commerce Searching System

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.13647
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13647
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Introduces ASH metric and ASMOL framework, achieving 1.2% GMV improvement on Taobao Search

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the traditional view of pre-ranking in e-commerce search systems by arguing that its primary goal should be returning an optimal unordered set of items rather than an ordered list. The authors introduce All-Scenario Hitrate (ASH) as a new evaluation metric that better aligns with online business metrics than traditional metrics like AUC. They propose an all-scenario-based multi-objective learning framework (ASMOL) that addresses sample selection bias and incorporates all-scenario labels, significantly improving ASH and achieving 1.2% GMV improvement on Taobao Search.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a novel framework that rethinks pre-ranking's objective from list ordering to set optimization. They introduce the All-Scenario Hitrate (ASH) metric to evaluate the quality of the output set rather than individual rankings. The proposed ASMOL framework addresses sample selection bias through careful training data curation and incorporates multiple scenario labels to improve set quality. The model is trained to optimize ASH directly rather than traditional ranking metrics, leading to improved business outcomes when outputting thousands of items.

## Key Results
- Proposed ASH metric shows better consistency with online business metrics than AUC
- ASMOL framework significantly improves ASH compared to baseline methods
- Pre-ranking model outperforms ranking model when outputting thousands of items
- Achieves 1.2% GMV improvement on Taobao Search platform

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed approach works because it realigns pre-ranking's objective with its actual business impact. By focusing on set quality rather than list ordering, the model better captures the real-world value of pre-ranking - presenting users with relevant items regardless of their specific order. The ASH metric directly measures this business-relevant outcome, while the ASMOL framework addresses the fundamental issue of sample selection bias that plagues traditional pre-ranking approaches.

## Foundational Learning
- **All-Scenario Hitrate (ASH):** A metric that evaluates set quality rather than ranking order; needed because traditional metrics don't align with business goals; quick check: verify ASH correlates better with GMV than AUC
- **Sample Selection Bias:** The systematic bias in training data where clicked items are overrepresented; needed because this bias degrades pre-ranking performance; quick check: compare model performance on biased vs. debiased datasets
- **Multi-Objective Learning:** Training framework that incorporates multiple scenario labels; needed to capture different user intents and contexts; quick check: verify performance improvement across different scenarios

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** User Query -> Pre-ranking Model -> ASH-optimized Item Set -> Ranking Model -> Final Results

**Critical Path:** The most critical components are the ASH metric calculation and the sample selection bias correction mechanism, as these directly impact the model's ability to optimize for business-relevant outcomes.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The framework trades computational efficiency (evaluating sets vs. individual items) for better business alignment. This tradeoff is justified by the significant GMV improvements observed.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance would manifest as ASH not correlating with business metrics, or the model failing to correct sample selection bias, leading to suboptimal item sets.

## Limitations
- Limited to single e-commerce platform (Taobao), raising questions about generalizability
- Theoretical framework for ASH superiority could benefit from more rigorous mathematical justification
- Mechanisms for addressing sample selection bias and their scalability to other domains remain unclear
