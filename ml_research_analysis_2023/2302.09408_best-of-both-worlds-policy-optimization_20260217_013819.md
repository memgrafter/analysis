# Best of Both Worlds Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09408
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09408
- Reference count: 40
- Achieves optimal √T adversarial and polylog(T) stochastic regret simultaneously in tabular MDPs

## Executive Summary
This paper develops policy optimization algorithms that achieve the best of both worlds - simultaneously optimal regret bounds in adversarial and stochastic Markov decision process environments. By carefully designing exploration bonuses and learning rate schedules for different regularizers (Tsallis entropy, Shannon entropy, and log barrier), the algorithms adapt to the underlying environment structure. The key insight is that proper tuning of these components enables √T regret in adversarial settings while achieving polylog(T) regret when losses are stochastic.

## Method Summary
The approach uses Follow-the-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) with adaptive learning rates and exploration bonuses for policy updates on each state. Three regularizers are analyzed: Tsallis entropy (achieves best rates but requires unique optimal actions), Shannon entropy (weaker rates but no uniqueness requirement), and log barrier (enables first-order bounds under known transitions via virtual episodes). The algorithms maintain confidence sets for transition estimation and use dynamic programming to compute value functions. Learning rates are tuned based on state visitation counts to balance exploration-exploitation across regimes.

## Key Results
- Achieves √H³SAT ln(T) worst-case regret in adversarial regime
- Obtains U + √UC gap-dependent regret in adversarial regime
- Achieves U + √UC + poly(H,S,A)ln(T) regret in stochastic regime
- Under known transitions, log-barrier yields first-order bound √H²SA(∑tVπ(s₀;ℓt)ln²(T)) + poly(H,S,A)ln²(T)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Tsallis entropy regularizer enables √T adversarial regret and polylog(T) stochastic regret simultaneously.
- Mechanism: Tsallis entropy allows self-bounding through its √π factor in the regularizer, which creates a penalty term that scales as 1/η while the stability term scales as η/√µ. This balances exploration-exploitation across both regimes.
- Core assumption: The optimal action is unique on all states, ensuring finite gap-dependent bounds.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "by properly designing the regularizer, the exploration bonus and the learning rates, one can achieve a more favorable polylog(T) regret when the losses are stochastic"
  - [section] "Tsallis entropy regularizer achieves √T regret in the adversarial regime and polylog(T) regret in the stochastic regime"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in corpus papers about Tsallis entropy specific mechanism
- Break condition: When optimal action is not unique on some states, gap-dependent bounds become infinite.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Virtual episodes with log barrier regularizer achieve first-order bounds by tuning learning rates dynamically.
- Mechanism: Virtual episodes occur when ηµ/t(s) exceeds threshold, reducing learning rate by factor (1 + 1/24H log T). This prevents ηp(a)βR > 1/H condition from being violated while maintaining first-order data-dependent regret.
- Core assumption: Known transition kernel is available.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "under known transitions, we can further obtain a first-order regret bound in the adversarial regime by leveraging the log barrier regularizer"
  - [section] "Our solution is to insert virtual episodes when ηt(s,a)/µt(s) is too large on some (s,a)"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus focuses on other BOBW techniques not involving virtual episodes
- Break condition: When transition is unknown, this technique cannot be applied directly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adaptive learning rate scheduling balances penalty and stability terms across regimes.
- Mechanism: Learning rate ηt(s) ≈ 1/H√∑τ≤t 1/µτ(s) ensures penalty term O(1/η) and stability term O(ηH²/√µ) are balanced, enabling regime adaptation.
- Core assumption: Implicit exploration factor γt = 1/t is small enough to allow logarithmic regret.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "This smaller γt allows us to achieve logarithmic regret in the stochastic regime"
  - [section] "The loss-shifting effectively creates a negative loss in the analysis"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers don't detail this specific learning rate adaptation
- Break condition: When γt cannot be made small enough (e.g., in unknown transition setting).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the log-barrier-based first-order bound (Theorem 3) be achieved under unknown transition dynamics?
- Basis in paper: The authors explicitly state this as an open question: "Whether it can be achieved under unknown transitions is an open question."
- Why unresolved: The algorithm relies on inserting virtual episodes to control learning rates, which requires knowing the transition to properly define the confidence set Pt.
- What evidence would resolve it: A modification of the algorithm that achieves a similar first-order bound without requiring known transitions, or a lower bound proving this is impossible.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the additional C(π) term in the unknown transition stochastic bound (4) be removed?
- Basis in paper: The authors note "It remains open whether it can be removed" after presenting the bound.
- Why unresolved: The term arises from the difference between upper and lower confidence measures in the unknown transition case, and the analysis hasn't found a way to eliminate it.
- What evidence would resolve it: Either a proof that C(π) is unavoidable in the unknown transition case, or an improved analysis that removes this term.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the fundamental reason for the √S dependence in the gap-dependent bound that prevents matching the lower bound of Xu et al. (2021)?
- Basis in paper: The authors note their bound involves √S even though Xu et al. (2021) show S/∆min is unavoidable, but explain their bound only holds when optimal actions are unique on all states.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a detailed analysis of whether the √S factor is an artifact of the analysis or a fundamental limitation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Either a matching lower bound for the unique-optimal-action case, or an improved algorithm that removes the √S factor while maintaining the same guarantees.

## Limitations

- Requires unique optimal actions across all states for gap-dependent bounds, a strong assumption rarely satisfied in practice
- Virtual episode mechanism adds significant computational overhead and algorithmic complexity
- First-order bounds require known transition kernels, substantially restricting practical applicability

## Confidence

- High confidence in the theoretical regret bounds and their derivation
- Medium confidence in the practical implementability of virtual episodes mechanism
- Low confidence in the tightness of the constants used in the analysis
