# Safe Policy Improvement for POMDPs via Finite-State Controllers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04939
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04939
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces a novel approach to safe policy improvement in partially observable Markov decision processes using finite-state controllers.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method for safe policy improvement in POMDPs by leveraging finite-state controllers to convert the partially observable problem into a fully observable finite-history MDP. The approach uses historical data to estimate this MDP and then applies offline safe policy improvement algorithms to compute an improved policy. The method constrains the policy space based on available data, ensuring that the new policy only deviates from the behavior policy when supported by sufficient evidence.

## Method Summary
The method represents the behavior policy as a finite-state controller (FSC) and assumes that finite memory is sufficient to derive optimal policies. This allows mapping the POMDP to a finite-history MDP, which is fully observable and more tractable. The approach estimates this MDP using historical data and the FSC's memory structure, then applies an off-the-shelf SPI algorithm to compute an improved policy. The method ensures that the new policy only differs from the behavior policy when sufficient data is available.

## Key Results
- The new policy, when converted back to an FSC, outperforms the behavior policy with high probability
- Experimental results demonstrate effectiveness across three standard POMDP benchmarks
- The approach works reliably across varying memory sizes and data quantities, even when finite memory is not generally sufficient

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by constraining the policy space to FSCs that operate on a finite history, which allows reliable estimation of the MDP from historical data. By using the FSC structure, the approach can effectively convert the partially observable problem into a fully observable one where standard safe policy improvement algorithms can be applied. The finite-history MDP estimation is more robust to partial observability because the FSC provides a structured way to condition on past observations and actions.

## Foundational Learning

- Finite-state controllers as policy representations
- Finite-history MDP conversion and its tractability benefits

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:**
Data Collection -> FSC Behavior Policy -> Finite-History MDP Estimation -> SPI Algorithm -> Improved FSC

**Critical Path:**
The critical path involves using the FSC to structure historical data into the finite-history MDP, then applying SPI to this MDP, and finally converting the result back to an FSC.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The method trades off the expressiveness of potentially infinite-memory policies for the tractability of finite-memory representations. This enables reliable improvement but may limit performance in scenarios requiring extensive memory.

**Failure Signatures:**
The approach may fail when the FSC representation is insufficient to capture optimal policies, or when historical data is too sparse to reliably estimate the finite-history MDP.

2. Vary memory size systematically while keeping data quantity constant to assess impact on performance
3. Reduce historical data quantity to evaluate the method's sensitivity to data sparsity

## Limitations
- The assumption of sufficient finite memory for optimal policies may not hold in all POMDPs
- Approximation errors from the FSC-to-MDP conversion are not fully characterized
- Scalability to high-dimensional observation spaces is unclear
- The method's performance in real-world scenarios with continuous observation spaces remains untested

## Confidence
- **High**: The core approach of converting POMDPs to finite-history MDPs is technically sound and well-established
- **Medium**: Theoretical guarantees hold under stated assumptions, but practical bounds are not provided
- **Medium**: Experimental results demonstrate effectiveness across benchmarks, but real-world applicability needs further validation
