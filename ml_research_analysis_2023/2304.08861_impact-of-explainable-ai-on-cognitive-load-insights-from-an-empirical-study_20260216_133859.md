# Impact Of Explainable AI On Cognitive Load: Insights From An Empirical Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08861
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08861
- Reference count: 0
- Local explanations significantly reduce cognitive load compared to global explanations in medical AI tasks

## Executive Summary
This empirical study investigates how different XAI explanation types affect cognitive load, task performance, and task time in a medical decision-making context. Using a within-subjects design with 246 prospective physicians, the research compares five XAI explanation types (Why, Why-Not, How, How-To, What-Else) against a baseline in COVID-19 chest X-ray classification tasks. The findings demonstrate that local explanations (Why, Why-Not) significantly outperform global explanations and baseline, reducing cognitive load while improving task performance and completion time.

## Method Summary
The study employed a within-subjects design where 246 prospective physicians completed COVID-19 chest X-ray classification tasks using different XAI explanation types. Participants were exposed to five local and global explanation types plus a baseline condition. Cognitive load was measured using mental effort self-assessment on a 7-point Likert scale, task performance was recorded as correct/incorrect classifications, and task time was measured in seconds. Mental efficiency was calculated as a composite metric combining standardized performance and reversed standardized time.

## Key Results
- Local explanations (Why, Why-Not) significantly reduce cognitive load compared to global explanations and baseline
- Local explanations improve task performance while reducing task completion time
- Mental efficiency calculations show local explanations are most effective, with Why achieving 0.34 and Why-Not achieving 0.23

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Local XAI explanations reduce cognitive load compared to global explanations and baseline
- Mechanism: Local explanations provide focused, feature-level justifications for individual predictions, which aligns better with human cognitive processing limits than global explanations that describe overall model behavior
- Core assumption: End-users can more efficiently process targeted explanations about specific decisions than comprehensive explanations about model logic
- Evidence anchors:
  - Results show that local explanations (Why, Why-Not) significantly reduce cognitive load and task time while improving task performance compared to global explanations (How, What-Else) and baseline
  - We found that these explanation types strongly influence end-users' cognitive load, task performance, and task time

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Lower cognitive load from XAI explanations correlates with better task performance and faster completion times
- Mechanism: Reduced mental effort frees cognitive resources for decision-making, allowing users to process information more efficiently and apply it effectively to classification tasks
- Core assumption: Cognitive load and task performance have a linear inverse relationship, where reduced load directly improves performance outcomes
- Evidence anchors:
  - Mental efficiency calculations confirm that local explanations are most effective
  - Less mental effort when using XAI explanations leads to improved end-user task performance and reduced end-user task time

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mental efficiency metric effectively combines cognitive load, performance, and time to evaluate XAI explanation quality
- Mechanism: The mental efficiency formula (ME = standardized performance × standardized reversed time) provides a composite measure that balances multiple performance dimensions against cognitive investment
- Core assumption: A single metric can capture the multidimensional nature of explanation effectiveness by weighting performance gains against cognitive effort
- Evidence anchors:
  - We contextualized a mental efficiency metric, ranking local XAI explanation types best
  - Since an ME above null would indicate that the end-users' performance was higher than expected compared with the mental effort invested

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do learning effects impact the cognitive load and task performance when users are repeatedly exposed to the same XAI explanation types over time?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that novice users were studied and suggests that results may change once end-users are taught how to use these types of systems or use them more frequently due to the iterative learning process
- Why unresolved: The study only measured immediate effects and did not examine longitudinal learning effects or how repeated exposure might change cognitive load, performance, or trust over time
- What evidence would resolve it: A longitudinal study tracking the same participants over multiple sessions with repeated exposure to XAI explanations, measuring changes in cognitive load, task performance, and trust metrics

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different levels of AI experience and domain expertise affect the perceived cognitive load and effectiveness of various XAI explanation types?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that experienced physicians might exhibit bias toward XAI-based DSS and that results may differ with different types of end-user groups
- Why unresolved: The study focused on novice medical students and did not compare results across different experience levels or domain expertise
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies with multiple user groups including novices, intermediate users, and experts, measuring cognitive load, task performance, and effectiveness across different experience levels

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different use cases and task complexities influence the optimal selection of XAI explanation types based on cognitive load considerations?
- Basis in paper: The paper focuses on a specific medical use case (COVID-19 classification) and notes that different task complexities might yield different results
- Why unresolved: The study only examined one specific use case and did not explore how different task complexities or domains might require different optimal explanation types
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies across multiple use cases with varying complexity levels, measuring cognitive load, performance, and efficiency for each explanation type in different contexts

## Limitations

- Generalizability limited to COVID-19 chest X-ray classification tasks, may not extend to other medical domains
- Sample of 246 prospective physicians provides reasonable power but may not capture full diversity of end-user backgrounds
- Mental efficiency metric combines multiple dimensions that may not equally weight across different user populations

## Confidence

- High confidence: Local explanations reduce cognitive load compared to global explanations and baseline
- Medium confidence: Specific mental efficiency rankings and mechanism explanations
- Medium confidence: Effectiveness of mental efficiency metric across different expertise levels
