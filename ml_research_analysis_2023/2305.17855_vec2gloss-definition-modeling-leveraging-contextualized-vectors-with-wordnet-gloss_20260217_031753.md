# Vec2Gloss: definition modeling leveraging contextualized vectors with Wordnet gloss

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.17855
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17855
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Key outcome:** Model generates dictionary glosses from contextualized embeddings using Chinese Wordnet, achieving competitive performance against original glosses

## Executive Summary
Vec2Gloss presents a novel approach to generating dictionary glosses from contextualized embeddings using Chinese Wordnet. The system encodes sentences containing target words into semantic vectors and decodes them into glosses using a pre-trained mT5 model. Human evaluations demonstrate the model achieves decent performance when compared with original glosses in Chinese Wordnet. The study addresses the interpretability of contextualized embeddings by producing human-readable glosses and analyzing them through semantic and contextual dependency indices.

## Method Summary
The Vec2Gloss system encodes input sentences containing target words into contextualized vectors using transformer-based models, then decodes these vectors into dictionary glosses using a pre-trained mT5 architecture. The approach leverages Chinese Wordnet as a resource for gloss generation, with the model learning to map semantic information from contextual embeddings to human-readable definitions. The system addresses the challenge of making contextualized embeddings interpretable by converting abstract vector representations into concrete, dictionary-style definitions.

## Key Results
- Human evaluations show Vec2Gloss achieves decent performance compared to original glosses in Chinese Wordnet
- Semantic dependency analysis reveals nouns and verbs have higher semantic dependency than adverbs
- The model successfully generates human-readable glosses from contextualized embeddings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The model works by leveraging the rich semantic information captured in contextualized embeddings and mapping it to structured dictionary definitions. The mT5 model serves as an effective decoder that can translate semantic vectors into natural language glosses. The Chinese Wordnet provides a structured knowledge base that helps guide the gloss generation process, ensuring the outputs align with established lexical knowledge.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contextualized embeddings** (why needed: capture word meaning in context; quick check: can distinguish "bank" as financial institution vs river edge)
- **mT5 architecture** (why needed: sequence-to-sequence generation capability; quick check: can produce coherent text from semantic vectors)
- **Semantic dependency metrics** (why needed: quantify relationship strength between concepts in glosses; quick check: higher values indicate stronger conceptual connections)
- **Chinese Wordnet structure** (why needed: provides authoritative gloss examples and lexical relations; quick check: contains structured definitions for validation)

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input sentence -> Contextualized encoder -> Semantic vector -> mT5 decoder -> Generated gloss

**Critical path:** The encoding-decoding pipeline is critical, where the contextualized vector representation directly influences the quality of generated glosses. The mT5 decoder's ability to produce coherent, dictionary-style definitions from semantic vectors is the key determinant of performance.

**Design tradeoffs:** The system trades off the flexibility of open-ended definition generation for the structured constraints of dictionary-style glosses. Using a pre-trained mT5 model reduces training requirements but may limit adaptation to specific gloss styles. The reliance on Chinese Wordnet provides quality ground truth but may introduce bias toward existing definitions.

**Failure signatures:** Poor performance manifests as incoherent glosses, failure to capture word senses accurately, or generation of definitions that don't align with Wordnet structure. The system may struggle with highly ambiguous words or those with subtle sense distinctions.

**First experiments:** 1) Test gloss generation on unambiguous words with clear senses, 2) Evaluate performance on words with known contextual ambiguity, 3) Compare generated glosses against Wordnet definitions using semantic similarity metrics

## Limitations
- Analysis of semantic dependency indices limited to nouns and verbs, with no systematic analysis of other POS categories
- Evaluation relies on Chinese Wordnet glosses as ground truth, which may not represent ideal definitions and could introduce bias
- No ablation studies to determine performance contribution from contextualized embeddings versus mT5 model architecture

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Gloss generation performance metrics and comparison with original glosses
- **Medium confidence:** Semantic dependency analysis patterns for nouns and verbs
- **Low confidence:** Claims about interpretability improvement and broader applicability to all POS categories
