# Video Pre-trained Transformer: A Multimodal Mixture of Pre-trained Experts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.10505
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10505
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Initial experiments demonstrate learning, but more experimentation needed to realize full potential

## Executive Summary
This paper presents VPT, a video pre-trained transformer that combines four state-of-the-art encoder models to convert videos into compact embeddings. The model uses a Flan-T5-11B backbone with autoregressive causal language modeling loss to learn universal video representations. The authors claim this is the first use of multiple frozen SOTA models as encoders in an "embedding -> backbone -> prediction head" design pattern, incorporating more modalities than current state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
VPT uses four frozen SOTA encoders (CLIP, Whisper, OpenPSG) to process YouTube videos into modality-specific embeddings that are concatenated and fed into a trainable Flan-T5-11B backbone. The model is pre-trained using autoregressive caption prediction on 28 million YouTube videos, then fine-tuned on downstream tasks with fully connected prediction heads. The architecture represents an innovative approach to multimodal video understanding by leveraging pre-trained knowledge while learning to fuse diverse information sources.

## Key Results
- Model is learning appropriately in initial experiments
- First use of multiple frozen SOTA encoders in "embedding -> backbone -> prediction head" design
- Includes more modalities than current state-of-the-art approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Combining frozen state-of-the-art encoders allows leveraging pre-trained knowledge without overfitting.
- Mechanism: Four SOTA models (CLIP, Whisper, OpenPSG) each encode different modalities into fixed-size embeddings, which are then fed into a trainable T5 backbone for joint representation learning.
- Core assumption: Each encoder's pre-training provides sufficient generalization to new video domains, and their embeddings can be effectively combined through a shared backbone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "VPT uses four SOTA encoder models from prior work to convert a video into a sequence of compact embeddings."
  - [section] "We use the most capable pre-trained CLIP model... and preprocess our entire YouTube dataset with both the CLIP text and image encoders."
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct citation of similar multi-encoder fusion strategies in the corpus neighbors.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using Whisper-generated captions improves caption quality over YouTube's auto-generated ones, enabling better grounding of visual features.
- Mechanism: Whisper provides word-level timestamps and higher transcription accuracy, allowing precise temporal alignment between spoken content and visual frames.
- Core assumption: Whisper's ASR performance is robust enough to handle diverse YouTube accents, background noise, and domain-specific terminology.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "While Merlot leverages YouTube's auto-generated captions, we attempt to improve upon model performance by utilizing Whisper, a SOTA ASR model for more accurate caption extraction."
  - [corpus] Weak. The corpus does not mention Whisper usage in multimodal training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Scene graphs provide structured context that enhances visual understanding beyond raw pixel embeddings.
- Mechanism: OpenPSG generates explicit object-relationship graphs for each frame, which are then encoded via CLIP text encoder and concatenated with other modality embeddings.
- Core assumption: Scene graph structure captures semantic relationships that pixel-level embeddings miss, and the CLIP text encoder can meaningfully represent this structured information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Scene graphs represent a web of relationships among interconnected objects... In our work, we employ OpenPSG... to construct a more comprehensive and clean scene graph representation."

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Autoregressive causal language modeling**
  - Why needed here: The backbone T5 must predict next tokens in the caption sequence given multimodal input embeddings, enabling self-supervised pre-training.
  - Quick check question: What loss function is used during pre-training to predict captions autoregressively?

- **Concept: Transfer learning via frozen encoders**
  - Why needed here: Avoids retraining large models from scratch and leverages existing multimodal expertise in CLIP, Whisper, OpenPSG.
  - Quick check question: Why are the encoder models kept frozen during backbone training?

- **Concept: Multimodal embedding fusion**
  - Why needed here: To create a unified representation space where vision, audio, text, and scene graph features can interact.
  - Quick check question: How are the different modality embeddings combined before being fed into the T5 backbone?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: YouTube video download → Whisper transcription → frame extraction → OpenPSG scene graph → CLIP encoding → T5 backbone training
- **Critical path**: Preprocess videos (Whisper → CLIP → OpenPSG) → Concatenate embeddings → Train T5 backbone → Evaluate on downstream tasks
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using frozen encoders reduces trainable parameters but limits fine-tuning flexibility.
  - Concatenating all modality embeddings is simple but may not optimally fuse structured scene graph information.
  - Whisper's high accuracy improves grounding but increases preprocessing time and compute.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Model collapses to trivial predictions (e.g., always "No") → likely overfitting or weak training objective.
  - Downstream accuracy much lower than baselines → possible embedding space misalignment or poor fusion strategy.
  - Slow training progress → inefficient preprocessing or GPU underutilization.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Ablation: Train backbone with only CLIP image embeddings (no audio, text, or scene graph) to measure contribution of each modality.
  2. Loss variation: Replace autoregressive caption prediction with contrastive image-caption matching to see if alignment improves.
  3. Scene graph integration: Replace concatenation with cross-attention from image embeddings to scene graph embeddings to test structured fusion.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of VPT scale with larger datasets beyond the initial 25k videos used in experiments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that scale is a key factor in large deep learning models and that training on more videos would likely improve performance.
- Why unresolved: The authors acknowledge that their current experiments use only a fraction of available preprocessed data, but have not yet conducted experiments with larger datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training VPT on increasingly larger portions of the 20M+ video dataset, with performance metrics compared across dataset sizes.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would incorporating audio embeddings significantly improve VPT's performance on downstream tasks compared to the current multimodal setup?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors identify audio as a major modification they plan to implement, noting that raw audio can encapsulate information that language cannot.
- Why unresolved: Audio embeddings have not been incorporated into the current model architecture yet.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing performance differences between VPT with and without audio embeddings on downstream benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal way to utilize scene graph information in the VPT architecture to improve performance rather than degrade it?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors found that including scene graphs hurt performance on VQA, suggesting their current usage approach is inappropriate.
- Why unresolved: The authors hypothesize that the current simple concatenation approach may be the issue, but have not tested alternative implementations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing different methods of incorporating scene graph information (e.g., cross-attention mechanisms) against performance metrics on downstream tasks.
