# Topics in Contextualised Attention Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04339
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04339
- Reference count: 40
- Pre-trained language models encode topical word clusters similar to those discovered by probabilistic topic models, with attention mechanisms playing a crucial role.

## Executive Summary
This study explores how pre-trained language models (PLMs) like BERT and DistilBERT implicitly learn thematic word clusters similar to those identified by probabilistic topic models (PTMs) such as LDA and NMF. Despite not being explicitly designed for topic modeling, PLMs appear to encode topical structures through their attention mechanisms. The authors investigate this phenomenon by clustering attention weights from different layers of PLMs and comparing the resulting clusters with those from PTMs using coherence scores and word overlap analysis. The findings reveal that higher layers of PLMs produce clusters with coherence comparable to PTMs, suggesting that attention weights capture thematic word patterns akin to topic models.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted probe experiments to investigate how PLMs encode topical word clusters. They extracted attention weights from different layers of BERT and DistilBERT, clustered these weights using K-means, and compared the resulting clusters with those from PTMs like LDA and NMF. Coherence scores and word overlap analysis were used to evaluate the quality and similarity of the clusters. The experiments were performed on datasets such as 20 Newsgroups and IMDB to assess the generalizability of the findings.

## Key Results
- Higher layers of PLMs produce attention-based clusters with coherence scores comparable to those from PTMs like LDA and NMF.
- Word overlap analysis shows significant commonality between PLM and PTM clusters, particularly in contextual layers.
- The attention mechanism within PLMs plays a crucial role in modeling thematic word clusters, suggesting implicit learning of topical structures.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The attention mechanism in PLMs allows the model to focus on relevant words and relationships within a sentence, which may naturally lead to the formation of thematic word clusters. By aggregating attention weights across layers, the model implicitly captures patterns that align with topics identified by PTMs. This mechanism enables PLMs to encode topical structures without explicit supervision.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Clustering Method**: K-means is simple but may not capture nuanced structures compared to hierarchical or spectral clustering.
- **Coherence as Sole Metric**: Provides a quantitative measure but may overlook interpretability or semantic diversity.
- **Dataset Selection**: 20 Newsgroups and IMDB are representative but may limit generalizability to other domains or languages.

### Failure Signatures
- Low coherence scores in PLM clusters compared to PTM clusters.
- Minimal word overlap between PLM and PTM clusters.
- Inconsistent results across different layers or datasets.

## Limitations
- The choice of K-means clustering may not fully capture the nuanced thematic structures that PTMs explicitly optimize for.
- Coherence scores as the primary metric may overlook other important aspects of topical representation, such as interpretability or semantic diversity.
- The focus on specific datasets (20 Newsgroups and IMDB) may limit the generalizability of findings to other domains or languages.

## Confidence
- **High**: Significant word overlap between PLM and PTM clusters is directly measurable and consistent across datasets.
- **Medium**: PLMs implicitly learn thematic word clusters, as evidence is compelling but not definitive.
- **Medium**: Higher layers of PLMs produce more coherent clusters than lower layers, relying on coherence scores as the sole measure of quality.
