# A Deep Learning-based Compression and Classification Technique for Whole Slide Histopathology Images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07161
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07161
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: Supervised autoencoder compression improves classification accuracy from 0.5 to 0.9 on histopathology images

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a supervised autoencoder method for compressing histopathology images while preserving diagnosis-relevant features. The approach trains an autoencoder to compress images guided by a trained classifier, resulting in significantly improved classification performance on reconstructed images compared to unsupervised compression. The method provides a simple and effective way to improve image compression while maintaining diagnostic information for whole slide images.

## Method Summary
The method involves training a convolutional autoencoder on histopathology images, followed by fine-tuning a pre-trained Xception model for binary classification. The autoencoder and classifier are then combined into an ensemble, with the classifier's weights frozen and the autoencoder trained to optimize classification accuracy on reconstructed images. The PCam dataset with 220,025 training patches and 57,458 test patches is used for evaluation.

## Key Results
- Classification accuracy on reconstructed images improves from 0.5 to 0.9 using supervised compression
- AUC-ROC scores demonstrate strong classification performance on compressed images
- Visual quality comparison shows supervised reconstructions retain more diagnostic features than unsupervised ones

- Core assumption: The classifier's predictions depend on features that are relevant for diagnosis, and the autoencoder will prioritize retaining these features during reconstruction.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Autoencoder (encoder + decoder) -> Latent representation -> Classifier
- Critical path: Autoencoder encoding → Latent representation → Autoencoder decoding → Classifier prediction
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Compression rate vs. reconstruction quality: Higher compression rates may lead to loss of important features, while lower rates may not provide sufficient compression.
  - Classifier complexity vs. training efficiency: More complex classifiers may provide better supervision but require more computational resources and training time.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor classification performance on reconstructed images: Indicates that the autoencoder is not retaining sufficient diagnosis-relevant features.
  - Overfitting during training: Suggests that the model is memorizing the training data rather than learning generalizable features.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train an unsupervised autoencoder on histopathology images and evaluate its reconstruction quality and classification performance.
