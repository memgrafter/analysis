# A Measure of Explanatory Effectiveness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12233
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12233
- Reference count: 0
- One-line primary result: This paper formalizes explanation as a two-agent cooperative game where an explainer seeks to maximize a measure of the explainee's understanding.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a theoretical framework for measuring explanatory effectiveness by formalizing explanation as a two-agent cooperative game. The authors define "understanding" using information-theoretic concepts including compression, utilization, and integration of knowledge. They introduce a quantitative measure that quantifies how an explainee's understanding changes over the course of an explanation, with values bounded between -1 and 1.

## Method Summary
The paper uses algorithmic information theory and universal intelligence theory to formalize explanation as a cooperative game between explainer and explainee agents. The measure of understanding combines information-theoretic compression, utilization, and integration factors with mutual information between the agent's internal state and the phenomenon being explained. The explanatory effectiveness is defined as the change in understanding over the course of the explanation process.

## Key Results
- Defines explanation as a two-agent cooperative game with aligned goals
- Proposes information-theoretic measures for compression, utilization, and integration of understanding
- Introduces a bounded measure of explanatory effectiveness (between -1 and 1)
- Notes computational complexity as a key limitation of the approach

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Explanatory effectiveness is measured by the change in the explainee's understanding of the explanandum over the course of an interaction.
- Mechanism: The measure uses information-theoretic compression, utilization, and integration of knowledge to quantify the explainee's internal state change.
- Core assumption: An agent's understanding can be represented as a partition of its internal state into p-relevant, p-irrelevant, p-specific, and p-background information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This measure serves as a foundation for the automated assessment of explanations, in terms of the effects that any given action in the game has on the internal state of the explainee."
  - [section] "Deﬁnition 10 (Explanatory Eﬀectiveness). The eﬀectiveness of an explanation is the change in explainee’s understanding of the explanandum p ∈ P over the course of the explanatory process."
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct evidence in neighbors about understanding measured as state change.
- Break condition: If the explainee's internal state cannot be partitioned meaningfully, or if the information-theoretic measures are not computable/applicable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The explainee's understanding is quantified using compression, utilization, and integration of p-relevant information.
- Mechanism: p-compression factor measures how concisely the explainee encodes p-relevant information, p-utilization measures how well the explainee can apply this information, and p-integration measures how well the p-relevant information is connected to the rest of the agent's knowledge.
- Core assumption: These three factors (compression, utilization, integration) together capture the essence of "understanding" in a measurable way.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We define 'understanding' in terms of information-theoretic compression, utilization, and integration of knowledge about the phenomenon in the explainee's internal state."
  - [section] "Deﬁnition 9 (Understanding). Given an agent X ∈ X with internal state zX and phenomenon p ∈ P , the measure of X’s understanding of phenomenon p, κ : X × P → [0, 1), is deﬁned as: κ(X, p) = ˆκ(X, p) · c(X, p) · φ(X, p) · Υp(X) · I(zX ; p) K(p)"
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct evidence in neighbors about these three specific factors.
- Break condition: If one of the three factors (compression, utilization, integration) does not meaningfully contribute to understanding, or if the measures are not well-defined.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Explanation games formalize the explanation process as a cooperative game where both agents seek to maximize explanatory effectiveness.
- Mechanism: The explainer and explainee are modeled as agents in a game, with the explainer's actions being observations made by the explainee, and the goal being to maximize the change in the explainee's understanding.
- Core assumption: Explanation can be modeled as a cooperative game between two agents with aligned goals.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We pose the problem 'explaining AI systems' in terms of a two-player cooperative game in which each agent seeks to maximise our proposed measure of explanatory effectiveness."

An explanation game G = ( A, B, p, τ) is a cooperative finite sequential game with asymmetric information in which the participants seek to maximise ξ(oB, p) over the course of τ time steps, where oB is the sequence of all observations made by B."

- Break condition: If the explainer and explainee do not have aligned goals, or if the game-theoretic formalization does not capture the essence of explanation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Explainer agent -> Explainee agent -> Explanandum -> Explanation game -> Understanding measure -> Explanatory effectiveness
- Critical path: Explainer agent takes action → Explainee agent observes action → Explainee agent's internal state updates → Understanding measure is computed → Explanatory effectiveness is computed
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational complexity vs. accuracy of understanding measure
  - Level of detail in internal state representation vs. tractability of information-theoretic measures
  - Cooperative vs. non-cooperative modeling of explanation process
- Failure signatures:
  - Low explanatory effectiveness despite high-quality explanations (possible issues with understanding measure)
  - Explainer agent taking actions that do not lead to increased understanding (possible issues with explanation game formalization)
  - Inability to compute understanding measure due to computational complexity (possible need for approximation techniques)

## Limitations
- The proposed measures rely heavily on information-theoretic concepts that may be computationally intractable in practice
- The formalization assumes agents with well-defined internal states that can be partitioned meaningfully
- The relationship between the theoretical measure and actual human understanding is not empirically validated

## Confidence
- High confidence: The mathematical formalization of explanation as a cooperative game is internally consistent and well-defined
- Medium confidence: The information-theoretic measures for understanding capture meaningful aspects of explanation quality
- Low confidence: The practical applicability and computational feasibility of the proposed measures for real-world explanation systems
