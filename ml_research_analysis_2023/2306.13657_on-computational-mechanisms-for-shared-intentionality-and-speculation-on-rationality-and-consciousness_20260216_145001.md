# On Computational Mechanisms for Shared Intentionality, and Speculation on Rationality and Consciousness

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the emergence of shared intentionality, rationality, and consciousness in humans by proposing a computational mechanism based on predictive processing. The theory suggests that shared intentionality evolved first through the abstraction and concretion of mental states, which then provided the foundation for rationality and consciousness. The paper uses a thought experiment with hypothetical agents Alice and Bob to illustrate how shared intentionality could emerge through communication mechanisms. It further argues that consciousness, both intentional and phenomenal, arises as a byproduct of the need to communicate and share mental states.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a theoretical approach using a thought experiment with humanoid agents to derive computational mechanisms for shared intentionality. It proposes predictive processing as the foundation for how agents abstract their computational states into explanations and how receivers concretize these explanations back into their own mental models. The method extends this framework to explain rationality through local loop mechanisms and consciousness through awareness of attention and control in an Upper System. The paper compares these proposed mechanisms to existing theories of brain function and dual-process architectures.

## Key Results
- Shared intentionality emerges through predictive processing-based abstraction and concretion of mental states
- Rationality develops as a byproduct of shared intentionality through local loop mechanisms
- Consciousness arises from awareness of attention and control in an Upper System, with phenomenal consciousness requiring abstraction of sensory experiences for communication

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Shared intentionality arises from predictive processing-based abstraction and concretion of mental states into explanations.
- Mechanism: Alice abstracts her computational state into a concept-based explanation using predictive processing, which Bob then concretizes back into his own mental model.
- Core assumption: The brain uses predictive processing to build probabilistic models and minimize prediction error, which can be repurposed for communication.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The theory, termed Shared Intentionality First Theory (SIFT), suggests that shared intentionality evolved first, providing the foundation for rationality and consciousness."
  - [section] "The goal for our algorithm is to construct an explanation... whose concretion by Bob will reproduce parts of Alice's computational state."
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers; average neighbor FMR=0.51. Top related titles include "Consciousness, natural and artificial: an evolutionary advantage for reasoning on reactive substrates" (FMR=0.48). Evidence for predictive processing in consciousness is mixed but present.
- Break condition: If the predictive processing model fails to explain sensory prediction error minimization in the brain, or if abstraction/concretion cannot be shown to produce shared mental states.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Intentional consciousness is awareness of attention and control over the Upper System's explanation construction.
- Mechanism: The Upper System maintains an abstract model and generates explanations; consciousness corresponds to awareness of this process and the ability to control it.
- Core assumption: The Upper System is a distinct computational layer that can attend to and manipulate conceptual explanations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SIFT explains consciousness as awareness of attention and control in an Upper System"
  - [section] "I suggest that intentional consciousness goes beyond awareness of attention and also has elements of executive control"
  - [corpus] Evidence for dual-process architectures in the brain is present but not definitive for consciousness specifically.
- Break condition: If attention and consciousness can be shown to operate independently, or if the Upper System cannot be identified as a distinct computational layer.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Phenomenal consciousness arises because we need to communicate sensory experiences, requiring their abstraction into the Upper System.
- Mechanism: Sensory information is abstracted into the Upper System so it can be referenced in explanations and communicated to others.
- Core assumption: The Upper System is the locus of consciousness and must contain abstractions of sensory experiences for communication.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "phenomenal consciousness arising from the need to communicate sensory experiences"
  - [section] "I submit that this is phenomenal consciousness: in order to communicate the things we sense and feel, selected abstractions and explanations about them must be present in the Upper System"
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence; related work on machine consciousness focuses on simulation rather than biological plausibility.
- Break condition: If sensory experiences can be communicated without abstraction into a conscious system, or if phenomenal consciousness can be shown to operate independently of communication needs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Lower System (subconscious, automated units) -> Upper System (abstraction/concretion, explanations) -> Global Workspace (shared memory)
- Critical path: Abstraction → Explanation Construction → Communication → Concretion → Behavioral Change
- Design tradeoffs: Predictive processing is biologically plausible but computationally expensive; dual-process architecture separates concerns but adds complexity
- Failure signatures: Prediction errors that cannot be resolved, inability to construct shared mental states, lack of behavioral change after communication
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement predictive processing for simple sensory prediction and error minimization
  2. Add abstraction layer to convert predictions to concept-based explanations
  3. Test communication between agents using constructed explanations and measure shared mental state formation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the specific neural correlates or biological mechanisms that correspond to the computational architecture proposed for shared intentionality in humans?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that while the computational mechanisms are biologically plausible, specific neural correlates have not been identified. It states: "As yet, I cannot identify biological mechanisms or structures or 'neural correlates' that correspond or bridge to my overall proposal."
- Why unresolved: The mechanisms proposed are abstract computational models, and bridging these to specific biological structures requires further neuroscientific research and experimental evidence.
- What evidence would resolve it: Identification of specific brain regions, neural pathways, or activity patterns that correspond to the abstraction, concretion, and explanation processes proposed in the computational model.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we empirically test whether phenomenal consciousness evolved after shared intentionality in humans, as predicted by SIFT?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the evolutionary timeline of these capabilities, stating: "SIFT predicts that phenomenal consciousness evolved with or later than shared intentionality, and that would imply it is unique to humans." However, it acknowledges the difficulty in assessing phenomenal consciousness in animals.
- Why unresolved: There is no accepted method for assessing phenomenal consciousness in animals, making it challenging to determine the evolutionary sequence of these capabilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of reliable behavioral or neuroscientific tests to assess phenomenal consciousness in animals, and application of these tests to species with varying levels of social complexity and shared intentionality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can artificial agents with the proposed computational architecture for shared intentionality exhibit intentional consciousness, and how would we detect it?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores the possibility of creating conscious robots based on the SIFT hypothesis, but concludes: "I suspect the same would be true of Alice and Bob, despite their design being based on mechanisms thought to correspond to those of humans." It also discusses the challenges in defining and detecting artificial consciousness.
- Why unresolved: There is no consensus on how to define or detect consciousness in artificial agents, and the proposed mechanisms may not be sufficient to generate the subjective experience of consciousness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful creation of artificial agents that exhibit behaviors and cognitive capabilities associated with intentional consciousness, along with a robust framework for assessing and confirming their conscious experience.

## Limitations

- The theory relies heavily on predictive processing as a foundational mechanism, yet the exact neural implementation in humans remains unclear.
- Evidence for the core claims linking predictive processing to shared intentionality and consciousness is limited and mixed.
- The argument that phenomenal consciousness evolved specifically for communication purposes is particularly speculative and lacks direct empirical support.

## Confidence

- High confidence: Predictive processing as a general brain mechanism for perception and prediction
- Medium confidence: Dual-process architecture as a framework for distinguishing conscious and unconscious processes
- Low confidence: The specific evolutionary sequence proposed (shared intentionality first) and its role in explaining consciousness
