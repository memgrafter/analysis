# MobileRec: A Large-Scale Dataset for Mobile Apps Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06588
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06588
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces MobileRec, a large-scale dataset for mobile app recommendation research with 19.3 million user interactions across 10K unique apps

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MobileRec, a large-scale dataset for mobile app recommendation research. The dataset contains 19.3 million user interactions with over 10K unique apps across 48 categories, recording sequential activity of 0.7 million users with at least 5 interactions each. MobileRec includes rich metadata such as app descriptions, ratings, and user reviews with sentiments. The authors evaluate several state-of-the-art recommendation approaches on MobileRec, establishing baseline results. Notably, they observe that MobileRec's dynamic user-item interactions pose challenges for existing models, with performance varying significantly from other datasets like Amazon Beauty.

## Method Summary
The authors created MobileRec by preprocessing Google Play app review data, applying 5-core filtering to ensure each user has at least 5 interactions. The dataset captures sequential user-app interactions with timestamps, user ratings, review texts, and app metadata including descriptions and categories. They evaluated multiple state-of-the-art recommendation models including SASRec, ELECRec, BERT4Rec, and GRU4Rec using Hit@K and NDCG@K metrics through leave-one-out validation with 100 negative samples per positive.

## Key Results
- MobileRec contains 19.3 million user interactions across 10,173 unique apps and 48 categories
- Models show varying performance on MobileRec compared to other datasets, with ELECRec dropping significantly in Hit@5 metric
- Sequential recommendation models generally outperform non-sequential baselines on the dataset

## Limitations

- MobileRec's user-item interaction patterns differ substantially from existing datasets, making it unclear how findings transfer to other domains
- The dataset represents only 0.7 million users with at least 5 interactions each, potentially limiting diversity of user behavior patterns
- Uses implicit feedback from reviews rather than explicit ratings, which may introduce noise in preference signals

## Confidence

- Dataset characteristics and scale: **High** - The paper provides clear statistics and download links
- Baseline model performance: **Medium** - Results are presented but limited to comparisons with Amazon-Beauty
- Generalization of findings: **Low** - The unique behavior patterns in MobileRec make it unclear how findings transfer to other domains
