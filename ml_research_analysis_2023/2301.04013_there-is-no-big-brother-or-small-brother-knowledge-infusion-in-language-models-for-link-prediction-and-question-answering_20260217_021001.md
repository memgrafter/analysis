# There is No Big Brother or Small Brother: Knowledge Infusion in Language Models for Link Prediction and Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.04013
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04013
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: Small and large language models achieve similar performance on link prediction and QA tasks when knowledge is infused from knowledge graphs

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether knowledge infusion from knowledge graphs (KGs) can level the playing field between small and large language models (LMs) for link prediction and question answering tasks. The authors fine-tune T5 and BLOOM models of varying sizes on KG triplets and QA pairs across three domains (Aviation, Movie, Web). Their results show that small LMs perform similarly to large LMs when knowledge is infused, with hits@1 scores of 0.2 for link prediction and 0.7 for QA. Statistical validation using paired t-tests and Cohen's kappa scores confirms substantial agreement between model sizes, suggesting that small LMs can substitute for large ones when knowledge is infused, reducing computational costs while maintaining performance.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune language models (T5 and BLOOM) on knowledge graph triplets for link prediction, then use the best checkpoint to fine-tune on QA pairs. They evaluate performance using hits@1 scores and validate similarity between small and large models using paired Student T-tests (α=0.1) and Cohen's kappa scores. The experiments are conducted across three domains with corresponding datasets: AviationKG with AviationQA, MovieKB with MetaQA, and Freebase with CWQ. The study compares three T5 model sizes (small, base, large) and BLOOM (1.72B parameters) to assess whether model size impacts performance when knowledge is infused.

## Key Results
- Small and large language models achieve similar hits@1 scores (0.2) for link prediction on AviationKG
- T5 models of all sizes achieve hits@1 scores of 0.7 on AviationQA
- Cohen's kappa scores between small and large models range from 0.6-0.8 for link prediction and 0.4-0.6 for QA, indicating substantial agreement
- Statistical tests confirm that performance differences between small and large models are negligible after knowledge infusion

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Knowledge infusion from KGs enables small and large LMs to achieve similar performance on link prediction and QA tasks. Fine-tuning on KG triplets transfers structural knowledge to the LM, allowing it to predict entities without requiring the full scale of a large pre-trained model. Core assumption: KG triplet knowledge is sufficient for downstream tasks regardless of model size. Evidence: hits@1 scores of 0.2 for link prediction across all models, Cohen's kappa scores of 0.6-0.8. Break condition: If the KG is incomplete or noisy, the performance gap may widen.

### Mechanism 2
The T5 architecture's multi-task pretraining allows effective transfer of KG knowledge to downstream tasks. T5's pretraining on diverse NLP tasks provides a strong foundation that, when combined with KG triplet fine-tuning, enables robust entity prediction and QA. Core assumption: Pretraining provides generalizable representations adaptable to KG-specific tasks. Evidence: T5 models achieve consistent hits@1 scores of 0.7 for QA across sizes. Break condition: If tasks require highly specialized knowledge not captured in KG or pretraining, performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
Hypothesis testing (paired t-test and Cohen's kappa) validates that performance differences between small and large models are negligible after knowledge infusion. Statistical tests confirm substantial agreement between models, supporting the claim that model size is inconsequential when knowledge is infused. Core assumption: Chosen metrics are appropriate for measuring model performance similarity. Evidence: Cohen's kappa of 0.76 for link prediction between T5-small and T5-large, paired t-test confirms similarity. Break condition: If statistical tests aren't sensitive enough to detect meaningful differences, the conclusion may be misleading.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data preprocessing (KG triplet extraction, QA pair formatting) -> Fine-tuning modules (link prediction on KG triplets, then QA on QA pairs) -> Evaluation components (hits@1 scoring, statistical tests) -> Model storage (checkpoints from best link prediction runs)

- **Critical path**: 1) Preprocess KG and QA data into required formats, 2) Fine-tune chosen LM on KG triplets for link prediction, 3) Select best checkpoint based on link prediction performance, 4) Fine-tune selected checkpoint on QA pairs, 5) Evaluate using hits@1 and perform statistical tests

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using non-pretrained vs. pretrained models (pretrained may require less data but are computationally expensive), KG size and quality (larger, cleaner KGs provide better knowledge infusion but require more resources), evaluation metrics (hits@1 is precise but may not capture semantic similarity)

- **Failure signatures**: Low hits@1 scores across all models (issues with data preprocessing or fine-tuning), high variance in performance between small and large models (KG knowledge not effectively transferred), statistical tests fail to show agreement (models learning different patterns or tests not sensitive enough)

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Fine-tune T5-small on AviationKG triplets and evaluate link prediction performance, 2) Fine-tune T5-base on the same data and compare performance to T5-small, 3) Perform statistical tests (paired t-test and Cohen's kappa) to validate that the performance difference is negligible

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How do small and large language models compare in performance when knowledge is infused from incomplete or noisy knowledge graphs? The study focuses on complete KGs, and there's a lack of research on the performance of models with incomplete or noisy KGs.

- To what extent can language models learn and utilize domain-specific knowledge from knowledge graphs for tasks beyond link prediction and question answering? The study is limited to link prediction and QA, with no exploration of broader applications of knowledge-infused LMs.

- How does the choice of knowledge graph embedding method affect the performance of language models with knowledge infusion in link prediction and question answering tasks? The paper uses a specific KG embedding method without comparing different embedding methods in the context of knowledge-infused LMs.

## Limitations

- The study lacks full reproducibility due to unspecified hyperparameters and data generation templates, particularly for the AviationQA dataset creation from NTSB reports.

- The statistical validation may not fully account for the possibility that the chosen metrics (hits@1, Cohen's kappa) are not sensitive enough to detect meaningful differences in model performance.

- The study focuses on specific domains (Aviation, Movie, Web) and knowledge graphs, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other domains or KG structures.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The claim that knowledge infusion enables small and large LMs to achieve similar performance on link prediction and QA tasks is well-supported by empirical results and statistical validation.

- **Medium Confidence**: The assertion that T5's multi-task pretraining allows effective transfer of KG knowledge is plausible but relies on the assumption that pretraining provides generalizable representations, which is not directly tested.

- **Low Confidence**: The conclusion that model size is inconsequential when knowledge is infused may be overstated, as the study does not explore edge cases where the KG is incomplete or noisy, which could widen the performance gap.
