# Depth Dependence of $μ$P Learning Rates in ReLU MLPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07810
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07810
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: Maximal update learning rate scales as L^{-3/2} with depth in ReLU MLPs

## Executive Summary
This paper extends the maximal update parametrization (μP) framework to deeper ReLU MLPs by deriving the depth dependence of optimal learning rates. While μP traditionally assumes learning rates are independent of network width, this work shows that for deep networks, the maximal learning rate scales as L^{-3/2} where L is the depth. The authors provide theoretical analysis using mean-field initialization and establish an explicit bound on the error of this scaling law, enabling more accurate hyperparameter selection for deep networks.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze random fully connected ReLU networks with mean-field weight initialization, deriving the maximal update learning rate through theoretical analysis of gradient flow dynamics. They establish that for large depths, the optimal learning rate scales as L^{-3/2}, extending the μP heuristic beyond its traditional width-independent regime. The analysis involves studying the evolution of network activations and gradients through the depth of the network.

## Key Results
- The maximal update learning rate scales as L^{-3/2} with network depth
- This scaling is independent of width n for all but the first and last layer weights
- Theorem 1.1 provides an explicit bound on the error of the scaling law
- The scaling law enables accurate estimation of optimal learning rates at large depth from small-depth experiments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The L^{-3/2} scaling emerges from the interaction between depth and gradient flow in deep ReLU networks. As depth increases, gradients must propagate through more layers, experiencing multiplicative effects that compound the learning rate requirements. The mean-field initialization ensures that the variance of activations and gradients remains controlled across depth, allowing the scaling law to emerge from the fundamental dynamics of gradient-based optimization in deep networks.

## Limitations

- Theoretical bounds may not be tight in practical implementations
