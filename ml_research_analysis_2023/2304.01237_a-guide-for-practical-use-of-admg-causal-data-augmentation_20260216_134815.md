# A Guide for Practical Use of ADMG Causal Data Augmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.01237
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01237
- **Reference count**: 14
- **Primary result**: ADMG causal data augmentation improves ML performance in small-data regimes when at least 300 observations are available, but is sensitive to outliers and hyperparameter settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates ADMG (Acyclic Directed Mixed Graph) causal data augmentation for improving machine learning model performance in small-data regimes. The method generates new samples by leveraging conditional independence relations encoded in a causal graph, aiming to increase both diversity and fidelity of the dataset. Through extensive synthetic experiments varying data generation mechanisms, dimensionality, and data quality, the study identifies key conditions for success and failure. The method shows promise in high-dimensional settings but requires careful hyperparameter tuning and is vulnerable to outliers.

## Method Summary
ADMG causal data augmentation generates new samples by sampling each variable conditioned on its ancestors in the causal graph, using kernel density estimation to compute probabilities. Weights are assigned based on the probability of generated values given ancestors, creating a weighted resampling that respects the causal structure. The augmented samples are pruned using a probability threshold hyperparameter. The method is evaluated by comparing distributions of original and augmented data using KL divergence, Wasserstein distance, and variance differences, and by benchmarking against XGBoost models trained on both original and augmented datasets.

## Key Results
- CausalDA requires at least 300 observations to improve model accuracy, limiting utility in small-data regimes
- The method propagates outliers to augmented datasets, degrading model performance
- Performance is highly sensitive to the probability threshold hyperparameter, requiring careful tuning
- Works best in high-dimensional settings where causal graph structure provides more conditional independence information

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: CausalDA improves model generalization by generating new samples that preserve conditional independence relations encoded in the causal graph.
- **Mechanism**: The method generates augmented samples by sampling each variable conditioned on its ancestors in the ADMG, using kernel density estimation. Weights are assigned based on the probability of the generated values given the ancestors, effectively creating a weighted resampling that respects the causal structure.
- **Core assumption**: The true causal graph is known and accurately represents the data-generating process, and the conditional independence relations implied by the graph hold in the observed data.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states causal data augmentation strategies solve challenges by relying on conditional independence encoded in causal graphs. Section describes the method generates new samples by leveraging conditional independence relations encoded in a causal graph.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: CausalDA does not improve performance in very small-data regimes due to kernel density estimation overfitting.
- **Mechanism**: When the number of training samples is small, the kernel density estimator used to compute the weights overfits, leading to unreliable probability estimates and poor augmentation quality.
- **Core assumption**: Kernel density estimation requires a sufficient number of samples to provide stable probability estimates, and the augmented samples are generated based on these probabilities.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states method requires at least 300 observations to improve model accuracy. Section shows Fig. 2 demonstrating CausalDA requires at least 300 observations to improve XGBoost's performance.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: CausalDA performance is sensitive to the probability threshold hyperparameter, which controls the pruning of low-probability augmented samples.
- **Mechanism**: The probability threshold θ is used to prune augmented samples with low weights, effectively filtering out unlikely combinations of variable values. The choice of θ affects the distribution of the augmented data and, consequently, the model performance.
- **Core assumption**: The probability threshold is a crucial hyperparameter that needs to be carefully tuned for each dataset, as it directly influences the quality and diversity of the augmented samples.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states method is sensitive to its hyperparameter (probability threshold) value. Section illustrates this finding showing the probability threshold parameter has a very narrow value range to improve XGBoost model performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Synthetic data generation -> ADMG construction -> CausalDA algorithm -> Distribution evaluation -> Model training and benchmarking

- **Critical path**:
  1. Generate synthetic data based on specified SCM and DAG
  2. Construct the ADMG from the DAG
  3. Apply CausalDA algorithm to generate augmented samples using ADMG and probability threshold
  4. Evaluate augmented data distribution and compare to original data
  5. Train XGBoost models on augmented and original data, compare performance

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Causal graph accuracy vs. computational complexity: Building accurate causal graph can be computationally expensive, especially in high-dimensional settings
  - Kernel choice vs. sample quality: Choice of kernel function and bandwidth affects kernel density estimation quality and augmented samples
  - Probability threshold vs. sample diversity: Higher threshold leads to more pruning and less diverse samples, while lower threshold includes more samples but may introduce noise

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor model performance: May indicate issues with causal graph, kernel density estimation, or probability threshold choice
  - Augmented data distribution significantly different from original: May suggest over-pruning or under-pruning of samples
  - Sensitivity to outliers: May indicate kernel density estimation is not robust to outliers in data

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify synthetic data generation by comparing generated data distribution to specified SCM and DAG
  2. Test CausalDA algorithm on simple, low-dimensional dataset with known causal graph, verify augmented samples respect conditional independence relations
  3. Evaluate impact of probability threshold on augmented data distribution and model performance using range of threshold values

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does CausalDA perform on real-world datasets compared to synthetic data?
  - **Basis in paper**: Paper acknowledges need to evaluate CausalDA on real data as future work
  - **Why unresolved**: Current study only uses synthetic data which may not capture complexities of real-world datasets
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Testing CausalDA on various real-world datasets and comparing performance to other data augmentation methods

- **Open Question 2**: How does CausalDA perform when the causal graph is partially or completely incorrect?
  - **Basis in paper**: Authors mention analyzing sensitivity to erroneous causal graph as future work
  - **Why unresolved**: Paper assumes known causal graph, but in practice causal graphs may be incomplete or incorrect
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Evaluating CausalDA's performance with different levels of causal graph errors and comparing to performance with correct causal graph

- **Open Question 3**: What is the optimal probability threshold value for CausalDA in different scenarios?
  - **Basis in paper**: Paper highlights importance of probability threshold parameter and its narrow value range for improving XGBoost model performance
  - **Why unresolved**: Current study does not provide procedure for choosing optimal probability threshold value
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Developing method to automatically select optimal probability threshold value for different datasets and causal graph structures

## Limitations

- Strong reliance on accurate causal graph structure, which is often unknown in real-world applications
- Kernel density estimation approach is inherently unstable in small-data regimes with 300-sample threshold being dataset-dependent
- Method's sensitivity to probability threshold hyperparameter requires extensive cross-validation impractical in truly small-data settings
- Vulnerability to outliers that propagate through augmentation process, indicating data quality issues

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: CausalDA requires at least 300 observations to improve model accuracy (well-supported by empirical evidence); outliers propagate to augmented datasets (clearly demonstrated through comparative metrics)
- **Medium confidence**: CausalDA is independent of underlying model mechanism (reasonable given evaluation across scenarios but would benefit from testing additional model types); sensitivity to probability threshold (observed but optimal range appears dataset-specific)
- **Low confidence**: CausalDA performs best in high-dimensional settings (based on theoretical reasoning rather than direct empirical comparison across dimensions)

## Next Checks

1. **Causal Graph Robustness Test**: Evaluate CausalDA performance when using estimated causal graphs from causal discovery algorithms (rather than ground-truth graphs) across datasets with varying degrees of graph misspecification to quantify impact of graph accuracy on augmentation quality.

2. **Alternative Kernel Density Estimators**: Replace current kernel density estimator with more robust alternatives (e.g., Gaussian mixture models or parametric density estimators) in small-data regimes to assess whether 300-sample threshold can be lowered while maintaining stable probability estimates.

3. **Multi-Model Generalization**: Apply CausalDA to at least three additional model types (e.g., neural networks, random forests, and linear models) on same synthetic datasets to verify model-agnostic behavior and identify any model-specific sensitivities or advantages.
