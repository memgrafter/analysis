# Evaluating Factual Consistency of Texts with Semantic Role Labeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13309
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13309
- Reference count: 20
- Introduces SRLScore, a reference-free metric for factual consistency evaluation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SRLScore, a novel reference-free metric for evaluating factual consistency in text summarization. The method leverages semantic role labeling (SRL) to generate fact tuples from both source and summary texts, then computes a factuality score based on the similarity between these tuples. The authors demonstrate that SRLScore achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art methods while maintaining stable generalization across datasets without requiring additional training or hyperparameter tuning.

## Method Summary
SRLScore generates fact tuples by applying semantic role labeling to input and summary texts. These tuples capture predicate-argument relationships extracted from the texts. The method then computes a factuality score by measuring the semantic similarity between fact tuples from the source text and those from the summary. This approach enables reference-free evaluation of factual consistency, distinguishing it from metrics that require reference summaries for comparison.

## Key Results
- Achieves Pearson correlation scores ranging from 0.39 to 0.45 with human judgments
- Achieves Spearman correlation scores ranging from 0.32 to 0.59 across evaluation datasets
- Demonstrates stable generalization across datasets without requiring further training or hyperparameter tuning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by extracting structured semantic information from text through SRL, converting unstructured text into structured fact tuples that capture predicate-argument relationships. By comparing these structured representations between source and summary texts, the metric can identify factual inconsistencies that might be missed by surface-level text similarity measures.

## Foundational Learning

**Semantic Role Labeling (SRL)**
- Why needed: Extracts predicate-argument structures that represent factual relationships in text
- Quick check: Can identify who did what to whom in a sentence

**Fact Tuples**
- Why needed: Provides structured representation of facts for comparison
- Quick check: Should capture core semantic relationships independent of surface form

**Semantic Similarity Measures**
- Why needed: Enables comparison of fact tuples regardless of exact wording
- Quick check: Should recognize semantically equivalent facts expressed differently

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Input Text -> SRL Parser -> Fact Tuple Generator -> Similarity Calculator -> Factuality Score

**Critical Path:** The SRL parsing step is the most computationally intensive and critical component, as it directly determines the quality of extracted fact tuples.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Reference-free evaluation enables broader applicability but may miss some nuanced factual inconsistencies that require context from reference summaries.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor SRL parsing quality, particularly with complex sentences or domain-specific terminology, will directly degrade fact tuple quality and overall metric performance.

**First Experiments:**
1. Evaluate SRLScore on a small, controlled dataset with known factual errors
2. Compare fact tuple extraction quality across different SRL parsers
3. Test sensitivity by introducing controlled variations in semantic content

## Limitations
- Computational overhead from parsing input texts may limit scalability
- Performance claims are primarily validated on summarization datasets, with uncertain generalization to other text generation tasks
- Moderate correlation values (0.39-0.59) suggest usefulness as a supplementary rather than definitive metric

## Confidence

**High confidence:** The core methodology of using semantic role labeling to generate fact tuples for factual consistency evaluation is technically sound and well-implemented

**Medium confidence:** The claim of stable generalization across datasets without additional tuning is supported but limited by the scope of evaluation datasets

**Medium confidence:** The competitive performance claims relative to state-of-the-art methods are substantiated but based on moderate correlation values

## Next Checks

2. Conduct ablation studies to quantify the specific contribution of semantic role labeling versus other potential fact extraction methods
3. Test scalability by measuring performance and runtime on large-scale document collections to assess practical deployment feasibility
