# NorBench -- A Benchmark for Norwegian Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03880
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03880
- Reference count: 21
- Introduces NorBench, a standardized benchmark for evaluating Norwegian language models across various tasks

## Executive Summary
NorBench is a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate Norwegian language models across multiple tasks including morphosyntactic tagging, sentiment analysis, question answering, and machine translation. The paper introduces two new Norwegian language models, NorBERT3 and NorT5, trained on various Norwegian corpora. These models are evaluated against existing models, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance, particularly for the largest NorBERT3 model. The study also explores the impact of different training corpora on model performance and assesses the models for gender bias and harmful language generation.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces NorBench, a standardized benchmark for evaluating Norwegian language models across a wide range of tasks including morphosyntactic tagging, sentiment analysis, question answering, and machine translation. It also presents new Norwegian language models (NorBERT3 and NorT5) trained on various Norwegian corpora. The models are evaluated and compared against existing models, showing that the largest NorBERT3 model achieves state-of-the-art performance on most tasks. Additionally, the paper explores the impact of different training corpora on model performance, finding that simple concatenation of all available resources does not always lead to the best results. The models are also assessed for gender bias and harmful language generation, with the NorBERT3 and NorT5 models showing lower levels of bias and harm compared to other Norwegian models.

## Key Results
- NorBERT3 and NorT5 models achieve state-of-the-art performance on most tasks in the NorBench benchmark
- Simple concatenation of all available resources does not always lead to the best results in training
- NorBERT3 and NorT5 models show lower levels of gender bias and harmful language generation compared to other Norwegian models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's approach works by introducing a comprehensive benchmark (NorBench) that covers a wide range of tasks relevant to Norwegian language processing. This allows for a thorough evaluation of language models across different domains and use cases. The introduction of new models (NorBERT3 and NorT5) trained on diverse Norwegian corpora enables the exploration of how different training data compositions affect model performance. By comparing these models against existing ones and assessing them for bias and harmful content, the study provides a holistic view of their capabilities and limitations.

**Critical Path**
The critical path involves pre-training the models on diverse Norwegian corpora, fine-tuning them on specific NorBench tasks, and then evaluating their performance. The quality and diversity of the pre-training data, as well as the effectiveness of the fine-tuning process, are crucial for achieving high performance on the benchmark tasks.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Model size vs. computational efficiency: Larger models like the biggest NorBERT3 may achieve better performance but require more resources for training and inference.
- Training data diversity vs. data quality: Including a wide range of data sources may improve generalization but could also introduce noise or biases.

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor performance on specific tasks may indicate insufficient fine-tuning or a mismatch between the pre-training data and the task requirements.
- High levels of gender bias or harmful content generation suggest inadequate bias mitigation strategies during training.

## Limitations
- The evaluation of gender bias and harmful language generation relies on subjective assessments, which may introduce variability in results.
- The study does not provide detailed methodology for the subjective evaluations, making it difficult to assess their reliability.
- The analysis of training corpus composition is limited to a few configurations, and the paper does not explore the impact of different data balancing strategies or the inclusion of additional specialized datasets.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The experimental results demonstrating the performance of NorBERT3 and NorT5 models on the NorBench benchmark tasks are well-supported by the data and methodology presented.
- **Medium Confidence**: The findings related to the impact of different training corpora on model performance are supported by the experiments conducted, but the limited exploration of alternative corpus configurations and balancing strategies introduces some uncertainty.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the study's findings to other languages or multilingual settings is uncertain due to the exclusive focus on Norwegian language models.
