# On Practical Aspects of Aggregation Defenses against Data Poisoning Attacks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.16415
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16415
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: DPA with k=3 achieves 54.20% attack success rate versus 70.27% for single model when 32 samples are poisoned

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates practical aspects of Deep Partition Aggregation (DPA) as a defense against data poisoning attacks in deep learning systems. The authors scale up evaluations to ImageNet64×64, proposing a width scaling rule of 1/√k for base models where k is the number of partitions. They demonstrate that DPA improves poisoning robustness through a phenomenon they call "poisoning overfitting" - where attack success rates decrease when training set size is reduced while keeping poisoned sample percentage constant. The work provides empirical guidance on selecting the maximum number of base models based on the data-to-complexity ratio, showing that DPA can substantially improve robustness compared to single-model training.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates DPA as a defense mechanism against data poisoning attacks by training multiple base models on different partitions of the training data and aggregating their predictions. To maintain efficiency while scaling to larger datasets, the authors propose scaling the width of base models by 1/√k where k is the number of partitions. The evaluation is conducted on ImageNet64×64, a downsampled version of ImageNet. The authors test various poisoning scenarios with different numbers of poisoned samples and examine how the number of base models affects attack success rates. They also investigate the relationship between training set size and poisoning effectiveness, observing the "poisoning overfitting" phenomenon where smaller training sets paradoxically lead to lower attack success rates.

## Key Results
- DPA with k=3 achieves 54.20% attack success rate versus 70.27% for single model when 32 samples are poisoned
- Poisoning overfitting phenomenon observed: attack success rates decrease when training set size is reduced while keeping poisoned sample percentage constant
- Proposed 1/√k width scaling rule maintains accuracy while improving efficiency
- Empirical guidance provided on maximum number of base models based on data-to-complexity ratio

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind DPA's effectiveness against data poisoning attacks relies on the aggregation of diverse base models trained on different data partitions. When base models are trained independently on disjoint subsets of the training data, they develop different decision boundaries and capture different aspects of the data distribution. A poisoning attack that succeeds on one partition may fail on others due to the diversity in learned representations. The aggregation step effectively creates a voting system where the poisoned model's influence is diluted by the majority of clean models. The "poisoning overfitting" phenomenon occurs because smaller training sets force base models to generalize more from limited data, making them less susceptible to localized poisoning patterns that exploit specific training examples.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Partitioner -> Base Model Trainer -> Model Aggregator -> Final Predictor

**Critical Path**: Training data is partitioned, each partition trains an independent base model with scaled width, predictions from all base models are aggregated (typically through averaging or voting), and the aggregated prediction is used for final inference

**Design Tradeoffs**: The number of base models (k) versus computational cost and memory usage; model width scaling (1/√k) versus maintaining sufficient model capacity; partition size versus diversity of base models

**Failure Signatures**: Attack success increases when base models are not sufficiently diverse; poor performance when width scaling factor is too aggressive; degradation when k exceeds data-to-complexity ratio threshold

## Limitations

- Evaluation limited to ImageNet64×64 dataset, raising questions about generalizability to full-resolution images and other datasets
- Width scaling factor of 1/√k lacks theoretical justification and may not generalize to different model architectures
- Data-to-complexity ratio guidance for base model selection appears heuristic rather than theoretically grounded
- Attack scenarios focus on relatively small poisoning fractions that may not reflect real-world attack scales
- No exploration of adaptive attacks specifically designed to circumvent DPA's aggregation mechanism
