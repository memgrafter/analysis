# On the Implicit Geometry of Cross-Entropy Parameterizations for Label-Imbalanced Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07608
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07608
- Reference count: 40
- This paper investigates the implicit geometry of classifiers and embeddings learned by two logit-adjusted parameterizations of the cross-entropy loss—class-dependent temperature (CDT) and label-dependent temperature (LDT)—when training on label-imbalanced data far beyond the zero-training error regime.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores how logit-adjusted parameterizations of cross-entropy loss affect the implicit geometry of learned classifiers and embeddings in the presence of label imbalance. By analyzing a non-convex cost-sensitive SVM problem under the unconstrained features model (UFM), the authors derive closed-form expressions for classifier angles and norms as functions of class imbalance, minority ratio, and hyperparameters. They introduce the (δ,R)-SELI geometry, which generalizes prior findings (ETF and SELI) and includes them as special cases. The study demonstrates that both CDT and LDT can mitigate minority collapse for appropriate hyperparameter tuning, with LDT requiring γ = 1/2 and CDT tolerating a wider range γ ≥ 1/6. Experimental results across UFM, MLP, and ResNet architectures validate the theoretical predictions, though convergence slows for extreme imbalance ratios.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze the implicit geometry of classifiers and embeddings under label imbalance by studying global minimizers of a non-convex cost-sensitive SVM problem. This problem serves as an abstraction of deep networks under the unconstrained features model (UFM), where the last layer is optimized while feature representations are held fixed. The analysis focuses on two logit-adjusted parameterizations: class-dependent temperature (CDT) and label-dependent temperature (LDT). For each parameterization, the authors derive closed-form formulas for classifier angles and norms as functions of the number of classes, imbalance ratio, minority ratio, and loss hyperparameters. These formulas reveal a new geometric structure termed (δ,R)-SELI, which unifies and generalizes prior ETF and SELI geometries. Experiments on UFM, MLP, and ResNet architectures confirm the theoretical predictions, though convergence becomes slower for larger imbalance ratios and hyperparameter values.

## Key Results
- Both CDT and LDT induce a new geometry termed (δ,R)-SELI, which generalizes previously discovered ETF and SELI geometries.
- CDT tolerates a wider range of hyperparameters (γ ≥ 1/6) to mitigate minority collapse, while LDT requires γ = 1/2.
- The (δ,R)-SELI geometry includes ETF and SELI as special cases, unifying prior findings.
- Experimental results confirm theoretical predictions across UFM, MLP, and ResNet architectures, though convergence slows for extreme imbalance ratios.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that logit-adjusted parameterizations of cross-entropy loss induce specific geometric structures in the classifier space that can mitigate minority collapse under label imbalance. By analyzing the global minimizers of a cost-sensitive SVM problem under UFM, the authors show that both CDT and LDT lead to the (δ,R)-SELI geometry, characterized by specific relationships between classifier angles and norms. This geometry emerges from the interplay between the loss hyperparameters, the class imbalance structure, and the regularization imposed by the parameterization. The mechanism works by adjusting the effective margin and scaling of minority classes, preventing their features from collapsing to zero while maintaining separation from majority classes.

Component Map:

Critical Path:
The critical path involves: 1) Feature extraction under UFM assumptions, 2) Optimization of the last linear layer under logit-adjusted cross-entropy, 3) Convergence to global minimizers exhibiting (δ,R)-SELI geometry, 4) Mitigation of minority collapse through appropriate hyperparameter tuning.

Design Tradeoffs:
- UFM assumption vs. full network training: The theoretical analysis assumes fixed features, but real networks jointly train all layers. This tradeoff enables tractable analysis but may not capture all deep network behaviors.
- Closed-form analysis vs. empirical validation: The paper provides exact formulas under idealized conditions but must validate them experimentally on real architectures.
- Geometric characterization vs. generalization: While the (δ,R)-SELI geometry is precisely characterized, its relationship to generalization performance requires further investigation.

Failure Signatures:
- Slow convergence for large imbalance ratios or extreme hyperparameter values
- Deviation from predicted (δ,R)-SELI geometry in experiments
- Minority collapse persisting despite logit adjustment
- Divergence from UFM assumptions in joint training scenarios

## Limitations
- The closed-form characterizations rely on a non-convex cost-sensitive SVM abstraction under UFM, which may not fully capture deep network behavior.
- The empirical validation is limited to UFM, MLP, and ResNet architectures without extensive testing on diverse datasets or architectures.
- The study notes slower convergence for extreme imbalance ratios and hyperparameter settings.
- The proposed post-hoc rescaling strategy for LDT is preliminary and lacks thorough analysis of its impact across different scenarios.
- The theoretical results assume convergence to global minimizers, but real training dynamics may involve saddle points or local minima.
