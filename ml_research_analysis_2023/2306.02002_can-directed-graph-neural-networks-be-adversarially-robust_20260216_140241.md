# Can Directed Graph Neural Networks be Adversarially Robust?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02002
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02002
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper introduces Biased Bidirectional Random Walk (BBRW) message-passing framework that substantially enhances the robustness of various GNN backbones against adversarial attacks on directed graphs.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical gap in graph neural network (GNN) research by investigating adversarial robustness on directed graphs, where existing work has focused primarily on undirected graphs. The authors identify that current directed GNNs are vulnerable to indirect attacks and propose a novel attack setting called Restricted Directed Graph Attack (RDGA) that differentiates between in-link and out-link attacks. To address these vulnerabilities, they introduce the Biased Bidirectional Random Walk (BBRW) framework, which leverages directional information in directed graphs to improve both clean and robust performance. BBRW is a plug-in layer that can be integrated with various GNN backbones to significantly enhance their robustness against transfer and adaptive attacks.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes the Restricted Directed Graph Attack (RDGA) setting, which differentiates between in-link and out-link attacks while restricting out-link attacks on target nodes. The key innovation is the Biased Bidirectional Random Walk (BBRW) message-passing framework, which uses a weighted sum of the adjacency matrix A and its transpose A⊤, controlled by hyperparameter β. This creates a propagation matrix that balances trust between out-links and in-links, avoiding catastrophic failures under adaptive attacks. BBRW can be readily used as a plug-in layer to improve the robustness of various GNN backbones including GCN, APPNP, and GCN-Soft-Median. The method is evaluated on directed graph datasets Cora-ML and Citeseer using PGD attack algorithms.

## Key Results
- BBRW-GCN outperforms standard GCN by 23.5%, 45.5%, and 73% under transfer attacks with increasing budgets on Cora-ML.
- BBRW achieves state-of-the-art robust performance compared to existing directed and undirected GNNs.
- The method prevents catastrophic failures under adaptive attacks that plague existing directed GNN approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** BBRW leverages both in-link and out-link information while trusting out-links more to improve clean accuracy and robustness.
- **Mechanism:** BBRW uses a weighted sum of the adjacency matrix A and its transpose A⊤, controlled by hyperparameter β. This creates a propagation matrix that balances trust between out-links and in-links.
- **Core assumption:** Out-links are more reliable than in-links in directed graphs, but both types of links contain useful topology information.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "BBRW is a plug-in layer that substantially enhances the robustness of various GNN backbones." [section 3.2] "Aβ is the weighted sum of A and A⊤ that combines the out-links (directed random walk) and in-links (inversely directed random walk)."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** BBRW avoids catastrophic failures under adaptive attacks by preventing unconditional trust in out-links.
- **Mechanism:** By using β < 1, BBRW incorporates some in-link information, which prevents indirect attacks on neighbors from catastrophically destroying target node predictions.
- **Core assumption:** Adversaries can attack neighbors of target nodes, and these indirect attacks can propagate through out-links to affect target predictions.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 3.1] "When attacking GCN-RWout, the adversary...conducts indirect out-link attacks on these neighbors using 65.55% budgets. As a result, it catastrophically destroys the predictions of target nodes indirectly." [section 3.2] "When β = 1, it reduces to RWout that fully trusts out-links. But RWout suffers from catastrophic failures under adaptive attacks."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** BBRW is universal and can be plugged into various GNN backbones to enhance their robustness.
- **Mechanism:** BBRW replaces the standard aggregation matrix in GNNs with its biased bidirectional random walk propagation matrix while maintaining the same computational complexity.
- **Core assumption:** The aggregation step is the critical vulnerability point in GNNs that can be improved without changing other architectural components.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "BBRW is a plug-in layer that substantially enhances the robustness of various GNN backbones." [section 3.2] "It is universal so that it can be readily used as a plug-in layer to improve the robustness of various GNN backbones."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** A → BBRW layer → GNN backbone → Predictions
- **Critical path:** A → BBRW layer → GNN backbone → Predictions
  The BBRW layer is the critical innovation that replaces standard aggregation matrices.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Simplicity vs. expressiveness: BBRW adds only one hyperparameter (β) while significantly improving robustness
  - Computational cost: Same as standard GNNs (no additional complexity)
  - Flexibility: Works as a plug-in layer but may not be optimal for all architectures

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor clean accuracy: β is set too far from 0.5, losing useful information from one direction
  - Catastrophic failure under adaptive attack: β is set too close to 1, making the model vulnerable to indirect attacks
  - No improvement over baseline: Incorrect implementation of the propagation matrix or hyperparameter tuning

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement BBRW with GCN backbone on Cora-ML dataset with β=0.7; verify clean accuracy improvement over standard GCN.
  2. Test BBRW-GCN against transfer attack on Cora-ML; confirm robustness improvement over standard GCN.
  3. Test BBRW-GCN against adaptive attack on Cora-ML; verify that β<1 prevents catastrophic failures seen in RWout.

## Limitations
- The paper's claims about BBRW's universality and plug-and-play nature remain largely theoretical, as experimental validation focuses on a limited set of GNN backbones and two datasets.
- The optimal β tuning procedure is not fully specified, and the claim that β < 1 universally prevents catastrophic failures across different graph structures needs further validation.
- The RDGA attack setting, while novel, may have implementation nuances that could affect reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of BBRW and its ability to balance in-link and out-link information through the β parameter is well-supported by both theoretical derivation and experimental results.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that BBRW is universally applicable as a plug-in layer to various GNN backbones is supported but not exhaustively tested.
- **Low confidence**: The paper's claims about BBRW's performance relative to state-of-the-art methods are difficult to fully verify without access to all baseline implementations and complete experimental details.
