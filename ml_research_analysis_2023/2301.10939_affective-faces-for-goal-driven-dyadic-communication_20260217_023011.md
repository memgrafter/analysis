# Affective Faces for Goal-Driven Dyadic Communication

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10939
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10939
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Video framework that retrieves listener facial expressions based on speaker speech and listener goals using LLMs and VLMs

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel framework for modeling dyadic communication by generating appropriate listener facial expressions in response to speaker speech. The system leverages large language models and vision-language models to create interpretable representations that condition video retrieval on specific listener goals, personalities, or backgrounds. The approach addresses the challenge of producing socially appropriate non-verbal responses in conversational contexts. A new dataset of unscripted conversations with diverse demographics is introduced to support the research. Experimental results demonstrate superior performance compared to baseline methods in predicting socially appropriate listener responses.

## Method Summary
The framework operates by taking speaker speech as input and using LLMs to generate interpretable representations of the conversational context and listener goals. These representations are then processed through vision-language models to retrieve or generate appropriate listener facial expressions from a curated video dataset. The system can condition responses on various factors including listener goals, personality traits, or demographic backgrounds. The video retrieval component searches for existing footage of listeners displaying contextually appropriate expressions rather than generating new video content from scratch. This hybrid approach combines the strengths of language understanding with visual expression modeling to create contextually appropriate dyadic interactions.

## Key Results
- Framework significantly outperforms baseline methods in predicting socially appropriate listener expressions
- New video dataset of unscripted conversations with diverse topics and demographics enables robust evaluation
- Visualizations demonstrate interpretable conditioning on listener goals and personalities
- Public release of dataset and code supports reproducibility and further research

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach succeeds by leveraging the contextual understanding capabilities of large language models to interpret conversational dynamics, combined with vision-language models that can map these interpretations to appropriate facial expressions. By conditioning on specific listener attributes (goals, personalities, backgrounds), the system can generate responses that are not just socially appropriate in general, but tailored to the specific conversational context and relationship dynamics. The video retrieval approach, rather than direct generation, ensures that the facial expressions are grounded in real human behavior captured from authentic conversations.

- **Dyadic communication dynamics**: Understanding the bidirectional nature of conversational exchanges where both parties influence each other's responses - needed to model realistic listener reactions that are contextually appropriate
- **Large language model contextualization**: How LLMs can extract meaningful representations from conversational speech - needed to bridge the gap between verbal content and appropriate non-verbal responses
- **Vision-language model integration**: The process of connecting language-based representations to visual expression retrieval - needed to map abstract conversational context to concrete facial expressions
- **Video retrieval vs generation**: Understanding when to use existing video content versus generating new content - needed to ensure expressions are grounded in authentic human behavior
- **Social appropriateness modeling**: Defining and operationalizing what constitutes contextually appropriate facial expressions - needed to evaluate and guide the system's output
- **Dataset curation for conversational AI**: Methods for collecting diverse, unscripted conversation data - needed to train and evaluate models on realistic interaction scenarios

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Speaker Speech -> LLM Contextualizer -> VLM Representation Mapper -> Video Retriever -> Output Listener Video

Critical Path:
The critical path flows from speaker speech through the LLM for contextual understanding, then through the VLM for representation-to-expression mapping, and finally to the video retriever for output generation. Each component must function correctly for appropriate listener expressions to be produced.

Design Tradeoffs:
- Video retrieval vs direct generation: Retrieval ensures authenticity but limits expressiveness; generation offers more flexibility but may produce less natural expressions
- LLM vs rule-based systems: LLMs provide nuanced understanding but introduce opacity; rule-based systems are interpretable but may miss subtle contextual cues
- Dataset size vs diversity: Larger datasets provide more examples but may lack diversity; smaller curated datasets may be more focused but less generalizable

Failure Signatures:
- Inappropriate expressions: Indicates issues with LLM contextualization or VLM mapping
- Generic expressions: Suggests insufficient conditioning on listener-specific attributes
- Timing mismatches: Points to problems in synchronizing retrieved expressions with speech content
- Cultural misalignment: Reveals limitations in dataset diversity or model training

## Limitations
- Human evaluation introduces subjectivity and potential cultural bias in determining social appropriateness
- Framework creates a "black box" effect where specific mechanisms linking speech to expressions remain opaque
- Dataset collection using scripted conversations may not fully capture spontaneity of real-world interactions
- Focus on facial expressions alone provides a partial view of non-verbal communication

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Technical implementation using LLMs and VLMs is sound and well-executed
- **Medium confidence**: Significant performance improvements over baselines, though evaluation is subjective
- **Medium confidence**: Dataset quality and diversity are reasonable but require real-world validation
