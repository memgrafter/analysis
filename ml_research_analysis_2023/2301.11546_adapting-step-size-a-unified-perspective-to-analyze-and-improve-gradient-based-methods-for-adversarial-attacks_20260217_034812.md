# Adapting Step-size: A Unified Perspective to Analyze and Improve Gradient-based Methods for Adversarial Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11546
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11546
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 32
- This paper provides a unified theoretical interpretation of gradient-based adversarial attack methods by analyzing their step-size strategies. The authors show that methods like FGSM, I-FGSM, and MI-FGSM are reformulations of their original gradient methods using step-size rules with only current gradient information. They then propose a broad class of adaptive gradient-based algorithms that integrate step-size strategies utilizing information from accumulated gradients. These adaptive methods normalize the scale of gradients and stabilize the optimization process, improving convergence. Experiments demonstrate that their AdaI-FGM consistently outperforms I-FGSM and AdaMI-FGM remains competitive with MI-FGSM for black-box attacks. The proposed adaptive step-size strategy effectively boosts the transferability and stability of gradient-based adversarial attack methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a unified theoretical framework for understanding gradient-based adversarial attack methods by analyzing their step-size strategies. The authors demonstrate that popular methods like FGSM, I-FGSM, and MI-FGSM can be reformulated as gradient methods with specific step-size rules. Building on this insight, they propose adaptive variants (AdaI-FGM and AdaMI-FGM) that use accumulated gradient information to normalize step sizes, improving convergence and stability. The adaptive methods show consistent performance improvements over baseline methods in white-box and black-box attack scenarios.

## Method Summary
The paper reformulates gradient-based adversarial attacks as constrained optimization problems and analyzes their step-size strategies. The authors show that FGSM, I-FGSM, and MI-FGSM are equivalent to gradient methods with step-sizes computed using only current gradient information. They then propose adaptive variants that integrate step-size strategies utilizing accumulated gradient information. The AdaI-FGM uses arithmetic average or EMA of historical gradients for step-size normalization, while AdaMI-FGM extends this with Heavy Ball momentum. These adaptive methods normalize gradient scales rather than using empirical clipping or sign functions, stabilizing the optimization process and improving convergence.

## Key Results
- AdaI-FGM consistently outperforms I-FGSM across different models and settings
- AdaMI-FGM remains competitive with MI-FGSM for black-box attacks
- Adaptive step-size strategies improve transferability and stability of gradient-based attacks
- Convergence is guaranteed and the optimization process is stabilized with adaptive step-sizes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The adaptive step-size strategy stabilizes the optimization process by normalizing gradient scales rather than using empirical clipping or sign functions.
- Mechanism: By using accumulated gradient information (via arithmetic average or EMA) to compute step-sizes, the algorithm adaptively scales each coordinate of the gradient, preventing issues with gradient magnitude variance that plague fixed step-size methods like I-FGSM.
- Core assumption: The accumulated gradient information provides a stable estimate of gradient scale across iterations, and normalizing with this information improves convergence properties.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Such adaptive step-size strategies directly normalize the scale of the gradients rather than use some empirical operations."
  - [section] "In contrast to the available gradient-based algorithms and our adaptive ones lies in the way of normalizing the scale of the gradients. The former uses the sign function or l1-norm...while the latter employs the step-size with the accumulated gradients."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations in corpus, but related to adaptive gradient methods like Adam and AdaGrad
- Break condition: If accumulated gradient statistics become unstable or if the optimization landscape changes rapidly, the adaptive step-size may overcompensate, leading to poor convergence.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Integrating momentum with adaptive step-size improves both convergence speed and adversarial transferability.
- Mechanism: The momentum term accumulates historical gradient directions while the adaptive step-size normalizes their magnitude, providing both direction stability and appropriate scaling. This dual benefit addresses both the "zig-zag" problem of pure gradient methods and the scaling issues of fixed step-size methods.
- Core assumption: The momentum direction remains meaningful when combined with adaptive scaling, and the combined effect preserves the benefits of both components.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Motivated by such analysis, we present a broad class of adaptive gradient-based algorithms...in which the step-size strategy utilizing information of the accumulated gradients is integrated."
  - [section] "AdaMI-FGM can be regarded as a natural extension of AdaI-FGM in terms of the HB momentum."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - mentions related work but no direct citations on this specific combination
- Break condition: If momentum and adaptive scaling interfere destructively (e.g., adaptive scaling cancels beneficial momentum effects), performance may degrade below baseline methods.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The adaptive methods provide theoretical convergence guarantees that the original sign-based methods lack.
- Mechanism: By replacing empirical operations (sign function, l1-norm) with principled adaptive step-size calculations, the algorithms can be analyzed using established convergence theory for adaptive gradient methods, providing stability guarantees.
- Core assumption: The adaptive step-size formulation is mathematically equivalent to or can be analyzed using the same techniques as established adaptive methods like AdaGrad and Adam.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The important benefit is that convergence for the iterative algorithms is guaranteed and then the whole optimization process can be stabilized."
  - [section] "For convex and strongly convex functions, AdaGrad (9) enjoys the same order of convergence rates of as PGD (8) [15, 25]."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus - this is a theoretical claim that would need separate verification
- Break condition: If the specific adaptive formulation deviates significantly from the assumptions of established convergence proofs, the guarantees may not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Gradient computation module (forward/backward pass)
  - Accumulated gradient statistics tracker (Vt computation)
  - Step-size calculator (normalization with Vt)
  - Projection/clip module (enforcing lp constraints)
  - Momentum accumulator (for AdaMI-FGM variant)
  - Loss evaluation module

- Critical path:
  1. Compute gradient of loss w.r.t. input
  2. Update accumulated gradient statistics (Vt)
  3. Compute adaptive step-size using Vt
  4. Update adversarial example with normalized gradient
  5. Project/clip to maintain constraint
  6. Evaluate loss and check stopping criteria

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. accuracy: Storing all past gradients vs. using EMA
  - Computational overhead: Additional operations for adaptive normalization
  - Hyperparameter sensitivity: β (EMA decay), δ (stabilization), momentum coefficient
  - Batch vs. single sample: Different gradient statistics collection strategies

- Failure signatures:
  - Vanishing gradients: δ too small, Vt grows too large
  - Overshooting: Step-size too aggressive, causing constraint violations
  - Oscillations: Momentum too high relative to adaptive scaling
  - Slow convergence: Accumulation window too short or β too small

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline comparison: Run I-FGSM, AdaI-FGM(1), AdaI-FGM(2), AdaI-FGM(3) on a simple CNN with CIFAR-10, measuring success rate and convergence speed
  2. Ablation study: Test AdaI-FGM with only momentum, only adaptive step-size, and both combined to isolate effects
  3. Constraint sensitivity: Vary ϵ constraint and measure how adaptive vs. non-adaptive methods perform across different perturbation budgets

## Limitations

- The theoretical convergence guarantees are not formally proven, and some empirical results contradict expected behavior
- Transfer attack experiments are limited to white-box attacks with specific Inception architectures, limiting generalization to real-world scenarios
- Additional hyperparameters (β for EMA, δ for stabilization) require careful tuning and may affect practical applicability

## Confidence

**High confidence** in the mathematical equivalence between sign-based methods and step-size formulations. The derivations connecting FGSM, I-FGSM, and MI-FGSM to their step-size representations are rigorous and well-established.

**Medium confidence** in the empirical performance claims. The results show consistent improvements for AdaI-FGM and competitive performance for AdaMI-FGM, but the effect sizes are modest and depend on specific experimental conditions.

**Low confidence** in the theoretical convergence guarantees. While the paper references convergence properties of related adaptive methods, it doesn't provide formal proofs for the specific adaptive formulations proposed, and some empirical results contradict the expected behavior.
