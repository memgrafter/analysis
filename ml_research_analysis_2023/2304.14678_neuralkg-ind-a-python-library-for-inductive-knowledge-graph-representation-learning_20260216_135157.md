# NeuralKG-ind: A Python Library for Inductive Knowledge Graph Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14678
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14678
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: First library specifically designed for inductive knowledge graph representation learning, achieving AUC-PR scores from 0.821 to 0.960 and Hit@10 scores from 0.847 to 0.964 on benchmark datasets

## Executive Summary
NeuralKG-ind is the first comprehensive Python library specifically designed for inductive knowledge graph representation learning (KGRL). It extends the NeuralKG framework by adding inductive-specific modules including subgraph extraction, node labeling, and meta-learning samplers. The library provides standardized pipelines, rich existing methods (GraIL, CoMPILE, SNRI, RMPI, MorsE), and comprehensive evaluation metrics to enable reproducible research in this emerging field. NeuralKG-ind addresses the challenge of enabling KGRL methods to extrapolate to unseen entities in dynamic knowledge graphs, which is crucial for real-world applications where knowledge graphs continuously evolve.

## Method Summary
NeuralKG-ind provides a standardized pipeline for inductive KGRL that includes data processing (Data2pkl, Gen_subgraph_datasets, Node_label), sampler modules (SubSampler, ValidSampler, TestSampler), trainer modules inherited from PyTorch Lightning, and evaluation tasks (link prediction and triple classification). The library implements inductive-specific techniques such as enclosing subgraph extraction around unseen entity pairs and distance-based node labeling to convert topological information into node features. It also includes meta-learning sampler design that splits subgraphs into support and query triples for task-based learning. The library builds upon the NeuralKG framework and adds comprehensive evaluation metrics including AUC-PR, MRR, and Hit@10 scores to enable reliable comparison across different inductive KGRL methods.

## Key Results
- Successfully replicates reported results on FB15k-237 and NELL-995 datasets with AUC-PR scores ranging from 0.821 to 0.960
- Achieves Hit@10 scores between 0.847 to 0.964 across different models and datasets
- Provides standardized implementation of 5 representative inductive KGRL methods including GraIL, CoMPILE, SNRI, RMPI, and MorsE
- Enables reproducible research through comprehensive evaluation metrics and standardized pipelines

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do inductive KGRL methods perform when unseen entities have textual information available?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions future plans to include methods that handle unseen entities with textual information, indicating this is an open research direction.
- Why unresolved: Current inductive KGRL methods focus on topology-based approaches without leveraging textual information, and the effectiveness of combining textual features with inductive reasoning remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of inductive KGRL methods with and without textual information on benchmark datasets, measuring improvements in metrics like AUC-PR and MRR.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different negative sampling strategies on inductive KGRL performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper describes multiple negative sampling approaches used in different samplers but does not systematically compare their impact on model performance.
- Why unresolved: Different inductive KGRL methods use various negative sampling strategies (e.g., SubSampler, TestSampler_hit, TestSampler_auc), but their relative effectiveness and impact on final results are not thoroughly investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing different negative sampling strategies across multiple inductive KGRL methods on the same datasets, measuring performance differences in AUC-PR, MRR, and Hit@10.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How well do inductive KGRL methods generalize across different knowledge graph domains?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper reports results on FB15k-237 and NELL-995 datasets but does not explore cross-domain generalization or performance on diverse KG domains.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation is limited to two specific datasets, and there is no analysis of how well inductive KGRL methods transfer across different knowledge graph domains or scales.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluation of inductive KGRL methods across multiple knowledge graphs from different domains (e.g., biomedical, social, general knowledge) with cross-domain training and testing experiments.

## Limitations
- Performance claims are based on specific datasets (FB15k-237 and NELL-995) with limited exploration of diverse knowledge graph domains
- Detailed experimental configurations and hyperparameters are not fully specified, potentially limiting reproducibility
- Implementation of some recent inductive KGRL methods may be incomplete in the current library version
