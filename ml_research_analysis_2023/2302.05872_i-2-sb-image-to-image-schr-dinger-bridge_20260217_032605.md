# I$^2$SB: Image-to-Image Schrödinger Bridge

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.05872
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05872
- **Reference count:** 35
- **Key outcome:** Novel conditional diffusion model for image-to-image translation that directly learns nonlinear diffusion bridges between degraded and clean image distributions, achieving superior performance on image restoration tasks with fewer function evaluations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces I²SB, a novel conditional diffusion model for image-to-image translation that directly learns nonlinear diffusion processes between degraded and clean image distributions. Unlike standard diffusion models that start from random noise, I²SB uses degraded images as the initial distribution and learns to reverse a nonlinear diffusion process to reconstruct clean images. The method exploits the linear structure hidden in the nonlinear coupling of the Schrödinger Bridge framework, making training tractable while inheriting the benefits of score-based models. Experimental results on ImageNet 256x256 show I²SB surpasses standard conditional diffusion models and matches diffusion-based inverse models without requiring knowledge of corruption operators.

## Method Summary
I²SB is a conditional diffusion model that learns the reverse process from degraded to clean images by exploiting the Schrödinger Bridge framework. It formulates image restoration as a nonlinear diffusion process between paired clean and degraded images, where the degraded image serves as the initial distribution. The method reformulates the Schrödinger bridge problem using score functions of linear SDEs, allowing adoption of scalable techniques from standard diffusion models. During training, I²SB samples from the posterior distribution q(Xt|X0,X1) and optimizes a denoising score matching objective. For generation, it starts from the degraded image and iteratively applies the learned denoising process to reconstruct the clean image.

## Key Results
- I²SB surpasses standard conditional diffusion models (Palette, ADM) on 5 out of 9 tested image restoration tasks
- Achieves comparable performance to diffusion-based inverse models without requiring knowledge of corruption operators
- Requires only 2-10 function evaluations compared to 100+ for standard models while maintaining similar performance
- Shows better interpretability with generation processes that naturally progress from corrupted to clean images

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Score network (U-Net) parameterized by θ
  - Time-symmetric diffusion coefficient β(t)
  - Analytic posterior sampling q(Xt|X0,X1)
  - DDPM posterior sampling for generation
  - Loss function based on denoising score matching

- Critical path:
  1. Sample clean image X0 and degraded image X1 from paired data
  2. Sample intermediate time t and compute posterior Xt from q(Xt|X0,X1)
  3. Compute loss between predicted score and analytical score
  4. Backpropagate through score network
  5. During generation, start from degraded image and iteratively apply DDPM

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Linear vs. nonlinear diffusion: I²SB uses nonlinear diffusion which is more flexible but requires tractable reformulation
  - Stochasticity: Full stochastic sampling vs. deterministic OT-ODE - stochasticity helps with uncertainty but is less efficient
  - Training proposal: Using q(Xt|X0,X1) vs. the distribution induced by the linear SDE - the former better covers traversed regions

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor FID or classifier accuracy indicates the model isn't learning the correct mapping
  - Large performance drops as NFE decreases suggests inefficient generation
  - OT-ODE failing on high-uncertainty tasks indicates stochasticity is necessary

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train I²SB on a simple image restoration task (e.g., Gaussian denoising) and compare NFE efficiency vs. standard CSGM
  2. Test the effect of different sampling proposals during training by mixing q(Xt|X0,X1) with the linear SDE distribution
  3. Compare stochastic I²SB vs. deterministic OT-ODE on a task with high uncertainty (e.g., JPEG restoration with low quality factor)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can I²SB be extended to handle unpaired image-to-image translation tasks where clean and degraded image pairs are not available during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper assumes pair information is available during training (p(X0,X1) = pA(X0)pB(X1|X0)) and constructs tractable SBs between individual data points X0 and their corresponding degraded distributions pB(X1|X0).
- Why unresolved: The current I²SB framework relies on having paired clean and degraded images for training. In real-world scenarios, paired data might not always be available, limiting the applicability of I²SB.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results demonstrating successful unpaired image-to-image translation using I²SB with techniques like cycle consistency or adversarial training to handle unpaired data.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different noise schedulers (βt) on the performance of I²SB, and how can they be optimized for specific image restoration tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a time-symmetric scheduling of βt where the diffusion shrinks at both boundaries, but mentions that other advanced parameterizations are also applicable.
- Why unresolved: While the paper uses a specific noise scheduler, it does not explore the impact of different schedulers on the performance of I²SB. Optimizing the noise scheduler for specific tasks could potentially improve results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing the performance of I²SB with various noise schedulers (e.g., linear, cosine, or learned schedulers) on different image restoration tasks, identifying the optimal scheduler for each task.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does I²SB perform on high-resolution image restoration tasks (e.g., 512x512 or 1024x1024) compared to lower resolutions (e.g., 256x256)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper validates I²SB on ImageNet 256x256 resolution, but does not explore its performance on higher resolutions.
- Why unresolved: The computational complexity of I²SB may increase significantly with higher resolution images, potentially affecting its performance and efficiency. It is unclear how well I²SB scales to high-resolution tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance and computational efficiency of I²SB on different image resolutions, including high-resolution tasks, to determine its scalability and identify any limitations.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness depends on structural informativeness of degraded-clean image pairs, which may break down for severe degradations
- Theoretical reformulation assumes specific conditions that may not hold in practice for complex image restoration tasks
- Computational efficiency gains need validation across diverse degradation types beyond those tested
