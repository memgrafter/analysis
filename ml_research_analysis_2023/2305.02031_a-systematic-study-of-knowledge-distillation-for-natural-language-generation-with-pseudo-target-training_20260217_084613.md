# A Systematic Study of Knowledge Distillation for Natural Language Generation with Pseudo-Target Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02031
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02031
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Closes 75%+ of student-teacher performance gap while reducing latency by 3.7x and improving throughput by up to 10x

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a systematic study of knowledge distillation for compressing large-scale natural language generation models. The authors extend sequence-level knowledge distillation using pseudo-targets generated by the teacher model and propose Joint-Teaching, which applies word-level distillation to pseudo-targets from both teacher and student. Experiments across four NLG tasks demonstrate that the proposed method significantly closes the performance gap between student and teacher models while achieving substantial computational efficiency gains.

## Method Summary
The method involves generating pseudo-targets (PTs) using a teacher model for both labeled and unlabeled data, then training a smaller student model using these PTs with word-level knowledge distillation. The Joint-Teaching approach specifically uses PTs generated by both the teacher and the student, with 50% probability each. The training pipeline includes generating PTs via beam search and sampling, applying various distillation techniques (logits, noisy, attention-relations), and fine-tuning on ground truth labels. The approach is evaluated across summarization, question generation, abductive reasoning, and style transfer tasks.

## Key Results
- Closes 75%+ of the student-teacher performance gap across all tasks
- Reduces model latency by 3.7x compared to teacher models
- Improves throughput by up to 10x while maintaining performance
- Joint-Teaching with word-level KD on teacher and student PTs shows consistent improvements
- Sampling multiple PTs instead of beam search improves performance by exposing student to diverse valid outputs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint-Teaching closes the student-teacher performance gap by applying word-level KD to pseudo-targets generated by both the teacher and the student.
- Mechanism: The student is trained on pseudo-targets generated by the teacher and its own PTs, with 50% probability each. This approach implicitly addresses exposure bias by exposing the student to diverse conditional distributions and explicitly teaches it to correct its own mistakes using the teacher as an oracle.
- Core assumption: The teacher models P(yi|x, ŷS<i) better than the student at all stages of training, making the teacher's continuation of student-generated sequences more reliable than the student's own continuation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose the Joint-Teaching method, which applies word-level KD to PTs generated by both the teacher and the student."
  - [section]: "By applying word-level KD on PTs generated by both the teacher and the student, the teacher can teach the student how to continue its generated sequence correctly and prevent a cascading effect."
- Break condition: If the teacher cannot model P(yi|x, ŷS<i) better than the student (e.g., if the student becomes as capable as the teacher), the reliability of the teacher's corrections diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Sampling multiple pseudo-targets rather than using beam search improves distillation by exposing the student to a larger portion of the learnable distribution.
- Mechanism: Instead of generating a single mode approximation PT via beam search, the teacher generates multiple PTs through sampling with controlled temperature. This exposes the student to diverse valid outputs, covering more of the conditional distribution factors P(yi|x, ŷT<i).
- Core assumption: NLG is a structured prediction problem where multiple candidates can form correct solutions, not just the single most likely sequence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose extensions of the common practice of generating a single mode approximation PT via beam search, instead we suggest sampling multiple PTs to facilitate higher exposure to conditional distribution factors."
  - [section]: "Unlike classification, NLG is a structured prediction problem and multiple candidates can form a correct solution."
- Break condition: If the diversity of sampled PTs becomes too high (very high temperature), the quality of individual PTs may degrade, making them poor training signals.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Utilizing unlabeled data through pseudo-target generation significantly improves student generalization by exposing it to more conditional distribution factors.
- Mechanism: The teacher generates PTs for abundant unlabeled examples, allowing the student to learn from P(yi|x, ŷT<i) factors that weren't present in the labeled data distribution. This exposure helps the student generalize beyond the labeled data distribution.
- Core assumption: Unlabeled data is abundant in realistic industrial setups and represents additional valid input distributions that the student should learn to handle.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Typically, in real-world applications, in addition to labeled data there is abundant unlabeled data (Oliver et al., 2018; Calderon et al., 2022) that may contribute to KD."
  - [section]: "Since in autoregressive NLG the LM learns to condition on the targets (y<i), PTs are essential for utilizing unlabeled data (inputs without corresponding targets)."
- Break condition: If the unlabeled data distribution significantly differs from the labeled data distribution, the PTs may teach the student patterns that don't generalize to the target task.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher model (T5-L/BART-L) -> Generate pseudo-targets -> Student model (T5-S/T5-base) with word-level KD -> Fine-tune on ground truth
- Critical path: 1) Generate pseudo-targets for labeled and unlabeled data using the teacher 2) Train student with word-level KD on both teacher and student-generated PTs 3) Fine-tune student on ground truth labels
- Design tradeoffs: Using encoder-decoder vs decoder-only architectures (ED performs better for conditional NLG), pruning decoder vs encoder layers (decoder pruning better for latency), single vs multiple PTs (multiple improves performance but increases computation), teacher vs student PTs (Joint-Teaching combines both benefits)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance gap closure (student doesn't learn effectively), high latency despite pruning (decoder pruning not applied), overfitting to PTs (student doesn't generalize to ground truth), exposure bias still present (student makes cascading errors during generation)
- First 3 experiments:

  2. Test single PT vs multiple PTs on SQuAD17 to validate PT diversity impact
  3. Implement Joint-Teaching on ART10 to verify the combined teacher-student PT approach

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of knowledge distillation vary across different NLG tasks with varying output lengths and complexity?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that their results are applicable to English-to-English NLG tasks with relatively short output lengths compared to inputs, but suggest the results may differ for tasks with much longer outputs or non-English tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on four specific NLG tasks with relatively short outputs, and does not explore tasks with longer outputs or different language pairs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments on NLG tasks with longer outputs (e.g., long-form generation) or different language pairs would provide evidence for how KD effectiveness varies across task types.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the computational cost of the knowledge distillation training process compare to the accumulated inference cost savings from deploying a compressed model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that generating pseudo-targets with the teacher for all training examples and unlabeled data is computationally expensive, and that Joint-Teaching can also be costly as the student generates PTs during training.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumes one-time training costs are negligible compared to inference costs, but does not quantify the actual training costs versus inference savings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed cost analysis comparing training time, computational resources, and monetary costs for KD versus inference cost savings from deploying compressed models would provide evidence.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive are the knowledge distillation results to the choice of teacher model and its hyperparameters?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses off-the-shelf models as teachers but does not explore how different teacher model choices or hyperparameter settings affect distillation effectiveness.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumes the teacher is a strong oracle but does not investigate how teacher model quality, size, or training hyperparameters impact student performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying teacher model types, sizes, and training hyperparameters while measuring student performance would provide evidence for sensitivity to teacher choices.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation on larger datasets that better represent industrial-scale scenarios
- Weak theoretical grounding for the Joint-Teaching mechanism and benefits of sampling multiple PTs
- Missing key implementation details including exact hyperparameters for each KD method

## Confidence

- **Claim cluster 1** (KD baselines improve over fine-tuning): High confidence - standard techniques with well-established results
- **Claim cluster 2** (PT augmentation with unlabeled data improves performance): Medium confidence - supported by experiments but limited by dataset scale
- **Claim cluster 3** (Joint-Teaching mechanism and sampling multiple PTs): Low confidence - novel claims with limited theoretical grounding and no related work found in corpus signals
