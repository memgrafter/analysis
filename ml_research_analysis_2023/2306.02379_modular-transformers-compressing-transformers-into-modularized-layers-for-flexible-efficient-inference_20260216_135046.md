# Modular Transformers: Compressing Transformers into Modularized Layers for Flexible Efficient Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.02379
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02379
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: Modular Transformers achieves flexible compression ratios from 1.1× to 6× with minimal performance loss, outperforming prior seq2seq compression techniques.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of compressing large pre-trained Transformer models like T5 and BART into smaller, faster models that retain strong performance while being able to dynamically adjust compression ratios to meet varying resource constraints. The authors propose Modular Transformers, a modularized encoder-decoder framework that trains modularized layers to replace sub-networks of different sizes in the original model via multi-grained module replacing and knowledge distillation. After training, these modularized layers can be flexibly assembled into sequence-to-sequence models with different performance-efficiency trade-offs. The method is evaluated on text summarization, machine translation, and question generation tasks, showing consistent improvements over prior seq2seq compression techniques.

## Method Summary
Modular Transformers trains modularized layers that can replace sub-networks of varying sizes in the original model. The approach uses multi-grained module replacing with random hybrid model assembly during training, combined with a curriculum replacing strategy that progressively increases replacement complexity. Attention and representation distillation is incorporated to better align modularized layers with their corresponding sub-networks. After training, modularized layers can be assembled using a deterministic strategy (decoder-first, top-down, fine-to-coarse) to create models with different compression ratios and performance-efficiency trade-offs.

## Key Results
- Achieves flexible compression ratios from 1.1× to 6× with little to moderate relative performance drop
- On CNN/DailyMail summarization, achieves 41.75 ROUGE-L score at 1.9× speed-up ratio, outperforming previous best by 0.23 points
- Consistently outperforms prior seq2seq compression techniques across text summarization, machine translation, and question generation tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Modularized layers trained via multi-grained module replacing can replace sub-networks of varying sizes while maintaining performance.
- Mechanism: Random assembly of hybrid models during training where original sub-networks are replaced with modularized layers of different granularity allows learning to map functionality across different network sizes.
- Core assumption: Original model sub-networks can be compressed into smaller modules without losing essential information.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Modular Transformers train modularized layers that have the same function of two or more consecutive layers in the original model via module replacing and knowledge distillation."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Curriculum replacing strategy improves training efficiency and final performance.
- Mechanism: Progressive increase in replacing probability from fine-to-coarse granularity allows easier training of smaller modules first before tackling larger replacements.
- Core assumption: Modularized layers with larger granularity are more difficult to train than those with smaller granularity.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.3] "motivated by the fact that modularized layers of larger granularity are more difficult to train, we propose to train the modularized layers of different granularity in a coarse-to-fine fashion."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Attention and representation distillation complements module replacing for better alignment.
- Mechanism: Distillation loss forces modularized layers to match both the attention distributions and hidden representations of the original sub-networks they replace.
- Core assumption: The attention patterns and intermediate representations contain crucial information for preserving functionality.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.3] "we also propose to leverage attention and hidden representation distillation (Sun et al., 2019; Jiao et al., 2020) to better align modularized layers and the corresponding sub-networks."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Original model T with n layers -> Modularized layers M with varying granularity {2, 3, 4, 6} -> Hybrid model H assembled from original and modularized layers -> Curriculum probability vector p controlling granularity sampling

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Define modularized layers corresponding to sub-networks of different sizes
  2. Train using multi-grained module replacing with random hybrid model assembly
  3. Apply curriculum replacing to gradually increase complexity
  4. Incorporate attention and representation distillation
  5. Assemble trained layers using deterministic strategy for deployment

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Granularity selection: More granularities provide finer control but increase training complexity
  - Replacement probability: Higher probability speeds training but may reduce stability
  - Distillation weight: Balancing module replacing loss vs distillation loss affects convergence

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Performance degradation with high compression ratios indicates insufficient model capacity
  - Training instability suggests improper curriculum scheduling or probability settings
  - Poor assembly results indicate inadequate training diversity in hybrid model structures

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train with only granularity 2 modularized layers and basic module replacing (no curriculum or distillation) to establish baseline
  2. Add curriculum replacing strategy while keeping other settings constant to measure improvement
  3. Incorporate attention and representation distillation to quantify complementary benefits

## Limitations

- Scalability concerns for truly massive models (e.g., T5-11B) where training modularized layers with multiple granularities may become computationally prohibitive
- Effectiveness on transformer variants beyond T5 and BART remains untested, limiting generalizability claims
- Optimal granularity selection is not analyzed, with {2, 3, 4, 6} used without justification or ablation study

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core modularization concept and training methodology are well-established and clearly explained
- **Medium confidence**: Performance improvements over baselines are demonstrated, but absolute gains vary significantly across tasks and compression ratios
- **Low confidence**: The claim about alleviating the need to retrain compact models is not directly validated
