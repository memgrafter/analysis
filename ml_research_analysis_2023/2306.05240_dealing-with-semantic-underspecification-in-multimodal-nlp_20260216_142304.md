# Dealing with Semantic Underspecification in Multimodal NLP

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05240
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05240
- Reference count: 28
- Key outcome: CLIP scores underspecified descriptions significantly lower than fully specified ones, even when both are valid for an image

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how multimodal NLP models handle semantic underspecification, where linguistic signals lack full information needed for understanding. The authors find that state-of-the-art models like CLIP struggle with semantically underspecified language, performing poorly on descriptions that omit details typically filled in by humans using visual context. Experiments show CLIP scores underspecified descriptions significantly lower than fully specified ones, even when both are valid for an image. The model also prefers unrelated but detailed descriptions over valid underspecified ones. The authors argue this limitation affects applications like image captioning, visual question answering, and goal-oriented dialogue, and propose several research directions to improve models' handling of semantic underspecification.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate CLIP's handling of semantic underspecification by testing it on systematically perturbed versions of COCO image descriptions. They manually create underspecified variants by applying perturbations to original descriptions (quantity, gender, location, object modifications). CLIPScore similarity scores are computed for each original and perturbed description-image pair to analyze how underspecification affects model performance. The evaluation uses 100 images with 495 original COCO descriptions.

## Key Results
- CLIP scores underspecified descriptions significantly lower than fully specified ones, even when both are semantically valid for the image
- The model prefers unrelated but detailed descriptions over valid underspecified ones
- This limitation affects downstream applications like image captioning, VQA, and goal-oriented dialogue

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multimodal models struggle with semantic underspecification because they conflate alignment with semantic validity
- Mechanism: CLIP and similar models use contrastive learning objectives that optimize for visual-text alignment based on matching visual features to linguistic tokens. When descriptions contain underspecified elements (pronouns, quantifiers, locatives), the model interprets the reduced lexical specificity as reduced alignment, even when the description remains semantically valid for the image.
- Core assumption: The model's training data and objective treat lexical specificity as a proxy for semantic validity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "CLIP scores underspecified descriptions significantly lower than fully specified ones, even when both are valid for an image"
  - [section] "the model could be 'dazzled' by the presence of words that have a grounded referent in the image... Conversely, the absence of words, and particularly nouns, with a clear grounded referent in the FULL description would be considered by the model as an indicator of misalignment"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on syntactic/semantic prompts but don't directly address underspecification in multimodal contexts

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Underspecification phenomena violate the implicit assumptions of standard multimodal training datasets
- Mechanism: Standard image description datasets like COCO are designed to provide complete, unambiguous descriptions for accessibility. This creates a training distribution where semantically underspecified language is rare or absent, leading models to treat such language as anomalous or invalid.
- Core assumption: The training distribution reflects standard captioning practices rather than natural communicative scenarios
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "standard image descriptions as those in COCO... are semantically overspecified... These descriptions fully specify a wide range of semantic aspects that would be reasonably left underspecified if the visual context was available to both interlocutors"
  - [section] "the contrastive learning objective being employed may be too aggressive with texts that do not conform to those typically seen in training"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - related papers don't address dataset biases in multimodal training

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multimodal models lack explicit mechanisms for resolving underspecification through pragmatic reasoning
- Mechanism: While multimodal models have access to visual context, they don't implement the pragmatic reasoning processes humans use to resolve underspecified references. They treat all tokens as having fixed visual referents rather than inferring referents from context, shared knowledge, or conversational goals.
- Core assumption: Pragmatic reasoning requires explicit modeling of communicative goals and contextual inference
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "human speakers can quickly and effortlessly integrate semantically-underspecified linguistic signals with a wide range of non-linguistic information"
  - [section] "models must be able to understand and generate sentences like They put the flowers there, provided that a relevant visual context is present and there is a clear communicative goal"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - related papers focus on syntax/semantics but not pragmatic reasoning in multimodal contexts

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image Encoder (ViT) → Image Embedding ← Contrastive Loss → Text Embedding ← Text Encoder (Transformer) ← Text
- Critical path: Text → Text Encoder → Text Embedding → Contrastive Loss ← Image Embedding ← Image Encoder ← Image
- Design tradeoffs: The model prioritizes alignment accuracy over semantic validity understanding, trading off robustness to underspecification for precision on standard captioning tasks. This creates excellent performance on FOIL-style tests but poor handling of naturally underspecified language.
- Failure signatures: Low alignment scores for valid underspecified descriptions, preference for unrelated but detailed descriptions over valid underspecified ones, inability to distinguish between semantic validity and lexical specificity.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test CLIPScore on systematically underspecified versions of COCO descriptions (quantity, gender, location, object perturbations) to quantify the sensitivity to each phenomenon
  2. Compare CLIP performance on underspecified descriptions against a baseline that ignores word-level alignment and focuses only on semantic validity
  3. Evaluate whether adding explicit underspecification indicators (e.g., special tokens) to descriptions improves model robustness to semantically underspecified language

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we design computational models that explicitly account for semantic underspecification when evaluating image descriptions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors demonstrate that CLIP assigns lower scores to underspecified descriptions even when they are semantically valid, suggesting current models conflate specification level with validity.
- Why unresolved: Existing models lack explicit mechanisms to distinguish between valid underspecified descriptions and invalid ones, leading to systematic biases against underspecified language.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing models that maintain consistent scores across semantically valid descriptions regardless of specification level, or showing improved performance on underspecified language tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What training objectives or data collection methods would better prepare multimodal models for real-world communicative scenarios involving underspecified language?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest that standard image captioning datasets and training objectives may not adequately represent real communicative contexts where underspecification is common.
- Why unresolved: Current training data and objectives are optimized for complete, detailed descriptions rather than the pragmatic and context-dependent uses of language seen in actual communication.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing improved model performance on underspecified language tasks after training with alternative objectives or data collection methods that better capture communicative goals.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we quantify and operationalize semantic underspecification in a way that is both theoretically sound and computationally tractable?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors call for formal definitions and operationalizations of semantic underspecification that can be used for computational modeling.
- Why unresolved: While linguists have studied underspecification theoretically, there is no consensus on how to measure or quantify it in a way that can be directly applied to model development and evaluation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and validating metrics or annotation schemes that accurately capture different types of underspecification and correlate with human judgments about language use in visual contexts.

## Limitations

- The experimental evaluation uses a relatively small dataset (100 images with 495 descriptions) and manual perturbation methods that may not fully capture the range of semantic underspecification phenomena
- The study focuses primarily on one model (CLIP) without comparing performance across different multimodal architectures or training objectives
- The proposed research directions remain largely speculative without concrete implementation proposals or preliminary results

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The empirical finding that CLIP scores underspecified descriptions lower than fully specified ones, even when both are semantically valid for the image. This is directly supported by the experimental results and represents a clear, reproducible observation about model behavior.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that this limitation affects downstream applications like image captioning, VQA, and goal-oriented dialogue. While logically sound, this connection relies on extrapolation from the core finding and assumes these applications would benefit from handling underspecification.

**Low Confidence**: The proposed mechanisms for why models struggle with underspecification (particularly Mechanism 1 about alignment-conflation) and the specific research directions suggested. These are reasonable hypotheses but lack direct experimental validation in this paper.

## Next Checks

1. **Expand empirical validation**: Test the underspecification sensitivity across multiple multimodal models (including non-contrastive approaches) and on larger, more diverse datasets to determine if this is a CLIP-specific limitation or a broader multimodal modeling challenge.

2. **Implement baseline comparisons**: Develop and evaluate baseline models that explicitly distinguish between lexical specificity and semantic validity, such as models that score semantic validity independently of alignment or use special tokens to mark underspecified elements.

3. **Analyze type-specific effects**: Conduct fine-grained analysis of how different types of underspecification (pronouns, quantifiers, locatives, etc.) affect model performance differently, and whether some phenomena are more problematic than others for current multimodal architectures.
