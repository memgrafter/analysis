# An Empirical Study of Leveraging Knowledge Distillation for Compressing Multilingual Neural Machine Translation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09388
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09388
- Reference count: 16
- 4-5× smaller models with 3.5 BLEU degradation compared to teacher model

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates knowledge distillation (KD) techniques for compressing large multilingual neural machine translation (MNMT) models, using Indic-to-English translation as a case study. The authors explore various KD approaches including sequence-level distillation, language-aware distillation, and architectural variations, training models ranging from 4.6M to 95.4M parameters from a 474M parameter teacher model. Experiments show that while KD can achieve significant parameter reduction, it comes at the cost of substantial performance degradation of up to 3.5 BLEU points compared to the teacher model.

## Method Summary
The study employs a teacher-student framework where a large IndicTrans model (474M parameters) generates distilled data via beam search, which is then used to train smaller student models. The primary KD approach is Sequence-Level Distillation, where the teacher's best beam search output becomes the pseudo-target for student training. The authors experiment with different student model configurations (varying depth and width), multi-stage training with high-quality data filtering using LaBSE similarity scores, and adapters for parameter-efficient adaptation. All models use Transformer architecture with various hyperparameter settings, and evaluation is performed using BLEU and Chrf++ metrics on Flores101 dataset.

## Key Results
- KD yields models that are 4-5× smaller but suffer performance drops of up to 3.5 BLEU compared to the teacher model
- Deeper but narrower models perform comparably to shallower but wider ones in terms of BLEU score
- Fine-tuning on high-quality data slightly improves translation quality (2-6 Chrf++ points)
- Language-aware distillation methods show minimal improvements over standard sequence-level distillation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Language-agnostic knowledge distillation transfers useful generalization from a large MNMT model to a smaller one by providing distilled hard pseudo-labels.
- Mechanism: The teacher MNMT model generates a high-quality translation (best beam search) for each source sentence. These outputs become pseudo-targets for training the smaller student model, allowing it to learn from the teacher's output distribution without direct access to the teacher's internal representations.
- Core assumption: The teacher's best beam search output approximates the optimal translation distribution that the student can learn from.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "commonly used language-agnostic and language-aware KD approaches yield models that are 4-5× smaller but also suffer from performance drops of up to 3.5 BLEU"
  - [section]: "Sequence-Level Distillation (SLD): (Kim and Rush, 2016) argued that the student model should capture the Sequence-Level distribution of the teacher model rather than the individual word-level distribution at each timestep."
  - [corpus]: Weak - related works focus on non-autoregressive MNMT and robustness but not specifically on sequence-level distillation effectiveness.
- Break condition: If the teacher model's beam search outputs are significantly worse than human reference translations, the student will learn suboptimal patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-stage training with High-Quality data fine-tuning improves distilled model performance by allowing the model to re-learn from cleaner examples after initial noisy training.
- Mechanism: First, the student model is trained on the full distilled dataset (which may contain noisy translations). Then, it's fine-tuned on a filtered subset where translation pairs have high LaBSE similarity scores, effectively "unlearning" noise and reinforcing better patterns.
- Core assumption: The distilled data contains noise that degrades performance, and filtering based on translation quality metrics can identify and remove this noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "fine-tuning a distilled model on a High-Quality subset slightly boosts translation quality"
  - [section]: "To address this issue, we experimented with a multi-stage training setup. First, we trained a smaller model on the complete dataset, and then we fine-tuned it on the High-Quality data filtered from the complete dataset."
  - [corpus]: Weak - related works don't specifically address high-quality data filtering in distillation contexts.
- Break condition: If the filtering threshold is too aggressive, valuable training data may be discarded, limiting the model's ability to learn diverse patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Deeper but narrower Transformer architectures can achieve comparable performance to shallower but wider ones while having fewer parameters, making them more suitable for compression.
- Mechanism: Increasing the number of layers (depth) while reducing model dimension and feedforward size allows the model to maintain representational capacity with fewer total parameters. Each layer can specialize in different aspects of the translation task.
- Core assumption: The representational power of a deep, narrow network is comparable to a shallow, wide network for translation tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "deeper compact models tend to be as good as shallower non-compact ones"
  - [section]: "We observe that deeper compact models tend to be as good as shallower non-compact ones, and that fine-tuning a distilled model on a High-Quality subset slightly boosts translation quality."
  - [corpus]: Weak - related works don't directly compare depth vs width trade-offs in MNMT distillation.
- Break condition: If the model becomes too deep, vanishing gradients or optimization difficulties may prevent effective training.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher model (474M parameters) -> Beam search generation -> Distilled data -> Student models (various configurations) -> Training pipeline -> Evaluation (BLEU, Chrf++)

- Critical path: 1. Generate distilled data using teacher model's beam search, 2. Train student model on distilled data using sequence-level distillation, 3. (Optional) Fine-tune on high-quality filtered subset, 4. Evaluate using BLEU and Chrf++ metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Depth vs width: Deeper models have fewer parameters but longer inference times
  - Data quality vs quantity: High-quality filtered data improves performance but reduces training data volume
  - Parameter sharing: Recurrent stacking reduces parameters but may limit model capacity

- Failure signatures:
  - Large BLEU gap between teacher and student indicates distillation inefficacy
  - Poor performance on low-resource languages suggests insufficient adaptation
  - Long inference times with deeper models indicate practical deployment issues

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train base model with sequence-level distillation on full distilled dataset
  2. Train deeper base model (e.g., base12L) with same distillation approach
  3. Apply multi-stage training: train on full data, then fine-tune on high-quality subset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the performance gap between teacher and distilled MNMT models change significantly when using different data filtering criteria for the High-Quality subset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses using High-Quality data filtering based on LaBSE cosine similarity scores to improve distillation performance, showing some improvements with a specific filtering threshold (μL+kσL).
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one specific filtering approach and threshold. Different filtering criteria or thresholds might yield different results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing distillation performance across various data filtering criteria (e.g., different similarity thresholds, filtering based on other metrics like TER or BERTScore) and their impact on BLEU scores.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the effectiveness of language-aware distillation techniques (like Global-Language-wise Distillation) vary with the proportion of low-resource versus high-resource languages in the training data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes Global-Language-wise Distillation to address bias toward high-resource languages in multilingual settings, but finds only minor improvements compared to standard sequence-level distillation.
- Why unresolved: The paper tests this approach on a specific mix of 11 Indic languages with varying resource levels but doesn't systematically vary the resource distribution to understand when language-aware techniques are most beneficial.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the proportion of low-resource to high-resource languages in the training data while measuring the relative performance of language-agnostic versus language-aware distillation methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would combining adapters with High-Quality fine-tuning data yield better performance than either approach alone?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that High-Quality fine-tuning improves performance, but adapters alone showed minimal improvement, possibly due to insufficient parameterization.
- Why unresolved: The paper tests adapters on unfiltered distilled data but doesn't investigate whether adapters trained on the High-Quality subset would be more effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing three approaches: (1) base model with High-Quality fine-tuning, (2) base model with adapters on unfiltered data, and (3) base model with adapters on High-Quality data, measuring the performance gains of each.

## Limitations
- Findings are specific to Indic-to-English translation and may not generalize to other language pairs
- Limited ablation studies on distillation hyperparameters make optimization difficult
- Quality filtering relies on LaBSE similarity scores which may not perfectly correlate with translation quality
- No comparison with other compression techniques to contextualize KD's effectiveness

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The observation that KD yields 4-5× smaller models with 3.5 BLEU degradation is well-supported by experimental results
- **Medium Confidence**: The finding that deeper but narrower models perform comparably to shallower ones is supported by experiments but lacks extensive ablation studies
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that fine-tuning on high-quality data improves performance is supported by limited experiments and needs more systematic evaluation
