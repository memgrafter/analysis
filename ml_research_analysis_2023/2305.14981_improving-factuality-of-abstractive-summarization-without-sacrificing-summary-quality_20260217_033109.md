# Improving Factuality of Abstractive Summarization without Sacrificing Summary Quality

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.14981
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14981
- **Reference count**: 18
- **Primary result**: EFACTSUM improves factuality metrics (up to 6 points on FactCC for XSUM, 11 points for CNN/DM) without negatively affecting summary quality metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces EFACTSUM, a contrastive learning approach for improving factual consistency in abstractive summarization. The method generates diverse candidate summaries using beam search and factually improved models, then ranks them using FactCC for factuality and ROUGE for summary quality. By training with a contrastive loss that prefers factual summaries, the approach achieves significant improvements in factuality metrics without sacrificing summary quality or abstractiveness. Experiments on XSUM and CNN/DM datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of this balanced selection process in addressing the trade-off between factuality and summary quality.

## Method Summary
EFACTSUM generates candidate summaries using diverse beam search with both base and factually improved models, then ranks them using a dual scoring technique (FactCC + ROUGE). The contrastive learning framework trains the model to prefer more factual summaries while maintaining summary quality. The approach involves generating multiple candidate summaries per document, scoring them for factuality and quality, selecting a balanced set of factual and non-factual summaries, and fine-tuning the model using combined cross-entropy and contrastive loss.

## Key Results
- EFACTSUM achieves up to 6 points of absolute improvement over baseline models on FactCC for XSUM dataset
- EFACTSUM achieves up to 11 points of absolute improvement over baseline models on FactCC for CNN/DM dataset
- The method maintains or improves summary quality metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore) and abstractiveness (MINT) while improving factuality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The contrastive loss encourages the model to assign higher probability to more factual summaries during training.
- Mechanism: For each document, the model generates multiple candidate summaries using beam search and factually improved models. The contrastive loss (Equation 1) ranks these summaries based on their factuality scores and pushes the model to prefer factual ones.
- Core assumption: The factuality scores used (FactCC) are reliable indicators of summary quality and correlate well with human judgments.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Models trained using our approach show up to 6 points of absolute improvement over the base model with respect to FactCC on XSUM and 11 points on CNN/DM..."
  - [section]: "In order to measure the factuality of Si, we choose FactCC (Kryscinski et al., 2020) because it correlates well with human judgments of faithfulness (Pagnoni et al., 2021) and it is also computationally more efficient than other question-answering based metrics (Scialom et al., 2021)."

- Break condition: If FactCC scores do not correlate well with human judgments of factuality, the contrastive loss would not effectively improve summary factuality.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The dual scoring technique (FactCC + ROUGE) prevents optimizing factuality at the expense of summary quality.
- Mechanism: The approach selects top factual and non-factual summaries using FactCC scores, then further refines this selection using ROUGE scores to maintain summary quality.
- Core assumption: ROUGE scores are good indicators of summary quality and can effectively balance factuality improvements.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Models trained using our approach show up to 6 points of absolute improvement over the base model with respect to FactCC on XSUM and 11 points on CNN/DM, without negatively affecting either similarity-based metrics or absractiveness."
  - [section]: "To measure the summary quality, we use the popular ROUGE metric (Lin, 2004). Now, amongst the set of candidate summaries that have been scored to be faithful, we further choose the top m summaries that have the highest ROUGE score."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence in corpus about the effectiveness of combining FactCC and ROUGE.
- Break condition: If ROUGE scores do not correlate well with human judgments of summary quality, the dual scoring technique would not effectively balance factuality and summary quality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Generating diverse candidate summaries using both base and factually improved models provides better training signals.
- Mechanism: The approach generates summaries using beam search with the base model and factually improved models, creating a diverse set of candidates for ranking.
- Core assumption: Summaries generated by factually improved models are more likely to be factual than those generated by the base model alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "To generate the candidate summarization set {Si}, we make use of an existing model and sample summaries using beam search (Vijayakumar et al., 2018). - Break condition: If factually improved models do not generate more factual summaries than the base model, the diverse candidate generation would not provide better training signals.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Candidate summary generation -> Ranking strategy -> Contrastive loss -> Cross-entropy loss
- Critical path:
  1. Generate candidate summaries using diverse beam search.
  2. Rank candidates using FactCC and ROUGE scores.
  3. Train model using contrastive loss to prefer factual summaries.
  4. Fine-tune model using combined cross-entropy and contrastive loss.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Balancing factuality and summary quality through dual scoring technique.
  - Computational cost of generating and ranking multiple candidate summaries.
  - Reliance on factuality metrics (FactCC) that may not perfectly align with human judgments.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on factuality metrics (FactCC) despite training with contrastive loss.
  - Decrease in summary quality metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore) after training.
  - Overfitting to specific types of factual errors in the training data.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate factuality improvement on a small subset of XSUM using FactCC.
  2. Compare summary quality (ROUGE, BERTScore) before and after training.
  3. Analyze the distribution of abstractiveness scores (MINT) to ensure no trade-off with factuality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper doesn't explicitly call out open questions, but the methodology raises several important questions about scalability, domain adaptation, and metric optimization that remain unexplored in the current work.

## Limitations

- Heavy reliance on FactCC as the sole metric for measuring factuality, which may not perfectly align with human judgments of factual consistency.
- Computational overhead from generating and ranking multiple candidate summaries, making the approach 3X slower than vanilla cross-entropy training.
- Limited evaluation to news summarization datasets (XSUM and CNN/DM), with unknown performance on other domains like scientific or legal documents.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The observation that EFACTSUM improves factuality metrics (FactCC, DAE) without sacrificing summary quality (ROUGE, BERTScore) is well-supported by experimental results on two datasets.
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism of using contrastive learning to prefer factual summaries is theoretically sound but relies heavily on the quality of the factuality metric.
- **Medium Confidence**: The dual scoring technique using FactCC and ROUGE effectively balances factuality and summary quality, though the exact weighting and interaction between these metrics is not fully explored.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct human evaluation studies to verify that improvements in FactCC scores correspond to actual improvements in factual consistency as judged by humans.

3. Investigate the sensitivity of the method to different values of the hyperparameters k (number of factual summaries) and m (number of final candidates) to determine optimal settings for various use cases.
