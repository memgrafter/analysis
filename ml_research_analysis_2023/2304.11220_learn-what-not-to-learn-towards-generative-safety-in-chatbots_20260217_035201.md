# Learn What NOT to Learn: Towards Generative Safety in Chatbots

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11220
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11220
- Reference count: 5
- Key outcome: LOT achieves 4x reduction in toxicity while improving fluency 4-6x over baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LOT (Learning What NOT to say), a novel contrastive learning framework for generative dialogue safety that addresses the critical challenge of reducing toxic responses in chatbots. Unlike previous approaches that focus on detecting and filtering unsafe responses after generation, LOT trains models to inherently avoid generating toxic content by learning from safe and toxic language distributions simultaneously. The framework uses Jensen-Shannon divergence to guide the model toward generating responses closer to safe distributions while maintaining distance from toxic ones, resulting in significant improvements in safety metrics without sacrificing fluency or engagement.

## Method Summary
LOT employs a contrastive learning approach where the model is trained using pre-learned safe and toxic language distributions. During training, the framework maximizes the likelihood of generating responses from the safe distribution while minimizing likelihood from the toxic distribution using Jensen-Shannon divergence as the contrastive objective. This dual-distribution approach allows the model to learn subtle distinctions between safe and unsafe content rather than simply memorizing blacklisted phrases. The method integrates seamlessly into the standard language model training pipeline without requiring specialized decoding strategies or additional computational overhead during inference.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 4x reduction in toxicity compared to baseline models
- Improves fluency metrics by 4-6x while maintaining safety gains
- Outperforms decoding-time methods that require additional inference-time computation
- Demonstrates higher engagingness and diversity scores than safety-focused baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism relies on contrastive learning between safe and toxic distributions rather than simple classification or filtering. By using Jensen-Shannon divergence, LOT creates a continuous optimization space where the model learns to navigate toward safe language patterns while avoiding toxic regions. This approach addresses the fundamental limitation of post-hoc filtering methods that can only remove obvious toxic content after generation, often at the cost of fluency and coherence.

## Foundational Learning
- Contrastive learning: Trains models to distinguish between similar and dissimilar examples - needed for learning nuanced differences between safe and toxic language; quick check: verify gradient flow in both positive and negative directions
- Jensen-Shannon divergence: Symmetric measure between probability distributions - needed for balanced optimization toward safe and away from toxic; quick check: confirm divergence values remain bounded and stable
- Distribution matching: Aligning generated distributions with target distributions - needed to ensure generated responses follow safe patterns; quick check: validate MAUVE scores improve consistently
- Language distribution modeling: Learning probability distributions over vocabulary - needed to represent safe/toxic patterns effectively; quick check: examine perplexity on held-out safe/toxic corpora
- Pre-training compatibility: Integration with existing model architectures - needed for practical deployment without full retraining; quick check: confirm convergence speed matches baseline training

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Pre-learned safe distribution <-[LOT contrastive objective]-> Model <-[LOT contrastive objective]-> Pre-learned toxic distribution

**Critical path:** Input text → Token embedding → LOT contrastive loss computation → Backpropagation → Parameter updates → Safer output distribution

**Design tradeoffs:** The framework trades off some diversity potential for safety gains, requiring careful balancing of the contrastive weight to avoid overly conservative responses that lack engagement

**Failure signatures:** Over-suppression of creative language when contrastive weight is too high; insufficient toxicity reduction when weight is too low; domain-specific failures when pre-learned distributions don't generalize

**First experiments:** 1) Measure toxicity reduction on standard benchmarks (RealToxicityPrompts) with varying contrastive weights; 2) Compare fluency (MAUVE, perplexity) against baseline safety methods; 3) Test cross-domain generalization by evaluating on out-of-domain toxic prompts

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on automated metrics rather than human judgment for toxicity detection
- Performance depends on quality and generalizability of pre-learned safe/toxic distributions
- Does not address potential biases in source distributions or edge cases where toxic and safe content distributions overlap
- Limited testing across different languages and cultural contexts

## Confidence
High: Claims about training efficiency and quantitative performance improvements (4x toxicity reduction, 4-6x fluency)
Medium: Claims about engagingness and diversity improvements based on automated metrics
Low: Claims about complete prevention of unsafe content without acknowledging false negatives or distribution dependency
