# Exploring the Viability of Synthetic Query Generation for Relevance Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11944
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11944
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Vanilla QGen underperforms transfer learning on e-commerce relevance prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates synthetic query generation (QGen) for fine-grained relevance prediction in e-commerce search. While prior QGen research focused on binary relevance or question-answering tasks, this work adapts it to multi-level relevance prediction by using label-conditioned variants that prepend relevance labels to document context. The authors conduct experiments on WANDS and HomeDepot datasets, finding that standard QGen approaches underperform zero-shot transfer learning from established datasets like MS-MARCO or ESCI. Although label-conditioned QGen shows improvement over vanilla QGen, it still cannot match transfer learning performance. A critical finding is that QGen models frequently generate identical queries across different relevance labels, indicating poor sensitivity to label distinctions. The work concludes that while QGen holds promise for e-commerce relevance tasks, significant refinement is needed to achieve parity with conventional transfer learning approaches.

## Method Summary
The paper explores synthetic query generation for multi-level relevance prediction by adapting QGen to prepend relevance labels to document context before query generation. The authors test both vanilla QGen and label-conditioned variants on e-commerce datasets WANDS and HomeDepot. They compare these approaches against zero-shot transfer learning baselines using MS-MARCO and ESCI datasets. The experiments evaluate the quality of generated queries for downstream relevance prediction tasks, measuring how well the synthetic queries capture the nuances of different relevance levels. The methodology focuses on assessing whether conditioning on relevance labels improves the model's ability to generate queries that reflect different degrees of document relevance.

## Key Results
- Vanilla QGen underperforms zero-shot transfer learning from MS-MARCO and ESCI on both WANDS and HomeDepot datasets
- Label-conditioned QGen improves over vanilla QGen but still falls short of transfer learning performance
- QGen models frequently generate identical queries across different relevance labels, demonstrating poor label sensitivity
- The models struggle to capture full nuance of the relevance space, limiting their effectiveness for fine-grained relevance prediction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that while QGen can generate plausible queries, the models struggle to capture the subtle distinctions between different relevance levels. The mechanism appears to be that standard QGen models lack the conditioning sophistication needed to generate queries that reflect fine-grained relevance distinctions. Even when relevance labels are prepended to the document context, the models often fail to produce meaningfully different queries across different relevance levels. This suggests that the models may be overly reliant on document content itself rather than effectively integrating the relevance label information. The poor label sensitivity indicates that the conditioning mechanism is insufficient for the nuanced task of multi-level relevance prediction, where small differences in relevance need to be captured in the generated queries.

## Limitations

- Limited scope to only two e-commerce datasets (WANDS and HomeDepot), raising questions about generalizability
- Does not explore whether different QGen architectures or fine-tuning approaches could overcome label sensitivity issues
- Focuses on relevance prediction as a proxy task, leaving open questions about QGen's effectiveness for other downstream tasks in e-commerce search

## Confidence

- High confidence in finding that vanilla QGen underperforms transfer learning baselines on WANDS and HomeDepot
- Medium confidence in label-conditioning improvements due to potential dataset-specific effects
- High confidence in observation about identical query generation across relevance labels, though potentially influenced by labeling schemes

## Next Checks

1. Test the same QGen approaches on additional e-commerce and non-e-commerce datasets with different relevance granularity to assess generalizability

3. Conduct ablation studies varying prompt templates and conditioning strategies to isolate whether poor label discrimination stems from model limitations or suboptimal prompting
