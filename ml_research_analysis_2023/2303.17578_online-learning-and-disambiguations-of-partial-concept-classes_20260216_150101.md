# Online Learning and Disambiguations of Partial Concept Classes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17578
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17578
- Reference count: 1
- The paper demonstrates that a partial concept class can be online learnable while no disambiguation has bounded VC dimension

## Executive Summary
This paper resolves a fundamental question in learning theory by constructing a partial concept class that is online learnable but cannot be extended to any total concept class that is learnable in either the online or PAC settings. The construction leverages a graph with small biclique partition number but large chromatic number, showing that disambiguating such partial classes necessarily results in unlearnable total classes. This work bridges graph theory and learning theory, providing the first explicit separation between learnability of partial and total concept classes.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a graph-theoretic construction where a partial concept class is built from a graph with specific properties: small biclique partition number but large chromatic number. The key insight is that while the partial class itself has bounded Littlestone dimension (making it online learnable), any disambiguation must have large VC dimension. The construction proceeds by associating graph vertices with instances and edges with labelings, creating a partial concept class where the learnability properties of the disambiguation are directly tied to the chromatic number of the original graph.

## Key Results
- Constructs a partial concept class with Littlestone dimension 1 that cannot be disambiguated into any total concept class with bounded VC dimension
- Resolves the open question of whether all online learnable partial classes can be disambiguated into PAC learnable total classes
- Provides improved bounds on the separation between chromatic number and biclique partition number in graphs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by exploiting the inherent tension between the structural constraints of partial concept classes and their disambiguations. The partial class is designed to be learnable by carefully constraining which labelings are allowed, while any disambiguation that fills in the missing labels must necessarily create a more complex concept class with higher combinatorial complexity (measured by VC dimension).

## Foundational Learning
- **Littlestone dimension**: Measures online learnability of concept classes; needed to establish when partial classes are learnable; quick check: verify dimension ≤ k for some small k
- **VC dimension**: Classical measure of PAC learnability; needed to prove disambiguations are unlearnable; quick check: show VC dimension grows with n
- **Biclique partition number**: Minimum number of complete bipartite subgraphs covering all edges; needed for graph construction; quick check: verify partition size is small
- **Chromatic number**: Minimum colors needed for proper vertex coloring; needed to force high VC dimension; quick check: verify chromatic number is large
- **Partial concept classes**: Classes where not all labelings are defined; needed as the learning framework; quick check: confirm partial nature of the construction
- **Disambiguation**: Process of extending partial to total concept classes; needed to study learnability preservation; quick check: verify all disambiguation attempts fail

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Graph vertices -> Partial concept class instances -> Littlestone dimension analysis -> Disambiguation attempts -> VC dimension lower bound

**Critical path**: The construction proceeds from graph selection through partial class definition to learnability analysis. The critical path is: (1) Select graph with desired biclique/chromatic properties, (2) Build partial concept class from graph structure, (3) Prove Littlestone dimension is bounded, (4) Show all disambiguations have unbounded VC dimension.

**Design tradeoffs**: The construction trades simplicity of the partial class (Littlestone dimension 1) for complexity in the disambiguation (unbounded VC dimension). Alternative constructions might achieve different tradeoffs between the parameters.

**Failure signatures**: If the chromatic number of the underlying graph is too small, disambiguations may remain learnable. If the biclique partition is too large, the partial class may become unlearnable. The construction must carefully balance these competing requirements.

## Limitations
- The construction relies on specific graph-theoretic properties that may not generalize to all partial concept classes
- The bounds achieved, while demonstrating separation, may not be tight
- The connection to biclique partitions, while mathematically elegant, may not be the most natural framework for studying partial concept class learnability

## Confidence
- **High**: The core construction demonstrating a learnable partial class with unlearnable disambiguation is mathematically sound and the proof techniques are rigorous
- **Medium**: The claimed separation between online learnability and PAC learnability in the disambiguation context follows from established learning theory principles
- **Medium**: The connection to biclique partition number and chromatic number, while novel, requires further exploration of whether this is the most efficient construction
