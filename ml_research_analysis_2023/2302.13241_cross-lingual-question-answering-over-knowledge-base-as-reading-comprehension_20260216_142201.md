# Cross-Lingual Question Answering over Knowledge Base as Reading Comprehension

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13241
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13241
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Achieves 74.37% hits@1 on WebQSP-zh and strong zero-shot performance on QALD-M across 12 languages

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses cross-lingual question answering over knowledge bases by converting KB subgraphs into natural language passages and applying multilingual pre-trained language models fine-tuned on cross-lingual machine reading comprehension datasets. The approach bridges the gap between structured knowledge base schemas and natural language questions, enabling effective cross-lingual question answering with limited training data. The method demonstrates strong performance on two datasets across 12 languages, achieving comparable results to baselines with only 10% of the training data.

## Method Summary
The approach converts KB subgraphs into natural language passages using a PLM-based KB-to-text generation model (JointGT on WebNLG), then applies multilingual pre-trained language models (MPLMs) fine-tuned on SQuAD, xMRC datasets (MLQA, MKQA, XQuAD), and finally on xKBQA data. This multi-stage fine-tuning strategy leverages existing MRC resources to overcome xKBQA data scarcity. The converted passages are processed by the fine-tuned MPLM to rank candidate answer spans, enabling cross-lingual question answering over knowledge bases.

## Key Results
- Achieves 74.37% hits@1 on WebQSP-zh dataset
- Demonstrates strong zero-shot performance on QALD-M across 11 languages
- Shows robust performance with limited training data (comparable to baselines with only 10% of training data)
- Improves average performance by 6.7% hits@1 after fine-tuning on xMRC datasets

- Claim: Cross-lingual mapping learned from xMRC datasets transfers to xKBQA task
- Mechanism: The approach converts KB subgraphs into natural language passages and applies multilingual pre-trained models (MPLMs) fine-tuned on xMRC datasets. These models have learned cross-lingual semantic mappings during pre-training and xMRC fine-tuning, which can be applied to xKBQA.
- Core assumption: Cross-lingual knowledge learned in xMRC domain is transferable to xKBQA domain when structured KB data is converted to natural language.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: KB-to-text conversion narrows the semantic gap between structured KB schemas and natural language questions
- Mechanism: By converting KB triples into coherent natural language sentences using PLM-based generation models, the method bridges the expression difference between KB predicates and natural language expressions.
- Core assumption: Natural language representations of KB data are more compatible with MPLM cross-lingual capabilities than raw structured triples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We convert KB subgraphs into passages to narrow the gap between KB schemas and questions"
  - [section 3.2] "To narrow down the gap of mapping, we propose to convert KB subgraphs to natural language passages, formulating xKBQA as an xMRC task"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-stage fine-tuning strategy effectively leverages existing MRC resources to overcome xKBQA data scarcity
- Mechanism: The approach uses three-stage fine-tuning: (1) SQuAD for general MRC ability, (2) xMRC datasets for cross-lingual capability, (3) xKBQA data for task-specific knowledge

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Cross-lingual transfer learning
  - Why needed here: The method relies on models trained on English data performing well on non-English languages without direct translation
  - Quick check question: Can you explain how multilingual pre-trained models like mBERT or XLM-R achieve cross-lingual transfer without explicit alignment during pre-training?

  - Why needed here: Understanding the difference between information extraction and semantic parsing approaches is crucial for appreciating why this method chooses the information extraction style
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between information extraction-style KBQA and semantic parsing-style KBQA, and what are the trade-offs between them?

- Concept: Data-to-text generation
  - Why needed here: The KB-to-text conversion is central to the approach, converting structured KB triples into natural language passages
  - Quick check question: How do modern data-to-text models like JointGT handle complex KB structures like CVT nodes differently from simple triple concatenation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Entity Linking -> KB Subgraph Extraction -> KB-to-Text Conversion -> Multi-stage Fine-tuning -> Cross-lingual MRC
- Critical path:
  1. Entity linking identifies topic entity
  2. Subgraph extraction retrieves relevant KB triples
  3. KB-to-text generation converts triples to passages
  4. Multi-stage fine-tuning prepares model
  5. MRC model ranks answer spans in passages

- Design tradeoffs:
  - KB-to-text vs. simple concatenation: More natural but computationally heavier vs. simpler but less coherent
  - Maximum passage length: Balances comprehensiveness against MPLM input limits
  - Multi-stage vs. direct fine-tuning: Better performance with limited data vs. simpler training pipeline

- Failure signatures:
  - Low hits@1 despite good entity linking: Indicates KB-to-text generation or MRC model issues
  - Good performance on single-hop questions but poor on multi-hop: Suggests subgraph extraction or passage generation limitations
  - Performance drops significantly when reducing training data: Indicates insufficient cross-lingual transfer capability

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate KB-to-text generation quality by manually checking sample outputs for coherence and information preservation
  2. Test cross-lingual transfer by evaluating model on QALD-M without xKBQA fine-tuning
  3. Assess multi-stage fine-tuning by comparing performance after each stage (SQuAD-only, SQuAD+xMRC, SQuAD+xMRC+xKBQA)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the upper limit on the complexity of questions that can be effectively answered using this KB-to-text conversion approach, particularly for multi-hop reasoning questions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge their focus on single-relation and CVT questions, and note that their vanilla BERT-based MRC model has limited capacity for complex questions involving constraints or multiple relations.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical data on performance degradation for increasingly complex questions or evaluate on datasets with more challenging multi-hop reasoning questions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing performance on datasets with varying levels of question complexity, or a systematic analysis of failure cases for multi-hop questions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of this approach scale with the size of the knowledge base and the number of entities, particularly for very large knowledge bases like Freebase?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that converted passages can be very long (up to 20k words) for hub entities like USA, and that current xMRC models struggle with such long passages.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the impact of knowledge base size on performance, or investigate techniques for handling very large knowledge bases efficiently.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance analysis on progressively larger knowledge bases, or experiments with different techniques for managing knowledge base size (e.g., entity pruning, passage summarization).

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of entity linking accuracy on the overall performance of the cross-lingual KBQA system, and how can entity linking be improved for cross-lingual settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors report a significant performance gap between using golden topic entities (65.9% and 56.5% hits@1) versus using two entity linking methods (89.1% and 76.8% recall@5), indicating that entity linking is a crucial issue.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the types of entity linking errors or propose specific improvements for cross-lingual entity linking.
- What evidence would resolve it: Error analysis of entity linking failures, or experiments with different entity linking methods specifically designed for cross-lingual settings.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the KB-to-text conversion quality affect the performance of the cross-lingual KBQA system, and what are the key factors that influence the quality of the converted passages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors conduct an ablation study showing that replacing the PLM-based KB-to-text model with a simple heuristic concatenation leads to a 2.13% decrease in hits@1, and that KB-to-text generation errors account for 12% of the sampled errors.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the factors that influence KB-to-text conversion quality, or explore different KB-to-text models or techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of the factors that contribute to high-quality KB-to-text conversion, or experiments with different KB-to-text models or techniques (e.g., fine-tuning on domain-specific data, incorporating question context).

## Limitations
- The approach relies heavily on the quality of KB-to-text generation and cross-lingual transfer capabilities of pre-trained models
- Manual translation of WebQSP to create WebQSP-zh may not fully capture linguistic diversity of naturally occurring Chinese questions
- Limited exploration of failure cases where converted passages fail to preserve essential KB relationships

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core methodology of converting KB subgraphs to natural language passages and applying xMRC fine-tuned models is well-established and produces strong empirical results (74.37% hits@1 on WebQSP-zh, strong zero-shot performance on QALD-M)
- **Medium Confidence**: The three-stage fine-tuning strategy's effectiveness is demonstrated but not extensively ablated. The specific contribution of each fine-tuning stage to the final performance is not fully isolated
- **Medium Confidence**: The KB-to-text generation approach using JointGT is assumed to produce coherent, information-preserving text, but this is not explicitly validated through human evaluation or automatic metrics beyond answer accuracy

## Next Checks

1. **Manual Evaluation of KB-to-Text Quality**: Conduct human evaluation of sample KB-to-text conversions to assess coherence, information preservation, and faithfulness to original KB relationships, particularly for complex CVT structures

2. **Cross-Lingual Transfer Robustness**: Systematically evaluate model performance across the 12 languages in QALD-M with varying degrees of linguistic similarity to English to identify which languages benefit most from the cross-lingual transfer and where the approach may fail

3. **Ablation of Fine-Tuning Stages**: Perform detailed ablation studies removing each fine-tuning stage (SQuAD, xMRC, xKBQA) to quantify their individual contributions and determine if the three-stage approach is necessary or if simpler alternatives could achieve comparable results
