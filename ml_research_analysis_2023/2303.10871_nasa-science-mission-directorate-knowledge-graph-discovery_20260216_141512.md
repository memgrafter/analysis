# NASA Science Mission Directorate Knowledge Graph Discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10871
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10871
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposed pipeline generates KGs representing five NASA SMD domains with >80% accuracy in domain distinction

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive pipeline for generating knowledge graphs (KGs) across five NASA Science Mission Directorate domains to enable dataset search and discovery of interdisciplinary connections. The approach combines large-scale data collection from NASA definitions, the Unified Astronomy Thesaurus, Wikipedia, and arXiv abstracts with modern NLP techniques including YAKE! keyword extraction and BERT-based semantic similarity. Initial results demonstrate the pipeline's ability to distinguish between domains with high accuracy, though validation remains a significant challenge due to technical terminology and computational constraints.

## Method Summary
The pipeline follows a five-stage process: (1) collecting and preprocessing textual data from multiple sources (3M+ definitions, 168K abstracts, 695 Wikipedia summaries), (2) extracting domain-specific keywords using YAKE! unsupervised algorithm, (3) creating edges through co-occurrence frequency and semantic similarity calculations, (4) validating KGs through expert review and automated metrics, and (5) visualizing results using multiple tools. The approach leverages both frequency-based and semantic similarity methods to link nodes, while addressing challenges like homonyms and cross-domain terminology overlap.

## Key Results
- Collected over 3 million NASA SMD definitions, 168,000 arXiv abstracts, and 695 Wikipedia summaries
- Achieved >80% accuracy in distinguishing between Planetary Science and Heliophysics domains using k-means clustering
- Generated domain-specific KGs with nodes representing scientific concepts and edges representing relationships

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection → Preprocessing (normalization, tokenization, lemmatization) → Entity extraction (YAKE! keyword generation) → Linkage (frequency counting/semantic similarity) → Validation (expert review/automated metrics) → Visualization (NetworkX, iGraph, Pyvis, Arena3D)

- Critical path: The most time-consuming and resource-intensive components are data collection (gathering millions of definitions and thousands of abstracts), entity extraction (processing large text corpora with NLP models), and validation (expert review of technical terminology). The computational constraints mentioned suggest that computational resources are likely to be the bottleneck.

- Design tradeoffs: Using YAKE! instead of fine-tuned BERT models trades accuracy for simplicity and speed, while using frequency-based linkage instead of semantic similarity trades precision for computational efficiency. The choice of multiple visualization tools provides flexibility but increases complexity.

- Failure signatures: Poor KG quality manifests as irrelevant nodes, weak or incorrect edges, inability to distinguish between domains, or visualizations that are too complex to interpret. Validation failures indicate that the NLP models are not capturing domain-specific concepts effectively.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test YAKE! keyword extraction on a small subset of NASA SMD definitions to verify it produces relevant scientific terms before scaling up
  2. Apply k-means clustering to a small sample of mixed-domain abstracts to verify the 80% accuracy claim before running on larger datasets
  3. Create a small-scale KG using a subset of collected data and visualize it to verify the entire pipeline works end-to-end before processing all data sources

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of YAKE! for extracting scientifically relevant keywords in highly technical NASA domains has not been rigorously validated against domain expert benchmarks
- The pipeline's handling of homonyms and cross-domain terminology overlap remains largely theoretical without demonstrated solutions
- The claim that the pipeline enables "discovery of new connections" lacks empirical evidence or specific examples of novel discoveries made possible by the KGs

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall pipeline architecture (data collection → preprocessing → entity extraction → linkage → validation → visualization) is methodologically sound and follows established KG construction practices
- **Medium Confidence**: The use of YAKE! for keyword extraction and k-means clustering for domain distinction are reasonable approaches, but their effectiveness specifically for NASA SMD domains requires further validation
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that the pipeline enables "discovery of new connections" lacks empirical evidence or specific examples of novel discoveries made possible by the KGs
