# An experimental study in Real-time Facial Emotion Recognition on new 3RL dataset

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03064
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03064
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: CNN achieves 91.4% accuracy on 3RL dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the 3RL dataset, created by merging and manually filtering CK+48, FER-2013, and a facial expression dataset to address issues like unbalanced classes and misleading images. The authors evaluate SVM and CNN models on this new dataset, reporting improved generalization and emotion classification accuracy compared to the original datasets. The CNN model achieves 91.4% accuracy on 3RL, significantly outperforming results on FER-2013 and CK+48.

## Method Summary
The authors created the 3RL dataset by combining three existing facial expression datasets (CK+48, FER-2013, and another facial expression dataset) and manually filtering out misleading images. They applied SVM with HOG and landmark features and CNN with image and landmark features to compare 3RL against the original datasets. The SVM used tuned hyperparameters (C=1, gamma=0.2), while the CNN architecture consisted of four convolutional layers with max-pooling and dropout, followed by dense layers with ReLU activation.

## Key Results
- 3RL dataset contains 24,394 images labeled with five emotions (happiness, fear, sadness, disgust, anger)
- CNN model achieves 91.4% accuracy on 3RL dataset
- CNN accuracy on FER-2013: 89%, on CK+48: 95.9%
- SVM with HOG and landmark features shows high training accuracy but modest generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Combining and filtering multiple facial expression datasets improves model robustness by increasing image diversity and class balance, reducing overfitting to noise. This assumes the three source datasets contain complementary, non-overlapping errors and coverage gaps.

### Mechanism 2
Reducing emotion classes from seven to five improves classifier accuracy by mitigating class overlap. Merging "anger & disgust" and "sad & fear" simplifies the decision boundary for the CNN and SVM models, based on the assumption that these emotion pairs are visually similar in facial features.

### Mechanism 3
Hyperparameter tuning (C=1, gamma=0.2) for SVM with RBF kernel yields high training accuracy but modest generalization. Proper regularization balances model complexity against overfitting on high-dimensional HOG/landmark features, assuming the chosen kernel and parameters generalize well to unseen data from the same distribution.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Dataset curation and class balancing
  - Why needed here: Raw FER datasets often have class imbalance and noisy labels, which degrade model performance; 3RL explicitly addresses this.
  - Quick check question: Why did the authors merge "anger" and "disgust" into one class, and what effect does this have on model complexity?

- Concept: Feature extraction (HOG + facial landmarks) for SVM
  - Why needed here: SVM works best with engineered features; HOG and landmarks capture shape and texture cues for facial expressions.
  - Quick check question: How does HOG with 16x16 cells and 8-bin gradients help differentiate between happy and neutral faces?

- Concept: CNN architecture design (layer depth, dropout, activation)
  - Why needed here: The final CNN uses 4 conv layers (32, 64, 128, 512 filters), dropout, and ReLU/softmax activations to balance capacity and overfitting.
  - Quick check question: What role does max-pooling play after each conv layer in the proposed CNN?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input(48x48 grayscale) -> Conv(32) -> MaxPool -> Conv(64) -> MaxPool -> Conv(128) -> MaxPool -> Conv(512) -> Flatten -> Dense(ReLU) -> Dropout -> Dense(Softmax)
- Critical path: Face image → Conv (32) → MaxPool → Conv (64) → MaxPool → Conv (128) → MaxPool → Conv (512) → Flatten → Dense (ReLU) → Dropout → Dense (Softmax) → Loss
- Design tradeoffs: Fewer classes (5 vs 7) → simpler model, higher accuracy but less granularity; Larger batch size (32) → stable gradients, slower per-epoch updates; ReLU activations → faster convergence vs sigmoid/tanh
- Failure signatures: Overfitting: Train accuracy >> test accuracy (observed in SVM experiments); Underfitting: Both train and test accuracy low, suggesting too shallow or weak model; Class imbalance: Skewed confusion matrix, especially if balancing step skipped
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train SVM on HOG + landmarks (C=1, gamma=0.2) on FER-2013 → expect high train, low test accuracy.
  2. Train CNN on CK+48 (small dataset) → expect high accuracy but poor generalization.
  3. Train CNN on 3RL with tuned architecture (4 conv layers, dropout 0.35/0.5/0.5) → expect balanced train/test accuracy (~91%).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the 3RL dataset's performance generalize to other facial emotion recognition models and algorithms beyond SVM and CNN? The authors mention that the 3RL dataset provides better generalization compared to original datasets, but only test SVM and CNN models. Testing with other models like RNN, LSTM, or newer architectures would provide evidence of its generalizability.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of the manual filtering process on the 3RL dataset's performance, and how does it compare to automated filtering techniques? The authors manually filtered misleading images from the 3RL dataset but do not compare this process to automated techniques. Comparing performance with manually filtered images to a version with automatically filtered images would provide evidence of the impact of the filtering process.

### Open Question 3
How does the 3RL dataset's performance vary across different demographic groups, and what steps were taken to ensure diversity and representation? The paper does not explicitly discuss the demographic diversity of the 3RL dataset, which is an important consideration for facial emotion recognition systems. Analyzing performance across different demographic groups and discussing steps taken to ensure diversity would provide evidence of its fairness and inclusivity.

## Limitations
- Manual filtering process lacks detailed documentation, making reproducibility challenging
- Class merging strategy is justified but not empirically validated
- No cross-dataset validation to confirm improvements generalize beyond constituent datasets

## Confidence
- High Confidence: CNN architecture design and reported 91.4% accuracy on 3RL dataset
- Medium Confidence: Claims about 3RL dataset superiority over FER-2013 and CK+48
- Low Confidence: Effectiveness of class merging strategy and manual filtering benefits

## Next Checks
1. Test the trained 3RL CNN model on an independent facial expression dataset (e.g., JAFFE or RaFD) to verify generalization claims.
2. Train separate binary classifiers for the merged classes (anger vs disgust, sad vs fear) to quantify the actual similarity between these emotions.
3. Create versions of 3RL with different filtering intensities and class configurations to isolate the impact of each curation step on final accuracy.
