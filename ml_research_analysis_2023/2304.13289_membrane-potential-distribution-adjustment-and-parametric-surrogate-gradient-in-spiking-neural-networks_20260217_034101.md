# Membrane Potential Distribution Adjustment and Parametric Surrogate Gradient in Spiking Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13289
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13289
- Reference count: 10
- One-line primary result: PDA and PSG methods enable SNNs to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy with fewer timesteps and reduced computational load.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces two methods to improve the training of deep spiking neural networks (SNNs): membrane potential distribution adjustment (PDA) and parametric surrogate gradient (PSG). PDA incorporates a potential distribution loss to normalize pre-activation distributions across layers, reducing quantization errors and improving convergence. PSG parametrizes surrogate gradients and optimizes their shape during training, overcoming the limitations of fixed, empirically chosen gradients. Together, these methods enhance SNN performance, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on static and neuromorphic datasets with fewer timesteps and reduced computational load.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes PDA and PSG to address the challenge of training deep SNNs efficiently. PDA adds a potential distribution loss (LPD) to the total loss, computed as the symmetric KL divergence between the actual potential distribution and a target Gaussian, to normalize membrane potential distributions. PSG parametrizes the surrogate gradient via auxiliary functions (arctan for conv layers, log-exp for FC layers) with a learnable parameter γ, allowing the gradient shape to be optimized during training. These methods are integrated with backpropagation through time (BPTT) and tested on ResNet architectures for static (MNIST, CIFAR10) and neuromorphic (NMNIST, DVS-CIFAR10) datasets.

## Key Results
- PDA and PSG together achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on static and neuromorphic datasets.
- The methods enable high performance with fewer timesteps, reducing memory and latency.
- PSG allows automatic discovery of optimal surrogate gradient shapes for different layers and tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PDA stabilizes training by minimizing the divergence between actual and target membrane potential distributions.
- Mechanism: A potential distribution loss (LPD) is added to the total loss, computed as the symmetric KL divergence between the actual potential distribution and a target Gaussian. This forces the network to redistribute membrane potentials during gradient descent.
- Core assumption: Membrane potential distributions in trained deep SNNs are unimodal and approximately Gaussian, so minimizing KL divergence with a Gaussian target leads to more suitable pre-activation distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose membrane potential distribution adjustment (PDA) method by incorporating potential distribution loss (LPD) to quantify the inconsistency between actual neural potential distribution and target."
  - [section] "It has been clarified in (Chen et al., 2021) that weight distribution in direct trained deep SNNs is normally unimodal so that neurons’ potentials should be analytically a Gaussian distribution for each layer..."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing corpus evidence.
- Break condition: If the actual potential distribution is multi-modal or highly skewed, KL divergence with a Gaussian target may misguide the optimization.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PSG improves performance by learning the optimal shape of the surrogate gradient during training.
- Mechanism: The surrogate gradient is parameterized via auxiliary functions (arctan for conv layers, log-exp for FC layers) with a learnable parameter γ. Gradient descent updates both weights and γ, allowing the SG to adapt to the task and network depth.
- Core assumption: Different layers and tasks benefit from different surrogate gradient shapes, and these shapes can be discovered automatically via backpropagation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose the parametric surrogate gradient (PSG) method to iteratively update SG and eventually determine an optimal surrogate gradient parameter, which calibrates the shape of candidate SGs."
  - [section] "We propose a systematic approach by defining the form of auxiliary functions for different types of layers and designing a gradient descent-based method to iteratively update surrogate gradient parameter to carve out the gradient shape during network training."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing corpus evidence.
- Break condition: If the parameterization cannot represent the optimal gradient shape for a given task, performance will plateau.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PDA and PSG together reduce the number of timesteps needed for inference by improving training efficiency.
- Mechanism: PDA normalizes potential distributions, reducing quantization error and allowing faster convergence; PSG finds better gradients, further accelerating convergence. Together, they enable high accuracy with fewer timesteps.
- Core assumption: Both normalization of pre-activations and adaptive gradients contribute to faster and more stable convergence, reducing the temporal resolution required for accurate inference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed methods can be readily integrated with backpropagation through time (BPTT) algorithm and help modulated SNNs to achieve state-of-the-art performance on both static and dynamic dataset with fewer timesteps."
  - [section] "Our SNNs generally require much fewer timesteps than other works, leading to significant improvement on both memory space occupancy and system latency."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing corpus evidence.
- Break condition: If other factors (e.g., dataset complexity) dominate the required timesteps, the reduction may be less pronounced.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) dynamics and spike generation
  - Why needed here: The LIF model defines how membrane potentials evolve and when spikes are emitted, forming the basis of SNN behavior and the non-differentiable activation function.
  - Quick check question: In the LIF model, what triggers a spike and what happens to the membrane potential immediately afterward?

- Concept: Surrogate gradients and their parameterization
  - Why needed here: Since the spike function is non-differentiable, surrogate gradients are used for backpropagation; PSG learns the best shape of these gradients.
  - Quick check question: How does the PSG method update the surrogate gradient parameter γ during training?

- Concept: KL divergence and distribution alignment
  - Why needed here: PDA uses KL divergence to measure and minimize the difference between actual and target membrane potential distributions.
  - Quick check question: Why is the KL divergence defined symmetrically in the PDA loss?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input encoding layer (spikes)
  - Convolutional layers with LIF neurons and PSG (arctan-based)
  - Fully connected layers with LIF neurons and PSG (log-exp-based)
  - PDA loss layer (distribution alignment)
  - Output layer (spikes or potentials)
- Critical path:
  1. Forward pass: inputs → spikes → LIF dynamics → potentials → output spikes/potentials
  2. Backward pass: compute total loss (task + PDA) → gradients for weights and γ via BPTT → update
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More learnable parameters (γ per layer) increases model capacity but also complexity
  - PDA loss weight (β) must be tuned to balance task and distribution alignment
  - PSG choice of auxiliary function shapes affects convergence and final accuracy
- Failure signatures:
  - Loss diverges or oscillates: likely γ or β misconfigured
  - Accuracy plateaus early: possible suboptimal γ initialization or PDA loss too strong
  - Extremely low firing rates: PDA loss may be over-suppressing activity
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a baseline SNN on MNIST with fixed SG, no PDA; record accuracy and timesteps.
  2. Add PDA only; compare convergence speed and final accuracy.
  3. Add both PDA and PSG; verify improved robustness to initial γ and reduced timesteps.

## Limitations
- The assumption that membrane potential distributions are Gaussian is not rigorously tested across different architectures or tasks.
- The choice of auxiliary functions for PSG is not justified beyond empirical performance.
- The corpus evidence for both methods is weak, suggesting the novelty and robustness claims may be overstated.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (PDA): Medium
- Mechanism 2 (PSG): Medium
- Mechanism 3 (Combined speedup): Low
