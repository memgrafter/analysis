# Jointly Optimizing Translations and Speech Timing to Improve Isochrony in Automatic Dubbing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12979
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12979
- Reference count: 0
- One-line primary result: Achieves 55% relative improvement in speech overlap with 2.7-9% BLEU trade-off

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to automatic dubbing that jointly optimizes translation quality and speech timing to improve isochrony. The authors propose training a single encoder-decoder model that generates phonemes and their durations, given source text and desired speech durations as input. This approach eliminates the need for separate prosodic alignment models and allows the model to make trade-offs between translation quality and isochrony. Experiments show that the proposed method achieves significantly higher speech overlap with the source speech (55% relative improvement) compared to baseline models, with a small trade-off in translation quality (2.7% to 9% relative decrease). The authors also release a new dubbing test set to facilitate future research in this area.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a Transformer encoder-decoder model that jointly optimizes translation and speech duration for automatic dubbing. The model takes source text and binned desired speech durations as input, and outputs target phonemes with interleaved duration tokens. Training data is prepared by aligning source speech with target transcripts using Montreal Forced Aligner to extract phonemes and durations. The model is trained using cross-entropy loss with equal weighting for phonemes and durations. To address the mismatch between training (perfect durations) and inference (noisier durations from VAD), Gaussian noise is added to source durations during training. The model is evaluated on new dubbing test sets, measuring both speech overlap and BLEU score using a phoneme-to-word seq2seq system.

## Key Results
- 55% relative improvement in speech overlap compared to baseline models
- 2.7% to 9% relative decrease in BLEU score compared to baselines
- Significant improvement over isometric machine translation and duration prediction baselines
- New dubbing test sets (test91 and test101) released for future research

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Source text + binned durations -> Transformer encoder-decoder -> Phonemes + durations -> TTS -> Dubbed speech
- Critical path: Source text → binned durations → Transformer → phonemes+durations → TTS → dubbed speech
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Phoneme-level vs. word-level modeling: Phonemes allow finer control over timing but require a phoneme-to-word mapping for evaluation.
  - Binning durations vs. continuous prediction: Binning is more stable but loses granularity.
  - Noise injection: Improves robustness but requires tuning the noise level.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low BLEU but high speech overlap: Model is prioritizing isochrony over translation quality.
  - Low speech overlap: Model is not learning to adjust durations, or binning is too coarse.
  - TTS artifacts: Duration tokens are not being respected by TTS, or phonemes are mispredicted.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train without noise, evaluate BLEU and speech overlap on a small validation set.
  2. Train with increasing noise levels, plot BLEU vs. speech overlap to find the optimal trade-off.
  3. Compare phoneme-to-word mapping accuracy with a baseline (e.g., phoneme overlap) to validate the evaluation method.

## Limitations
- The evaluation methodology relies on phoneme-to-word mapping, which is known to be ill-defined and ambiguous, potentially introducing noise into BLEU scores.
- The noise injection mechanism's effectiveness depends critically on the standard deviation parameter, but the paper does not provide ablation studies showing how different noise levels affect the isochrony-BLEU trade-off.
- The binning of durations into 100 categories is arbitrary and may mask important timing nuances, without systematic exploration of different granularities.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core architectural claim that jointly modeling translation and duration in a single Transformer model is feasible and produces coherent outputs.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that this approach achieves "significantly higher speech overlap" (55% relative improvement), subject to evaluation methodology limitations.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that this is the "first work" to tackle the joint optimization problem, given related work on isochrony in speech translation.
