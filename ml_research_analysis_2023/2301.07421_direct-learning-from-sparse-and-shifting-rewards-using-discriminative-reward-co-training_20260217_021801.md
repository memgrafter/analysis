# DIRECT: Learning from Sparse and Shifting Rewards using Discriminative Reward Co-Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07421
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07421
- Reference count: 40
- Direct is a reinforcement learning method designed to tackle sparse and shifting reward scenarios by introducing an imitation buffer and discriminator network.

## Executive Summary
DIRECT is a reinforcement learning method designed to tackle sparse and shifting reward scenarios. It builds upon self-imitation learning (SIL) by introducing an imitation buffer that stores beneficial trajectories from previous policies, determined by their return. A discriminator network is trained to distinguish between trajectories generated by the current policy and those from the buffer. This discriminator's verdict is used to construct a reward signal that guides policy optimization toward more valuable regions of the reward landscape.

DIRECT outperforms state-of-the-art algorithms like PPO, A2C, DQN, and SIL in sparse- and shifting-reward environments. In experiments, DIRECT-trained policies showed superior performance and safety, achieving near-optimal returns and avoiding failure terminations (e.g., running into lava fields). It demonstrated robustness to distributional shifts, adapting more effectively to changes in obstacles or target positions compared to other methods. Overall, DIRECT provides a surrogate reward signal that enhances exploration and adaptability in challenging reinforcement learning settings.

## Method Summary
DIRECT builds upon self-imitation learning (SIL) by incorporating a self-imitation buffer that stores beneficial trajectories from previous policies, determined by their return. A discriminator network is trained concurrently to distinguish between trajectories generated by the current policy and beneficial trajectories generated by previous policies. The discriminator's verdict is used to construct a reward signal for optimizing the policy. This approach enables the policy to learn from both the environmental reward and the discriminator's assessment of trajectory quality, improving exploration in sparse reward environments and adaptation to shifting reward distributions.

## Key Results
- DIRECT outperforms state-of-the-art algorithms like PPO, A2C, DQN, and SIL in sparse- and shifting-reward environments.
- DIRECT-trained policies achieve near-optimal returns and avoid failure terminations (e.g., running into lava fields) in safety-critical tasks.
- DIRECT demonstrates robustness to distributional shifts, adapting more effectively to changes in obstacles or target positions compared to other methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The discriminator network acts as a surrogate reward signal that guides policy optimization toward regions of the reward landscape associated with high-return trajectories.
- Mechanism: The discriminator is trained to distinguish between trajectories from the current policy and those from the self-imitation buffer (which stores high-return trajectories). When the discriminator easily tells them apart, it signals that the current policy underperforms, prompting further training. When the discriminator struggles, it indicates that the current policy matches the high-return behavior stored in the buffer.
- Core assumption: The self-imitation buffer reliably stores beneficial trajectories whose returns are meaningfully higher than the current policy's performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DIRECT's self-imitation buffer enables exploration of sparse reward landscapes by storing and prioritizing high-return trajectories for policy optimization.
- Mechanism: After each rollout, trajectories are evaluated by their discounted return, and only those with returns exceeding the estimated value (positive advantage) are stored in the buffer. The policy is then optimized using both environmental rewards and the discriminator-based reward derived from these stored trajectories.
- Core assumption: The advantage criterion (return > estimated value) effectively identifies trajectories that are genuinely beneficial for learning optimal behavior.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The discriminator's conditional input (state, action, accumulated return) allows it to learn a reward landscape that incorporates both immediate actions and long-term consequences.
- Mechanism: By including the accumulated return as part of the discriminator's input, the discriminator learns to associate state-action pairs with their eventual outcomes, creating a more informative reward signal than environmental rewards alone.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Environment -> Policy network (πθ) -> Trajectory generation -> Return calculation -> Buffer update -> Discriminator update -> Reward calculation -> Policy update -> Environment

- Critical path: Environment → Policy → Trajectory generation → Return calculation → Buffer update → Discriminator update → Reward calculation → Policy update → Environment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Buffer size vs. computational overhead: Larger buffers store more diverse experiences but require more memory and processing
  - Discriminator update frequency vs. policy stability: More frequent discriminator updates provide better reward signals but may destabilize policy learning
  - Mixture parameter χ vs. exploration exploitation: Higher χ emphasizes discriminator rewards, potentially improving exploration but reducing direct environmental feedback

- Failure signatures:
  - Policy collapses to random behavior: Likely caused by discriminator providing zero or uniform rewards
  - Slow convergence or no improvement: May indicate poor buffer selection criteria or insufficient discriminator training
  - Overfitting to specific buffer trajectories: Could result from discriminator becoming too strong relative to policy updates

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify buffer population: Run DIRECT on a simple environment with known optimal policy and check that the buffer stores trajectories with returns exceeding the current policy's average return
  2. Test discriminator learning: Disable policy updates and verify that the discriminator can distinguish between random trajectories and optimal trajectories from the buffer
  3. Validate reward shaping: Compare policy learning curves with and without the discriminator reward signal in a sparse reward environment to confirm the surrogate reward improves exploration

## Limitations
- The computational overhead introduced by maintaining and training the discriminator network alongside the policy may limit scalability to complex, high-dimensional environments.
- The performance improvements are primarily demonstrated in relatively simple grid-world and navigation tasks, raising questions about direct applicability to more complex real-world scenarios.
- The paper lacks extensive testing in environments with higher-dimensional state and action spaces.

## Confidence
- High: DIRECT's effectiveness in the tested environments, supported by direct experimental comparisons showing consistent improvements over baselines.
- Medium: The generalizability of these results to more complex domains, as the paper lacks extensive testing in environments with higher-dimensional state and action spaces.
- Low: The robustness of the approach to extreme distributional shifts, as the shifting reward scenarios tested involve relatively modest changes to the environment.
