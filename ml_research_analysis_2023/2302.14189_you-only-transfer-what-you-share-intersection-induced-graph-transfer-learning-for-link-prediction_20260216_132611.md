# You Only Transfer What You Share: Intersection-Induced Graph Transfer Learning for Link Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14189
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14189
- Reference count: 40
- Proposes intersection-induced graph transfer learning for link prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for Graph Intersection-induced Transfer Learning (GITL) that leverages shared nodes and edges between source and target graphs to improve link prediction performance, particularly when the target graph is sparse. The framework addresses a critical challenge in graph transfer learning where traditional methods struggle with negative transfer effects due to structural differences between source and target graphs. By focusing on the intersection subgraph and employing a two-stage process of instance selection and knowledge broadcast, the method effectively utilizes structural priors from shared nodes while avoiding the pitfalls of direct transfer learning approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework operates in two stages: first, instance selection involves training on the shared intersection subgraph between source and target to identify relevant patterns; second, knowledge broadcast transfers these learned patterns to the target graph using either edge-centric label propagation (Logit-LP) or a teacher-student GNN-MLP approach. The edge-centric variant, Logit-LP, achieves the best performance by propagating link prediction logits through the graph structure. This approach is particularly effective for sparse target graphs where traditional link prediction methods fail to capture meaningful patterns.

## Key Results
- Logit-LP variant outperforms existing methods including GNN-based approaches, heuristics, and self-supervised pretraining techniques
- Framework achieves superior link prediction performance on proprietary e-commerce graphs and public citation datasets
- Particularly effective in sparse target graphs where traditional methods struggle significantly

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework leverages the structural overlap between source and target graphs by focusing on shared nodes and edges, which provides a natural prior for knowledge transfer. The two-stage process ensures that only relevant patterns from the source graph are transferred to the target, avoiding negative transfer effects that occur when source and target have fundamentally different structures. The edge-centric label propagation approach effectively captures the relational nature of the problem by propagating prediction confidence scores through the graph structure.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Input graphs → Intersection subgraph extraction → GNN training on intersection → Knowledge broadcast (Logit-LP/Teacher-Student) → Link prediction on target

**Critical path:** The most important sequence is: extract intersection → train on intersection → broadcast knowledge → evaluate on target

**Design tradeoffs:** The framework trades computational complexity of training on intersection against improved transfer performance; edge-centric propagation trades potential label noise against better structural propagation

**Failure signatures:** Poor performance indicates insufficient intersection overlap, structural mismatch between source and target, or ineffective knowledge propagation

## Limitations
- Primary datasets are proprietary, limiting reproducibility and external validation
- Method assumes meaningful structural overlap between source and target graphs
- Two-stage process may be computationally expensive for very large graphs
- Theoretical justification for Logit-LP superiority over teacher-student approach is lacking

## Confidence
- High: Proposed framework outperforms existing methods on tested datasets
- Medium: Generalizability to other graph domains beyond proprietary e-commerce graphs
- Low: Theoretical guarantees and convergence properties of the method
