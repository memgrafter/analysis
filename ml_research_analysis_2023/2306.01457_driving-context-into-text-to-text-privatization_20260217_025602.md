# Driving Context into Text-to-Text Privatization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01457
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01457
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: 6.05% increase in classification accuracy on WiC dataset through context-aware sense embeddings

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses text-to-text privatization under metric differential privacy by introducing a context-aware mechanism that leverages word sense embeddings. The core innovation replaces traditional word embeddings with sense embeddings constructed through ego-network clustering of word neighbors, followed by a sense disambiguation step before noise injection. This approach enables more appropriate substitutions for ambiguous words while maintaining privacy guarantees. The method demonstrates significant improvements in both privacy metrics (plausible deniability for higher word quantiles) and utility (downstream classification accuracy).

## Method Summary
The proposed method constructs sense embeddings by building a 300-dimensional word embedding on Wikipedia, retrieving 200 nearest neighbors per word, and performing ego-network clustering to induce senses. For privatization, the mechanism first disambiguates words using a context window of size 5 by computing cosine similarity between sense vectors and the mean vector of context words. Multivariate Laplace noise scaled by privacy budget ε is then injected into the sense vectors, followed by projection back to vocabulary via nearest neighbor search. The approach is evaluated across multiple datasets including WordSim-353, SimLex-999, SCWS for utility, and WikiText corpus for privacy analysis.

## Key Results
- 6.05% increase in word sense disambiguation classification accuracy on Words in Context dataset
- Improved downstream task performance on GLUE benchmark tasks
- Higher plausible deniability (Nw, Sw statistics) for a greater quantile of words compared to baseline mechanisms

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by incorporating semantic context through sense embeddings, allowing the privatization process to distinguish between different meanings of ambiguous words. By disambiguating words before noise injection using surrounding context, the method ensures that substitutions maintain semantic coherence while still providing differential privacy guarantees. The multivariate Laplace noise preserves the geometric structure needed for meaningful substitutions.

## Foundational Learning

**Sense Embeddings**: Represent multiple meanings of words through clustered ego-networks
*Why needed*: Traditional embeddings conflate different word senses, reducing substitution quality
*Quick check*: Verify that sense clusters capture distinct semantic meanings through nearest neighbor analysis

**Ego-network Clustering**: Clustering algorithm applied to word neighbor graphs to induce senses
*Why needed*: Provides a data-driven way to discover word senses without external knowledge bases
*Quick check*: Confirm that clustering produces coherent semantic groupings across vocabulary

**Context Window Similarity**: Uses cosine similarity between sense vectors and context word mean vectors
*Why needed*: Enables disambiguation by comparing candidate senses against surrounding words
*Quick check*: Test that correct senses are selected for polysemous words in various contexts

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Wikipedia embedding construction -> Ego-network clustering -> Sense embedding generation -> Context disambiguation -> Noise injection -> Nearest neighbor projection

**Critical Path**: The disambiguation step followed by noise injection is critical - errors here directly impact both privacy and utility

**Design Tradeoffs**: Larger context windows improve disambiguation accuracy but increase computational cost; finer sense granularity improves utility but may reduce privacy guarantees

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Poor disambiguation leading to semantically inappropriate substitutions
- Insufficient noise causing privacy violations (low Nw/Sw statistics)
- Overly aggressive noise destroying semantic relationships (poor utility metrics)
