# Stochastic Ratios Tracking Algorithm for Large Scale Machine Learning Problems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.09978
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09978
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces SRT algorithm that automatically adjusts step lengths in SGD by tracking progress and variance ratios

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the Stochastic Ratios Tracking (SRT) algorithm for automatic step length selection in stochastic gradient descent and its variants. The method tracks two key ratios - a progress ratio to assess alignment between stochastic objectives and their local linear models, and a variance ratio to estimate gradient noise - using mini-batch updates with minimal computational overhead via the Backpack library. The algorithm adjusts step lengths based on average progress ratio values, increasing them when progress is high and decreasing them when progress is low. Experiments on logistic regression and deep neural networks demonstrate that SRT automatically identifies step lengths comparable to the best hand-tuned values across different batch sizes and initial step length choices.

## Method Summary
The SRT algorithm operates by maintaining a buffer of recent progress ratio values (ˆρ) and variance ratio estimates (ˆMV) computed from mini-batch updates. When the average progress ratio exceeds a threshold, step lengths increase; when it falls below another threshold, they decrease. The variance ratio inversely scales the step size to account for gradient noise. The algorithm uses the Backpack library to efficiently compute variance estimates with minimal computational overhead. Under smoothness assumptions and bounded gradient noise, the method converges to a stationary neighborhood of the solution with a linear convergence rate after initial iterations.

## Key Results
- SRT automatically identifies step lengths comparable to best hand-tuned values across different batch sizes and initial step length choices
- The algorithm successfully adapts step lengths to avoid excessive oscillations (with large steps) and training stalling (with small steps)
- Under reasonable smoothness assumptions, SRT converges to a stationary neighborhood of the solution in expectation with linear convergence rate
- Numerical experiments on logistic regression and deep neural networks validate the algorithm's effectiveness

## Limitations
- Convergence analysis relies on strong smoothness assumptions that may not hold for all machine learning problems, particularly deep neural networks with non-smooth activation functions
- Theoretical guarantees provide convergence to a stationary neighborhood rather than the global optimum, with neighborhood size dependent on noise constant M
- The exact parameter sensitivity (c1, c2, τ, buffer size N) and their impact on performance across different problem domains remains unclear
