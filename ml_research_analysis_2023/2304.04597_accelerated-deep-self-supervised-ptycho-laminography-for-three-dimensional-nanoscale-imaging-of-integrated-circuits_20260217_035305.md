# Accelerated deep self-supervised ptycho-laminography for three-dimensional nanoscale imaging of integrated circuits

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04597
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04597
- Reference count: 40
- The paper presents ADePt, achieving 9.57x time savings while reconstructing 3D IC structures from 16x fewer angular samples

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Accelerated Deep self-supervised Ptycho-laminography (ADePt), a physics-regularized deep learning approach for 3D nanoscale imaging of integrated circuits. The method addresses the computational expense of traditional ptycho-laminography by combining a lightweight pre-processing step with a deep neural network that learns to reconstruct high-quality 3D structures from significantly fewer angular measurements. The self-supervised learning framework fills missing Fourier domain information without requiring ground truth data, achieving reconstruction quality comparable to densely sampled methods while reducing acquisition and computation time substantially.

## Method Summary
ADePt uses a two-stage approach: first, experimental ptycho-laminographic measurements undergo 100 iterations of maximum-likelihood ptychography with projection matching alignment and center cropping to create clean intermediate projections. These pre-processed projections are then fed into a modified U-net architecture with random noise input, which learns to reconstruct the 3D IC structure through a physics-informed loss function incorporating high-pass filtering and total variation regularization. The network is trained using Adam optimizer for 1500 iterations, learning to fill missing Fourier domain information while suppressing artifacts, ultimately producing high-fidelity reconstructions from 16x fewer angular samples than traditional methods.

## Key Results
- Achieves 9.57x aggregate time savings compared to densely sampled reconstruction
- Maintains high fidelity with BER values comparable to densely sampled reconstructions
- Successfully fills missing cone artifacts in Fourier domain better than traditional methods
- Demonstrates 4.67x faster computation while preserving reconstruction quality

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data acquisition -> Pre-processing (100 iterations ptychography, PMA, cropping) -> Network forward pass (U-net with random noise) -> Loss computation (physics-informed with HPF and TV) -> Backpropagation -> Weight update

- Critical path: Data acquisition → Pre-processing → Network forward pass → Loss computation → Backpropagation → Weight update

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fewer pre-processing iterations (100 vs 1000) for speed vs. potential loss in projection quality
  - Random noise input for self-supervised learning vs. need for sufficient network capacity
  - High-pass filtering for high-frequency bias vs. potential amplification of noise
  - TV regularization for artifact suppression vs. potential over-smoothing of fine features

- Failure signatures:
  - Misaligned projections leading to ghosting artifacts
  - Over-regularization causing loss of fine features
  - Under-regularization allowing spurious high-frequency artifacts
  - Insufficient network capacity failing to capture complex IC structures

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test pre-processing pipeline with synthetic data to verify projection alignment and quality
  2. Train network on a small subset of projections to verify convergence and loss behavior
  3. Compare reconstructions with varying regularization strengths to find optimal balance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How would ADePt perform on different types of specimens beyond integrated circuits, particularly those with varying geometric properties?

  - Why unresolved: The current study only tested the method on integrated circuits with flat geometry, and the performance on specimens with different structural characteristics remains unknown.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of ADePt on various specimen types (biological samples, porous materials, irregularly shaped objects) with different geometric properties and material contrasts.

- How would the performance of ADePt change if an attentional scheme were incorporated to weight different projections differently?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "In previous work [41] we have demonstrated that there is benefit to weighing each [projection] differently, according to an attentional scheme [42,43]."
  - Why unresolved: The current ADePt implementation treats all projections as equal, but the authors acknowledge that weighting projections differently could improve performance.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of ADePt with and without attention mechanisms across various sampling conditions and specimen types.

- Would coordinate-based learning methods provide better reconstruction quality and/or allow larger reconstruction volumes compared to the current convolutional neural network approach?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes "coordinate-based learning methods [32–35] may be beneficial to further increase the reconstruction volume as the methods generally take fewer trainable parameters than convolutional neural network architectures."
  - Why unresolved: The current implementation uses a modified U-net architecture, but the potential benefits of coordinate-based approaches have not been explored in this work.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison of ADePt with coordinate-based learning implementations, measuring reconstruction quality, computational efficiency, and maximum achievable reconstruction volume.
