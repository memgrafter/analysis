# Few-Shot Structured Policy Learning for Multi-Domain and Multi-Task Dialogues
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11199
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11199
- Reference count: 13
- GNNs achieve >80% success rates with 50 dialogues when trained on simulated experts

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the sample efficiency of graph neural networks (GNNs) for structured policy learning in multi-domain and multi-task dialogue systems. The authors evaluate how learning from human expert demonstrations compares to learning from simulated experts, finding that GNNs perform remarkably well with minimal training data from simulators but experience significant performance degradation with human data. This highlights potential challenges in capturing the variability and complexity of human interaction strategies within current structured policy frameworks.

## Method Summary
The paper employs graph neural networks as structured policies for multi-domain and multi-task dialogue management. The experimental setup involves training GNNs on both human expert demonstrations and simulated expert dialogues, then evaluating their success rates across various dialogue scenarios. The comparison focuses on few-shot learning scenarios where models are trained on limited dialogue datasets (as few as 50 dialogues). The evaluation measures task completion success rates to quantify the effectiveness of different training data sources.

## Key Results
- GNNs achieve success rates above 80% when trained on only 50 simulated expert dialogues
- Performance drops significantly when training on human expert demonstrations instead of simulated data
- The results indicate a gap between human interaction patterns and what current GNN architectures can effectively model

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that graph neural networks can effectively capture the structural relationships in dialogue state spaces when trained on consistent, rule-based simulated data. The success with simulated experts suggests that GNNs excel at modeling the formal structure of multi-domain dialogues when the underlying patterns are regular and predictable. However, the performance degradation with human data indicates that GNNs struggle to generalize across the inherent variability and non-deterministic aspects of human decision-making in dialogue contexts.

## Limitations
- The paper does not analyze what specific aspects of human dialogue variability cause performance degradation
- No exploration of techniques to bridge the gap between simulated and human data distributions
- Limited investigation into whether the GNN architecture itself is the limiting factor or just the training data
