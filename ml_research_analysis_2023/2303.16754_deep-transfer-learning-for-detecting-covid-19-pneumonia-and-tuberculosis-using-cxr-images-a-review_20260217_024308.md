# Deep transfer learning for detecting Covid-19, Pneumonia and Tuberculosis using CXR images -- A Review

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16754
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16754
- Reference count: 40
- This paper reviews deep transfer learning techniques for detecting COVID-19, pneumonia, and tuberculosis from chest X-ray images.

## Executive Summary
This paper reviews deep transfer learning (DTL) techniques for detecting COVID-19, pneumonia, and tuberculosis from chest X-ray (CXR) images. The review synthesizes findings from over 60 scientific articles published between 2016 and 2023, highlighting that DTL approaches such as ResNet, VGG16, DenseNet, and InceptionV3 have shown high classification accuracies (often >90%) in detecting lung diseases. Key advantages of DTL include reduced need for large labeled datasets, shorter training times, and improved performance compared to traditional deep learning or machine learning methods. The paper also identifies challenges such as small dataset sizes, class imbalance, and limited focus on multi-disease detection.

## Method Summary
The paper synthesizes findings from over 60 scientific articles on deep transfer learning for CXR-based lung disease detection. The review covers studies using pre-trained CNN models (ResNet, VGG16, DenseNet, InceptionV3) that were fine-tuned on various CXR datasets containing COVID-19, pneumonia, and tuberculosis cases. The analysis focuses on classification accuracies, dataset characteristics, and challenges faced in the field.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deep transfer learning leverages pre-trained CNN models (e.g., ResNet, VGG16) to extract relevant features from chest X-ray images, achieving high classification accuracy (>90%) for lung diseases.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained models trained on large image datasets (e.g., ImageNet) have learned general image features (edges, textures, shapes) that are transferable to medical image tasks. By fine-tuning these models on smaller, labeled CXR datasets, the model adapts learned features to detect disease-specific patterns like lung opacities, consolidations, and pleural effusions.
- Core assumption: The features learned from natural images are sufficiently similar to those needed for medical image analysis, and fine-tuning can bridge domain gaps.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "DTL approaches such as ResNet, VGG16, DenseNet, and InceptionV3 have shown high classification accuracies (often >90%) in detecting lung diseases."
  - [section] "DTL does not involve training models from scratch, however, it uses pre-trained models similarly to the ones trained on classical DL models like Convolution Neural Network(CNN) to solve one problem, and then, use them to solve different but related problems."
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus neighbors focus on Vision Transformers and other architectures but lack direct evidence on transfer learning performance claims.
- Break condition: If the domain gap between natural images and CXR images is too large (e.g., drastically different feature distributions), or if fine-tuning fails to adapt features sufficiently for the target task.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Transfer learning reduces the need for large labeled datasets and shortens training time compared to training deep models from scratch.
- Mechanism: By initializing model weights from a pre-trained network, transfer learning starts from a good feature representation, requiring fewer training samples to achieve high performance. The model only needs to adjust final layers for the specific classification task, drastically reducing training epochs.
- Core assumption: A pre-trained model's weights provide a better starting point than random initialization for related tasks, and the target dataset is sufficiently representative.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Key advantages of DTL include reduced need for large labeled datasets, shorter training times, and improved performance compared to traditional deep learning or machine learning methods."
  - [section] "DTL does not involve training models from scratch...These DTL approaches have been widely used in the medical analysis of lung diseases which is evidenced in several works."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct corpus evidence quantifying dataset size reduction or training time savings; claims are inferred from general transfer learning literature.
- Break condition: If the target dataset is too small or unrepresentative, or if the pre-trained model's architecture is poorly suited to the target task.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning strategies (freezing layers, adjusting learning rates) optimize transfer learning performance by balancing feature preservation and task-specific adaptation.
- Mechanism: Early layers of CNNs learn general features (edges, textures) that are broadly useful, while later layers become task-specific. Freezing early layers prevents overfitting on small datasets and preserves useful features, while fine-tuning later layers adapts the model to disease-specific patterns.
- Core assumption: The hierarchical feature learning of CNNs is consistent across domains, and selective fine-tuning can efficiently adapt the model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In solving domain problems, DTL adopts strategies like freezing layers, fine tuning and usage of fixed feature extractor (freeze all layers except for final fully connected layer)."
  - [corpus] Weak: No explicit corpus evidence on fine-tuning strategies for this specific application; claim is based on standard deep learning practice.
- Break condition: If the disease-specific features are distributed across all layers, or if the frozen layers contain irrelevant or misleading features for the target task.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing: Image resizing (e.g., 224x224), normalization, histogram equalization (to reduce bias and improve contrast)
  - Feature extraction: Pre-trained CNN backbone (ResNet, VGG16, DenseNet, InceptionV3) with initial layers frozen or fine-tuned
  - Classification head: Fully connected layers adapted for the specific task (e.g., 3-class classification for COVID-19, pneumonia, tuberculosis)
  - Training pipeline: Data augmentation (flipping, rotating, zooming), loss function (e.g., categorical cross-entropy), optimizer (e.g., Adam), evaluation metrics (accuracy, AUC-ROC, F1-score)
  - Deployment: Model inference on new CXR images, integration with clinical workflow

- Critical path:
  1. Data collection and preprocessing (including augmentation)
  2. Model selection and initialization (pre-trained CNN)
  3. Fine-tuning strategy (layer freezing, learning rate scheduling)
  4. Training and validation (monitor for overfitting, class imbalance)
  5. Evaluation and deployment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model complexity vs. dataset size: Larger, more complex models (e.g., DenseNet) may overfit small datasets; simpler models (e.g., ResNet-18) may underfit complex patterns
  - Fine-tuning vs. feature extraction: Fine-tuning all layers may improve accuracy but increase training time and overfitting risk; feature extraction is faster but may limit adaptation
  - Class imbalance handling: Techniques like weighted loss, oversampling, or data augmentation are needed to prevent biased models

- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: High training accuracy but low validation accuracy; training loss plateaus while validation loss increases
  - Underfitting: Low training and validation accuracy; both losses decrease slowly
  - Class imbalance: Model consistently predicts the majority class; confusion matrix shows poor performance on minority classes
  - Data leakage: Validation/test performance much higher than expected; check for data split integrity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline experiment: Train a simple CNN from scratch on the CXR dataset; compare performance to transfer learning baseline
  2. Fine-tuning experiment: Use a pre-trained ResNet-50, freeze early layers, fine-tune final layers; compare to full fine-tuning
  3. Data augmentation experiment: Apply aggressive data augmentation (rotation, zoom, contrast changes) to the CXR dataset; evaluate impact on model robustness and generalization
