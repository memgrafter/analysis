# A Survey of Quantum-Cognitively Inspired Sentiment Analysis Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03608
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03608
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comprehensive review of quantum-cognitively inspired sentiment analysis models leveraging quantum probability and deep learning to address ambiguity, multimodality, and interrelatedness challenges.

## Executive Summary
This survey comprehensively reviews quantum-cognitively inspired sentiment analysis models that employ quantum probability theory and complex-valued Hilbert space representations to address key challenges in sentiment analysis. The surveyed models use quantum concepts like superposition, measurement, interference, entanglement, and incompatibility to model cognitive aspects of sentiment analysis, achieving competitive performance compared to classical models while offering improved interpretability. The work identifies limitations and outlines future research directions including quantum computer adaptations and benchmark development.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes recent quantum-cognitively inspired sentiment analysis models through systematic review of literature, focusing on how quantum probability theory and complex-valued representations address ambiguity, conversationality, multimodality, and interrelatedness in sentiment analysis. It examines theoretical foundations, architectural components, and experimental results across multiple quantum-inspired approaches, organizing findings by specific sentiment analysis challenges and quantum mechanisms employed.

## Key Results
- Quantum-inspired models encode sentiment information into complex-valued Hilbert space representations
- Models achieve competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art classical approaches
- Survey identifies key research directions including quantum computer adaptations and benchmark development

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Quantum probability theory provides a more general formalism than classical probability, allowing non-commutative events and thus modeling incompatibility in human cognitive processes.
- Mechanism: The paper uses Hilbert space representations where incompatible sentiment aspects are modeled as measurements on separate subspaces, preventing their joint evaluation without considering order.
- Core assumption: Human cognitive decisions violate the law of total probability and exhibit order effects analogous to quantum measurements.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "encode sentiment information into complex-valued Hilbert space representations and employ quantum concepts like superposition, measurement, interference, entanglement, and incompatibility to model cognitive aspects"
  - [section 2.2] "Quantum incompatibility indicates that the measurements cannot be accessed jointly without interfering with each other"
- Break condition: If sentiment aspects can be evaluated independently without order effects, classical probability suffices.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Quantum interference enables nonlinear multimodal fusion by modeling the complex interaction between different modalities.
- Mechanism: Multimodal sentiment decisions are represented as quantum states combining textual, visual, and acoustic features, with interference terms capturing their nonlinear interaction beyond simple additive fusion.
- Core assumption: The final sentiment judgment from multiple modalities is not simply the sum of individual modality judgments but involves interaction effects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "quantum concepts like superposition, measurement, interference... to model cognitive aspects of sentiment analysis"
  - [section 3.2] "the two paths interfering with each other affect the probability distribution... This is similar to the human cognition process in which textual and visual information disturb each other"
- Break condition: If multimodal sentiment can be accurately modeled through linear combination, interference modeling is unnecessary.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Quantum entanglement captures nonseparable interactions between modalities, allowing context from one modality to affect interpretation of another.
- Mechanism: Cross-modal interactions are modeled as entangled quantum states, where the state of one modality cannot be described independently of the others.
- Core assumption: Sentiment expressed through different modalities forms an inseparable whole rather than independent components.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "quantum concepts like... entanglement... to model cognitive aspects of sentiment analysis"
  - [section 3.2] "quantum entanglement offers a nonseparable method for modeling the interactions of different modalities"
- Break condition: If modalities can be processed independently without loss of performance, entanglement modeling adds unnecessary complexity.

  - Why needed here: Sentiment and semantics are encoded in complex vectors where amplitude represents semantics and phase represents sentiment
  - Quick check question: How does the polar decomposition of complex numbers separate semantic and sentiment information in the representation?

  - Why needed here: Sentiment judgments are modeled as quantum measurements that collapse indeterminate states to definite decisions
  - Quick check question: What happens to a quantum state when a measurement is performed, and how does this relate to human decision-making?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How can quantum-cognitively inspired models be adapted to run on actual quantum computers?
  - Basis in paper: explicit - "Possible adaptations on quantum computers" section
  - Why unresolved: While the paper discusses potential benefits of quantum computers for quantum-inspired models, it does not provide concrete approaches or implementations for adapting these models to run on quantum hardware.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Development and demonstration of quantum-cognitively inspired sentiment analysis models that are specifically designed to run on quantum computers, along with experimental results comparing their performance to classical implementations.

- Question: What is the most effective method for initializing the complex phases in quantum-inspired sentiment analysis models?
  - Basis in paper: explicit - "In the text modality, the amplitudes are constructed from the pretrained word embeddings through a deep neural network. The amplitudes of visual and acoustic modalities are obtained via two other deep neural networks from the respective input feature vectors. For the phases of each modality, sentiment polarity is used to initialize arguments for text modality and trained end-to-end."
  - Why unresolved: The paper mentions using sentiment polarity to initialize phases for the text modality but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of different initialization strategies or their impact on model performance.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of various phase initialization methods and their effects on model performance across different datasets and tasks.

- Question: How can the efficiency issues of quantum-cognitively inspired models be addressed?
  - Basis in paper: explicit - "Solving efficiency issues" section
  - Why unresolved: The paper identifies computational challenges due to the use of complex-valued density matrices but does not propose specific solutions or optimizations to improve efficiency.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Development and demonstration of efficient algorithms, hardware optimizations, or approximations that significantly reduce the computational complexity of quantum-cognitively inspired models while maintaining or improving their performance.

## Limitations

- The specific datasets and evaluation metrics used in the surveyed models are not explicitly mentioned, which could affect the reproducibility of results
- Implementation details of some quantum-inspired components, such as quantum measurement and quantum evolution, are not fully explained
- Computational efficiency and scalability concerns when implementing quantum-inspired components in large-scale production systems are not addressed

## Confidence

- **Medium confidence**: Claims about quantum probability theory's superiority in modeling cognitive phenomena, as the theoretical framework is well-established but empirical validation in sentiment analysis contexts remains limited
- **High confidence**: Architectural descriptions of surveyed models, as these are directly extracted from published papers with clear mathematical formulations
- **Low confidence**: Performance comparisons with classical models, as the survey lacks systematic benchmarking across identical datasets and evaluation metrics
