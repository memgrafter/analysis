# Artificial muses: Generative Artificial Intelligence Chatbots Have Risen to Human-Level Creativity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12003
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12003
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: No significant difference in originality between human and AI-generated ideas on the Alternative Uses Test

## Executive Summary
This study compared the creativity of human participants and six generative AI chatbots using the Alternative Uses Test (AUT). Results showed that AI-generated ideas were rated as equally original to human-generated ideas by both human raters and a specially trained AI evaluator. While chatbots produced 2-3 times more ideas than humans, approximately 9.4% of human participants outperformed the most creative AI across all prompts. The findings suggest that generative AI chatbots can serve as valuable assistants in the creative process, matching human-level creativity in everyday tasks.

## Method Summary
The study collected AUT responses from 100 human participants and 5-6 generative AI chatbots (Alpa.ai, Copy.ai, ChatGPT-3, ChatGPT-4, Studio.ai, and YouChat) using five everyday objects (pants, ball, tire, fork, toothbrush) with a 3-minute time limit per prompt. Originality ratings were obtained from 6 human raters and 1 AI trained for AUT assessment, using a 1-5 scale. Data analysis employed linear mixed-effects models with random intercepts and slopes for prompts to compare creativity levels between humans and AI chatbots.

## Key Results
- No significant difference in originality between human and AI-generated ideas on the AUT
- Chatbots produced 2-3 times more ideas than humans while maintaining similar originality
- 9.4% of human participants outperformed the most creative AI (GPT-4) across all prompts
- High correlation (rs = .78-.94) between human and AI evaluator ratings of originality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Generative AI chatbots can produce creative output at human levels in everyday creativity tasks by recombining existing knowledge to generate novel ideas that human raters judge as equally original to human-generated ideas. The Alternative Uses Test provides an objective measure where originality scores from human raters and AI evaluators align. Break condition: If human raters disagree substantially with AI evaluators on originality, or if the AUT fails to capture true creative potential.

### Mechanism 2
Chatbots generate more ideas (fluency) than humans while maintaining similar originality by leveraging vast training data to produce multiple responses quickly, creating quantity without sacrificing quality. Fluency and originality are distinct but related dimensions of creativity that can be independently measured. Break condition: If increased fluency leads to significantly lower originality, or if the AUT doesn't adequately measure idea quantity.

### Mechanism 3
Chatbots serve as valuable assistants in the creative process rather than replacements for human creativity by providing idea generation support while humans retain problem definition and implementation responsibilities. Creative process involves multiple stages beyond idea generation, and human judgment remains essential for contextual application. Break condition: If chatbots can eventually handle problem definition and implementation stages autonomously.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Can generative AI chatbots be used to support Big-C creativity (creative work with far-reaching consequences for a domain or social area)? The paper explicitly discusses whether GAIs can support "Big-C" creativity, noting that while current chatbots excel at everyday creativity tasks, it remains unclear if they can contribute to domain-changing innovations. This remains unresolved because the study focused only on everyday creativity using the Alternative Uses Test, not testing GAIs on complex, domain-altering creative tasks that require extended time, deep expertise, and testing phases. Comparative studies testing GAIs on complex, long-term creative projects in fields like scientific discovery, technological innovation, or artistic movements, measuring their impact on breakthrough achievements, would resolve this.

### Open Question 2
How does the creativity of GAIs vary when given more sophisticated prompts versus simple prompts? The paper notes that "smart prompting is how the best answers are obtained" and that their direct comparison approach likely underestimated both human and AI creativity by not using tailored prompts. This remains unresolved because the study used standardized, simple prompts without prompt engineering, while acknowledging that sophisticated prompting could yield significantly better results. Controlled experiments comparing creativity outputs from simple versus sophisticated prompts, measuring originality and fluency across multiple complexity levels, would resolve this.

### Open Question 3
What is the ethical impact of using GAIs for creative assistance in professional and educational contexts? The paper mentions "the ethical dilemma needs to be properly addressed" regarding potential misuse or harmful applications of GAIs in creative processes. This remains unresolved because the study focused on measuring creativity outputs without examining the broader ethical implications of AI-assisted creativity, including issues of authorship, originality, and potential misuse. Longitudinal studies examining the effects of AI-assisted creativity on professional practices, educational outcomes, and intellectual property frameworks, including surveys of stakeholders and analysis of real-world applications, would resolve this.

## Limitations

- Moderate inter-rater reliability (ICC = .56) between human evaluators weakens confidence in originality measurements
- Potential confound between fluency and originality metrics due to chatbots generating 2-3x more ideas
- Standardized AUT prompts may not capture full spectrum of creative potential for complex problem-solving

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Chatbots produce more ideas (fluency) than humans
- **Medium confidence**: No significant difference in originality between human and AI-generated ideas
- **Medium confidence**: AI chatbots serve as valuable creative assistants
