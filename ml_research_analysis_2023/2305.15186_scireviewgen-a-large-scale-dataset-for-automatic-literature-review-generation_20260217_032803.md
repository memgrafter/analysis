# SciReviewGen: A Large-scale Dataset for Automatic Literature Review Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15186
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15186
- Reference count: 27
- Introduces SciReviewGen, a large-scale dataset for automatic literature review generation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SciReviewGen, a large-scale dataset for automatic literature review generation, consisting of over 10,000 literature reviews and 690,000 cited papers. The authors define the task as query-focused multi-document summarization, using abstracts of cited papers as input and literature review/chapter titles as queries. They propose Query-weighted Fusion-in-Decoder (QFiD), an extension of Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) that explicitly considers each paper's relevance to the queries. Experiments on the dataset show that QFiD outperforms baseline methods, including FiD and Big Bird, achieving ROUGE-1 of 34.00, ROUGE-2 of 7.75, and ROUGE-L of 16.52. Human evaluation reveals that approximately 30% of the generated chapters are competitive or superior to human-written reviews, while highlighting challenges such as hallucinations and lack of detailed information.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce SciReviewGen, a large-scale dataset for automatic literature review generation. They define the task as query-focused multi-document summarization, where the input consists of abstracts of cited papers and the queries are literature review and chapter titles. The proposed method, Query-weighted Fusion-in-Decoder (QFiD), extends the Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) model by incorporating query-weighted encoding. QFiD separately encodes each cited paper, computes the inner product similarity between the averaged paper representation and the query, and applies a softmax weighting to the paper representations before concatenation and decoding. The model is trained on the SciReviewGen dataset using AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 5e-5 and evaluated using ROUGE scores and human evaluation.

## Key Results
- QFiD achieves ROUGE-1 of 34.00, ROUGE-2 of 7.75, and ROUGE-L of 16.52 on the SciReviewGen dataset
- Approximately 30% of generated chapters are rated as competitive or superior to human-written reviews in human evaluation
- QFiD outperforms baseline methods, including FiD and Big Bird, on the literature review generation task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: QFiD improves relevance by weighting each cited paper's encoder hidden states according to the inner product similarity between its averaged representation and the query representation.
- Mechanism: During encoding, each paper is separately encoded, its hidden states are averaged into a single vector, and a soft weighting is applied before concatenating into the decoder. This biases the decoder to focus more on papers that are semantically closer to the query.
- Core assumption: The query and paper representations are in the same semantic space and inner product is a meaningful similarity measure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The similarity between the query and the m-th cited paper wm ∈ R is obtained as the inner product of these vectors... the hidden states of the m-th cited paper are weighted by wm and input to the BART decoder."
  - [abstract]: "We propose Query-weighted Fusion-in-Decoder (QFiD), an extension of Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) that explicitly considers each paper's relevance to the queries."
- Break condition: If the query and paper representations are not well aligned (e.g., due to domain shift), the inner product will not be meaningful and weighting will hurt performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Splitting literature reviews into chapters allows FiD-based models to generate coherent summaries because each chapter is a self-contained topic and FiD can handle multiple documents in parallel.
- Mechanism: By treating each chapter as a separate summarization task with its own set of cited papers and query, the model avoids the difficulty of generating very long sequences and can focus on topic-specific content.
- Core assumption: Each chapter is largely independent in content and can be generated without considering the broader review structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we split a review into chapters and evaluate each of the generated chapters... we assume that each chapter can be generated independently as the first step for automatic literature review generation."
  - [abstract]: "Based on the dataset, we evaluate recent transformer-based summarization models on the literature review generation task, including Fusion-in-Decoder... extended for literature review generation."
- Break condition: If chapters are not actually independent (e.g., if a later chapter depends heavily on context from an earlier one), the model will generate incoherent or redundant content.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The high novel n-gram rate in the target summaries (~30-35%) means the task is abstractive, not extractive, so neural encoder-decoder models like FiD outperform extractive baselines like LEAD or LexRank.
- Mechanism: FiD's encoder-decoder architecture can generate novel phrases and recombine information, matching the abstractive nature of literature reviews better than extractive methods.
- Core assumption: The high novelty indicates abstractive content, and neural models are better suited for that than extractive ones.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Table 1 also lists the percentage of novel n-grams in the target summary... The target summaries in SciReviewGen contain more novel n-grams than those in Multi-News and MS^2, indicating that SciReviewGen is more challenging and suitable for abstract summarization."
  - [section]: "FiD-based models outperform ext-oracle regarding ROUGE-1 and ROUGE-L, which shows the difficulty of our task since simply copying sentences from the cited papers does not work well."
- Break condition: If the novelty is due to errors or hallucinations rather than genuine abstractive rewriting, neural models might generate misleading content.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Query-focused multi-document summarization
  - Why needed here: Literature reviews are about summarizing many cited papers in the context of a specific research question or topic, so the model must condition on query titles.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain why conditioning on the review and chapter titles is critical for generating on-topic literature review chapters?

- Concept: Sparse attention for long sequences
  - Why needed here: The input (multiple abstracts) can be very long (>1K tokens), exceeding vanilla Transformer limits; Big Bird and FiD rely on approximations like sparse attention to handle this.
  - Quick check question: Why does Big Bird's sparse attention allow it to process ~16K tokens, and what trade-off does this introduce?

- Concept: ROUGE metric for summarization evaluation
  - Why needed here: ROUGE scores are the standard automatic evaluation for summarization quality, measuring n-gram overlap between generated and reference text.
  - Quick check question: What does a higher ROUGE-1 score indicate about a generated summary compared to the reference?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Review title -> Chapter title -> Cited abstracts (separated by special tokens) -> QFiD encoder -> Weighted representations -> BART decoder -> Generated chapter
- Critical path: Encode each cited paper -> average hidden states -> compute similarity to query -> weight -> concatenate -> decode
- Design tradeoffs:
  - FiD's per-paper encoding allows parallelism and avoids memory blowup from long sequences, but loses cross-paper attention.
  - Simple inner product weighting is fast but may not capture complex relevance; more sophisticated rerankers could help.
  - Splitting chapters trades off coherence between chapters for manageable generation lengths.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low ROUGE scores and high human factuality issues suggest weighting or query encoding is misaligned.
  - Repetitive or off-topic content indicates chapter independence assumption is violated.
  - Hallucinations in generated text point to lack of grounding in source abstracts.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run FiD without weighting to confirm the improvement from QFiD's relevance weighting.
  2. Evaluate performance when using full text of cited papers (if available) instead of abstracts to see if informativeness improves.
  3. Test joint generation of all chapters in a review (rather than independently) to measure coherence gains/losses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the SciReviewGen dataset be effectively utilized for end-to-end literature review generation, including citation selection and chapter grouping?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that SciReviewGen contains citation information such as citation sentences and citation networks, which can be used as information sources to complement abstracts. It also states that the dataset can be used for end-to-end literature review generation.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments focus on generating individual chapters using only abstracts as input, not leveraging the full potential of the dataset's citation information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating improved literature review generation quality when incorporating citation information, chapter grouping, and citation selection into the model.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of literature review generation models compare across different scientific domains (e.g., computer science, medical, biological sciences)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that SciReviewGen was created in a domain-agnostic way and can be used to create datasets in other scientific fields.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments and dataset are focused on computer science literature reviews. Performance may vary across different scientific domains due to differences in writing styles, technical terminology, and citation patterns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of literature review generation models trained and evaluated on datasets from multiple scientific domains, reporting domain-specific performance metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of incorporating full-text information (instead of just abstracts) on the quality of generated literature reviews?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that only 70% of the cited papers have access to the body text in SciReviewGen, and that adding other input information such as body text, citation sentences, and text of co-cited papers could improve informativeness and factuality.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments use only abstracts as input, which may limit the model's ability to generate detailed and accurate literature reviews.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing literature review generation models trained on abstracts-only versus full-text information, measuring improvements in informativeness, factuality, and overall quality.

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies heavily on ROUGE metrics, which do not capture factual accuracy or hallucination frequency.
- The assumption that chapters can be generated independently may not hold for all literature reviews, potentially leading to coherence issues across chapters.
- The use of abstracts rather than full-text papers limits the informativeness of generated content, as noted by human evaluators.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The dataset construction methodology and statistics (10,147 reviews, 690k cited papers) are well-documented and reproducible. The extension of FiD to include query-weighted encoding is clearly described and implementable.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that QFiD outperforms baselines (ROUGE-1 34.00, ROUGE-2 7.75, ROUGE-L 16.52) is supported by automatic metrics, but the human evaluation results are limited by lack of detail on annotation procedures and agreement statistics.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that approximately 30% of generated chapters are competitive or superior to human-written reviews is based on limited human evaluation data and does not provide information on the distribution of quality scores or inter-annotator reliability.

## Next Checks
