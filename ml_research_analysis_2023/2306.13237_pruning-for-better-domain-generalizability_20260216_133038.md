# Pruning for Better Domain Generalizability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13237
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13237
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: DSS pruning improves domain generalization by 5+ points on MNIST-M and 1 point on PACS/OfficeHome when combined with MIRO

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates pruning as a method to improve domain generalization in deep neural networks. The authors propose DSS (Domain Similarity Scoring), a novel pruning method that evaluates feature similarity between source and target domains to select channels for pruning, unlike typical pruning methods that focus on maintaining source accuracy. Experiments on MNIST-M show that DSS improves baseline performance by over 5 points with 60% channel sparsity. When combined with the state-of-the-art MIRO algorithm on DomainBed benchmarks (PACS and OfficeHome), DSS further boosts performance by 1 point using only 10% sparsity. Results demonstrate that pruning can enhance generalization and robustness when guided by domain-aware criteria.

## Method Summary
The method combines domain-aware pruning with fine-tuning to improve model generalization. DSS computes feature similarity scores between source and target domains for each channel, then prunes channels with lowest similarity scores. After pruning, the model undergoes fine-tuning to recover accuracy. The approach can be applied to any CNN architecture and is particularly effective when combined with existing domain generalization techniques like MIRO. The key innovation is using domain similarity rather than weight magnitude to guide pruning decisions.

## Key Results
- L2 pruning already provides small generalization improvements by reducing model complexity
- DSS pruning improves target domain accuracy by 5+ points on MNIST-M at 60% sparsity
- Combining DSS with MIRO yields 1 point improvement on PACS/OfficeHome benchmarks at only 10% sparsity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Pruning with DSS reduces domain-sensitive spurious features while preserving domain-general features. The DSS score measures feature map similarity between source and target domains at each channel. Channels with low similarity are considered domain-sensitive and pruned. This removes spurious correlations that help on source but hurt on target. Core assumption: Features that differ significantly between source and target domains are likely spurious and should be removed for better generalization.

### Mechanism 2
Combining DSS pruning with MIRO creates complementary generalization improvements. MIRO learns oracle-like representations through mutual information maximization, while DSS removes domain-sensitive channels. Together, they address generalization from different angles - representation quality and feature selection. Core assumption: MIRO and DSS improve generalization through orthogonal mechanisms that can be combined for additive benefits.

### Mechanism 3
Simple L2 pruning already provides small generalization benefits by reducing model complexity. L2 pruning removes channels with smallest weights, implicitly reducing model capacity and potentially reducing overfitting to source-specific patterns. Core assumption: Reduced model complexity through weight-based pruning can improve generalization even without domain-aware selection criteria.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base CNN architecture -> DSS scoring module -> Pruning pipeline -> Fine-tuning module
- Critical path: Forward pass → compute activation maps for source and target → calculate DSS scores per channel → prune bottom-n channels → fine-tune pruned model → evaluate on target domain
- Design tradeoffs: DSS requires target domain data for scoring (unlike MIRO), but provides more targeted pruning. The method trades off some source accuracy for better target performance.
- Failure signatures: If DSS score computation is incorrect, pruning may remove useful features. If fine-tuning is insufficient, accuracy may not recover after pruning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the theoretical explanation for why pruning improves domain generalization performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper observes empirically that pruning methods like L2 and their proposed DSS method improve target domain accuracy, but does not provide a theoretical framework for this phenomenon.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on empirical validation rather than theoretical analysis. While it shows that pruning improves generalization, it doesn't explain the underlying mechanism.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal theoretical analysis demonstrating how reducing model capacity through pruning leads to better domain invariance and generalization, possibly through regularization effects or reduced overfitting to source domain-specific features.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the DSS pruning method compare to other domain adaptation techniques in terms of computational efficiency and performance trade-offs?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows DSS improves domain generalization when combined with MIRO, but doesn't compare its computational efficiency to other domain generalization methods or analyze the trade-offs between pruning-based approaches and traditional domain generalization techniques.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating the effectiveness of DSS but doesn't conduct a comprehensive comparison of efficiency and performance trade-offs.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic comparison of DSS with other domain generalization methods including computational complexity analysis, memory usage, and accuracy-speed trade-offs across multiple benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal pruning strategy (pruning ratio, layer selection, pruning schedule) for maximizing domain generalization across different datasets and model architectures?
- Basis in paper: The paper experiments with different pruning ratios but doesn't provide a systematic analysis of optimal pruning strategies across different architectures or datasets.
- Why unresolved: The experiments show improvements with specific pruning ratios, but there's no analysis of how optimal pruning parameters might vary with architecture, dataset, or domain shift characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation study examining how different pruning strategies (which layers to prune, when to prune during training, how pruning ratios affect different types of domain shifts) impact generalization performance across various architectures and datasets.

## Limitations

- Experimental validation primarily conducted on synthetic domain shift (MNIST-M) rather than real-world scenarios
- Method requires target domain data for scoring, which may not always be available
- Computational overhead of DSS scoring could be significant for larger models

## Confidence

- High confidence: L2 pruning provides small generalization improvements by reducing model complexity
- Medium confidence: DSS effectively identifies and removes domain-sensitive spurious features
- Medium confidence: DSS and MIRO are complementary mechanisms that can be combined for additive benefits
