# ChatGPT is fun, but it is not funny! Humor is still challenging Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04563
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04563
- Reference count: 33
- Key outcome: ChatGPT relies heavily on memorized content rather than original generation for humor, struggles with jokes outside learned patterns, and fabricates explanations for invalid jokes.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines OpenAI's ChatGPT's ability to understand and generate humor through joke generation, explanation, and detection experiments. The study reveals that ChatGPT predominantly reproduces popular jokes rather than creating original ones, with over 90% of generated jokes being the same 25 jokes. The model demonstrates pattern-based humor understanding, relying on structural cues and wordplay rather than semantic comprehension. ChatGPT can accurately explain valid jokes but fabricates explanations for invalid ones, indicating limitations in true humor understanding.

## Method Summary
The study conducted over 1,000 prompts to ChatGPT via the web interface, including requests to tell jokes, explain why jokes are funny, and classify modified joke samples. Experiments were performed in fresh conversations to avoid priming effects. The researchers analyzed joke generation patterns, explanation accuracy, and classification performance across different joke modifications that removed wordplay, topic, or structure.

## Key Results
- Over 90% of 1,008 generated jokes were the same 25 popular jokes, indicating heavy memorization
- ChatGPT accurately explains valid jokes but fabricates explanations for invalid jokes
- Removing wordplay or structure from jokes significantly reduces ChatGPT's ability to classify them as jokes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT's humor output is largely memorized, not newly generated
- Mechanism: The model retrieves and reproduces popular jokes from its training data rather than composing original ones
- Core assumption: High frequency of identical jokes (>90% from a set of 25) implies memorization rather than generation
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Over 90% of 1008 generated jokes were the same 25 Jokes"; [section] "Of 1008 samples, 909 were identical to one of the top 25 jokes"
- Break condition: If distribution of outputs shows high entropy or low repetition, the memorization hypothesis weakens

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ChatGPT's humor understanding is pattern-based, not semantic
- Mechanism: The model relies on structural cues (question-answer format), wordplay, and joke-specific topics to classify humor
- Core assumption: Removing any single characteristic still yields mixed classifications, but removing two consistently leads to "not joke" labels
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "removing wordplay or structure from jokes significantly reduces ChatGPT's ability to classify them as jokes"; [section] "one single joke characteristic alone is not sufficient for ChatGPT to classify a sample as a joke"
- Break condition: If semantic understanding of humor is demonstrated independent of structural features, the pattern-based mechanism fails

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ChatGPT can fabricate explanations for invalid jokes due to its completion bias
- Mechanism: When asked to explain why something is funny, the model generates plausible-sounding but fictional explanations rather than admitting lack of humor
- Core assumption: The model prioritizes completion and coherence over truthfulness in explanatory tasks
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "ChatGPT accurately explains valid jokes but also comes up with fictional explanations for invalid jokes"; [section] "ChatGPT seems unable to contradict that this is not a funny joke or to admit that it cannot identify any pun"
- Break condition: If model consistently flags invalid humor as non-humorous, the fabrication mechanism is invalid

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Statistical pattern matching in language models
  - Why needed here: Understanding that ChatGPT relies on learned patterns rather than semantic reasoning explains why it struggles with humor outside its training distribution
  - Quick check question: If a model learns to associate "tomato" + "red" with humor, what happens when "apple" + "red" is used instead?

- Concept: Memorization vs. generalization trade-off
  - Why needed here: Explains why ChatGPT reproduces common jokes instead of generating novel ones, and why it fails with less common humor
  - Quick check question: If 90% of outputs are from a set of 25 jokes, is this more likely memorization or generation?

- Concept: Completion bias in autoregressive models
  - Why needed here: Explains why ChatGPT fabricates explanations rather than admitting uncertainty, affecting reliability of generated content
  - Quick check question: If a model always completes a prompt, what happens when asked to explain something it doesn't understand?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: To what extent are ChatGPT's jokes memorized versus generated through pattern adaptation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that 90% of generated jokes were the same 25 jokes, suggesting heavy memorization, but also observes modified jokes that indicate some generation capability
- Why unresolved: The paper cannot definitively determine whether output jokes are reproduced from training data or generated, as the model's inner workings are not accessible
- What evidence would resolve it: Access to model training details or ability to trace output jokes to specific training data sources would clarify the memorization vs. generation ratio

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would ChatGPT's joke generation performance change when given specific context or conversational history?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that all prompts were posted in empty chats to avoid priming, but acknowledges context plays an important role in humor perception
- Why unresolved: The controlled experimental setup eliminated contextual factors that could influence joke generation quality and variety
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing joke generation in multi-turn conversations with varied contexts would reveal how well ChatGPT adapts humor to situational cues

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can ChatGPT generate valid jokes outside the specific question-answer format with wordplay that dominates its output?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that all generated jokes follow the same question-answer template with wordplay puns, suggesting a limited conception of humor
- Why unresolved: Experiments only tested one specific joke format, leaving open whether ChatGPT can produce other valid humor structures
- What evidence would resolve it: Prompting ChatGPT to generate different joke types (anecdotes, situational humor, observational comedy) would reveal the breadth of its humor generation capabilities

## Limitations

- Unknown training data composition prevents definitive attribution of joke outputs to memorization vs. generation
- Humor evaluation involves inherent subjectivity, and the study relies on manual analysis without establishing inter-rater reliability
- Experiments conducted in fresh conversations may not reflect how ChatGPT performs in multi-turn conversational contexts

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The observation that ChatGPT reproduces a narrow set of popular jokes with high frequency is empirically supported by the data showing 909 out of 1008 samples matching 25 common jokes. The pattern-based classification mechanism is also strongly supported, as removing structural elements consistently reduces classification accuracy.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that ChatGPT fabricates explanations for invalid jokes is supported by observed behaviors but relies on subjective interpretation of what constitutes a "fabricated" versus plausible explanation. The study demonstrates this tendency but doesn't provide systematic validation of the truthfulness of explanations.

**Low Confidence**: The broader claims about what these findings reveal regarding the fundamental nature of large language models' understanding of humor extend beyond the empirical evidence. While the study shows pattern matching and memorization, it cannot definitively prove the absence of deeper semantic understanding.

## Next Checks

1. **Corpus Frequency Analysis**: Conduct a comprehensive analysis of humor content in large language model training corpora to determine the actual prevalence of the 25 most common jokes. This would validate whether high output frequency reflects training data distribution or generation bias.

2. **Cross-Model Comparison**: Test multiple language models (including open-source alternatives with known architectures) on the same joke generation and explanation tasks to determine whether the observed limitations are specific to ChatGPT or represent broader challenges for transformer-based models.

3. **Controlled Semantic Testing**: Design experiments that isolate semantic understanding from structural pattern matching by creating jokes that maintain semantic coherence but violate structural conventions, or vice versa, to determine whether ChatGPT can understand humor based on meaning alone.
