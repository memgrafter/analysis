# Reducing Popularity Bias in Recommender Systems through AUC-Optimal Negative Sampling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01348
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01348
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves significant reduction in popularity bias without sacrificing recommendation accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of popularity bias in recommender systems by introducing a novel negative sampling strategy that maximizes partial AUC. The method intervenes in model training by modifying both sample information (model-dependent bias) and prior information (model-independent bias) to guide negative sampling. Unlike previous approaches that treat popularity bias as a trade-off with accuracy, this work demonstrates that reducing bias can actually improve model performance under certain conditions.

## Method Summary
The proposed method modifies the negative sampling distribution during model training to maximize partial AUC while simultaneously correcting for both model-dependent and model-independent sources of popularity bias. The approach uses posterior probability estimation based on order statistics to compute sampling probabilities, with parameters α and β controlling the correction of sample information and prior information respectively. The method is integrated into existing recommender architectures (matrix factorization and LightGCN) through a modified negative sampling algorithm.

## Key Results
- Significantly reduces over-recommended hot item rate (OHR) by up to 50% on MovieLens-100k without compromising accuracy
- Achieves a win-win situation where popularity bias is reduced while maintaining or improving top-k recommendation metrics
- Outperforms baseline methods including RNS, PNS, DNS, IPS-CN, MACR, SAMREG, CausE, and BC LOSS across three real-world datasets

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of partial AUC as a surrogate for recommendation accuracy needs more rigorous validation across diverse datasets
- The zero-mean noise assumption in the bias-variance decomposition may be violated in practical applications
- The method's performance on datasets with extreme popularity distributions is not thoroughly explored
