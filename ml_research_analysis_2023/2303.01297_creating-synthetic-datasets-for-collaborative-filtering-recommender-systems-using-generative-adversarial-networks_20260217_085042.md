# Creating Synthetic Datasets for Collaborative Filtering Recommender Systems using Generative Adversarial Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01297
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01297
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GANRS generates parameterized synthetic datasets for collaborative filtering with distributions and quality measures comparable to source datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GANRS, a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)-based method for creating parameterized synthetic datasets for collaborative filtering recommender systems. The method uses DeepMF to extract dense user and item embeddings, which are then fed into a GAN to generate synthetic samples. A clustering process converts these dense samples into sparse ones, enabling the creation of datasets with specified numbers of users, items, samples, and stochastic variability. Experiments on Movielens, Netflix, and MyAnimeList datasets demonstrate that GANRS generates datasets with distributions and quality measures comparable to the source datasets, offering a flexible way to produce diverse, representative datasets for research and education in machine learning.

## Method Summary
GANRS generates synthetic datasets for collaborative filtering recommender systems through a multi-stage process. First, it uses DeepMF to learn dense user and item embeddings from real CF data. These embeddings are then fed into a GAN, where a generator creates synthetic user-item interactions from random noise, while a discriminator learns to distinguish between real and synthetic data. The generated dense samples are subsequently clustered and discretized to create sparse synthetic datasets with user-defined parameters for dataset size and variability. The method addresses the need for diverse, representative synthetic data in CF research by maintaining statistical properties of source datasets while allowing for controlled variations in dataset characteristics.

## Key Results
- GANRS generates synthetic datasets with distributions comparable to source datasets (Movielens, Netflix, MyAnimeList)
- Quality metrics (MAE, precision, recall, F1-score) of synthetic datasets are similar to source datasets
- Method enables creation of datasets with specified numbers of users, items, samples, and stochastic variability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GANRS works by leveraging the complementary strengths of DeepMF and GAN architectures. DeepMF learns compact, dense representations of users and items that capture latent features from sparse rating data, effectively bridging the sparsity gap. These embeddings contain sufficient information about user preferences and item characteristics to serve as a foundation for synthetic data generation. The GAN then learns to map random noise to realistic user-item interactions by learning the underlying distribution of the embedding space. The generator learns to produce samples that fool the discriminator into classifying them as real, while the discriminator becomes increasingly adept at distinguishing real from synthetic data. This adversarial process results in synthetic samples that closely match the statistical properties of the source data. The clustering discretization step preserves the essential relationships learned by DeepMF while reintroducing the sparsity characteristic of real CF datasets, ensuring the synthetic data remains representative of practical scenarios.

## Foundational Learning
- DeepMF (Matrix Factorization): A neural network-based approach to learn user and item embeddings from sparse rating matrices. Why needed: Provides dense representations that capture latent features in CF data. Quick check: Compare embedding quality with traditional MF methods using reconstruction error.
- Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs): A framework with generator and discriminator networks that compete to create realistic synthetic data. Why needed: Enables generation of new user-item interactions that follow the distribution of real data. Quick check: Monitor GAN training with discriminator accuracy and generator loss curves.
- Clustering Discretization: Process of converting dense embeddings into sparse user-item interactions. Why needed: Recreates the characteristic sparsity of CF datasets while maintaining learned relationships. Quick check: Verify sparsity levels match source datasets after discretization.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Real CF Data -> DeepMF -> Dense Embeddings -> GAN Generator -> Synthetic Dense Samples -> Clustering Discretization -> Synthetic Sparse Dataset

Critical Path:
DeepMF embedding extraction → GAN generation → Clustering discretization → Quality evaluation

Design Tradeoffs:
- Embedding size vs. model complexity: Larger embeddings capture more information but increase computational cost
- GAN architecture depth vs. training stability: Deeper networks may model data better but are prone to mode collapse
- Clustering granularity vs. sparsity preservation: Finer clustering maintains more detail but may reduce sparsity

Failure Signatures:
- Poor distribution matching: Indicates inadequate GAN training or suboptimal clustering parameters
- Mode collapse in GAN: Results in lack of diversity in generated samples, visible as repeated patterns
- Incorrect sparsity levels: Suggests issues in the clustering discretization process

First Experiments:
1. Train DeepMF with embedding size 5 on Movielens 100K, evaluate reconstruction error
2. Train GAN with 3-layer dense networks for 20 epochs, monitor discriminator accuracy
3. Generate synthetic dataset with 1000 users, 500 items, cluster and evaluate sparsity preservation

## Limitations
- Clustering discretization process lacks specific implementation details and threshold values
- Unknown impact of different embedding sizes beyond the reported size of 5
- Sensitivity to GAN architecture and training procedure variations not thoroughly explored
- Limited evaluation on datasets beyond Movielens, Netflix, and MyAnimeList
- Potential computational overhead of the multi-stage generation process
