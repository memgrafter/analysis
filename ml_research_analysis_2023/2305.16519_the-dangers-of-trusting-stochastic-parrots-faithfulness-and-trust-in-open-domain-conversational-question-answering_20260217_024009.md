# The Dangers of trusting Stochastic Parrots: Faithfulness and Trust in Open-domain Conversational Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16519
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16519
- Reference count: 9
- Users prefer unfaithful but lexically aligned responses in conversational QA

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how linguistic phenomena like lexical alignment (repeating user words) and ellipsis (fragments, pronouns) affect trust and preference in open-domain conversational QA. Using GPT-3 variants and a fine-tuned retrieve-and-generate model on TopiOCQA, the authors analyze automatic metrics, human judgments of faithfulness, and preference/trust tasks. They find that GPT-3 variants produce more verbose, lexically aligned, but less faithful responses than the fine-tuned model. Surprisingly, users prefer GPT-3 outputs even when unfaithful, and trust highly aligned responses more, while dispreferring fragments and pronouns. This highlights the risk of systems that appear trustworthy via surface linguistic cues while being factually incorrect, potentially misleading users.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate three system variants: a fine-tuned retrieve-and-generate model, GPT-3 Davinci, and GPT-3 Curie, all on the TopiOCQA dataset. They measure automatic metrics (faithfulness, verbosity, lexical alignment, ellipsis), conduct human preference and trust experiments (100-125 ratings per task), and perform regression analysis to correlate linguistic features with human judgments. The study analyzes 200 test conversations and compares responses across systems using statistical significance testing.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 variants produce more verbose, lexically aligned, but less faithful responses than the fine-tuned model
- Users prefer GPT-3 outputs even when unfaithful, and trust highly aligned responses more
- Users disprefer responses containing fragments and pronouns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The preference for unfaithful but lexically aligned responses suggests users rely on surface linguistic cues (repetition of their words) as signals of relevance and understanding, even when the underlying content is inaccurate. Lexical alignment creates an illusion of coherence and responsiveness that overrides concerns about factual correctness. Conversely, fragments and pronouns are dispreferred because they violate conversational expectations about complete, self-contained responses, regardless of their faithfulness. This reveals a fundamental disconnect between linguistic form and semantic content in shaping user trust.

## Foundational Learning
- **Lexical alignment**: Systems repeat user words to appear responsive and relevant. Quick check: Measure overlap between user and system utterances.
- **Ellipsis**: Using fragments or pronouns instead of complete answers. Quick check: Identify pronoun usage and sentence fragments.
- **Faithfulness**: Whether system responses accurately answer the question based on retrieved evidence. Quick check: Compare response claims against source documents.
- **Conversational QA**: Question-answering in multi-turn dialogue contexts requiring context tracking. Quick check: Ensure systems maintain conversation history.
- **User trust vs. preference**: Users may prefer certain responses but not trust them, or vice versa. Quick check: Separate preference and trust ratings in human studies.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on single dataset (TopiOCQA), limiting generalizability
- Small sample sizes (100-125 ratings) may not capture full behavioral variation
- Limited system variants tested (two GPT-3 models, one fine-tuned model)
- Automated evaluation depends on external tools with uncharacterized performance

## Confidence
- Users prefer unfaithful but aligned responses: High confidence
- Faithfulness correlates negatively with verbosity and alignment: High confidence
- Causal mechanisms linking features to trust: Medium confidence
- Generalizability across domains and systems: Low confidence
