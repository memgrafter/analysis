# A Survey of Explainable AI in Deep Visual Modeling: Methods and Metrics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.13445
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13445
- **Reference count:** 1
- **Primary result:** First comprehensive survey organizing explainable AI methods and evaluation metrics for deep visual models

## Executive Summary
This survey presents the first systematic organization of methods and metrics for explainable deep visual models. The paper categorizes approaches into inherently explainable model design and post-hoc explanation techniques, providing a taxonomy that covers model-agnostic methods like LIME and SHAP, neuron activation-based methods such as CAM and Grad-CAM, and backpropagation-based techniques including smoothGrad and integrated gradients. The survey also organizes evaluation metrics by the properties they measure, including model fidelity, localization ability, stability, conciseness, sanity preservation, and axiomatic properties. Key challenges identified include the fundamental limitation that post-hoc methods do not address model opacity, the distinction between correlation and causation in explanations, and the need for more comprehensive evaluation frameworks.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically organizes explainable AI approaches into two main paradigms: inherently explainable models designed from scratch using interpretable components or generalized additive models, and post-hoc explanation techniques applied to existing black-box models. The taxonomy distinguishes between model-agnostic methods (LIME, SHAP), neuron activation-based methods (CAM, Grad-CAM), and backpropagation-based methods (smoothGrad, integrated gradients). Evaluation metrics are categorized by the properties they assess, including model fidelity measures (Pixel Flipping, ROAR, ROAD), localization ability (Pointing Game, attribution localization), stability measures (Lipschitz continuity, sensitivity), and other properties like conciseness and sanity preservation.

## Key Results
- Comprehensive categorization of explainable AI methods into inherently explainable and post-hoc approaches
- Systematic taxonomy of evaluation metrics organized by measured properties (fidelity, localization, stability, etc.)
- Identification of key challenges including post-hoc methods not addressing model opacity and correlation vs causation issues
- Recognition of the need for more comprehensive evaluation metrics in the field

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The survey's systematic approach works by providing a comprehensive framework that bridges the gap between method development and evaluation in explainable AI. By categorizing methods based on their fundamental approach (inherently interpretable versus post-hoc) and organizing evaluation metrics by the properties they measure, the survey creates a structured vocabulary for the field. This organization enables researchers to understand not just what methods exist, but how they relate to each other and what aspects of model behavior they can evaluate. The mechanism succeeds by acknowledging the limitations of current approaches while providing a roadmap for future method development and evaluation standardization.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Explainable AI taxonomy**: Understanding the distinction between inherently explainable models and post-hoc methods is crucial for selecting appropriate approaches based on application requirements. Quick check: Can you classify a given method as either inherently interpretable or post-hoc?

2. **Evaluation metric properties**: Different metrics measure different aspects of explanation quality (fidelity, localization, stability). Why needed: Different applications prioritize different properties. Quick check: Given an application scenario, can you identify which metrics are most relevant?

3. **Model-agnostic vs. model-specific methods**: Model-agnostic methods can be applied to any model, while model-specific methods may be more efficient but less general. Why needed: Understanding this trade-off helps in method selection. Quick check: For a new model architecture, which types of explanation methods are immediately applicable?

4. **Correlation vs causation in explanations**: Current explanation methods often capture correlations rather than true causal relationships. Why needed: This limitation affects the reliability and interpretability of explanations. Quick check: Can you identify situations where an explanation might show correlation without causation?

5. **Evaluation framework standardization**: The lack of standardized evaluation frameworks makes it difficult to compare methods across studies. Why needed: Standardization is essential for method development and adoption. Quick check: Can you describe the key properties that a comprehensive evaluation framework should measure?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Input Data -> Model Architecture -> Explanation Method -> Evaluation Metrics -> Interpretation

**Critical Path:** Input Data → Model Architecture → Explanation Method → Evaluation Metrics
The critical path involves feeding input data through the model to generate predictions, applying the explanation method to understand decision-making, and evaluating the quality of explanations using appropriate metrics.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Inherently explainable models offer transparency but may sacrifice performance, while post-hoc methods preserve model performance but don't address fundamental opacity. Model-agnostic methods provide generality but may be computationally expensive, whereas model-specific methods are more efficient but less flexible.

**Failure Signatures:** Common failures include explanations that highlight irrelevant features (poor localization), explanations that vary significantly with small input changes (instability), and explanations that don't improve with model improvements (poor fidelity). Sanity checks should verify that explanations are consistent with model behavior.

2. Test explanation stability by adding small perturbations to inputs and measuring explanation variation across methods.
3. Evaluate explanation fidelity using Pixel Flipping and ROAR metrics on a diverse set of visual classification tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How to develop evaluation metrics that better capture the causal relationships between input features and model decisions?
- What are the most effective ways to combine multiple explanation methods for more comprehensive understanding?
- How can we create standardized benchmark suites that enable fair comparison across different explanation approaches?
- What are the best practices for selecting explanation methods based on specific application requirements and user needs?

## Limitations
- The categorization of methods into inherently explainable versus post-hoc approaches may oversimplify the continuum between these paradigms
- Many contemporary methods blur the boundaries between different categories, particularly those combining interpretable architectures with post-hoc refinements
- The survey acknowledges but does not fully resolve the fundamental challenge that correlation-based explanations may not reflect true causal relationships in model decisions
