# Analyzing Populations of Neural Networks via Dynamical Model Embedding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14078
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14078
- Reference count: 8
- One-line primary result: DYNAMO learns low-dimensional embeddings where nearby points correspond to neural networks with similar computational processes, enabling clustering, averaging, and optimization in model space.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DYNAMO, a novel algorithm for constructing low-dimensional embedding spaces where each point represents a neural network and nearby points correspond to networks with similar high-level computational processes. The method learns a meta-model that emulates the dynamics and outputs of a collection of pre-trained base models when conditioned on a model embedding vector. This embedding vector serves as the coordinates for that base model in the learned manifold.

The authors demonstrate that DYNAMO enables several novel applications: clustering of neural networks based on their computational processes in a manner less sensitive to reparameterization, model averaging to arrive at new operable networks with similar performance, and semi-supervised learning via optimization in the model embedding space. Using RNNs trained on NLP tasks and CNNs for image classification, they show that the model embeddings capture semantically meaningful structure, such as clustering by training set size or task type. Additionally, they find that optimizing in the low-dimensional embedding space can yield models that outperform all base models, suggesting the meta-model generalizes beyond the training set.

## Method Summary
DYNAMO learns a meta-model (GRU for RNNs, modified ResNet for CNNs) that takes a low-dimensional embedding vector as input alongside the regular input, and aims to reproduce the dynamics and outputs of pre-trained base models when conditioned on their respective embedding vectors. The training procedure optimizes the meta-model parameters, the embedding vectors, and linear transformation maps between meta-model and base-model hidden states to minimize a combined loss over hidden state similarity and output accuracy. The resulting embedding space captures semantically meaningful structure where computational similarity maps to proximity.

## Key Results
- DYNAMO learns embedding spaces where RNNs cluster by training set size, model architecture, and task
- Model averaging in embedding space produces viable new models with performance similar to originals
- Optimization in embedding space can find models that outperform all base models in the training set
- The method is less sensitive to reparameterization than traditional weight-space clustering approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The meta-model generalizes beyond base models by interpolating/extrapolating in low-dimensional model embedding space.
- Mechanism: DYNAMO constructs a smooth manifold where nearby points correspond to similar computational processes. The meta-model is trained to emulate base models' dynamics and outputs when conditioned on embedding vectors. Because the embedding space is low-dimensional and smooth, optimization can find embedding vectors outside the convex hull of base models that yield better performance.
- Core assumption: The high-level computational processes are smooth functions of the embedding vector, and the meta-model can generalize beyond training data in embedding space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "optimizing in the low-dimensional embedding space can yield models that outperform all base models, suggesting the meta-model generalizes beyond the training set"
  - [section]: "We observe that the embedding vector that realizes the highest test accuracy lies outside the convex hull of the base model embeddings"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains papers on dynamical systems and neural networks but none directly address this specific generalization mechanism

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Model averaging in embedding space produces viable new models because nearby points implement similar high-level computational processes.
- Mechanism: DYNAMO learns a mapping where similar computational processes map to nearby points in embedding space. Averaging embedding vectors corresponds to averaging the underlying computational processes, producing a model that performs similarly to the originals.
- Core assumption: The embedding space captures semantically meaningful structure where computational similarity maps to proximity, and this structure is preserved under linear combinations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "model averaging of several neural networks trained on the same task to arrive at a new, operable neural network with similar task performance"
  - [section]: "Given two base models assigned to parameters θ1 and θ2, we can consider the averaged model corresponding to the value (θ1 + θ2)/2. We find that this averaged model performs similarly on the task"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus includes papers on neural network similarity but not specifically on averaging in learned embedding spaces

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Clustering in embedding space is more robust to reparameterization because it groups models by computational processes rather than weight similarity.
- Mechanism: DYNAMO learns embeddings where distance reflects similarity of high-level computational processes. This means models with different weights but similar algorithms cluster together, while models with similar weights but different algorithms are separated.
- Core assumption: The meta-model successfully learns to map models with similar computational processes to nearby points regardless of their parameter configurations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "clustering of neural networks on the basis of their high-level computational processes in a manner that is less sensitive to reparameterization"
  - [section]: "we observe a clear separation of these networks according to the size of the available training data, the RNN model architecture, and the specific NLP task used for training"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains papers on neural network similarity and dynamical systems but none specifically on reparameterization-robust clustering

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base models -> Meta-model -> Model embedding vectors -> Transformation maps Vn -> Loss functions
- Critical path: 1. Train base models on their respective tasks 2. Initialize meta-model and embedding vectors 3. For each training iteration: sample base model, compute hidden states and outputs, compute mapped meta-model states, update meta-model, embeddings, and transformation maps 4. Use trained meta-model for clustering, averaging, or optimization in embedding space
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Output loss weight λ: Higher λ emphasizes matching outputs over hidden dynamics; lower λ emphasizes dynamics over outputs
  - Embedding dimension: Higher dimensions capture more variation but may overfit; lower dimensions provide better generalization but may miss important structure
  - Distance metric: L2 vs L1 for comparing hidden states affects clustering quality depending on representation sparsity
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor clustering: Embedding space doesn't capture computational similarity; check if λ is appropriate and if base models are sufficiently diverse
  - Meta-model fails to emulate base models: Check transformation maps Vn, ensure hidden state dimensions are compatible, verify training loss is decreasing
  - No improvement from optimization in embedding space: Meta-model may not generalize beyond base models; try increasing embedding dimension or adding more diverse base models
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train meta-model on a small set of RNNs with varying training set sizes; visualize embeddings with PCA to check if clusters form by training size
  2. Test model averaging by computing embeddings for two base models, averaging them, and evaluating the resulting model's performance
  3. Optimize in embedding space starting from a base model embedding to see if better-performing models can be found

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the specific architectures of meta-models (e.g., GRUs for RNNs, ResNet variants for CNNs) impact the quality and interpretability of the learned model embeddings? 
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper describes using GRUs for RNN meta-models and modified ResNets for CNN meta-models, but does not systematically explore the impact of architectural choices.
- Why unresolved: The authors present results using specific architectures without comparing alternatives or analyzing sensitivity to architectural changes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing meta-model performance and embedding quality across different architectures (e.g., LSTMs vs GRUs, different ResNet depths) would clarify the role of architecture choice.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can DYNAMO effectively cluster and compare models trained on fundamentally different tasks (e.g., language modeling vs. image classification) when input structures differ?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes a current limitation: "DYNAMO is the need for all base models to have the same input structure" and cannot presently compare models with different encodings.
- Why unresolved: The method requires consistent input structures across base models, preventing cross-task comparisons.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and validating extensions to DYNAMO that can handle heterogeneous input structures would enable cross-task model analysis.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between topological conjugacy of dynamics and the loss function L_hidden used in DYNAMO?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Appendix C discusses topological conjugacy and its relationship to L_hidden, but frames it as speculation about interpreting results rather than a proven connection.
- Why unresolved: The authors suggest topological conjugacy as motivation for L_hidden but do not establish rigorous theoretical connections or prove that minimizing L_hidden leads to topological conjugacy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal mathematical proofs establishing conditions under which L_hidden minimization implies topological conjugacy, or counterexamples showing where this fails, would clarify the theoretical foundations.

## Limitations
- DYNAMO requires access to multiple pre-trained base models with the same input/output dimensions, which may be computationally expensive to obtain
- The method cannot currently compare models with different input structures, limiting cross-task analysis
- While meta-model generalization is demonstrated, the theoretical understanding of when and why this occurs remains incomplete

## Confidence

**High**: The core mechanism of learning a smooth embedding space where computational similarity maps to proximity is well-supported by experimental results showing meaningful clustering and model averaging capabilities

**Medium**: The claim that optimization in embedding space can yield models outperforming all base models is supported by experiments but requires further validation across diverse tasks and model architectures

**Medium**: The assertion that clustering is less sensitive to reparameterization compared to weight-space methods is demonstrated but would benefit from systematic comparison studies

## Next Checks

2. **Scaling experiment**: Apply DYNAMO to larger models (e.g., Transformers) and evaluate how embedding dimension and meta-model capacity scale with base model size
