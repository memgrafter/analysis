# CIMLA: Interpretable AI for inference of differential causal networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12523
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12523
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: CIMLA achieves superior performance in inferring differential causal networks from single-cell data, especially with confounding variables, and identifies novel Alzheimer's disease regulators.

## Executive Summary
CIMLA introduces a novel framework for inferring differential causal networks from observational data by leveraging SHAP feature attribution to measure causal influence changes between conditions. The method trains separate machine learning models for each condition, calculates local SHAP values to approximate causal associations, and aggregates these to produce a global differential score. Benchmarking on synthetic single-cell expression data demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods, especially in the presence of confounding variables. In Alzheimer's disease analysis, CIMLA identifies CREB3 and NEUROD6 as key regulatory drivers, consistent with prior findings, and suggests novel candidates like LYL1 and HOXD1. The approach is robust to technical noise and outperforms both correlation-based and non-linear modeling approaches.

## Method Summary
CIMLA infers differential causal networks by training separate machine learning models (random forests or neural networks) on data from two conditions to predict target gene expression from transcription factor expressions. For each model and sample, SHAP values are computed to estimate local causal influence of each transcription factor on the target. The difference in SHAP values between conditions is calculated for each sample, then aggregated across samples using root-mean-square to produce a global differential score. This score quantifies how much a transcription factor's regulatory influence on a target gene changes between conditions. The method is specifically designed for single-cell gene expression data but theoretically applicable to other high-dimensional biological data.

## Key Results
- CIMLA outperforms existing methods in synthetic benchmarks, especially when hidden confounders are present
- In Alzheimer's disease analysis, CIMLA identifies CREB3 and NEUROD6 as key differential regulators, consistent with prior literature
- The method is robust to technical noise including dropout and library size effects in single-cell data
- CIMLA captures non-linear relationships that correlation-based methods miss while remaining interpretable

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SHAP values estimate a causal estimand under specific assumptions
- Mechanism: By treating feature attribution as a causal inference problem, the SHAP value approximates the local treatment effect (LTE) when averaging over all possible causal structures consistent with the observed dependencies
- Core assumption: The underlying causal graph includes an unobserved confounder affecting all covariates but not the outcome, and the machine learning model can reliably predict the outcome from covariates
- Evidence anchors:

  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on causal SHAP but don't validate the specific estimand claim
- Break condition: Multi-collinearity in the data severely impacts ML model accuracy, violating assumption 1

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Differential SHAP scores between conditions identify causal associations that change across populations
- Mechanism: By training separate ML models on data from each condition and computing the difference in SHAP scores, the method captures changes in causal influence while being robust to confounders
- Core assumption: The ML models are transferable between conditions (positivity assumption) and the conditions differ only in the causal associations of interest, not in the underlying data distribution
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Building on the newfound intuition about the SHAP score as a measure of causal influence, we propose a framework to quantify how much a feature's influence on a target variable differs between two data sets."
  - [section] "Finally, the difference in local contribution scores for the two models is computed for each sample and aggregated across all samples"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus; related papers discuss causal inference but not this specific differential approach
- Break condition: Significant distributional shift between conditions violates the transferability assumption, causing ML models to fail in predicting counterfactual outcomes

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Root-mean-square aggregation of local SHAP differences provides a robust global differential score
- Mechanism: Aggregating local SHAP differences using root-mean-square (rather than mean-absolute) preserves the scale of feature importance and aligns with how linear model coefficients are interpreted
- Core assumption: Features are normalized to have unit variance, making SHAP differences comparable across different genes/targets
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "By extension from this specific case, we aggregate local CIMLA scores 𝛥((𝑥) into a global measure using root-mean-square"
  - [abstract] "Finally, the difference in local contribution scores for the two models is computed for each sample and aggregated across all samples in 𝐷)*$ using a 'root mean square' function"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors don't discuss aggregation methods in detail
- Break condition: When data are noisy and noise characteristics differ across genes, the comparability assumption weakens and the global scores become unreliable

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Question**: How transferable are CIMLA's ML models when applied to observational data from populations with different underlying distributions?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges this as a limitation, stating that CIMLA "employs ML models to impute counterfactuals" and relies on the "strong assumption of transferability of the ML models to data distributions that have not been seen during training."
  - Why unresolved: The authors note that while the transferability assumption is consistent with the positivity assumption, the tradeoff between positivity and conditional ignorability assumptions raises concerns about the practical transferability of ML models.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing CIMLA's performance on observational data from populations with known distributional differences, and developing methods to explicitly account for distributional shifts in the ML models.

- **Question**: Can CIMLA be extended to infer differential gene regulatory networks from more than two conditions?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that "future studies might consider using data from both groups" when aggregating local CIMLA scores into a global measure, and suggest that this could "facilitate the extension of this tool to dGRN inference from more than two conditions."
  - Why unresolved: The current CIMLA implementation focuses on comparing two conditions, and the authors suggest that incorporating data from both conditions in the local interpretation step could improve performance.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of a multi-condition version of CIMLA, demonstrating its effectiveness in inferring differential gene regulatory networks from datasets with more than two conditions.

- **Question**: How robust is CIMLA to different distributions of reference values (x_0) assumed in the causal estimand formulation?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors state that "any of the three stated assumptions can be impacted by problem characteristics and observational data," and specifically mention that "data characteristics, including multi-collinearity, might also adversely impact the distributions assumed over causal structures and reference values."
  - Why unresolved: The authors acknowledge that the choice of distribution for the reference value x_0 can affect the causal estimand, but do not provide a systematic evaluation of CIMLA's robustness to different choices.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing CIMLA's performance under different distributions of reference values, and developing methods to automatically select the most appropriate distribution based on the data.

## Limitations
- Causal assumptions validity relies on absence of hidden confounders specific to certain feature pairs
- Generalizability to data types beyond single-cell gene expression remains untested
- Computational scalability becomes challenging for genome-wide applications with thousands of genes

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using SHAP values as causal influence measures and the differential framework between conditions
- **Medium Confidence**: Performance on real Alzheimer's disease data and biological interpretation of specific gene findings
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about robustness to all types of technical noise and multi-collinearity effects
