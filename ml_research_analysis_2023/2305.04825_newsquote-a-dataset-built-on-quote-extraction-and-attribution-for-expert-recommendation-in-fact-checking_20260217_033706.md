# NewsQuote: A Dataset Built on Quote Extraction and Attribution for Expert Recommendation in Fact-Checking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04825
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04825
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: Document retrieval approach achieves MAP of 0.2903 and NDCG of 0.3590 for expert recommendation

## Executive Summary
NewsQuote is a novel dataset designed to support expert recommendation in fact-checking workflows. The dataset contains 24,031 quote-speaker pairs extracted from COVID-19 news articles, encompassing direct, indirect, and mixed quotations with sources linked to DBpedia entities. The authors propose an automatic pipeline using BERT-based Question Answering for quote extraction and attribution, and evaluate two expert recommendation approaches: document retrieval and expert retrieval. Results show that document retrieval, which retrieves relevant quotes as an intermediate step for expert identification, outperforms expert retrieval in identifying relevant experts for given news topics.

## Method Summary
The authors developed an automatic pipeline for constructing the NewsQuote dataset using BERT-based Question Answering models to extract and attribute quotes from COVID-19 news articles. The dataset construction process involved parsing articles to identify quote-speaker pairs, with sources mapped to DBpedia entities for entity linking. Expert recommendation was formulated as a document retrieval task where relevant quotes are first retrieved, followed by expert identification based on these quotes. Two retrieval approaches were evaluated: document retrieval (retrieving quotes first) and expert retrieval (directly retrieving sources based on query-conditional probabilities). The dataset and pipeline were evaluated using standard information retrieval metrics including Mean Average Precision and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain.

## Key Results
- Document retrieval approach achieved MAP of 0.2903 for expert recommendation
- Document retrieval achieved NDCG of 0.3590, outperforming expert retrieval baseline
- The quote extraction and attribution pipeline successfully processed 24,031 quote-speaker pairs from COVID-19 news articles

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging structured quote-speaker pairs as intermediate representations that bridge news topics and expert identities. By first retrieving relevant quotes based on the news topic, the system can identify experts who are contextually appropriate for the fact-checking task. The BERT-based Question Answering model effectively identifies both the quoted text and its speaker, creating a rich dataset that captures expert statements in context. This intermediate representation allows for more precise expert matching than direct retrieval approaches, as quotes provide specific evidence linking experts to relevant topics.

## Foundational Learning
- BERT-based Question Answering: A transformer model fine-tuned for extractive question answering tasks; needed for accurate quote extraction from complex news text, check by evaluating on quote extraction benchmarks
- Document retrieval vs Expert retrieval: Two different approaches to finding relevant information; document retrieval retrieves intermediate representations (quotes) while expert retrieval directly retrieves sources; needed to compare effectiveness of different recommendation strategies, check by comparing retrieval metrics
- DBpedia entity linking: Mapping textual entities to structured knowledge base entries; needed to standardize expert identities across different mentions, check by measuring entity linking accuracy
- Information retrieval metrics (MAP, NDCG): Standardized measures for evaluating retrieval effectiveness; needed to quantitatively compare different recommendation approaches, check by computing metrics on test collections
- COVID-19 news corpus: Domain-specific news articles about the pandemic; needed to create relevant expert recommendations for fact-checking, check by analyzing topic coverage and diversity

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: News articles -> BERT-QA pipeline -> Quote-speaker pairs -> DBpedia entity linking -> Document/Expert retrieval -> Expert recommendations

Critical path: The BERT-QA pipeline is the critical component, as its accuracy directly impacts the quality of quote-speaker pairs, which in turn affects both retrieval approaches and final expert recommendations.

Design tradeoffs: The system trades off computational efficiency for accuracy by using BERT-based models, which are computationally intensive but provide state-of-the-art performance in quote extraction. The choice to focus on COVID-19 news provides domain specificity but limits generalizability.

Failure signatures: Poor quote attribution accuracy in the BERT-QA pipeline will cascade through the system, leading to incorrect expert recommendations. Ambiguous speaker references or complex sentence structures may cause extraction errors that manifest as noisy or missing quote-speaker pairs.

## Limitations
- Dataset focus on COVID-19 news limits generalizability to other domains and topics
- Automated extraction pipeline may introduce noise or errors in quote attribution
- Performance metrics (MAP 0.2903, NDCG 0.3590) indicate moderate effectiveness with room for improvement
- BERT-based extraction may struggle with complex sentence structures or ambiguous speaker references
