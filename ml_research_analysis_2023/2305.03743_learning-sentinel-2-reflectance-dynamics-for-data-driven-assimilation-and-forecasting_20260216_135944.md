# Learning Sentinel-2 reflectance dynamics for data-driven assimilation and forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03743
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03743
- Reference count: 18
- Key outcome: Self-supervised deep learning approach models Sentinel-2 reflectance dynamics using Koopman operator theory, enabling effective data assimilation for gap-filling and forecasting with 25% MSE reduction compared to Cressman interpolation.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a self-supervised deep learning approach to model reflectance dynamics in Sentinel-2 satellite image time series. The core method involves training a Koopman operator-inspired architecture to predict long-term reflectance changes, using both pixelwise and spatial models. The learned representations enable effective data assimilation for gap-filling and forecasting in irregular time series. Experiments on two French forest datasets show that data assimilation significantly outperforms simple predictions from initial conditions, with mean squared errors reduced by 25% compared to Cressman interpolation. The method also generalizes well to new regions without retraining, demonstrating strong potential for gap-filling in real-world remote sensing data. The authors release their datasets and code for community use.

## Method Summary
The method uses a Koopman operator-inspired deep learning model with an autoencoder (φ, ψ) and trainable Koopman matrix K to capture reflectance dynamics. The model is trained in two stages: first learning short-term predictions (1-5 time steps) then extending to long-term predictions (100 time steps) using curriculum learning. Data assimilation is performed by optimizing the initial latent state to best fit available observations through gradient descent. A CNN correction can be applied post-hoc to address spatial artifacts from pixelwise predictions. The approach handles irregular time series and enables gap-filling and forecasting without retraining on new regions.

## Key Results
- Data assimilation with the learned model reduces MSE by 25% compared to Cressman interpolation on irregularly sampled data
- The method generalizes well to new regions (Orléans forest) without retraining, showing strong potential for real-world applications
- Pixelwise predictions achieve good results, with optional CNN correction improving spatial consistency when needed

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Koopman operator theory allows the model to capture long-term reflectance dynamics by representing nonlinear time series as linear transformations in a learned latent space.
- Mechanism: The neural Koopman architecture combines a deep autoencoder with a trainable Koopman matrix K that advances time in the latent space. The autoencoder encodes the augmented state (reflectance + derivatives) into a lower-dimensional latent representation, where K acts as a linear operator to predict future states.
- Core assumption: The reflectance dynamics of Sentinel-2 pixels can be well-approximated by a finite-dimensional Koopman invariant subspace, meaning there exists a set of observable functions on which the restriction of the Koopman operator is finite-dimensional and captures the essential dynamics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we train a deep learning model inspired from the Koopman operator theory to model long-term reflectance dynamics in an unsupervised way"
  - [section II] "Our approach to learning time series dynamics is based on the Koopman operator theory [8]... which aims at finding a Koopman Invariant Subspace, i.e. a set of observation functions on which the restriction of the Koopman operator is finite-dimensional"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus papers discuss Koopman operators for time series forecasting but don't specifically validate this assumption for satellite reflectance data
- Break condition: The assumption breaks if the reflectance dynamics exhibit strong nonlinearities that cannot be captured by a finite-dimensional linear operator in any latent space, or if the temporal patterns are too complex to be represented by the learned observation functions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The two-stage training approach (short-term then long-term prediction) with curriculum learning enables the model to learn stable long-term predictions that would otherwise get stuck in poor local minima.
- Mechanism: The model first learns to predict 1-5 time steps ahead using a simpler loss function (Lshort), which provides a good initialization. Then it extends to 100 time steps ahead using Llong, building on the learned short-term dynamics. The orthogonality loss encourages K to be close to an orthogonal matrix, promoting long-term stability.
- Core assumption: Learning easier short-term prediction tasks first provides a better initialization for the harder long-term prediction task, avoiding bad local minima that arise from directly optimizing for long-term predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II] "It is crucial to obtain a model that is able to make good predictions over several years, yet the long-term optimisation problem is highly nonconvex, usually leading to a poor local minimum. Therefore, the easier short-term prediction task provides a warm-start initialization, avoiding bad local minima."
  - [section II] "Such a procedure is related to curriculum learning [12], which we believe to be crucial when learning difficult physics-related tasks"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus mentions Koopman operators for time series but doesn't specifically discuss this two-stage training approach
- Break condition: This mechanism breaks if the short-term and long-term dynamics are fundamentally different, or if the model capacity is insufficient to capture the full range of dynamics needed for both short and long-term predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The data assimilation framework allows the model to incorporate sparse and irregular observations to significantly improve predictions beyond what's possible with initial conditions alone.
- Mechanism: By treating the learned model as a differentiable prior, variational data assimilation can optimize the initial latent state to best fit available observations. This is formulated as minimizing the prediction error between model predictions and actual data points, using gradient descent through the entire network.
- Core assumption: The trained Koopman model captures sufficient information about the underlying reflectance dynamics that optimizing the initial latent state can effectively interpolate and extrapolate from sparse observations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The learned representations enable effective data assimilation for gap-filling and forecasting in irregular time series"
  - [section IV-B] "using a learned model, we look for the latent initial condition z* from which the propagation by the model best corresponds to the training data"
  - [section IV-C] "One can conjecture that, although the pseudo-periodic pattern of the reflectance dynamics does not depend on the initial condition in the same way in the Orléans data than in the Fontainebleau data, the model can still identify a known pattern when fed with more data"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - the corpus papers discuss data assimilation with Koopman operators and ML models, supporting this general approach
- Break condition: This mechanism fails if the model's learned dynamics are too far from the true dynamics of the test region, or if the observations are too sparse to provide sufficient constraint on the optimization problem.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input (20-dim augmented state) -> Encoder φ (maps to 32-dim latent) -> Koopman matrix K (advances time) -> Decoder ψ (maps to observation space) -> CNN correction (optional spatial refinement) -> Output predictions

- Critical path: Input → Encoder φ → Latent space → Koopman matrix K → Latent space → Decoder ψ → Output predictions

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pixelwise vs. spatial models: Pixelwise is simpler and faster but loses spatial context; spatial models can capture neighborhood information but are more complex
  - Latent dimension size: Larger latent spaces can capture more complex dynamics but increase computational cost and risk overfitting
  - Training stages: Two-stage training (short-term then long-term) improves stability but increases training time

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor short-term predictions indicate issues with the autoencoder or Koopman matrix learning
  - Good short-term but poor long-term predictions suggest instability in the Koopman dynamics
  - Spatial artifacts in pixelwise predictions indicate need for spatial correction
  - Data assimilation not improving predictions suggests model doesn't capture true dynamics well

  2. Extend training to include long-term loss (Llong) and compare long-term prediction performance with and without CNN spatial correction

## Limitations

- The method primarily operates pixelwise, with spatial structure only incorporated through post-hoc CNN correction rather than directly in the Koopman autoencoder
- Validation is limited to two geographically proximate French forest datasets, raising questions about generalization to diverse ecosystems and climates
- The approach assumes reflectance dynamics can be captured by a finite-dimensional Koopman invariant subspace, which may not hold for all types of satellite image time series

## Confidence

- High confidence in the general methodology and experimental results for the tested French forest datasets
- Medium confidence in the claim that the method works "without retraining" on new regions, given limited geographic diversity in validation
- Low confidence in scalability to global applications without further validation across diverse ecosystems
