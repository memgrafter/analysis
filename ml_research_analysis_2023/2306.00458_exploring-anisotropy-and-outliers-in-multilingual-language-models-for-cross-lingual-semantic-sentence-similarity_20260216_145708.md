# Exploring Anisotropy and Outliers in Multilingual Language Models for Cross-Lingual Semantic Sentence Similarity

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.00458
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00458
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Removing outlier dimensions and applying isotropy-enhancing transformations improves cross-lingual semantic similarity performance in XLM-R and mBERT.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the presence of anisotropy and outlier dimensions in multilingual language models, specifically XLM-R and mBERT, and their impact on cross-lingual semantic similarity tasks. The authors demonstrate that XLM-R exhibits extreme anisotropy due to a small number of outlier dimensions, which negatively affects performance on tasks like Tatoeba and BUCC. By removing these outlier dimensions and applying isotropy-enhancing transformations such as ZCA whitening and cluster-based isotropy enhancement, the model's performance improves significantly. However, a multilingual sentence-transformer, fine-tuned on parallel data, already outperforms these models out-of-the-box and benefits less from these transformations. The study concludes that while anisotropy and outliers can be mitigated through simple techniques, fine-tuning with parallel data remains the most effective approach for cross-lingual semantic similarity tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze sentence representations from XLM-R and mBERT, focusing on the presence of outlier dimensions and anisotropy. They use the TOPS technique to identify and remove outlier dimensions, which leads to improved performance on cross-lingual semantic similarity tasks. Additionally, they apply isotropy-enhancing transformations, such as ZCA whitening and cluster-based isotropy enhancement, to further improve model performance. The study compares these techniques with a multilingual sentence-transformer, which is fine-tuned on parallel data and shows superior performance out-of-the-box.

## Key Results
- XLM-R exhibits extremely high anisotropy due to a few outlier dimensions.
- Removing outlier dimensions and applying isotropy-enhancing transformations improves performance on tasks like Tatoeba and BUCC.
- A multilingual sentence-transformer, fine-tuned on parallel data, outperforms XLM-R and mBERT out-of-the-box and benefits less from anisotropy mitigation techniques.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The presence of outlier dimensions and high anisotropy in multilingual language models like XLM-R leads to suboptimal performance in cross-lingual semantic similarity tasks. By removing outlier dimensions and applying isotropy-enhancing transformations, the model's representations become more uniform, improving their ability to capture semantic similarities across languages. The multilingual sentence-transformer, which is fine-tuned on parallel data, already has optimized representations, explaining its superior performance and reduced need for anisotropy mitigation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Anisotropy:** The property of data distributions where the variance is not uniform across all directions. *Why needed:* Understanding anisotropy is crucial for analyzing the quality of sentence representations in multilingual models. *Quick check:* Verify if the variance-covariance matrix is diagonal or if eigenvalues differ significantly.
- **Outlier dimensions:** Dimensions in the embedding space that contribute disproportionately to the variance. *Why needed:* Identifying and removing outlier dimensions can improve model performance by reducing bias in the representation space. *Quick check:* Use statistical techniques like TOPS to detect dimensions with unusually high variance.
- **Isotropy-enhancing transformations:** Techniques like ZCA whitening and cluster-based isotropy enhancement that make the data distribution more uniform. *Why needed:* These transformations can mitigate the negative effects of anisotropy on downstream tasks. *Quick check:* Apply transformations and measure changes in anisotropy metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Input sentences -> Multilingual model (XLM-R/mBERT) -> Sentence embeddings -> Outlier detection/removal -> Isotropy enhancement -> Similarity computation -> Downstream task evaluation
- **Critical path:** The sequence from input sentences to similarity computation is critical, as it directly impacts task performance.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Removing outlier dimensions and applying isotropy enhancements can improve performance but may also remove useful information. Fine-tuning on parallel data is more effective but requires additional resources.
- **Failure signatures:** High anisotropy and the presence of outlier dimensions can lead to poor performance on cross-lingual semantic similarity tasks.
- **First experiments:** 1) Measure anisotropy in XLM-R and mBERT embeddings, 2) Apply TOPS to remove outlier dimensions and evaluate performance, 3) Compare isotropy-enhanced models with a multilingual sentence-transformer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

## Limitations
- The analysis focuses primarily on XLM-R and mBERT, leaving open questions about other multilingual models.
- Performance improvements from anisotropy mitigation may not translate to all downstream tasks beyond semantic similarity benchmarks.
- The comparison with a multilingual sentence-transformer is based on a limited evaluation, and broader comparisons are needed.

## Confidence

- **Medium:** The claim that isotropy-enhancing transformations improve performance is supported but may have task-dependent effects.
- **Medium:** The conclusion that fine-tuning with parallel data remains superior is reasonable but based on a limited comparison with one sentence-transformer model.
