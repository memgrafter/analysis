# A Study on FGSM Adversarial Training for Neural Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10576
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10576
- Reference count: 31
- One-line primary result: FGSM adversarial training improves robustness of neural retrieval models, increasing dense bi-encoder MRR@10 by 2.25 on MS MARCO and BEIR nDCG@10 by 1.89 on average

## Executive Summary
This study investigates Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) adversarial training for neural retrieval models, focusing on dense and sparse bi-encoders. The key insight is that applying FGSM perturbations during training smooths the representation space and acts as regularization, improving robustness against typos, distribution shifts, and adversarial attacks. The method involves adding small perturbations to input embeddings based on the gradient of the loss, effectively creating augmented training samples. Experiments on MS MARCO and BEIR datasets demonstrate consistent improvements in both in-domain and out-of-domain performance, with particular benefits for queries containing typos.

## Method Summary
The study applies FGSM adversarial training to neural retrieval models by computing perturbations on input embeddings during training. For each training triplet, FGSM calculates the gradient of the loss with respect to the input embeddings and adds a small perturbation in the direction that maximizes the loss. The perturbed and original samples are then both used in the InfoNCE loss function. The approach is evaluated by first training standard models on MS MARCO for 5 epochs, then resuming training with FGSM for 2 additional epochs using a perturbation norm (rmax) of 0.01. Performance is assessed on MS MARCO dev set, TREC DL 2019/2020, and BEIR benchmark datasets using MRR@10, Recall@1k, and nDCG@10 metrics.

## Key Results
- FGSM improves dense bi-encoder MRR@10 by 2.25 on MS MARCO dataset
- FGSM increases BEIR nDCG@10 by 1.89 on average across multiple datasets
- The method prevents overfitting during domain adaptation with limited training data
- FGSM helps improve performance on queries with typos and other variations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FGSM adversarial training smooths the representation space by forcing the model to produce similar outputs for perturbed inputs.
- Mechanism: FGSM computes the gradient of the loss with respect to the input embeddings and adds a small perturbation in the direction that maximizes the loss. This makes the model robust to small changes in the input, effectively regularizing it.
- Core assumption: The model's performance degrades significantly when small perturbations are added to the input embeddings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "FGSM perturbations during training, which smooths the representation space and acts as a form of regularization."
  - [section] "Intuitively, this helps the model to smooth the representation space, and act as a regularization."
- Break condition: If the perturbation norm (rmax) is too large, it could lead to significant changes in the input embeddings, causing the model to overfit to adversarial examples rather than learn robust representations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: FGSM adversarial training acts as a form of data augmentation by creating new training samples from existing ones.
- Mechanism: For each training triplet, FGSM creates a perturbed version by adding a small perturbation to the input embeddings. This effectively doubles the training data, helping the model generalize better.
- Core assumption: The model benefits from seeing perturbed versions of the training data, which helps it learn more robust representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "FGSM helps prevent overfitting during domain adaptation with limited training data."
  - [section] "From another perspective, this can also be seen as a data augmentation, as we simply create one new sample for each original sample."
- Break condition: If the perturbation is too small, it might not provide enough diversity in the training data. If it's too large, it could lead to overfitting to adversarial examples.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: FGSM adversarial training improves the model's robustness to typos and other query variations.
- Mechanism: By adding perturbations to the input embeddings, FGSM forces the model to learn representations that are invariant to small changes in the input. This makes the model more robust to typos and other query variations.
- Core assumption: The model's performance degrades significantly when typos or other query variations are present.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "FGSM increases models performances on both in-domain and out-of-domain distributions, and also on queries with typos, for multiple neural retrievers."
  - [section] "FGSM-AT enables a better adaptation to new domains, even on top of distilled models."
- Break condition: If the perturbation is not representative of the types of noise present in the data (e.g., typos), the model might not learn to be robust to those specific types of noise.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Dense bi-encoder -> Sparse bi-encoder (SPLADE) -> FGSM adversarial training module
  Base model training -> FGSM fine-tuning -> Evaluation on in-domain and out-of-domain datasets

- Critical path:
  1. Train the base model on the original training data
  2. Resume training with FGSM adversarial training for a few epochs
  3. Evaluate the model's performance on in-domain and out-of-domain datasets

- Design tradeoffs:
  - FGSM adversarial training can improve robustness but may also increase training time and computational cost
  - The perturbation norm (rmax) needs to be carefully tuned to balance robustness and performance

- Failure signatures:
  - If the model's performance on the original training data degrades significantly after FGSM adversarial training, it might indicate overfitting to adversarial examples
  - If the model's performance on out-of-domain datasets does not improve, it might indicate that the perturbations are not representative of the types of noise present in those datasets

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a dense bi-encoder on the MS MARCO dataset without FGSM adversarial training and evaluate its performance on the MS MARCO dev set
  2. Train the same dense bi-encoder with FGSM adversarial training and evaluate its performance on the MS MARCO dev set
  3. Compare the performance of the two models on the BEIR benchmark to assess the impact of FGSM adversarial training on out-of-domain robustness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does FGSM adversarial training consistently improve robustness across all types of query variations, including semantic-preserving and semantic-altering ones?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows FGSM-AT improves performance on paraphrasing and typos, but results vary by dataset and query type.
- Why unresolved: The paper tested only a limited set of query variations and datasets; broader evaluation across more diverse semantic changes is needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments on a wider range of semantic-preserving and semantic-altering query variations, with statistical significance testing across multiple datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does FGSM adversarial training compare to other adversarial training methods (e.g., PGD, universal perturbations) in terms of effectiveness and computational efficiency for neural retrieval?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares FGSM-AT to universal-AT and random baselines but does not compare to other adversarial methods like PGD.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on FGSM-AT and universal-AT, leaving a gap in understanding how FGSM-AT stacks up against other established adversarial training techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison of FGSM-AT with PGD and other adversarial training methods on the same retrieval tasks, measuring both effectiveness and computational cost.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the benefits of FGSM adversarial training be transferred to rerankers, and does this improve downstream distillation performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions future work to investigate adversarial training on rerankers and its potential impact on distillation.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on first-stage rankers and did not explore the effects of FGSM-AT on rerankers or the subsequent distillation process.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying FGSM-AT to rerankers and evaluating the resulting performance improvements in both reranking and distillation scenarios.

## Limitations

- The perturbation mechanism's effectiveness depends heavily on the rmax hyperparameter, which was set to 0.01 without systematic sensitivity analysis
- Limited comparison with other adversarial training methods like PGD to establish FGSM's relative effectiveness
- The 2-epoch fine-tuning protocol's generalizability to other training scenarios and base model qualities is not thoroughly evaluated

## Confidence

- Claims about FGSM's effectiveness receive Medium confidence due to implementation dependencies and limited ablation studies
- Data augmentation interpretation receives Medium confidence as the paper frames FGSM as creating new samples without comparing against explicit data augmentation baselines
- Domain adaptation claims receive Medium confidence as improvements on BEIR don't systematically vary in-domain training data amounts
