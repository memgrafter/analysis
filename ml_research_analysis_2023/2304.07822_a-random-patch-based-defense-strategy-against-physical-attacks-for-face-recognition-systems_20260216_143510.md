# A Random-patch based Defense Strategy Against Physical Attacks for Face Recognition Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07822
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07822
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: Nearly 100% detection accuracy against white-box attacks on VGGFace dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a random-patch based defense strategy to detect physical attacks against face recognition systems. The method splits input images into patches (evenly or randomly) and trains a DNN to detect attacked patches. Experimental results show that the proposed defense method achieves nearly 100% detection accuracy against white-box attacks on the VGGFace dataset. The method is also robust against adaptive attacks, including defense-strategy-leaked and defense-model-leaked attacks. The paper demonstrates the effectiveness and simplicity of the proposed defense strategy, which can be easily applied to real-world face recognition systems.

## Method Summary
The proposed defense strategy splits input face images into patches (either evenly or randomly) and trains a detection model to identify attacked patches. The method uses a ResNet-18, Inception-v3, MobileNet-v3-small, or VGG-11 backbone to classify patches as normal or attacked. During inference, an image is rejected if any patch is detected as attacked. The approach is trained using normal images and white-box attacks generated by attacking the Arcface model, and demonstrates robustness against various attack types including adaptive attacks where attackers have knowledge of the defense strategy or model.

## Key Results
- Nearly 100% detection accuracy against white-box attacks on VGGFace dataset
- Robust performance against adaptive attacks (defense-strategy-leaked and defense-model-leaked)
- Random patch division provides better robustness than even patch division
- Method achieves minimal False Acceptance Rate (FAR) while maintaining high True Positive Rate (TPR)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Splitting images into patches disrupts the structure of physical attacks, making them easier to detect.
- Mechanism: Physical attacks typically target specific regions of the face (e.g., glasses or hat areas). By dividing the image into smaller patches, the attack's influence is confined to fewer patches, allowing the detection model to identify anomalies more effectively.
- Core assumption: Physical attacks cannot simultaneously and effectively target multiple small patches.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Splitting an image into small patches can destruct the structure of an attack for the whole image."
  - [section] "Attacking many patches simultaneously is exponentially difficult compared to attacking one whole image."
- Break condition: If the attacker can generate attacks that simultaneously and effectively target multiple patches, the defense mechanism would fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Randomizing the patch sizes and positions increases the robustness of the defense model against adaptive attacks.
- Mechanism: By introducing randomness in the patch division, the defense model becomes less predictable, making it harder for attackers to craft attacks that bypass the detection.
- Core assumption: Randomness in the patch division increases the difficulty for attackers to predict the defense model's behavior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The use of randomness further improves the robustness of the proposed defense model."
  - [section] "Motivated by [21] that defenses with random strategies are more robust than a specific transform."
- Break condition: If the attacker can determine the randomness pattern or generate attacks that are effective regardless of patch positions, the defense mechanism would fail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Training the detection model on both normal images and white-box attacks enables it to detect various types of attacks.
- Mechanism: The detection model is trained to recognize both normal and attacked patches, allowing it to generalize and detect unseen attacks.
- Core assumption: The detection model can learn to differentiate between normal and attacked patches from the training data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We train the detection model only once purely by the normal images and the white-box attacks which are generated by attacking the Arcface model only."
  - [section] "Once the model is trained, it can be used to detect various attacks including the white-box attacks, the defense-strategy-leaked attacks, and the defense-model-leaked attacks."
- Break condition: If the detection model overfits to the training data and fails to generalize to new types of attacks, the defense mechanism would fail.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input -> Patch Division -> Detection Model -> Output (Reject if any patch flagged)

- Critical path:
  1. Input face image
  2. Divide image into patches
  3. Analyze each patch with the detection model
  4. Reject image if any patch is detected as attacked

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Even vs. random patch division: Even division is simpler but less robust; random division is more robust but introduces complexity.
  - Patch size: Smaller patches increase detection accuracy but may increase computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - High false positive rate: The model is rejecting too many normal images.
  - High false negative rate: The model is failing to detect actual attacks.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the detection model on images with known attacks to measure accuracy.
  2. Compare the performance of even vs. random patch division.
  3. Evaluate the model's robustness against adaptive attacks.

## Limitations

- Experimental evaluation limited to a single dataset (VGGFace) with relatively small test samples (526 images), raising questions about generalizability.
- Computational overhead analysis is insufficient for assessing practical deployment in real-time systems.
- Robustness claims against adaptive attacks are based on simulated attacks, not thoroughly validated against real-world adaptive attacks with full defense knowledge.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The basic mechanism of splitting images into patches to disrupt attack structure is well-established and the experimental results on VGGFace are clearly presented.
- Medium Confidence: The claims about random patch division improving robustness are supported by experiments but could benefit from more extensive testing across different attack types.
- Medium Confidence: The assertion that training on white-box attacks enables detection of various attack types is plausible but would require testing against a broader range of attack methodologies.
