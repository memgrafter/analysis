# Sound reconstruction from human brain activity via a generative model with brain-like auditory features

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11629
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11629
- Reference count: 40
- Novel approach reconstructs arbitrary sounds from fMRI activity using brain-like auditory features

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a novel method for reconstructing sounds from human brain activity by combining hierarchical auditory features from deep neural networks with audio-generative models. The approach decodes brain activity patterns from fMRI scans and translates them into high-resolution audio through a transformer-based reconstruction pipeline. The method demonstrates superior performance compared to traditional spectrotemporal approaches and generalizes to sound categories not seen during training, offering new possibilities for understanding and externalizing auditory perception from brain signals.

## Method Summary
The approach involves decoding auditory features from fMRI responses using a sound recognition DNN model, then converting these features into high-resolution sounds using an audio transformer and spectrogram vocoder. The model leverages hierarchical features from a VGGish-inspired network to capture brain-like auditory representations, which are then processed through a transformer architecture to recover temporal information and generate the final audio output.

## Key Results
- Hierarchical DNN features achieved over 85% identification accuracy, outperforming spectrotemporal features
- Reconstruction quality generalized to sound categories not included in the training dataset
- Similar reconstruction quality from different auditory ROIs suggests distributed auditory representations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hierarchical DNN features extracted from VGGish-ish model can be better decoded from fMRI responses than raw spectrotemporal features
- Mechanism: The VGGish-ish model's architecture mimics the hierarchical processing of the human auditory system, producing features at different levels that correspond to different stages of auditory cortex processing
- Core assumption: The VGGish-ish model's feature hierarchy is functionally homologous to human auditory cortex processing stages
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we found that the hierarchical sound features of a DNN model could be better decoded than spectrotemporal features"
  - [section] "the DNN features, although displaying decoding performance similar to the spectro-temporal features in the early auditory cortex, contain higher-level information through hierarchical processing akin to human auditory system"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct comparison of VGGish-ish to other models in corpus
- Break condition: If the model's feature hierarchy doesn't match human auditory processing, decoding performance would degrade

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Audio transformer can disentangle compressed temporal information from DNN features to enable high-resolution sound reconstruction
- Mechanism: The audio transformer acts as a sequence-to-sequence model that learns to map compressed DNN feature representations back to codebook indices representing detailed temporal information
- Core assumption: The compressed temporal information in DNN features can be recovered through autoregressive prediction of codebook indices
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we then reconstructed the sound using an audio transformer that disentangled compressed temporal information in the decoded DNN features"
  - [section] "To convert these features back into their high-dimensional sound form, it is necessary to disentangle the compressed temporal information in DNN features"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus focuses on different transformer applications, not this specific disentanglement
- Break condition: If the temporal information is too compressed or the transformer architecture is insufficient, reconstruction quality will degrade

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Reconstructed sounds from different auditory ROIs remain similar to actual sounds, indicating distributed auditory representations
- Mechanism: Multiple auditory regions contribute overlapping information about sound content, allowing reconstruction from any ROI to capture similar perceptual features
- Core assumption: Auditory representations are distributed rather than strictly hierarchical, enabling multiple ROIs to contain sufficient information for reconstruction
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Reconstructions from different auditory regions remain similar to actual sounds, highlighting the distributed nature of auditory representations"
  - [section] "We observed that the reconstructed sounds bore a resemblance to the original sounds, irrespective of the auditory ROI from which they were derived"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus doesn't directly address distributed auditory representations
- Break condition: If certain ROIs contain critical information not present in others, reconstruction from those ROIs would fail

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Hierarchical auditory processing
  - Why needed here: Understanding why DNN features outperform raw spectrograms requires knowing how human auditory cortex processes sounds in stages
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between how primary auditory cortex and higher auditory areas process sound information?

- Concept: fMRI signal characteristics
  - Why needed here: The approach leverages fMRI's spatial patterns to compensate for temporal limitations
  - Quick check question: How does the hemodynamic response limit fMRI's ability to capture rapid temporal changes in auditory stimuli?

- Concept: Vector quantization for audio
  - Why needed here: The codebook representation is central to the reconstruction pipeline
  - Quick check question: Why use discrete codebook representations rather than directly predicting spectrograms from DNN features?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: fMRI preprocessing -> brain decoder -> DNN feature extraction -> audio transformer -> codebook decoder -> spectrogram vocoder -> audio output
- Critical path: fMRI -> brain decoder -> DNN features -> audio transformer -> reconstruction
- Design tradeoffs: Spatial resolution vs temporal resolution in fMRI, model complexity vs interpretability
- Failure signatures: Poor decoding correlation indicates brain decoder issues; temporal artifacts indicate transformer problems
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify brain decoder performance on validation set before full reconstruction
  2. Test audio transformer with ground truth DNN features (bypassing brain decoding)
  3. Compare reconstruction quality across different DNN layers and ROIs

## Limitations

- The alignment between VGGish-ish model features and human auditory processing remains largely theoretical
- Generalization claims are based on performance metrics rather than comprehensive perceptual validation
- fMRI's temporal resolution limitations may still constrain reconstruction quality despite learned compensation

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The basic feasibility of sound reconstruction from fMRI signals using the proposed pipeline
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about superior performance of hierarchical DNN features over spectrotemporal features
- **Medium confidence**: Generalization to untrained sound categories based on identification accuracy metrics
- **Low confidence**: Interpretation of distributed auditory representations from reconstruction similarity across ROIs

## Next Checks

1. Conduct a human listener experiment to evaluate whether the reconstructed sounds are perceptually similar to the originals, beyond what identification accuracy metrics suggest

2. Test whether the brain decoder trained on one participant can decode fMRI responses from another participant, establishing the generalizability of the feature mapping

3. Compare reconstruction quality when using ground truth DNN features versus decoded features to isolate whether temporal information loss in the decoding step limits overall performance
