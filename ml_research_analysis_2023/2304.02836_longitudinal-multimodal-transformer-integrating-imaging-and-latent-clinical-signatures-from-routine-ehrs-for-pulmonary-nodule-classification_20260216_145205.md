# Longitudinal Multimodal Transformer Integrating Imaging and Latent Clinical Signatures From Routine EHRs for Pulmonary Nodule Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02836
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02836
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: AUC of 0.824 on SPN classification task

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a transformer-based approach for classifying solitary pulmonary nodules (SPNs) by integrating longitudinal imaging data from chest CT scans with clinical signatures extracted from electronic health records. The method uses Independent Component Analysis (ICA) to disentangle latent clinical signatures from longitudinal EHR data, then employs a multimodal transformer with time-distance scaled self-attention to jointly learn from both imaging and clinical modalities. The approach demonstrates significant improvement in classification accuracy over baselines, achieving an AUC of 0.824 on a challenging SPN cohort.

## Method Summary
The method converts EHR event streams into longitudinal curves by accumulating clinical values over time, then applies ICA to extract independent latent clinical signatures. These signatures, along with chest CT scan features, are processed by a transformer-based encoder with time-distance scaled self-attention that emphasizes recent observations. The model is pretrained on a public CT dataset and evaluated on a cohort of challenging SPNs, demonstrating effective fusion of asynchronous imaging and clinical data through the multimodal transformer architecture.

## Key Results
- Achieved AUC of 0.824 on SPN classification task
- Outperformed single-modality baselines and standard temporal attention approaches
- Demonstrated effectiveness of unsupervised ICA-based clinical signature extraction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Time-distance scaled self-attention allows the model to emphasize recent observations over older ones when attending across time.
- Mechanism: The time-distance matrix R is computed as the absolute difference between token acquisition days, then transformed via a flipped sigmoid (TEM) to produce attention scaling factors that decrease monotonically with temporal distance.
- Core assumption: Medical data sampled at different times represents a continuously progressing phenotype where recent observations are more informative for classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Due to the importance of time dynamics in SPN classification, we use the time interval between samples to scale self-attention with the intuition that recent observations are more important to attend to than older observations."
  - [section] "we intuit that if medical data is sampled as a cross-sectional manifestation of a continuously progressing phenotype, we can use a temporal emphasis model (TEM) emphasize the importance of recent observations over older ones."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers address this specific temporal emphasis mechanism in transformer architectures for medical imaging.
- Break condition: If the disease progression is non-monotonic or if older observations contain critical context not captured in recent data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Unsupervised disentanglement of latent clinical signatures via ICA captures independent disease mechanisms that contribute to SPN classification.
- Mechanism: ICA is applied to longitudinal EHR curves to estimate independent latent sources (clinical signatures), which are then used as interpretable features in the multimodal transformer.
- Core assumption: Distinct disease mechanisms manifest independently of one another in a probabilistic manner, and these independent components reflect known pathophysiology relevant to SPN risk.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We perform unsupervised disentanglement of latent clinical signatures and leverage time-distance scaled self-attention to jointly learn from clinical signatures expressions and chest CT scans."
  - [section] "Given that several clinical risk factors have been shown to independently contribute to lung cancer risk, these signatures are well poised for this predictive task."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers demonstrate ICA-based disentanglement specifically for SPN classification in multimodal settings.
- Break condition: If the underlying disease mechanisms are not truly independent or if ICA fails to capture the most relevant pathophysiological features.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The multimodal transformer architecture effectively integrates asynchronous imaging and clinical signature data through token and segment embeddings with time-distance attention.
- Mechanism: Each modality (imaging and clinical signatures) is converted to token embeddings, combined with positional and segment embeddings, then processed through a transformer with time-distance scaled self-attention to handle asynchronous sampling.
- Core assumption: Asynchronous and irregularly sampled data can be effectively synchronized by sampling at scan dates and using time-distance attention to model temporal relationships.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We leverage a transformer-based encoder to fuse features from both longitudinal imaging and clinical signature expressions sampled at intervals ranging from weeks to up to five years."
  - [section] "This is a flexible approach that handles asynchronous modalities, incompleteness over varying sequence lengths, and irregular time intervals."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers demonstrate this specific approach to handling asynchronous multimodal data in transformers for medical applications.
- Break condition: If the sampling frequency is too sparse to capture meaningful temporal patterns or if the time-distance attention mechanism fails to properly weight the relationships between modalities.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: EHR curves → ICA signatures, imaging patches → Token embedding layer → Positional and segment embeddings → Time-distance attention mechanism → Transformer encoder → Classification head
- Critical path: Clinical signature extraction via ICA → Token embedding generation → Time-distance scaled self-attention → Classification
- Design tradeoffs: Computational efficiency vs. temporal modeling accuracy (time-distance attention vs. simple positional encoding); interpretability vs. performance (ICA signatures vs. raw features)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on asynchronous data (indicates time-distance attention issues); lack of improvement over single-modality models (indicates fusion problems); overfitting on small datasets (indicates regularization needs)

  3. Benchmark performance with and without segment embeddings to validate modality fusion

## Limitations
- Relies on assumptions about independent disease mechanisms that may not hold for complex conditions
- Assumes recent observations are always more informative than older ones for disease progression
- Sampling strategy at imaging dates may miss important temporal patterns between scans

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Multimodal transformer can integrate imaging and clinical data; model outperforms single-modality baselines; time-distance attention handles asynchronous data
- **Medium Confidence**: ICA extracts clinically meaningful signatures; TEM appropriately weights temporal relationships; approach generalizes to other multimodal tasks
- **Low Confidence**: Optimal hyperparameter choices; sampling strategy captures all relevant temporal information; AUC improvement translates to clinical impact
