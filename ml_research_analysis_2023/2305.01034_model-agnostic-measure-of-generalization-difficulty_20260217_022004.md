# Model-agnostic Measure of Generalization Difficulty

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01034
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01034
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First model-agnostic measure of generalization difficulty across supervised learning, RL, and meta-learning tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces inductive bias complexity, a model-agnostic measure that quantifies the inherent generalization difficulty of tasks by measuring the information content of inductive biases required beyond what training data provides. The measure is defined as the negative log probability that a hypothesis fitting training data also generalizes well, capturing the fraction of the hypothesis space that generalizes. It scales exponentially with the intrinsic dimensionality of the task manifold but only polynomially with resolution per dimension, providing a quantitative metric to guide the construction of more complex benchmarks requiring greater inductive bias.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a hypothesis space using eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on data manifolds, estimates intrinsic dimensionality and spatial resolution through nearest-neighbor analysis, computes Wasserstein distance between training and test distributions, and applies a formula combining these components to quantify inductive bias complexity. For real datasets, the approach estimates manifold properties from data and approximates optimal transport distances, while for synthetic data on spheres it uses exact geometric calculations.

## Key Results
- The measure successfully ranks tasks by difficulty in intuitive ways (MNIST < CIFAR10 < ImageNet, fully observable MDPs < partially observable MDPs)
- Task difficulty increases exponentially with intrinsic dimensionality but only polynomially with spatial resolution
- Adding Gaussian noise to inputs increases intrinsic dimensionality and task difficulty as theoretically predicted
- The measure correlates with human intuition about task difficulty across diverse learning scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Inductive bias complexity quantifies generalization difficulty by measuring the volume of well-generalizing hypotheses in the hypothesis space given training data.
- **Mechanism**: The measure defines inductive bias complexity as the negative log probability that a hypothesis fitting training data also generalizes well. This probability is proportional to the fractional volume occupied by generalizing hypotheses in the space of all hypotheses that fit the training data.
- **Core assumption**: Hypotheses that fit training data form a low-dimensional manifold within the larger hypothesis space, and well-generalizing hypotheses occupy a small fraction of this manifold.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Our inductive bias complexity measure quantifies the total information required to generalize well on a task minus the information provided by the data."
  - [section 3.1] "Our measure quantiﬁes the fraction of the entire hypothesis space that is consistent with inductive biases for a task given that hypotheses interpolate the training data."
- **Break condition**: If the hypothesis space contains very few hypotheses that fit the training data, the measure becomes undefined or unstable.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Inductive bias complexity scales exponentially with the intrinsic dimensionality of the task manifold but only polynomially with resolution per dimension.
- **Mechanism**: The hypothesis space is parameterized using eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the data manifold, with a frequency cutoff that depends on desired spatial resolution. The dimensionality of this hypothesis space grows exponentially with the intrinsic dimensionality of the manifold but only polynomially with the inverse of the spatial resolution.
- **Core assumption**: The data manifold has a well-defined intrinsic dimensionality that can be estimated, and the hypothesis space constructed using eigenfunctions captures the relevant function space for the task.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "It scales exponentially with the intrinsic dimensionality of the space over which the model must generalize but only polynomially in resolution per dimension."
  - [section 3.3] "Since E∈O(Mm), inductive bias complexity is exponential in the data's intrinsic dimensionalitym."
- **Break condition**: If the data manifold is not smooth or has complex structure not well-captured by Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions, the scaling may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Adding Gaussian noise to task inputs increases the intrinsic dimensionality of the task manifold, thereby increasing the inductive bias complexity and making the task more difficult.
- **Mechanism**: Gaussian noise adds a new dimension of variation to the input space that is not directly relevant to the task. This increases the intrinsic dimensionality of the manifold on which the data lies. Since inductive bias complexity scales exponentially with intrinsic dimensionality, the task becomes significantly more difficult.
- **Core assumption**: The added noise dimension is orthogonal to task-relevant dimensions and does not provide useful information for solving the task.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Our measure provides a quantitative metric to guide the construction of more complex tasks requiring greater inductive bias."
  - [section 4.3] "Figure 3d shows that task difﬁculty drastically increases with added intrinsic dimensions."
- **Break condition**: If the noise is structured in a way that is relevant to the task, it may not increase difficulty as expected.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Inductive bias
  - **Why needed here**: The measure of generalization difficulty is fundamentally based on quantifying the amount of inductive bias required to solve a task.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the difference between inductive bias and the expressivity of a model class?

- **Concept**: Hypothesis space
  - **Why needed here**: The measure operates within the framework of a hypothesis space, which is the set of all possible functions that could be learned for a given task.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the dimensionality of the hypothesis space relate to the complexity of the task?

- **Concept**: Intrinsic dimensionality
  - **Why needed here**: The measure shows that inductive bias complexity scales exponentially with the intrinsic dimensionality of the data manifold.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the intrinsic dimensionality of a dataset relate to the number of features in the data?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data manifold analysis -> Hypothesis space construction -> Volume calculation -> Wasserstein distance estimation -> Task difficulty estimation

- **Critical path**: The critical path involves first estimating intrinsic dimensionality and spatial resolution of the data, then constructing the hypothesis space, and finally computing the volume of well-generalizing hypotheses and the Wasserstein distance.

- **Design tradeoffs**: One key tradeoff is between the generality of the hypothesis space and the computational complexity of working with it. Another tradeoff is between the accuracy of the measure and the ease of computation.

- **Failure signatures**: If the measure produces very large or very small values for tasks that are intuitively easy or hard, respectively, this may indicate a problem with the implementation. If the measure is highly sensitive to small changes in input parameters, this may indicate that the measure is not robust.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify the scaling of the measure with intrinsic dimensionality by constructing synthetic datasets with varying intrinsic dimensionality and computing the measure for each.
  2. Verify the scaling of the measure with spatial resolution by constructing synthetic datasets with varying spatial resolution and computing the measure for each.
  3. Verify the effect of adding Gaussian noise on the measure by constructing synthetic datasets with varying levels of Gaussian noise and computing the measure for each.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does the inductive bias complexity scale with the choice of basis functions used to construct the hypothesis space?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states that a different choice of basis may lead to a hypothesis space with different properties and mentions using eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only uses one specific basis (eigenfunctions of Laplace-Beltrami) and does not explore how alternative bases affect the inductive bias complexity measure.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Empirical studies comparing inductive bias complexity across different basis function choices for the same task.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can the inductive bias complexity measure be extended to the non-interpolating case where training error ε > 0?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper acknowledges this limitation and briefly outlines how the measure might be extended in Appendix G.1.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper leaves a formal extension to non-interpolating hypotheses as future work without providing a complete framework.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A rigorous mathematical extension of the inductive bias complexity definition and estimation procedure to cases where models don't perfectly fit training data.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does the inductive bias complexity measure compare to sample complexity in terms of predicting generalization performance?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper contrasts inductive bias complexity with sample complexity but doesn't empirically compare their predictive power for generalization.
- **Why unresolved**: While the paper proposes inductive bias complexity as an alternative to sample complexity, it doesn't validate which measure better predicts actual generalization performance across different tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Empirical studies showing whether inductive bias complexity or sample complexity better correlates with generalization error across diverse learning scenarios.

## Limitations

- The measure assumes hypotheses form a low-dimensional manifold within the larger hypothesis space, which may not hold for complex neural network architectures
- Estimating intrinsic dimensionality for high-dimensional real-world data remains challenging and could introduce significant error
- The computational cost of exact Wasserstein distance calculations scales poorly with dataset size, requiring approximations that may affect accuracy
