# Instruction Position Matters in Sequence Generation with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2308.12097
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12097
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: Relocating task instructions after input sentences (Post-Instruction) improves sequence generation performance, achieving up to 9.7 BLEU points improvement on zero-shot translation tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the instruction forgetting problem in large language models (LLMs) during conditional sequence generation tasks. When input sentences are long, LLMs risk forgetting the initial task instruction when generating responses. The authors propose a simple solution called Post-Instruction (Post-Ins), which relocates the task instruction after the input sentence. This method emphasizes instruction-following capabilities by altering the model's learning focus. Experimental results demonstrate that Post-Ins consistently outperforms traditional settings across various model scales (1B / 7B / 13B) and different sequence generation tasks (translation and summarization), without additional data or annotation costs.

## Method Summary
The Post-Instruction method involves reformatting instruction-following data by positioning the task instruction after the input sentence and before the response. Models are fine-tuned on this data using teacher-forcing with cross-entropy loss calculated on responses. The approach is evaluated across multiple model scales (BLOOMZ and LLaMA architectures from 1B to 13B parameters) on machine translation and summarization tasks. Performance is measured using standard metrics like BLEU, COMET22, and ROUGE scores, with particular attention to zero-shot performance on unseen translation directions.

## Key Results
- Post-Instruction format consistently outperforms traditional Pre-Instruction format across model scales (1B/7B/13B)
- Achieved up to 9.7 BLEU points improvement on WMT zero-shot translation tasks
- Improvements observed in both machine translation and text summarization tasks
- Benefits extend across different model architectures (BLOOMZ and LLaMA)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Relocating the task instruction after the input sentence reduces instruction forgetting during sequence generation.
- Mechanism: The self-attention mechanism of LLMs prioritizes nearby tokens. When the instruction follows the input, it is closest to the generation point, ensuring the model focuses on the instruction rather than distant input tokens.
- Core assumption: The self-attention mechanism is inherently biased toward nearby tokens, and longer input sequences exacerbate the risk of forgetting the instruction.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Post-Instruction format emphasizes modeling task instruction-following capability.
- Mechanism: By positioning the instruction last, the model is trained to predict the instruction given the input and output, rather than the output given the instruction and input. This shifts the model's learning focus toward understanding and following the instruction.
- Core assumption: The model learns conditional probabilities in the order they appear in the input, and the position of elements influences what the model prioritizes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Post-Instruction format improves zero-shot performance on unseen tasks.
- Mechanism: By emphasizing instruction-following during training, the model becomes better at generalizing to new instructions it hasn't seen before, as it has learned to prioritize understanding the instruction over memorizing specific input-output pairs.
- Core assumption: Zero-shot performance improves when the model has a stronger ability to follow instructions rather than relying on task-specific memorization.

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Prepare instruction-following data in Post-Instruction format
  2. Fine-tune LLM on this data
  3. Evaluate performance on downstream tasks
  4. Analyze attention distributions and zero-shot capabilities
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Simplicity vs. potential gains: The Post-Instruction approach is simple but may not always yield improvements
  - Generalization vs. task-specific performance: Emphasizing instruction-following may improve zero-shot performance but could slightly reduce performance on seen tasks
- **Failure signatures**: 
  - No improvement or degradation in performance on seen tasks
  - Increased hallucination or non-compliance with instructions
  - Reduced performance on tasks where input sentence context is crucial
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement Post-Instruction format and fine-tune a small LLM (e.g., BLOOMZ-1.7B) on a machine translation task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise mechanism by which relocating the instruction after the input sentence alters the model's learning focus and enhances instruction-following capabilities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper provides a theoretical analysis using the trinomial Bayes' theorem to derive the differences between Pre-Instruction and Post-Instruction formats. However, the exact mechanism by which the position of the instruction affects the model's learning focus is not fully explained.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that Post-Instruction format leads to better performance, it does not provide a detailed explanation of how the model's internal representations and attention mechanisms are affected by the instruction position.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of the model's attention patterns and internal representations when trained with Pre-Instruction and Post-Instruction formats could provide insights into the mechanism behind the observed performance differences.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the effectiveness of Post-Instruction format vary across different types of sequence generation tasks beyond translation and summarization?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of Post-Instruction format on machine translation and summarization tasks. However, it does not explore its performance on other sequence generation tasks such as question answering, dialogue generation, or text completion.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on two specific tasks and does not provide evidence for the generalizability of the Post-Instruction approach to other sequence generation tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on a diverse range of sequence generation tasks and comparing the performance of Pre-Instruction and Post-Instruction formats would provide insights into the generalizability of the approach.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of the length of the input sentence on the effectiveness of Post-Instruction format, and is there an optimal length beyond which the benefits diminish?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that Post-Instruction format is particularly effective for long input sentences, as it helps alleviate the instruction forgetting issue. However, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how the effectiveness varies with the length of the input sentence.
- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests that Post-Instruction format is beneficial for long input sentences, it does not provide a quantitative analysis of the relationship between input length and effectiveness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with input sentences of varying lengths and analyzing the performance of Pre-Instruction and Post-Instruction formats across different length ranges would provide insights into the impact of input length on the effectiveness of Post-Instruction format.

## Limitations
- The theoretical analysis of instruction forgetting relies on assumptions about self-attention locality that lack direct empirical validation
- The ablation studies only compare two extremes (instruction first vs last) without exploring intermediate positions
- The paper does not address potential confounding factors such as tokenization artifacts or positional encoding effects

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The empirical results showing consistent BLEU and ROUGE score improvements across multiple model scales and tasks are robust and well-documented
- **Medium confidence**: The theoretical mechanism invoking self-attention locality and conditional probability modeling is plausible but not definitively proven
- **Low confidence**: The claim that Post-Ins is universally superior for all conditional sequence generation tasks, without qualification about task type, input length, or model architecture, is overstated

## Next Checks
1. **Attention Distribution Analysis**: Use attention visualization tools (e.g., BertViz) to directly measure and compare attention weights on the instruction token during generation for Pre- vs Post-Instruction models, particularly for long input sequences where forgetting is most problematic.
2. **Intermediate Position Ablation**: Design an experiment testing instruction positions at 25%, 50%, and 75% through the input sequence, rather than just the extremes, to determine whether the improvement is monotonic and to identify potential optimal positions.
3. **Cross-Architecture Validation**: Test the Post-Instruction format on non-decoder-only architectures (e.g., encoder-decoder models like T5) and with different positional encoding schemes (e.g., relative vs absolute) to determine whether the benefits generalize beyond the specific model class used in the paper.
