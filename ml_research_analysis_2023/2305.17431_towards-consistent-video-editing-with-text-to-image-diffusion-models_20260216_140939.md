# Towards Consistent Video Editing with Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17431
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17431
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: EI2 outperforms state-of-the-art methods on frame consistency and textual alignment, as measured by CLIP score and user study.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of inconsistent video editing when extending Text-to-Image (TTI) diffusion models to Text-to-Video (TTV) tasks. Existing methods introduce temporal modules for video processing, but these harm the editing capability due to covariate shift in feature space. The proposed EI2 model solves this by introducing a Shift-restricted Temporal Attention Module (STAM) that prevents mean and variance shifts, and a Fine-coarse Frame Attention Module (FFAM) that leverages coarse features from other frames to improve temporal consistency while keeping computational cost low.

## Method Summary
EI2 is built by inflating a pre-trained TTI diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1-4) into a TTV model through pseudo 3D convolutions. The model incorporates two key modules: STAM, which replaces Layer Normalization with Instance Centering to prevent mean shift and constrains variance shift via normalized attention mapping, and FFAM, which uses coarse features from other frames to improve temporal consistency while maintaining computational efficiency. The model is fine-tuned on 8-frame videos with text prompts using AdamW optimizer (lr=3e-5, 500 steps) and inference via DDIM inversion with classifier-free guidance (7.5).

## Key Results
- EI2 outperforms state-of-the-art methods on frame consistency and textual alignment
- CLIP score improvements demonstrate better semantic consistency across frames
- User studies confirm superior temporal consistency and semantic alignment
- Computational efficiency maintained through fine-coarse attention mechanism

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The semantic disparity in video editing is caused by covariate shift in the feature space due to newly added temporal modules.
- Mechanism: Newly introduced parameters in the Temporal Attention (TA) module transform the statistics of features, leading to covariate shift. This shift weakens or eliminates the editing capability of the pre-trained TTI model.
- Core assumption: The Gaussian assumption is common and basic in modern neural networks, as it is efficient to characterize feature distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "These modules lead to covariate shift in the feature space, which harms the editing capability."
  - [section]: "We attribute this phenomenon to the covariate shift of generative feature space, as the extra added parameters of Temporal Attention (TA) without any constraint inevitably transform the statistics of features."
- Break condition: If the Gaussian assumption does not hold in practical settings, the analysis and proof of covariate shift may be invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Layer Normalization in the TA module is the prime cause of covariate shift.
- Mechanism: The computation of Layer Normalization across all dimensions fails to properly center the feature along the temporal dimension, resulting in non-zero values for the mean of the feature distribution.
- Core assumption: The Layer Normalization operation is essential but contributes significantly to the issue of covariate shift.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We identify that the Layer Norm in TA module is the prime cause of covariate shift."
  - [section]: "As an essential operation, nevertheless, we find Layer Norm in the TA Module significantly contributes to the issue of covariate shift."
- Break condition: If the Layer Normalization operation is modified or replaced with a different normalization technique that does not cause covariate shift, this mechanism may not apply.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The Fine-coarse Frame Attention Module (FFAM) effectively enhances temporal consistency by leveraging fine-coarse spatial information of overall frames.
- Mechanism: FFAM respects the vanilla attention design to preserve fine information in the current frame but downsamples non-current frames to obtain coarse features for interaction, reducing computational complexity while maintaining temporal consistency.
- Core assumption: The non-autoregressive nature of the diffusion model demands considering global relationships for temporal consistency.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "As the second part, we incorporate STAM with a novel FFAM, which efficiently leverages fine-coarse spatial information of overall frames to further enhance temporal consistency."
  - [section]: "We improve SCA with a novel fine-coarse interactive mechanism to establish an efficient spatial-temporal attention, which considers all frames while achieving low computational complexity."
- Break condition: If the downsampling ratio is too large or too small, the balance between computational efficiency and temporal consistency may be disrupted.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input video clip V = {v1, ..., vn} and text prompt P -> Inflated pre-trained TTI diffusion model with STAM and FFAM modules -> Output edited video with enhanced temporal consistency and textual alignment

- Critical path:
  1. Inflate pre-trained TTI diffusion model into TTV model
  2. Incorporate STAM and FFAM modules into the inflated model
  3. Optimize the inflated model to minimize diffusion loss
  4. Use altered prompt to guide the diffusion reverse process based on DDIM inversion

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational efficiency vs. temporal consistency: FFAM reduces computational complexity by downsampling non-current frames, but the downsampling ratio must be carefully chosen to maintain temporal consistency.
  - Flexibility vs. stability: EI2 introduces additional parameters in the form of STAM and FFAM modules, which may increase the model's flexibility but also introduce potential stability issues.

- Failure signatures:
  - Insufficient editing capability: If the covariate shift is not effectively addressed, the edited video may not align well with the textual description.
  - Temporal inconsistency: If the FFAM module is not properly implemented or the downsampling ratio is not optimal, the edited video may exhibit cross-frame flickering.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study on the Layer Normalization in the TA module to demonstrate its impact on covariate shift.
  2. Comparison of FFAM with SCA in terms of temporal consistency and computational efficiency.
  3. Evaluation of EI2 on a diverse set of video editing tasks, including object replacement and style transfer.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the Instance Centering layer in STAM compare to Instance Normalization in terms of preventing covariate shift in diffusion models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that Instance Centering "preserves the temporal variance of the data" while Instance Normalization "normalizes the data along the sequence dimensions."
- Why unresolved: While the paper claims Instance Centering is more effective, it does not provide a direct comparison between the two methods in terms of their impact on covariate shift.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing the performance of models using Instance Centering versus Instance Normalization in preventing covariate shift, with quantitative metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal scale ratio for the Fine-coarse Frame Attention Module (FFAM) in terms of balancing computational efficiency and temporal consistency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that FFAM reduces complexity to O(n-1+r^2/r^2 nl^2) and suggests that when r is set to 2 or 4, FFAM obtains improved temporal consistency while achieving comparable speed to SCA.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how different scale ratios affect the performance of FFAM, leaving the optimal value unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying the scale ratio and measuring the impact on temporal consistency and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed EI2 model perform on long-form videos compared to short clips?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on 8-frame videos of size 512x512 and does not discuss the model's performance on longer videos.
- Why unresolved: The scalability of EI2 to longer videos is not addressed, which is crucial for real-world applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating EI2's performance on videos with varying lengths, including long-form content.

## Limitations

- Experimental validation lacks ablation studies specifically isolating the impact of Layer Normalization removal
- Computational efficiency claims for FFAM are supported by FLOPs analysis but lack empirical validation across diverse video resolutions and durations
- The paper focuses on 512×512, 8-frame videos, leaving uncertainty about performance at different scales

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The theoretical framework for addressing covariate shift through normalization modifications is well-established in the literature.
- Medium Confidence: The effectiveness of EI2 in improving temporal consistency and textual alignment is demonstrated through quantitative metrics and user studies.
- Low Confidence: The generalizability of the approach to longer videos, different resolutions, and diverse content types remains unproven.
