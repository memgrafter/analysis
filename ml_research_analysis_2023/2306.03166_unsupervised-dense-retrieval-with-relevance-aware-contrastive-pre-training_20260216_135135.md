# Unsupervised Dense Retrieval with Relevance-Aware Contrastive Pre-Training

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.03166
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Primary result**: Proposes relevance-aware contrastive learning for unsupervised dense retrieval, achieving NDCG@10 of 37.8 on BEIR, the best among unsupervised models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces relevance-aware contrastive learning for unsupervised dense retrieval, addressing the challenge of false positives in contrastive pre-training where randomly cropped query-passage pairs may be irrelevant. The method estimates the relevance of positive pairs using the current model itself and adaptively weighs the contrastive loss of different pairs according to the estimated relevance. This approach consistently improves the state-of-the-art unsupervised Contriever model on BEIR and open-domain QA retrieval benchmarks, achieving the best performance among unsupervised models. The method also demonstrates effectiveness in low-resource settings, outperforming BM25 after further pre-training on the target corpus and serving as a good few-shot learner.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a relevance-aware contrastive learning approach for unsupervised dense retrieval. The method generates multiple positive pairs from a single document through random cropping, then uses the intermediate-trained model to estimate the relevance of each pair. These relevance scores are normalized within each document and used to weight the InfoNCE loss, so more relevant pairs contribute more to the gradient update. The approach combines this relevance weighting with a one-document-multiple-pair strategy to ensure effective use of all data while focusing learning on more relevant pairs. The model is pre-trained on Wikipedia and CCNet for 500,000 steps, then optionally further pre-trained on target domain corpora.

## Key Results
- Achieves NDCG@10 of 37.8 on BEIR, the best among unsupervised models
- Consistently improves over Contriever on 10/15 BEIR datasets with gains ranging from +1.4 to +5.4 NDCG@10
- Outperforms BM25 after further pre-training on target domain corpora in low-resource settings
- Demonstrates strong few-shot learning capabilities for new retrieval tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using the intermediate model itself as an imperfect oracle to estimate relevance weights reduces the impact of false positives in contrastive learning.
- Mechanism: At each training step, the current model θ computes relevance scores sθ(q, d+) for all positive pairs. These scores are then normalized across pairs from the same document and used to weight the InfoNCE loss for each pair, so pairs with higher estimated relevance contribute more to the gradient update.
- Core assumption: The intermediate model's relevance estimates, while imperfect, are positively correlated with true relevance and can distinguish more relevant from less relevant pairs within the same document.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It takes the intermediate-trained model itself as an imperfect oracle to estimate the relevance of positive pairs and adaptively weighs the contrastive loss of different pairs according to the estimated relevance."
  - [section 2.2] "We utilize the trained models at the current step itself to estimate the relevance of all the positives. Then the losses of different positive pairs are adaptively weighed using the estimated relevance..."
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus mentions other works on pseudo-relevance labeling but no direct evidence about intermediate model as oracle for relevance weighting.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Generating multiple positive pairs from a single document and normalizing relevance scores within that document ensures effective use of all data while still focusing on more relevant pairs.
- Mechanism: For each document, n+1 random spans are cropped to create n positive (query, passage) pairs sharing the same query. Relevance scores are normalized across these n pairs from the same document before weighting the contrastive loss, preventing documents with many irrelevant pairs from being underutilized.
- Core assumption: Documents contain at least some relevant pairs, and normalizing within-document prevents over-penalizing entire documents due to a few irrelevant pairs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2] "Moreover, simply applying lower weights to irrelevant pairs will result in insufficient usage of data, since many documents will contribute less to training. Therefore, we also introduce a one-document-multiple-pair strategy..."
  - [section 3.4] "Applying the one-document-multi-pair strategy can obtain a slight improvement which can be attributed to the effective usage of the unlabeled data."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for within-document normalization strategy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of relevance-aware weighting and multiple pairs per document provides consistent improvements across diverse retrieval tasks.
- Mechanism: The two strategies work synergistically - relevance weighting focuses learning on more useful pairs while multiple pairs ensure all documents contribute. This combination yields consistent gains on 10/15 BEIR tasks and open-domain QA retrieval.
- Core assumption: The retrieval tasks share enough similarity that improvements on one generalize to others, and that the benefits of both strategies compound rather than interfere.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our method consistently improves the SOTA unsupervised Contriever model on the BEIR and open-domain QA retrieval benchmarks."
  - [section 3.2.1] "ReContriever achieves consistent improvements over Contriever on 10/15 datasets..."
  - [section 3.4] "Combining both strategies (i.e., ReContriever) can lead to an obvious improvement, which demonstrates the necessity of both components in our method."
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence for task generalizability of combined strategy.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: BERT-base encoder → query/passage representation → inner product similarity → relevance score → weighted InfoNCE loss → gradient update. The relevance weighting component sits between similarity computation and loss calculation.
- **Critical path**: Data loading → random cropping → model forward pass → relevance score computation → weighted loss calculation → backward pass → optimizer step. The relevance estimation step is on the critical path for each training iteration.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using the intermediate model as oracle trades off computation (relevance scoring) for better gradient quality. Multiple pairs per document increase data efficiency but require more memory and computation per document.
- **Failure signatures**: If relevance estimates are noisy, training may become unstable or converge slowly. If too many pairs are irrelevant, the model may learn poor representations despite weighting.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement basic relevance-aware weighting with fixed dummy relevance scores to verify the weighting mechanism works without the oracle component.
  2. Add the intermediate model oracle to compute relevance scores and verify they vary meaningfully across pairs from the same document.
  3. Implement the one-document-multiple-pair strategy and test that all pairs contribute to training while more relevant ones have higher weight.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of relevance estimation may degrade when the intermediate model's confidence is low or when documents contain mostly irrelevant pairs.
- The method requires additional computation for relevance score estimation at each training step, increasing training time.
- The approach's performance on highly specialized domains or languages beyond English Wikipedia/CCNet remains uncertain.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of relevance-aware weighting and the one-document-multiple-pair strategy are clearly described and implemented, with direct evidence of their effectiveness through ablation studies.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that this is the first unsupervised dense retrieval method to consistently outperform BM25 on BEIR benchmarks, as this depends on comparing against multiple unpublished methods and specific implementation choices.
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of the approach to highly specialized domains or languages beyond English Wikipedia/CCNet, as the evaluation is limited to BEIR and open-domain QA tasks.

## Next Checks
1. Test the method's sensitivity to relevance estimation quality by systematically varying the number of training steps before applying relevance weighting, measuring how early/late application affects final performance.
2. Evaluate the approach on a held-out domain (e.g., medical or legal documents) not represented in BEIR to assess cross-domain generalization of the unsupervised pre-training.
3. Conduct a controlled experiment comparing the relevance-aware weighting against alternative weighting schemes (e.g., uniform weighting, frequency-based weighting) using identical data augmentation and pre-training settings to isolate the contribution of the relevance estimation component.
