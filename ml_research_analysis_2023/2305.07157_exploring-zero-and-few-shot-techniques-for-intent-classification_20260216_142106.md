# Exploring Zero and Few-shot Techniques for Intent Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07157
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07157
- Reference count: 3
- This paper explores zero and few-shot techniques for intent classification in conversational AI systems, demonstrating that parameter-efficient fine-tuning with T-few recipe achieves best performance even with one sample per intent.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of scaling intent classification models across thousands of customers with limited training data. The authors propose four approaches: domain adaptation of sentence encoders, data augmentation, zero-shot classification using intent descriptions with large language models, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning of instruction-finetuned language models. They evaluate these methods on both public and private enterprise datasets, demonstrating that all approaches are effective in low-resource settings. The best performance is achieved through parameter-efficient fine-tuning of Flan-T5 using the T-few recipe, even with just one sample per intent, while zero-shot classification using intent descriptions with LLMs offers a promising solution for rapid deployment without extensive training data.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate four approaches for zero and few-shot intent classification: (1) domain adaptation using an enterprise-specific sentence encoder (ELMSE) pre-trained on intent taxonomies and synthetic data, (2) data augmentation through LLM-generated paraphrases and intent descriptions, (3) zero-shot classification by prompting LLMs with intent descriptions and utterances, and (4) parameter-efficient fine-tuning using the T-few recipe with IA3 adapters on Flan-T5 models. They test these methods on the public MASSIVE dataset and private enterprise datasets across ITSM, HR, and CSM domains, measuring in-scope accuracy and out-of-scope recall to evaluate both classification performance and robustness to irrelevant queries.

## Key Results
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning of Flan-T5 using T-few recipe achieves the best performance, even with just one sample per intent
- Zero-shot classification using intent descriptions with LLMs is competitive with few-shot approaches, offering rapid deployment without training data
- Domain adaptation of sentence encoders improves few-shot performance by leveraging enterprise-specific semantic structures
- All four approaches demonstrate effectiveness in low-resource settings, providing multiple viable solutions depending on deployment constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Domain adaptation of sentence encoders improves few-shot intent classification accuracy by leveraging domain-specific semantic structures.
- Mechanism: Pre-training a sentence encoder (ELMSE) on enterprise-specific intent taxonomies and synthetic data allows it to capture domain-relevant semantic relationships, which improves the quality of embeddings used for classification.
- Core assumption: The enterprise intent taxonomy captures meaningful semantic distinctions relevant to customer queries, and synthetic data is sufficiently representative of real usage patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We use a sentence encoder that is pre-trained with our domain knowledge and show that it performs well in a few-shot setting compared to off-the-shelf sentence encoders."
  - [section] "Starting with the LaBSE checkpoint we train it further by converting intent classification, paraphrasing, etc, as sentence similarity tasks."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations found, but the general effectiveness of domain adaptation is well-established in the literature.
- Break condition: If the domain taxonomy is too narrow or the synthetic data poorly represents actual customer utterances, the adapted encoder may not generalize well to real-world data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Zero-shot classification using intent descriptions with LLMs achieves competitive accuracy by leveraging the model's understanding of semantic relationships between natural language descriptions and user utterances.
- Mechanism: By prompting LLMs with intent descriptions and user utterances, the model can infer the correct intent based on its pre-trained understanding of language semantics and intent representations.
- Core assumption: The LLM's pre-training has captured sufficient semantic knowledge to map between intent descriptions and utterances without additional fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Zero-shot intent classification using descriptions large language models (LLMs)... works well in practice for zero/few-shot intent classification."
  - [section] "We use intent names and descriptions for prompting language models to perform zero-shot prediction."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the specific approach is novel, though zero-shot learning with LLMs is a known technique.
- Break condition: If intent descriptions are ambiguous, incomplete, or the LLM lacks sufficient semantic understanding for the domain, accuracy will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) using the T-few recipe enables high performance with minimal training data by adding and fine-tuning only a small number of parameters.
- Mechanism: Adding IA3 adapters to instruction-finetuned models like Flan-T5 and fine-tuning them on few examples allows the model to adapt to specific intents without full fine-tuning, preserving most parameters while achieving high accuracy.
- Core assumption: The instruction-finetuned base model has sufficient general capability that can be adapted with minimal parameter changes to achieve high performance on specific intent classification tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Parameter-efficient fine-tuning using T-few recipe (Liu et al., 2022) on Flan-T5 (Chung et al., 2022) yields the best performance even with just one sample per intent."
  - [section] "Taking inspiration from the T-Few recipe (Liu et al., 2022), we add and finetune IA3 adapters from scratch in Flan-T5 models in a few-shot setting."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - PEFT techniques are well-established, and the T-few recipe is cited from Liu et al. 2022.
- Break condition: If the base model lacks sufficient capability or the few examples are too noisy or unrepresentative, PEFT may not achieve competitive performance.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Sentence embeddings and semantic similarity
  - Why needed here: The domain adaptation approach relies on high-quality sentence embeddings to represent utterances in a semantic space where similar intents are close together.
  - Quick check question: How would you evaluate whether two different sentence encoders produce comparable quality embeddings for intent classification?

- Concept: Prompt engineering for LLMs
  - Why needed here: Zero-shot classification depends on crafting effective prompts that guide the LLM to correctly interpret intent descriptions and map them to user utterances.
  - Quick check question: What are the key components of an effective prompt for zero-shot intent classification, and how would you test different prompt variations?

- Concept: Parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques
  - Why needed here: PEFT methods like IA3 adapters allow adaptation of large models with minimal computational resources, which is critical for scaling to thousands of customers.
  - Quick check question: What are the trade-offs between different PEFT methods (LoRA, IA3, etc.) in terms of parameter count, performance, and ease of deployment?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: ELMSE sentence encoder -> dense classification layer -> intent prediction OR LLM prompting -> intent ranking OR Flan-T5 + IA3 adapters -> fine-tuning -> intent prediction
- Critical path: For new customers, the typical flow is: intent description collection → zero-shot classification for initial deployment → PEFT fine-tuning as more data becomes available → continuous improvement through data augmentation
- Design tradeoffs: Zero-shot offers fastest deployment but lower accuracy; domain adaptation provides moderate accuracy with minimal data; PEFT achieves highest accuracy but requires some training examples and compute resources
- Failure signatures: Domain adaptation fails when test data distribution differs significantly from training; zero-shot fails with ambiguous intent descriptions; PEFT fails with insufficient or noisy training examples
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare ELMSE vs LaBSE sentence encoders on a small internal dataset with 5 examples per intent to validate domain adaptation effectiveness
  2. Test zero-shot classification with GPT-3 and Flan-T5-XXL on the same dataset using identical intent descriptions to compare performance
  3. Apply PEFT to Flan-T5-Base with 3 examples per intent and compare against zero-shot results to measure the benefit of fine-tuning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of domain adaptation with ELMSE compare to fine-tuning larger models like GPT-3 or Flan-T5-XXL on the same low-resource datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares ELMSE (a smaller, domain-adapted model) to larger models like GTR-3b and Flan-T5-XXL, but does not directly compare ELMSE to fine-tuning these larger models on the same datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing different approaches (domain adaptation, data augmentation, zero-shot prediction, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning) but does not explore the combination of domain adaptation with fine-tuning larger models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that fine-tune larger models like GPT-3 or Flan-T5-XXL on the same low-resource datasets used for ELMSE, and comparing their performance to ELMSE's results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of intent descriptions impact the performance of zero-shot classification using large language models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates that zero-shot classification using intent descriptions with LLMs is effective, but it does not explore how the quality or specificity of intent descriptions affects performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumes that intent descriptions are provided and focuses on their effectiveness, but does not investigate how variations in description quality impact results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying quality or specificity of intent descriptions and measuring their impact on zero-shot classification accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the long-term performance of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) with the T-few recipe compared to full fine-tuning of larger models on low-resource intent classification tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that PEFT of Flan-T5 models using the T-few recipe outperforms other methods, including zero-shot approaches, but does not compare it to full fine-tuning of larger models.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the efficiency of PEFT but does not explore how it compares to full fine-tuning in terms of long-term performance or scalability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting long-term studies comparing PEFT with full fine-tuning of larger models on low-resource intent classification tasks, measuring factors such as accuracy, resource usage, and scalability over time.

## Limitations

- Limited analysis of precision-recall trade-offs and error patterns across different intent categories
- Domain adaptation effectiveness lacks comparative analysis against alternative domain adaptation methods
- Zero-shot classification claims rely heavily on LLM capabilities without extensive ablation studies on prompt design or intent description quality

## Confidence

- Domain adaptation effectiveness: Medium
- Zero-shot classification with LLMs: Medium
- PEFT performance claims: High
