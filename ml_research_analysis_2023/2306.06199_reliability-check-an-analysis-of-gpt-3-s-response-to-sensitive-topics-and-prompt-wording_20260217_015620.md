# Reliability Check: An Analysis of GPT-3's Response to Sensitive Topics and Prompt Wording

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.06199
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06199
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: GPT-3's responses are highly sensitive to prompt wording and often inconsistent, even when responding with high confidence

## Executive Summary
This study systematically examines GPT-3's reliability when responding to sensitive topics and how minor changes in prompt wording affect its responses. Using a dataset of 1268 statements across six categories (Fact, Conspiracy, Controversy, Misconception, Stereotype, Fiction), the researchers tested GPT-3 with four different prompts. The results demonstrate that while GPT-3 correctly identifies factual statements and disagrees with conspiracy theories and stereotypes, it makes errors on misconceptions and controversial topics. Most critically, minor changes in prompt wording can significantly alter both the responses and confidence levels, highlighting the model's unreliability and the need for careful prompt design before deployment.

## Method Summary
The researchers curated a dataset of 1268 statements across six categories and used text-davinci-003 to generate responses using four different prompts. Each statement was tested with prompts asking "Is this true?", "Is this true in the real world?", "As a rational being who believes in scientific knowledge, do you think the following statement is true?", and "I think [statement]. Do you think I am right?". For each prompt, they collected both YES/NO responses with confidence scores and full text responses. The analysis compared confidence levels, consistency across prompts, and alignment with known ground truths where available.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 correctly identifies factual statements and disagrees with conspiracy theories and stereotypes
- The model makes errors on misconceptions and controversial statements despite responding with high confidence
- Minor changes in prompt wording can significantly alter both responses and confidence levels
- GPT-3 struggles with understanding sentences containing negation or limiting words like "only" or "alone"

## Limitations

- The study relies on a single LLM (text-davinci-003) without comparing results across different models or versions
- The dataset composition and exact statement content remain unspecified beyond general categories
- The analysis focuses primarily on YES/NO responses with confidence scores, potentially missing nuanced understanding

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The observation that GPT-3's responses vary significantly across different prompts is well-supported by the experimental results and aligns with established research on prompt sensitivity in LLMs.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that GPT-3 often responds with high confidence even when incorrect is supported by the data, though the exact frequency and distribution of such errors requires more detailed statistical analysis.
- **Medium Confidence**: The finding that GPT-3 struggles with negation and limiting words is demonstrated through specific examples, but the scope and generalizability of this limitation across different contexts remains unclear.
