# What is Essential for Unseen Goal Generalization of Offline Goal-conditioned RL?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18882
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18882
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes GOAT, a method that improves OOD goal generalization in offline GCRL by combining weighted imitation learning with advantage re-weighting, data selection, density re-weighting, and goal relabeling.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization in offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL), where agents must achieve unseen goals not present in the training data. The authors identify that pessimism-based offline RL methods like CQL are inhibited from generalizing to OOD goals, while weighted imitation learning methods show superior performance. They develop a theoretical framework based on domain generalization theory to characterize essential techniques for OOD generalization, including advantage re-weighting, data selection, density re-weighting, and goal relabeling. Based on this analysis, they propose Generalizable Offline goAl-condiTioned RL (GOAT), a new method that combines these techniques into a weighted imitation learning framework. GOAT is evaluated on a new benchmark of 26 tasks (9 IID and 17 OOD) and significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods on OOD generalization, improving average success rates from 62.1% to 67.9%.

## Method Summary
GOAT combines weighted imitation learning with four key techniques for OOD generalization. It uses an ensemble of Q-functions to estimate uncertainty, which approximates the reciprocal of density in high-dimensional state-goal space. The method applies exponential advantage re-weighting to focus on high-quality transitions, uses data selection to filter samples below a threshold, and employs goal relabeling to augment the dataset. The policy is trained to maximize a weighted imitation objective that combines these elements. During training, GOAT maintains FIFO queues to normalize uncertainty and advantage values, and updates the value ensemble to minimize TD loss while simultaneously learning the policy.

## Key Results
- GOAT significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods on OOD generalization, improving average success rates from 62.1% to 67.9%
- Weighted imitation learning methods generalize better than pessimism-based offline RL methods for OOD goals
- The combination of advantage re-weighting, data selection, density re-weighting, and goal relabeling is essential for OOD generalization
- GOAT improves efficiency in online fine-tuning for unseen goals

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Weighted imitation learning outperforms pessimism-based offline RL for OOD goal generalization.
- Mechanism: Weighted imitation learning minimizes the empirical imitation loss by directly imitating a surrogate expert policy, while pessimism-based methods like CQL restrict policy to in-dataset regions, limiting generalization to unseen goals.
- Core assumption: The surrogate expert policy can be constructed effectively using weighted behavior policy with advantage weighting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we observe that weighted imitation learning enjoys better generalization than pessimism-based offline RL method"
  - [section]: "pessimism-based offline RL methods are inhibited from generalizing to OOD goals, while weighted imitation learning methods show better generalization"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Re-weighting training distribution with reciprocal of density minimizes worst-case distribution shift.
- Mechanism: By approximating the reciprocal of density with uncertainty (e.g., standard deviation of ensemble Q-values), GOAT downweights samples from high-density regions, effectively re-weighting the training distribution toward uniform and minimizing worst-case distribution shift.
- Core assumption: Uncertainty (standard deviation of ensemble Q-values) is approximately proportional to reciprocal of density in high-dimensional state-goal space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we utilize uncertainty to replace density as a fact that the bootstrapped uncertainty is approximately proportional to the reciprocal of density in tabular MDP"
  - [corpus]: "uncertainty is measured by the variance of ensemble value functions" (from didactic example analysis)

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Advantage re-weighting and data selection narrow the expert estimation gap.
- Mechanism: By weighting samples with exponential advantage and selecting samples above a threshold, GOAT focuses on high-quality transitions that are closer to expert behavior, reducing the gap between surrogate and true expert policies.
- Core assumption: The advantage function can be estimated accurately enough to distinguish high-quality from low-quality samples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "advantage re-weighting ˆπE(a|s, g) ∝ πb(a|s, g) exp(β · A(s, a, g)) brings improved expected value over πb"
  - [corpus]: "advantage re-weighting and data selection contribute the most by improving the surrogate expert policy for imitating"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Policy network (πθ) -> Value ensemble (Q1...QN) -> FIFO queues -> Goal relabeling module -> Weighting module -> Weighted imitation objective

- Critical path:
  1. Sample mini-batch from offline dataset
  2. Perform goal relabeling with probability prelabel
  3. Update value ensemble to minimize TD loss
  4. Estimate advantage values and store in queue
  5. Calculate uncertainty weight from ensemble standard deviation
  6. Update policy to maximize weighted imitation objective

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Ensemble size vs. computational cost: Larger N provides better uncertainty estimation but increases training time
  - Hyperparameter w for uncertainty weight: Controls proportion of downweighted samples, requiring task-specific tuning
  - Expectile regression parameter τ: Smaller τ improves relative advantage ranking but may amplify estimation errors

- Failure signatures:
  - Policy collapses to in-dataset behavior: Indicates insufficient uncertainty weighting or overly conservative data selection
  - Poor performance on easy goals: Suggests over-emphasis on high-advantage samples, neglecting simpler cases
  - Instability during training: May indicate improper normalization of uncertainty or advantage values

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify uncertainty correlates with distance from training distribution using ensemble standard deviation
  2. Test effect of ensemble size on OOD performance while keeping other components fixed
  3. Evaluate impact of goal relabeling probability on dataset diversity and generalization performance

## Limitations
- Weighted imitation learning may suffer from distributional shift and lack safety guarantees compared to pessimism-based methods
- Uncertainty approximation using ensemble standard deviation may not capture true density in high-dimensional spaces
- Theoretical framework assumes sufficient expert demonstrations in offline dataset, which may not hold in practice

## Confidence
- High confidence in weighted imitation learning advantage for OOD goals
- Medium confidence in uncertainty as density proxy approximation
- Medium confidence in theoretical bounds applicability to high-dimensional continuous domains
