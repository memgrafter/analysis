# Mitigating Inappropriateness in Image Generation: Can there be Value in Reflecting the World's Ugliness?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18398
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18398
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Instruction-based mitigation strategies can significantly reduce inappropriate content generation across text-to-image models, with Semantic Guidance showing superior performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of inappropriate content generation across 11 state-of-the-art text-to-image models, including Stable Diffusion variants and other architectures. The study introduces the Inappropriate-image-prompts (I2P) benchmark and demonstrates that all evaluated models can generate inappropriate content, with varying rates of explicit material generation. The authors evaluate two instruction-based mitigation strategies - negative prompting and Semantic Guidance (SEGA) - on a dataset of over 4.7k real user prompts. Their findings show that instructing models about inappropriate content at inference time is more effective than pre-filtering training data, as models can leverage their learned representations of inappropriate concepts to align with human preferences when properly guided.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 11 text-to-image models including Stable Diffusion variants (SD 1.4, 1.5, 2.0, 2.1), AltDiffusion, MultiFusion, Paella, and DeepFloyd-IF. Two instruction-based mitigation strategies are tested: negative prompting (asking models not to generate certain concepts) and Semantic Guidance (SEGA) which adds a steering term to guide generation away from inappropriate content. The evaluation uses the I2P benchmark with 4.7k real user prompts across multiple inappropriate content categories. Models generate 10 images per prompt, and appropriateness is assessed using Q16 and NudeNet classifiers to calculate the probability of generating inappropriate content and expected maximum inappropriateness scores.

## Key Results
- All evaluated text-to-image models can generate inappropriate content, with SD 2.1 showing the highest baseline inappropriateness rate
- Instruction-based mitigation significantly reduces inappropriate content generation, with both negative prompting and SEGA showing effectiveness
- SEGA outperforms negative prompting, particularly for models with higher baseline inappropriateness rates, achieving up to 40% reduction in inappropriate content generation
- Models with more powerful text encoders (like MultiFusion) show better mitigation performance, suggesting semantic understanding is crucial for instruction compliance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Models can leverage their learned representations of inappropriate content to align with human preferences when properly guided.
- Mechanism: The model's training on large-scale web data gives it an implicit understanding of what constitutes inappropriate content. By using this knowledge through instruction-based mitigation (negative prompting and SEGA), the model can be directed to avoid generating such content while maintaining its general generation capabilities.
- Core assumption: The model has effectively learned representations of inappropriate concepts during pre-training, even without explicit filtering.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our findings show that we can use models' representations of the world's ugliness to align them with human preferences."
  - [section 2] "In contrast, we here explore the idea of leveraging a model's learned representations of inappropriate content for mitigation of such material."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this specific mechanism

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Semantic Guidance (SEGA) is more effective than negative prompting because it provides more precise control over content suppression.
- Mechanism: SEGA adds an additional guidance term to the noise prediction that steers generation away from inappropriate concepts while minimizing changes to the original image. This allows for targeted suppression rather than broad avoidance.
- Core assumption: The semantic understanding provided by SEGA is more precise than the directional avoidance of negative prompting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2] "SEGA adds an additional guidance term to ϵ̃θ that allows us to steer the generation away from s, while keeping changes to the original image minimal."
  - [section 3] "SEGA seems more reliable... SEGA removes the inappropriateness with minimal adjustments"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence comparing SEGA vs negative prompting effectiveness

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Models with more powerful text encoders show better mitigation performance because they can better understand and process safety instructions.
- Mechanism: The text encoder's ability to semantically understand and process safety-related instructions determines how effectively the model can be guided to avoid inappropriate content.
- Core assumption: The quality of the text encoder directly impacts the model's ability to process and act on safety instructions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "MULTIFUSION achieves the lowest inappropriateness scores... The key difference lies in MULTI FUSION's more powerful text-encoder that is aligned for semantic understanding."
  - [section 4] "Semantically grounded input encodings clearly play a crucial role in enabling a model to understand and suppress inappropriate concepts."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence on text encoder impact on safety mitigation

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text encoder -> Latent space diffusion UNet -> Classifier-free guidance mechanism -> Instruction processing module -> Output filtering/classification

- Critical path:
  1. Prompt input → text encoder processing
  2. Text embedding combined with noise prediction
  3. Guidance application (classifier-free + instruction guidance)
  4. Iterative denoising steps
  5. Output classification for safety evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Training data filtering vs. instruction-based mitigation
  - Precision of content suppression vs. image quality
  - Computational cost of mitigation at inference vs. pre-filtering

- Failure signatures:
  - Persistent generation of inappropriate content despite instructions
  - Excessive image distortion when applying mitigation
  - High false positive rate in inappropriate content detection

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test baseline inappropriate content generation on I2P benchmark
  2. Apply negative prompting with default parameters and measure effectiveness
  3. Apply SEGA with tuned parameters and compare performance to negative prompting

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different definitions of "inappropriate content" across cultures and applications affect the effectiveness of instruction-based mitigation strategies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that determining inappropriate content is subjective and dependent on societal norms and use cases, suggesting future work should address balancing datasets.
- Why unresolved: Different cultural contexts may require different instruction prompts, but the paper only tested one general concept across all experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies testing multiple instruction prompts tailored to different cultural contexts and application domains, measuring their effectiveness across diverse user groups.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between the quality and style of AI-generated images and the performance of inappropriate content classifiers?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the metrics and classifiers used in the I2P benchmark were not specifically developed for assessing AI-generated images, raising questions about whether image quality and style influence inappropriateness measurement.
- Why unresolved: Current classifiers may not be optimized for the unique characteristics of AI-generated imagery, potentially leading to false positives or negatives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying image quality and artistic styles while keeping content constant, measuring classifier performance across these variations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do instruction-based mitigation strategies affect the creative capabilities and prompt understanding of text-to-image models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates that instruction methods reduce inappropriate content generation but doesn't explore potential trade-offs with creative expression or prompt fidelity.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows effectiveness in reducing inappropriate content, it doesn't investigate whether the instruction methods might also constrain legitimate creative expression or alter the model's interpretation of non-inappropriate prompts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies measuring creative output quality, prompt adherence, and user satisfaction with and without instruction-based mitigation across a diverse set of creative prompts.

## Limitations

- The study relies exclusively on image-based classifiers for evaluating inappropriateness, which may not capture nuanced or context-dependent violations
- SEGA parameter tuning is described as "empirically tuned" without systematic sensitivity analysis
- The causal relationship between text encoder architecture and safety instruction comprehension is not rigorously established

## Confidence

- Overall effectiveness of instruction-based mitigation: Medium
- SEGA superiority over negative prompting: Medium
- Text encoder quality impact on mitigation: Low to Medium
