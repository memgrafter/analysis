# Efficient and Effective Tree-based and Neural Learning to Rank

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08680
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08680
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comprehensive survey of efficiency and effectiveness in learning to rank, focusing on tree-based and neural models, with exploration of multiple efficiency dimensions and optimization methods.

## Executive Summary
This monograph provides a comprehensive survey of the literature on efficiency and effectiveness in learning to rank (LtR), focusing on tree-based and neural models. It explores the challenges of balancing efficiency and effectiveness in ranking, covering topics such as efficient training, inference, and data structures. The monograph highlights the importance of considering multiple dimensions of efficiency, including time, space, sample, energy, and resource efficiency. It presents various methods for optimizing inference efficiency, including efficient traversal of decision forests, early exit strategies, and cascading models. Additionally, it discusses the emergence of neural LtR models and their unique challenges, such as dense retrieval and knowledge distillation.

## Method Summary
The monograph conducts a comprehensive literature review of 40 references, categorizing them based on topics like tree-based models, neural models, and efficiency metrics. It synthesizes key findings and methods from each reference, highlighting connections between tree-based and neural approaches. The content is organized into chapters covering the document ranking problem, efficiency challenges, tree-based LtR, neural LtR, and open challenges. The review focuses on methods like cost-aware learning, pruning, knowledge distillation, and early-exit strategies for improving efficiency.

## Key Results
- Early exit strategies can significantly reduce inference time by terminating evaluation of low-scoring documents early in the ranking process.
- Knowledge distillation enables training smaller, more efficient student models that approximate larger, more effective teacher models.
- Dense retrieval methods can improve retrieval quality by learning dense vector representations that capture semantic similarity, but at increased computational complexity.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Early exit strategies can significantly reduce inference time by terminating evaluation of low-scoring documents early in the ranking process.
- Mechanism: At predefined "exit points" within a model's computation (e.g., after a fixed number of trees or transformer layers), classifiers predict whether a document is unlikely to rank in the top-k results. If so, evaluation stops early, saving computation.
- Core assumption: The ranking scores of non-relevant documents change less dramatically after the initial stages of computation, so early decisions are reliable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Cambazoglu et al.(2010) placeexit points at fixed positions in a given forest (e.g., every 100 trees) and introduce fourearly-exit algorithms that decide at each exit point whether the evaluation of a document can be terminated early."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus does not contain specific studies on early exit strategies in neural rankers, but mentions the concept in related works.
- Break condition: If the distribution of relevance scores does not follow a skewed pattern, or if the model's early-stage predictions are unreliable, early exit strategies may degrade ranking quality.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge distillation enables the training of smaller, more efficient student models that approximate the behavior of larger, more effective teacher models.
- Mechanism: A large teacher model is first trained on a dataset. A smaller student model is then trained to minimize the deviation from both the ground-truth labels and the teacher's predictions, effectively learning from the teacher's "cleaner" signal.
- Core assumption: The teacher model, due to its greater complexity, captures complex relationships in the data and removes noise, providing a better training signal for the student.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In knowledge distillation, a complexteacher model is first trained on a given dataset, then a simpler student model is trained by minimizing the deviation of its prediction both from the ground-truth labels and from the teacher’s predictions."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus does not contain specific studies on knowledge distillation in the context of learning to rank, but mentions the concept in related works.
- Break condition: If the teacher model is not significantly more effective than the student model, or if the student model cannot adequately approximate the teacher's behavior, knowledge distillation may not yield efficiency gains.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Dense retrieval methods can significantly improve retrieval quality by learning dense vector representations that capture semantic similarity, but at the cost of increased computational complexity.
- Mechanism: Documents and queries are encoded as dense vectors in a high-dimensional space. During retrieval, the closest document vectors to the query vector are found using approximate nearest neighbor search algorithms.
- Core assumption: The structure of the vector space preserves semantic similarity, so vectors that are close to each other are semantically similar.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "One of the major innovations in neuralLtR research was the evolution of 'cross-encoders' such as monoBERT and duoBERT to two-tower or 'bi-encoder' models. Instead of learning a parameterized function...that takes a query-document pair (or multiple documents) as input and simultaneously learns representations...and predicts their relevance, bi-encoders disentangle the relevance prediction function from representation learning, and simplify the former as much as possible."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus does not contain specific studies on dense retrieval, but mentions the concept in related works.
- Break condition: If the vector representations do not effectively capture semantic similarity, or if the ANN search algorithm is not efficient, dense retrieval may not provide the expected quality or efficiency gains.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Query processing pipeline: Retrieval (e.g., BM25, dense retrieval) -> Ranking (LtR model) -> Re-ranking (optional)
  - LtR model: Feature extraction -> Scoring function -> Optimization (e.g., gradient boosting, neural network training)
  - Efficiency optimizations: Multi-stage ranking, early exit strategies, knowledge distillation, efficient data structures

- Critical path:
  - For tree-based models: Feature extraction -> Tree traversal (efficient implementation) -> Score aggregation
  - For neural models: Feature extraction (or raw text input) -> Neural network inference (efficient implementation) -> Score aggregation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Effectiveness vs. efficiency: More complex models and features generally lead to better ranking quality but increased computational cost.
  - Training vs. inference efficiency: Some optimizations may improve inference speed but require additional training time or resources.
  - Model size vs. accuracy: Smaller models are more efficient but may sacrifice ranking quality.

- Failure signatures:
  - Degraded ranking quality: May indicate issues with model training, feature extraction, or efficiency optimizations that compromise effectiveness.
  - High latency: May indicate inefficient data structures, lack of early exit strategies, or overly complex models.
  - Memory issues: May indicate large model size or inefficient data structures.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we design a unified framework that combines stochastic ranking with cascade architectures to improve both efficiency and effectiveness in retrieval and ranking systems?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper discusses the potential of stochastic ranking and cascade architectures separately, but a unified framework that combines these ideas has not been fully explored. The implications for efficiency and effectiveness trade-offs in this combined approach are unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a theoretical model that integrates stochastic ranking with cascade architectures and conducting empirical studies to evaluate the efficiency and effectiveness trade-offs of the unified framework.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we develop retrieval algorithms that efficiently handle hybrid vectors (combining dense and sparse representations) to improve both the quality and efficiency of retrieval and ranking systems?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights the challenges of handling hybrid vectors in retrieval algorithms, particularly in terms of scalability and efficiency. Existing algorithms are not optimized for this joint retrieval problem, and the trade-offs between quality and efficiency in this regime are not well understood.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing and implementing retrieval algorithms specifically tailored for hybrid vectors, followed by extensive empirical evaluation to assess their performance and efficiency compared to existing methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we develop formal definitions and standardized metrics to measure and report the efficiency of retrieval and ranking systems across multiple dimensions (e.g., time, space, sample, energy, resource efficiency)?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper emphasizes the need for a broader definition of efficiency that encompasses multiple dimensions, but lacks formal definitions and standardized metrics. Without these, it is challenging to compare and evaluate the efficiency of different retrieval and ranking approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proposing a comprehensive set of metrics and guidelines for measuring efficiency across multiple dimensions, and conducting studies to validate their effectiveness and applicability in various retrieval and ranking scenarios.

## Limitations
- The monograph lacks quantitative evidence for many claimed efficiency gains, with most claims supported by theoretical reasoning rather than empirical validation.
- Claims about specific efficiency improvements from emerging techniques like dense retrieval and neural LtR are largely theoretical, with minimal empirical evidence provided.
- The trade-offs between different efficiency dimensions (e.g., time vs. energy efficiency) are discussed qualitatively without quantitative benchmarks.

## Confidence
- High confidence: The fundamental mechanisms of tree-based ranking (gradient boosting, feature extraction) and basic neural ranking architectures (bi-encoders, cross-encoders) are well-established and clearly explained.
- Medium confidence: The survey's coverage of efficiency optimization techniques (early exit, knowledge distillation, cascading) is comprehensive, but lacks quantitative validation of claimed benefits.
- Low confidence: Claims about specific efficiency improvements from emerging techniques like dense retrieval and neural LtR are largely theoretical, with minimal empirical evidence provided in the corpus.
