# Combining Stochastic Explainers and Subgraph Neural Networks can Increase Expressivity and Interpretability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07152
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07152
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Achieves accuracy scores of 92.1% on MUTAG, 82.9% on NCI1, 76.3% on PROTEINS, 76.0% on IMDB-BINARY, and 53.5% on IMDB-MULTI, comparable to state-of-the-art models while providing interpretable explanations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework that combines subgraph-enhanced graph neural networks with explainability techniques to jointly predict the class of a graph and provide explanatory subgraphs. The core idea is to train a base GNN classifier, then apply a modified PGExplainer to generate multiple "hard" explanation masks, which are used as a bag of subgraphs to train a DSS-GNN model. The proposed method achieves comparable classification accuracy to state-of-the-art models on standard graph datasets, with the added benefit of providing explanations in the form of heatmaps highlighting the most relevant parts of the graph responsible for the prediction.

## Method Summary
The framework combines a pre-trained GNN backbone with a modified PGExplainer to generate multiple "hard" explanation masks, which are used as a bag of subgraphs to train a DSS-GNN model. This approach leverages the expressive power of subgraph-enhanced GNNs while incorporating explainability techniques. The key innovation is the use of "hard" explanation masks instead of "soft" masks, which are created by binarizing the soft weights and applying a straight-through estimator to allow gradient flow during training.

## Key Results
- Achieves comparable classification accuracy to state-of-the-art models on standard graph datasets
- Provides interpretable explanations in the form of heatmaps highlighting relevant graph parts
- Performance scores: 92.1% on MUTAG, 82.9% on NCI1, 76.3% on PROTEINS, 76.0% on IMDB-BINARY, and 53.5% on IMDB-MULTI
- Demonstrates the potential of combining stochastic explainers with subgraph neural networks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The framework achieves comparable classification accuracy to state-of-the-art models while providing interpretable explanations by combining a pre-trained GNN backbone with a modified PGExplainer to generate multiple "hard" explanation masks, which are used as a bag of subgraphs to train a DSS-GNN model. This approach leverages the expressive power of subgraph-enhanced GNNs while incorporating explainability techniques. The core assumption is that the explanatory subgraphs generated by the modified PGExplainer are both meaningful for classification and interpretable for human understanding.

### Mechanism 2
The use of "hard" explanation masks instead of "soft" masks improves the interpretability of the explanations by binarizing the soft weights to create discrete subgraphs that can be more easily interpreted by human experts. Since "hard" explanations are not differentiable, a straight-through estimator is applied to allow the computation and flowing of gradients in the backward pass. The core assumption is that discrete subgraphs are more interpretable than continuous probability distributions over edges.

### Mechanism 3
The combination of stochastic explainers and subgraph neural networks increases the expressive power of the base GNN classifier by using a modified PGExplainer to generate multiple diverse subgraphs, which are then processed by a DSS-GNN model. This approach allows the model to consider different views of the graph, potentially capturing more complex patterns and relationships. The core assumption is that multiple diverse subgraphs provide complementary information that enhances the model's ability to distinguish between different graph classes.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Backbone GNN classifier (e.g., GIN) -> Modified PGExplainer for generating "hard" explanation masks -> Subgraph selection strategies (noise-based and top-K selection) -> DSS-GNN model for processing the bag of explanation subgraphs -> Set learning module for aggregating subgraph representations

- **Critical path**: 1. Train the backbone GNN classifier on original graphs 2. Apply the modified PGExplainer to generate explanation subgraphs 3. Use the explanation subgraphs to train the DSS-GNN model 4. Combine subgraphs into a heatmap for interpretation

- **Design tradeoffs**: Balancing between classification accuracy and interpretability, choosing between noise-based and top-K selection strategies for subgraph diversity, deciding on the number of subgraphs to include in the bag

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the proposed stochastic explainers compare to other state-of-the-art post-hoc explainability methods for GNNs in terms of explanation quality metrics (e.g., fidelity, sparsity, or stability)?
- Basis in paper: The paper introduces a novel framework combining stochastic explainers with SGNNs but only compares against standard subgraph extraction policies rather than existing post-hoc explainers.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing classification accuracy with SGNN variants rather than evaluating the quality of explanations against other explainability methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive comparison of explanation quality metrics (fidelity, sparsity, stability) between the proposed stochastic explainers and established post-hoc methods like GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, or XGNN on the same datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for selecting the number and composition of explanatory subgraphs (K edges) to balance interpretability and classification performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions two strategies for obtaining heterogeneous subgraphs but notes that "we did not notice any empirical difference when using a subset of the bag of subgraphs instead of the complete one" and leaves this as an open question.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide systematic analysis of how different K values affect both interpretability and accuracy, nor does it compare the noise-based versus progressive top-K selection strategies comprehensively.
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation study showing classification accuracy and explanation quality metrics across different K values and selection strategies, with visualization of how explanation subgraphs change with K.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed framework perform on larger, more complex real-world graph datasets beyond the TUDataset?
- Basis in paper: The paper only evaluates on standard TUDataset benchmarks, which are relatively small and clean compared to real-world graph data.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation is limited to benchmark datasets, leaving questions about scalability and performance on larger graphs with noise, missing edges, or heterogeneous node features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on larger real-world datasets (e.g., social networks, biological interaction networks, or knowledge graphs) with varying graph sizes, feature heterogeneity, and noise levels to assess scalability and robustness.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation scope to standard graph classification benchmarks, raising questions about generalizability to other domains
- Lack of thorough analysis of the quality and diversity of generated subgraphs
- Additional computational overhead introduced by combining PGExplainer and DSS-GNN is not extensively discussed

## Confidence
- **Classification Performance Claim**: High confidence - The paper demonstrates comparable accuracy to state-of-the-art models on standard benchmarks.
- **Interpretability Claim**: Medium confidence - While the framework provides explanations in the form of heatmaps, the actual interpretability and alignment with domain knowledge is not rigorously validated.
- **Expressive Power Claim**: Medium confidence - The paper argues that the combination of stochastic explainers and subgraph neural networks increases expressive power, but this is primarily inferred from performance results rather than direct analysis of the learned representations.
