# Improving Rare Words Recognition through Homophone Extension and Unified Writing for Low-resource Cantonese Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00836
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00836
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Homophone extension and unified writing improve rare word recognition in low-resource Cantonese ASR, reducing CER by ~5% in-domain and ~18% out-of-domain.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of recognizing rare or unseen characters in low-resource Cantonese speech recognition. The authors propose two complementary methods: homophone extension (HE) and unified writing (UW). HE leverages a homophone lexicon to expand beam search candidates during decoding, while UW merges character variants to increase training data and reduce decoding complexity. Experimental results demonstrate significant CER reductions on both in-domain and out-of-domain datasets, with the combined approach yielding the best performance.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes homophone extension (HE) and unified writing (UW) to improve rare word recognition in low-resource Cantonese ASR. HE integrates a homophone lexicon into beam search decoding, adding homophones of candidate characters with adjusted acoustic probabilities, followed by language model re-scoring. UW merges character variants by replacing lower-frequency variants with higher-frequency ones based on pronunciation and glyph similarity, increasing training samples and reducing decoding space. Both methods are evaluated on fine-tuned XLS-R models with CTC loss, using 5-gram KenLM for shallow fusion.

## Key Results
- Homophone extension and unified writing significantly improve CER on both in-domain and out-of-domain datasets.
- Absolute CER reduction of approximately 5% on in-domain data and 18% on out-of-domain data.
- Combined approach outperforms individual methods, especially on out-of-domain test sets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Homophone extension integrates human knowledge of homophone lexicon into beam search, enabling rare or unseen words to be recognized during inference.
- Mechanism: During beam search decoding, for each candidate character, its homophone characters are added to the search space with adjusted acoustic probabilities. A language model then re-scores all extended candidates, allowing rare homophones to compete with more frequent ones.
- Core assumption: Homophone characters share identical pronunciations (Jyutping codes) and can be reliably identified using a lexicon.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "HE integrates human knowledge of a homophone lexicon into the beam search process and improves recognition of rare words through their homophone characters."
  - [section] "For each candidate character, we extend the search space by adding their homophone characters. Then, a language model is adopted to re-score all the extended candidates and output the final new candidates."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: corpus neighbors discuss similar beam search and language model integration but do not explicitly mention homophone extension.
- Break condition: If the homophone lexicon is incomplete or if homophones have drastically different acoustic likelihoods due to speaker or recording variability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Unified writing merges character variants to increase training samples and reduce decoding complexity.
- Mechanism: Variants of Cantonese characters (same pronunciation and meaning but different writing) are identified using glyph and pronunciation similarity metrics. Lower-frequency variants are replaced with higher-frequency ones, increasing the number of training samples for merged characters and simplifying the decoding space.
- Core assumption: Variants are semantically equivalent and can be merged without loss of meaning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "UW merges the variants of a Cantonese character and leads to performance gains due to more training samples for the character and reduced decoding space."
  - [section] "For the approach of unified writing, we merge the variants of Cantonese characters and unify them with the most frequent one to avoid unnecessary complexities caused by the variants."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence: corpus neighbors do not discuss character variant unification.
- Break condition: If variants have subtle semantic differences or if the frequency-based replacement changes intended meaning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Both methods together yield greater improvements than either alone, especially on out-of-domain datasets.
- Mechanism: Homophone extension addresses unseen words, while unified writing consolidates training data. Combined, they reduce the effective vocabulary size and increase robustness to rare or out-of-domain inputs.
- Core assumption: The out-of-domain dataset contains more rare/unseen characters than the in-domain set.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experimental results show that the proposed two methods improve the recognition performance significantly on both in-domain and out-of-domain datasets, with an absolute Character Error Rate (CER) decrease of around 5% and 18%."
  - [section] "It can be seen that both HE and UW are effective in reducing the CER, particularly on the Yueqie dataset."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: corpus neighbors discuss low-resource ASR but not the specific combination of homophone extension and unified writing.
- Break condition: If the out-of-domain dataset contains entirely new words not covered by the homophone lexicon or unified writing rules.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - XLS-R backbone (fine-tuned with CTC loss)
  - Beam search decoder (modified for homophone extension)
  - Language model (5-gram KenLM for re-scoring)
  - Homophone lexicon (Jyutping-based)
  - Unified writing pipeline (variant detection and replacement)
- Critical path: Audio → XLS-R → CTC probabilities → Beam search (with HE) → LM re-scoring → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a large character tokenizer (32,693 chars) increases coverage but also model size and complexity.
  - Homophone extension increases decoding time due to larger candidate sets.
  - Unified writing risks semantic drift if variants are not truly equivalent.
- Failure signatures:
  - High CER on out-of-domain data suggests homophone lexicon or unified writing rules are incomplete.
  - Degradation in in-domain performance may indicate over-aggressive variant merging or incorrect homophone injection.
  - Slow decoding times may signal inefficient candidate expansion in beam search.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement homophone extension on a small Cantonese dataset and compare CER with and without HE.
  2. Build the unified writing pipeline and test variant merging on a labeled Cantonese corpus.
  3. Combine both methods and evaluate on both in-domain and out-of-domain test sets, measuring CER and decoding speed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the performance of homophone extension vary with different sizes of the homophone lexicon or different discount factors γ?
- How robust is the unified writing approach across different languages or writing systems with character variants?
- What is the computational overhead introduced by the homophone extension and unified writing methods during inference?

## Limitations
- The homophone lexicon is not publicly specified, limiting reproducibility.
- Unified writing semantic preservation relies on automated checks with underspecified thresholds.
- Out-of-domain evaluation is based on a small test set (171 utterances).

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The effectiveness of unified writing in increasing training samples and reducing decoding space is well-supported by the theoretical mechanism and experimental results.
- Medium Confidence: The homophone extension mechanism is clearly explained and theoretically valid, but the lack of specification of the homophone lexicon and potential for semantic ambiguity introduce uncertainty.
- Low Confidence: The claim of combined improvements exceeding the sum of individual improvements is based on limited data and requires further validation.
