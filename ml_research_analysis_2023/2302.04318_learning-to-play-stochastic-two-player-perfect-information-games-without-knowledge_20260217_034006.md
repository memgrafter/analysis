# Learning to Play Stochastic Two-player Perfect-Information Games without Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04318
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04318
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Extending Descent to stochastic games via expectiminimax averaging outperforms both determinization approximation and baseline algorithms on EinStein würfelt nicht!

## Executive Summary
This paper extends the Descent framework, previously developed for deterministic two-player games, to stochastic two-player perfect-information games. The authors propose two approaches: generalizing the search algorithm to directly handle chance nodes through expectiminimax averaging, and approximating stochastic games by determinizing them into deterministic games. Both methods are evaluated on EinStein würfelt nicht! against state-of-the-art algorithms including Expectiminimax and Polygames (AlphaZero). The generalization of Descent to expectiminimax achieves the best results, though the determinization approach also performs well, suggesting it may be advantageous in certain contexts.

## Method Summary
The authors extend the Descent framework to stochastic games through two complementary approaches. The first generalizes the search algorithm to stochastic games by implementing expectiminax-style averaging at chance nodes, recursively computing child values weighted by outcome probabilities. The second approach approximates stochastic games by determinizing them—sampling fixed outcomes for chance events and applying the original Descent minimax algorithm to the resulting deterministic game. Both methods use the same neural network architecture (residual CNN) and learning loop: self-play matches generate game states with estimated values, which are used to update network weights via SGD+ADAM with experience replay. The game EinStein würfelt nicht! serves as the testbed, with algorithms evaluated in round-robin tournaments.

## Key Results
- Descent Expectiminimax outperforms both Expectiminimax and Polygames baselines in tournament play
- Determinization achieves competitive performance, with win rates very close to Expectiminimax
- The depth heuristic significantly improves performance across both stochastic search approaches
- The network converges to better policies than those used in Polygames during evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Extending Descent Minimax to stochastic games by generalizing to Expectiminimax improves performance over using Determinization.
- Mechanism: Descent Expectiminimax directly handles chance nodes by recursively averaging child values weighted by outcome probabilities, preserving the best-first deep search advantage of Descent.
- Core assumption: The chance outcomes are independent and known at each chance node, so the averaging of child values approximates the true expectiminimax value.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "It is our generalization of Descent which obtains the best results."
  - [section 3.1.3]: "Descent Expectiminimax is described in Algorithm 2... In a chance state, it is the average of the values of the child states weighted by their probability of occurrence."
- Break condition: If chance outcomes are correlated or the stochastic tree is extremely large, the averaging may lose critical information and convergence to optimal strategy is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Approximating stochastic games by determinizing them enables applying deterministic search algorithms like Descent Minimax.
- Mechanism: For each match, a deterministic game instance is sampled by fixing all chance outcomes; Descent Minimax then explores this smaller, chance-free tree; learning data from chance nodes is approximated by propagating values from their deterministic children.
- Core assumption: The determinization process accurately captures the probability distribution of stochastic outcomes so that the learned policy transfers to the stochastic game.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1.1]: "This technique, which we call stochastic game determinization, approximates a stochastic game by a set of deterministic games."
  - [section 3.2.2]: "win rates of Expectiminimax and Descent with Determinization are very close and quite close to Descent Expectiminimax."
- Break condition: If the number of possible chance outcomes is very large (e.g., card games), the determinization set becomes intractable and the approximation loses fidelity.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using the depth heuristic in the Descent framework improves performance by favoring quick wins and slow defeats.
- Mechanism: The depth heuristic assigns higher values to terminal states reached sooner in the game, biasing the search to propagate values from shallow terminal nodes faster, thus guiding the agent toward decisive wins.
- Core assumption: In stochastic games, quicker resolution of outcomes reduces exposure to further stochastic variance, so depth-based preference remains valid.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2.2]: "the use of the depth heuristic significantly improves performance (44.5% without the depth heuristic and 55.5% with it; 95% conﬁdence radius: 0.5%)."
  - [section 3.2.3]: "Again, the depth heuristic improves performance. And even without the depth heuristic, Descent Expectiminimax performs better than Polygames."
- Break condition: If the game dynamics reward long-term positional advantage over quick resolution, the depth heuristic may mislead the agent.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Perfect-information vs stochastic games
  - Why needed here: Understanding the difference clarifies why standard minimax cannot directly handle chance nodes and why expectiminimax is required.
  - Quick check question: In a stochastic game, what distinguishes a chance node from a max/min node in the game tree?

- Concept: Expectiminimax value propagation
  - Why needed here: The core of Descent Expectiminimax is recursively averaging child values at chance nodes; without this, the algorithm would degenerate to plain minimax.
  - Quick check question: How is the value of a chance node computed differently from a max node in expectiminimax?

- Concept: Determinization as a variance reduction technique
  - Why needed here: Determinization simplifies the stochastic search space by sampling fixed outcomes, trading completeness for computational tractability.
  - Quick check question: What is the main computational advantage of determinization over direct expectiminimax in games with many chance outcomes?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Self-play matches -> Game state generation -> Neural network value estimation -> Tree search (Descent Expectiminimax or Descent Minimax with determinization) -> Value backpropagation -> Learning set accumulation -> Network weight updates via SGD+ADAM

- Critical path:
  1. Sample determinization (if using Determinization) or start stochastic tree
  2. Recursive search with value averaging at chance nodes (Expectiminimax) or fixed children (Determinization)
  3. Backpropagate values up tree
  4. Select move via ordinal law with simulated annealing
  5. Append game states to learning set with computed values
  6. Update network weights via SGD+ADAM
  7. Repeat for N matches

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Direct expectiminimax preserves stochastic fidelity but scales poorly with branching chance nodes
  - Determinization reduces branching but may introduce bias if sample set is small
  - Omitting policy head simplifies network but requires explicit action encoding elsewhere
  - No parallel self-play trades wall-clock time for richer per-game data

- Failure signatures:
  - Convergence to suboptimal policies: likely if chance outcome distribution is not well represented in determinization samples
  - Overfitting to specific determinizations: check variance of learned values across different determinizations
  - Slow learning: may indicate insufficient search depth or too aggressive pruning

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run Descent Expectiminimax with depth heuristic on a simple dice-based game; measure win rate vs Expectiminimax baseline
  2. Apply Determinization on the same game; compare convergence speed and final policy quality
  3. Disable depth heuristic; quantify performance drop to confirm its contribution

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what specific conditions does the Determinization technique outperform Descent Expectiminimax for stochastic games?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest Determinization might be more effective for games with a large stochastic branching factor, but this is not empirically validated beyond EinStein würfelt nicht!.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only tested EinStein würfelt nicht!, which has limited stochastic branching. The authors hypothesize better performance in games with more complex chance nodes but don't provide evidence.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments on stochastic games with significantly larger chance node branching factors (e.g., certain card games) would demonstrate whether Determinization consistently outperforms Descent Expectiminimax in such cases.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the depth heuristic's effectiveness generalize across different stochastic games and learning frameworks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show the depth heuristic improves performance for both Descent Expectiminimax and Expectiminimax on EinStein würfelt nicht!, but don't test other games or frameworks.
- Why unresolved: The improvement is demonstrated on a single game and within the Descent framework. It's unclear if this heuristic provides similar benefits for other stochastic games or reinforcement learning approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments applying the depth heuristic across multiple stochastic games and comparing it with other reinforcement learning frameworks (like AlphaZero variants) would show its broader applicability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical convergence guarantee for the Determinization technique in stochastic games?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "convergence towards the optimal stochastic game strategy may not be guaranteed in general" but don't provide theoretical analysis.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges potential convergence issues but doesn't explore the theoretical foundations or provide conditions under which convergence might be guaranteed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical proofs establishing convergence conditions for the Determinization technique, or counterexamples demonstrating non-convergence in specific game classes, would clarify its theoretical limitations.

## Limitations

- The paper does not specify exact rules for piece movement and collision resolution in EinStein würfelt nicht!, critical for reproduction
- Implementation details of the "depth heuristic" that significantly improved performance are not provided
- Relative computational cost between Descent Expectiminimax and the determinization approach is not quantified

## Confidence

- High confidence: Theoretical validity of extending Descent to stochastic games via expectiminimax averaging
- Medium confidence: Practical effectiveness of determinization as an approximation method
- Medium confidence: Depth heuristic contribution to policy quality
