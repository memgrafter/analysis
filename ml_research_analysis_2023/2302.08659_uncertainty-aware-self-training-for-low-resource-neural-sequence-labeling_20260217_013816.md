# Uncertainty-aware Self-training for Low-resource Neural Sequence Labeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08659
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08659
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: SeqUST achieves average F1 score of 77.78% with only 10 labeled examples per class on six sequence labeling benchmarks

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes SeqUST, a novel semi-supervised learning framework for low-resource neural sequence labeling. The key innovation is using token-level uncertainty estimation and reliable token selection to improve self-training in scenarios where labeled data is scarce. By combining Monte Carlo dropout for uncertainty estimation with a joint confidence-certainty selection strategy, the framework effectively addresses the confirmation bias problem in self-training. The approach demonstrates consistent improvements over strong baselines across six benchmarks including SNIPS, Multiwoz, and CoNLL-03 datasets.

## Method Summary
SeqUST is a semi-supervised sequence labeling framework that uses token-level uncertainty estimation for reliable self-training. It fine-tunes a pre-trained BERT model on limited labeled data to create a teacher model, then uses Monte Carlo dropout to estimate uncertainty at the token level. Reliable tokens are selected based on both model confidence and certainty scores, then used to train a student model with masked sequence labeling and Gaussian-based consistency regularization. The process iterates until convergence, with the student model replacing the teacher at each iteration.

## Key Results
- Achieves average F1 score of 77.78% using only 10 labeled examples per class
- Consistently outperforms strong baselines by large margins across six benchmarks
- Demonstrates effectiveness across diverse sequence labeling tasks including NER and slot filling
- Shows robust performance across different levels of labeled data scarcity (10, 20, 50, 100 examples per class)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Monte Carlo dropout approximates the posterior uncertainty of token predictions in BNN, enabling token-level reliable selection
- Mechanism: MC dropout samples T masked model weights from the current model to approximate the intractable posterior distribution over model parameters. The entropy difference between final and current prediction distributions yields information gain, inversely related to certainty
- Core assumption: Dropout distribution is a good surrogate for true posterior, and entropy difference captures meaningful uncertainty for token selection
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we incorporate Monte Carlo (MC) dropout in Bayesian neural network (BNN) to perform uncertainty estimation at the token level"
- Break condition: If dropout masks are too sparse or too frequent, the approximation becomes poor and uncertainty estimates lose reliability

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Combining confidence and certainty scores for token selection reduces confirmation bias and improves label denoising
- Mechanism: Confidence is average probability assigned to pseudo label across T samples, while certainty is derived from uncertainty estimate. Final sampling weight is product of these two, normalized across tokens
- Core assumption: High confidence alone is insufficient due to neural network miscalibration; certainty provides complementary information about label reliability
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we judiciously select the reliable tokens from each unlabeled sentence based on the model confidence and certainty"
- Break condition: If pseudo labels are systematically wrong, even high confidence tokens may be selected incorrectly, amplifying errors

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Gaussian-based consistency regularization (GCR) prevents overfitting to frequently selected tokens by encouraging consistent predictions under Gaussian-perturbed embeddings
- Mechanism: Token embeddings follow Gaussian distributions; K perturbed representations are generated using reparameterization trick. KL divergence between original and perturbed predictions serves as regularization
- Core assumption: Token embeddings follow Gaussian distributions and perturbations within this space preserve semantic meaning while regularizing the model
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we develop a Gaussian-based consistency regularization technique to further improve the model robustness on Gaussian-distributed perturbed representations"
- Break condition: If Gaussian assumption is violated or perturbations are too large, regularization may distort semantic representations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher model (fine-tuned PLM with MC dropout) -> Token-level uncertainty estimator (entropy difference calculation) -> Reliable token selector (confidence-certainty weighted sampling) -> Masked sequence labeling module (PHCE loss with token masking) -> Gaussian-based consistency regularizer (KL divergence on perturbed embeddings) -> Student model (same architecture as teacher, updated via robust objectives)

- Critical path: 1) Fine-tune teacher on labeled data, 2) Generate pseudo labels for unlabeled data, 3) Estimate uncertainty and select reliable tokens, 4) Train student with masked sequence labeling and GCR, 5) Update teacher with student weights, 6) Iterate until convergence

- Design tradeoffs: MC dropout samples (T): Higher T gives better uncertainty estimates but increases computation; PHCE loss parameter (τ): Controls robustness to noisy labels; GCR regularization weight (λ): Balances consistency vs. fitting pseudo labels; Number of perturbed samples (K): More samples give better approximation but increase cost

- Failure signatures: Performance plateaus early (likely overfitting to noisy pseudo labels despite GCR), high variance across runs (token selection might be too aggressive or uncertain), performance worse than supervised baseline (uncertainty estimation might be poor or selection criteria too strict)

- First 3 experiments: 1) Ablation study: Remove token-level selection and use all tokens to measure impact of selection strategy, 2) Hyperparameter sweep: Vary T (MC dropout samples) and λ (GCR weight) to find optimal tradeoff, 3) Data efficiency test: Compare performance with different amounts of labeled data (10, 20, 50, 100 shots) to validate low-resource effectiveness

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of MC dropout approximation depends heavily on the quality of dropout mask sampling and may not generalize well to all token types
- Gaussian assumption for token embeddings in consistency regularization may not hold for complex, multimodal embedding distributions
- Token-level selection strategy assumes independent reliability assessment, but sequence labeling tokens have strong dependencies that may affect reliability estimates
- PHCE loss temperature parameter requires careful tuning and may not generalize across different datasets without significant hyperparameter optimization

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Overall effectiveness of SeqUST in improving F1 scores on low-resource sequence labeling tasks
- **Medium confidence**: Specific mechanisms of token-level uncertainty estimation via MC dropout and confidence-certainty selection strategy
- **Medium confidence**: Gaussian-based consistency regularization approach is novel but superiority over other perturbation methods not fully established
- **Low confidence**: Claim that this is "first work to incorporate MC dropout in BNN for token-level uncertainty estimation" in sequence labeling

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation of selection strategy**: Run experiments with three variants - (a) select all tokens without uncertainty estimation, (b) select only high-confidence tokens without certainty, and (c) use random token selection to quantify the exact contribution of joint confidence-certainty approach

2. **Distribution validation**: Analyze actual distribution of token embeddings in model's latent space using t-SNE or similar visualization techniques to compare Gaussian assumption's validity across different token types

3. **Cross-domain robustness test**: Apply SeqUST to sequence labeling task from completely different domain (e.g., medical named entity recognition) with same hyperparameter settings used for original six benchmarks to test generalizability beyond evaluated domains
