# People and Places of Historical Europe: Bootstrapping Annotation Pipeline and a New Corpus of Named Entities in Late Medieval Texts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16718
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16718
- Reference count: 25
- Presents a pipeline for bootstrapping NER training data from historical texts, creating 3.6M sentences of silver-standard annotations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to automatically generate training data for named entity recognition (NER) in historical texts. The authors develop a bootstrapping pipeline that starts with a list of known named entities and an unannotated corpus, using information retrieval techniques to find and annotate relevant sentences. This creates a silver-standard dataset of 3.6M sentences from late medieval texts. The resulting corpus is used to train NER models that achieve high precision (72.81-93.98%) with moderate recall (58.14-81.77%) on manually annotated test data. The work also demonstrates the effectiveness of weighted loss functions in addressing class imbalance in token classification tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors present a bootstrapping pipeline for creating NER training data from historical texts. Starting with a list of 41,483 known named entities and an unannotated corpus of late medieval texts, they use information retrieval to identify sentences containing these entities. These sentences are then automatically annotated to create a silver-standard dataset of 3.6M sentences. This corpus is used to train NER models, which are evaluated on a manually annotated test set of 3,366 sentences. The authors also explore the use of weighted loss functions to address class imbalance in the token classification task, showing improved performance over unweighted approaches.

## Key Results
- Achieved 72.81-93.98% entity-level precision on manually annotated test data
- Obtained 58.14-81.77% recall across different entity types
- Created a new corpus of 3.6M silver-standard annotated sentences from late medieval texts
- Demonstrated effectiveness of weighted loss functions in addressing class imbalance for token classification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The bootstrapping approach works by leveraging existing knowledge (a list of named entities) to efficiently create training data for NER models. By using information retrieval to find sentences containing known entities, the method can scale to large corpora without requiring extensive manual annotation. The silver-standard annotations, while potentially noisier than fully manual annotations, provide a sufficient signal for training effective NER models. The use of weighted loss functions helps the models focus on underrepresented classes, improving overall performance in the face of class imbalance.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Entity List -> Information Retrieval -> Silver-standard Annotation -> NER Model Training -> Evaluation

**Critical Path:**
The most critical components are the information retrieval step and the NER model training. The quality of the retrieved sentences directly impacts the usefulness of the silver-standard annotations, which in turn affects model performance. The choice of NER architecture and training methodology (including the use of weighted loss functions) is crucial for achieving good results on the imbalanced entity distribution typical in historical texts.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Silver-standard vs. gold-standard annotations: Efficiency vs. data quality
- Entity list completeness: Coverage of known entities vs. potential bias in the resulting corpus
- NER model complexity: Performance vs. computational requirements and generalizability

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor information retrieval results in low-quality silver-standard annotations
- Class imbalance leads to poor performance on underrepresented entity types
- Historical language variation causes the model to underperform on texts from different periods

## Limitations
- Method relies heavily on the completeness and accuracy of the initial named entity list, potentially biasing results
- Silver-standard annotation process may introduce noise affecting downstream model performance
- Entity type taxonomy is relatively coarse, limiting granularity of historical analysis
- Effectiveness for languages other than English or texts from different historical periods remains untested
