# To be Robust and to be Fair: Aligning Fairness with Robustness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00061
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00061
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Unified framework for fairness attack that aligns fairness and accuracy robustness in adversarial training

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical intersection of fairness and robustness in machine learning systems by proposing a unified framework that treats fairness attacks as a specific form of adversarial attack. The authors demonstrate that adversarial training methods designed to improve model robustness against accuracy attacks can be extended to handle fairness attacks simultaneously. Through theoretical analysis and empirical validation on benchmark datasets, they show that improving robustness in one dimension (fairness or accuracy) provides benefits to the other, creating an alignment between these traditionally separate concerns in ML systems.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a unified framework that treats fairness attacks as a form of adversarial attack, proving equivalence between different fairness attack notions and showing alignment with accuracy attacks. They theoretically demonstrate that robustness to one metric benefits the other, then incorporate fair classification into adversarial training to improve robustness against fairness attacks. The method extends standard adversarial training by including fairness considerations in the optimization objective, allowing models to simultaneously defend against both accuracy-based and fairness-based adversarial perturbations.

## Key Results
- Theoretical equivalence established between different fairness attack notions
- Demonstrated alignment between fairness and accuracy attack strategies
- Showed that robustness to one metric benefits robustness to the other
- Achieved improved performance on four benchmark datasets for both fairness and accuracy robustness
- Proposed method outperforms standard adversarial training in fairness-robustness alignment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's mechanism relies on the observation that fairness violations can be framed as specific types of adversarial perturbations. By treating protected attribute-based discrimination as an adversarial objective, the same mathematical tools used for adversarial training against accuracy attacks can be applied to fairness concerns. The unified framework leverages gradient-based optimization to identify worst-case scenarios for both metrics simultaneously, allowing the model to learn robust decision boundaries that respect both accuracy and fairness constraints.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
- Input data -> Feature extraction -> Adversarial perturbation generation -> Combined loss computation -> Model parameters update

**Critical Path**
The critical path involves computing adversarial perturbations for both accuracy and fairness objectives, then optimizing the model parameters to minimize the worst-case loss across both dimensions simultaneously.

**Design Tradeoffs**
The method trades increased computational complexity for improved fairness-robustness alignment. The unified approach simplifies implementation compared to separate fairness and accuracy defenses but requires careful hyperparameter tuning to balance the two objectives.

**Failure Signatures**
Models may exhibit degraded performance on either accuracy or fairness metrics if the balance between objectives is poorly tuned. The unified framework may also struggle with datasets where fairness and accuracy objectives are fundamentally at odds.
