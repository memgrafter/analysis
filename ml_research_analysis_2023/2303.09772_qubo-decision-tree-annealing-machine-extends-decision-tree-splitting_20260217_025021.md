# QUBO Decision Tree: Annealing Machine Extends Decision Tree Splitting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09772
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09772
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents a method for improving regression trees by reformulating the splitting optimization as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) problem solvable by annealing machines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance regression tree splitting by leveraging annealing machines to explore complex multi-feature splitting conditions. The key innovation is representing complex splitting conditions as logical-product conditions encoded by binary vectors, enabling formulation as a QUBO problem. Experiments demonstrate the method's ability to find better splits than conventional single-feature approaches, particularly for datasets where optimal splits involve multiple features combined through logical operations.

## Method Summary
The method converts continuous and categorical features into binary basic conditions (e.g., "feature > threshold" or "feature = category"). Complex splitting conditions are represented as logical-product conditions encoded by binary vectors where each element indicates whether a basic condition is used. The optimization problem is formulated as minimizing square weighted mean squared error (SWMSE) subject to constraints ensuring valid partitions. This QUBO formulation is solved using annealing machines (simulated annealing or momentum annealing), which can efficiently explore the exponential search space of possible splitting conditions.

## Key Results
- The method successfully finds complex splitting conditions that achieve lower mean squared error than conventional single-feature splits on both synthetic and real datasets
- For synthetic datasets with known optimal multi-feature splits, the method finds the optimal solution in most trials
- On the Ames Housing dataset, the method achieves comparable or better performance than conventional regression trees while enabling more expressive splitting rules

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Complex splitting conditions in regression trees can be represented as logical-product conditions encoded by binary vectors and optimized via QUBO formulation.
- Mechanism: The paper binarizes continuous and categorical features into basic conditions, then represents complex multi-feature splitting rules as binary vectors (splitting vectors) where each element indicates whether a basic condition is used. This logical-product representation enables encoding complex multi-dimensional boundaries as binary variables suitable for QUBO optimization.
- Core assumption: Any multi-dimensional splitting condition can be expressed as a logical product of basic conditions without loss of expressiveness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The key idea is to binarize continuous and categorical features into basic conditions and represent complex splitting conditions as logical-product conditions encoded by binary vectors."
  - [section]: "The logical-product condition used for splitting is represented by a binary vector of length NB... Each element indicates whether to use each basic condition: if the value is 1, the condition is used; and if the value is 0, it is not used."

- Break condition: If the logical-product representation cannot capture certain complex splitting patterns (e.g., disjunctions or non-linear boundaries that cannot be factored), the approach would fail to find optimal splits.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Annealing machines can efficiently explore the exponentially large search space of complex splitting conditions.
- Mechanism: By formulating the splitting optimization as a QUBO problem, the annealing machine can simultaneously explore multiple potential splitting configurations through quantum or simulated annealing, avoiding the exponential complexity of exhaustive search.
- Core assumption: The QUBO formulation accurately captures the optimization objective (SWMSE) and constraints while being solvable by annealing hardware within practical time limits.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed method transforms the training process to QUBO, which enables an annealing machine to solve this problem."
  - [section]: "The annealing technique is a stochastic approximation algorithm for finding a state such that a given function is minimized... we propose two techniques. First, for the search problem of finding a splitting to reduce the prediction error in regression trees, we formulate the problem as a QUBO problem that can be solved by an annealing machine."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors don't discuss annealing machines or QUBO formulations.
- Break condition: If the annealing machine cannot find low-energy states corresponding to good solutions, or if the QUBO problem size exceeds hardware capabilities, the approach would fail to find better splits.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The square weighted mean squared error (SWMSE) can be formulated as a QUBO problem while preserving the optimization objective of MSE.
- Mechanism: The paper transforms the MSE calculation by using squared weights instead of proportional weights, eliminating division operations involving variables and making the objective function quadratic in the QUBO variables.
- Core assumption: The SWMSE transformation preserves the relative ordering of split quality compared to MSE, so minimizing SWMSE leads to minimizing MSE.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "However, the MSE is difficult to formulate in a QUBO problem because it involves division using the variable NSb... Here, because NS is the total number of samples and thus a constant, this is not a problem. Hence, we propose the square weighted MSE (SWMSE)."

- Break condition: If the SWMSE transformation distorts the optimization landscape significantly, the annealing machine might find splits that minimize SWMSE but don't minimize true MSE.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Binarization -> QUBO formulation -> Annealing optimization -> Solution extraction -> Tree construction
- Critical path: Feature binarization → QUBO formulation → Annealing optimization → Solution validation → Split application
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of basic conditions (NB) vs. QUBO problem size: More basic conditions increase expressiveness but exponentially increase search space
  - Maximum conditions per split (M) vs. constraint tightness: Larger M allows more complex splits but may lead to local optima
  - SWMSE vs. MSE: SWMSE enables QUBO formulation but may distort optimization slightly
  - Annealing method selection: Quantum vs. simulated vs. momentum annealing based on problem size and hardware availability
- Failure signatures:
  - No valid splits found: Constraints may be too tight or basic conditions insufficient
  - Poor split quality: Annealing not converging to good solutions, or SWMSE transformation distorting optimization
  - Excessive computation time: QUBO problem too large for available annealing resources
  - Redundant conditions in final splits: M parameter too large, causing overfitting to noise
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Single-feature split validation: Verify the method finds the same splits as conventional decision trees when M=1 and basic conditions are properly prepared
  2. Synthetic multi-feature split: Use the K=2 synthetic dataset to confirm the method can find logical-product conditions that perfectly separate the classes
  3. Real data comparison: Apply to Ames Housing dataset with M=2-3 to find splits that achieve lower MSE than single-feature splits while maintaining interpretability

## Limitations
- The logical-product representation may not capture all possible complex splitting patterns, particularly disjunctive or highly non-linear boundaries
- SWMSE transformation may introduce optimization distortions that affect split quality
- Computational complexity grows exponentially with the number of basic conditions, potentially limiting scalability

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core QUBO formulation approach and the basic mechanism of binarizing features and encoding logical-product conditions as binary vectors
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that annealing machines can find better splits than conventional greedy methods, based on limited synthetic and real-world experiments
- **Low confidence**: The general applicability of this approach to diverse real-world datasets and the scalability to large feature spaces
