# Absformer: Transformer-based Model for Unsupervised Multi-Document Abstractive Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04787
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04787
- Reference count: 40
- Proposes Absformer, a Transformer-based unsupervised abstractive multi-document summarization method

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Absformer, a novel approach for unsupervised abstractive multi-document summarization (MDS) that leverages pretrained Transformers in a two-phase process. The method first clusters documents using a DistilBERT encoder (optionally fine-tuned with labels), then generates summaries using a Transformer decoder trained to reconstruct documents from frozen encoder embeddings. The decoder is initialized with encoder weights for faster convergence. Experiments on Amazon, Yelp, and network ticket datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, particularly on Amazon and Yelp, though the evaluation on network ticket data relies on surrogate metrics rather than reference summaries.

## Method Summary
Absformer operates in two distinct phases: document clustering and summary generation. In the clustering phase, documents are encoded using a frozen or fine-tuned DistilBERT model, and clusters are formed using either the K-means algorithm or by incorporating label information. In the generation phase, a Transformer-based decoder is trained to reconstruct documents from the frozen encoder embeddings, then generates summaries by producing text from cluster centers. A key innovation is initializing the decoder weights using the encoder weights, which accelerates convergence and improves performance. The model employs a weighted loss function that emphasizes important words in the reconstruction process.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art ROUGE scores on Amazon and Yelp datasets compared to existing unsupervised abstractive MDS methods
- Demonstrates improved surrogate metrics (FAISS similarity) on network ticket data
- Ablation study confirms the importance of decoder initialization, pretraining, label information, and weighted loss components
- Shows consistent performance across three diverse domains: product reviews, business reviews, and technical support tickets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Absformer method succeeds by leveraging the semantic understanding capabilities of pretrained Transformers while maintaining the unsupervised nature of the task. The two-phase approach allows the model to first identify coherent document clusters before generating summaries, ensuring that generated summaries capture the essential information from each cluster. The decoder initialization with encoder weights provides a strong starting point for the generation task, reducing the learning burden on the decoder. The weighted loss function helps the model focus on reconstructing important content words rather than function words, leading to more informative summaries.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
DistilBERT encoder → K-means clustering → Transformer decoder (initialized from encoder) → Summary generation

**Critical Path**
Document encoding → Clustering → Decoder training (reconstruction) → Summary generation from cluster centers

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Uses frozen encoder for stability vs. fine-tuning for domain adaptation
- Two-phase approach for better coherence vs. end-to-end training for simplicity
- Weighted loss for important content vs. uniform loss for training stability

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor clustering leads to incoherent summaries mixing unrelated topics
- Inadequate decoder initialization results in slow convergence or suboptimal summaries
- Overfitting to reconstruction task may produce summaries that don't generalize well

**First Experiments**
1. Test document clustering quality using FAISS similarity on held-out documents
2. Evaluate decoder reconstruction accuracy on validation documents before summary generation
3. Measure impact of different cluster numbers on summary coherence and ROUGE scores

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, though several areas for future research are implied by the limitations discussed.

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on ROUGE metrics which may not fully capture semantic quality of abstractive summaries
- Limited evaluation scope to three specific domains without testing on diverse or multilingual datasets
- Network ticket dataset results based on surrogate metrics rather than human-annotated reference summaries
