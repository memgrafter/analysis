# Interpretability from a new lens: Integrating Stratification and Domain knowledge for Biomedical Applications

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09322
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09322
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Proposes a computational strategy for integrating domain knowledge interpretation techniques into interpretable ML frameworks for biomedical applications using stratified k-fold cross-validation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a computational strategy for integrating domain knowledge interpretation techniques into interpretable machine learning (IML) frameworks specifically for biomedical applications. The approach addresses the curse of dimensionality in biomedical datasets by implementing stratified k-fold cross-validation and aggregating feature importance scores across folds. The aggregated feature importance scores are then linked to domain knowledge databases for pathway functional enrichment, drug targeting, and repurposing analyses. The authors emphasize the importance of involving end-users and clinicians in focus group discussions to guide testable hypotheses and improve performance metrics.

## Method Summary
The method involves stratifying biomedical datasets into k-fold cross-validation splits, training IML models on each fold, extracting and aggregating feature importance scores, and linking these aggregated scores to domain knowledge databases for pathway enrichment and drug repurposing analyses. The framework aims to improve model stability and interpretability in high-dimensional biomedical data by preserving class distributions across folds and translating abstract ML outputs into clinically meaningful insights through domain knowledge integration.

## Key Results
- Stratified k-fold cross-validation preserves class distributions and prevents data leakage
- Aggregated feature importance scores provide more stable and generalizable rankings across folds
- Domain knowledge integration improves interpretability and trust for domain experts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Stratified k-fold cross-validation improves model stability in high-dimensional biomedical data.
- **Mechanism:** Splitting the dataset into stratified folds preserves class distributions across training and testing sets, preventing data leakage.
- **Core assumption:** The stratification process correctly preserves the underlying class balance and does not introduce bias.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "stratification of biomedical problem datasets into k-fold cross-validation (CVs)"; [section] "The introduction of cross-validation splits before training will improve model stability and generalizability abilities."
- **Break condition:** If stratification fails to preserve class distributions, model stability gains may be minimal or reversed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Aggregating feature importance scores across k-fold cross-validation folds provides more stable and generalizable importance rankings.
- **Mechanism:** Individual folds may overfit or underfit specific subsets of the data. Aggregating importance scores across folds smooths out fold-specific noise.
- **Core assumption:** Feature importance rankings are consistent across folds and not dominated by noise in individual folds.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "the model outcome, such as aggregated feature weight importance across the k-fold CVs"; [section] "Feature importance from the best estimator models can then be aggregated for further domain knowledge analysis."
- **Break condition:** If feature importance rankings vary wildly across folds due to high instability, aggregation may produce misleading rankings.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Linking aggregated feature importance scores to domain knowledge databases improves interpretability and trust for domain experts.
- **Mechanism:** Raw feature importance scores from ML models are abstract numbers. Mapping these scores to known biological pathways or drug targets translates decisions into domain-relevant insights.
- **Core assumption:** The features in the dataset have meaningful mappings to existing domain knowledge databases.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "the model outcome, such as aggregated feature weight importance, can be linked to further domain knowledge interpretations using techniques like pathway functional enrichment"; [section] "automatically linking the most ranked important features from trained IML models with potential domain knowledge databases can ease interpretations and implications of findings for domain experts."
- **Break condition:** If feature names or identifiers do not map cleanly to domain databases, the linking process may fail or produce irrelevant associations.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: k-fold cross-validation**
  - **Why needed here:** To assess model performance and stability across different data subsets, reducing overfitting risk in high-dimensional biomedical datasets.
  - **Quick check question:** In k-fold cross-validation, how many times is the model trained and tested when k=5?

- **Concept: Feature importance aggregation**
  - **Why needed here:** To combine feature rankings from multiple model runs (folds) into a single, more stable ranking.
  - **Quick check question:** If a feature ranks 1st in fold 1, 3rd in fold 2, and 2nd in fold 3, what is its aggregated rank?

- **Concept: Domain knowledge integration**
  - **Why needed here:** To translate abstract model outputs into clinically meaningful insights by mapping features to known biological or medical entities.
  - **Quick check question:** Why is it important to link feature importance scores to pathway databases in biomedical ML?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data preprocessing → Stratification → k-fold CV splitter → Model training pipeline → Feature importance extraction → Aggregation module → Domain knowledge linking → Visualization and reporting
- **Critical path:** Data → Stratification → k-fold CV → Train models → Extract importances → Aggregate → Link to domain → Report
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - More folds (higher k) → better stability but higher computational cost
  - More IML models → potentially better feature coverage but increased complexity
  - Granularity of domain mapping → more detailed insights but risk of missing mappings
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High variance in feature importance rankings across folds → stratification or model instability
  - Poor or no mappings to domain databases → feature naming/identifier issues
  - Aggregated importances do not correlate with known biology → model or data quality issues
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run stratified 5-fold CV on a small biomedical dataset with a single IML model; verify class balance in each fold.
  2. Train multiple IML models (e.g., Random Forest, XGBoost) on the same stratified folds; compare feature importance stability.
  3. Link top 10 aggregated features to a pathway database (e.g., Reactome); manually verify at least one correct mapping.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks detailed methodological specifics including exact biomedical datasets, specific IML algorithms, and precise domain knowledge databases integrated.
- The feature importance aggregation mechanism is described but not empirically validated within the paper.
- The involvement of end-users and clinicians in focus group discussions is mentioned but not detailed, raising questions about reproducibility.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The general framework concept of integrating domain knowledge into IML for biomedical applications is sound and aligns with established ML practices.
- **Medium Confidence:** The proposed mechanisms for stratified k-fold CV and feature importance aggregation are theoretically plausible but lack direct empirical validation in the paper.
- **Low Confidence:** The specific implementation details (datasets, algorithms, domain databases) are insufficiently specified, making exact reproduction challenging.
