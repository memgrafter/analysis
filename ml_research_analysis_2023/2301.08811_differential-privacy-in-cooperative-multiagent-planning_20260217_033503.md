# Differential Privacy in Cooperative Multiagent Planning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08811
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08811
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: Framework for differential privacy in cooperative multiagent planning, achieving 3% performance loss under private communications versus 86% for baseline policies

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for applying differential privacy to cooperative multiagent planning problems, specifically Markov games with reach-avoid objectives. The authors develop an online privacy mechanism that perturbs agents' communicated symbolic state trajectories while preserving team performance. They demonstrate that the tradeoff between privacy strength and team performance critically depends on the total correlation among agents' state-action processes, and synthesize policies robust to privacy by minimizing this correlation.

## Method Summary
The framework applies an online differential privacy mechanism to privatize inter-agent communications in Markov games with reach-avoid objectives. During policy synthesis, a central planner optimizes local policies to balance reach-avoid success probability against total correlation among agents. During execution, each agent generates private state trajectories using the privacy mechanism and executes local policies based on these private communications. The approach uses a convex-concave procedure to solve the synthesis optimization problem that combines both objectives.

## Key Results
- Minimum-dependency policies achieve only 3% performance loss under private communications versus 86% for baseline policies
- Privacy-performance tradeoff depends critically on total correlation among agents' state-action processes
- The framework provides formal differential privacy guarantees while maintaining team performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Differential privacy preserves agent privacy while maintaining team performance by perturbing state trajectories with controlled noise
- Mechanism: An online privacy mechanism perturbs each agent's symbolic state trajectory at every timestep using a conditional probability distribution that depends on the current state and previous private state. The mechanism ensures differential privacy by controlling the probability of outputting the true state
- Core assumption: The mechanism can generate private state trajectories that are feasible with respect to the underlying MDP dynamics while providing formal privacy guarantees
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We apply a differential privacy mechanism to privatize agents' communicated symbolic state trajectories"
  - [section 4.1]: "Algorithm 1: Online Mechanism Construction" describes the conditional probability distribution for generating private states
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence of similar mechanisms for symbolic state trajectories in multiagent systems
- Break condition: If the probability of outputting the true state is too low, the policy execution will degrade significantly even with total correlation minimization

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Total correlation between agents' state-action processes determines the performance tradeoff under private communications
- Mechanism: The total correlation measures dependencies between agents' behaviors. Policies with lower total correlation are more robust to privacy because agents' actions depend less on each other's states
- Core assumption: Minimizing total correlation during policy synthesis will produce policies that maintain high performance even when communications are private
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5]: "The total correlation Cπ of a joint policy π = {π i}N i=1 is Cπ = Σ N i=1H(Si 0Ai 0 . . . S i η ) − H(S0A0 . . . Sη )"
  - [section 6]: "We aim to maximize the reach-avoid probability under private communications by minimizing the lower bound on vpr given in Theorem 1"
  - [corpus]: Weak - limited corpus evidence of total correlation being used as a regularizer for privacy-robust multiagent policies
- Break condition: If the minimum achievable total correlation is still high, even the optimized policies will suffer significant performance degradation under privacy

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Optimizing for both success probability and total correlation produces policies that outperform baseline policies under private communications
- Mechanism: The synthesis optimization problem includes terms for both the truthful communication success probability and total correlation, allowing a tradeoff between performance and privacy robustness
- Core assumption: The concave-convex procedure can find local optima that balance these competing objectives effectively
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6]: "supπvtr − δltr − βC π" shows the optimization objective combining success probability and total correlation
  - [section 7]: "Minimum-dependency policies are 80% more performant than the baseline under private communications"
  - [corpus]: Weak - limited corpus evidence of this specific optimization formulation for privacy-robust multiagent policies
- Break condition: If the coefficients δ and β are poorly chosen, the optimization may prioritize one objective too heavily at the expense of the other

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Online privacy mechanism (Algorithm 1) - generates private state trajectories
  - Policy execution with private communications (Algorithm 2) - agents execute local policies using private information
  - Policy synthesis optimization - finds policies that balance success probability and total correlation
  - Reach-avoid objective - defines the team's shared goal

- Critical path:
  1. Central planner synthesizes local policies using optimization
  2. During execution, each agent generates private states using the online mechanism
  3. Agents share private states and execute their local policies based on this information
  4. The team attempts to reach target states while avoiding avoid states

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Privacy strength (epsilon) vs. performance - stronger privacy reduces performance
  - Total correlation vs. performance - lower correlation improves privacy robustness but may reduce performance
  - Communication overhead - agents must share private states at every timestep

- Failure signatures:
  - Large gap between truthful and private communication performance indicates poor privacy robustness
  - High total correlation values suggest policies are too interdependent
  - Low probability of true transitions (tau_epsilon) indicates very strong privacy that may degrade performance

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare performance of minimum-dependency policies vs. baseline policies under varying privacy levels (epsilon)
  2. Measure the relationship between total correlation values and performance under private communications
  3. Test policy robustness across different initial configurations and privacy levels in the SysAdmin example

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the minimum-dependency policies scale with the number of agents N?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The theoretical bound in Theorem 1 shows that privacy performance depends on the term ((ρm − 1)e−ϵ/k + 1)^N, suggesting potential exponential degradation with N
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates two-agent and four-agent scenarios, not larger teams
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments scaling to 10+ agents would reveal whether the N-dependence is manageable or prohibitive

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal value of the adjacency parameter k for maximizing privacy-performance tradeoff?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper fixes k = 3 for two-agent navigation and k = 1 for SysAdmin, but doesn't explore this parameter systematically
- Why unresolved: Different values of k could significantly affect the privacy mechanism's effectiveness
- What evidence would resolve it: A parameter sweep over k values for the same problems would identify the optimal setting

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust are the minimum-dependency policies to different types of transition dynamics beyond the slip probability used?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The experiments use a fixed 5% slip probability, but don't test other stochasticity levels or different transition structures
- Why unresolved: The total correlation regularization might behave differently under varying dynamics
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing with different transition probabilities (0%, 10%, 20%) and non-grid environments would show sensitivity to dynamics

## Limitations

- The framework relies on a central planner for policy synthesis, which may not be practical in decentralized systems
- The total correlation metric may not capture all aspects of inter-agent dependencies that affect privacy robustness
- The convex-concave procedure for optimization provides only local optima, potentially missing globally optimal privacy-performance tradeoffs

## Confidence

- **High**: The mathematical formulation of differential privacy and total correlation metrics
- **Medium**: The effectiveness of total correlation minimization for improving privacy robustness
- **Medium**: The 3% vs 86% performance comparison between minimum-dependency and baseline policies
