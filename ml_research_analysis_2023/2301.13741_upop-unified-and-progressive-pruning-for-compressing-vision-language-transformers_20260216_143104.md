# UPop: Unified and Progressive Pruning for Compressing Vision-Language Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13741
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13741
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Achieves up to 10× compression of vision-language Transformers while maintaining or improving accuracy across multiple tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UPop, a unified and progressive pruning framework for compressing vision-language Transformers. The method enables adaptive assignment of pruning ratios across different modalities and structures through unified search, and progressively prunes and retrains the model to maintain convergence at higher compression ratios. Experiments demonstrate significant performance improvements compared to mask-based pruning baselines, achieving up to 10× compression while maintaining accuracy across generative and discriminative vision-language tasks as well as unimodal image classification.

## Method Summary
UPop combines unified search and progressive pruning to compress vision-language Transformers. The unified search phase jointly optimizes model parameters and trainable masks with ℓ1 regularization, grouping compressible components by computation mechanisms (attention vs MLP) and ranking weights across different components within each group. The progressive pruning phase uses a custom optimizer to update masks based on accumulated gradients, ensuring mask magnitudes converge to zero before retraining. This eliminates the parameter gap between searched model and pruned subnet, enabling successful convergence at higher compression ratios. The framework is validated on multiple tasks including visual reasoning (NLVR2), image captioning (COCO), visual question answering (VQA), image-text retrieval (Flickr30K), and unimodal image classification (ImageNet).

## Key Results
- Achieves 3.76-3.88% accuracy improvement on NLVR2 at 2× compression compared to mask-based pruning
- Successfully converges at 3× compression ratio while mask-based pruning fails
- Maintains performance at extreme 10× compression on multiple tasks
- Demonstrates versatility across both generative and discriminative vision-language tasks as well as unimodal image classification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Unified Pruning enables adaptive assignment of pruning ratios across different modalities and structures.
- Mechanism: Groups masks by computation mechanisms and ranks weights across different components within each group using z-score standardization to make magnitudes comparable.
- Core assumption: Z-score standardization can make mask magnitudes comparable across different structures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "unifiedly searching multimodal subnets in a continuous optimization space from the original model, which enables automatic assignment of pruning ratios among compressible modalities and structures"
  - [section 3.2.1] "We notice that simply uniting different structures degrades performance, and the reason is that the magnitude of the learned masks ζi used for different structures vary greatly...For the simplicity of implementation, we individually transform the mean and standard deviation of magnitudes distributions of different structures' mask to the 0 and 1 by z-score standardization"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Progressive Pruning maintains convergence between search and retrain phases, enabling higher compression ratios.
- Mechanism: Progressively updates trainable masks with custom optimizer ensuring mask magnitudes converge to zero before retraining, eliminating parameter gap.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "progressively searching and retraining the subnet, which maintains convergence between the search and retrain to attain higher compression ratios"
  - [section 3.2.2] "the core idea is to ensure each magnitude of the trainable mask ζ corresponding to the eliminated neurons in compressible components converges to 0...which ensures that as pt progressively increases to p, each magnitude of mask ζ corresponding to the removed neurons in compressible components will exactly converge to 0"
  - [section 4.3] "At the higher 4×, 5×, and 10× compression ratio, the Progressive Pruning can still enable the compressed model to converge successfully, while both Mask-based Pruning and Unified Pruning fail"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The unified search space enables better compression performance than separate modality-specific pruning.
- Mechanism: Combines all compressible components into unified search space and ranks them together to discover more optimal compression patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "At the 2× compression ratio, Table 3 shows that compared to the Mask-based Pruning, Unified Pruning gains 3.76% and 3.88% accuracy improvement on the dev set and test set, respectively"
  - [section 4.2] "Furthermore, Unified Pruning converges successfully at the 3× compression ratio, while Mask-based Pruning does not"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Original model: Contains vision encoder (ViT), language encoder (BERT), and cross-attention layers
  - Trainable masks: ζv_att, ζv_mlp, ζl_att, ζl_mlp, ζc0_att, ζc1_att for NLVR2 task
  - Unified search space: Combines attention masks and MLP masks separately
  - Progressive compression: Custom optimizer that updates masks based on accumulated gradients

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize trainable masks to ones
  2. Search phase: Jointly optimize model parameters and masks with ℓ1 regularization
  3. Progressive mask updates based on accumulated gradients
  4. Retrain phase: Fine-tune pruned subnet

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Unified vs separate search: Unified enables adaptive ratio assignment but increases search space complexity
  - Progressive vs two-stage pruning: Progressive maintains convergence but requires more complex optimization
  - Structured vs unstructured pruning: Structured enables deployment but may be less flexible

- Failure signatures:
  - Mask magnitudes not converging to zero during progressive pruning
  - Performance degradation when unifying search across different structures
  - Convergence failure at high compression ratios

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare Unified Pruning vs Mask-based Pruning at 2× compression on NLVR2 task
  2. Test Progressive Pruning by comparing search-only vs search+retrain scenarios
  3. Validate on unimodal image classification task (DeiT on ImageNet) to verify versatility

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed unified search strategy compare to a manual assignment of compression ratios across different modalities and structures in terms of computational efficiency and final performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that unified search enables adaptive assignment of pruning ratios, which is more efficient and likely sub-optimal compared to manual assignment.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a direct comparison between unified search and manual assignment in terms of computational cost or performance metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing unified search to manual assignment in terms of training time, final accuracy, and compression ratio achieved.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal frequency for updating the compression mask ζ during the progressive pruning process?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores different frequencies (1, 10, 50) and finds that updating too frequently leads to overfitting.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a theoretical framework for determining the optimal frequency, and the optimal frequency may vary depending on the model architecture and dataset.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying the frequency of mask updates across different models and datasets to identify the optimal frequency for each scenario.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed progressive pruning paradigm affect the convergence of the pruned subnet compared to traditional two-stage pruning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper argues that progressive pruning eliminates the parameter gap between the searched model and the pruned subnet, leading to better convergence.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the convergence behavior of the pruned subnet under different pruning paradigms.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study of the convergence curves of the pruned subnet under progressive pruning and traditional two-stage pruning, using metrics such as training loss and validation accuracy over time.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks ablation studies on the necessity of z-score standardization for unified mask magnitudes, which is a critical component of the unified pruning mechanism.
- The progressive pruning mechanism relies on a custom optimizer without rigorous theoretical convergence guarantees.
- The framework's effectiveness on very high compression ratios (10×) is demonstrated only on a limited set of tasks and models.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The unified search mechanism's ability to adaptively assign pruning ratios across components is well-supported by experimental results showing consistent improvements over baseline methods.
- Medium confidence: The progressive pruning mechanism's success in maintaining convergence at high compression ratios is empirically demonstrated but lacks rigorous theoretical justification.
- Medium confidence: The versatility claim across both generative and discriminative tasks is supported by experiments on multiple benchmarks, but the paper does not explore edge cases or failure scenarios in depth.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct ablation studies testing the impact of z-score standardization on unified pruning performance by comparing against alternative normalization methods or no normalization at all.
2. Implement theoretical analysis of the progressive pruning optimizer to derive convergence guarantees and identify conditions under which it might fail, particularly at extreme compression ratios.
3. Test the UPop framework on additional vision-language architectures beyond BLIP and CLIP, including models with different attention mechanisms or tokenization strategies, to assess generalization capabilities.
