# RobArch: Designing Robust Architectures against Adversarial Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03110
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03110
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RobArch models achieve state-of-the-art adversarial robustness, with RobArch-S outperforming ResNet-50 by 9.18% on AutoAttack

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first systematic large-scale study on how deep neural network architecture components affect adversarial robustness under fixed parameter budgets. Through training over 150 models on ImageNet, the authors distill 18 actionable guidelines for designing robust architectures. The key insight is that architectural choices significantly impact robustness beyond what can be achieved through training alone. Based on these guidelines, the authors introduce RobArch models that outperform state-of-the-art ConvNets and Transformers against strong adversarial attacks, achieving new records on the RobustBench ImageNet leaderboard.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted an extensive empirical study by systematically varying architectural components while keeping parameter budgets fixed. They trained over 150 models on ImageNet, systematically testing the robustness impact of various design choices including network depth vs width, normalization layers, downsampling factors, SE blocks, inverted bottlenecks, dilation rates, and activation layer configurations. The robustness was evaluated using AutoAttack, a strong adversarial attack benchmark. Based on these empirical findings, the authors distilled 18 actionable guidelines and used them to design the RobArch family of models that optimize for both accuracy and robustness.

## Key Results
- Deeper networks are more effective than wider networks for improving robustness under fixed parameter budgets
- Adding Squeeze and Excitation (SE) blocks and removing the first normalization layer effectively boost robustness
- Reducing the downsampling factor in the stem stage improves robustness
- Inverted bottlenecks, large dilation factors, Instance Normalization, and reducing activation layers harm robustness
- RobArch-S achieves 9.18 percentage points higher AutoAttack accuracy than ResNet-50 on RobustBench ImageNet
- RobArch-L achieves new state-of-the-art AutoAttack accuracy on the RobustBench leaderboard

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that architectural design choices directly impact a model's ability to maintain clean accuracy under adversarial perturbations. By systematically testing components, the authors show that certain architectural patterns create inherent resistance to adversarial examples through improved feature representation and gradient flow. The guidelines distill which components provide robustness benefits versus those that create vulnerabilities, enabling the design of architectures that are intrinsically more robust rather than relying solely on robust training procedures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Adversarial robustness**: The ability of models to maintain performance under adversarial attacks - needed to understand the research problem; quick check: can be measured by attack success rate on perturbed inputs
- **Architectural component analysis**: Systematic study of how individual design choices affect model properties - needed to isolate robustness factors; quick check: involves controlled experiments with one variable changed at a time
- **Parameter budget constraints**: Fixed model capacity requirements - needed to ensure fair architectural comparisons; quick check: total number of learnable parameters remains constant across experiments
- **AutoAttack evaluation**: Multi-method adversarial attack benchmark - needed to assess true robustness; quick check: combines multiple attack strategies to avoid overestimation
- **SE blocks (Squeeze-and-Excitation)**: Channel-wise attention mechanism - needed to understand one of the key robustness-enhancing components; quick check: recalibrates channel responses through learned weights
- **Normalization layers**: Batch/Instance Normalization - needed to understand their differential impact on robustness; quick check: stabilizes training but may affect adversarial vulnerability differently

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Input -> Stem (conv + BN + activation) -> Multiple Residual Blocks -> Classification Head

**Critical Path:** The stem stage and residual blocks are most critical for robustness. The first normalization layer removal and downsampling factor in the stem, plus the choice of bottleneck type and SE block inclusion in residual blocks, form the critical path for robustness.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Depth vs width shows that adding layers provides better robustness gains than increasing channel width under fixed parameter budgets. This contradicts conventional wisdom that favors width for accuracy. The tradeoff involves balancing architectural robustness benefits against potential training difficulty and computational costs.

**Failure Signatures:** Models with inverted bottlenecks, large dilation factors, or Instance Normalization show significant robustness degradation. Removing activation layers also harms robustness, indicating that non-linear transformations are crucial for creating robust feature representations.

## Limitations
- Findings are based exclusively on ImageNet, limiting generalizability to other domains
- Primary evaluation uses AutoAttack, potentially missing robustness against other threat models
- The study represents a finite subset of the vast architectural design space
- Fixed parameter budget constraint may mask budget-dependent architectural trade-offs

## Confidence
**High confidence:** Basic depth/width relationships and specific component-level findings (SE blocks improve, first BN removal helps)
**Medium confidence:** Cross-component interactions and specific RobArch designs due to precise hyperparameter dependencies
**Low confidence:** Generalization to non-ImageNet tasks and robustness against non-AutoAttack threat models
