# Self-supervised Auxiliary Loss for Metric Learning in Music Similarity-based Retrieval and Auto-tagging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07449
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07449
- Reference count: 15
- This study improves music similarity-based retrieval and auto-tagging by integrating self-supervised auxiliary loss with metric learning

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of improving music similarity-based retrieval and auto-tagging systems by leveraging self-supervised learning. The authors propose a model that integrates metric learning with a self-supervised auxiliary loss, diverging from conventional methods by concurrently training with both self-supervision and supervision signals without freezing pre-trained models. Their approach also refrains from using augmentation during the fine-tuning phase, yielding better results. Experiments on MagnaTagATune and MTG-Jamendo datasets demonstrate that the proposed methodology enhances retrieval and tagging performance metrics, particularly in scenarios where human-annotated tags are limited.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses a SampleCNN architecture with layer normalization, trained in two phases: pre-training with contrastive self-supervised loss (CLMR) and fine-tuning with combined self-supervised and metric learning losses. The model processes 59049-sample audio excerpts at 22.05 kHz, creating positive pairs from augmented excerpts of the same track for self-supervised learning. During fine-tuning, the entire network is trained end-to-end without freezing pre-trained weights, and augmentation is omitted for self-supervised learning to improve data quality. The method uses a balancing factor λ to combine the self-supervised contrastive loss and supervised metric learning loss.

## Key Results
- The proposed method improves retrieval and tagging performance on MagnaTagATune and MTG-Jamendo datasets
- Concurrent training without freezing pre-trained models yields better results than conventional self-supervised approaches
- Omitting augmentation during fine-tuning produces higher quality training data and improved performance metrics

- Mechanism: The model uses contrastive learning (SimCLR) to create positive pairs from augmented excerpts of the same track, forcing the network to learn representations where similar tracks are closer in the embedding space. This self-supervised signal acts as an auxiliary loss alongside the supervised metric learning loss, allowing the model to capture both tag-based and audio-based similarity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Audio excerpt (59049 samples) -> SampleCNN -> Hidden representation -> Layer normalization -> Embedding vector -> Classification weights -> Tag probabilities
- Critical path:
  1. Audio excerpt → SampleCNN → Hidden representation
  2. Hidden representation → Layer normalization → Embedding vector
  3. Embedding vector → Classification weights → Tag probabilities
  4. Compute LSSL from augmented pairs and LML from labeled data
  5. Combine losses and backpropagate through entire network
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Freezing vs. fine-tuning pre-trained models: Concurrent training without freezing provides better performance but requires more careful hyperparameter tuning
  - Augmentation during fine-tuning: Omitting augmentation improves quality but may reduce robustness
  - Balance between LSSL and LML: The weighting factor λ needs careful tuning based on dataset characteristics
- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting to training data: Check if validation performance degrades while training performance improves
  - Mode collapse in contrastive learning: Check if embeddings become too similar or too dissimilar
  - Conflicting gradients: Check if training becomes unstable when combining supervised and self-supervised losses
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with only LML (supervised metric learning) to establish baseline performance
  2. Train with only LSSL (self-supervised contrastive learning) to evaluate self-supervised learning capability
  3. Train with LSSML using λ=1 to test the combined approach and find optimal λ value through grid search

## Limitations

- The findings may not generalize beyond the two specific datasets (MagnaTagATune and MTG-Jamendo) used in experiments
- The concurrent training approach without freezing pre-trained models introduces additional hyperparameter tuning complexity that isn't fully explored
- The claim about omitting augmentation during fine-tuning improving results lacks extensive ablation studies across different dataset sizes and characteristics
