# Reverse Engineering Breast MRIs: Predicting Acquisition Parameters Directly from Images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04911
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04911
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 37
- Key outcome: A multi-task neural network predicts 12 MRI acquisition parameters (IAPs) from breast MRI images with >97% top-1 accuracy for 6 out of 10 categorical IAPs and >84% for contrast agent type, enabling improved model selection for cancer classification.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method to predict MRI acquisition parameters directly from breast MRI images using a multi-task neural network. The model predicts 12 IAPs including field strength, echo and repetition times, scanner model, and contrast agent type. By training on a dataset of 14,000 2D slices from 922 patients, the authors demonstrate that images contain sufficient information to accurately infer their acquisition parameters. The model achieves high accuracy for most IAPs and shows practical utility in improving cancer classification model selection by 8.13% when sorting unlabeled data into domains.

## Method Summary
The method uses a ResNet-18 backbone with a final fully-connected layer sized to predict all 12 IAPs simultaneously. The model outputs predictions for 10 categorical IAPs (field strength, contrast agent, scanner manufacturer, etc.) and 2 continuous IAPs (TE and TR) using a combined loss function of cross-entropy for categorical and MSE for continuous outputs. Trained on 9,952 breast MRI slices with Adam optimizer (lr=0.001, wd=0.0001) for 100 epochs, the model processes 224x224 images and performs a single forward pass to predict all IAPs.

## Key Results
- >97% top-1 accuracy for 6 out of 10 categorical IAPs (Field Strength, Contrast Agent, Scanner Manufacturer, Scanner Model, RF Coils, Pulse Sequence)
- >84% top-1 accuracy for the more challenging Contrast Agent Type parameter
- Top-2 accuracy for all categorical IAPs is at least 5% higher than top-1 accuracy
- Using predicted IAPs for model selection in cancer classification improves accuracy by 8.13% compared to random assignment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The model can infer image acquisition parameters from image appearance because certain IAPs induce predictable changes in image appearance.
- Mechanism: The model learns the mapping between IAP-induced visual patterns and their corresponding parameter values during training. For example, field strength affects contrast-to-noise ratio and spatial resolution, while echo and repetition times affect tissue contrast. These visual patterns serve as cues for the model to predict the IAPs.
- Core assumption: The relationship between IAPs and image appearance is sufficiently deterministic and consistent across the dataset.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Predicting IAPs from the images is an important step toward better understanding the relationship between image appearance and IAPs."
  - [section] "Even if large training datasets are used that cover a variety of IAPs, it can still be common for datasets to only represent a fraction of all possible MRI IAP settings"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss similar inverse problems but not specifically IAP prediction from MRI images.
- Break condition: If IAPs produce visually similar images across different parameter values, the model cannot distinguish them.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-task learning enables the model to learn shared representations that improve IAP prediction accuracy.
- Mechanism: By predicting all IAPs simultaneously with a single model, the network learns a shared feature representation that captures common information across IAPs. This is more efficient than training separate models for each IAP and allows the model to leverage correlations between IAPs.
- Core assumption: There are shared features in the image that are informative for predicting multiple IAPs simultaneously.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We thought it more practical and scalable to use a single network/forward pass to predict all IAPs at once, rather than train a seperate model for each IAP."
  - [section] "We use a loss function that combines cross-entropy losses for the categorical IAPs with mean squared error (MSE) losses for the continuous IAPs"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss multi-task learning but not specifically for IAP prediction.
- Break condition: If different IAPs require completely different feature representations with no overlap, multi-task learning could hurt performance compared to specialized models.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The model generalizes to unseen IAP combinations because it learns fundamental relationships between IAPs and image appearance.
- Mechanism: The model captures underlying principles of how IAPs affect image formation rather than memorizing specific combinations. This allows it to handle new combinations of IAPs that weren't present in the training data.
- Core assumption: The model learns generalizable patterns rather than overfitting to specific IAP combinations in the training set.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the majority of unique IAP combinations of images in the test set are not present in the training set"
  - [section] "The generalizability our model is promising given that the majority of unique IAP combinations present in test images were unseen during training"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss generalization but not specifically for IAP prediction.
- Break condition: If the relationship between IAPs and image appearance is highly non-linear or depends on complex interactions that weren't captured in the training data, the model will fail on unseen combinations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MRI slice → ResNet-18 feature extraction → Final fully-connected layer → IAP predictions
- Critical path: Input MRI slice → ResNet-18 feature extraction → Final fully-connected layer → IAP predictions. The most critical component is the feature extraction stage that must capture IAP-relevant information from the image.
- Design tradeoffs: Single model vs. separate models for each IAP (efficiency vs. specialization), ResNet-18 vs. deeper architectures (computational cost vs. representation power), multi-task loss weighting (balancing categorical vs. continuous IAP performance)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on specific IAPs (particularly challenging ones like FOV Computed), inability to generalize to new IAP combinations, overfitting to training data characteristics, sensitivity to image quality or preprocessing variations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the model on a held-out validation set to verify training is proceeding correctly and identify which IAPs are easiest/hardest to predict.
  2. Evaluate the model's ability to generalize to completely new IAP combinations not present in training data.
  3. Test the downstream application of using IAP predictions for model selection in cancer classification to verify the practical utility of the approach.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Can the IAP prediction model generalize to other MRI modalities beyond breast MRI?
- How would using 3D convolutional networks instead of 2D affect the accuracy of IAP prediction?
- Can the IAP prediction model be used for real-time IAP inference during MRI acquisition?
- How do the correlations between IAPs affect the model's ability to distinguish between similar IAP values?

## Limitations
- The model's performance on continuous IAPs (TE and TR) is not reported, only categorical accuracy is provided.
- The paper lacks analysis of failure modes - which specific IAP combinations cause prediction errors and why.
- The generalization capability is asserted but not thoroughly validated on truly out-of-distribution data.

## Confidence
- High confidence: Claims about multi-task learning architecture and its basic functionality
- Medium confidence: Predictions for the 6 categorical IAPs with >97% accuracy
- Low confidence: Claims about generalizability to unseen IAP combinations without extensive validation
