# Privacy Inference-Empowered Stealthy Backdoor Attack on Federated Learning under Non-IID Scenarios

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08011
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08011
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: PI-SBA scheme increases backdoor attack success rate by 20%-60% in non-IID FL and evades Neural Cleanse and Activation Clustering defenses.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a privacy inference-empowered stealthy backdoor attack (PI-SBA) scheme designed for federated learning under non-IID scenarios. The authors propose a GAN-based diverse data reconstruction mechanism to generate supplementary data, improving the attacker's local data distribution and enabling more sophisticated backdoor strategies. A source-specified backdoor learning (SSBL) strategy is also designed, allowing attackers to specify which classes are susceptible to backdoor triggers. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR10, and Youtube Aligned Face datasets demonstrate that PI-SBA can significantly increase attack success rate while evading state-of-the-art defense methods.

## Method Summary
The PI-SBA scheme consists of a GAN-based diverse data inference mechanism with pre-poisoning strategy, and a source-specified backdoor learning (SSBL) strategy. The GAN generates supplementary data across multiple classes to reduce the gap between local and global data distributions. The pre-poisoning strategy injects generated samples mislabeled as the backdoor target class to improve generator performance. The SSBL strategy allows specifying which classes are susceptible to backdoor triggers, enabling differential treatment of classes. The attack is evaluated on MNIST, CIFAR10, and Youtube Aligned Face datasets with label distribution skew across 100 clients.

## Key Results
- PI-SBA increases attack success rate by 20%-60% compared to regular backdoor attacks in non-IID FL scenarios
- The scheme successfully evades Neural Cleanse and Activation Clustering defense methods
- Source-specified backdoor learning allows targeted attacks on specific classes while maintaining main task performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- GAN-based data inference improves backdoor effectiveness by generating diverse supplementary data
- Core assumption: Generated data maintains sufficient authenticity and diversity to effectively augment local training data
- Break condition: If generated data lacks authenticity or diversity, or if pre-poisoning strategy fails to improve generator performance

### Mechanism 2
- Source-specified backdoor learning allows targeted attacks on specific classes
- Core assumption: The attacker can effectively train the backdoor model to misclassify only specified classes while maintaining performance on others
- Break condition: If the backdoor model cannot effectively differentiate between specified and non-specified classes, or if the backdoor score (BS) becomes too low

### Mechanism 3
- Pre-poisoning strategy improves the quality of generated data
- Core assumption: The pre-poisoning strategy effectively guides the generator to produce higher quality samples for the target class
- Break condition: If the pre-poisoning strategy does not significantly improve the quality of generated data or if it introduces too much noise into the training process

## Limitations

- Generalizability of GAN-based data inference mechanism beyond evaluated datasets remains uncertain
- Pre-poisoning strategy's effectiveness in improving data quality is demonstrated but not extensively validated across different scenarios
- Source-specified backdoor learning strategy's robustness against adaptive defenses needs further investigation

- GAN-based data inference mechanism: Medium
- Source-specified backdoor learning (SSBL) strategy: Medium
- Defense evasion capability: Low
