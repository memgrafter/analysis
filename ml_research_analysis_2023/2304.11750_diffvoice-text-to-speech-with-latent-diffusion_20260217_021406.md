# DiffVoice: Text-to-Speech with Latent Diffusion

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
DiffVoice presents a novel text-to-speech system based on latent diffusion models. The key innovation is encoding speech signals into a phoneme-rate latent representation using a variational autoencoder with adversarial training, then jointly modeling phoneme durations and the latent representation using a diffusion model. This approach enables DiffVoice to outperform existing publicly available systems in naturalness on both LJSpeech and LibriTTS datasets. Additionally, by leveraging recent generative inverse problem solving algorithms for diffusion models, DiffVoice achieves state-of-the-art performance in text-based speech editing and zero-shot adaptation tasks without requiring specialized model designs or training procedures.

## Method Summary
DiffVoice first encodes speech signals into a phoneme-rate latent representation using a variational autoencoder enhanced by adversarial training. This latent space captures essential speech information while reducing dimensionality. A diffusion model then jointly models phoneme durations and the latent representation, allowing for high-quality speech synthesis. For inference, the diffusion model generates latent representations that are decoded back to speech using the VAE decoder. The system leverages generic inverse problem solving algorithms for tasks like text-based speech editing and zero-shot adaptation, eliminating the need for specialized architectures for these applications.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art naturalness scores on LJSpeech and LibriTTS datasets compared to publicly available TTS systems
- Demonstrates superior performance in text-based speech editing tasks without requiring specialized model architectures
- Successfully performs zero-shot speaker adaptation using generic diffusion inversion techniques

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Mel-spectrogram -> VAE encoder -> Latent representation -> Score estimator -> Latent diffusion -> VAE decoder -> Reconstructed Mel-spectrogram
- Critical path: Mel-spectrogram → VAE encoder → Latent representation → Score estimator → Latent diffusion → VAE decoder → Reconstructed Mel-spectrogram
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a phoneme-rate latent representation instead of a frame-rate representation reduces the dimensionality and focuses the diffusion model on higher-level features, but may lose some fine-grained details.
  - Jointly modeling duration with other speech factors allows for generic inverse problem solving, but may make the diffusion model more complex to train.
  - Using adversarial training to improve VAE reconstruction quality can lead to better results, but may introduce additional instability or artifacts.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor speech quality: May indicate issues with the VAE encoder or decoder, or with the latent diffusion model.
  - Artifacts or instability in generated speech: May indicate issues with the adversarial training or the diffusion process.
  - Slow sampling: May indicate issues with the SDE solver or the complexity of the diffusion model.
