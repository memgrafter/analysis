# LeXFiles and LegalLAMA: Facilitating English Multinational Legal Language Model Development

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07507
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07507
- Reference count: 40
- New diverse English legal corpus (LeXFiles) and legal knowledge probing benchmark (LegalLAMA) released to facilitate training and analysis of legal language models.

## Executive Summary
This work presents a comprehensive analysis of legal-oriented pre-trained language models, introducing two new resources: LeXFiles, a diverse English multinational legal corpus with 19 billion tokens across 11 sub-corpora, and LegalLAMA, a legal knowledge probing benchmark with 8 subtasks. The study trains two new LexLM models on LeXFiles and evaluates them alongside existing legal PLMs across upstream MLM, probing, and downstream tasks. The research reveals that probing performance strongly correlates with upstream performance in related legal topics, and that downstream performance is primarily driven by model size and prior legal knowledge. Both dimensions are shown to be crucial for developing effective domain-specific language models.

## Method Summary
The study introduces LeXFiles, a diverse English legal corpus containing 19 billion tokens across 11 sub-corpora covering legislation and case law from multiple jurisdictions. LegalLAMA, an 8-subtask probing benchmark, is developed to assess legal knowledge using zero-shot prompting. Two LexLM models are pre-trained on LeXFiles for 1M steps with 20-30% masking rate using a sentence sampler with exponential smoothing. All models are evaluated on upstream MLM accuracy across LeXFiles sub-corpora, probed using LegalLAMA with macro-averaged MRR, and fine-tuned for single epochs on LexGLUE downstream tasks with micro/macro F1 metrics.

## Key Results
- LexLM models trained on diverse LeXFiles consistently outperform RoBERTa on legal-specific upstream tasks.
- Probing performance on LegalLAMA strongly correlates with upstream performance on related legal topics.
- Downstream performance is primarily driven by model size and prior legal knowledge, which can be estimated through upstream and probing performance metrics.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Probing performance strongly correlates with upstream performance in related legal topics.
- Mechanism: Legal knowledge acquired during pre-training enables both masked language modeling and legal concept probing, as both rely on the same underlying knowledge structures.
- Core assumption: The legal knowledge acquired during pre-training is sufficiently comprehensive to enable both tasks.
- Evidence anchors: LegalLAMA analysis shows consistent patterns where models with better upstream performance achieve higher scores on legal tasks; LeXFiles provides sufficient coverage with 11 legal sub-corpora.
- Break condition: Correlation weakens if pre-training corpus lacks diversity or contains insufficient legal content.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Downstream performance is mainly driven by model size and prior legal knowledge.
- Mechanism: Larger models have more capacity to encode legal knowledge, while legal pre-training provides the actual knowledge enabling better downstream performance.
- Core assumption: Legal knowledge from pre-training is retained and accessible during fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors: LexLM-L consistently outperforms other models across downstream tasks; legal pre-trained models outperform generic models like RoBERTa.
- Break condition: Performance degrades if fine-tuning overwrites pre-trained knowledge or downstream tasks require knowledge absent from pre-training corpus.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Domain-specific pre-training corpora lead to better upstream performance compared to generic corpora.
- Mechanism: Legal corpora contain distinctive legal terminology and structures that generic corpora lack, enabling better language modeling for legal text.
- Core assumption: Legal language has distinctive features requiring specialized training data.
- Evidence anchors: LegalBERT and LexLM models outperform RoBERTa on legal sub-corpora; LeXFiles contains 11 distinct legal sub-corpora from multiple legal systems.
- Break condition: Legal-specific features aren't distinctive enough or legal corpora are too small to capture them effectively.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Masked Language Modeling (MLM)
  - Why needed here: Essential for understanding how language models learn representations during pre-training and how upstream performance is measured.
  - Quick check question: How does the MLM objective differ from traditional language modeling, and why is it more effective for pre-training?

- Concept: Zero-shot probing
  - Why needed here: LegalLAMA uses zero-shot probing to assess legal knowledge without fine-tuning, crucial for understanding pre-training effects.
  - Quick check question: What are the advantages and limitations of using zero-shot probing versus fine-tuning for knowledge assessment?

- Concept: Multi-label classification
  - Why needed here: Several downstream tasks (ECtHR, EURLEX) involve multi-label classification, affecting how performance is evaluated.
  - Quick check question: How does multi-label classification differ from multi-class classification in terms of evaluation metrics and model architecture?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Tokenizer -> Transformer encoder (RoBERTa/BERT variants) -> Pre-training pipeline (MLM) -> Probing framework (LegalLAMA) -> Downstream evaluation (LexGLUE)

- Critical path: 1) Tokenize legal corpora using custom tokenizer; 2) Pre-train language model on LeXFiles with MLM objective; 3) Evaluate upstream performance on LeXFiles sub-corpora; 4) Evaluate probing performance on LegalLAMA; 5) Fine-tune on downstream tasks for single epoch; 6) Compare performance across model variants

- Design tradeoffs: Model size vs. training efficiency (larger models perform better but require more resources); corpus diversity vs. specialization (more diverse corpora provide broader coverage but may dilute domain-specific knowledge); single-epoch fine-tuning vs. full fine-tuning (faster evaluation but may not reach optimal performance)

- Failure signatures: Poor upstream performance indicates issues with pre-training data quality or model architecture; low probing performance suggests insufficient legal knowledge acquisition; downstream performance not correlating with upstream/probing performance indicates domain mismatch

- First 3 experiments: 1) Train small legal model on single legal sub-corpus and evaluate on that sub-corpus only; 2) Compare upstream performance on legal vs. generic corpora using same model architecture; 3) Test zero-shot probing performance on LegalLAMA before and after pre-training on legal corpora

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does pre-training data diversity improve downstream performance beyond what can be explained by upstream and probing performance?
- Basis in paper: The study found mixed results for how legal knowledge correlates with downstream performance, particularly when comparing models of different sizes trained on diverse vs. specialized corpora.
- Why unresolved: Legal knowledge as expressed by upstream and probing performance doesn't fully explain downstream performance differences, especially for diverse vs. specialized corpora comparisons.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing models of equal size trained on corpora with varying diversity levels while controlling for legal content, measuring downstream performance after fine-tuning.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the impact of model size on downstream performance vary across different legal domains and task types?
- Basis in paper: Model capacity strongly correlates with downstream performance overall, but exceptions exist in contract NLI and case hold tasks where smaller LegalBERT outperforms larger models.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies exceptions but doesn't systematically analyze how model size effects vary across legal domains or task types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive analysis across all downstream tasks broken down by legal domain and task type, examining whether model size effects are consistent or domain-specific.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between pre-training corpus diversity and domain specificity for legal language models?
- Basis in paper: LexLMs trained on diverse LeXFiles generally outperform more specialized models, but LegalBERT (trained on EU, UK, US data) performs well on certain sub-corpora included in its training.
- Why unresolved: The study suggests both diversity and domain specificity have benefits but doesn't identify the optimal trade-off or how this varies by legal system or task type.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying corpus composition along diversity-specificity spectrum, measuring both upstream/probing performance and downstream performance across different legal systems and task types.

## Limitations

- The correlation between upstream and probing performance may be inflated by model size effects rather than genuine legal knowledge transfer.
- Single-epoch fine-tuning may not reveal the full potential of models with stronger pre-training, potentially underestimating the importance of prior legal knowledge.
- The study doesn't fully address whether larger models simply memorize more patterns that appear useful for both upstream and probing tasks.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: LexLM models outperform RoBERTa on legal-specific tasks due to legal corpus pre-training, well-supported by controlled experiments comparing similar-sized models.
- Medium Confidence: Probing performance correlates with upstream performance in legal topics, though this correlation could be partially explained by model capacity.
- Medium Confidence: Both model size and prior legal knowledge are essential for downstream performance, though single-epoch fine-tuning limits assessment of long-term benefits.

## Next Checks

1. Extended Fine-tuning Analysis: Re-run downstream experiments with full fine-tuning (multiple epochs) to determine if legal pre-training advantages become more pronounced when models have more time to adapt pre-trained knowledge.

2. Cross-Domain Probing: Evaluate all models on non-legal probing tasks to test whether the upstream-probing correlation is specific to legal domains or a general pattern across knowledge types.

3. Transfer Learning Analysis: Measure retention of legal knowledge after fine-tuning by comparing pre- and post-fine-tuning performance on LegalLAMA, testing whether pre-training legal knowledge is preserved during downstream adaptation.
