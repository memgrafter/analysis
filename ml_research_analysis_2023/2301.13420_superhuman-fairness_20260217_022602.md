# Superhuman Fairness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13420
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13420
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Achieves γ-superhuman performance that increases rapidly with reference decision noise and significantly outperforms baseline methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces superhuman fairness, a novel approach to fairness-aware classification that avoids explicit performance-fairness trade-off specification. Instead of optimizing a specific trade-off, the method aims to simultaneously outperform human decisions across multiple predictive performance and fairness measures with maximal frequency. The approach reformulates fair machine learning as an imitation learning task using subdominance minimization and policy gradient optimization methods. Experiments on Adult and COMPAS datasets show that the method achieves high levels of superhuman performance that increase rapidly with reference decision noise and significantly outperform methods based on narrower fairness-performance objectives.

## Method Summary
The superhuman fairness approach frames fairness-aware classification as an imitation learning problem where the goal is to Pareto dominate reference human decisions across all metrics. It uses subdominance minimization as a convex upper bound on non-dominance, enabling optimization across multiple conflicting metrics without explicit trade-off specification. Policy gradient optimization is employed to update model parameters based on the gradient of expected subdominance with respect to the policy. The method is trained on noisy versions of ground truth data to simulate human decision-making, then evaluated against baseline fairness methods and reference decisions on held-out test data.

## Key Results
- Achieves γ-superhuman performance that increases rapidly with reference decision noise
- Significantly outperforms baseline methods based on narrower fairness-performance objectives
- Demonstrates promise for facilitating transitions from manual to automated decisions preferred by all stakeholders

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Subdominance minimization enables simultaneous optimization of multiple conflicting fairness and performance metrics without explicit trade-off specification.
- Mechanism: The method frames fairness-aware classification as an imitation learning problem where the goal is to Pareto dominate reference human decisions across all metrics. By minimizing the subdominance (a convex upper bound on non-dominance), the approach learns a classifier that outperforms reference decisions on multiple metrics simultaneously.
- Core assumption: Reference decisions reflect desired performance-fairness trade-offs but are noisy and suboptimal, providing an opportunity for improvement.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We instead re-cast fair machine learning as an imitation learning task by introducing superhuman fairness, which seeks to simultaneously outperform human decisions on multiple predictive performance and fairness measures."
  - [section]: "We consider the motivating scenario of a fairness-aware decision task currently being performed by well-intentioned, but inherently error-prone human decision makers... We propose a more modest, yet more practical objective: outperform human decisions across all performance and fairness measures with maximal frequency."
  - [corpus]: Weak - The related papers focus on clustering fairness, societal impact, and consistency checks rather than subdominance minimization approaches.
- Break condition: If reference decisions are already optimal across all metrics, or if the metrics are fundamentally incompatible such that no Pareto improvement exists.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Policy gradient optimization enables flexible probabilistic decision policies to be optimized for fairness and performance.
- Mechanism: The method uses gradient descent to update model parameters based on the gradient of expected subdominance with respect to the policy. This allows the use of expressive model classes beyond those directly conditioned on cost features.
- Core assumption: The policy can be parameterized in a way that allows effective gradient-based optimization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We develop policy gradient optimization methods that allow flexible classes of probabilistic decision policies to be optimized for given sets of performance/fairness measures and demonstrations."
  - [section]: "We use the subgradient of subdominance with respect to θ and α to update these variables iteratively, and after convergence, the best learned weights θ* are used in the final model."
  - [corpus]: Weak - Related papers don't specifically address policy gradient methods for fairness optimization.
- Break condition: If the policy parameterization is too simple to capture the optimal decision boundary, or if the optimization landscape is too rugged for gradient methods to find good solutions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The approach is robust to noise in reference decisions, with superhuman performance increasing as reference decision noise increases.
- Mechanism: As reference decisions become noisier (less optimal), there is more room for improvement, allowing the method to achieve higher levels of superhuman performance.
- Core assumption: Some degree of noise in reference decisions is necessary to create opportunities for improvement.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We employ a synthetic decision-maker with differing amounts of label and group membership noise to identify sufficient conditions for superhuman fairness of varying degrees. We find that our approach achieves high levels of superhuman performance that increase rapidly with reference decision noise."
  - [section]: "To emulate human decision-making with various levels of noise, we add noise to the ground truth data of benchmark fairness datasets... We observe that in the case of learning from noisy demonstrations, our approach still outperforms the reference decisions."
  - [corpus]: Weak - Related papers don't discuss the relationship between noise in reference decisions and method performance.
- Break condition: If reference decisions are too noisy, the method may optimize for the wrong target, or if they are too clean, there may be insufficient room for improvement.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the superhuman fairness approach perform when human decision-makers have systematic biases rather than random noise?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses synthetic noise added to labels and group membership to simulate human decision-making, but real human biases may be more systematic.
  - Why unresolved: The experiments only consider random noise corruption of human decisions, not systematic bias patterns that humans might exhibit.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing superhuman fairness performance on datasets with known systematic human biases versus random noise corruption.

- Question: What is the computational complexity of the superhuman fairness approach as the number of performance/fairness measures increases?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions interest in scaling to larger numbers of measures but doesn't provide complexity analysis.
  - Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates feasibility with K=4 and K=5 measures but doesn't analyze how runtime or convergence scales with K.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Empirical runtime analysis and theoretical complexity bounds showing how training time scales with the number of fairness measures.

- Question: How robust is the superhuman fairness approach to adversarial manipulation of the training data?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper assumes human demonstrations are well-intentioned but doesn't consider adversarial scenarios where training data might be manipulated.
  - Why unresolved: The theoretical generalization bounds and experiments assume honest demonstrations without considering potential adversarial manipulation.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing superhuman fairness performance when training data contains adversarial examples designed to mislead the approach.

## Limitations

- The approach relies on synthetic noise generation rather than real human demonstrations, limiting ecological validity.
- Performance claims are based on only two benchmark datasets, requiring broader validation.
- The method's behavior with more complex model classes beyond logistic regression is not thoroughly explored.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core mechanism of subdominance minimization for multi-objective optimization is well-founded and mathematically sound. The theoretical framework connecting imitation learning to fairness-aware classification is clearly articulated.

**Medium Confidence**: The empirical results demonstrating superhuman performance are promising but limited to two datasets with synthetic noise patterns. The claim that this approach "significantly outperforms" baseline methods needs more diverse dataset validation.

**Low Confidence**: The scalability of the approach to real-world scenarios with actual human decision-makers remains speculative. The paper does not address computational costs or convergence guarantees for larger-scale problems.

## Next Checks

1. **Real Human Demonstration Validation**: Test the approach using actual human decision-maker demonstrations from domains like medical diagnosis or loan approval, comparing performance against synthetic noise baselines.

2. **Robustness to Metric Selection**: Evaluate how the method performs when the set of fairness metrics changes, particularly with metrics that may be fundamentally incompatible (e.g., group fairness vs individual fairness).

3. **Scaling Analysis**: Assess computational requirements and convergence behavior as the number of features, samples, and reference decisions increases to understand practical deployment constraints.
