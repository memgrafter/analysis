# Towards Explainable and Safe Conversational Agents for Mental Health: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13191
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13191
- Reference count: 5
- Key outcome: Proposes knowledge-infused learning (KIL) and user-level explainability to improve safety and explainability of virtual mental health agents

## Executive Summary
This survey addresses critical safety and explainability gaps in current virtual mental health agents (VMHAs) like Woebot and Wyssa by proposing knowledge-infused learning (KIL) as a framework for incorporating clinical knowledge and guidelines. The authors present a comprehensive taxonomy of mental health conversations and argue for revamped evaluation metrics that include qualitative measures such as adherence, harmlessness, and transparency alongside traditional semantic similarity metrics. The work emphasizes user-level explainability as essential for building trust and identifies emerging mental health conversation areas including triage, motivational interviewing, and clinical diagnostic interviewing.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes existing literature on VMHAs to identify limitations in current approaches, particularly around safety and explainability. It proposes KIL as a methodology to incorporate multiple forms of knowledge—lexical, general-purpose, commonsense, domain-specific, and procedural—into AI models through integration with clinical knowledge bases like PHQ-9 and SNOMED-CT. The authors advocate for evaluation frameworks that combine qualitative metrics (harmlessness, adherence, transparency) with knowledge infusion metrics, while also exploring the role of user-level explainability in enhancing trust and safety.

## Key Results
- KIL can improve VMHA safety by grounding responses in clinical guidelines and structured medical terminologies
- User-level explainability enhances trust by providing traceable explanations linked to real-world clinical entities
- Current evaluation metrics inadequately capture qualitative aspects of safety and explainability needed for mental health applications
- Augmenting training datasets with clinical knowledge can significantly improve conversation quality and safety

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: KIL improves VMHA safety by incorporating clinical knowledge into AI models
- Mechanism: Clinical knowledge bases provide medically validated questions and responses that ground VMHA outputs in established guidelines, reducing unsafe or incoherent responses
- Core assumption: Clinical knowledge bases are comprehensive and accurate enough to cover diverse mental health scenarios
- Evidence anchors: Survey proposes KIL for incorporating clinical guidelines; found 25 related papers with moderate relevance
- Break condition: Incomplete or outdated clinical knowledge could lead to unsafe responses

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: User-level explainability builds trust through transparent AI reasoning
- Mechanism: Post-hoc explanations connect AI decisions to clinical guidelines and real-world entities, helping users understand the reasoning behind recommendations
- Core assumption: Users can comprehend and value explanations linking AI outputs to clinical standards
- Evidence anchors: Survey emphasizes qualitative metrics including transparency; limited direct citations for UsEx mechanisms
- Break condition: Explanations that are too complex or fail to connect meaningfully to user concerns

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Dataset augmentation with clinical knowledge improves conversation quality
- Mechanism: Enriching training datasets with clinical lexicons and guidelines enables models to generate medically validated responses
- Core assumption: Augmented datasets accurately represent real-world mental health conversations
- Evidence anchors: Study showed 85% improvement in safety across language models; moderate relevance in related literature
- Break condition: Inconsistent annotations or poor integration of clinical knowledge

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
  - Why needed here: CBT forms the therapeutic foundation for many VMHAs, providing the framework for effective conversation guidance
  - Quick check question: What are the core components of CBT that a VMHA should incorporate to provide effective mental health support?

- Concept: Clinical guidelines and lexicons (e.g., PHQ-9, SNOMED-CT)
  - Why needed here: These provide medically validated questions and terminology ensuring VMHA responses are clinically appropriate
  - Quick check question: How does the PHQ-9 questionnaire structure its questions to assess depression severity, and why is this structure important for a VMHA?

- Concept: User-level explainability (UsEx)
  - Why needed here: UsEx is critical for building trust by making AI reasoning transparent and traceable to clinical entities
  - Quick check question: What forms can UsEx take in a VMHA, and how do they help users understand the AI's decision-making process?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data Ingestion -> Knowledge Infusion Engine -> Response Generation -> Safety Validator -> Explanation Generator -> User Output
- Critical path: User input → Knowledge Infusion Engine → Response Generation Module → Safety Validator → User output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Balancing explainability with response fluency: More detailed explanations may disrupt conversational flow
  - Dataset augmentation vs. model complexity: Richer datasets improve safety but increase training time
  - Real-time vs. batch processing: UsEx may require additional computation, affecting latency
- Failure signatures:
  - Unsafe responses despite clinical knowledge: Indicates gaps in knowledge base or training
  - Overly complex explanations: Suggests UsEx module is not user-friendly
  - Slow response times: Points to inefficiencies in knowledge infusion or validation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate safety improvements by comparing VMHA outputs with and without clinical knowledge augmentation
  2. Assess user comprehension of UsEx through user studies rating clarity and helpfulness
  3. Measure adherence to clinical guidelines by auditing VMHA conversations against PHQ-9 checklist

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the most effective evaluation metrics for assessing user-level explainability in VMHAs?
- Basis in paper: Survey discusses need for qualitative metrics including transparency and adherence
- Why unresolved: Current metrics focus on semantic similarity and may not capture nuanced aspects of user-level explainability
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different evaluation metrics' correlation with human judgment

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can knowledge-infused learning (KIL) be effectively implemented in VMHAs to improve contextualization and safety?
- Basis in paper: Survey highlights KIL's potential but notes applicability in mental health context needs examination
- Why unresolved: While KIL shows promise, lack of concrete evidence on its effectiveness in improving VMHAs
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies evaluating VMHA performance with and without KIL

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the key factors that influence the perceived quality and trustworthiness of VMHAs by users?
- Basis in paper: Survey discusses importance of trust and user perception of quality
- Why unresolved: While importance of trust is highlighted, comprehensive analysis of influencing factors is lacking
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies examining relationship between factors and perceived quality/trust

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on conceptual frameworks without detailed implementation specifics for KIL or UsEx mechanisms
- Most cited works focus on survey-level synthesis rather than empirical validation of proposed approaches
- Limited quantitative evidence for safety improvements, with only one study reporting 85% improvement

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Identified need for safer, more explainable VMHAs is well-supported by existing limitations
- **Medium confidence**: Theoretical benefits of KIL and UsEx are plausible based on alignment with established AI explainability principles
- **Low confidence**: Specific implementation details for KIL and UsEx are underspecified, making faithful reproduction challenging
