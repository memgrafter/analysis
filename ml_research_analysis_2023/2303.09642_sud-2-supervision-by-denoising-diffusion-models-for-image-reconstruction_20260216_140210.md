# SUD$^2$: Supervision by Denoising Diffusion Models for Image Reconstruction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09642
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09642
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Achieves 48% higher PSNR and 43% lower FID than CycleGAN-SSL on CelebAMask-HQ inpainting, and 17% lower LPIPS than supervised baseline on REVIDE dehazing

## Executive Summary
SUD$^2$ (Supervision by Denoising Diffusion Models) addresses the challenge of training image reconstruction networks when paired data is scarce. The method uses pre-trained denoising diffusion models to supervise reconstruction networks on unpaired data by projecting network outputs onto the manifold of natural images. This semi-supervised approach enables learning from unpaired measurements and images, significantly improving reconstruction quality in tasks like image inpainting and dehazing while avoiding mode collapse through noise injection and correlation minimization.

## Method Summary
SUD$^2$ trains image reconstruction networks using a semi-supervised framework that combines paired data with unpaired measurements and images. The method employs a U-Net architecture trained with a denoiser loss (using pre-trained diffusion models), correlation loss, and noise injection. During training, the reconstruction network's outputs are passed through the diffusion model, and the network is trained to minimize the difference between its outputs and the diffusion model's projections. This approach effectively uses the diffusion model as a strong prior for the reconstruction task while noise injection and correlation minimization prevent mode collapse.

## Key Results
- On CelebAMask-HQ in-painting: 48% higher average PSNR and 43% lower average FID score compared to CycleGAN-SSL
- On REVIDE image dehazing: 17% lower median LPIPS score than supervised baseline
- Demonstrated effectiveness of denoising diffusion models for semi-supervised image reconstruction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: A pre-trained denoising diffusion model can supervise the training of an image reconstruction network in the absence of paired data.
- Mechanism: The diffusion model projects network outputs onto the manifold of natural images, providing a strong prior for the reconstruction task. During training, the reconstruction network's outputs are passed through the diffusion model, and the network is trained to minimize the difference between its outputs and the diffusion model's projections.
- Core assumption: The diffusion model has learned a meaningful representation of the target image distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "leverage denoising diffusion models to supervise the training of image reconstruction networks, enabling them to learn from unpaired data."
  - [section 3.3.3]: "We propose replacing our single-step MMSE denoising algorithm with a multi-step denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPMs)"
  - [corpus]: Weak. Related papers discuss diffusion models for MRI and CT reconstruction, but not specifically for semi-supervised training with unpaired data.
- Break condition: If the diffusion model is not well-trained or does not capture the target image distribution well, its projections will not provide a useful prior for the reconstruction network.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Minimizing the denoising loss encourages mode collapse, leading to repetitive and low-quality reconstructions.
- Mechanism: The denoising loss minimizes the cross-entropy between the distribution of the reconstruction network's outputs and a smoothed version of the true image distribution. This encourages the network to output images that maximize the probability of the smoothed distribution, which often leads to blurry or repetitive outputs.
- Core assumption: The denoising loss is minimized during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "minimizingLdenoiser minimizes the cross entropy betweenpfθ(y) andpx+ν. The cross entropy of H(p,q ) of two distributions p and q is minimized with respect to p whenp is a dirac distribution with a non-zero support where distributionq is largest, i.e., a mode."
  - [section 3.3.1]: "To ﬁght mode collapse, we introduce an additional penalty,Lreg into the SUD loss which encourages diverse outputs."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Related papers discuss diffusion models for image reconstruction but do not specifically address the mode collapse issue.
- Break condition: If the correlation loss or noise injection is not used to counteract mode collapse, the reconstructions will become repetitive and low-quality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Noise injection and correlation minimization improve the quality of reconstructions by encouraging diverse outputs and preventing mode collapse.
- Mechanism: Noise injection adds Gaussian noise to the reconstruction network's outputs before passing them through the diffusion model, which helps to prevent the network from collapsing to a mode. Correlation minimization penalizes correlations between intermediate activations in the network, encouraging diverse outputs.
- Core assumption: The noise injection and correlation minimization losses are effective in preventing mode collapse.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3.2]: "To alleviate this problem, we inject noise onto the reconstructionsfθ(yu) before passing them through the denoiser."
  - [section 3.3.1]: "we encourage sample diversity as follows: (1) we use a U-net architecture as our reconstruction networkfθ(·), (2) we let au represent the intermediate activation offθ to inputsyu, and (3) we then penalize correlations between activations."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Related papers discuss diffusion models for image reconstruction but do not specifically address the use of noise injection and correlation minimization.
- Break condition: If the noise injection and correlation minimization losses are not tuned properly, they may not effectively prevent mode collapse or may introduce other artifacts in the reconstructions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image reconstruction network (U-Net) -> Denoising diffusion model -> Correlation minimization loss -> Noise injection -> Perceptual loss

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train denoising diffusion model on unpaired images
  2. Train image reconstruction network using paired data and denoising loss
  3. Apply noise injection and correlation minimization to prevent mode collapse
  4. Evaluate reconstructions using perceptual loss and other metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a stronger prior (e.g., a better-trained diffusion model) can improve reconstruction quality but may also increase the risk of mode collapse.
  - Increasing the weight on the denoising loss can improve reconstruction quality but may also increase the risk of mode collapse.
  - Increasing the weight on the correlation minimization loss can improve diversity but may also introduce artifacts in the reconstructions.

- Failure signatures:
  - Repetitive or low-quality reconstructions indicate mode collapse.
  - Blurry reconstructions indicate that the denoising loss is not effectively preventing mode collapse.
  - Artifacts in the reconstructions indicate that the correlation minimization loss is not tuned properly.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the image reconstruction network using only paired data and the denoising loss. Evaluate the reconstructions and check for mode collapse.
  2. Add noise injection to the training pipeline and evaluate the reconstructions again. Check if noise injection helps to prevent mode collapse.
  3. Add correlation minimization to the training pipeline and evaluate the reconstructions again. Check if correlation minimization helps to improve diversity and prevent mode collapse.

## Limitations
- The core assumption that diffusion models provide effective supervision for semi-supervised reconstruction lacks rigorous mathematical justification
- Mode collapse analysis is limited, with weak ablation studies on the effectiveness of noise injection and correlation minimization
- Empirical results are only demonstrated on relatively constrained datasets (faces and bedroom scenes), leaving scalability to more diverse image distributions unclear

## Confidence
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical results showing SUD2 outperforming baselines on CelebAMask-HQ and REVIDE datasets
- **Low confidence**: The theoretical explanation of why denoising diffusion models provide effective supervision
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that mode collapse is a significant problem in this setup and that the proposed countermeasures are effective

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study on mode collapse**: Systematically vary the noise injection magnitude (σ) and correlation loss weight (λ2) to quantify their individual contributions to preventing mode collapse. Measure reconstruction diversity using metrics like LPIPS and KID scores.

2. **Cross-dataset validation**: Test SUD2's generalization by training the denoising diffusion model on one dataset (e.g., CelebA) and using it to supervise reconstruction on a different dataset (e.g., FFHQ). This would validate whether the diffusion model truly learns a generic image manifold.

3. **Comparison with simpler priors**: Replace the denoising diffusion model with simpler image priors (e.g., total variation, GAN-based) to quantify the added value of using diffusion models specifically. This would help determine if the complexity of diffusion models is justified.
