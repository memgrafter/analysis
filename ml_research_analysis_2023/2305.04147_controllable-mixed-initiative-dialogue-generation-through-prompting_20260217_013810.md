# Controllable Mixed-Initiative Dialogue Generation through Prompting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04147
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04147
- Reference count: 31
- One-line primary result: Prompting large language models outperforms fine-tuning for controllable mixed-initiative dialogue generation, achieving higher human ratings and better performance in interactive settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes using prompt engineering as an alternative to fine-tuning for controllable mixed-initiative dialogue generation. The authors formalize prompt construction by integrating task background information with conversation history and dialogue intents, using natural language forms of support and persuasion strategies. Evaluations on two datasets (Emotional Support Conversations and PersuasionForGood) show that prompt-generated responses achieve higher human ratings across accuracy, coherence, consistency, and engagingness compared to fine-tuned models and even ground truth responses. In interactive settings, the prompting-based system outperformed the fine-tuning baseline on persuasiveness and donation intent.

## Method Summary
The method uses systematic prompt construction with task background and conversation history, employing natural language forms of dialogue intents rather than fine-tuning. The approach uses InstructGPT "text-davinci-003" to generate responses conditioned on these carefully constructed prompts, avoiding the tradeoff between response quality and semantic control accuracy faced by fine-tuning approaches.

## Key Results
- Prompt-generated responses achieved higher human ratings than fine-tuned models and ground truth on accuracy, coherence, consistency, and engagingness
- In interactive evaluation, the prompting-based system outperformed the fine-tuning baseline on persuasiveness and donation intent
- Prompting demonstrated robustness to annotation errors and domain transfer challenges common in fine-tuning approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Prompting LLMs provides stronger semantic control over dialogue generation than fine-tuning
- Mechanism: LLMs trained on massive corpora have robust representations of linguistic variety, making them more resilient to overfitting and better at following semantic instructions through natural language forms of dialogue intents
- Core assumption: The natural language forms of dialogue intents (e.g., "The Therapist acknowledges the Patient's feelings by paraphrasing their situation") are sufficient for the LLM to understand and follow the intended control
- Evidence anchors:
  - Evaluations yielded strong performance on two popular English mixed-initiative tasks: Emotional Support Conversations (ESC) and PersuasionForGood (P4G)
  - Table 1 shows that prompt-generated responses are more highly rated in terms of quality compared to responses generated from competitive fine-tuned dialogue models as well as ground truth responses, in terms of all human evaluation metrics
- Break condition: If the natural language intent descriptions are ambiguous or not well-aligned with the task, the LLM may misinterpret the intent

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prompting avoids the tradeoff between response quality and semantic control accuracy faced by fine-tuning
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning requires balancing the loss function to optimize both generation quality and intent adherence, which can lead to one being sacrificed for the other. Prompting directly injects intent control into the prompt, allowing the LLM to optimize response quality while naturally following the intent
- Core assumption: The LLM's pre-training provides sufficient capability to generate high-quality responses while following the intent instructions
- Evidence anchors:
  - Prompting large language models as a drop-in replacement to fine-tuning on conditional generation
  - Fine-tuning approaches like both RAP and Oracle-BlenderBot involve balancing a tradeoff between response quality and semantic control accuracy
- Break condition: If the LLM's pre-training is insufficient for the specific task domain, response quality may suffer even with good intent control

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Prompting is more robust to annotation errors in dialogue datasets
- Mechanism: Fine-tuned models learn directly from potentially noisy annotations, propagating errors. Prompting relies on natural language instructions rather than learned representations of control codes, making it less sensitive to annotation noise
- Core assumption: The LLM's understanding of natural language is robust enough to handle variations in how intents are described
- Evidence anchors:
  - Training corpora often differ in demographic and distribution compared to production environments, which can lead to deteriorating response quality
  - While LLMs may generate "better" responses than crowdworkers, we do not expect them to be better than expert therapists
- Break condition: If annotation errors are systematic or the natural language instructions are themselves ambiguous, prompting may still be affected

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Policy planner -> Prompt constructor -> LLM (InstructGPT) -> Knowledge retrieval (optional)
- Critical path: 1. Policy planner outputs dialogue intent for current turn 2. Prompt constructor builds prompt using conversation history, task background, and natural language intent 3. Prompt sent to LLM for response generation 4. Response returned and added to conversation history
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Prompt complexity vs. LLM performance: More detailed prompts may improve control but could exceed context limits or confuse the model
  - Task background inclusion: Provides context but increases prompt length
  - Natural language vs. structured intent representation: Natural language is more flexible but may be less precise
- Failure signatures:
  - Intent mismatch: Generated response doesn't match intended dialogue strategy
  - Context disconnect: Response ignores relevant conversation history
  - Incoherence: Response is grammatically or logically flawed
  - Knowledge errors: Factual information in response is incorrect
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Static evaluation comparing prompt-generated responses to fine-tuned models and ground truth on human ratings for accuracy, coherence, consistency, and engagingness
  2. Automatic metric evaluation (Distinct-N, QuantiDCE) comparing prompt-generated responses to baselines
  3. Interactive evaluation deploying the prompting-based system in a live dialogue task and collecting user ratings on persuasiveness, naturalness, etc.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of prompt-based dialogue generation compare to fine-tuned models when using open-source LLMs of varying sizes?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that they used InstructGPT due to access and computational constraints, and that smaller models like GPT-J 6B had higher variance in performance. They state that many reasoning capabilities do not seem possible with models smaller than 175B parameters
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated InstructGPT (175B parameters) and did not compare against other open-source models of different sizes. The authors acknowledge this limitation and note that they did not have access to models like OPT 175B or the appropriate hardware to load such models
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of prompt-based dialogue generation using various open-source LLMs (e.g., GPT-Neo, OPT series) of different sizes, comparing performance metrics against fine-tuned models on the same tasks

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is prompt-based dialogue generation to distribution shifts in user language patterns and conversation topics?
- Basis in paper: The authors provide examples of conversations with users using varied language patterns, including non-fluent English and all-caps responses. They note that InstructGPT appeared robust to this user's language usage patterns. They also mention that the generated responses appear coherent despite the wide range of user-side linguistic variety
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides some anecdotal evidence of robustness, it does not systematically test the system's performance on out-of-distribution user inputs or across diverse demographic groups
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluation of the prompt-based system on a diverse set of user inputs, including non-native English speakers, users with different educational backgrounds, and conversations on topics outside the training data distribution

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can prompt-based dialogue systems be effectively combined with dialogue policy planning for more complex mixed-initiative tasks?
- Basis in paper: The authors state that their work focuses on improving the controllable generation portion of mixed-initiative dialogue systems, but acknowledge that dialogue policy planning is still an important problem. They mention that a mixed-initiative dialogue system cannot consist solely of a generation module powered by prompting and needs a set of rules or models that govern how a system can regain control of a conversation
- Why unresolved: The paper evaluates prompt-based generation by holding dialogue policies fixed, either using ground truth intents or following the same intents prescribed by an existing system (RAP). It does not explore how to develop effective policy planners for novel tasks or how to integrate them with prompt-based generation
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of a complete mixed-initiative dialogue system that combines prompt-based generation with a learned or rule-based dialogue policy planner, tested on tasks beyond the emotional support and persuasion domains used in this study

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on human judgment, introducing subjectivity and potential inconsistency in ratings
- Study focuses on two specific dialogue tasks with relatively controlled conditions, limiting generalizability
- Absolute performance gap and practical significance in real-world deployment scenarios remain unclear

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core finding that prompting can serve as a viable alternative to fine-tuning for controllable dialogue generation
- Medium confidence in the superiority of prompting over fine-tuning across all evaluation metrics, given the human evaluation component
- Medium confidence in the robustness claims regarding annotation errors and domain transfer, as these are not directly validated
