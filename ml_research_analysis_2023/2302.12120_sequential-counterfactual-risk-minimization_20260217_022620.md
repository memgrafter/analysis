# Sequential Counterfactual Risk Minimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12120
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12120
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Extends CRM to sequential setting with improved variance control, achieving O(n^{-1/2-β} log n) excess risk vs O(log(n)/√n) for CRM

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Sequential Counterfactual Risk Minimization (SCRM), extending counterfactual risk minimization to a sequential setting where learned policies are deployed multiple times to gather new data. The authors develop a novel variance-controlled counterfactual estimator (IPS-IX) and show that under a Hölderian error bound assumption, sequential deployment accelerates convergence rates compared to classical CRM. The method demonstrates both theoretical improvements in excess risk bounds and practical performance gains across multiple datasets with discrete and continuous action spaces.

## Method Summary
SCRM extends CRM by sequentially deploying learned policies to collect new data, using a novel IPS-IX estimator that incorporates implicit exploration and variance control. The method minimizes a sample variance penalized objective that balances empirical risk reduction with variance minimization. Under a Hölderian error bound assumption, the authors prove that this sequential approach achieves faster convergence rates than CRM by reducing the variance of importance weights between logging and optimal policies. The framework includes a new variance estimator using control variates and a learning strategy that optimizes for high-probability low-risk policies.

## Key Results
- Achieves O(n^{-1/2-β} log n) excess risk under Hölderian error bound vs O(log(n)/√n) for CRM
- Demonstrates faster convergence and lower variance than CRM on both discrete and continuous action spaces
- Outperforms related batch bandit and RL approaches, especially in underexplored action space scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Sequential deployment reduces variance of importance weights between logging and optimal policies
- Mechanism: At each rollout, the learned policy is closer to optimal, yielding lower variance importance weights. The variance-dependent excess risk bound shows this directly improves convergence rates.
- Core assumption: Hölderian error bound holds (Varx,θm(πθ∗/πθm) ≤ C(L(θm) - L(θ∗))^β)
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "error bound sequentially concentrates through CRM rollouts"; section states "This allows us to improve the excess risk bounds convergence rate"
- Break condition: If Hölderian bound fails (β = 0 or too loose), acceleration effect disappears

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: IPS-IX estimator controls variance better than standard importance sampling
- Mechanism: Lower bounds propensities with απθi to prevent infinite weights; uses control variate ζi(θ) = (πθi/(πmi+απθi) - 1)ymi to reduce variance
- Core assumption: Policies have valid importance sampling support
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "novel counterfactual estimator"; section describes variance estimator using control variate
- Break condition: α too small loses variance benefits; α too large introduces bias

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sample variance penalization learns high-probability low-risk policies
- Mechanism: Minimizes L(θ) ≤ ˆLIPS-IX_m(θ) + λm√(ˆVIPS-IX_m(θ)/nm) + O(1/nm), directly controlling true risk
- Core assumption: Complexity measure Cm(Θ) grows logarithmically with sample size
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "improve performance of CRM"; section shows minimization of penalized objective
- Break condition: If Cm(Θ) grows polynomially, variance control becomes ineffective

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection -> Estimator module (IPS-IX) -> Learning module (variance penalized optimization) -> Evaluation module -> Hyperparameter selection
- Critical path: Initialize with logged data from θ0 → For each rollout m: build objective from current data, optimize to find θm+1, deploy θm+1 to collect new data, evaluate on test set → Stop when converged or budget exhausted
- Design tradeoffs: Geometric vs. linear sample size growth (theoretical vs. practical); implicit exploration parameter α tradeoff (variance vs. bias); hyperparameter selection λm (cross-validation vs. heuristic)
- Failure signatures: Slow convergence (poor α/λm choice or Hölderian assumption failure); high variance (insufficient variance control); degradation (distributional shift or overfitting)
- First 3 experiments: 1) Reproduce Gaussian policies example to verify basic functionality; 2) Implement IPS-IX and compare with standard IPS/SNIPS; 3) Run SCRM on Advertising dataset with varying exploration budgets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what conditions does Hölderian error bound assumption hold for practical policy classes beyond exponential families?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper discusses Hölderian error bound for exponential families and Gaussian policies but acknowledges broader applicability remains open
- Why unresolved: Assumption is crucial for theoretical acceleration but requires case-by-case analysis; paper provides examples but no general characterization
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive study characterizing Hölderian error bound conditions for various policy classes (neural networks, kernel methods) and loss functions

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does choice of exploration parameter α affect bias-variance tradeoff and convergence rate in practice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper introduces IPS-IX with α and discusses theoretical properties but lacks empirical guidance on optimal choice
- Why unresolved: Paper analyzes theoretical properties but doesn't study α's impact on bias-variance tradeoff and convergence rate empirically
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic empirical study varying α across different problem settings and policy classes

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can SCRM framework be extended to handle non-stationary environments?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper focuses on stationary environments; real-world applications often involve non-stationary settings
- Why unresolved: Paper doesn't address non-stationary environments; extension would require significant modifications
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and analyzing SCRM extension for non-stationary environments with empirical validation

## Limitations
- Theoretical claims heavily rely on Hölderian error bound assumption, which may not hold in all practical scenarios
- Choice of implicit exploration parameter α and variance penalty λm lacks systematic sensitivity analysis
- Claim of outperforming other batch bandit/RL approaches lacks comprehensive systematic comparison

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Variance control mechanism of IPS-IX estimator and its theoretical properties are well-established
- **Medium confidence**: Theoretical acceleration rates are proven under specific assumptions, but practical significance depends on assumption validity
- **Low confidence**: Claim of "often outperforming" other approaches lacks systematic comparison across broader range of methods
