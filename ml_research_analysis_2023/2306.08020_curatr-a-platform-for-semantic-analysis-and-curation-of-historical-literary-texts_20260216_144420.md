# Curatr: A Platform for Semantic Analysis and Curation of Historical Literary Texts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08020
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08020
- Reference count: 38
- Curatr is an online platform using neural word embeddings to help humanities scholars discover and curate relevant texts from large historical literary collections

## Executive Summary
Curatr is an innovative platform designed to address the challenge of identifying relevant content from large digital collections of historical literary texts. The system combines neural word embeddings with expert domain knowledge to enable the generation of thematic lexicons, allowing researchers to curate relevant sub-corpora from 18th and 19th century digitized texts. Through an interactive "human in the loop" approach, researchers can iteratively refine semantic lexicons and discover less well-known texts and authors that may challenge existing theories about cultural attitudes and historical narratives.

## Method Summary
Curatr employs a text mining workflow that integrates neural word embeddings (specifically word2Vec) with expert domain knowledge to generate thematic lexicons. The platform uses Apache Solr for information retrieval and provides an interactive web interface where researchers can build lexicons from seed terms, iteratively refine them through domain expertise, and rank documents based on term frequency. The system includes close reading functionality and allows export of curated sub-corpora for further analysis, supporting the iterative nature of humanities research.

## Key Results
- Successfully uncovered less well-known texts and authors challenging existing theories about migration and contagion in 19th century Britain and Ireland
- Demonstrated effectiveness in combining machine learning with domain expertise to improve relevance of retrieved texts
- Provided a transparent, interpretable process that maintains the close reading and iterative refinement central to humanities research methods

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Frontend -> Text processing pipeline -> Word embedding model generation -> Apache Solr information retrieval -> Database for storing lexicons and curated sub-corpora

- Critical path:
  1. User provides seed terms for a research theme.
  2. Backend generates word embeddings and recommends semantically similar words.
  3. User iteratively refines the lexicon by selecting relevant words.
  4. Backend ranks documents based on the frequency of occurrence of lexicon terms.
  5. User examines and curates the retrieved documents using the close reading interface.
  6. User saves, modifies, or exports the curated sub-corpora.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using word2Vec instead of more complex language models to handle OCR errors and lack of structure in historical texts.
  - Prioritizing transparency and interpretability over potential accuracy gains from more complex models.
  - Focusing on the specific needs of humanities researchers rather than general-purpose text mining.

- Failure signatures:
  - Irrelevant or misleading recommended words and retrieved documents.
  - Poor performance of the word embedding model on archaic language or OCR errors.
  - Difficulties in the iterative lexicon refinement process due to lack of domain knowledge or time constraints.
  - Issues with the close reading interface or export functionality limiting the benefits of iterative corpus refinement.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the word embedding model on a small sample of historical texts to assess its performance on archaic language and OCR errors.
  2. Conduct a user study with humanities researchers to evaluate the effectiveness of the interactive lexicon building process and the relevance of retrieved documents.
  3. Test the close reading interface and export functionality with a sample corpus to ensure they support the iterative nature of humanities research.
