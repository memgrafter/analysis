# Balancing Accuracy and Training Time in Federated Learning for Violence Detection in Surveillance Videos: A Study of Neural Network Architectures

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2308.05106
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05106
- Reference count: 13
- One-line primary result: Modified Diff-Gated architecture achieves higher accuracy with reduced computation compared to Flow-Gated while enabling privacy-preserving federated learning

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of violence detection in surveillance videos through federated learning, balancing accuracy and computational efficiency. The research introduces the Diff-Gated architecture, which uses frame differences instead of optical flow for motion representation, significantly reducing preprocessing time while maintaining or improving detection accuracy. The work demonstrates successful adaptation of centralized video datasets to federated learning contexts through stratified client partitioning, achieving state-of-the-art results while preserving data privacy across surveillance sites.

## Method Summary
The research employed transfer learning with pre-trained networks and One-Cycle training to accelerate convergence while maintaining high accuracy. The Diff-Gated architecture was developed to process frame differences alongside RGB channels, reducing computation time from 9 seconds to 0.065 seconds per video compared to optical flow preprocessing. Federated learning was implemented using the Flower framework with stratified dataset splits to prevent non-IID issues across clients. The model was trained and evaluated on the RWF-2000 dataset (2000 videos) along with AIRTLab, Crowd Violence, and Hockey Fights datasets, using accuracy metrics and ROC curve analysis to assess performance.

## Key Results
- Diff-Gated architecture achieved improved accuracy while reducing computation compared to original Flow-Gated architecture
- Frame differences preprocessing reduced computation time from 9 seconds to 0.065 seconds per video
- Federated learning with stratified splits maintained balanced class representation and achieved better accuracy than centralized baselines
- Transfer learning with one-cycle training enabled faster convergence without sacrificing detection performance

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Frame differences reduce preprocessing time compared to optical flow while retaining movement information needed for violence detection.
- Mechanism: Frame differences capture pixel-level changes between consecutive frames, highlighting motion without requiring complex optical flow computation, thus speeding up preprocessing.
- Core assumption: Movement information alone (without direction) is sufficient for detecting violent events.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Diff-Gated architecture... showed improved accuracy while reducing computation compared to the original Flow-Gated architecture"
  - [section] "we opted to use frame differences instead... requires significantly less computation time (0.065 seconds compared to 9 seconds for optical flow)"

- Break condition: If the violence detection task relies heavily on movement direction (e.g., punches vs. pushes), frame differences may lose critical information.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Federated learning with stratified dataset splits prevents Non-IID issues and maintains balanced class representation across clients.
- Mechanism: By ensuring each client receives a proportional mix of violent and non-violent samples, the model avoids skewed local updates that could harm global convergence.
- Core assumption: Balanced local data distribution approximates IID conditions well enough for effective federated training.
- Evidence anchors:

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input preprocessing → Diff-Gated CNN backbone (depth-wise separable conv layers) → Frame difference channel + RGB channel → Merging block → Fully connected layers → Output classification
- Critical path: Frame difference computation → CNN feature extraction → Merging of RGB and motion features → Classification
- Design tradeoffs: Frame differences reduce computation vs. optical flow but lose directional info; deeper networks improve accuracy but increase training time; federated learning preserves privacy but introduces communication overhead
- Failure signatures: High variance across clients in federated training; preprocessing bottleneck due to video decoding; overfitting on small local datasets

## Limitations
- Transfer learning performance heavily depends on the quality and relevance of pre-trained features to violence detection tasks
- Frame differences may miss directional movement information critical for distinguishing certain violent actions
- Federated learning introduces communication overhead and potential convergence issues with non-IID data distributions
- Dataset bias in RWF-2000 and other video datasets may limit generalizability to real-world surveillance scenarios
