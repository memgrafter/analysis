# Sensitivity of Slot-Based Object-Centric Models to their Number of Slots

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.18890
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18890
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Slot-based object-centric models exhibit substantial sensitivity to the number of slots (K) during training, leading to oversegmentation or undersegmentation that persists across methods and datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a systematic study of slot-based object-centric models, focusing on their sensitivity to the number of slots (K). The authors propose new metrics based on Adjusted Rand Index to quantify oversegmentation vs. undersegmentation, and evaluate three recent models (Slot Attention, SA Vi, OSRT) on five datasets. They find that incorrect choices of K during training lead to substantial oversegmentation or undersegmentation, and this issue persists across methods and datasets. The choice of objective function and conditioning on instance-level annotations can mitigate this behavior to some extent, but not fully resolve it. The paper highlights the importance of addressing this issue for future slot-based models to be effective in real-world settings with unknown numbers of objects.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct a comprehensive empirical study evaluating three slot-based object-centric models (Slot Attention, OSRT, and SA Vi) across five datasets (CLEVR, MSN, CATER, MOVi-A, MOVi-C). They systematically vary the number of slots (K) during both training and evaluation, introducing new metrics (Adjusted Rand Precision and Adjusted Rand Recall) to distinguish between oversegmentation and undersegmentation. The study compares random versus learned slot initialization, evaluates different training objectives (reconstruction vs novel-view synthesis), and tests conditioning on bounding boxes. All models use the same basic architecture: encoder → slot attention with K slots → decoder, with variations in training objectives and conditioning mechanisms.

## Key Results
- Slot-based models exhibit substantial sensitivity to K, with incorrect choices leading to either oversegmentation (when K is too high) or undersegmentation (when K is too low)
- Novel-view synthesis objectives significantly reduce sensitivity to K compared to reconstruction objectives
- Conditioning on bounding boxes can partially mitigate sensitivity, improving both oversegmentation and undersegmentation metrics
- The sensitivity pattern persists across all three models and five datasets, indicating this is a fundamental challenge for slot-based approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Slot-based object-centric models exhibit sensitivity to the number of slots (K) during training, leading to oversegmentation or undersegmentation.
- Mechanism: The model's internal notion of objectness is directly influenced by K. Too few slots force the model to merge objects or split them arbitrarily, while too many slots lead to splitting ground-truth objects into parts.
- Core assumption: The model's learned object decomposition is primarily determined during training and persists at inference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "incorrect choices of K during training lead to substantial oversegmentation or undersegmentation"
  - [section 4.1] "If Ktrain is set too high the model yields a poor ARR while maintaining a high ARP, indicating that severe oversegmentation occurs"
- Break condition: If the model uses a different strategy for handling excess slots (e.g., leaving them unused) or if training with an optimal K range exists where this sensitivity disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The choice of training objective influences the model's sensitivity to K.
- Mechanism: Objectives that enforce stronger spatial or structural consistency (e.g., novel-view synthesis) bias the model against oversegmentation by preventing parts of objects from being independently varied.
- Core assumption: The training objective imposes inductive biases that affect how the model allocates slots to objects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "the three models that are trained with the NVS objective are substantially less sensitive to the chosen number of model slots"
  - [section 4.2] "we conclude that architectural nuances or the number of input/target views for the NVS objective are less important than the inductive bias induced by the objective itself"
- Break condition: If other objectives (e.g., reconstruction loss) can achieve similar robustness without the spatial constraints of NVS.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Conditioning information (e.g., bounding boxes) can mitigate the sensitivity to K by steering the model's internal granularity level.
- Mechanism: External supervision provides the model with explicit cues about object boundaries, allowing it to adjust its internal notion of objectness and reduce oversegmentation/undersegmentation.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively integrate conditioning signals into its slot allocation mechanism.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "conditioning is helpful: for MOVi-A it improves the ARR without affecting the ARP and for the more complex MOVi-C dataset, it improves both ARR and ARP"
  - [section 4.3] "we use the bounding-box information in the last frame as a regression signal (supervised)"
- Break condition: If the conditioning signal is ambiguous, noisy, or if the model cannot effectively utilize it due to architectural limitations.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) and its extensions (Adjusted Rand Precision (ARP) and Adjusted Rand Recall (ARR))
  - Why needed here: To quantify and distinguish between oversegmentation and undersegmentation, which ARI alone cannot do.
  - Quick check question: If a model has perfect ARP but low ARR, is it oversegmenting or undersegmenting?
    - Answer: Oversegmenting (it's splitting objects into parts but not merging them).

- Concept: Slot Attention and its variants (SA Vi, OSRT)
  - Why needed here: These are the specific slot-based models being evaluated for sensitivity to K.
  - Quick check question: What is the key architectural difference between SA and OSRT that affects their sensitivity to K?
    - Answer: OSRT uses a novel-view synthesis objective, which imposes stronger spatial consistency.

- Concept: Object-centric representation learning
  - Why needed here: To understand the goal of slot-based models and why K is a critical hyperparameter.
  - Quick check question: Why is K typically chosen to match the number of ground-truth objects in the data?
    - Answer: Because in controlled datasets, the number of objects is known, but this assumption breaks down in real-world settings.

- Failure signatures:
  - High ARP, low ARR: Oversegmentation (splitting objects into parts)
  - Low ARP, high ARR: Undersegmentation (merging objects)
  - Low ARP, low ARR: Both oversegmentation and undersegmentation

## Limitations
- The study focuses primarily on synthetic datasets which may not fully capture real-world complexity
- Experiments rely on ground-truth bounding boxes for conditioning, which may not be available in practical applications
- The analysis doesn't explore models trained without object-level supervision
- Limited investigation of whether certain architectures are inherently more robust to K variation

## Confidence

- High confidence: The systematic sensitivity to K across different architectures and datasets is well-established through controlled experiments with clear metric patterns
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of novel-view synthesis objectives in reducing sensitivity, as the comparison is limited to three models and may not generalize to all slot-based architectures
- Medium confidence: The utility of conditioning signals, as experiments use ground-truth annotations rather than learned predictors
