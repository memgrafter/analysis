# Topic-oriented Adversarial Attacks against Black-box Neural Ranking Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14867
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14867
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed RELEVANT framework achieves up to 36.7% improvement in average boosted ranks and 100% success rate in promoting target documents

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a topic-oriented adversarial attack task against black-box neural ranking models (NRMs), where the goal is to promote a target document in rankings for a group of queries sharing the same topic. The authors propose RELEVANT, a reinforcement learning framework that trains a surrogate model to mimic the target NRM's behavior and uses policy gradient methods to generate imperceptible document perturbations. Experiments demonstrate that RELEVANT significantly outperforms existing attack strategies under both static and dynamic scenarios where the target model updates over time.

## Method Summary
RELEVANT addresses the topic-oriented adversarial ranking attack (TARA) task through a two-phase approach: first training a surrogate ranking model to imitate the black-box target NRM using relative relevance information from ranked lists, then employing reinforcement learning with a policy network to generate adversarial document perturbations. The framework uses a topic-oriented reward function that encourages the perturbed document to outrank anchor documents across all queries in a topic group. Two attack strategies are implemented: trigger generation (adding topic-irrelevant tokens) and word substitution (replacing words with synonyms). The method operates under both static settings and dynamic scenarios where the target model and corpus evolve over time.

## Key Results
- RELEVANT achieves up to 36.7% improvement in average boosted ranks compared to baseline attacks
- 100% success rate in promoting target documents for some cases
- Maintains effectiveness across dynamic scenarios including document updates and model changes
- Outperforms existing attack strategies in both static and dynamic environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The surrogate ranking model can effectively imitate the target NRM's ranking behavior.
- Mechanism: The surrogate model is trained using relative relevance information from the target model's ranked lists, creating pseudo-labels that capture the same ranking patterns.
- Core assumption: The target model's relative ranking information is sufficient to train a surrogate that mimics its behavior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We train a surrogate ranking model that can mimic the ranked list of the black-box target NRM"
  - [section 4.2.1] "we leverage the relative relevance information among the ranked result list returned by the target model to construct a synthetic dataset"
  - [corpus] Weak - no specific comparison of surrogate vs target model performance metrics provided
- Break condition: If the target model's ranking decisions rely on features or patterns that cannot be captured through pairwise comparisons of ranked documents, the surrogate will fail to accurately mimic behavior.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Topic-oriented rewards guide the RL agent to find perturbations effective across entire query groups.
- Mechanism: The reward function contrasts the relevance scores between the current perturbed document and anchor documents across all queries in the group, providing positive feedback when the perturbed document outranks its anchor.
- Core assumption: The surrogate model's relevance scores correlate with the target model's ranking behavior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a topic-oriented reward function guides the policy to find a successful adversarial example that can be promoted in rankings to as many queries as possible in a group"
  - [section 4.3.1] "We define the anchor document...as the subgoal...The reward function should be related to the document's ranking to each query in a group"
  - [corpus] Moderate - Table 2 shows RELEVANT outperforms baselines, suggesting effective reward shaping
- Break condition: If the surrogate model's relevance scores diverge significantly from the target model's actual ranking decisions, the reward signals will misguide the agent.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The RL framework adapts to dynamic environments through continuous interaction and learning.
- Mechanism: The agent receives real-time feedback from the environment as the target model updates, allowing it to adjust its attack strategy based on current conditions.
- Core assumption: The RL agent can learn generalizable attack strategies that transfer across model updates.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experimental results under both static and dynamic scenarios demonstrate...the effectiveness of our adversarial attack method"
  - [section 4.2.2] "we envisage three dynamic cases of the environment given the dynamic nature of the web"
  - [corpus] Moderate - Table 3 shows RELEVANT maintains effectiveness across document incremental, removal, and ranking-incentivized scenarios
- Break condition: If model updates are too rapid or fundamentally alter the ranking mechanism, the agent may not have sufficient time to adapt its strategy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the dynamic setting affect the effectiveness of topic-oriented adversarial attacks over time compared to static settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly defines both static and dynamic settings for the TARA task, with the dynamic setting being more challenging and practical as it accounts for real-world search engine updates.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that RELEVANT adapts to dynamic environments, but the long-term effectiveness and evolution of attacks under continuous updates are not fully explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed longitudinal studies showing attack success rates and adaptation strategies over multiple dynamic updates, including varying frequencies and types of updates.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the potential impacts of topic-oriented adversarial attacks on the fairness and diversity of search results?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on promoting target documents in rankings, but does not address broader implications on search result diversity or fairness.
- Why unresolved: The primary focus is on attack effectiveness rather than broader societal impacts, and the potential for targeted manipulation of search results is not discussed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of how topic-oriented attacks affect the diversity of returned documents and potential biases introduced in search rankings.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can defensive mechanisms be designed to mitigate topic-oriented adversarial attacks without significantly degrading ranking performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates the vulnerability of NRMs to adversarial attacks, implying a need for robust defenses, but does not propose or evaluate any countermeasures.
- Why unresolved: The study is focused on attack methodologies, and developing effective defenses is a separate challenge that requires different approaches and evaluations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of defensive mechanisms that can detect or neutralize adversarial perturbations while maintaining ranking accuracy and efficiency.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks quantitative comparisons of surrogate vs. target model performance, making it difficult to assess the quality of mimicry
- The topic-oriented reward function's effectiveness depends on surrogate model relevance scoring correlating with target behavior, but this correlation is not empirically validated
- The method's effectiveness in truly black-box settings where perfect surrogate mimicry is impossible is not demonstrated

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The basic RL framework architecture (surrogate training + policy gradient optimization) is sound and well-established in the literature
- **Medium confidence**: The topic-oriented reward function provides meaningful guidance across query groups, based on observed performance improvements over baselines
- **Low confidence**: The method's effectiveness in truly black-box settings where the surrogate cannot perfectly mimic target behavior

## Next Checks

1. **Surrogate Model Fidelity**: Compare the surrogate model's MRR@10 and nDCG@20 scores against the target model on a held-out validation set to quantify the quality of mimicry before testing attack effectiveness.

2. **Reward Function Isolation**: Run ablations where RELEVANT uses topic-agnostic rewards versus topic-oriented rewards while keeping all other components constant, to measure the specific contribution of the topic-oriented formulation.

3. **Dynamic Adaptation Limits**: Test attack success rates across increasingly frequent model updates (weekly, daily, hourly) to identify the breaking point where the RL agent cannot adapt quickly enough to maintain effectiveness.
