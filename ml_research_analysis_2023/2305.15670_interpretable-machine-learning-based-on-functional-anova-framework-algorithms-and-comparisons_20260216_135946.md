# Interpretable Machine Learning based on Functional ANOVA Framework: Algorithms and Comparisons

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.15670
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15670
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: GAMI-Lin-T outperforms EBM in identifying true interactions and achieves competitive predictive performance, especially in correlated data scenarios

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GAMI-Lin-T, a tree-based algorithm for interpretable machine learning using the functional ANOVA framework. The method fits main effects and second-order interactions using linear models within tree nodes, incorporating iterative refinement and interaction filtering to improve accuracy and interpretability. Evaluations on simulated and mortgage datasets demonstrate that GAMI-Lin-T generally outperforms existing methods like EBM and GAMI-Net, with particular advantages in identifying true interactions and handling correlated predictors.

## Method Summary
GAMI-Lin-T is a tree-based interpretable machine learning algorithm that uses linear models within tree nodes to fit main effects and second-order interactions. The method includes a novel interaction filtering algorithm and an iterative fitting approach that alternates between updating main effects and interactions. A purification step ensures hierarchical orthogonality of the estimated components. The algorithm is evaluated against EBM and GAMI-Net on simulated datasets with known interaction structures and a real mortgage dataset.

## Key Results
- GAMI-Lin-T identifies more true interactions than EBM while maintaining competitive predictive performance
- Iterative refinement between main effects and interactions improves accuracy in correlated data scenarios
- The method ensures hierarchical orthogonality of main effects and interactions, enhancing interpretability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Iterative refinement between main effects and interactions yields better model performance than sequential two-stage fitting. When predictors are correlated, the interaction term is partially captured by main effects. By iteratively updating both components, the algorithm recovers more accurate interaction estimates and avoids underfitting. This works when the underlying function can be decomposed into additive main effects and interactions that are not perfectly orthogonal due to correlation.

### Mechanism 2
Linear models within tree nodes capture complex nonlinearities more parsimoniously than piecewise constant trees. Model-based trees fit linear functions in each partition, reducing the number of splits needed to model nonlinear relationships compared to constant fits, leading to fewer trees and less overfitting. This approach works when the true response surface is locally smooth and can be approximated by linear models within partitions.

### Mechanism 3
Hierarchical orthogonality purification ensures main effects and interactions are mathematically disentangled, improving interpretability. After fitting, the algorithm orthogonalizes interactions by removing their projections onto main effects, then reassigns these projections back to the main effects, guaranteeing the orthogonality condition. This works when functional ANOVA decomposition requires orthogonal components for unique interpretation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Main-effect stage -> Interaction filtering -> Interaction stage -> Purification -> Iterative refinement
- **Critical path**: Main-effect fitting → Interaction filtering → Interaction fitting → Purification → Iterative refinement
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Linear models within nodes vs. piecewise constants: More flexible but computationally heavier
  - Iterative vs. two-stage fitting: Better accuracy but slower convergence
  - Purification step: Ensures interpretability but adds post-processing cost
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor performance on highly discontinuous response surfaces
  - Computational bottlenecks with very large datasets
  - Missed interactions when filtering threshold is too high

  3. Evaluate the impact of purification on interpretability by comparing main-effect plots before and after orthogonalization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of GAMI-Lin-T compare to other interpretable machine learning methods when dealing with higher-order interactions (beyond two-way interactions)? The paper primarily focuses on main effects and second-order interactions, but mentions that GAMI models can capture projections of higher-order interactions into one and two-dimensions. It also notes that when third-order interactions are large, GAMI models may not perform as well as XGBoost.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of the correlation structure among predictors on the accuracy and interpretability of the main effects and interaction effects estimated by GAMI-Lin-T? The paper discusses the challenges of dealing with correlated predictors, noting that part of the interaction effects can be captured as main effects, creating a quadratic pattern. It also mentions that the iterative fitting method in GAMI-Lin-T can help reverse the identification of false main effects from the first round.

### Open Question 3
How does the choice of the number of knots in the B-spline transformations used in GAMI-Lin-T affect the model's performance and interpretability? The paper mentions that GAMI-Lin-T uses B-spline transformations with a default of 5 knots to transform the modeling variable in interaction trees.

## Limitations

- Performance on datasets with significant higher-order interactions beyond two-way interactions remains unverified
- Computational complexity of iterative refinement with purification steps for large datasets is not thoroughly benchmarked
- Method's robustness to severe multicollinearity and its impact on interaction detection accuracy needs further validation

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Iterative refinement mechanism improves accuracy over two-stage fitting
- **Medium confidence**: Linear models within tree nodes provide superior parsimony compared to piecewise constants
- **Medium confidence**: Hierarchical orthogonality purification meaningfully improves interpretability
