# SAILER: Structure-aware Pre-trained Language Model for Legal Case Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.11370
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11370
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Achieves NDCG@10 of 0.7979 on LeCaRD and 0.8823 on COLIEE2020 in zero-shot setting

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SAILER, a structure-aware pre-trained language model designed specifically for legal case retrieval. The model addresses the challenge of retrieving relevant legal cases from large corpora where documents are long and have complex logical structures. SAILER uses an asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture that reconstructs masked reasoning and decision sections using fact embeddings, encouraging the model to capture key legal elements and long-distance dependencies. The model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on Chinese and English legal benchmarks without requiring legal annotation data.

## Method Summary
SAILER employs an asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture with a deep Fact Encoder and two shallow decoders (Reasoning Decoder and Decision Decoder). The model is pre-trained using three objectives: Masked Language Modeling on fact sections, Reasoning Reconstruction, and Decision Reconstruction. During pre-training, a small percentage of tokens in the fact section are masked, while the reasoning and decision sections are aggressively masked (30-60%). The model learns to reconstruct these masked sections using only the fact vector representation. For downstream legal case retrieval, the fact vectors are compared using cosine similarity. The model is evaluated on Chinese and English legal benchmarks using NDCG, Precision, Recall, and F1 metrics.

## Key Results
- SAILER achieves NDCG@10 of 0.7979 on LeCaRD and 0.8823 on COLIEE2020 in zero-shot setting
- Outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods by significant margins on both Chinese and English legal benchmarks
- Demonstrates strong discriminative ability to distinguish cases with different charges without legal annotation data
- On COLIEE2021, SAILER is the only pre-trained model that exceeds QL at Recall@100 metric

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture reconstructs masked reasoning and decision sections using the fact vector, forcing the encoder to focus on key legal elements.
- Mechanism: By aggressively masking reasoning and decision sections and requiring reconstruction based on fact embeddings, the model learns to extract and encode the most salient legal elements during encoding.
- Core assumption: The reasoning and decision sections contain all key legal elements, and these elements are consistently mentioned in the reasoning section.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SAILER employs an asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture to integrate several different pre-training objectives. In this way, rich semantic information across tasks is encoded into dense vectors."
  - [section 4.2]: "We design the Reasoning Decoder to model the logical relationships between Reasoning and Fact, aiming to improve the alignment between the focus of the encoder and the underlining key legal elements in case documents."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. No direct citations found for this specific mechanism, but related works like SEED and RetroMAE also use autoencoder architectures for dense retrieval.
- Break condition: If key legal elements are not consistently mentioned in reasoning sections, or if reasoning sections contain non-essential information that confuses the reconstruction task.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pre-training with legal document structure improves discrimination between cases with different charges.
- Mechanism: The decision decoder reconstructs formatted legal decisions (charges, law articles, penalties) using fact embeddings, creating discriminative vectors that separate cases by legal outcome.
- Core assumption: Legal cases with different charges should have more distant vector representations, and decision formatting is consistent across cases.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SAILER has powerful discriminative ability, even without any legal annotation data. It can distinguish legal cases with different charges accurately."
  - [section 4.3]: "The Decision Decoder also relies on the dense vector ℎ𝐹 to recover the original decision. Specifically, the input of the Decision Decoder is: [ℎ𝐹 , 𝑒𝑑1 + 𝑝𝑑1, ..., 𝑒𝑑𝑛 + 𝑝𝑑𝑛 ]"
  - [section 5.4.2]: "On COLIEE2021, SAILER is the only pre-trained model that exceeds QL at Recall@100 metric, indicating it can better distinguish confused cases in a large corpus."
- Break condition: If legal cases with different charges share too many similar facts, or if decision formatting varies significantly across jurisdictions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Modeling long-distance dependencies between document structures improves retrieval performance.
- Mechanism: The encoder processes the full fact section while decoders must use this representation to reconstruct distant reasoning and decision sections, forcing the model to capture relationships across document structures.
- Core assumption: Legal case documents have clear logical structures where facts directly influence reasoning and decisions, and these relationships span hundreds to thousands of words.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Legal case documents are typically long text sequences with intrinsic logical structures. However, most existing language models have difficulty understanding the long-distance dependencies between different structures."
  - [section 4.1]: "We first randomly replace some tokens of the Fact with a special token [MASK]. Only a small percentage of tokens is replaced, as the majority of the Fact need to be preserved."
  - [section 5.6.1]: "An appropriate encoder mask ratio helps improve the performance. However, SAILER's performance decreases slightly when the encoder masking ratio is too high. This is because a too-large masking ratio will prevent the generation of high-quality sentence embeddings"
- Break condition: If legal case documents lack clear structural relationships, or if the average distance between related sections exceeds the model's attention capacity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Fact Encoder -> ℎ𝐹 -> (Reasoning Decoder -> Reconstruction) + (Decision Decoder -> Reconstruction)
- Critical path: Fact → Encoder → ℎ𝐹 → (Decoder 1 → Reconstruction) + (Decoder 2 → Reconstruction)
  The fact encoding must capture all information needed for both reconstruction tasks.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Shallow decoders vs deep decoders: Shallow decoders reduce parameter count but may limit reconstruction capacity
  - Aggressive vs conservative masking: Higher masking ratios increase difficulty but may hurt learning stability
  - Shared vs separate decoders: Separate decoders allow task-specific learning but increase parameters
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor retrieval performance: Fact encoder not capturing discriminative information
  - Unstable training: Aggressive masking causing gradient issues or poor reconstruction
  - Suboptimal discrimination: Decision decoder not learning to separate different charges effectively
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test different mask ratios (encoder: 0-30%, decoder: 15-60%) to find optimal balance between difficulty and learning
  2. Compare shared vs separate decoders to measure impact on performance and parameter efficiency
  3. Evaluate encoder-only vs full model to quantify contribution of reconstruction objectives

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How can the position of key legal elements in the Reasoning section be determined to improve the effectiveness of the Reasoning Decoder?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the random masking used in the Reasoning Decoder could not effectively mask all the key legal elements and thus provides limited guidance for SAILER.
- Why unresolved: Determining the exact positions of key legal elements requires a deeper understanding of legal case documents and may involve complex analysis of legal concepts and their relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study that proposes and evaluates a method for accurately identifying the positions of key legal elements in the Reasoning section of legal case documents, and demonstrates its impact on the performance of the Reasoning Decoder in SAILER.

## Limitations

- Structural assumptions about legal documents may not generalize across different legal systems, particularly for English cases with inconsistent decision formatting
- Limited analysis of how reconstruction objectives translate to discriminative power in dense vector space
- Zero-shot results may be influenced by specific characteristics of the benchmark datasets

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The architectural design and training methodology are clearly specified and technically sound
- **Medium Confidence**: The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements over baselines, but warrant cautious interpretation
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of SAILER's approach across different legal systems remains uncertain
