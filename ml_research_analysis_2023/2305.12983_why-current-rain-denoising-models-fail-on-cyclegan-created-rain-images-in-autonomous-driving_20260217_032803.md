# Why current rain denoising models fail on CycleGAN created rain images in autonomous driving

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12983
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12983
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: Current de-raining models struggle with CycleGAN-generated rain conditions, achieving only ~15% quality gain versus rainy images

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why existing rain removal models fail when processing CycleGAN-generated rain images for autonomous driving applications. The authors create synthetic rainy/clear image pairs using CycleGAN and train two state-of-the-art de-raining models (Restormer and RESCAN) on these datasets. Despite human raters finding the synthetic rain realistic in 7 out of 10 cases, the de-raining models showed limited performance improvement. The results suggest that current de-raining architectures, designed primarily for simple rain streak overlays, cannot effectively handle the more complex rain phenomena captured by CycleGAN, including reflections and road spray. This highlights a significant gap between synthetic training data and real-world rain conditions.

## Method Summary
The authors generated synthetic rainy images using CycleGAN to create paired datasets of clear and rainy conditions. They then trained two de-raining models - Restormer and RESCAN - on these synthetic datasets. Human evaluation confirmed that the CycleGAN-generated rain appeared realistic in most cases (7/10). The performance of both de-raining models was evaluated by comparing the quality of output images against both the rainy inputs and the original clear images, measuring the improvement achieved.

## Key Results
- Human raters found CycleGAN-generated rain realistic in 7 out of 10 cases
- Both Restormer and RESCAN achieved only ~15% quality gain compared to rainy images
- Current de-raining models struggle with complex rain phenomena like reflections and road spray
- Performance degradation suggests fundamental limitations in model architecture design

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study demonstrates that the failure of current de-raining models on CycleGAN-generated rain stems from architectural mismatches. Traditional de-raining models are optimized for simple rain streak patterns that can be modeled as additive noise or straightforward overlays. However, CycleGAN-generated rain includes more complex phenomena like specular reflections, dynamic spray patterns, and spatially varying intensity distributions that interact with scene geometry. These complex patterns require different feature extraction mechanisms and spatial reasoning capabilities that current architectures lack. The limited 15% quality gain indicates that models cannot effectively separate these complex rain artifacts from underlying scene content.

**Component Map:** CycleGAN -> Synthetic Dataset -> De-raining Model (Restormer/RESCAN) -> Quality Evaluation

**Critical Path:** The critical processing path involves CycleGAN generating paired rainy/clear images, which are then used to train de-raining models. The models process rainy images through convolutional/feature extraction layers, apply attention mechanisms (transformers for Restormer, channel attention for RESCAN), and reconstruct clean images through decoder layers.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The study prioritizes synthetic data generation over real-world data collection, trading authenticity for control and scale. De-raining models balance computational efficiency against reconstruction quality. The choice of only two models limits architectural diversity exploration but allows focused analysis.

**Failure Signatures:** Limited quality gain (~15%) indicates models cannot separate complex rain artifacts from scene content. Persistent rain streaks, unnatural smoothing artifacts, and loss of fine details are common failure modes. ## Limitations
- Single synthetic dataset created through one CycleGAN approach limits generalizability
- Only two de-raining models tested, leaving architectural limitations unclear
- Human perceptual validation lacks detailed metrics and larger sample sizes
- No comparison against real rain datasets to determine if synthetic rain is genuinely harder

## Confidence

**High confidence:** CycleGAN-generated rain presents challenges for current de-raining models
**Medium confidence:** Performance gap is specifically due to model architecture limitations versus training data issues  
**Low confidence:** The 15% quality gain figure represents a generalizable performance ceiling for current approaches
