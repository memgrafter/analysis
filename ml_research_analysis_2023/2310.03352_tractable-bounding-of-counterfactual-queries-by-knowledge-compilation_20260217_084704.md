# Tractable Bounding of Counterfactual Queries by Knowledge Compilation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2310.03352
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03352
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: Symbolic knowledge compilation enables up to an order of magnitude speedup in computing counterfactual query bounds compared to standard Bayesian network inference

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational challenge of bounding partially identifiable counterfactual queries in Pearlian structural causal models. The authors propose a method that leverages symbolic knowledge compilation to convert Bayesian networks into arithmetic circuits with symbolic parameters representing exogenous probabilities. By compiling a single circuit structure and substituting actual exogenous probabilities for different queries, the approach achieves significant runtime improvements. Experiments on a synthetic benchmark of 335 models demonstrate speedups of up to an order of magnitude, particularly for large models with high-cardinality exogenous variables.

## Method Summary
The method uses symbolic knowledge compilation to convert Pearlian structural causal models (PSCMs) into arithmetic circuits (ACs) where exogenous probabilities are treated as symbolic parameters. This allows the same circuit structure to be reused across multiple queries by simply substituting different exogenous probability values. The approach exploits deterministic structural equations to produce more compact circuits and parallelizes computations across c-components (connected components of the endogenous variables). An iterative Expectation-Maximization (EM) scheme approximates bounds for partially identifiable counterfactual queries by repeatedly computing joint probabilities over the compiled circuit.

## Key Results
- Runtime improvements of up to an order of magnitude compared to standard Bayesian network inference methods
- Significant speedups particularly for large models (21 nodes) and high-cardinality exogenous variables (up to 256)
- The method scales better than standard BN approaches, which time out on larger models
- Parallelization across c-components provides additional computational efficiency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Symbolic knowledge compilation enables reusing the same circuit structure for multiple queries with different exogenous probabilities.
- Mechanism: The compiler produces an arithmetic circuit (AC) where all exogenous probability values are treated as symbolic parameters. When computing bounds for different queries, these symbolic parameters are substituted with the actual exogenous probabilities for each query, avoiding recompilation.
- Core assumption: The endogenous CPTs (structural equations) remain constant across queries while only exogenous probabilities change.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "a single symbolic knowledge compilation allows us to obtain the circuit structure with symbolic parameters to be replaced by their actual values when computing the different queries"
  - [section]: "We might intend PSCM compilation as a symbolic compilation achieved by treating the exogenous PMFs as parameters. In contrast, the endogenous CPTs, implementing the SEs and remaining the same for all the models, are treated as constant numerical values."
- Break condition: If the structural equations change between queries, the symbolic AC must be recompiled.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Parallelization across c-components significantly reduces computation time.
- Mechanism: The exogenous-to-endogenous arcs define c-components (connected components). The EM algorithm can be run independently on each c-component in parallel, since their parameter updates don't interfere with each other.
- Core assumption: The c-components are independent given their parents, allowing parallel EM execution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "For each c-component, we define a subgraph... The procedure can be executed in parallel, separately for each c-component."
- Break condition: If c-components are not truly independent (violating the c-component definition), parallelization may produce incorrect results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Degenerate CPTs representing deterministic structural equations yield more compact ACs.
- Mechanism: When structural equations are deterministic (CPTs with 0/1 values), the knowledge compiler can exploit context-specific independence to produce smaller ACs with fewer nodes and arcs.
- Core assumption: Structural equations in the SCM are deterministic or have repeated parameter values.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The degenerate nature of the CPTs can be exploited by the compiler to achieve smaller ACs and hence faster inferences (e.g., with the FSCM in Fig. 3 as input, ACE returns an AC with 96 arcs if the determinism of the CPTs is not exploited and 23 arcs otherwise)."
- Break condition: If structural equations are non-deterministic with unique parameter values, AC size reduction will be minimal.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: PSCM compiler -> Symbolic arithmetic circuit (AC) -> EM algorithm with parameter substitution -> Parallel c-component execution
- Critical path: The AC traversal for joint probability computation θ(u,v) during each EM iteration
- Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoff is between compilation time and inference speed. Pre-compiling a symbolic AC takes time but pays off when computing many queries. The parallelization tradeoff is between the overhead of managing multiple processes and the speedup from parallel execution.
- Failure signatures: Slow performance likely indicates that the AC is not being properly exploited (perhaps due to non-deterministic CPTs) or that parallelization is not effective (perhaps due to insufficient c-components). Incorrect bounds may indicate violations of c-component independence assumptions.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run the EM algorithm on a small PSCM with known bounds to verify correctness of the AC-based approach versus standard BN inference.
  2. Measure compilation time versus inference speedup by varying the number of queries computed for the same PSCM.
  3. Test parallelization efficiency by varying the number of c-components and measuring speedup on different core counts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the symbolic knowledge compilation approach scale with the size of the structural causal models, particularly for models with more than 21 nodes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the experiments were conducted on models with up to 21 nodes and that the BN approaches cannot handle bigger networks in reasonable time limits. The authors intend to use the approach for very large models as future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents results for models up to 21 nodes, and the authors acknowledge that they plan to explore larger models in future work. The scalability of the approach for models significantly larger than 21 nodes is not yet established.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the runtime and accuracy of the symbolic knowledge compilation approach for structural causal models with significantly more than 21 nodes, compared to standard BN inference methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the cardinality of exogenous variables affect the performance of the symbolic knowledge compilation approach, especially for very high-cardinality exogenous variables?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the cardinality of exogenous variables ranges between 3 and 256 in the experiments, with an average of 29.6. The authors intend to explore models with high-cardinality exogenous variables as future work.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only covered exogenous variables with cardinalities up to 256, and the authors plan to explore models with even higher cardinalities. The impact of very high-cardinality exogenous variables on the approach's performance is not yet known.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results demonstrating the runtime and accuracy of the symbolic knowledge compilation approach for models with exogenous variables having significantly higher cardinalities than those tested in the paper.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the symbolic knowledge compilation approach perform on structural causal models with large treewidth, which are generally intractable by variable elimination?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the authors intend to explore more in-depth problems with network structures with large treewidth that may thus be intractable by variable elimination.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not present any results or analysis on the performance of the approach for models with large treewidth. The authors acknowledge that this is an area for future exploration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance of the symbolic knowledge compilation approach to standard BN inference methods for structural causal models with varying treewidth, particularly for models with large treewidth.

## Limitations

- Compilation scalability to extremely large SCMs (hundreds of variables) remains uncertain
- EM convergence properties and required iteration counts need further validation
- Parallelization assumes c-component independence, which may be violated in practice

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The core mechanism of using symbolic compilation with parameter substitution for multiple queries
- Medium Confidence: The claimed speedups (up to an order of magnitude) are supported by experimental data
- Low Confidence: Long-term stability and convergence properties of the EM algorithm in this specific application context
