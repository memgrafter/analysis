# Centralized control for multi-agent RL in a complex Real-Time-Strategy game

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13004
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13004
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Centralized actor-critic pixel-to-pixel architecture trained with PPO learns resource gathering and survival in Lux AI v2 1v1 scenario

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a centralized training framework for multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) in the complex Real-Time Strategy game Lux AI v2. The authors tackle the challenges of variable-sized fleets of units, resource gathering, and allocation in a 1v1 scenario against other competitors. The proposed approach uses a centralized actor-critic pixel-to-pixel architecture trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The results show that the agent learns to gather resources and survive for the 1,000 steps of the game in many episodes, but there is a high degree of variance due to the complexity of the environment. The paper provides an open-source codebase and shares the logs for all the experiments.

## Method Summary
The authors implement a centralized actor-critic pixel-to-pixel architecture trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to control variable-sized fleets of units in Lux AI v2. The approach uses a 48x48 grid map with 17 feature channels representing units, factories, and resources. A ResNet backbone with squeeze-and-excitation layers produces low-dimensional representations that are fed to separate actor heads for factories and robots. The centralized critic evaluates the joint state, enabling credit assignment across agents. Reward shaping is used to encourage water generation, and prioritized fictitious self-play provides stable learning dynamics against non-stationary opponents.

## Key Results
- Agent learns to gather resources and survive for 1,000 steps in many episodes
- High degree of variance observed due to complexity of environment
- Open-source codebase and experiment logs provided for reproducibility

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Centralized actor-critic pixel-to-pixel architecture enables scalable joint action learning in variable-sized fleets.
- **Mechanism:** The architecture encodes the full 48x48 grid map into stacked feature maps (C=17) representing units, factories, and resources. A ResNet backbone with squeeze-and-excitation layers produces low-dimensional representations that are fed to separate actor heads for factories and robots. The centralized critic evaluates the joint state, enabling credit assignment across agents.
- **Core assumption:** Independence assumption between factory and robot actions holds sufficiently for effective joint policy learning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "An actor-critic pixel-to-pixel architecture [Han et al., 2019] is used, which is similar to Gridnet [Chen et al., 2023], and trained with PPO."
  - [section] "During training, the model computes the joint action probabilities of all valid robot and factory actions by summing their logits. The latter corresponds to multiplications in the space of probabilities which assumes independence between factory and robot actions."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in neighbor papers about this independence assumption.
- **Break condition:** If agent interactions require strong coordination where robot actions critically depend on factory states (or vice versa), the independence assumption breaks down and performance degrades.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Reward shaping with explicit water generation rewards accelerates learning in sparse-reward RTS environments.
- **Mechanism:** Dense rewards are provided for robots digging ice and factories refining it, bypassing the need to learn these critical survival skills from sparse win/loss signals. This curriculum-like approach ensures agents survive long enough to learn late-game dynamics.
- **Core assumption:** The hand-crafted water generation reward distribution captures the essential skills needed for survival and lichen growth.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "We use reward shaping to define a hand-crafted reward distribution that encourages players to generate water... Arguably, the latter is a too complex sequence of actions to be discovered from sparse or delayed rewards."
  - [section] "While players win by growing as much lichen in 1000 game steps, resource requirements are too critical in Lux to have RL agents learn to generate water only from game-winning rewards."
  - [corpus] Missing - neighbor papers don't discuss reward shaping in this specific context.
- **Break condition:** If the reward shaping overemphasizes water generation at the expense of strategic considerations (like resource allocation efficiency or opponent interaction), agents may develop myopic behaviors.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Self-play with prioritized fictitious self-play (PFSP) provides stable learning dynamics against non-stationary opponents.
- **Mechanism:** The learner agent plays against a queue of its own checkpoints, with the most recent checkpoint sampled 50% of the time. This creates a curriculum of increasingly challenging opponents while maintaining training stability.
- **Core assumption:** The non-stationary nature of the opponent (another learning agent) can be managed through self-play against past checkpoints.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "Prioritized Fictitious Self-play (PFSP) [Vinyals et al., 2019] is implemented to provide a stable training pipeline for the RL agents."
  - [section] "During training, the most recent checkpoint is sampled with a 50% chance and any other one randomly otherwise."
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbor papers mention self-play but not specifically PFSP implementation details.
- **Break condition:** If the opponent's strategy space is too large or evolves too quickly, the fixed-size checkpoint queue may not provide sufficient diversity or appropriate difficulty progression.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Observation preprocessor -> ResNet backbone -> Actor heads (factory + robot) + Critic head -> PPO trainer
- **Critical path:** Observation → ResNet encoding → Actor heads + Critic → Action sampling with masks → Environment interaction → Reward shaping → PPO update
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Centralized vs. decentralized: Centralized provides better credit assignment but scales poorly; chosen for variable fleet sizes
  - Pixel-to-pixel vs. entity-based: Pixel-to-pixel leverages spatial structure but requires fixed map size
  - Dense vs. sparse rewards: Dense rewards accelerate learning but may bias behavior; implemented water generation rewards with sparse endgame rewards
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High value loss with spikes indicates poor value estimation, often from exploring high-value states
  - KL divergence spikes suggest large policy updates causing catastrophic forgetting
  - Entropy decreasing to near-zero indicates premature convergence to suboptimal deterministic policies
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train with only factory actions enabled to verify the centralized architecture works for single-agent control
  2. Add robot actions with simplified action space (movement only) to test multi-agent coordination
  3. Implement and test the invalid action masking to ensure illegal actions are properly filtered before policy updates

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that future work includes "better definitions of the observation and action spaces".

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests using "other architectures whose inductive biases are more appropriate for grid-like RTS games" as future work.
- Why unresolved: The current architecture may not be the most suitable for the Lux AI v2 environment, and alternative architectures might offer better performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of different architectures, including U-Net and LSTM, in the Lux AI v2 environment would provide insights into their effectiveness.

### Open Question 3

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions implementing population-based training as future work.
- Why unresolved: The current self-play approach using prioritized fictitious self-play may not be the most effective way to train the agent, and population-based training could potentially improve the learning dynamics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of the agent trained with population-based training to the current approach would provide insights into the effectiveness of population-based training.

## Limitations
- Centralized approach faces scalability challenges with larger fleet sizes and more complex scenarios
- Pixel-to-pixel architecture may not generalize to larger or dynamically-sized maps
- Independence assumption between factory and robot actions could limit performance in scenarios requiring tight coordination

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The pixel-to-pixel architecture's ability to process spatial features and enable centralized training for variable-sized fleets
- **Medium Confidence:** The effectiveness of reward shaping in accelerating learning, as this is empirically validated but could lead to myopic behaviors
- **Medium Confidence:** The stability of self-play with PFSP, given the theoretical basis but limited empirical evidence in this specific context
