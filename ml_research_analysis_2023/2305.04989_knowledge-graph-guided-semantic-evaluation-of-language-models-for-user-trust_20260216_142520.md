# Knowledge Graph Guided Semantic Evaluation of Language Models For User Trust

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04989
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04989
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Language models show diminishing returns in semantic understanding beyond ~1B parameters for knowledge graph path reconstruction

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the semantics encoded in self-attention transformers by leveraging explicit knowledge graph structures. We propose novel metrics to measure reconstruction error when providing graph path sequences from knowledge graphs and attempting to reproduce them from transformer outputs. The opacity of language models has immense bearing on societal issues of trust and explainable decision outcomes. Our findings suggest that language models are models of stochastic control processes for plausible language pattern generation, but do not ascribe object and concept-level meaning and semantics to the learned stochastic patterns such as those described in knowledge graphs.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates semantics in self-attention transformers using knowledge graph path reconstruction. Researchers extract approximately 300K knowledge graph links from multiple sources (DBPedia, ConceptNet, Wiktionary, WordNet, OpenCyc Ontology) with various relationships. They mask tokens in multi-hop knowledge graph paths and evaluate whether language models can predict the correct token within top-5 ranks (%Top@5). The method calculates reconstruction error by measuring the percentage of times the correct answer appears in the top five probabilities from softmax logits. The study also constructs an augmented GLUE benchmark to test concept understanding.

## Key Results
- Performance measured by %Top@5 increases steadily with model parameters but plateaus around 1 billion parameters
- False paths in reconstruction almost exclusively involve knowledge of strongly typed objects and their properties in the real world
- Language models show limited ability to ascribe object and concept-level meaning to learned patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Masked token prediction in knowledge graph paths provides a direct test of semantic understanding beyond simple link prediction.
- Mechanism: By masking tokens in multi-hop knowledge graph paths and evaluating the model's ability to predict the correct token within top-5 ranks (%Top@5), the approach tests whether the model captures relational semantics and conceptual understanding required to reconstruct full paths.
- Core assumption: Knowledge graph paths encode richer semantic relationships than single links, and successful reconstruction requires understanding of both concepts and their relations.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We propose novel metrics to measure the reconstruction error when providing graph path sequences from a knowledge graph and trying to reproduce/reconstruct the same from the outputs of the self-attention transformer models."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Language models exhibit a performance plateau in semantic understanding beyond a certain parameter threshold (~1B parameters).
- Mechanism: As model size increases, %Top@5 performance improves until approximately 1 billion parameters, after which performance plateaus, suggesting diminishing returns for semantic understanding at scale.
- Core assumption: There is a fundamental limit to what can be learned from statistical patterns alone, regardless of model size.
- Evidence anchors: [section III.A] "The performance measured using %Top@5 increases steadily with the number of model parameters. However, after a certain amount of parameters is reached ( ~ 1 billion), the performance starts to flat-line."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Failure cases in knowledge graph path reconstruction reveal fundamental gaps in conceptual world modeling, particularly for strongly typed real-world concepts.
- Mechanism: By analyzing false paths where models fail, the study identifies specific types of semantic relationships (e.g., object capabilities, contextual knowledge) that models struggle with, indicating lack of true conceptual understanding.
- Core assumption: The types of errors made by models reveal the nature of their semantic understanding or lack thereof.
- Evidence anchors: [section III.B] "Interestingly, the false paths almost exclusively involve knowledge of strongly typed objects and their properties as seen in the real world."

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can language models be effectively modified or extended to reliably encode and understand concept-level semantics as found in knowledge graphs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes that language models currently do not ascribe object and concept-level meaning and semantics to learned patterns, suggesting a fundamental limitation in their ability to understand knowledge graph semantics.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore potential modifications to language models that could improve their ability to encode knowledge graph semantics, leaving open the question of whether this is a solvable problem.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective methods for evaluating a language model's understanding of real-world concepts and relationships beyond simple knowledge graph path reconstruction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a knowledge-augmented GLUE benchmark and the %Top@5 metric for evaluating concept understanding, but acknowledges that these are initial steps and more robust evaluation methods are needed.
- Why unresolved: The paper's proposed evaluation methods, while novel, may not fully capture the complexity of real-world concept understanding and may need to be refined or expanded.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the opacity of language models be addressed to improve trust and explainability in high-stakes applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper emphasizes the importance of addressing the opacity of language models for trust and explainable decision outcomes, particularly in high-stakes applications.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide concrete solutions for making language models more transparent or explainable, focusing instead on the problem of their current opacity.

## Limitations

- The study relies on specific knowledge graph sources which may have inherent biases and coverage limitations
- The masking strategy and path construction process could significantly influence results but are not fully specified
- The augmented GLUE benchmark construction process is not detailed enough for direct replication

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Language models show diminishing returns in semantic understanding beyond ~1B parameters for knowledge graph path reconstruction
- Current language models struggle with strongly typed real-world concept understanding as measured by knowledge graph path reconstruction

**Medium Confidence:**
- The %Top@5 metric provides a valid measure of semantic understanding beyond simple link prediction
- Error patterns in knowledge graph reconstruction reveal fundamental gaps in conceptual modeling

**Low Confidence:**
- The observed performance plateau represents a fundamental limit of statistical learning rather than architecture-specific constraints
- The augmented GLUE benchmark will reliably measure improvements in semantic understanding across different language model architectures

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Architecture Validation**: Test whether the performance plateau at ~1B parameters holds across different transformer architectures (e.g., DeBERTa, T5, RoBERTa) and training approaches to determine if this represents a fundamental limit or architecture-specific constraint.

2. **Knowledge Graph Source Variation**: Replicate the experiments using different knowledge graph sources with varying ontological commitments and coverage (e.g., Wikidata, YAGO, Freebase) to assess whether the observed semantic understanding gaps are consistent across different knowledge representations.

3. **Controlled Concept Understanding Tests**: Design targeted experiments that isolate specific types of semantic relationships (e.g., capability relations, taxonomic relations, causal relations) to determine whether the observed failures reflect genuine conceptual gaps or artifacts of the knowledge graph construction and evaluation methodology.
