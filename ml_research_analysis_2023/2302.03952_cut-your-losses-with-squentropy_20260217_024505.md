# Cut your Losses with Squentropy

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03952
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03952
- Reference count: 40
- Combines cross-entropy loss with average square loss over incorrect classes for improved classification accuracy and calibration

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces squentropy, a novel loss function that combines cross-entropy with average square loss over incorrect classes for multi-class classification. The authors demonstrate that squentropy outperforms both pure cross-entropy and rescaled square losses across various datasets and neural architectures, achieving better classification accuracy and model calibration while maintaining compatibility with standard optimization parameters.

## Method Summary
Squentropy combines the standard cross-entropy loss with an additional term that applies squared loss to all incorrect class predictions. This dual-loss approach anchors incorrect logits at zero while driving correct logits to infinity, creating a more balanced optimization landscape. The method can be implemented as a direct replacement for cross-entropy loss without requiring hyperparameter tuning or architectural modifications.

## Key Results
- Outperforms both cross-entropy and square loss baselines in classification accuracy across 121 tabular datasets and various computer vision/NLP tasks
- Achieves superior model calibration as measured by Expected Calibration Error (ECE) compared to standard cross-entropy
- Shows significantly reduced variance in test performance across different random initializations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Squentropy improves model calibration by anchoring incorrect logits at zero, reducing overconfidence.
- Mechanism: The second term in squentropy loss pushes all incorrect logits to zero, preventing them from drifting toward negative infinity as in cross-entropy loss. This creates a more stable logit configuration where the model is less likely to assign near-certain probabilities to predictions.
- Core assumption: The logits of incorrect classes remain bounded and do not diverge to negative infinity during training.
- Evidence anchors: abstract shows squentropy can be trained using same optimization parameters as cross-entropy; paper explains incorrect logits are anchored at zero.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Squentropy provides implicit regularization by controlling weight norms during training.
- Mechanism: By preventing logits from diverging to extreme values, squentropy implicitly constrains the weight norms in the final linear layer, leading to smoother decision boundaries and better generalization.
- Core assumption: Smaller weight norms correlate with improved generalization and calibration.
- Evidence anchors: paper shows squentropy produces smaller weight norms than cross-entropy during training; average confidence for predicted label in cross-entropy is close to 1.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Squentropy reduces variance in test performance across different random initializations.
- Mechanism: The combination of cross-entropy and squared loss terms creates a more balanced optimization landscape that is less sensitive to initialization noise.
- Core assumption: The optimization landscape for squentropy is more stable than for cross-entropy or square loss alone.
- Evidence anchors: abstract states squentropy has less variance with respect to random initialization; paper reports standard deviation of accuracy across initialization randomness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does squentropy loss improve model robustness to adversarial attacks compared to cross-entropy loss?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that squentropy provides smoother decision boundaries and larger margins, which could suggest improved robustness to perturbations.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test squentropy against adversarial examples or attacks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing squentropy-trained models against standard adversarial attack methods (e.g., FGSM, PGD) and comparing their performance and robustness metrics to cross-entropy models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does squentropy loss affect the training dynamics and convergence speed compared to cross-entropy loss?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that squentropy can be trained using the same optimization parameters as cross-entropy, but does not provide detailed analysis of training dynamics.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on final performance metrics but does not analyze the training process itself.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed comparison of training curves, convergence rates, and gradient behaviors between squentropy and cross-entropy models across various tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can squentropy loss be effectively combined with other regularization techniques (e.g., dropout, weight decay) to further improve model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "various calibration techniques can potentially be applied with squentropy in the same way they are used with cross entropy."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not experiment with combining squentropy with other regularization methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing combinations of squentropy with common regularization techniques, measuring their effects on accuracy, calibration, and generalization.

## Limitations
- Theoretical understanding of why squentropy works remains limited, with most insights derived from empirical observations
- The "plug-and-play" advantage hasn't been systematically tested across all possible architectures and learning rate schedules
- No analysis of how squentropy performs on extremely large-scale problems or under data distribution shift scenarios
