# Neural Continuous-Discrete State Space Models for Irregularly-Sampled Time Series

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11308
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11308
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed NCDSSM outperforms existing models on imputation and forecasting tasks for irregularly-sampled time series

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Neural Continuous-Discrete State Space Models (NCDSSM) to address the challenge of modeling irregularly-sampled time series with continuous-time dynamics. The framework uses auxiliary variables to separate recognition from dynamics, enabling efficient Bayesian inference. Three variants are proposed with different dynamics parameterizations: linear time-invariant, non-linear, and locally-linear. The model is trained using an evidence lower bound that marginalizes dynamic states during inference. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate superior performance in imputation and forecasting tasks compared to existing approaches.

## Method Summary
The NCDSSM framework addresses irregularly-sampled time series by modeling underlying continuous-time dynamics with discrete observations. The core innovation is the use of auxiliary variables that disentangle recognition from dynamics, enabling efficient Bayesian inference. Three variants are proposed: a linear time-invariant model, a non-linear model, and a locally-linear model. The models are trained using an evidence lower bound (ELBO) that marginalizes over the dynamic states during inference, allowing for principled handling of uncertainty in the continuous-time representation.

## Key Results
- NCDSSM outperforms existing models on both imputation and forecasting tasks for irregularly-sampled time series
- The framework demonstrates improved performance and robustness, particularly for long-term predictions beyond the training context
- Three variants (linear, non-linear, and locally-linear) provide flexibility in modeling different types of underlying dynamics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The key mechanism behind NCDSSM's effectiveness is the disentanglement of recognition and dynamics through auxiliary variables. This separation allows the model to efficiently perform Bayesian inference while maintaining the ability to capture complex continuous-time dynamics. By marginalizing over dynamic states during training via the ELBO framework, the model can handle irregularly-spaced observations without requiring ad-hoc interpolation or discretization. The auxiliary variables act as a bridge between the continuous latent states and the discrete observations, enabling principled uncertainty quantification and more robust long-term predictions.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Observations -> Auxiliary Variables -> Continuous Latent States -> Dynamics Model -> ELBO Objective

**Critical Path**: Irregular observations are first mapped to auxiliary variables, which then parameterize the inference network for continuous latent states. These latent states evolve according to the chosen dynamics model (linear, non-linear, or locally-linear). The ELBO objective is computed by marginalizing over the latent states and optimized end-to-end.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The use of auxiliary variables adds model complexity but enables more efficient inference. The choice between linear, non-linear, and locally-linear dynamics involves a tradeoff between model expressiveness and computational efficiency. The variational approach provides uncertainty quantification but may struggle with highly complex posterior distributions.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance on long-term forecasting may indicate inadequate modeling of temporal dependencies. Failure to handle highly irregular sampling patterns could suggest limitations in the recognition network's ability to adapt to varying time intervals. Degraded performance on high-dimensional data may indicate scalability issues with the variational inference approach.

## Limitations
- Scalability to high-dimensional time series data remains uncertain due to computational complexity of variational inference
- The assumption that underlying dynamics can be captured by proposed parameterizations may not hold for all real-world time series
- Limited diversity in experimental datasets raises questions about generalizability to all types of irregularly-sampled time series

## Confidence
- High confidence: Effectiveness of variational inference approach for handling irregularly-sampled observations
- Medium confidence: Superiority of NCDSSM over existing models (dataset and metric dependent)
- Low confidence: Generalizability of results to all types of irregularly-sampled time series
