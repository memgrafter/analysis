# Towards Deployable RL -- What's Broken with RL Research and a Potential Fix

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01320
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01320
- Reference count: 2
- The paper identifies five key problems hindering practical deployment of reinforcement learning and proposes a "deployable RL" approach focused on real-world challenges rather than abstract benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
The paper identifies five fundamental problems with current reinforcement learning research that prevent practical deployment: overfitting to specific benchmarks, misplaced focus on sample complexity over system engineering, detached theoretical work, uneven playing fields between research groups, and lack of experimental rigor. To address these issues, the authors propose a "deployable RL" framework that shifts focus from benchmark performance to solving concrete real-world challenges. This approach emphasizes measuring progress through real-world impact rather than synthetic metrics, grounding theory in specific applications, and adopting design-pattern oriented research to address system lifecycle issues. The paper calls for a coordinated effort from the research community to reframe how RL research is conducted, evaluated, and rewarded.

## Method Summary
The proposed "deployable RL" approach replaces traditional benchmark-based research with challenge-oriented frameworks where progress is measured by real-world impact. The method involves three key principles: (1) using challenges sponsored by researchers from academia and industry with quantifiable measures of real-world progress, (2) grounding theoretical work in specific challenges and considering system lifecycle issues like data acquisition, debugging, and testability, and (3) adopting design-pattern oriented research to address recurring deployment problems. The approach requires coordinated effort from researchers, reviewers, and conference organizers to credit challenge contributions and reframe evaluation criteria away from benchmark performance toward practical deployment value.

## Key Results
- Identifies five critical problems with current RL research: benchmark overfitting, wrong focus on sample complexity, detached theory, uneven playing fields, and lack of experimental rigor
- Proposes shifting from benchmarks to challenges as the primary research framework
- Suggests design-pattern oriented research to address system lifecycle management issues
- Calls for community-wide adoption of new evaluation criteria focused on real-world impact

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Shifting from benchmarks to challenges realigns RL research with practical impact by creating problem domains where progress is measured by real-world value rather than synthetic performance metrics.
- Mechanism: The paper proposes that challenges sponsored by researchers from academia/industry with clear quantifiable measures of real-world progress will incentivize research that directly addresses deployable RL problems rather than abstract benchmark optimization.
- Core assumption: That the research community will value and contribute to challenges with real-world impact metrics over traditional benchmark performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "progress is measured by real-world impact"
  - [section] "Credit for contributing challenges... A fundamental criterion for evaluating the challenge would be a quantifiable measure of making real, and well-accepted progress on it"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains papers with similar themes of "broken" systems needing fixes, but no direct evidence of challenge-based RL research frameworks
- Break condition: If researchers continue to prioritize publication in traditional venues that reward benchmark performance over real-world impact, or if challenges cannot establish meaningful quantifiable measures of progress.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Grounding theory papers in specific challenges ensures theoretical work addresses practical deployment issues rather than abstract mathematical properties.
- Mechanism: By requiring theory papers to be "grounded in a real challenge" and consider "problems that have to do with the system life cycle focusing on issues such as data acquisition, debugging, testability and performance deterioration," theoretical work becomes more relevant to deployable RL.
- Core assumption: That theoretical frameworks can be effectively developed and tested against concrete challenges rather than abstract models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "When a theory paper proposes a model, an algorithm, or a solution, the problem should be clear and grounded in a real challenge"
  - [section] "Theory papers should also consider problems that have to do with the system life cycle focusing on issues such as data acquisition, debugging, testability and performance deterioration"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus; this appears to be a novel proposal
- Break condition: If theoretical frameworks become too narrowly focused on specific challenges and lose generalizability, or if challenges cannot be clearly defined for certain theoretical problems.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Design-pattern oriented research addresses the system engineering gaps in RL deployment by providing reusable solutions for lifecycle management issues.
- Mechanism: The paper proposes that RL systems should incorporate "conceptual solutions to problems where issues such as testability, debuggability, and other system life-cycle issues are addressed," similar to how software engineering uses design patterns.
- Core assumption: That RL deployment challenges can be decomposed into recurring patterns that have generalizable solutions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Software design is based on plugging multiple design patterns, massaging them, and repackaging a software system"
  - [section] "Approaches to life-cycle management that solve problems grounded in applications are a welcome addition to RL research"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus; this appears to be a novel proposal drawing from software engineering principles
- Break condition: If RL deployment problems are too diverse and context-specific to benefit from generalized design patterns, or if the community lacks the software engineering expertise to develop these patterns.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we effectively measure and quantify real-world progress on RL challenges compared to benchmark performance metrics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose that "A fundamental criterion for evaluating the challenge would be a quantifiable measure of making real, and well-accepted progress on it."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a key principle but does not provide concrete methodologies for measuring real-world impact. There's a fundamental tension between standardized evaluation and domain-specific impact.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating specific metrics and evaluation frameworks that successfully predict real-world deployment success based on challenge performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between theoretical foundations and practical engineering considerations in deployable RL research?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors criticize "Detached Theory" and suggest theory should "address specific challenges" and consider "problems that have to do with the system life cycle."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the disconnect between theory and practice but doesn't provide a framework for integrating theoretical rigor with practical deployment constraints.
- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies of successful deployable RL systems showing how theoretical insights directly contributed to practical solutions, along with failure cases where theory-practical disconnect led to deployment issues.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the research community effectively incentivize and reward contributions to challenges rather than benchmark-focused papers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose that "A rigorous presentation of a challenge should be credited as an important contribution" and suggest special "contributed challenges" papers.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this requires "a coordinated effort from researchers, reviewers, and senior area chairs" but doesn't specify implementation mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of publication patterns showing successful integration of challenge-focused contributions into the standard publication ecosystem, including citation patterns and conference acceptance rates.

## Limitations

- The proposals rely on untested assumptions about research community behavior and lack concrete implementation details for the proposed "deployable RL" approach
- No concrete examples of successful "challenges" are provided, making it difficult to understand the exact format and requirements
- The design-pattern approach draws from software engineering without demonstrating how RL-specific patterns would be systematically developed or validated

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The identification of five key problems in current RL research (overfitting to benchmarks, wrong focus, detached theory, uneven playing field, lack of rigor) is well-supported by observable patterns in the literature
- **Medium confidence**: The proposed shift from benchmarks to challenges represents a reasonable conceptual solution, but its effectiveness depends on community adoption and the ability to establish meaningful real-world metrics
- **Low confidence**: The design-pattern oriented research approach and specific mechanisms for grounding theory in challenges are novel proposals without empirical validation
