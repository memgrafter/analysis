# Exemplars and Counterexemplars Explanations for Image Classifiers, Targeting Skin Lesion Labeling

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel explainable AI framework that provides exemplars and counterexamples to interpret decisions made by a skin lesion classifier. The approach uses a convolutional neural network for classification and an adversarial autoencoder to generate synthetic images that explain the model's decisions. The method generates local neighborhoods in the latent feature space and uses decision trees to extract rules for creating exemplars and counterexamples. The system was tested on the ISIC 2019 skin lesion dataset and achieved a balanced multi-class accuracy of 0.838 on the validation set.

## Method Summary
The framework consists of two main components: a CNN classifier based on ResNet50 architecture for skin lesion classification, and a Progressive Growing Adversarial Autoencoder (PGAAE) for generating explanations. The PGAAE is trained incrementally from low to high resolution with denoising and mini-batch discrimination to improve stability and prevent mode collapse. For explanations, the system encodes input images into the latent space, generates local neighborhoods, learns decision trees on these neighborhoods to extract rules, and then generates exemplars and counterexamples based on these rules. Saliency maps are created by computing median pixel differences between original images and exemplars.

## Key Results
- Achieved 0.838 balanced multi-class accuracy on ISIC 2019 validation set
- Successfully generated synthetic exemplars and counterexamples that maintain semantic similarity to real skin lesions
- Demonstrated that the explanation framework can provide meaningful insights into classifier decisions for medical image classification tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial Autoencoders (AAE) enable realistic synthetic exemplars and counterexamples for image classification explanations.
- Mechanism: AAE combines a generative adversarial network (GAN) with autoencoder representation learning, where the encoder maps images to a latent space, the decoder reconstructs them, and the discriminator ensures the latent distribution matches a prior distribution. This allows generation of realistic synthetic images that maintain the identity of the original distribution.
- Core assumption: The latent space learned by the AAE preserves semantic similarity between synthetic and real images.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The exemplars are generated via an adversarial autoencoder."
  - [section 3.1] "AAEs are probabilistic autoencoders that aim at generating new random items that are highly similar to the training data."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on explainable AI for skin lesions but don't directly confirm AAE effectiveness for exemplars.
- Break condition: If the AAE fails to learn a meaningful latent space or suffers from mode collapse, generated exemplars will be unrealistic or non-diverse.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Progressive growing architecture stabilizes AAE training for high-resolution medical images.
- Mechanism: Starting with low-resolution images and incrementally adding convolutional layers allows the model to first learn large-scale structure before focusing on fine details. This progressive initialization acts as regularization, reducing mode collapse and improving convergence.
- Core assumption: Gradually increasing image resolution during training is more stable than training on full resolution from the start.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2.3] "The main idea behind such construction relies on the instability caused to the training process by heavy structured high dimensional data."
  - [section 4.2.2] "The incremental addition of the layers allows the models to first learn large scale structure and progressively shift the attention to finer detail."
  - [corpus] No direct confirmation in related papers - this is the paper's novel contribution.
- Break condition: If progressive growing is not properly implemented or if the resolution steps are too large, training may still fail to converge.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Denoising autoencoder and noise injection improve generalization and mitigate identity function learning.
- Mechanism: Randomly corrupting input images during training forces the autoencoder to learn robust representations rather than simply copying inputs. Adding noise to discriminator inputs prevents it from becoming too confident and improves convergence.
- Core assumption: Introducing noise during training prevents overfitting and improves the model's ability to generalize to unseen data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2.4] "Denoising autoencoders are a stochastic version of basic autoencoders. A Denoising autoencoder attempts to address the identity function issue by randomly corrupting input images that the autoencoder must then reconstruct."
  - [section 4.2.4] "Another way to gain more generalization, reduce the vanishing gradient problem and improve convergence is to add noise also to the discriminator inputs."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers don't specifically discuss denoising autoencoders for this application.
- Break condition: If noise levels are too high, the model may fail to learn meaningful representations; if too low, the benefits may be negligible.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  CNN Classifier (ResNet50-based) -> AAE (Encoder, Decoder, Discriminator) -> Progressive Growing -> Denoising -> Mini Batch Discrimination -> Explanation Extraction (Local Classifier Rule Extraction)

- Critical path:
  1. Train CNN classifier on ISIC dataset
  2. Train AAE on same dataset using progressive growing and denoising
  3. For explanation: encode input image, generate latent neighborhood, learn decision tree, extract exemplars and counterexamples, generate saliency map

- Design tradeoffs:
  - High-dimensional latent space (256 features) provides more expressive power but requires more training data and is prone to mode collapse
  - Progressive growing adds implementation complexity but significantly improves training stability
  - Denoising improves generalization but may reduce reconstruction quality if noise is too high

- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse: AAE generates only a few types of images or identical images
  - Poor reconstruction: Generated exemplars are blurry or don't resemble skin lesions
  - Unstable training: Loss values fluctuate wildly or fail to converge
  - Overfitting: Generated images are too similar to training data and lack diversity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train AAE on ISIC dataset without progressive growing or denoising - observe if mode collapse occurs
  2. Implement progressive growing with denoising but without mini batch discrimination - check if training stabilizes
  3. Test explanation generation on a held-out validation set - verify if generated exemplars are realistic and useful for understanding classifier decisions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses various techniques used to improve the PGAAE's performance, such as denoising autoencoder, mini-batch discrimination, and progressive growing. However, it also mentions that training generative models is challenging and there is room for improvement.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on addressing specific issues like mode collapse and training stability, but does not explore all possible techniques to enhance the quality and diversity of generated images.

- Why unresolved: While the paper presents a framework for generating explanations, it does not fully address how users can interact with and explore the latent space beyond viewing static exemplars and counterexamples.
- What evidence would resolve it: A user study or prototype demonstrating an interactive interface that allows medical practitioners to navigate and query the latent space effectively.
