# Inferring dynamic regulatory interaction graphs from time series data with perturbations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07803
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07803

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RiTINI, a novel method for inferring dynamic regulatory interaction graphs from time series data with perturbations. Traditional causal inference methods are limited to acyclic and static graphs, but RiTINI overcomes these limitations by combining graph attention mechanisms and graph neural ODEs to learn time-varying, cyclic, and directed graphs. The method is evaluated on both simulated and real-world datasets, including neuronal networks, gene regulatory networks, and dynamical systems. RiTINI demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in inferring interaction graphs, outperforming other methods in terms of graph edit distance.

## Method Summary
RiTINI combines graph attention mechanisms and graph neural ODEs to learn time-varying, cyclic, and directed graphs from time series data with perturbations. The method consists of two main components: a graph neural ODE encoder and a graph attention decoder. The graph neural ODE encoder processes the time series data and learns a continuous-time representation of the nodes, while the graph attention decoder predicts the interaction weights between nodes at each time step. RiTINI can be trained with node perturbations, which enhances its ability to learn network structure. The method is evaluated on simulated and real-world datasets, and its performance is compared to other state-of-the-art methods in terms of graph edit distance.

## Key Results
- RiTINI outperforms traditional causal inference methods in inferring interaction graphs from time series data with perturbations.
- The method demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in terms of graph edit distance on both simulated and real-world datasets.
- RiTINI can effectively learn time-varying, cyclic, and directed graphs, which is a significant improvement over existing methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RiTINI works by combining the strengths of graph neural ODEs and graph attention mechanisms. Graph neural ODEs allow for the continuous-time representation of nodes, capturing the dynamic nature of regulatory interactions. The graph attention decoder then uses this representation to predict the interaction weights between nodes at each time step, allowing for the inference of time-varying, cyclic, and directed graphs. By training RiTINI with node perturbations, the method can better learn the network structure and handle the complexity of real-world data.
