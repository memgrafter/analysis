# Inferring the Goals of Communicating Agents from Actions and Instructions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16207
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16207
- Reference count: 18
- One-line primary result: Model achieves R = 0.96 correlation with human goal inferences by combining actions and instructions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Bayesian inverse planning model that infers the shared goals of human-robot teams from their actions and natural language instructions. The model treats the team as a single group agent with a joint plan and shared goal, using GPT-3 to evaluate instruction likelihoods and real-time A* search for planning. Experiments in a multi-agent gridworld show the model's inferences closely match human judgments and outperform action-only inference, especially early in the task when instructions provide crucial disambiguation.

## Method Summary
The method uses Bayesian inverse planning to compute goal posteriors over a gridworld task with colored gems as goals. For each goal hypothesis, the model computes a joint plan via real-time A* search, extracts salient actions, and evaluates instruction likelihood using GPT-3. The final posterior is computed via exact Bayesian inference by updating weights as new evidence arrives. The model assumes agents follow a Boltzmann-rational policy and treats instructions as communicating actions from the optimal plan.

## Key Results
- Model achieves R = 0.96 correlation with human goal inferences
- Instructions reduce uncertainty and accelerate goal inference convergence
- Model outperforms action-only inference, especially early in the task
- Instructions provide crucial early disambiguation of goals

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The model's strong correlation with human goal inferences (R = 0.96) demonstrates that combining action and instruction data captures human-like reasoning.
- Mechanism: By treating the human-robot team as a single group agent with a shared goal, the model bypasses the complexity of recursive mental modeling while still capturing the joint inference humans perform.
- Core assumption: Humans implicitly model the team as having a shared mental state rather than tracking individual mental states recursively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "finding that our model's inferences closely correlate with human judgments (R = 0.96)"
  - [section]: "We model them as a singular mind with a shared goal g, and a joint plan π"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Instructions provide crucial early disambiguation of goals, reducing uncertainty and accelerating convergence.
- Mechanism: Language instructions communicate salient actions that narrow the hypothesis space of possible goals before sufficient actions are observed, effectively providing a "jump start" to goal inference.
- Core assumption: Human communicators provide informative instructions that are strongly correlated with the true goal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "instructions lead to more rapid and less uncertain goal inference"
  - [section]: "instructions greatly accelerate the convergence of inferences to the true goal"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The probabilistic program architecture enables flexible integration of neural language models with classical planning.
- Mechanism: By using probabilistic programming, the model can treat the LLM as a modular component (utterance likelihood function) while maintaining exact Bayesian inference through deterministic planning components.
- Core assumption: Neural language models can provide calibrated likelihoods for instruction generation given hypothesized goals and plans.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "using GPT-3 as a likelihood function for instruction utterances"
  - [section]: "using the CURIE variant of GPT-3... to serve as the utterance likelihood P (u|α1:k, E)"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Goal prior -> Joint planner (A* search) -> Utterance model (GPT-3) -> Action model (Boltzmann policy) -> Posterior update
- Critical path: For each goal hypothesis: 1) Compute joint plan via A* search, 2) Extract salient actions, 3) Compute utterance likelihood with GPT-3, 4) Compute action likelihoods from policy, 5) Update posterior weights
- Design tradeoffs: Using exact inference vs. sampling for accuracy vs. computational cost; modeling team as group agent vs. individual agents for simplicity vs. expressiveness; fixed vs. learned temperature parameter for flexibility vs. complexity
- Failure signatures: Poor correlation with human inferences (indicates model doesn't capture human reasoning), slow convergence (indicates insufficient information from actions/instructions), high variance in human judgments (indicates ambiguous stimuli or model uncertainty)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test correlation between model inferences and human judgments on a small subset of stimuli with known ground truth
  2. Compare inference accuracy with vs. without instructions on identical action sequences
  3. Vary temperature parameter T to find optimal setting for different levels of action noise

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model's performance degrade with increasing numbers of goals beyond the four-color gem scenario tested?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper tests the model in a gridworld with exactly 4 possible goals. There's no mention of scaling to larger goal spaces or how performance would change with more complex goal sets.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only evaluate the model on a limited, simple environment with 4 discrete goals. The paper doesn't explore performance on more complex scenarios with larger goal spaces or continuous goals.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the model on environments with 10+ possible goals, or with continuous goal spaces, and comparing performance metrics like correlation coefficients and Brier scores to the current results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is the model to real-world deviations from the assumed optimal planning behavior, such as human mistakes or bounded rationality?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Discussion section explicitly states "our model assume[s] that agents follow a Boltzmann-rational policy, which does not account for bounded rationality or other systematic deviations from optimality."
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this limitation but doesn't empirically test how well the model handles suboptimal or irrational behavior, which is common in real human cooperation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with human participants intentionally making suboptimal moves or deviating from optimal plans, then measuring how well the model infers goals compared to when humans act optimally.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the model handle scenarios where the principal communicates actions from non-optimal plans, rather than just the optimal lowest-cost plan?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The Discussion section states "we have modeled utterances as communicating actions from only the optimal lowest-cost plan" and notes this is "overly rational" compared to real-world communication.
- Why unresolved: The model currently assumes speakers are perfectly rational in choosing what to communicate, but real humans might mention actions from suboptimal plans or omit important actions by mistake.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with stimuli where the instruction describes actions from a sub-optimal plan or omits key actions, then measuring whether the model can still accurately infer the true goal compared to when instructions are perfectly optimal.

## Limitations

- Performance depends on quality of GPT-3 utterance likelihood function which may not generalize beyond specific few-shot examples
- Fixed temperature parameter and single shared goal assumption may limit real-world applicability
- Evaluation based on single experimental condition with relatively small sample (20 stimuli)

## Confidence

- High confidence: The general framework of Bayesian inverse planning for goal inference
- Medium confidence: The specific performance metrics (R = 0.96) and their interpretation
- Medium confidence: The claim that instructions significantly improve early goal inference

## Next Checks

1. **Generalization test**: Evaluate the model on a held-out set of instructions with different linguistic patterns to assess the robustness of the GPT-3 utterance likelihood function.
2. **Ablation study**: Systematically remove components (instructions, joint planning, language model) to quantify their individual contributions to inference accuracy.
3. **Human-subject replication**: Replicate the human experiment with a larger sample size and additional stimuli to verify the stability of the reported correlation.
