# Large Language Models Need Holistically Thought in Medical Conversational QA

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05410
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05410
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Introduces HoT method for medical CQA with state-of-the-art performance on correctness, professionalism, and considerateness.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the Holistically Thought (HoT) method, a novel approach for guiding large language models in medical conversational question answering. The method combines diffused and focused thinking strategies to improve the quality of responses in medical CQA tasks. HoT was evaluated on three medical CQA datasets in English and Chinese, demonstrating significant improvements over state-of-the-art approaches. The zero-shot nature of the method, coupled with its adaptability, makes it a promising solution for enhancing medical conversational AI systems.

## Method Summary
The Holistically Thought (HoT) method guides large language models by integrating diffused and focused thinking strategies. Diffused thinking allows the model to explore a wide range of information, while focused thinking narrows down the search to relevant and accurate responses. This dual approach is designed to enhance the correctness, professionalism, and considerateness of medical conversational QA outputs. The method is implemented through carefully crafted prompts and does not require additional model training or data annotation, making it a zero-shot solution adaptable to various scenarios.

## Key Results
- HoT achieves state-of-the-art performance on three medical CQA datasets in English and Chinese.
- BLEU-2 improvements exceed 100% on some datasets compared to prior approaches.
- Medical expert evaluations confirm enhanced accuracy and professionalism of responses.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Holistically Thought (HoT) method works by balancing two complementary thinking strategies: diffused and focused thinking. Diffused thinking enables the model to consider a broad spectrum of information, which is crucial in medical contexts where diverse knowledge sources may be relevant. Focused thinking then refines this broad exploration into precise, accurate, and contextually appropriate responses. This combination helps the model avoid common pitfalls such as hallucination or overly generic answers, leading to improved correctness, professionalism, and considerateness in medical conversational QA.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
HoT Prompt → Diffused Thinking Module → Focused Thinking Module → Response Generation

### Critical Path
The critical path is: HoT Prompt → Diffused Thinking → Focused Thinking → Response Generation. Each stage builds upon the previous one, with the prompt setting the context, diffused thinking exploring possibilities, focused thinking refining the answer, and the final module producing the response.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Zero-shot vs. Fine-tuning**: Zero-shot avoids additional data/annotation but may limit adaptability to highly specialized domains.
- **Diffused vs. Focused Balance**: Too much diffusion can lead to irrelevant content; too much focus may miss important context.
- **Prompt Complexity**: Complex prompts can improve guidance but may be harder to generalize across languages or domains.

### Failure Signatures
- **Fluency Issues**: Responses may be grammatically correct but lack natural conversational flow.
- **Overfitting to Prompts**: Model may rely too heavily on prompt structure rather than genuine understanding.
- **Domain Limitations**: Performance may degrade when applied to non-medical or highly specialized medical subfields.

### First 3 Experiments
1. **Ablation on Thinking Strategies**: Remove diffused or focused thinking to measure individual impact.
2. **Cross-Dataset Generalization**: Apply HoT to a new medical dataset not seen during development.
3. **Prompt Complexity Sweep**: Vary prompt detail to find optimal balance between guidance and flexibility.

## Limitations
- Fluency of responses remains a challenge, with low scores reported in evaluations.
- The method’s generalizability to other domains or languages requires further validation.
- Comparison with few-shot methods is not exhaustive, and robustness across model sizes is untested.

- **Fluency**: Medium — improvements noted, but persistent issues remain.
