# From Zero to Hero: Examining the Power of Symbolic Tasks in Instruction Tuning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.07995
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07995
- **Reference count:** 27
- **Primary result:** Symbolic tasks, particularly SQL execution, significantly improve zero-shot generalization for table reasoning and mathematical reasoning without hurting general task performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the use of symbolic tasks for instruction tuning to enhance zero-shot generalization in language models. The authors focus on SQL execution as a representative symbolic task to improve table reasoning capabilities. They demonstrate that symbolic tasks can be generated in large quantities with high quality, providing a unique advantage over crowdsourced or model-generated tasks. Through extensive experiments on various benchmarks, they show that fine-tuning models jointly on natural language tasks and symbolic SQL execution tasks leads to significant improvements in zero-shot performance on table reasoning, outperforming much larger models like GPT-3 and ChatGPT. The method also improves mathematical reasoning while maintaining performance on general tasks.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a method for instruction tuning using symbolic tasks, specifically SQL execution, to improve zero-shot generalization. The approach involves synthesizing SQL execution examples from tables using SQL templates from the SQUALL dataset and fine-tuning FLAN-T5 models using a rehearsal strategy with a mixture of natural language tasks (~600K examples) and symbolic SQL execution tasks (~200K examples). The fine-tuned models are then evaluated on various table reasoning benchmarks and multi-choice tasks in zero-shot settings to assess their performance.

## Key Results
- TAPEX ZERO significantly improves zero-shot table reasoning performance, surpassing fine-tuned TAPAS on WTQ.
- The approach does not hurt performance on general tasks, maintaining comparable results on BBH and MMLU benchmarks.
- Zero-shot performance on table reasoning tasks exceeds that of much larger models like GPT-3 and ChatGPT.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Symbolic tasks provide structured, rule-based problems that improve zero-shot generalization by exposing models to consistent, executable patterns.
- **Mechanism:** The model learns to manipulate symbolic representations and apply logical rules, which transfers to unseen natural language tasks requiring similar reasoning patterns.
- **Core assumption:** The symbolic manipulation skills learned are abstract enough to transfer to diverse natural language contexts.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "symbolic tasks present a unique advantage as they can be easily generated in vast quantities, theoretically providing an infinite supply of high-quality training instances."
  - [section 1] "symbolic tasks are characterized by the use of symbols in formal systems, such as logic, mathematics, or programming languages, instead of relying on natural language."
  - [corpus] Weak correlation (0.587 FMR) with similar symbolic instruction-tuning papers suggests limited direct precedent but some related work exists.
- **Break condition:** If symbolic representations are too domain-specific and fail to abstract to natural language reasoning patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** SQL execution as a symbolic task improves table reasoning by teaching models to parse structured data and execute logical operations.
- **Mechanism:** Training on SQL execution tasks teaches models to understand table structures, apply logical operations, and extract information, which transfers to table reasoning tasks.
- **Core assumption:** The ability to execute SQL queries on tables directly improves the ability to answer questions about tables.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Empirical results on various benchmarks validate that the integration of SQL execution leads to significant improvements in zero-shot scenarios, particularly in table reasoning."
  - [section 4.2] "Our proposed TAPEX ZERO significantly boosts performance based on the FLAN-T5 models, surpassing all of baseline methods significantly. Taking the WTQ task as an example, the performance of TAPEX ZERO (XL) even exceeds 50%, which is superior to the fine-tuned performance of TAPAS."
  - [corpus] Related work exists but with moderate FMR, suggesting some precedent but not overwhelming support.
- **Break condition:** If the SQL execution does not require understanding table semantics, limiting transfer to natural language table reasoning.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Symbolic tasks do not harm general task performance, allowing improvement in specific domains without catastrophic forgetting.
- **Mechanism:** The model maintains performance on general tasks while improving on symbolic tasks, indicating the symbolic training does not interfere with general knowledge.
- **Core assumption:** The model's capacity allows learning both symbolic and general tasks without interference.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "experimental results on BBH (27 tasks) and MMLU (57 tasks) show that fine-tuning with symbolic tasks does not hurt the model performance on generic held-out tasks."
  - [section 4.2] "the performance of TAPEX ZERO and FLAN-T5 on these two datasets is comparable... These experimental results demonstrate that our proposed symbolic tasks does not hurt the model performance on generic held-out tasks."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence from corpus, but the claim is directly supported by experimental results in the paper.
- **Break condition:** If the symbolic tasks require so much capacity that they interfere with the model's ability to maintain general knowledge.

- **Why unresolved:** The paper only examines SQL execution, leaving the effectiveness of other symbolic tasks unexplored.

- **What evidence would resolve it:** Ablation studies varying the proportion of symbolic tasks in the training mixture and measuring the resulting performance on diverse NLP benchmarks.

- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not provide a direct comparison between symbolic tasks and other synthetic task generation methods.

## Limitations

- **Limited ablation studies:** The paper does not provide ablation studies on the contribution of SQL execution tasks versus other symbolic tasks, making it difficult to assess whether SQL is uniquely effective or if other symbolic tasks would yield similar results.
- **Evaluation scope constraints:** While the paper demonstrates improvements on table reasoning and mathematical reasoning, the evaluation is limited to specific benchmarks, leaving the generalizability to other domains requiring symbolic reasoning untested.
- **Symbolic task generation assumptions:** The paper assumes that SQL execution tasks can be easily generated in vast quantities with high quality, but does not address potential issues with SQL query diversity or the representativeness of synthesized queries for real-world table reasoning scenarios.

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** The claim that symbolic tasks improve zero-shot generalization on table reasoning tasks is supported by strong empirical evidence across multiple benchmarks (WTQ, WikiSQL-Weak, SQA).
- **Medium confidence:** The claim that symbolic tasks do not hurt general task performance is supported by experimental results on BBH and MMLU, but the evaluation scope is limited.
- **Low confidence:** The mechanism by which symbolic tasks transfer to natural language reasoning is not fully explained, and the exact nature of this transfer is not demonstrated or quantified.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on symbolic task diversity:** Systematically vary the types and quantities of symbolic tasks (e.g., SQL execution, logical reasoning, mathematical expressions) to determine which symbolic domains contribute most to zero-shot generalization improvements.

2. **Cross-domain transfer evaluation:** Test the zero-shot performance of models fine-tuned with symbolic tasks on benchmarks outside of table reasoning and mathematical reasoning, such as logical inference, program synthesis, or scientific reasoning tasks.

3. **Comparison with fine-tuning baselines:** Implement and evaluate fine-tuning approaches for table reasoning tasks to determine whether the improvements from symbolic task instruction tuning exceed what can be achieved through traditional fine-tuning methods.
