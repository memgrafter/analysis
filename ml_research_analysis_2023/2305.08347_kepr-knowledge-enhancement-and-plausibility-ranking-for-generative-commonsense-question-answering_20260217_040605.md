# KEPR: Knowledge Enhancement and Plausibility Ranking for Generative Commonsense Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08347
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08347
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: KEPR achieves an Inc@3 score of 60.91% on the ProtoQA dataset, outperforming existing GenCQA models

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces KEPR, a novel approach for generative commonsense question answering that addresses the dual challenges of generating diverse answers and properly ranking them. KEPR enhances the generative model by incorporating commonsense knowledge into the question encoding, enabling more diverse answer generation. It also employs an ELECTRA-based ranking model to distinguish positive answers from negative ones, improving plausibility estimation and ranking. The method is evaluated on the ProtoQA benchmark, demonstrating substantial improvements over strong baselines, particularly when using T5 as the base generative model.

## Method Summary
KEPR follows a Generate-Then-Rank pipeline architecture, combining knowledge enhancement and plausibility ranking to improve generative commonsense question answering. The approach enhances the generative model by incorporating commonsense knowledge of keywords into the encoding of questions, enabling the generation of diverse answers. Additionally, it develops an ELECTRA-based answer ranking model that learns to distinguish positive answers from negative ones, enhancing the probabilistic estimation of plausibility and the plausibility-based ranking. The method utilizes statistical keyword extraction, dense passage retrieval, and pattern-based question rewriting to achieve its objectives.

## Key Results
- KEPR achieves the best performance on the ProtoQA leaderboard with an Inc@3 score of 60.91%
- The T5-based GenCQA with KEPR outperforms existing GenCQA models on the ProtoQA dataset
- Substantial improvements are observed compared to strong baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
KEPR works by enhancing the generative model with commonsense knowledge and improving the plausibility ranking of generated answers. The knowledge enhancement allows the model to generate more diverse answers by incorporating relevant commonsense knowledge into the question encoding. The ELECTRA-based ranking model then learns to distinguish positive answers from negative ones, improving the plausibility estimation and ranking of the generated answers.

Failure Signatures: Potential failure modes include:
- Incorrect or irrelevant keyword extraction leading to poor knowledge incorporation
- Inefficient dense passage retrieval resulting in missing relevant knowledge
- Ineffective pattern-based question rewriting causing suboptimal answer generation

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on statistical keyword extraction and dense passage retrieval may not consistently capture nuanced commonsense knowledge across diverse domains
- Pattern-based question rewriting mechanism may face challenges when applied to other commonsense QA benchmarks with different question structures
- ELECTRA-based ranking model's performance could be sensitive to the quality and diversity of negative samples used during training, potentially limiting its generalizability

## Confidence
- High confidence in the effectiveness of knowledge enhancement for improving answer diversity
- Medium confidence in the ELECTRA-based ranking model's ability to enhance plausibility estimation
- Low confidence in the scalability of KEPR to other commonsense QA benchmarks beyond ProtoQA

## Next Checks
