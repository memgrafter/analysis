# Reducing ANN-SNN Conversion Error through Residual Membrane Potential

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02091
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02091
- Reference count: 13
- Top-1 accuracy of 64.32% on ImageNet with 10 time-steps

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of unevenness error in ANN-SNN conversion by analyzing residual membrane potential. The authors identify that the most significant source of conversion error occurs when the ANN output is zero but the SNN output is greater than zero. They propose a novel optimization strategy based on residual membrane potential analysis that can reduce this specific type of error without requiring extensive retraining. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet datasets, demonstrating significant improvements particularly under low latency conditions.

## Method Summary
The method involves training a source ANN with a quantization clip-floor-shift (QCFS) activation function to eliminate clipping and quantization errors, then converting it to an SNN using the "reset-by-subtraction" mechanism. During inference, a two-stage strategy is employed where residual membrane potential is first read out at time τ to identify and suppress neurons likely to cause unevenness error, followed by normal inference with the suppressed neurons. The approach specifically targets the case where ANN output is zero but SNN output is greater than zero, which the authors prove accounts for the largest percentage of conversion errors.

## Key Results
- Achieves 64.32% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with only 10 time-steps
- State-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet datasets
- Method can be generalized to other ANN-SNN conversion methods and significantly improves their performance under low latency
- Successfully reduces unevenness error by analyzing and utilizing residual membrane potential

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Unevenness error is dominated by the case where ANN output is zero but SNN output is larger than zero.
- Mechanism: By analyzing residual membrane potential, the method can identify when this specific error case occurs and suppress the spike firing to correct it.
- Core assumption: The residual membrane potential (vl(T)) provides sufficient information to distinguish between the different cases of unevenness error.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We point out that the case of the ANN output being zero while the SNN output being larger than zero accounts for the largest percentage."
  - [section] "Observation 1... Case 1 (al = 0,φl(T ) > al) plays a more important role among four kinds of errors, which has a significant impact in almost every layer."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus contains related papers on ANN-SNN conversion but none specifically discuss residual membrane potential or this particular case analysis.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The quantization clip-floor-shift (QCFS) activation function eliminates clipping and quantization errors, leaving only unevenness error to be addressed.
- Mechanism: By replacing ReLU with QCFS in the source ANN, the method ensures that any error after conversion is due solely to unevenness error, making the residual membrane potential analysis valid.
- Core assumption: QCFS function accurately approximates the activation behavior of SNNs while eliminating other error sources.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Thus, the error (Errorl) here is caused by unevenness error" and "we can use the quantization clip-floor-shift (QCFS) function... to eliminate clipping and quantization errors."
  - [appendix] Detailed proof showing QCFS eliminates both clipping and quantization errors.
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus contains papers on ANN-SNN conversion but none specifically discuss QCFS or its error-elimination properties.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The two-stage inference strategy using residual membrane potential effectively reduces unevenness error without requiring extensive retraining.
- Mechanism: First stage reads residual membrane potential to identify and suppress neurons likely to fire spurious spikes; second stage runs normal inference with the suppressed neurons.
- Core assumption: A single time-step τ is sufficient to estimate residual membrane potential and make reliable suppression decisions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In the inference stage, we use a two-stage strategy" and "For the first stage, we readout residual membrane potential vl(τ )"
  - [section] "We train a source ANN on CIFAR-100/ImageNet dataset and then convert it to SNNs with different τ. The performance... generally improves as τ increases."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus - papers focus on conversion methods but not on this specific two-stage inference approach.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal residual membrane potential readout time τ for different network architectures and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that there is a trade-off between performance and latency with different τ values, and that τ should be nearly equal to the quantization step L for optimal performance, but doesn't specify exact values for different architectures.
- Why unresolved: The optimal τ likely depends on factors like network depth, dataset complexity, and the specific quantization step used in the source ANN. The paper only provides general recommendations for CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet without detailed analysis of why these values are optimal.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying τ across different network architectures (e.g., ResNet-18, ResNet-34, VGG variants) and datasets, with analysis of how τ affects performance, latency, and the distribution of unevenness error in each case.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the SRP method be extended to address unevenness error in the case where ANN output is non-zero but SNN output is larger than ANN output?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on reducing unevenness error when ANN output is zero but SNN output is larger, but mentions this case only accounts for a smaller percentage of total errors. The method is specifically designed for Case 1 of unevenness error.
- Why unresolved: The mathematical framework for Case 1 may not directly apply to Case 2 where ANN output is non-zero. The residual membrane potential analysis would need to be extended to handle this different error scenario.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing a modified SRP method that addresses Case 2 errors, with experimental results showing improved performance on datasets where Case 2 errors are more prevalent.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the SRP method perform on temporal/spatiotemporal datasets compared to static image datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] All experiments are conducted on static image datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet). The paper doesn't mention or test temporal data.
- Why unresolved: Spiking neural networks are particularly suited for temporal data processing, and the residual membrane potential mechanism might have different effects on temporal versus static data. The current analysis doesn't capture temporal dependencies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing SRP on temporal datasets like neuromorphic vision datasets (N-MNIST, DVS-Gesture) or sequential data, comparing performance with static datasets, and analyzing whether the residual membrane potential mechanism provides additional benefits for temporal data.

## Limitations
- The paper's claims about QCFS eliminating all clipping and quantization errors are not independently verified in the corpus.
- The specific mechanism by which residual membrane potential at τ predicts and corrects unevenness error for all four cases is only demonstrated for Case 1.
- The two-stage inference strategy's effectiveness depends critically on choosing an appropriate τ value, but the paper doesn't provide systematic guidance for selecting τ across different architectures or datasets.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The observation that Case 1 (ANN output zero, SNN output >0) dominates unevenness error is well-supported by the data analysis presented.
- **Medium Confidence**: The QCFS activation function successfully eliminates clipping and quantization errors, based on the theoretical proof provided.
- **Medium Confidence**: The residual membrane potential provides sufficient information to suppress neurons causing Case 1 errors during inference.
- **Low Confidence**: The method's generalization to other ANN-SNN conversion techniques without modification is demonstrated but not extensively validated.
