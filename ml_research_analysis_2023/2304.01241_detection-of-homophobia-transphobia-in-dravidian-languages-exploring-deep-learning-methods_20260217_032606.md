# Detection of Homophobia & Transphobia in Dravidian Languages: Exploring Deep Learning Methods

- **arXiv ID**: 2304.01241

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the detection of homophobia and transphobia in social media comments written in Malayalam and Tamil, two low-resource Dravidian languages. The authors implement and compare four deep learning models: CNN, LSTM with GloVe embeddings, multilingual BERT (mBERT), and IndicBERT. The models are evaluated on a dataset from the DravidianLangTech challenge containing 3,114 Malayalam and 2,662 Tamil comments labeled as homophobic, transphobic, or non-anti-LGBT+ content. IndicBERT demonstrates superior performance with weighted average F1-scores of 0.86 for Malayalam and 0.77 for Tamil, establishing its effectiveness for detecting offensive content in these languages and suggesting potential applications for other Indian languages.

## Method Summary
The study implements four deep learning architectures for classifying social media comments into homophobic, transphobic, or non-anti-LGBT+ categories. Text preprocessing involves removing punctuation, numbers, URLs, and extra spaces. CNN and LSTM models use GloVe embeddings, while mBERT and IndicBERT leverage pre-trained multilingual transformer models. All models are trained for multiple epochs (100 for CNN/LSTM, 5 for transformers) and evaluated using weighted average F1-score. The dataset is sourced from the DravidianLangTech challenge, providing labeled comments in both Malayalam and Tamil languages.

## Key Results
- IndicBERT achieves highest weighted average F1-scores: 0.86 for Malayalam and 0.77 for Tamil
- mBERT follows as second-best performer for both languages
- CNN and LSTM models show lower performance compared to transformer-based approaches
- Transformer models demonstrate significant advantages for this task in low-resource Dravidian languages
