# Towards Practical Preferential Bayesian Optimization with Skew Gaussian Processes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01513
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01513
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Hallucination Believer method achieves competitive sample efficiency with MCMC while being significantly faster for preferential Bayesian optimization

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of preferential Bayesian optimization when only pairwise comparisons are available. The core difficulty lies in modeling the posterior distribution using skew Gaussian processes, which is computationally intractable. The authors systematically evaluate existing approaches - showing Gaussian approximations fail for joint predictions while MCMC methods are accurate but slow. They then propose improved Gibbs sampling and derive low-variance Monte Carlo estimators. The key contribution is the hallucination believer (HB) method, which conditions on random samples from the posterior to enable fast and accurate optimization. Experiments on 12 benchmark functions demonstrate that HB-based acquisition functions outperform Gaussian approximations and match MCMC's sample efficiency while being significantly faster.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a practical solution to preferential Bayesian optimization with skew Gaussian processes by introducing the hallucination believer (HB) method. This approach conditions the acquisition function on a random sample (hallucination) drawn from the intractable posterior distribution. The authors first establish that Gaussian approximations fail to correctly predict duel outcome probabilities, then improve MCMC efficiency using Gibbs sampling (achieving 3× speedup over LinESS). They also derive low-variance Monte Carlo estimators for better posterior sampling. The HB method combines these improvements to enable fast and accurate optimization by effectively approximating the intractable posterior through conditioning on sampled values.

## Key Results
- Gaussian approximation fails to correctly predict probability of duel outcomes
- Gibbs sampling achieves 3× speedup over LinESS MCMC variant
- HB-EI/UCB outperforms Gaussian approximation methods
- HB achieves competitive sample efficiency with MCMC while being much faster
- Experiments conducted on 12 benchmark functions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that preferential Bayesian optimization with skew Gaussian processes requires handling an intractable posterior distribution. Gaussian approximations, while computationally efficient, fail to capture the true posterior behavior for joint predictions of duel outcomes. MCMC methods can accurately sample from the true posterior but are computationally expensive. The hallucination believer method works by effectively approximating the intractable posterior through conditioning on random samples (hallucinations) drawn from it. This approach maintains the accuracy benefits of MCMC while achieving the computational efficiency needed for practical optimization.
