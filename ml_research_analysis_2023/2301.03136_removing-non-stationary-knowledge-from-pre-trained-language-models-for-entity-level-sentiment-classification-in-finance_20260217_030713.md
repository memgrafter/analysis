# Removing Non-Stationary Knowledge From Pre-Trained Language Models for Entity-Level Sentiment Classification in Finance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03136
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03136
- Reference count: 38
- Key outcome: Achieves 22.63% improvement in Korean finance-specific ASC accuracy using TGT-Masking and intermediate transfer learning

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses aspect-level sentiment classification (ASC) in finance for low-resource languages, particularly Korean. The authors identify "non-stationary knowledge" as a key issue, where financial entities' sentiment associations become outdated over time, causing pre-trained language models to encode unreliable entity-specific information. They propose TGT-Masking, a novel masking technique that replaces target entities with a special token to prevent models from memorizing outdated sentiment associations. Through intermediate transfer learning experiments using datasets from different languages, domains, and tasks, they achieve significant improvements in classification accuracy on their newly created KorFinASC dataset compared to standalone models.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of two main components: TGT-Masking and intermediate transfer learning. TGT-Masking replaces target entities with a special [TGT] token during both training and inference, forcing the model to rely on contextual sentiment expressions rather than memorized entity-sentiment associations. The intermediate transfer learning approach leverages datasets from different domains, languages, and tasks to improve performance on the target low-resource language task. The authors create a Korean finance-specific ASC dataset (KorFinASC) with 12,613 human-annotated samples and evaluate their approach using multilingual models (XLM-RoBERTa-Large, mT5-Large) across various transfer learning scenarios.

## Key Results
- 22.63% improvement in classification accuracy on KorFinASC dataset compared to standalone models
- TGT-Masking prevents encoding of outdated entity-specific sentiment associations
- Intermediate transfer learning from different languages, domains, and tasks improves low-resource language performance
- Perturbation sensitivity analysis shows models are sensitive to entity changes, validating the non-stationary knowledge problem

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Replacing target entities with [TGT] prevents the model from encoding outdated entity-specific sentiment associations.
- Mechanism: The TGT-Masking technique substitutes financial entities with a special token during both training and inference, forcing the model to rely on contextual sentiment expressions rather than memorized entity-sentiment associations.
- Core assumption: Financial entities' sentiment associations are non-stationary and change over time, making memorized associations unreliable for prediction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We postulate that such behavior origins from 'non-stationary knowledge', once right facts that shift with time."
  - [section]: "We hypothesize that the non-stationarity of financial data is likewise represented in financial corpora, making PLMs trained on such corpora equally non-stationary."
- Break condition: If financial entities' sentiment associations remain stable over time, or if contextual sentiment cues are insufficient for accurate classification.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Intermediate transfer learning improves performance by transferring task-relevant knowledge while reducing domain-specific biases.
- Mechanism: By training on datasets from different domains, languages, or tasks before fine-tuning on the target dataset, the model learns more generalizable patterns that transfer better to the target task.
- Core assumption: Knowledge from related tasks and domains can be effectively transferred to improve performance on the target task, even when source and target differ in domain, language, or task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We discover that datasets of different domains, languages, and tasks can be leveraged to improve classification performance for low-resource languages on finance-specific ASC tasks."
  - [section]: "We find that the nature of finance-specific ASC problems differs significantly from that of general domain tasks requiring additional measures to be properly transferred."
- Break condition: If source and target tasks are too dissimilar for effective knowledge transfer, or if transfer learning introduces more noise than benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Low-resource languages benefit from transfer learning because they can leverage larger datasets from high-resource languages.
- Mechanism: Multilingual models trained on large English datasets can transfer knowledge to Korean through shared linguistic representations and cross-lingual embeddings.
- Core assumption: Multilingual models learn language-agnostic representations that enable effective transfer between languages, particularly from high-resource to low-resource languages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Accordingly, it is necessary for non-English languages to leverage datasets and pre-trained language models (PLM) of different domains, languages, and tasks to best their performance."
  - [section]: "We find that datasets of different domains, languages, and tasks can be leveraged to improve classification performance for low-resource languages on finance-specific ASC tasks."
- Break condition: If the linguistic distance between source and target languages is too large, or if cultural and domain-specific differences outweigh shared representations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do non-stationary knowledge effects vary across different types of financial entities (e.g., stocks vs. commodities vs. currencies)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses non-stationary knowledge affecting financial entities in general but doesn't explore variations across different financial markets or entity types.
- Why unresolved: The experimental setup focuses on stock market entities, leaving open the question of whether non-stationary knowledge manifests differently for other financial instruments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing the TGT-Masking approach across different financial domains (stocks, commodities, forex) with their respective entity types and market dynamics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal frequency for updating or retraining language models to mitigate non-stationary knowledge effects?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "current PLMs, in which training is ceased once served, will become obsolete" but doesn't investigate how frequently retraining should occur.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem of PLMs becoming outdated but doesn't provide guidance on optimal update frequencies for maintaining model relevance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking model performance degradation over time across different financial domains and determining optimal retraining intervals.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does non-stationary knowledge affect language models' ability to generalize to entirely new financial entities not seen during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The perturbation sensitivity analysis shows models perform worse with out-of-sample entities, suggesting generalization limitations.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates sensitivity to perturbations, it doesn't systematically explore how models handle genuinely novel entities that emerge in financial markets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments introducing newly created or emerging financial entities to trained models and measuring classification accuracy and bias patterns compared to established entities.

## Limitations

- Limited quantitative evidence directly demonstrating that non-stationary knowledge is the primary factor affecting model performance
- TGT-Masking mechanism not fully explained with internal model analysis
- Potential negative transfer effects from cross-lingual and cross-domain transfer learning not adequately addressed

## Confidence

**Confidence: Low-Medium** - The core hypothesis that non-stationary knowledge significantly impacts finance-specific ASC performance is theoretically sound but lacks direct empirical validation.

**Confidence: Medium** - The TGT-Masking technique appears effective based on reported performance improvements, but the mechanism is not fully explained.

**Confidence: Medium** - Intermediate transfer learning shows promising results, but potential negative transfer effects are not adequately addressed.
