# Towards Robust FastSpeech 2 by Modelling Residual Multimodality

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01442
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01442
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: TVC-GMM reduces over-smoothness in FastSpeech 2 predictions, improving perceptual audio quality on expressive speech datasets.

## Executive Summary
FastSpeech 2 audio quality degrades on expressive speech datasets due to over-smooth spectrogram predictions induced by mean-squared-error loss. The authors propose TVC-GMM, a mixture model of Trivariate-Chain Gaussian distributions, to model residual multimodality in the spectrogram distribution. TVC-GMM reduces spectrogram smoothness and improves perceptual audio quality compared to FastSpeech 2, as shown by lower Perceptual Audio Distance (CDPAM) and higher Mean Opinion Scores (MOS) on LJSpeech, VCTK, and LibriTTS datasets. Conditional sampling further reduces sampling noise. Training time and inference speed remain competitive.

## Method Summary
The method replaces FastSpeech 2's MSE loss with a negative log-likelihood loss over a Trivariate-Chain Gaussian Mixture Model (TVC-GMM). For each spectrogram bin, the model predicts parameters for K trivariate Gaussian distributions over the bin and its two immediate neighbors (right and below). This captures local statistical dependencies and allows multiple modes to represent the conditional distribution after pitch, energy, and phoneme conditioning. Training optimizes the NLL loss against ground-truth spectrograms and their shifted versions. At inference, spectrograms are sampled either naively (parallel draws with overlap averaging) or conditionally (iterative sampling conditioned on previous bins) to reduce sampling noise.

## Key Results
- TVC-GMM reduces variance of Laplacian-filtered mel-spectrogram (VarL) by 22% compared to FastSpeech 2 on LJSpeech
- CDPAM scores improve from 2.03 to 1.79 on LJSpeech and from 2.34 to 2.14 on VCTK
- MOS scores increase from 3.58 to 3.74 on LJSpeech and from 3.40 to 3.57 on VCTK
- Conditional sampling further improves CDPAM to 1.76 on LJSpeech and MOS to 3.76
- Training requires 40k steps on NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti, with inference speed competitive to FastSpeech 2

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Over-smoothing occurs when MSE loss forces the model to predict conditional averages in multimodal distributions.
- Mechanism: FastSpeech 2's MSE loss assumes each spectrogram bin follows a unimodal Gaussian. When the true conditional distribution is multimodal, the learned mean does not match any natural sample, producing over-smooth predictions that degrade vocoder reconstruction.
- Core assumption: The residual distribution after conditioning (pitch, energy, phoneme) is still multimodal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "With MSE loss FastSpeech 2 is limited to learn conditional averages of the training distribution, which might not lie close to a natural sample if the distribution still appears multimodal after all conditioning signals."
  - [section 2] "FastSpeech 2 deploys MSE loss, which optimises training data likelihood only under an assumed gaussian distribution [16] and thus relies on conditioning on external control signals to simplify the complex speech distribution."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus; this is the paper's core claim.
- Break condition: If conditioning fully removes multimodality, then MSE loss would suffice and TVC-GMM adds no benefit.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: TVC-GMM reduces over-smoothing by modeling local spectrogram dependencies and multimodality.
- Mechanism: TVC-GMM replaces each spectrogram bin prediction with a mixture model over the bin and its two neighbors. This captures local correlations and allows multiple modes, producing less smooth predictions that better match natural samples.
- Core assumption: Local dependencies in the spectrogram are trivariate (one bin + right neighbor + below neighbor).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we introduce TVC-GMM, a mixture model of Trivariate-Chain Gaussian distributions, to model the residual multimodality."
  - [section 3.2] "We increase the flexibility of ˜Y by replacing ˜Yt,f with a mixture model... to further address residual multimodality."
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence; mechanism is novel in this paper.
- Break condition: If the true spectrogram distribution has no local dependencies, or dependencies extend beyond the immediate neighbors, the trivariate assumption fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Conditional sampling reduces sampling noise while preserving reduced smoothness.
- Mechanism: Instead of sampling each trivariate triplet independently (naive sampling), conditional sampling fixes the known value from the previous bin and samples only the new dimensions, ensuring consistency across overlapping triplets and reducing jagged artifacts.
- Core assumption: Sampling noise from independent triplet draws introduces over-sharpness that harms perceptual quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "conditional sampling uses an iterative algorithm which conditions each ˜Yt,f on the values drawn in previous bins... to further increase consistency and reduce sampling noise."
  - [abstract] "Conditional sampling further reduces sampling noise."
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence; this is the paper's novel sampling method.
- Break condition: If the trivariate Gaussian chains are already perfectly consistent, conditioning adds no benefit and only increases computation.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multimodal distribution modeling
  - Why needed here: FastSpeech 2's MSE loss assumes unimodal Gaussians, but natural speech has multiple valid realizations for the same conditioning, causing over-smoothness.
  - Quick check question: What happens to the MSE loss optimum when the target distribution is bimodal?

- Concept: Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM)
  - Why needed here: GMMs can represent multimodal distributions by mixing several Gaussians, allowing TVC-GMM to capture the residual multimodality after conditioning.
  - Quick check question: How does increasing the number of mixture components affect the model's flexibility?

- Concept: Local statistical dependencies in time-frequency data
  - Why needed here: Speech spectrograms have strong local correlations; modeling only marginal distributions ignores this and produces artifacts.
  - Quick check question: Why might adjacent spectrogram bins be correlated?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input conditioning (phonemes, pitch, energy) -> FastSpeech 2 layers -> trivariate-chain GMM layer -> NLL loss
- Critical path:
  1. Input conditioning (phonemes, pitch, energy) → FastSpeech 2 layers → trivariate-chain GMM layer → NLL loss
  2. Inference: same path, then sample from GMMs
- Design tradeoffs:
  - K=1 vs K=5: more modes → better expressiveness but more parameters and sampling cost
  - Naive vs conditional sampling: speed vs reduced sampling noise
  - Trivariate vs full spectrogram: tractability vs completeness
- Failure signatures:
  - Over-smoothness persists → conditioning insufficient or K too low
  - Over-sharp artifacts → sampling noise from naive sampling
  - Training instability → covariance regularization or numerical issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replace FastSpeech 2's MSE loss with NLL loss using K=1 trivariate Gaussians; compare VarL and CDPAM.
  2. Increase K to 5; compare naive vs conditional sampling on LJSpeech.
  3. Evaluate on expressive dataset (VCTK); measure degradation vs LJSpeech baseline.

## Limitations
- The trivariate-chain assumption may oversimplify local spectrogram dependencies, potentially limiting expressiveness for complex speech patterns
- Empirical validation of residual multimodality assumption is limited to indirect evidence through improved metrics
- Computational cost increases with number of mixture components and conditional sampling complexity

## Confidence
- High confidence: FastSpeech 2's MSE loss produces over-smooth spectrograms that degrade audio quality on expressive datasets
- Medium confidence: TVC-GMM effectively reduces over-smoothness by modeling trivariate local dependencies
- Medium confidence: Conditional sampling reduces sampling noise compared to naive sampling
