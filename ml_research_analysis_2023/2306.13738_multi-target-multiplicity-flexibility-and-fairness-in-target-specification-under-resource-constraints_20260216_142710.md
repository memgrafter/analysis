# Multi-Target Multiplicity: Flexibility and Fairness in Target Specification under Resource Constraints

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13738
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13738
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces multi-target multiplicity framework for assessing flexibility and fairness when choosing target variables in predictive allocation under resource constraints.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces multi-target multiplicity, a framework for assessing the flexibility in individual outcomes and group fairness that stems from uncertainty about which target variable to predict in predictive allocation tasks. The authors refine existing notions of predictive multiplicity to account for resource constraints, introducing new measures (top-κ ambiguity) and computational methods (mixed-integer programming). Applying their framework to a healthcare dataset, they show that the level of multiplicity stemming from target variable choice can be greater than that stemming from nearly-optimal models of a single target, and demonstrate how index models can improve fairness by re-weighting predictions across targets.

## Method Summary
The framework extends predictive multiplicity to settings with multiple target variables and resource constraints. Key innovations include the top-κ ambiguity measure, which quantifies the maximum number of individuals who could receive different allocation decisions across optimal models for different targets while respecting resource limits. The authors develop mixed-integer programming formulations to compute this measure and introduce index models that combine predictions across targets with fairness-aware weights. The approach is validated on healthcare data, demonstrating that target choice can induce more outcome variation than model selection within a single target.

## Key Results
- Multi-target multiplicity can exceed the multiplicity from model variation within a single target
- Index models with fairness-aware weights can improve group fairness compared to single-target approaches
- Top-κ ambiguity provides a concrete measure of flexibility in allocation decisions under resource constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by recognizing that when multiple target variables are available, the optimal allocation can vary significantly depending on which target is chosen. This creates a form of multiplicity distinct from traditional model multiplicity. By incorporating resource constraints through the top-κ measure, the framework captures the practical limitations on how much these allocations can differ. The index model approach leverages this by creating weighted combinations of predictions that can balance competing objectives across targets.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Target variables (T) -> Prediction models (M) -> Resource constraints (κ) -> Allocation decisions (A) -> Fairness metrics (F)

**Critical path**: T → M → κ → A → F (from target specification through fairness evaluation)

**Design tradeoffs**: Discrete target selection vs. continuous weighting; exact optimization vs. approximation; individual fairness vs. group fairness

**Failure signatures**: High multiplicity with low practical impact (overly pessimistic); low multiplicity with high fairness violations (overly constrained); computational intractability for large κ

**First experiments**:
1. Compute top-κ ambiguity for varying κ values on benchmark datasets
2. Compare fairness metrics for index models vs. single-target approaches

## Limitations
- The fixed κ value may not capture dynamic allocation scenarios where resources vary over time
- The assumption of discrete target variables limits applicability to continuous trade-off scenarios
- The framework assumes optimal models can be found for each target, which may not hold for complex, noisy datasets

## Confidence
**High** confidence in theoretical foundations and mathematical definitions

**High** confidence in empirical findings showing greater target-induced multiplicity
