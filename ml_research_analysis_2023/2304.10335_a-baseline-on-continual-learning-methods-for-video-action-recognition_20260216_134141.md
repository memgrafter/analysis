# A baseline on continual learning methods for video action recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10335
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10335
- Reference count: 0
- This paper presents a benchmark of continual learning methods for video action recognition, evaluating state-of-the-art approaches on the UCF-101 dataset using task-incremental and class-incremental learning setups.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a benchmark for continual learning methods in video action recognition, focusing on the UCF-101 dataset. The authors evaluate a range of state-of-the-art approaches under both task-incremental and class-incremental learning settings, using a 30-class subset divided into 15 tasks. They propose two memory-efficient variants for rehearsal methods: confidence-driven rehearsal, which prioritizes storing high-confidence samples, and information-driven downsampling, which uses optical flow to select informative frames. Results show that rehearsal methods, particularly DER++ and DER, significantly outperform other paradigms, and the proposed memory-efficient variants improve performance, especially when reducing buffer size.

## Method Summary
The authors benchmark continual learning methods for video action recognition using the UCF-101 dataset, specifically a subset of 30 classes divided into 15 tasks (2 classes per task). They evaluate both task-incremental learning (T-IL) and class-incremental learning (C-IL) settings, using an R(2+1)D backbone trained from scratch for 80 epochs per task with RMSProp. Two memory-efficient variants are proposed: confidence-driven rehearsal (CDR), which buffers samples based on model confidence exceeding a threshold δ, and information-driven downsampling (IDD), which uses optical flow magnitude to select informative frames. The methods are compared using classification accuracy and buffer size as key metrics.

## Key Results
- Rehearsal methods, particularly DER++ and DER, significantly outperform other continual learning paradigms in video action recognition.
- The confidence-driven rehearsal variant is more effective than information-driven downsampling in retaining performance with a smaller buffer.
- Memory-efficient variants improve performance, especially when reducing buffer size from 200 to 100 samples.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Confidence-driven rehearsal improves continual learning by prioritizing storage of samples the model classifies with high confidence.
- Mechanism: For each training sample, the model computes prediction confidence for the true class. If confidence exceeds threshold δ, the sample is added to the replay buffer. This biases the buffer toward high-confidence, high-informativeness samples, reducing buffer size while maintaining performance.
- Core assumption: High-confidence samples capture the most discriminative modes of the class distribution and generalize well to future tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "conﬁdence-driven rehearsal, which selects samples based on model confidence"
  - [section] "a sample (x, y) is eligible for buffering if the model’s prediction for class y is above a threshold δ"
  - [corpus] weak/absent; no related papers explicitly discuss confidence-based sample selection in rehearsal
- Break condition: If δ is set too high, the buffer may miss under-represented class modes, causing poor generalization on rare or ambiguous samples.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Information-driven downsampling improves memory efficiency by selecting only the most informative frames from video sequences using optical flow magnitude.
- Mechanism: For each video, compute optical flow between consecutive frames. Keep only frames whose motion magnitude (L2 norm) is highest, preserving informative motion while reducing total frame count.
- Core assumption: High optical flow magnitude corresponds to visually informative frames; low-magnitude frames are redundant.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "information-driven downsampling, which uses optical flow to select informative frames"
  - [section] "we propose to employ the norm of optical ﬂow [...] as a measure of importance"
  - [corpus] weak/absent; no related papers discuss optical-flow-based frame selection for continual learning
- Break condition: In videos with uniform motion or static scenes, optical flow may fail to distinguish informative from uninformative frames, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knowledge distillation from buffered samples (as in DER/DER++) mitigates catastrophic forgetting more effectively than rehearsal alone.
- Mechanism: During training on new task Ti, the loss includes both cross-entropy on Ti and KL-divergence between logits of the current model and a frozen copy of the model trained on Ti-1, applied to buffered samples.
- Core assumption: Buffered samples from previous tasks provide stable targets for distillation, preserving old-task knowledge while learning new tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "rehearsal methods, particularly DER++ and DER, significantly outperform other paradigms"
  - [section] "DER/DER++ [13] regularize a cross-entropy loss by enforcing logit similarity of buffered past samples"
  - [corpus] weak/absent; related papers discuss rehearsal and distillation separately but not their combination for video
- Break condition: If the buffer is too small or unrepresentative, distillation targets become noisy, harming performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - R(2+1)D backbone (video encoder)
  - Classifier head (task-specific)
  - Buffer storage (experience replay)
  - Confidence scorer (thresholded by δ)
  - Optical flow module (frame scoring)
- Critical path:
  1. Forward pass through R(2+1)D
  2. Compute confidence for true class
  3. Store sample if confidence ≥ δ
  4. Apply optical flow if IDD enabled
  5. Compute total loss (XE + distillation)
  6. Backpropagate and update weights
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Larger δ → fewer buffer samples → more memory saved but risk of under-representation
  - Higher frame sampling rate → better motion capture but more storage
  - Distillation weight λ → controls trade-off between new-task learning and old-task retention
- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy collapse on old tasks → buffer too small or δ too high
  - Training instability → learning rate too high or λ mis-tuned
  - Optical flow errors → incorrect frame selection, especially in low-texture scenes
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train DER++ with buffer=200, δ=0.8; measure accuracy vs baseline.
  2. Reduce buffer to 100, adjust δ to maintain accuracy; record memory savings.
  3. Enable IDD on DER++; compare frame retention rate and accuracy to baseline.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the long-term effects of using the proposed confidence-driven rehearsal (CDR) and information-driven downsampling (IDD) variants on model performance and catastrophic forgetting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces CDR and IDD as memory-efficient variants to reduce buffer size while preserving classification accuracy. It shows that CDR is more effective than IDD in retaining performance with a smaller buffer.
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents short-term results from experiments with a fixed sequence of 5 tasks. It does not explore the impact of these variants on long-term continual learning scenarios with many more tasks or in a lifelong learning setting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Long-term experiments tracking model performance over many more tasks or in a lifelong learning setting, comparing the effects of CDR and IDD on catastrophic forgetting and overall accuracy.

## Limitations
- The exact list of 30 selected UCF-101 classes is not provided, making it difficult to reproduce the exact benchmark setup.
- The task sequence generation procedure is not fully specified, which could affect the reproducibility of results.
- The paper does not explore the long-term effects of the proposed memory-efficient variants on model performance and catastrophic forgetting.
