# On the Interventional Kullback-Leibler Divergence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05380
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05380
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: Introduces IKL divergence to quantify differences between causal models using multi-environment interventional data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Interventional Kullback-Leibler (IKL) divergence as a principled way to quantify differences between causal models using multi-environment interventional data. The IKL divergence extends the classical KL divergence by averaging local divergences between model estimates and interventional distributions. Under certain conditions on intervention targets, the authors establish that IKL=0 if and only if the causal models are identical, providing operational guarantees for model evaluation across environments.

## Method Summary
The IKL divergence is computed by averaging local divergences between model estimates and interventional distributions across environments. It operates on multi-environment distributions where intervention targets are known, requiring that each mechanism remains unchanged in at least one environment. The method can handle partial observability by restricting the divergence to observed variable subsets, and provides operational guarantees that bounded IKL implies similar statistics across environments for most bounded functions.

## Key Results
- IKL divergence extends classical KL divergence to handle interventional distributions
- Under conditions where each mechanism remains unchanged in at least one environment, IKL=0 iff models are identical
- Provides operational guarantees: bounded IKL implies similar statistics across environments for most functions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The IKL divergence identifies structural differences between causal models when intervention conditions are met.
- Mechanism: Averages local divergences between model estimates and interventional distributions. Under conditions where each mechanism remains unchanged in at least one environment, IKL=0 iff models are identical.
- Core assumption: True causal structure GP is known or intervention targets satisfy specific conditions (unblocked paths exist in GQ).
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] IKL extends classical KL by averaging local divergences; [section 3.4] Theorem establishing equivalence between DIKL(PE∥Q)=0 and model equality.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: IKL can identify partial structural differences with limited observational information.
- Mechanism: Restricting IKL to observed variable subsets and known edge conditions verifies edge orientations.
- Core assumption: Partial observability available and Theorem 19 conditions satisfied for edge subset.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.4] Corollary 20 on partial structural differences; [section 3.3] Allowing marginal information for subsets.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: IKL provides operational guarantees for model evaluation across environments.
- Mechanism: Bounded IKL ensures similar statistics when executing mechanism changes in "Nature" and model Q.
- Core assumption: Function f mapping to [-B,B] is bounded and IKL is small.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4] Theorem 22 on concentration bounds for bounded functions.

- Concept: Markov factorization property
  - Why needed here: IKL relies on decomposing joint distributions into conditional distributions based on causal graph structure.
  - Quick check question: Given P(X1,X2,X3) factorizing as P(X1)·P(X2|X1)·P(X3|X1), what are parent sets for each variable in DAG?

- Concept: Conditional independence and d-separation
  - Why needed here: Understanding conditional independence is crucial for identifying structural differences between causal models.
  - Quick check question: If X1 and X3 are d-separated by X2 in a DAG, what does this imply about their conditional independence?

- Concept: Interventional distributions and mechanism shifts
  - Why needed here: IKL quantifies differences based on how models respond to interventions changing causal mechanisms.
  - Quick check question: How does changing P(X1|X2) to P̃(X1|X2) while other mechanisms remain constant affect interventional distribution P̃(X)?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Multi-environment data -> Causal graphical models (P,GP) and estimates (Q,GQ) -> IKL divergence computation -> Theorem-based verification
- Critical path: Collect multi-environment data → Compute IKL divergence → Apply Theorem 19 or Corollary 20 → Verify structural differences
- Design tradeoffs: Computational complexity vs. precision (exponential growth with variables); intervention requirements (more interventions better identification but costly); partial observability (reduces burden but may miss differences)
- Failure signatures: DIKL(PE∥Q) > 0 despite observational similarity (structural differences not captured); restricted IKL=0 but models differ (insufficient intervention information); large computational overhead (need efficient implementation)
- First 3 experiments: 1) Verify IKL on synthetic data with known ground truth; 2) Test restricted IKL on partially observable data; 3) Evaluate operational significance with bounded IKL

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what conditions can IKL distinguish Markov equivalent but not causally equivalent models?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Discusses challenge of distinguishing Markov equivalent models in Section 3.4.
- Why unresolved: Paper establishes conditions for identifying differences under partial observability but doesn't fully characterize when IKL can distinguish all Markov equivalent models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Complete characterization of intervention target sets guaranteeing IKL distinguishes all non-equivalent causal models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does choice of intervention targets affect sample complexity for accurate causal model identification using IKL?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Discusses relationship between intervention targets and identifiability in Theorem 19 and Corollary 20.
- Why unresolved: Paper establishes theoretical conditions for identifiability but provides no concrete bounds on number of interventions needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical/theoretical bounds on minimum number of intervention environments required as function of graph properties.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can IKL be efficiently estimated from finite samples in high-dimensional settings?
- Basis in paper: Inferred - Paper introduces IKL theoretically but doesn't address practical estimation challenges in high dimensions.
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on theoretical properties, not computational/statistical challenges in estimating IKL from finite samples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing estimation approaches and performance as function of sample size and dimensionality.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does IKL behave under violations of pseudo causal sufficiency assumption?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Makes Assumption 7 explicit but doesn't explore its necessity or violation consequences.
- Why unresolved: Paper establishes theoretical results under Assumption 7 but doesn't investigate robustness to hidden confounding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical/empirical analysis showing how hidden confounders affect IKL estimation and identifiability.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees require specific intervention conditions that may be difficult to verify in practice
- Computational complexity grows exponentially with number of variables due to parent distribution expectations
- Assumes intervention targets are known, which is often not the case in real-world scenarios

## Confidence
- High confidence: Mathematical formulation and relationship to classical KL divergence
- Medium confidence: Theoretical guarantees under stated conditions (practical applicability depends on verifiable intervention requirements)
- Medium confidence: Operational interpretation (theoretically valid but may be conservative due to concentration bounds)

## Next Checks
1. **Intervention Condition Verification**: Implement systematic procedure to verify whether intervention targets satisfy Theorem 19 conditions for practical datasets, including metrics for assessing when mechanisms are "sufficiently unchanged"
2. **Computational Efficiency**: Develop and benchmark approximation methods for computing IKL divergence that scale to larger causal graphs, focusing on Monte Carlo methods for estimating parent distribution expectations
3. **Partial Observability Robustness**: Design experiments to test sensitivity of restricted IKL divergence to missing intervention information, quantifying how many edge orientations can be reliably identified with subset of intervention targets
