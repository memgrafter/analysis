# Unsupervised Paraphrasing of Multiword Expressions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01443
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01443
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: Unsupervised MWE paraphrasing approach achieves 65.31 Spearman correlation on idiomatic STS and 86.91 on general STS

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an unsupervised approach for paraphrasing multiword expressions (MWEs) in context using only monolingual corpus data and pre-trained language models. The method clusters sentences containing target MWEs, generates paraphrase candidates for each cluster, re-ranks them based on outer probability, and retrieves the most relevant paraphrase for the target context. The approach demonstrates strong performance on the SemEval 2022 idiomatic semantic text similarity task, outperforming all unsupervised systems and rivaling supervised approaches.

## Method Summary
The unsupervised paraphrasing system operates by first clustering sentences containing the target MWE, then generating paraphrase candidates for each cluster using pre-trained language models. The candidates are re-ranked based on outer probability metrics, and the most relevant paraphrase is retrieved for the target context. The entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning and operates solely on monolingual corpus data, making it a fully unsupervised approach to MWE paraphrasing.

## Key Results
- Achieves Spearman's rank correlation of 65.31 on MWE data and 86.91 on general STS data
- Outperforms all unsupervised systems on the SemEval 2022 idiomatic semantic text similarity task
- Approaches performance levels of supervised systems while requiring no labeled training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages pre-trained language models to understand contextual relationships between MWEs and their paraphrases. By clustering sentences with similar contexts, the system identifies semantic patterns that guide paraphrase generation. The outer probability re-ranking ensures that generated paraphrases are not only semantically related but also contextually appropriate for the target usage.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multiword Expressions (MWEs)**: Why needed - Understanding the linguistic phenomenon being targeted; Quick check - Can identify verb+noun, nominal, and prepositional MWEs in text
- **Semantic Text Similarity (STS)**: Why needed - The evaluation metric for paraphrase quality; Quick check - Can compute cosine similarity between sentence embeddings
- **Outer Probability**: Why needed - For re-ranking paraphrase candidates based on contextual appropriateness; Quick check - Can calculate marginal probabilities from language model outputs
- **BERT Clustering**: Why needed - To group sentences with similar MWE usage patterns; Quick check - Can implement k-means clustering on sentence embeddings
- **Unsupervised Learning**: Why needed - The paradigm that enables learning without labeled data; Quick check - Can implement clustering and generation without training data
- **Paraphrase Generation**: Why needed - The core task of producing alternative expressions; Quick check - Can generate multiple variations of a given sentence

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: Sentence Clustering -> Paraphrase Generation -> Outer Probability Re-ranking -> Context-aware Retrieval
Critical Path: The clustering step is critical as it determines the semantic space for paraphrase generation. Poor clustering leads to irrelevant paraphrases regardless of generation quality.
Design Tradeoffs: The choice of pre-trained models without fine-tuning prioritizes generalization over task-specific optimization. This enables unsupervised operation but may limit performance on specialized MWE types.
Failure Signatures: System fails when: (1) clustering cannot capture MWE semantics, (2) language model generation produces non-idiomatic paraphrases, (3) outer probability metric cannot distinguish context-appropriate paraphrases
Three First Experiments: (1) Test clustering quality on held-out MWE types, (2) Evaluate paraphrase generation with different language model variants, (3) Measure outer probability effectiveness on contrastive paraphrase pairs

## Limitations
- Performance on diverse MWE types (nominal, prepositional, compound) remains untested
- Method's reliance on BERT may not capture non-compositional MWE semantics effectively
- Clustering methodology may not fully capture semantic nuances of MWE paraphrases

## Confidence
- High: The unsupervised approach achieves competitive performance on evaluated datasets
- Medium: The clustering methodology improves over previous unsupervised systems
- Low: Generalization to diverse MWE types and domains

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate the system on a broader range of MWE types (nominal, prepositional, compound) and domains (clinical, technical, literary)

3. Test the system's robustness by evaluating on datasets with different MWE annotation schemas and varying levels of compositionality
