# A Multilingual Evaluation of NER Robustness to Adversarial Inputs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18933
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18933
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: NER models show significant drops in F1 score when tested on adversarial datasets across English, German, and Hindi, with adversarial fine-tuning and re-training improving robustness.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the robustness of Named Entity Recognition (NER) models to adversarial inputs across three languages (English, German, and Hindi). The authors create adversarial test sets using language-agnostic methods such as entity replacement, context masking, and paraphrasing, and find that NER models are not very robust to small perturbations in the input. The paper then explores two approaches to improve NER robustness: data augmentation (re-training with adversarial data) and adversarial fine-tuning. Both methods improve performance on both original and adversarial test sets, with re-training being significantly better than fine-tuning for German and Hindi.

## Method Summary
The authors generate adversarial test sets for NER datasets using five language-agnostic methods: Random Sampling, Faker gazetteer, Masking, Paraphrasing, and Masking+Random Sampling. They evaluate the robustness of existing NER models (TNER for English, fine-tuned BERT for German and Hindi, plus Stanza and Flair) on the original and adversarial test sets. The models are then fine-tuned or re-trained using 60% of the adversarial data, and their performance is compared on both original and adversarial test sets using micro-F1 score and fine-grained error analysis.

## Key Results
- NER models show significant drops in F1 score when tested on adversarial datasets across all three languages.
- Both adversarial fine-tuning and re-training improve performance on original and adversarial test sets.
- For German and Hindi, re-training is significantly better than fine-tuning, while no significant difference is found for English.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial test sets expose weaknesses in NER models by altering context or entities in ways that fool the model.
- Mechanism: Small perturbations (e.g., masking, random replacement, paraphrasing) break the model's learned associations between tokens and entity labels.
- Core assumption: NER models rely heavily on surface patterns and do not generalize well to unseen variations of entities or contexts.

  3. Fine-tune or re-train the NER models using the adversarial data.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Language coverage vs. depth of analysis (limited to 3 languages)
  - Complexity of adversarial generation methods vs. generalizability (simple, language-agnostic methods)
  - Size of adversarial test sets vs. quality and diversity of examples
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on adversarial test sets indicates model brittleness
  - No significant improvement from fine-tuning or re-training suggests limitations in the adversarial data or training approach
  - Disproportionate impact on certain entity types or languages reveals biases or weaknesses in the model

  1. Generate adversarial test sets for a small subset of the original test data using each of the proposed methods.
  2. Evaluate the performance of a pre-trained NER model on the original and adversarial test sets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of NER models vary across different adversarial generation methods (e.g., masking vs. paraphrasing vs. entity replacement)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares the performance of NER models on various adversarial test sets created using different methods (RS, Faker, Mask, Para, M+R) and notes differences in performance across these methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper observes performance differences but does not deeply investigate the underlying reasons for these differences or provide a comprehensive analysis of which methods are most effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the types of errors introduced by each method and their impact on NER performance, possibly through error categorization or correlation with specific model weaknesses.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different NER model architectures (e.g., transformer-based vs. BiLSTM+CRF) compare in their robustness to adversarial inputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates several NER models (TNER, fine-tuned BERT, Stanza, Flair) but does not directly compare their robustness to adversarial inputs.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses different models for different languages/datasets, making it difficult to draw conclusions about architecture-specific robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing the same set of NER models across multiple languages and adversarial test sets, using consistent evaluation metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How effective are other data augmentation techniques (e.g., synonym replacement, back-translation) in improving NER robustness to adversarial inputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores adversarial fine-tuning and re-training but does not investigate other data augmentation techniques.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a limited set of approaches and does not explore the broader landscape of data augmentation methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the effectiveness of various data augmentation techniques (including those not explored in the paper) in improving NER robustness to adversarial inputs.

## Limitations
- The adversarial methods are not truly language-agnostic, as they vary significantly in implementation across languages, particularly for the paraphrasing method.
- The sample size of the adversarial test sets (10% of original test sets) may be too small to draw robust conclusions about the effectiveness of the two approaches across different languages and entity types.
- The paper does not explore whether the adversarial methods disproportionately affect certain entity types or whether the models are more sensitive to specific types of perturbations.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The claim that NER models are not robust to small perturbations is well-supported by the significant drops in F1 score on adversarial test sets across all three languages and model types.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that adversarial fine-tuning and re-training can improve NER robustness is supported by the experimental results, but the effectiveness varies by language and model performance. The reasons for this variation are not fully explored.
- **Low confidence**: The claim that the adversarial methods are truly language-agnostic is questionable given the different implementations for each language, especially for the paraphrasing method.

## Next Checks
