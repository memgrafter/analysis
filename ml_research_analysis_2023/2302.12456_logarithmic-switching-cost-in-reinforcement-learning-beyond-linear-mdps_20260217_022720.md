# Logarithmic Switching Cost in Reinforcement Learning beyond Linear MDPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12456
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12456
- Reference count: 37
- This paper studies reinforcement learning with low switching cost beyond linear MDPs, proposing an algorithm achieving near-optimal regret with switching cost logarithmic in episodes.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of designing reinforcement learning algorithms that achieve near-optimal regret while maintaining low switching cost - the number of times the policy changes during learning. The authors propose the ELEANOR-LowSwitching algorithm that uses a "doubling trick" to update exploration policies only when information gain doubles for some layer, achieving a switching cost of O(∑H h=1 dh log K) while maintaining near-optimal regret. The approach extends beyond linear MDPs to linear Bellman-complete MDPs with low inherent Bellman error, and further to generalized linear function approximation.

## Method Summary
The ELEANOR-LowSwitching algorithm tracks the determinant of the empirical covariance matrix Σk_h for each layer h. When det(Σk_h) ≥ 2·det(Σh) for any layer h, it triggers a policy update and resets the reference determinant. This ensures each layer's information gain doubles at most O(log K) times across K episodes. The algorithm uses global optimism, computing optimistic Q-value estimates only at the initial state by solving an optimization problem that maximizes φ₁(s₁,a)⊤θ₁ subject to estimation error constraints. This reduces the dimensionality of the covering set compared to fully adaptive methods.

## Key Results
- ELEANOR-LowSwitching achieves switching cost of O(∑H h=1 dh log K) and regret of Õ(∑H h=1 dh √K + ∑H h=1 √dhIK)
- The algorithm extends to generalized linear function approximation with switching cost O(dH log K) and similar regret bounds
- A nearly matching switching cost lower bound of Ω(∑H h=1 dh) is proven for any algorithm with sublinear regret

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Linear Bellman-complete MDPs with low inherent Bellman error
  - Why needed here: This is the primary setting where the algorithm achieves near-optimal regret with logarithmic switching cost. Understanding this generalization beyond linear MDPs is crucial for grasping the algorithm's contribution.
  - Quick check question: What is the inherent Bellman error, and why does it measure the gap between linear function approximation and the true Bellman operator?

- Concept: Information gain measured by determinant of empirical covariance
  - Why needed here: The doubling trick relies on this specific measure to determine when sufficient new information has been collected to warrant a policy update.
  - Quick check question: Why does the determinant of the empirical covariance matrix serve as a valid measure of information gain in this context?

- Concept: Global vs. local optimism in RL
  - Why needed here: The algorithm uses global optimism (optimistic only at initial state) rather than local optimism (optimistic everywhere), which is key to reducing computational complexity while maintaining performance.
  - Quick check question: How does global optimism differ from local optimism in terms of computational complexity and regret bounds?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Information tracking: det(Σk_h) computation for each layer h
  - Policy update trigger: comparison of det(Σk_h) with reference determinants
  - Optimistic planning: optimization problem (Definition 3.1) to compute θ̄h
  - Policy deployment: greedy policy w.r.t. φh(·,·)⊤θ̄kh
  - Data collection: trajectory generation using current policy

- Critical path: Data collection → Information gain computation → Policy update check → Optimistic planning (if triggered) → Policy deployment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Switching cost vs. adaptivity: More frequent updates reduce regret but increase switching cost
  - Computational complexity: Global optimism reduces complexity compared to local optimism but requires solving an optimization problem
  - Function approximation generality: Low inherent Bellman error setting is more general than linear MDP but requires careful handling of model misspecification

- Failure signatures:
  - High regret despite low switching cost: May indicate insufficient optimism or poor feature map choice
  - Frequent policy updates: May suggest information gain measure is too sensitive or feature map is degenerate
  - Optimization infeasibility: May indicate overly conservative confidence bounds or numerical issues

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the algorithm on a simple linear MDP with known feature map and verify logarithmic switching cost empirically
  2. Test the algorithm on a misspecified linear bandit (H=1) to verify the corollary about switching cost and regret
  3. Implement the generalized linear version and test on a logistic MDP to verify the extension works beyond linear models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the switching cost lower bound be improved beyond Ω(dH) for generalized linear function approximation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proves a lower bound of Ω(dH) for switching cost in generalized linear function approximation, but notes this is adapted from linear MDP lower bounds.
- Why unresolved: The authors do not explore whether this bound is tight or if it can be improved for the more general GLM setting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving a matching upper bound of Ω(dH) for any algorithm with sublinear regret would show the bound is tight. Alternatively, finding an algorithm with better switching cost than Ω(dH) would show the bound is not tight.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the switching cost scale with the inherent Bellman error I in the low IBE setting?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The regret bound depends on I, but the switching cost bound O(sum H h=1 d_h log K) does not explicitly depend on I. The lower bound proof assumes I=0.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not analyze how switching cost changes with varying levels of inherent Bellman error, or whether the logarithmic dependence on K remains optimal for nonzero I.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the switching cost as a function of I and proving matching upper and lower bounds would clarify the relationship.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the computational inefficiency of the global optimism optimization be addressed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that the optimization problem in Definition 3.1 is computationally inefficient due to the max operator breaking quadratic structure, and leave improving computation as future work.
- Why unresolved: No concrete proposals or analysis are provided for making the optimization tractable while preserving the theoretical guarantees.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing an efficient approximation or reformulation of the optimization that maintains the same regret and switching cost bounds would resolve this.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the doubling trick extend to more general MDP settings like low Bellman eluder dimension?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that low Bellman eluder dimension and differentiable function approximation are more general than the settings considered, and leave extending low switching cost to these as future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates the doubling trick for low IBE and GLM settings, and does not explore its applicability to other general settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving that the doubling trick can be applied to obtain low switching cost in low Bellman eluder dimension or differentiable function approximation settings would resolve this.

## Limitations

- The algorithm's performance depends on accurate estimation of the inherent Bellman error I, which may be challenging in practice
- The global optimism optimization problem (Definition 3.1) is computationally inefficient, requiring solving a non-convex optimization with max operators
- The analysis assumes well-behaved feature maps and does not address potential numerical instability in computing determinants for high-dimensional matrices

## Confidence

- High confidence: The logarithmic switching cost bound O(∑H h=1 dh log K) - this follows directly from the doubling trick argument which is well-established in the bandit literature
- Medium confidence: The regret bound of Õ(∑H h=1 dh √K + ∑H h=1 √dhIK) - relies on assumptions about feature completeness and accurate estimation of I
- Medium confidence: Extension to generalized linear function approximation - while the mechanism is clear, the analysis depends on additional regularity conditions on the link function
