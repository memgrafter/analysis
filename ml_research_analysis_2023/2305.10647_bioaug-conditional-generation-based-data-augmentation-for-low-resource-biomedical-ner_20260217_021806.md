# BioAug: Conditional Generation based Data Augmentation for Low-Resource Biomedical NER

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10647
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10647
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BioAug achieves 1.5%-21.5% absolute improvement in F1 score over existing data augmentation methods for BioNER on 5 benchmark datasets.

## Executive Summary
BioAug addresses the low-resource challenge in biomedical Named Entity Recognition (BioNER) by generating high-quality augmentations using a transformer-based encoder-decoder model. The method fine-tunes BioBART on a novel text reconstruction task that selectively masks tokens while retaining named entities and keywords, and incorporates biomedical relation triples as external knowledge. During generation, the model conditions on corrupted sentences to produce diverse and factual augmentations that significantly improve BioNER performance.

## Method Summary
BioAug fine-tunes BioBART-large on a text reconstruction task with selective masking and knowledge augmentation. The process involves extracting keywords using SciSpaCy, retrieving biomedical relation triples, and masking all tokens except named entities and a small percentage of keywords. Knowledge triples are concatenated to the corrupted sentence during fine-tuning. Post-training, BioAug generates augmentations by conditioning on corrupted test sentences using top-k random sampling with beam search. The generated sentences are added to training data for the final BioBERT NER model.

## Key Results
- Achieves 1.5%-21.5% absolute improvement in F1 score over existing data augmentation methods across 5 benchmark BioNER datasets
- Generates augmentations with lower perplexity and higher diversity compared to baselines
- Demonstrates consistent performance improvements across low-resource settings (100, 200, 500 samples per dataset)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Selective masking that retains named entities and keywords forces the model to focus on preserving critical semantic units during reconstruction. By masking all tokens except NEs and a small percentage of keywords, the fine-tuning task emphasizes learning to reconstruct context around entities rather than memorizing full sentences.

### Mechanism 2
Knowledge augmentation with biomedical relation triples provides global contextual facts that prevent hallucination and improve factuality. After selective masking, the model is trained with concatenated relation triples from a biomedical RE dataset, guiding the model to generate augmentations consistent with real-world biomedical facts.

### Mechanism 3
Conditional generation on corrupted sentences trained via selective masking and knowledge augmentation produces both diverse and factual augmentations. After fine-tuning, the model generates augmentations by corrupting test sentences similarly to training and performing top-k random sampling with beam search.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input sentences → keyword extraction (SciSpaCy) → relation extraction → selective masking → sequence linearization → knowledge augmentation → BioBART fine-tuning → conditional generation → augmentations → BioBERT NER training

- **Critical path**: 1. Extract keywords and relations from input sentences, 2. Perform selective masking and sequence linearization, 3. Concatenate knowledge triples and fine-tune BioBART, 4. Generate augmentations using conditional generation, 5. Train final NER model on augmented dataset

- **Design tradeoffs**: Selective masking vs. random masking (factuality vs. diversity), knowledge augmentation (global context vs. noise), top-k sampling vs. greedy decoding (diversity vs. coherence)

- **Failure signatures**: Low diversity (check masking restrictiveness), factual errors (inspect relation extraction quality), poor NER performance (verify augmentation integration)

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Ablation study: Remove knowledge augmentation and compare factuality metrics, 2. Parameter sweep: Vary percentage of keywords retained during selective masking and measure diversity vs. factuality tradeoff, 3. Cross-dataset evaluation: Apply BioAug trained on one BioNER dataset to another and assess generalization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of BioAug change when different percentages of keywords are retained during the selective masking process? The paper mentions that a percentage of keywords is randomly selected and retained during masking, but does not explore the impact of varying this percentage.

### Open Question 2
How does BioAug's performance compare to other data augmentation techniques when applied to non-biomedical NER tasks? The paper focuses on biomedical NER and does not explore the generalizability of BioAug to other domains.

### Open Question 3
What is the impact of incorporating different types of external knowledge (e.g., knowledge graphs, ontologies) on BioAug's performance? The paper uses relation extraction from a biomedical dataset as external knowledge, but does not explore the impact of different knowledge sources.

## Limitations

- **Evaluation Scope Uncertainty**: Improvements shown on 5 datasets with low-resource splits (100, 200, 500 samples) but unclear if this represents truly low-resource scenarios or real-world annotation constraints
- **Knowledge Quality Dependence**: Performance heavily depends on quality and coverage of biomedical relation extraction dataset; noise in knowledge triples may affect augmentation quality
- **Generative Model Stability**: Fine-tuning involves multiple complex steps (selective masking, knowledge concatenation, linearization) that may be sensitive to hyperparameter choices

## Confidence

**High Confidence (8-10/10)**: Core claim that BioAug improves BioNER F1 scores over existing augmentation methods is well-supported by experimental results across 5 datasets with substantial improvements (1.5%-21.5% absolute F1 gains).

**Medium Confidence (5-7/10)**: Claims about generating more diverse and factual augmentations are supported by perplexity and diversity metrics, but evaluation methodology has limitations including lack of human evaluation or semantic consistency checks.

**Low Confidence (1-4/10)**: Claims about specific contribution of knowledge augmentation to factuality improvements are not well-isolated due to lack of ablation studies removing the knowledge component.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study on Knowledge Component**: Remove the biomedical relation triples from the fine-tuning process and retrain BioAug to quantify the exact contribution of knowledge augmentation to factuality and performance improvements.

2. **Robustness Testing Across Annotation Budgets**: Evaluate BioAug on BioNER datasets with varying annotation levels (10, 50, 100, 200 samples) to understand minimum viable training set size and identify point of diminishing returns.

3. **Cross-Domain Generalization Test**: Apply BioAug fine-tuned on one biomedical domain (e.g., BC2GM) to another domain (e.g., NCBI) without domain-specific fine-tuning to assess generality of learned augmentation patterns.
