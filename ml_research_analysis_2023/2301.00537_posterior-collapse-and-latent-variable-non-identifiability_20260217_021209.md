# Posterior Collapse and Latent Variable Non-identifiability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00537
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00537
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: The paper proves posterior collapse in VAEs occurs if and only if latent variables are non-identifiable, and proposes Latent-Identifiable VAEs (LIDVAEs) using Brenier maps to resolve this issue.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a theoretical connection between posterior collapse in VAEs and latent variable non-identifiability in the generative model. The authors prove that posterior collapse occurs precisely when the likelihood function does not depend on the latent variable, making this phenomenon applicable to both classical probabilistic models and VAEs. Based on this insight, they propose LIDVAEs that enforce latent variable identifiability through bijective Brenier maps parameterized by input convex neural networks, achieving improved performance without modifying VAE objectives or requiring special optimization tricks.

## Method Summary
The authors propose Latent-Identifiable VAEs (LIDVAEs) that address posterior collapse by enforcing latent variable identifiability through Brenier maps. These maps are bijective transformations parameterized by input convex neural networks that ensure the likelihood function depends on the latent variable. The approach integrates seamlessly into the standard VAE framework without requiring modifications to the objective function or special optimization techniques. The Brenier map parameterization provides a principled way to ensure that the latent representation captures meaningful information from the data.

## Key Results
- LIDVAEs outperform existing methods in mitigating posterior collapse across synthetic and real datasets
- The approach achieves similar data fidelity to standard VAEs while resolving the collapse issue
- The method works without modifying VAE objectives or applying special optimization tricks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works because posterior collapse is fundamentally a problem of latent variable non-identifiability - when the likelihood function does not depend on the latent variable, the posterior becomes uninformative. By enforcing identifiability through Brenier maps, the generative model is constrained to depend on the latent variable in a meaningful way, preventing the collapse. The input convex neural networks ensure that the Brenier map is both bijective and computationally tractable, providing a practical solution to the theoretical problem.

## Limitations
- Theoretical proofs assume idealized conditions that may not hold with finite data and approximate inference
- Brenier map parameterization introduces significant computational overhead
- The approach modifies the generative model structure despite claims of not modifying VAE objectives
- Evaluation focuses on quantitative metrics with limited qualitative analysis of learned representations

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the theoretical connection between posterior collapse and non-identifiability
- **Medium confidence** in the practical effectiveness of LIDVAEs across diverse datasets
- **Medium confidence** in the scalability claims for high-dimensional latent spaces
