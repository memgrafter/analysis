# Hardware-aware training for large-scale and diverse deep learning inference workloads using in-memory computing-based accelerators

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08469
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08469
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Hardware-aware training significantly improves DNN accuracy on analog in-memory computing (AIMC) accelerators, with 5 of 11 models reaching iso-accuracy (within 1% of FP32 reference) after accounting for PCM device nonidealities.

## Executive Summary
This work presents a comprehensive study of hardware-aware training for analog in-memory computing (AIMC) accelerators. A realistic AIMC crossbar model incorporating device and circuit nonidealities is developed and used to retrain various DNN topologies including CNNs, RNNs, and transformers for improved accuracy on AIMC hardware. Across 11 diverse DNNs and AI workloads, the hardware-aware training significantly improves robustness to AIMC nonidealities. RNNs and transformers are generally more robust than CNNs. Sensitivity analysis reveals that noise at inputs/outputs has largest impact on accuracy, and identifies specific DNN layers most sensitive to nonidealities. The open-source AIHWKit enables future studies on AIMC noise robustness.

## Method Summary
The method involves retraining DNNs with hardware-aware (HWA) training, injecting AIMC nonidealities (noise, drift, IR-drop, quantization) into the forward pass. The key steps are: 1) Clone the AIHWKit repository and install dependencies. 2) Load a pre-trained FP32 model for one of the benchmark DNNs. 3) Configure the AIMC crossbar model with hardware-calibrated parameters. 4) Implement the HWA training loop by injecting AIMC nonidealities into the forward pass. 5) Train the model using SGD with noise injection, optimizing input range, weight-to-conductance mapping, and digital peripheral parameters. 6) Evaluate inference accuracy on AIMC after 1 hour of drift.

## Key Results
- Hardware-aware training significantly improves DNN accuracy on analog in-memory computing (AIMC) accelerators.
- 5 of 11 models reach iso-accuracy (within 1% of FP32 reference) after accounting for PCM device nonidealities.
- RNNs and transformers are generally more robust to AIMC nonidealities than CNNs.
- Noise at inputs and outputs has the largest impact on accuracy, and specific DNN layers exhibit varying degrees of sensitivity to nonidealities.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Hardware-aware training improves DNN accuracy on AIMC accelerators by retraining models to be robust to the specific nonidealities introduced by analog in-memory computing. By injecting noise, drift, IR-drop, and quantization effects into the forward pass during training, the model learns to compensate for these hardware limitations. This allows the retrained model to maintain high accuracy even when deployed on AIMC hardware.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Pre-trained FP32 model -> AIMC crossbar model with hardware nonidealities -> HWA-trained model -> AIMC inference
- Critical path: The HWA training loop, where nonidealities are injected into the forward pass and the model is optimized using SGD.
- Design tradeoffs: Balancing the strength of nonideality injection (to ensure robustness) with the stability of training (to avoid divergence). The choice of hyperparameters (learning rate, noise injection strength, weight decay) also impacts the effectiveness of HWA training.
- Failure signatures: If the model accuracy drops significantly on AIMC after HWA training, it may indicate insufficient noise injection or poor hyperparameter tuning. If convergence issues arise during training, it may suggest incorrect input range or weight-to-conductance mapping.
- First experiments: 1) Train a simple CNN (e.g., ResNet-18) on CIFAR-10 with HWA training and evaluate its accuracy on AIMC. 2) Compare the robustness of CNNs, RNNs, and transformers to AIMC nonidealities by training each architecture with HWA training and measuring their accuracy on AIMC. 3) Perform a sensitivity analysis by varying the strength of different nonidealities (e.g., noise, drift, IR-drop) and measuring their impact on model accuracy.

## Limitations
- The hyperparameter tuning for each DNN is not fully specified, which may affect reproducibility.
- The PCM device parameters used in the crossbar model are not provided, introducing uncertainty in the exact noise characteristics.
- The study focuses on 1 hour of PCM drift, and longer-term stability is not evaluated.

## Confidence
High: The main claims about hardware-aware training improving DNN accuracy on AIMC accelerators are well-supported by the extensive evaluation across diverse DNNs and AI workloads. The reported results (5 of 11 models reaching iso-accuracy) are significant and demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach.

Medium: The specific hyperparameter values and PCM device parameters used in the crossbar model are not fully specified, which may introduce some uncertainty in the exact implementation details. However, the open-source AIHWKit provides a starting point for reproducing the results.
