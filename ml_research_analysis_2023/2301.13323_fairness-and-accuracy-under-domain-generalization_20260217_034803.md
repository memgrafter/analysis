# Fairness and Accuracy under Domain Generalization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.13323
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13323
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes FATDM framework achieving state-of-the-art fairness and accuracy under domain generalization on MIMIC-CXR clinical data

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of maintaining both fairness and accuracy when deploying machine learning models across different domains where target data is unavailable during training. The authors develop theoretical bounds on expected loss and unfairness using Jensen-Shannon distance, identifying conditions for perfect transfer via invariant representation learning. Based on these insights, they propose a two-stage learning framework called FATDM that learns density matching functions across source domains while enforcing domain-invariant representations. The framework is evaluated on real-world clinical data, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing domain generalization methods.

## Method Summary
The authors propose FATDM (Fairness and Accuracy via Transfer under Domain Generalization), a two-stage learning framework. First, they learn density matching functions across source domains to align distributions. Second, they enforce domain-invariant representations that simultaneously minimize classification loss and fairness violations. The method leverages theoretical bounds on expected loss and unfairness derived from Jensen-Shannon distance between source and target domains. The framework jointly optimizes for accuracy metrics (AUROC, AUPR, F1, cross-entropy) and fairness metrics (equalized odds, equal opportunity) without requiring target domain data during training.

## Key Results
- FATDM achieves state-of-the-art performance on MIMIC-CXR dataset for both fairness (equalized odds and equal opportunity) and accuracy (AUROC, AUPR, F1, cross-entropy)
- Theoretical bounds on expected loss and unfairness in target domains are established using Jensen-Shannon distance
- Demonstrates that fairness and accuracy can be perfectly transferred when conditions for invariant representation learning are met
- Outperforms existing domain generalization methods when deployment environments differ from training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework succeeds by establishing theoretical foundations for when fairness and accuracy transfer between domains. By learning density matching functions and enforcing domain-invariant representations, FATDM creates representations that capture essential patterns while minimizing domain-specific variations. The two-stage approach first aligns source domain distributions, then learns representations that generalize across unseen domains while maintaining fairness constraints.

## Foundational Learning

**Jensen-Shannon Distance**: Measures similarity between probability distributions, needed to bound transfer performance between domains. Quick check: Verify JS divergence calculations are symmetric and bounded between 0 and 1.

**Domain Generalization**: Learning from multiple source domains to perform well on unseen target domains. Quick check: Ensure source domains are sufficiently diverse to capture domain shift patterns.

**Invariant Representation Learning**: Finding features that remain consistent across domains. Quick check: Validate representations maintain predictive power while being domain-invariant.

**Density Matching**: Aligning probability distributions across domains. Quick check: Monitor KL divergence between source domain distributions during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Augmentation -> Density Matching -> Invariant Representation Learning -> Fairness-Aware Classification

**Critical Path**: The density matching stage is critical as it establishes the foundation for invariant representation learning. Poor density alignment leads to suboptimal invariant representations.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The framework balances between perfect density matching (computationally expensive) and approximate matching (faster but potentially less effective). The two-stage approach trades off end-to-end optimization for modularity and interpretability.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Poor density matching leads to domain-specific artifacts in representations
- Over-regularization of invariance causes accuracy degradation
- Insufficient source domain diversity results in poor generalization

2. Test invariant representation learning by training on one source domain and evaluating on others

## Limitations
- Theoretical bounds rely on Jensen-Shannon distance estimates without addressing practical computation when target data is unavailable
- Assumes linear relationships between domains which may not hold for complex, high-dimensional data like medical imaging
- Requires careful hyperparameter tuning with limited practical implementation guidance

## Confidence
- **High**: Experimental results showing FATDM's performance improvements on MIMIC-CXR data
- **Medium**: Theoretical bounds and their relationship to practical performance (depends on assumptions about domain shifts)
- **Low**: Claims about framework's generalizability to non-medical domains and tasks beyond clinical setting studied
