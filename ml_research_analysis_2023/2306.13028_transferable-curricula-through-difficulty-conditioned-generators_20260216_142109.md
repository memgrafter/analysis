# Transferable Curricula through Difficulty Conditioned Generators

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13028
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13028
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: PERM generates effective curricula by directly modeling environment difficulty and agent ability using Item Response Theory, achieving better sample efficiency and transfer capabilities than baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Parameterized Environment Response Model (PERM), a novel approach for generating curricula in reinforcement learning environments. PERM leverages Item Response Theory to directly model the difficulty of environments and the ability of agents, rather than relying on surrogate objectives. The key innovation is a variational autoencoder that estimates agent ability and environment difficulty from reward data, then generates new environments matching the agent's current ability level. Experiments on LunarLander and BipedalWalker demonstrate that PERM outperforms several baseline methods in terms of agent performance during training and evaluation, while also being transferable between different agents without requiring retraining.

## Method Summary
PERM uses a variational autoencoder to learn latent representations of agent ability and environment difficulty from reward data. The model directly estimates these latent variables rather than using proxy measures like regret. It can be trained offline on data from one agent and transferred to others. During training, PERM generates new environment parameters that match the estimated ability level of the agent, creating a curriculum that keeps tasks in the "zone of proximal development." The approach is evaluated against baselines including PLR⊥, PAIRED, and Domain Randomization on LunarLander and BipedalWalker environments using PPO agents.

## Key Results
- PERM achieves higher final performance than baseline methods on both LunarLander and BipedalWalker environments
- PERM-Offline demonstrates successful transfer between agents without retraining
- PERM shows improved sample efficiency compared to Domain Randomization
- The IRT-based difficulty estimation provides more stable and transferable representations than non-stationary alternatives

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PERM can generate environments that match the student's current ability level.
- Mechanism: The model uses Item Response Theory (IRT) to directly estimate both the student's ability and the difficulty of environments from reward data, then generates new environments with difficulty matching the current ability estimate.
- Core assumption: Learning is most effective when environment difficulty matches student ability (zone of proximal development).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "PERM seeks to model difficulty of environments and ability of RL agents directly" and "generates a curriculum by matching the difficulty of an environment to the current ability of the student"
  - [section]: "The IRT allows educators to quantify the 'difficulty' of a given test item by modelling the relationship between a test taker's response to the test item and the test taker's overall ability"
- Break condition: The model fails when the reward signal becomes uninformative or when the relationship between ability and difficulty breaks down in highly stochastic environments.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PERM can be trained offline and transferred between different agents without performance loss.
- Mechanism: By modeling difficulty and ability directly rather than using non-stationary objectives like regret, PERM creates a stable representation that can be learned from one agent's data and applied to others.
- Core assumption: The relationship between environment parameters and difficulty is consistent across agents with similar capabilities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "PERM can be trained offline and does not employ non-stationary measures of student ability, making it suitable for transfer between students"
  - [section]: "Unlike previous UED methods which relies on proxy estimates of difficulty and environment feasibility (e.g. Regret), we propose to directly estimate difficulty"
- Break condition: Transfer fails when agents have fundamentally different capabilities or when environment parameters don't map consistently to difficulty across agent types.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PERM outperforms baselines by avoiding infeasible environments and maintaining sample efficiency.
- Mechanism: By generating environments within the student's capability range, PERM reduces the proportion of failed episodes and focuses training on learnable tasks.
- Core assumption: Environments that are too difficult or too easy provide less learning value than those at the appropriate difficulty level.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "PERM, by providing environments that are constantly within the student's capabilities, is more sample efficient than DR" and "episode lengths for students trained under DR are shorter (mean of 244 timesteps vs 311 timesteps for PERM)"
- Break condition: The mechanism breaks when the environment parameter space is too large or complex for PERM to adequately cover all difficulty levels.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Variational Autoencoder (VAE) core with encoder qφ(a,d|r,λ) and decoder pθ(r|a,d) + pθ(λ|d)
  - PERM objective function combining reconstruction losses and KL divergences
  - Environment parameter generator that samples λt+1 from pθ(λ|dt+1)
  - Student agent training loop that collects rewards and updates PERM periodically

- Critical path:
  1. Student agent interacts with environment parameterized by λt
  2. Collect reward rt and store (πt, λt, rt)
  3. Every k episodes, update PERM using Equation 3
  4. Generate new difficulty dt+1 = at from PERM
  5. Generate new parameters λt+1 from pθ(λ|dt+1)
  6. Continue training student agent

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Offline vs online PERM training: Offline is transferable but may not adapt to unique agent characteristics
  - Frequency of PERM updates: More frequent updates adapt better but increase computational cost
  - Latent space dimensionality: Higher dimensions capture more nuance but risk overfitting and increase complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - PERM generates parameters that lead to NaNs or infinite rewards
  - Ability and difficulty estimates diverge from actual reward trends
  - Student performance plateaus despite curriculum changes
  - Generated environments fall outside the valid parameter ranges

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train PERM offline on data from a simple DR-trained agent, then evaluate ability/difficulty reconstruction accuracy
  2. Train a student agent with PERM-Online on LunarLander and compare to DR baseline in terms of sample efficiency
  3. Transfer PERM-Offline to a different agent type and verify performance retention without additional training

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several implications arise from the work:
- How well does PERM transfer between different types of agents (e.g., humans, different RL algorithms) in more complex environments beyond LunarLander and BipedalWalker?
- How does PERM handle non-stationary environments where the difficulty of the tasks changes over time?
- How does PERM compare to other curriculum learning methods in terms of sample efficiency and final performance in complex environments?

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to two relatively simple environments (LunarLander and BipedalWalker)
- Transfer capabilities only demonstrated between agents trained on the same tasks
- Comparison to baseline methods lacks recent curriculum learning approaches
- Potential instability in latent space representations during extended training runs

## Confidence
- High Confidence: PERM's ability to model difficulty and ability directly using IRT (validated through reconstruction experiments)
- Medium Confidence: Claims about transfer between agents (limited to same task domain, small sample size)
- Medium Confidence: Claims about sample efficiency improvements (based on limited comparison to DR baseline)
- Low Confidence: Claims about superiority over all baseline methods (lacks comparison to more recent curriculum learning approaches)

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-task Transfer Validation**: Test PERM-Offline transfer between agents trained on different environments (e.g., from LunarLander to a different locomotion task) to assess true generalization capabilities beyond the same-task domain.

2. **Latent Space Stability Analysis**: Monitor the evolution of ability and difficulty embeddings during extended training runs to identify potential collapse or drift that could affect curriculum quality over time.

3. **Baseline Expansion**: Compare PERM against recent curriculum learning methods like Goal-Conditioned VAE approaches and Dynamic Curriculum Learning to establish whether the IRT-based approach provides unique advantages beyond traditional reward-based curricula.
