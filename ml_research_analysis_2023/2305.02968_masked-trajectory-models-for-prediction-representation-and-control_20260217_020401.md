# Masked Trajectory Models for Prediction, Representation, and Control

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.02968
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02968
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: MTM matches or exceeds specialized models and state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms across multiple benchmarks

## Executive Summary
Masked Trajectory Models (MTM) introduces a generic self-supervised learning framework for sequential decision making. MTM trains a transformer to reconstruct trajectories conditioned on randomly masked views of the same trajectory. A single MTM model can serve multiple roles—such as forward dynamics, inverse dynamics, imitation learning, and offline RL—by changing the inference-time masking pattern. Empirical results on D4RL, Adroit, and ExORL benchmarks show that MTM matches or exceeds specialized models and state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms, despite lacking explicit RL components.

## Method Summary
MTM uses a bidirectional transformer encoder-decoder architecture with random autoregressive masking. During training, the model reconstructs trajectories from masked views, learning to predict missing states, actions, and returns. At inference, different masking patterns enable different capabilities: predicting next actions for behavior cloning, predicting actions from states for inverse dynamics, or reconstructing full trajectories for offline RL. The model handles heteromodal datasets by treating missing data as masked during training.

## Key Results
- MTM matches or exceeds specialized models on behavior cloning and inverse dynamics tasks
- MTM performs competitively with state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms like CQL and IQL
- MTM's learned state representations significantly accelerate downstream RL training with algorithms like TD3

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: MTM learns versatile models that can be repurposed for different tasks by changing the masking pattern at inference time
- **Mechanism**: The model is trained to reconstruct trajectories from randomly masked views, forcing it to learn relationships between states, actions, and returns. Different inference-time masking patterns activate different learned capabilities
- **Core assumption**: Relationships learned during training with random masks are sufficiently general to support multiple inference-time tasks
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states MTM can take on different roles by choosing appropriate masks; corpus papers don't directly address multi-task versatility
- **Break condition**: If relationships learned are too specialized to training masking pattern, model fails to generalize to new inference-time masks

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: MTM can consume heteromodal datasets and effectively use them to improve performance
- **Mechanism**: During training, any missing data is treated as if it were masked, allowing the model to handle datasets where some trajectories have action labels and others do not
- **Core assumption**: Treating missing data as masked during training allows the model to learn robust representations that generalize across modalities
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions MTM's unique capability for heteromodal data; corpus papers don't discuss heteromodal data handling in detail
- **Break condition**: If proportion of missing data is too high, model may not learn meaningful relationships between modalities

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: MTM learns state representations that accelerate downstream RL training
- **Mechanism**: Self-supervised objective of reconstructing trajectories forces model to learn rich representations of states, actions, and returns that capture underlying structure of environment and policy
- **Core assumption**: Representations learned by MTM are sufficiently informative and disentangled to benefit downstream RL algorithms
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions state representations accelerate traditional RL algorithms like TD3; corpus papers don't discuss representation transfer
- **Break condition**: If representations are not informative enough or too entangled, they will not provide benefit to downstream RL algorithms

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Masked autoencoding
  - **Why needed here**: MTM is based on idea of masked autoencoding where model is trained to reconstruct missing parts of sequence, forcing it to learn underlying structure and relationships within data
  - **Quick check question**: What is the main difference between standard autoencoding and masked autoencoding?

- **Concept**: Transformer sequence models
  - **Why needed here**: MTM uses bidirectional transformer to process trajectory sequences, which are well-suited for capturing long-range dependencies and have been successful in various sequential decision-making tasks
  - **Quick check question**: How does a bidirectional transformer differ from a causal transformer in terms of information flow?

- **Concept**: Self-supervised learning
  - **Why needed here**: MTM is self-supervised learning method that doesn't require explicit labels for training, allowing it to learn from unlabeled data which is often more abundant in sequential decision-making domains
  - **Quick check question**: What are the advantages of using self-supervised learning in domains where labeled data is scarce?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Raw trajectory -> Modality-specific encoders -> Bidirectional transformer encoder (unmasked tokens) -> Bidirectional transformer decoder (predicts original sequence) -> MLP heads -> Reconstructed trajectory

- **Critical path**: 1) Tokenize raw trajectory using modality-specific encoders 2) Apply random autoregressive masking pattern to tokenized sequence 3) Feed masked sequence into bidirectional transformer encoder 4) Use bidirectional transformer decoder to predict original sequence 5) Compute reconstruction loss and update model parameters

- **Design tradeoffs**: Masking ratio (higher ratio more challenging but may lead to better representations), trajectory length (longer trajectories provide more context but increase computational cost), modality embeddings (distinguish between different elements but add complexity)

- **Failure signatures**: High reconstruction loss (model not learning underlying structure), poor performance on downstream tasks (learned representations not informative enough), overfitting to training masking pattern (model doesn't generalize to new inference-time masks)

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Train MTM on small dataset with high masking ratio and evaluate reconstruction loss 2) Train MTM with different masking ratios and evaluate performance on downstream task like behavior cloning 3) Train MTM on heteromodal dataset and evaluate ability to handle missing modalities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does MTM's heteromodal capability extend to other missing data scenarios beyond state-only trajectories?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Paper mentions MTM can handle heteromodal datasets where different trajectories contain different modalities, demonstrates this with state-only vs state-action data
- **Why unresolved**: Paper only tests heteromodal learning with state-only data; other missing data scenarios remain unexplored
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments showing MTM performance when trained on datasets with various combinations of missing modalities

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What is the theoretical foundation explaining why MTM's random autoregressive masking pattern leads to better performance than purely random masking?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Paper observes random autoregressive masking often outperforms purely random masking and is competitive with specialized masks, but doesn't provide theoretical justification
- **Why unresolved**: Paper empirically demonstrates benefit but doesn't explain underlying mechanism or provide theoretical guarantees
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Mathematical analysis or theoretical framework explaining how autoregressive constraint affects learned representations and downstream task performance

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does MTM's performance scale with trajectory length and dataset size compared to specialized models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] Paper shows longer trajectory lengths improve performance and MTM is more data-efficient than MLPs, but only tests up to trajectory length 16 and provides limited scaling analysis
- **Why unresolved**: Paper doesn't systematically explore how performance scales with much longer trajectories or much larger datasets
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Comprehensive experiments varying trajectory length and dataset size to establish scaling relationships and identify bottlenecks

## Limitations
- Generality of masking pattern to enable multiple capabilities without retraining is largely demonstrated empirically but lacks theoretical grounding
- Mechanism by which MTM handles heteromodal data is underspecified - treating missing data as masked is intuitive but not rigorously validated
- Transfer of learned representations to accelerate downstream RL is shown on specific algorithms but conditions for successful transfer are not characterized

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: MTM can learn to reconstruct trajectories from masked views (core training objective is standard)
- **Medium confidence**: MTM matches or exceeds specialized models and state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms (empirical results are strong but comparisons may have implementation differences)
- **Low confidence**: The learned representations consistently accelerate downstream RL training (limited to specific algorithms and environments)

## Next Checks
