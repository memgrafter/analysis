# Evaluating OpenAI's Whisper ASR for Punctuation Prediction and Topic Modeling of life histories of the Museum of the Person

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14580
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14580
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Whisper achieved an overall F1 score of 46.4% for five punctuation marks, with the highest F1 score of 77.5% for comma and 68% for question marks.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates OpenAI's Whisper ASR model for punctuation prediction and topic modeling in Portuguese transcripts of Museum of the Person life histories. The researchers found that Whisper significantly outperforms previous ASR approaches, achieving an overall F1 score of 46.4% for five punctuation marks. The model shows particular strength in predicting commas (77.5%) and question marks (68%). When applied to transcript-based video topic modeling, Whisper's punctuation prediction enabled the generation of coherent topics with BLANC scores ranging from 0.1140 to 0.1795, demonstrating clear advantages over approaches without punctuation.

## Method Summary
The researchers used Whisper's large multilingual model to generate transcriptions and punctuation for 10 Portuguese life history interviews from the Museum of the Person (MuPe), totaling approximately 17 hours of audio. They developed a custom alignment algorithm to match Whisper's automatic transcriptions with manually transcribed and punctuated reference data. For topic modeling, they segmented videos based on Whisper-generated punctuation, associated segments with reference topics using cosine similarity in a vector space from a language model, and evaluated coherence using BLANC scores. The study also explored gender-based performance differences and potential applications for video summarization.

## Key Results
- Whisper achieved an overall F1 score of 46.4% for five punctuation marks (comma, exclamation, question, fullstop, ellipsis)
- Comma punctuation prediction showed the highest accuracy at 77.5% F1 score
- Topic modeling using Whisper-generated punctuation achieved BLANC scores of 0.1140-0.1795, significantly outperforming unpunctuated approaches
- Gender analysis revealed lower punctuation prediction performance for female speakers compared to male speakers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Whisper's punctuation prediction capability significantly improves readability and downstream NLP tasks compared to previous ASR models.
- Mechanism: Whisper was trained on a large multilingual dataset (680,000 hours) with unnormalized transcriptions, allowing it to learn punctuation patterns during training rather than requiring a separate post-processing step.
- Core assumption: The diverse training data includes sufficient examples of Portuguese speech with natural punctuation patterns for the model to generalize effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Recently Whisper ASR was proposed by OpenAI, a general-purpose speech recognition model that has generated great expectations in dealing with such limitations."
  - [section 3.1] "The Whisper model was trained with unnormalized transcriptions and it can predict automatically the transcription's punctuation."
- Break condition: If the Portuguese training data is insufficient or lacks diversity in punctuation patterns, the model may fail to generalize effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Whisper's punctuation prediction enables effective transcript-based video topic modeling by identifying coherent segments.
- Mechanism: Punctuation marks generated by Whisper are used to define video segments, which are then associated with reference topics based on cosine similarity in a vector space obtained through a language model.
- Core assumption: Correct punctuation identification leads to coherent segments that accurately represent the underlying topics discussed in the interview.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For transcript-based video topic modeling, Whisper's punctuation prediction enabled the generation of coherent topics, as measured by BLANC scores ranging from 0.1140 to 0.1795, compared to lower scores when punctuation was absent."
  - [section 4.2.2] "The correct identification of punctuation marks generated by Whisper during ASR is essential for the entire transcript-based video topic modeling process."
- Break condition: If Whisper fails to correctly identify punctuation marks, the segment identification and topic association steps will be compromised, leading to poor topic modeling results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Whisper achieves state-of-the-art results for Portuguese punctuation prediction, with the highest F1 score of 77.5% for comma and 68% for question marks.
- Mechanism: The large-scale multilingual training allows Whisper to learn nuanced patterns for different punctuation marks across languages, including Portuguese.
- Core assumption: The model's architecture and training data are sufficient to capture the specific patterns and contexts that indicate different punctuation marks in Portuguese spontaneous speech.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Whisper achieved an overall F1 score of 46.4% for five punctuation marks, with the highest F1 score of 77.5% for comma and 68% for question marks."
  - [section 5.1] "Our best results, in terms of the overall F1 score, were for the comma punctuation (77.5%), which has the higher distribution in the MuPe testset."
- Break condition: If certain punctuation marks (like exclamation, semicolon, and colon) have insufficient training examples or are underrepresented in the data, the model may consistently underperform on those specific marks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Whisper ASR model (multilingual large version) -> Diarization tool (Pyannote) -> Alignment tool -> Language model (paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2) -> Reference topic taxonomy (IPTC Media Topics)

- Critical path:
  1. Diarize audio to create smaller segments
  2. Generate automatic transcription with Whisper
  3. Align automatic transcription with manual reference
  4. Evaluate punctuation prediction performance
  5. Use punctuation to define segments for topic modeling
  6. Associate segments with reference topics
  7. Select representative segments to create summarized video

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a large multilingual model vs. a smaller, language-specific model
  - Integrating punctuation prediction vs. using a separate post-processing step
  - Using a simple segment selection strategy vs. more complex approaches for topic modeling

- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 scores for specific punctuation marks indicate issues with learning those patterns
  - Poor BLANC scores in topic modeling suggest punctuation is not effectively defining coherent segments
  - High discrepancy between automatic and manual transcription token counts may indicate alignment issues

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate Whisper's punctuation prediction performance on a held-out test set of MuPe life histories
  2. Compare topic modeling results using Whisper-generated transcripts vs. manually punctuated transcripts
  3. Analyze performance differences between male and female speakers to identify potential biases or areas for improvement

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does Whisper's performance vary between different textual genres in the NURC-SP Minimal Corpus (conversations vs. formal elocutions)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6 states "Evaluate whether there is a differentiated performance regarding the automatic prediction of the Whisper marks between textual genres of the NURC-SP Minimal Corpus"
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated Whisper on MuPe life history interviews, which are conversational. Performance on other genres like formal elocutions remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of Whisper's punctuation prediction accuracy across different genres in the NURC-SP Minimal Corpus, measuring F1 scores for each genre.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What acoustic features (beyond those used in Sunkara et al. 2020) contribute to Whisper's punctuation prediction performance in spontaneous speech?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6 mentions "Evaluate the cause of the different performance between genders (men and women) in the MuPe dataset, with the help of metrics such as pitch and the set of ASR wav2vec metrics"
- Why unresolved: While pitch was mentioned, the paper did not systematically analyze which acoustic features most strongly correlate with punctuation prediction errors in Whisper.
- What evidence would resolve it: Correlation analysis between various acoustic features (pitch, energy, duration, etc.) and Whisper's punctuation prediction errors across different speakers and speech contexts.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can multimodal sentiment analysis improve the coherence of topic modeling in MuPe life history interviews beyond punctuation-based segmentation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6 states "Extend MuPe's video topic modeling application by also considering sentiment analysis of parts of the interview"
- Why unresolved: The current study only evaluated punctuation's impact on topic modeling coherence. The potential contribution of sentiment information remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative evaluation of topic modeling coherence (using BLANC scores) between punctuation-only segmentation versus segmentation that incorporates both punctuation and sentiment information.

## Limitations
- The study uses only 10 life narratives from the Museum of the Person, totaling approximately 17 hours of audio, limiting generalizability.
- The alignment algorithm between Whisper outputs and manual transcriptions uses a custom three-step process with undisclosed parameter details, introducing potential variability in reported F1 scores.
- The study doesn't explore potential dialectal variations or regional differences in Portuguese that could affect performance.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Whisper's superior performance compared to previous ASR models for Portuguese punctuation prediction is well-supported by empirical results.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that Whisper's punctuation prediction enables effective transcript-based video topic modeling is supported but requires additional validation.
- **Low confidence**: The assertion that Whisper achieves "state-of-the-art results" for Portuguese punctuation prediction lacks comparative benchmarks against other recent multilingual models.
