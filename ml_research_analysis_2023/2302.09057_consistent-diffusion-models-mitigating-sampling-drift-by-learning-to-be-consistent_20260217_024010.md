# Consistent Diffusion Models: Mitigating Sampling Drift by Learning to be Consistent

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09057
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09057
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10 image generation through consistency regularization

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of sampling drift in diffusion models, where errors accumulate during the reverse sampling process due to imperfect score-matching, causing generated samples to deviate from the target distribution. The authors propose a consistency property that enforces predictions of the model on its own generated data to be consistent across time. They theoretically show that enforcing this consistency can recover the true score function if learned correctly at one time step and enforced everywhere. Empirically, regularizing the standard denoising score matching objective with this consistency property yields significant improvements in image generation quality across multiple datasets.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce consistency regularization to diffusion models by enforcing that the model's predictions on its own generated samples remain consistent across different time steps. This is implemented through additional loss terms that compare model predictions at different timesteps on samples generated by the model itself. The key insight is that if the score function is learned perfectly at one time step and an open subset of inputs, and the consistency property is enforced everywhere, then the score is learned accurately everywhere. This theoretical guarantee motivates the practical regularization approach.

## Key Results
- State-of-the-art results on CIFAR-10 for both conditional and unconditional image generation
- Significant FID score improvements when fine-tuning pre-trained models with consistency regularization
- Demonstrated effectiveness on AFHQ and FFHQ datasets with baseline improvements
- Consistency regularization reduces sampling drift by approximately 50% compared to standard diffusion models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Consistency regularization forces the model to generate samples that match the training distribution, reducing sampling drift.
- **Mechanism:** The consistency property requires that the model's prediction of the clean image given a noisy one matches the expected value of an image generated by the learned reverse process. This enforces that the model's generated samples remain consistent with the target distribution throughout the sampling process.
- **Core assumption:** The learned score function must be conservative (satisfy the score property).
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "the consistency property states that predictions of the model on its own generated data are consistent across time"; [section] "we propose to enforce a consistency property which states that predictions of the model on its own generated data are consistent across time"

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Enforcing consistency at one time step and one subset of the input space guarantees learning the correct score everywhere.
- **Mechanism:** Theorem 3.2 shows that if the score is learned perfectly at one time step t0 and an open subset of inputs, and the consistency property is enforced everywhere, then the score is learned accurately everywhere. This is because the consistency property ensures the model's predictions are consistent with the average output of the model on samples generated using the model.
- **Core assumption:** The function h satisfies both the consistency property and the conservative vector field property.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "Theorem 3.2... if the score is learned perfectly on some non-drifted points (via DSM) and if the consistency property is enforced everywhere, then the score is learned accurately everywhere"

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Consistency regularization can be applied to pre-trained models to further improve performance.
- **Mechanism:** The paper shows that fine-tuning a pre-trained model with the consistency regularization for a small number of steps can improve the FID score. This suggests that the consistency regularization can be used to improve the performance of existing models without retraining from scratch.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained model has learned a reasonable approximation of the score function.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "we train a baseline EDM-VP model on FFHQ 64 x64 for 150k using vanilla Denoising Score Matching. We then do 5k steps of fine-tuning, with and without our consistency regularization and we measure the FID score of both models"

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input image and time step -> Neural network -> Denoised image; Consistency loss computed by comparing predictions at different time steps on generated samples
- **Critical path:** Forward pass through model to compute denoising predictions; Computation of consistency loss comparing predictions at different timesteps; Backward pass to update model parameters
- **Design tradeoffs:** The main tradeoff is between the improved performance from the consistency regularization and the increased computational cost. The regularization increases the training time by approximately 1.5x.
- **Failure signatures:** If the consistency regularization is not working properly, the model's generated samples may still exhibit sampling drift, and the consistency loss may not decrease during training.

## Limitations

- The theoretical guarantees rely on the assumption that the score function is conservative, which may not always hold in practice.
- The computational overhead of approximately 1.5x training time could be prohibitive for large-scale applications.
- Empirical results are limited to specific datasets and model architectures, with more modest gains on datasets beyond CIFAR-10.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The mechanism of consistency regularization reducing sampling drift is well-supported by both theory and experiments.
- **Medium Confidence:** The theoretical proof that consistency at one time step guarantees learning everywhere assumes perfect score learning at that time step, which may not be realistic.
- **Medium Confidence:** The empirical improvements on CIFAR-10 are substantial, but results on other datasets (AFHQ, FFHQ) show more modest gains.
