# Multi-Object Self-Supervised Depth Denoising

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05778
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05778
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Achieved 60.28% noise reduction (IT/OT metric) on YCB objects using self-supervised denoising pipeline

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a self-supervised depth denoising pipeline that leverages high-quality depth sensors as supervisory signals to improve lower-quality depth maps. The method aligns depth frames from two cameras using a fixed extrinsic transformation, applies object masking to focus on regions of interest, and uses a UNet-based neural network for denoising. The approach achieves significant noise reduction (60.28% IT/OT improvement) compared to input depth maps, particularly for large depth deviations (>10 mm), while maintaining edge preservation. The pipeline is particularly relevant for robotic manipulation and visual servoing tasks where high-quality depth data is crucial but expensive sensors may not be available for all applications.

## Method Summary
The pipeline captures synchronized RGB-D frames from two sensors with different quality levels, then calibrates their relative positions once to establish a fixed extrinsic transformation. Depth frames are converted to 3D point clouds, aligned using the pre-computed transformation, and masked to isolate regions of interest through a two-stage clustering algorithm. The aligned and masked data serves as training pairs where the high-quality sensor provides ground truth labels for denoising the low-quality input. Data augmentation is performed in 3D point cloud space through random rigid transformations, and a UNet-based neural network learns to map noisy depth maps to clean versions. The self-supervised nature allows training without manual labeling, while the masking strategy focuses the network on objects rather than background noise.

## Key Results
- Achieved 60.28% reduction in noise (IT/OT metric) compared to input depth maps on YCB object dataset
- Outperformed baseline denoising methods, particularly for depth deviations exceeding 10 mm
- Demonstrated improved edge preservation while reducing overall noise levels
- Showed computational efficiency through one-time calibration and small dataset training requirements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-sensor calibration using fixed extrinsic transformation reduces per-sequence recalibration cost and stabilizes pixel-wise depth correspondence.
- Mechanism: By mounting two sensors in reproducible relative positions and computing the extrinsic transformation once, the method avoids repeated alignment steps. Point clouds from the higher-quality sensor are transformed into the lower-quality sensor's coordinate frame using a pre-computed rigid body transform.
- Core assumption: Sensor mounting repeatability ensures that the computed transformation remains valid across all acquisition sessions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We are leveraging on the fact that our cameras can be mounted in the same relative position repeatedly, which reduces the calibration algorithm massively."
  - [section] "The construction chosen should ensure that both sensors are mounted in the very same relative position across several mounts resulting in consistent data over several acquisitions."
- Break condition: Mechanical shifts or vibrations that alter the relative sensor positions invalidate the pre-computed extrinsic matrix.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Point-cloud augmentation in 3D space preserves depth consistency and enables effective data expansion without synthetic label corruption.
- Mechanism: RGB-D frames are unprojected into 3D point clouds, transformed via random rigid motions, and re-projected back into image space. This ensures that the augmented depth values correspond physically to the new spatial positions.
- Core assumption: The random transformations applied are small enough to remain within the valid field of view and not introduce occlusions or self-intersections that break the depth map structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Instead of augmenting the frames of each data tuple, we applied the augmentation in three-dimensional space."
  - [section] "The augmentations were generated with a maximal translation of 10 cm and a maximal rotation of 5 degrees."
- Break condition: If transformations exceed physical constraints (e.g., moving objects out of the camera's view), depth maps will contain invalid regions that degrade training quality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Masking of regions of interest focuses the network on objects of interest and prevents background noise from biasing depth predictions.
- Mechanism: A two-stage clustering algorithm in 3D point cloud space separates foreground objects from the background surface. The resulting mask is applied to both input and target depth frames so the network trains only on relevant pixels.
- Core assumption: Objects are positioned on a flat, distinct background with sufficient depth contrast to allow reliable clustering.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We implemented a classical algorithm to generate accurate masks for our dataset... operates on the normal difference of adjacent points."
  - [section] "The usage of high-quality masks was crucial for the generation of our results."
- Break condition: In complex scenes with cluttered or non-planar backgrounds, the masking algorithm may incorrectly include or exclude regions, reducing denoising effectiveness.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Point cloud unprojection and reprojection
  - Why needed here: The pipeline relies on spatial alignment of depth data across two sensors, which requires converting 2D depth maps into 3D space and back.
  - Quick check question: Given a pixel (u,v) with depth d and intrinsic parameters fx, fy, cx, cy, what are its 3D coordinates in the camera frame?

- Concept: Rigid body transformation in 3D
  - Why needed here: Aligning the higher-quality sensor's point cloud to the lower-quality sensor's coordinate system requires a 4x4 extrinsic matrix composed of rotation and translation.
  - Quick check question: If a point cloud is transformed by a 4x4 matrix T, which components of the matrix affect rotation and which affect translation?

- Concept: Density-based spatial clustering (DBSCAN)
  - Why needed here: The masking pipeline uses DBSCAN to remove outliers and group points belonging to the same object, ensuring clean object masks.
  - Quick check question: How does DBSCAN differentiate between core points, border points, and noise in a point cloud?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Sensor rig → Calibration module → Point cloud alignment → Masking pipeline → Data augmentation → UNet denoising network → Evaluation metrics
- Critical path: Raw frame capture → Calibration → Alignment → Masking → Augmentation → Training. Any failure in calibration or masking directly corrupts the ground truth and masks used for training.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed extrinsic transformation vs. per-sequence calibration: fixed saves computation but risks drift if mechanical setup changes.
  - Heavy 3D augmentation vs. 2D augmentation: 3D preserves physical consistency but is computationally heavier.
  - Precise masking vs. broader training: precise masks improve focus but require clean background assumptions.
- Failure signatures:
  - Calibration failure: misaligned objects between LQ and HQ frames; visible offset in aligned images.
  - Masking failure: background pixels leaking into object mask; object parts missing from mask.
  - Augmentation failure: depth holes or invalid regions after reprojection; unrealistic object positions.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify calibration: capture a static scene, apply fixed extrinsic, and visually inspect alignment of LQ/HQ depth maps.
  2. Test masking: run the masking pipeline on a single frame and inspect the binary mask overlaid on the RGB image.
  3. Validate augmentation: unproject a frame, apply a small random transform, reproject, and confirm depth consistency with the original.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the denoising performance change if the pipeline were applied to robotic environments with complex backgrounds instead of flat, distant backgrounds?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states that the masking algorithm relies on flat underground and distant backgrounds, which may not be present in real-world robotic environments.
- Why unresolved: The paper explicitly notes that deploying the pipeline in action on a robot may result in poorer results and may require a revised masking tool, but does not provide experimental data or analysis of performance in complex environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the pipeline on robotic datasets with cluttered backgrounds and varying depths, comparing denoising performance metrics (MSE, IT/OT) to those achieved in controlled environments.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the network be trained to denoise the entire depth map without relying on object masks, and what would be the impact on performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that with a large enough dataset, the masking of ROIs could eventually be omitted, as the network could learn to denoise the whole input depth map.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore training without masks or evaluate the trade-offs in denoising accuracy and computational efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating a model on unmasked depth maps, comparing denoising metrics and runtime performance to the masked approach.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of sensor pairing (e.g., different combinations of low- and high-quality RGB-D sensors) affect the denoising performance and dataset generation efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses RealSense D435 as the lower-quality sensor and Zivid One+ as the higher-quality sensor, but does not explore other sensor combinations.
- Why unresolved: The impact of sensor resolution, frame rate, and noise characteristics on the alignment accuracy and denoising results is not investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimenting with different sensor pairs, measuring alignment error, denoising accuracy (MSE, IT/OT), and dataset acquisition time across configurations.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the effect of increasing the diversity of YCB objects (e.g., more objects, varied shapes, and textures) on the generalization ability of the denoising network?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses a reduced set of 20 YCB objects, but does not analyze how object diversity impacts the model's ability to generalize to unseen objects or scenes.
- Why unresolved: The dataset composition and its influence on network robustness and adaptability to new objects or environments is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating models on datasets with increasing object diversity, testing on novel objects, and measuring performance degradation or improvement.

## Limitations
- Calibration approach assumes fixed sensor mounting, making it sensitive to mechanical shifts or vibrations that could invalidate the pre-computed extrinsic transformation
- Masking pipeline relies on clean background assumptions (flat, distinct surfaces with sufficient depth contrast), limiting applicability to complex or cluttered scenes
- Data augmentation is constrained by small transformation bounds (10 cm translation, 5° rotation) to avoid physical inconsistencies, potentially limiting training diversity

## Confidence
- Calibration mechanism (High): The fixed extrinsic transformation approach is well-justified and experimentally validated through consistent results across acquisitions
- Point cloud augmentation (Medium): While the 3D augmentation preserves physical consistency, the limited transformation bounds may restrict its effectiveness in highly diverse scenarios
- Masking effectiveness (Medium): The two-stage clustering approach shows good performance on controlled datasets but may struggle with complex backgrounds or overlapping objects

## Next Checks
1. Test calibration robustness: Intentionally introduce small mechanical shifts between sensors and measure the impact on depth alignment quality
2. Evaluate masking on complex scenes: Apply the masking pipeline to scenes with cluttered backgrounds or multiple overlapping objects to assess failure modes
3. Expand augmentation range: Gradually increase transformation bounds beyond the current limits and analyze the trade-off between training diversity and physical consistency preservation
