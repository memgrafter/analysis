# Towards a Better Understanding of Representation Dynamics under TD-learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18491
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18491
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: End-to-end TD-learning decreases value approximation error when environments are reversible

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a theoretical analysis of representation dynamics under end-to-end TD-learning, where both representations and weights are jointly learned. The authors show that when environments are reversible, end-to-end TD-learning strictly decreases value approximation error over time, improving upon classic TD-learning with fixed representations. They also establish connections between the representation dynamics and spectral decomposition over the transition matrix, demonstrating that fitting multiple value functions from randomly generated rewards can be a useful auxiliary task for representation learning.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes end-to-end linear TD-learning where both the representation matrix Φ and weight matrix w are jointly updated using semi-gradient descent. Under reversibility assumptions on the Markov chain, the authors prove that the value approximation error decreases over time. They further show that under additional conditions (symmetric transition matrix, random rewards), the representation learning dynamics can be interpreted as gradient-based spectral decomposition. Empirical validation on Atari games demonstrates marginal improvements from using random value predictions as an auxiliary task.

## Key Results
- End-to-end TD-learning decreases value approximation error when environments are reversible
- Representation dynamics connect to spectral decomposition of transition matrix under symmetry assumptions
- Random value predictions as auxiliary task provides marginal gains over tuned DQN baselines on Atari games

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: End-to-end TD-learning decreases value approximation error over time when environments are reversible.
- Mechanism: Under reversibility (DπPπ = (Pπ)ᵀDπ), the end-to-end linear TD dynamics becomes gradient descent on the weighted value approximation error E(Φ,w).
- Core assumption: Markov chain is reversible.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "when the environments are reversible, end-to-end TD-learning strictly decreases the value approximation error over time"
  - [section 4]: Theorem 3 shows the dynamics is effectively gradient descent on E(Φ,w) under reversibility
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus neighbors, weak signal
- Break condition: If the Markov chain is not reversible, the error may not decrease monotonically.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: End-to-end linear TD learns useful representations by executing gradient-based spectral decomposition on the transition matrix.
- Mechanism: Under two time-scale dynamics with symmetric Pπ and Rπ(Rπ)ᵀ = I, the trace objective f(Φ) = Tr(Φᵀ(I-γPπ)⁻¹Φ) is non-decreasing, and critical points span invariant subspaces of Pπ.
- Core assumption: Transition matrix Pπ is symmetric and Rπ(Rπ)ᵀ = I (or approximately true with many random rewards).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the representation dynamics can be connected to spectral decomposition over the transition matrix"
  - [section 5]: Theorem 8 and Corollary 9 establish this connection under the stated assumptions
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus neighbors, weak signal
- Break condition: If Pπ is not symmetric or the reward condition doesn't hold, the trace objective may not increase.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fitting multiple value functions from randomly generated rewards is a useful auxiliary task for representation learning.
- Mechanism: When entries of each reward column are sampled i.i.d. from zero-mean distributions, as the number of rewards h increases, Rπ(Rπ)ᵀ approaches σ²I, satisfying the conditions for Mechanism 2.
- Core assumption: Each reward column is sampled i.i.d. from zero-mean distributions with finite variance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "fitting multiple value functions from randomly generated rewards as a useful auxiliary task for representation learning"
  - [section 5.4]: Lemma 10 shows Rπ(Rπ)ᵀ → σ²I as h → ∞ for i.i.d. rewards
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus neighbors, weak signal
- Break condition: If rewards are not sampled independently or have non-zero mean, the approximation may not hold.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Reversible Markov chains (detailed balance condition)
  - Why needed here: The reversibility assumption (DπPπ = (Pπ)ᵀDπ) is critical for Theorem 3 to hold
  - Quick check question: If Pπ is symmetric and the stationary distribution is uniform, is the chain reversible?

- Concept: Spectral decomposition and PCA
  - Why needed here: The paper connects representation learning to spectral decomposition of the transition matrix (Theorem 8)
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between maximizing Tr(ΦᵀAΦ) and finding top eigenvectors of A?

- Concept: Two-time scale stochastic approximation
  - Why needed here: The two-time scale dynamics (ηΦ ≪ ηw) is used to derive the representation learning results
  - Quick check question: What happens to the dynamics if both learning rates are equal vs. very different?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MDP (states, actions, rewards) -> Transition matrix Pπ and reward matrix Rπ -> Representation matrix Φ -> Weight matrix w -> Value function estimates

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize Φ and w
  2. Sample transitions from Pπ
  3. Update w using TD target
  4. Update Φ using semi-gradient of L(Φ,w)
  5. Monitor E(Φ,w) and f(Φ) for convergence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single vs. multiple reward functions (h=1 vs h>1)
  - Symmetric vs. general Pπ
  - Fixed vs. adaptive learning rates for Φ and w
  - Orthonormal vs. unconstrained Φ initialization

- Failure signatures:
  - E(Φ,w) not decreasing despite reversibility
  - f(Φ) not increasing despite symmetric Pπ and random rewards
  - Φ columns collapsing to same direction
  - Oscillations in Φ or w updates

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify E(Φ,w) decreases under reversibility assumption with varying learning rates
  2. Test f(Φ) increase with symmetric Pπ and varying number of random rewards h
  3. Compare representation quality (eigenvector alignment) between single and multiple reward settings

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the end-to-end TD-learning dynamics converge to the representation that minimizes the weighted value approximation error among all critical points?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Theorem 3 shows the weighted value approximation error decreases over time, but does not address whether it converges to the minimum error critical point.
- Why unresolved: The paper states "Answering such questions requires a refined analysis of the dynamics, which we leave to future work."
- What evidence would resolve it: A rigorous proof showing the convergence of the error function to the minimum error critical point, or counterexamples demonstrating non-convergence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the representation learned by end-to-end TD-learning compare to representations learned by other representation learning methods (e.g., Proto-value Functions, successor representations)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows connections between the representation dynamics and spectral decomposition, and mentions prior work on good fixed representations, but does not compare to other representation learning methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on theoretical analysis of end-to-end TD-learning dynamics and does not include empirical comparisons to other representation learning approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the representations learned by end-to-end TD-learning to those learned by other methods on various tasks, evaluating their performance and properties.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of the random value prediction auxiliary task vary with the choice of reward distribution and the number of random rewards?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the use of randomly sampled rewards as an auxiliary task and provides some ablation studies, but does not extensively explore the impact of different reward distributions and numbers of rewards.
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions some empirical findings but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the impact of these factors.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive empirical studies varying the reward distribution and number of random rewards, evaluating the performance of the auxiliary task across different tasks and environments.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis limited to linear function approximation, not applicable to deep RL directly
- Reversible Markov chain assumption rarely holds in practical RL problems
- Symmetric transition matrix assumption for spectral decomposition connection is strong and unrealistic

## Confidence
- Error reduction under reversibility: Medium
- Spectral decomposition connection: Low
- Practical utility of random value predictions: Low
