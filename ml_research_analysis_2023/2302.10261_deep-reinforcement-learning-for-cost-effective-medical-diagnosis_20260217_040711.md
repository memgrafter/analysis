# Deep Reinforcement Learning for Cost-Effective Medical Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.10261
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10261
- **Reference count**: 37
- **Primary result**: Proposes SM-DDPO framework achieving up to 85% reduction in testing cost while maintaining state-of-the-art diagnostic accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses cost-effective medical diagnosis by formulating it as a multi-objective RL problem to maximize F1 score while minimizing testing costs. The authors develop a reward shaping approach that leverages F1 score monotonicity and policy optimization duality to provably find Pareto-optimal policies. They propose SM-DDPO, a semi-model-based framework with three modules: posterior state encoder, classifier, and panel selector. Tested on three clinical tasks, SM-DDPO achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with significant cost reduction compared to baselines.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a Semi-Model-based Deep Diagnosis Policy Optimization (SM-DDPO) framework that addresses cost-effective medical diagnosis through multi-objective reinforcement learning. The framework consists of three modules: an EMFlow-based posterior state encoder for handling partially observed data, a 3-layer DNN classifier for diagnosis prediction, and a PPO-based test panel selector for choosing which tests to administer. The method combines pretraining, policy update, and model-based RL components to efficiently train in high-dimensional, combinatorially complex state spaces while provably finding all Pareto-optimal policies through reward shaping that transforms the non-concave F1 score optimization into tractable cumulative-reward MDPs.

## Key Results
- SM-DDPO achieves up to 85% reduction in testing cost compared to baselines
- Maintains state-of-the-art diagnostic accuracy (F1 score and AUROC) across three clinical tasks
- Provably finds all Pareto-optimal policies through reward shaping and duality-based reformulation
- Demonstrates effective performance on ferritin abnormality detection, sepsis mortality prediction, and acute kidney injury diagnosis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The F1 score's monotonicity in true positives and true negatives allows reformulation of the multi-objective optimization as a constraint-based problem.
- **Mechanism**: The F1 score is monotonic in both TP and TN. For any cost budget B, the optimal policy maximizing F1 is also optimal for a constrained problem that fixes the TP level and maximizes TN. This reformulation makes the problem tractable by reducing it to a constrained linear program.
- **Core assumption**: F1(π) increases monotonically with both TP(π) and TN(π).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "Step 1: utilizing monotonicity of F1 score To start with, we note that F1 score is monotonically increasing in both TP and TN."
  - [abstract] "However, optimizing the non-concave F1 score is not a classic RL problem, thus invalidates standard RL methods. To remedy this issue, we develop a reward shaping approach..."
- **Break condition**: If the F1 score were not monotonic in TP or TN, this reformulation would fail and the subsequent duality-based solution would not hold.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The duality of policy optimization allows conversion of the constrained F1 maximization into a collection of unconstrained cumulative-reward MDPs.
- **Mechanism**: By reformulating the constrained optimization in terms of occupancy measures and applying minimax duality, the problem becomes a max-min program. Swapping the min and max operations yields a collection of standard RL problems with reshaped rewards, each corresponding to a specific (λ, ρ) pair.
- **Core assumption**: The max-min objective is linear in λ, ρ, and µ, allowing minimax duality to hold.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "Step 3: utilizing hidden minimax duality... Note the max-min objective is linear in terms of λ, ρ and µ. Thus, minimax duality holds..."
  - [abstract] "...we develop a reward shaping approach, leveraging properties of the F1 score and duality of policy optimization..."
- **Break condition**: If the objective were non-linear in the dual variables or if minimax duality did not hold, the conversion to standard RL problems would not be valid.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The modular architecture with semi-model-based updates enables efficient training in high-dimensional, combinatorially complex state spaces.
- **Mechanism**: The SM-DDPO framework separates the policy into a posterior state encoder, a classifier, and a panel selector. The classifier is trained directly on cross-entropy loss from adaptively collected data, while the panel selector is updated via PPO using a clipped surrogate objective. This hybrid approach reduces the complexity of the state-action space.
- **Core assumption**: The state embedding captures sufficient information for both classification and action selection, and the semi-model-based approach converges to optimal policies.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "We propose a Semi-Model-based Deep Diagnostic Policy Optimization (SM-DDPO) framework... Its architecture comprises three modules and can be trained efficiently by combining pretraining, policy update, and model-based RL."
  - [abstract] "...we propose a Semi-Model-based Deep Diagnosis Policy Optimization (SM-DDPO) framework that is compatible with end-to-end training and online learning."
- **Break condition**: If the state embedding fails to capture relevant information or if the semi-model-based updates do not converge, the policy performance would degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Patient observation → State embedding → Classifier output → Panel selector action → Next observation (or diagnosis)
- **Critical path**: Patient observation → State embedding → Classifier output → Panel selector action → Next observation (or diagnosis)
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Modular vs. end-to-end training: Modular approach reduces complexity but may limit information flow between components
  - Pre-training vs. online learning: Pre-training the imputer and classifier speeds up convergence but requires labeled data; online learning is more flexible but slower
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor imputation quality leading to uninformative state embeddings
  - Classifier not improving during RL training, indicating insufficient exploration or reward signal
  - Panel selector converging to suboptimal policies due to exploration-exploitation imbalance
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train the EMFlow imputer on the training set and evaluate imputation quality (RMSE) on a held-out validation set
  2. Train the classifier on static data (randomly masked observations) and evaluate F1 score and AUROC
  3. Run SM-DDPO with a small number of RL iterations and visualize the learned cost-F1 Pareto front to check if policies are being discovered

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Can the reward shaping approach for finding the Pareto front be extended to handle more than two conflicting objectives (e.g., accuracy, cost, and testing time)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors develop a reward shaping approach leveraging properties of the F1 score and policy optimization duality to find Pareto-optimal policies for budget-constrained F1 score maximization. They mention that their approach directly applies to linear metrics like the AM score.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only considers a bi-objective optimization problem (maximizing F1 score and minimizing cost). It is unclear whether the proposed reward shaping approach can be generalized to handle three or more conflicting objectives.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Developing and testing a reward shaping approach for multi-objective RL with more than two conflicting objectives, and comparing its performance to existing multi-objective RL methods.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does the performance of the proposed SM-DDPO framework compare to other deep RL methods for dynamic feature selection, such as Q-learning for classification with costly features (CWCF)?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors compare SM-DDPO to CWCF and show that CWCF performs poorly on all three clinical datasets. However, the comparison is limited to a single baseline method.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison of SM-DDPO to other deep RL methods for dynamic feature selection, making it difficult to assess its relative performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conducting a thorough comparison of SM-DDPO to multiple deep RL methods for dynamic feature selection, using the same datasets and evaluation metrics.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the proposed SM-DDPO framework be adapted to handle sequential decision-making problems with continuous state and action spaces, such as robotics control tasks?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors propose a semi-model-based deep RL framework that combines pretraining, policy update, and model-based RL. This modular architecture could potentially be adapted to handle continuous state and action spaces.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only demonstrates the performance of SM-DDPO on clinical diagnosis tasks with discrete state and action spaces. It is unclear whether the framework can be effectively adapted to handle continuous state and action spaces.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Adapting SM-DDPO to a continuous control task, such as a robotics control problem, and evaluating its performance compared to existing deep RL methods for continuous control.

## Limitations
- Theoretical claims about F1 score monotonicity and minimax duality rely on idealized assumptions that may not hold in noisy medical data
- Claim of "provably finding all Pareto-optimal policies" depends critically on theoretical foundations with limited empirical validation
- Semi-model-based approach introduces additional approximation layers that could compound errors

## Confidence

**Confidence labels**:
- **High confidence**: The modular architecture design and PPO-based training approach are standard and well-validated in RL literature
- **Medium confidence**: The reward shaping approach and duality-based reformulation appear theoretically sound, but their practical effectiveness depends on proper implementation and data quality
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical results showing 85% cost reduction are impressive but need replication on additional datasets to establish generalizability

## Next Checks

1. **Theoretical validation**: Verify the monotonicity assumptions for F1 score on the specific clinical datasets used, as medical data often contains noise and imbalanced classes that could violate these assumptions
2. **Ablation study**: Test the necessity of each module by comparing against baselines that remove the semi-model-based components or use different state encodings
3. **Generalization test**: Apply SM-DDPO to an additional clinical dataset not used in the original paper to assess robustness and identify potential overfitting to the three specific tasks
