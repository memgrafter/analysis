# GPT-Calls: Enhancing Call Segmentation and Tagging by Generating Synthetic Conversations via Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07941
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07941
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: 12% relative improvement in Pk score and 8% in WinDiff score for Sports dataset compared to second-best method

## Executive Summary
GPT-Calls introduces a novel method for call segmentation and topic extraction that leverages GPT-3 to generate synthetic conversations for a given set of topics. The approach combines offline synthetic data generation with online similarity scoring and temporal smoothing to achieve accurate segmentation without requiring labeled training data. The method demonstrates superior performance compared to state-of-the-art alternatives across multiple domains including Sports, Gaming, and Insurance.

## Method Summary
GPT-Calls operates in two phases: offline and online. In the offline phase, GPT-3 generates thousands of semantically correlated sentences for each topic, which are then embedded using SBERT and clustered with DBSCAN to extract representative anchor vectors. During the online phase, incoming call transcripts are processed by embedding each utterance with SBERT, scoring its similarity to all topic anchors using cosine similarity, and converting these scores to probabilities via softmax. A heat diffusion strategy then smooths these probabilities across consecutive utterances, followed by a sliding window approach to identify segment boundaries based on cumulative topic probabilities.

## Key Results
- Achieved 12% relative improvement in Pk score and 8% in WinDiff score for Sports dataset compared to second-best method
- Outperformed TextTiling, BERT-TSeg, and GSGST across multiple datasets and domains
- Showed largest improvement in Identification topic during topic-specific evaluations

- Concept: Sentence embeddings via SBERT
  - Why needed here: To map utterances and synthetic sentences into shared semantic vector space for similarity scoring

- Concept: Density-based clustering (DBSCAN)
  - Why needed here: To extract representative anchors from topic-related sentences without assuming fixed number of clusters
  - Quick check question: What happens if DBSCAN finds no dense regions in synthetic sentence embeddings?

- Concept: Sliding window segmentation
  - Why needed here: To group consecutive utterances into segments based on cumulative topic probabilities
  - Quick check question: How does changing window width affect false positive vs false negative segmentation errors?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GPT-3 prompt generator → Synthetic sentence pool → SBERT → DBSCAN → Topic anchor extraction → Online: SBERT → Utterance embeddings → Cosine similarity engine → Heat diffusion module → Sliding window + thresholding → Final segmentation

- Critical path: 1. Generate synthetic sentences for each topic 2. Embed and cluster to extract anchors 3. For each call: embed utterances → score vs anchors → smooth via heat diffusion → apply sliding windows → output segmentation

- Design tradeoffs: Prompt quality vs diversity in synthetic data, SBERT model choice vs inference latency, Heat diffusion parameters vs oversmoothing vs sensitivity, Window size vs segment granularity

- Failure signatures: Synthetic sentences are repetitive or off-topic → poor anchors, Cosine similarity scores are uniformly low → weak topic discrimination, Heat diffusion creates overly long segments → loss of topic boundaries, Sliding window thresholds too high → many short segments

- First 3 experiments: 1. Ablate GPT-3: Replace synthetic sentences with curated anchor set; measure drop in segmentation accuracy 2. Ablate heat diffusion: Run segmentation without smoothing; compare Pk and WinDiff scores 3. Vary SBERT model size: Test compact vs full model; measure trade-off between accuracy and latency

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on GPT-3 for synthetic data generation without prompt sensitivity analysis or robustness testing across domains
- Heat diffusion mechanism lacks theoretical grounding and empirical validation with arbitrary parameter choices
- Evaluation limited to synthetic datasets (Sports, Gaming) and single real-world domain (Insurance), limiting generalizability claims

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Overall architecture is sound and follows established NLP patterns (embedding, clustering, similarity scoring). Mathematical formulation of sliding window segmentation is clear and reproducible.

**Medium confidence**: Performance improvements over baselines demonstrated on tested datasets, but magnitude may be dataset-dependent rather than method-dependent.

**Low confidence**: Claims about heat diffusion effectiveness, prompt engineering robustness, and cross-domain applicability lack sufficient empirical support. Method's behavior under realistic operational conditions (noisy transcription, diverse accents, domain shifts) is not characterized.

## Next Checks

1. **Prompt sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary GPT-3 prompts for synthetic sentence generation across different formulations and measure impact on segmentation accuracy to reveal robustness to prompt engineering variations.

2. **Cross-domain anchor transferability**: Train anchors on one domain (e.g., Sports) and evaluate segmentation performance on different domain (e.g., Technical Support) without domain-specific fine-tuning to measure domain generalization limits.

3. **Heat diffusion parameter sensitivity**: Conduct grid search over diffusion parameters (diffusion strength, window size, decay rate) and measure trade-off between segmentation precision and recall to identify optimal parameter ranges and characterize failure modes.
