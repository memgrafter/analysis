## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HEROS, a novel dataset designed to evaluate sentence encoders by examining their behavior on sentence pairs with high lexical overlap. Unlike traditional semantic textual similarity benchmarks, HEROS reveals blind spots in how SEs perceive sentence similarity through systematic transformations like synonym/antonym replacement, typos, random words, and negation. By comparing over 60 supervised and unsupervised SEs on HEROS, the authors demonstrate that most unsupervised models are insensitive to negation and that training data significantly influences SE behavior. This work highlights the limitations of existing evaluation methods and provides a more nuanced understanding of SE capabilities.

## Method Summary
The HEROS dataset is constructed by transforming sentences into minimal pairs using controlled rule-based operations. The authors systematically apply transformations including synonym replacement, antonym replacement, typo insertion, random word addition, and negation to create sentence pairs that test specific aspects of semantic understanding. The dataset contains 2,100 sentence pairs carefully designed to probe how different SEs handle various types of semantic changes. The evaluation methodology involves computing similarity scores for these pairs across 60+ SE models and analyzing their behavior patterns, particularly focusing on how they respond to different transformation types.

## Key Results
- Most unsupervised sentence encoders show significant insensitivity to negation, treating negated and non-negated sentences as highly similar
- The training data composition strongly influences how sentence encoders perceive similarity, with models trained on certain data types showing predictable blind spots
- Traditional STS benchmarks fail to capture these behavioral patterns, making HEROS a more comprehensive evaluation tool for understanding SE limitations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HEROS works by creating controlled minimal pairs that isolate specific linguistic phenomena while maintaining high lexical overlap. This design forces sentence encoders to rely on semantic understanding rather than surface-level features. The systematic transformation rules create a spectrum of semantic changes from minimal to substantial, allowing researchers to map the boundaries of what each SE considers similar. By maintaining high lexical overlap, HEROS eliminates the confounding factor of vocabulary differences, focusing evaluation purely on semantic understanding.

## Limitations

- The dataset construction relies on controlled rule-based transformations that may not fully capture real-world sentence modifications
- The 2,100 sentence pairs represent a relatively small sample of possible semantic variations
- The evaluation focuses primarily on lexical overlap and specific transformation types, potentially overlooking other semantic aspects
