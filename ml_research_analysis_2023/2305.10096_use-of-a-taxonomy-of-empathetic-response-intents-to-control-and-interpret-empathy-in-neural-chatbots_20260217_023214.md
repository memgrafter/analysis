# Use of a Taxonomy of Empathetic Response Intents to Control and Interpret Empathy in Neural Chatbots

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10096
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10096
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Taxonomy-based approaches improve empathetic response quality over end-to-end models

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a taxonomy of eight empathetic response intents plus 32 emotion categories to control and interpret empathy in chatbot responses. The approach separates response emotion/intent prediction from response generation, using either rule-based decision trees or neural predictors to classify the appropriate emotion and intent for the next response. Experimental results on two dialogue datasets demonstrate that conditioning response generation on predicted emotion/intent labels produces more diverse and empathetically appropriate responses compared to end-to-end models.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two modules: a response emotion/intent prediction module and a response generation module. For prediction, the authors propose both rule-based decision tree approaches (argmax and probabilistic sampling) and a neural BERT-based predictor. The decision trees leverage observed emotion-intent flow patterns from training data, while the neural predictor uses semantic content through BERT embeddings. For generation, a transformer encoder-decoder architecture produces responses conditioned on the predicted emotion and intent labels. The approach is evaluated on EmpatheticDialogues and OpenSubtitles datasets using both automatic metrics (precision, recall, F1, perplexity, diversity) and human evaluation.

## Key Results
- The neural response emotion/intent predictor outperforms both rule-based approaches and end-to-end models in prediction accuracy metrics
- Using the taxonomy of empathetic response intents improves response diversity and empathetic appropriateness compared to end-to-end models
- Rule-based decision tree approaches achieve competitive performance, especially in resource-limited environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The taxonomy of eight empathetic response intents plus 32 emotion categories improves response quality over end-to-end models
- Mechanism: By conditioning response generation on both predicted emotion and intent labels, the model gains controllability and interpretability, allowing it to produce more empathetic and contextually appropriate responses
- Core assumption: The taxonomy accurately captures the range of empathetic responses humans use in emotional conversations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The automatic and human evaluation results emphasize the importance of the use of the taxonomy of empathetic response intents in producing more diverse and empathetically more appropriate responses than end-to-end models."
  - [section]: "It was observed that the neural response emotion/intent predictor we proposed outperformed the rest including the end-to-end model in terms of evaluation metrics related to both prediction and generation."
- Break condition: If the taxonomy does not align with actual human empathetic response patterns, or if the predicted labels are frequently inaccurate, the conditioning will not improve response quality

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Rule-based decision tree approaches can achieve performance comparable to neural approaches in predicting response emotions/intents, especially in resource-limited environments
- Mechanism: Decision trees leverage observed emotion-intent flow patterns from training data to predict the next response's emotion or intent based on the sequence of previous dialogue turns
- Core assumption: The emotion-intent flow patterns in training data are representative and stable enough to generalize to new conversations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The DT (argmax) method had considerable improvement in recall, F1, and accuracy scores over the equally sampled baseline."
  - [section]: "In the human evaluation stage, we saw that the DT (argmax) method produced the most number of combined Good and Okay responses in ED and EDOS test datasets."
- Break condition: If the decision trees become too deep or the emotion-intent flow patterns in new data differ significantly from training data, the performance will degrade

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Neural response emotion/intent predictors outperform rule-based approaches by leveraging semantic content of dialogue turns in addition to emotion-intent flow
- Mechanism: A BERT-based encoder with attention layers and emotion/intent embeddings as additional input features can capture both the semantic content and the flow of emotions and intents to predict the next response's emotion or intent
- Core assumption: The semantic content of dialogue turns contains useful signals for predicting the appropriate emotion or intent of the next response
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The neural predictor achieves the best performance, while rule-based approaches provide a viable alternative when resources are limited."
  - [section]: "According to the weighted precision, recall, F1, and accuracy scores, the neural emotion/intent predictor performed the best compared to other prediction methods."
- Break condition: If the BERT model is not pre-trained on relevant data or the attention mechanism fails to properly aggregate token representations, the performance will suffer

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dialogue context → Response emotion/intent prediction → Response generation conditioned on predicted labels → Empathetic response output
- Critical path: Dialogue context → Response emotion/intent prediction → Response generation conditioned on predicted labels → Empathetic response output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Rule-based vs. neural prediction: Rule-based is interpretable and resource-efficient but may not capture all nuances; neural is more accurate but requires more resources
  - Emotion/intent granularity: Using a taxonomy with more categories allows for finer control but may increase prediction difficulty
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor prediction performance: Inaccurate or irrelevant emotion/intent labels lead to inappropriate responses
  - Repetitive responses: Lack of diversity in generated responses
  - Uninterpretable responses: Generated responses do not align with the predicted emotion/intent labels

## Limitations

- The method relies on a pre-trained emotion/intent classifier (65.88% accuracy) that may contain systematic biases or errors
- Decision tree approaches assume stable emotion-intent flow patterns that may not generalize to new conversation domains
- The neural predictor requires substantial computational resources and training data, limiting practical deployment in resource-constrained environments

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall framework of separating emotion/intent prediction from response generation is technically sound and the experimental methodology (including both automatic and human evaluations) is rigorous
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific performance gains of the neural predictor over rule-based approaches, while demonstrated, may be somewhat dataset-dependent
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the eight empathetic response intents taxonomy to real-world conversational scenarios beyond the specific datasets used
