# MO-DEHB: Evolutionary-based Hyperband for Multi-Objective Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04502
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04502
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Extends DEHB with multi-objective optimization for HPO and NAS

## Executive Summary
MO-DEHB addresses the challenge of optimizing multiple conflicting objectives in hyperparameter optimization and neural architecture search problems. The method extends the DEHB algorithm by incorporating multi-objective optimization techniques, specifically using non-dominated sorting with either NSGA-II or EpsNet for ranking solutions, and a dominated hypervolume contribution strategy for selection. When evaluated on 15 diverse benchmarks spanning NAS, joint NAS & HPO, and algorithmic fairness domains, MO-DEHB variants consistently outperform state-of-the-art baselines, achieving superior performance across most benchmarks.

## Method Summary
MO-DEHB extends the DEHB framework by incorporating multi-objective optimization techniques. It uses non-dominated sorting (NDS) with either NSGA-II or EpsNet for ranking solutions, and a dominated hypervolume contribution strategy for selection. The method adapts both the Hyperband component (using NDS to rank configurations across fidelity levels) and the DE search component (using NDS and HV contribution in selection strategy) to handle multiple objectives. MO-DEHB maintains diversity in Pareto front approximations while balancing exploration and exploitation across different fidelity levels.

## Key Results
- MO-DEHB variants consistently outperform state-of-the-art baselines on 15 diverse benchmarks
- Achieves superior performance across most benchmarks spanning NAS, joint NAS & HPO, and algorithmic fairness domains
- Demonstrates excellent anytime performance and establishes new state-of-the-art in multi-objective HPO and NAS optimization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Non-dominated sorting with hypervolume contribution effectively balances exploration and exploitation across fidelity levels. MO-DEHB ranks configurations using NDS to create fronts, then within each front uses dominated hypervolume contribution to select candidates for evolution. This ensures that both convergence toward the Pareto front and diversity along it are maintained while leveraging multi-fidelity evaluations. Core assumption: Configurations that contribute least to the hypervolume in a front are the least promising for approximating the Pareto front trade-offs.

### Mechanism 2
Extending Hyperband with multi-objective ranking preserves the anytime performance advantage while handling multiple objectives. MO-DEHB adapts the Hyperband component by using NDS (with NSGA-II or EpsNet) to rank configurations at each fidelity level instead of single-objective ranking. This allows the algorithm to select diverse, non-dominated configurations for promotion across fidelity levels. Core assumption: The parent pool constructed from NDS-based ranking will contain configurations that are both high-performing and diverse across objectives.

### Mechanism 3
Differential Evolution with NDS-based selection maintains evolutionary pressure while respecting Pareto dominance. MO-DEHB extends the DE search component by using NDS to determine front rankings of offspring vs. parents, and only replacing parents if offspring are on better fronts or contribute more to hypervolume when on the same front. Core assumption: Configurations on higher NDS fronts are meaningfully better trade-offs than those on lower fronts, and hypervolume contribution within fronts identifies the least useful configurations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Multi-objective Hyperband wrapper -> Fidelity-specific DE sub-populations -> Global population -> Evaluation manager -> Selection logic
- **Critical path**: Initialize SH bracket with random configurations at lowest fidelity → Evaluate and rank using NDS + crowding/EpsNet → Select top configurations for promotion to next fidelity → Evolve each fidelity population using DE with MO-selection → Forward evolved populations to next SH bracket → Repeat until highest fidelity reached, then start new iteration
- **Design tradeoffs**: NDS vs. scalarization (NDS handles complex Pareto geometries but is computationally heavier; scalarization is faster but may miss non-convex regions); NSGA-II vs. EpsNet for within-front ranking (NSGA-II uses crowding distance geometric, EpsNet uses iterative distance maximization distribution-based); Population size vs. computational budget (larger populations improve exploration but increase evaluation costs)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor anytime performance (indicates ranking/selecting configurations too conservatively or having expensive ranking operations); Loss of diversity (suggests HV contribution or within-front ranking isn't maintaining spread across objectives); Slow convergence (may indicate DE parameters need tuning for MO setting)
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Implement MO-DEHB NSGA-II variant on NAS-Bench-101 with two objectives using small population sizes to verify basic functionality; 2) Compare anytime performance against single-objective DEHB baseline on same benchmark to validate multi-objective extension; 3) Test sensitivity to population size by running with small, medium, and large populations on NAS-Bench-1shot1 to find sweet spot between exploration and computational cost

## Limitations
- Computational scalability concerns with hypervolume contribution calculations in high-dimensional objective spaces
- Performance may degrade with more than 2-3 objectives due to exponential complexity of HV computation
- Reliance on DEHB framework means inheriting any limitations of that foundation

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of extending Hyperband with NDS-based ranking is sound and well-supported
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of HV contribution for within-front selection is plausible but lacks direct empirical validation
- **Low Confidence**: The claim of establishing "new state-of-the-art" performance is based on LogHVDiff metric but doesn't account for computational efficiency differences
