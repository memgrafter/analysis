# DATED: Guidelines for Creating Synthetic Datasets for Engineering Design Applications

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09018
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09018
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes guidelines for creating synthetic datasets for engineering design applications, demonstrating effectiveness through a centrifugal compressor case study

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of data scarcity in engineering design applications by proposing a comprehensive framework for creating synthetic datasets. The authors introduce various sampling methods including random, space-filling, data-driven, active-learning, data augmentation, and latent-space sampling to generate diverse and realistic design data. A case study on centrifugal compressor design demonstrates that combining diverse and augmented sampling improves dataset quality and machine learning performance, with 10^6 samples achieving good classification results. The work provides a structured approach to balance diversity, realism, and task-specific relevance in synthetic datasets.

## Method Summary
The method involves defining a design space with 21 features for centrifugal compressors and using computational mean-line analysis to generate performance labels. Three sampling strategies were employed: random sampling (19M samples), data augmentation of 14 real designs with Gaussian noise (1.37M samples), and restricted random sampling in specific regions (1M samples). The combined dataset was used to train an AutoGluon classifier for working vs. non-working compressor classification. Multiple test sets were created to evaluate performance across different characteristics, and feature importance was analyzed using SHAP values.

## Key Results
- Combining diverse and augmented sampling methods improves ML model performance compared to random sampling alone
- 10^6 samples with mixed sampling strategies achieved good F1 scores on classification tasks
- Design diversity does not equate to performance diversity or realism, necessitating balanced sampling objectives
- Multiple test sets with different characteristics provide comprehensive evaluation of dataset quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Synthetic data generation with diverse sampling methods improves ML model performance on engineering design tasks compared to random sampling alone.
- Mechanism: The paper proposes combining different sampling strategies (random, space-filling, data-driven, active-learning, data augmentation, and latent-space sampling) to create datasets that better cover both design and performance spaces while maintaining realism.
- Core assumption: Different sampling methods capture complementary aspects of the design space, and their combination provides better coverage than any single method.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] states that combining diverse and augmented sampling improves dataset quality and ML performance

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Including realism as a sampling objective improves model performance on real-world designs.
- Mechanism: The paper emphasizes that datasets should not only be diverse in design and performance space but also realistic, meaning they should reflect the characteristics of real-world data.
- Core assumption: Real-world designs follow specific patterns and biases that synthetic data should replicate to ensure good generalization.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] highlights that "design diversity does not equate to performance diversity or realism"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using multiple test sets with different characteristics provides a more comprehensive evaluation of dataset quality and model performance.
- Mechanism: The paper recommends creating test sets that are similar to training data, diverse in design space, diverse in performance space, similar to real designs, composed of designs outside the design space, and task-specific.
- Core assumption: Different downstream applications may require different characteristics from the dataset, and a single test set cannot capture all relevant performance aspects.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions employing "test sets that represent uniform, real, or task-specific samples"

- Component map: Design representation -> Data generation -> Annotation -> Validation -> Performance evaluation
- Critical path: Design representation → Data generation → Annotation → Validation → Performance evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: Diversity vs. realism: More diverse datasets may be less realistic, while realistic datasets may lack diversity
- Failure signatures: Poor ML performance despite large dataset size; High imbalance between working and non-working designs
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Create a small synthetic dataset using random sampling and train a simple ML model to establish a baseline performance
  2. Apply space-filling sampling (e.g., latin hypercube) to the same design space and compare ML performance
  3. Generate a dataset using data augmentation on real designs and evaluate how it affects model performance on both synthetic and real test sets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the trade-off between design diversity, performance diversity, and realism be optimally balanced when creating synthetic datasets for engineering design?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that design diversity does not equate to performance diversity or realism
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the problem but does not provide a definitive solution or method to optimally balance these three aspects
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the performance of machine learning models trained on datasets with different balances of design diversity, performance diversity, and realism

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the minimum sample size required to achieve satisfactory machine learning performance on specialized test sets that evaluate the edge of the working domain?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper's case study on centrifugal compressor design shows that while 10^6 samples achieved good performance on a uniform test set, performance on specialized test sets did not reach very good levels
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a clear threshold for the minimum sample size required for satisfactory performance on specialized test sets
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments with varying sample sizes and more diverse test sets

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can realism be effectively incorporated as an objective in dataset sampling methods for machine learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that considering realism as an objective for dataset sampling methods means prioritizing data representative of real-world scenarios
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific methods or strategies for incorporating realism as an objective in dataset sampling methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of new sampling methods that explicitly incorporate realism as an objective

## Limitations
- Claims are supported by a single case study on centrifugal compressors, limiting generalizability
- Computational cost analysis of large synthetic dataset generation is incomplete
- Relationship between dataset size and ML performance lacks systematic investigation of diminishing returns
- Validation framework relies heavily on downstream ML performance

## Confidence

- High confidence: The importance of combining diversity, realism, and task-specific relevance in synthetic datasets
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of specific sampling methods (latin hypercube, data augmentation, active learning) for engineering design datasets
- Low confidence: The scalability of proposed guidelines to extremely high-dimensional design spaces and generalizability to domains beyond turbomachinery
