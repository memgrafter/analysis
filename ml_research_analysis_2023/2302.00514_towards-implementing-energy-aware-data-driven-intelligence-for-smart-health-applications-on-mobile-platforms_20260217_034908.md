# Towards Implementing Energy-aware Data-driven Intelligence for Smart Health Applications on Mobile Platforms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00514
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00514
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: EAMCR framework extends operating time by up to 30 minutes through dynamic model and accelerator selection

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Energy-aware, Adaptive Model Comprehension and Realization (EAMCR) framework for deploying deep learning models on mobile devices for smart health applications. The framework dynamically selects between different deep learning models and hardware accelerators (CPU, GPU) based on battery level and user-defined accuracy requirements. Evaluated across three healthcare tasks (eardrum, fingernail, and skin lesion diagnosis), the study demonstrates that EAMCR significantly improves energy efficiency without sacrificing accuracy, making it practical for long-duration use in resource-constrained environments.

## Method Summary
The EAMCR framework implements a dynamic selection mechanism that chooses the optimal deep learning model and hardware accelerator combination based on real-time battery levels and user-specified accuracy requirements. The study evaluates multiple state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks (including EfficientNet, ResNet, and Inception) and conventional machine learning models across three distinct healthcare diagnostic tasks. Model performance and energy consumption are measured across both CPU and GPU hardware configurations, with the framework making runtime decisions to balance accuracy needs against available battery power.

## Key Results
- EfficientNet B0 achieves the best energy efficiency when running on CPU
- Inception networks perform optimally on GPU configurations
- EAMCR framework extends device operating time by up to 30 minutes compared to static model selection approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework's effectiveness stems from its adaptive decision-making process that matches computational intensity to available resources. By dynamically selecting lighter models like EfficientNet B0 when battery levels are low, the system maintains acceptable accuracy while significantly reducing power consumption. Conversely, when battery levels are sufficient, the framework can leverage more computationally intensive models like Inception on GPU for improved accuracy. This dynamic balancing act between model complexity and hardware utilization directly translates to extended operational time without compromising the quality of healthcare diagnostics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dynamic model selection** - why needed: Different healthcare tasks require different accuracy-energy trade-offs; quick check: verify framework correctly switches models based on battery thresholds
- **Hardware accelerator awareness** - why needed: CPU and GPU have fundamentally different energy profiles and performance characteristics; quick check: confirm power measurements align with expected hardware behavior
- **Battery-level monitoring** - why needed: Real-time battery state is critical for making informed decisions about computational intensity; quick check: validate battery level readings match actual device power consumption
- **Multi-task healthcare applications** - why needed: Different medical imaging tasks have varying complexity and accuracy requirements; quick check: ensure model performance meets clinical accuracy thresholds for each task

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Battery Monitor -> Decision Engine -> Model Selector -> Hardware Allocator -> Inference Engine

**Critical Path:**
Battery Monitor (real-time) → Decision Engine (instantaneous) → Model Selector (instantaneous) → Hardware Allocator (instantaneous) → Inference Engine (task-dependent)

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The framework prioritizes battery life extension over maximum possible accuracy when power is limited, accepting minor diagnostic precision reductions to maintain device usability. This trade-off is configurable by users based on their specific needs.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Battery level monitoring failures lead to incorrect model selection
- Decision engine latency can cause suboptimal selections
- Hardware allocation errors may result in models running on inappropriate accelerators

**First Experiments:**
1. Test baseline energy consumption of each model on both CPU and GPU
2. Verify battery level accuracy across different device states
3. Validate decision engine response time under varying computational loads

## Limitations
- Does not address model quantization or pruning techniques for additional energy savings
- Limited to CPU and GPU accelerators, ignoring emerging NPUs and DSPs in modern mobile devices
- Assumes static power profiles without accounting for temperature, battery age, or usage pattern variations
- No consideration of network connectivity impacts or cloud-based fallback scenarios

## Confidence
- High confidence: EfficientNet B0 provides best CPU energy efficiency
- Medium confidence: EAMCR framework's practical utility due to limited real-world testing duration
- Low confidence: Generalizability of results across different mobile hardware platforms

## Next Checks
1. Test EAMCR across multiple device generations (e.g., Snapdragon 8 Gen 1 vs Gen 2) to assess hardware dependency

3. Implement and measure impact of model compression techniques (quantization, pruning) on the proposed framework's energy-accuracy trade-off
