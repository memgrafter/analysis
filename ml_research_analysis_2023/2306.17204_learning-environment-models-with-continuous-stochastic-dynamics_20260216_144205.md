# Learning Environment Models with Continuous Stochastic Dynamics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17204
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17204
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: CASTLE learns MDP models that can compute policies achieving similar or higher rewards than deep RL on classic control tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CASTLE, a novel approach for learning environment models with continuous stochastic dynamics using automata learning. The core idea is to combine dimensionality reduction, clustering, and passive automata learning to learn a Markov decision process (MDP) model of the environment under an agent's control. The approach iteratively refines the learned model by actively sampling new trajectories using policies computed from the model. The authors apply CASTLE to popular reinforcement learning benchmark environments from OpenAI Gym, including LunarLander, CartPole, Mountain Car, and Acrobot. The results show that the learned models are precise enough to compute policies that solve the respective control tasks, with some models even achieving similar or higher rewards than deep RL policies learned with stable-baselines3. The learned models are more concise and general than neural-network-based policies and enable the use of MDP analysis tools.

## Method Summary
CASTLE combines dimensionality reduction, clustering, and passive automata learning to learn a Markov decision process (MDP) model of the environment under an agent's control. The approach starts with passive observation of the environment, followed by dimensionality reduction and clustering of the observed states. An initial MDP model is then learned using automata learning techniques. The learned model is iteratively refined by actively sampling new trajectories using policies computed from the model. This process continues until the model converges or a stopping criterion is met. The resulting MDP model can be used to compute policies that solve the respective control tasks.

## Key Results
- CASTLE learns MDP models that are precise enough to compute policies solving classic control tasks from OpenAI Gym
- The learned models achieve similar or higher rewards compared to deep RL policies learned with stable-baselines3 on some environments
- The learned models are more concise and general than neural-network-based policies, enabling the use of MDP analysis tools

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CASTLE works by combining dimensionality reduction, clustering, and passive automata learning to learn an MDP model of the environment. The iterative refinement process allows the model to capture the essential dynamics of the environment by actively sampling new trajectories based on the current model. The learned MDP model can then be used to compute policies that solve the control tasks, leveraging the model's ability to generalize and reason about the environment's dynamics.

## Limitations
- The evaluation is primarily focused on classic control tasks from OpenAI Gym, which represent relatively simple benchmark environments. The approach's effectiveness on more complex, high-dimensional environments with continuous state spaces remains uncertain.
- The paper does not extensively discuss the computational efficiency of the iterative refinement process or the scalability of the dimensionality reduction and clustering steps for larger state spaces.
- The comparison with deep RL policies is limited to a few examples, and a more comprehensive benchmark against state-of-the-art model-based RL methods would strengthen the claims.
- The learned models are described as more concise and general than neural-network-based policies, but the criteria for this assessment are not clearly defined.
- The paper does not address the potential limitations of the automata learning algorithm in capturing complex stochastic dynamics or handling non-Markovian environments.

## Confidence

- Computational efficiency of iterative refinement: Medium
- Comparison with state-of-the-art model-based RL: Low
