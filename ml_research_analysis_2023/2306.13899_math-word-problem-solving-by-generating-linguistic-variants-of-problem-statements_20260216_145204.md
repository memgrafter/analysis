# Math Word Problem Solving by Generating Linguistic Variants of Problem Statements

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13899
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13899
- Reference count: 28
- Key outcome: The authors propose a method for math word problem solving that generates paraphrased linguistic variants of problem statements, trains a DeBERTa model on these variants, and uses majority voting on candidate predictions, achieving improved accuracy on multiple datasets.

## Executive Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of math word problem (MWP) solving by proposing a method that generates paraphrased linguistic variants of problem statements to improve mathematical reasoning and robustness. The authors introduce PARA MAWPS, a dataset of paraphrased, adversarial, and inverse variants of MWPs from MAWPS. They train a DeBERTa model on these variants and use majority voting on candidate predictions to enhance performance. Experiments show that the proposed approach outperforms baseline models on MAWPS, SVAMP, and PARA MAWPS datasets.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a method for math word problem (MWP) solving that generates paraphrased linguistic variants of problem statements to improve mathematical reasoning and robustness. They introduce PARA MAWPS, a dataset of paraphrased, adversarial, and inverse variants of MWPs from MAWPS. The method involves training a DeBERTa model on these variants and using majority voting on candidate predictions to enhance performance. The authors evaluate their approach on multiple datasets, including MAWPS, SVAMP, and PARA MAWPS, and demonstrate improved accuracy compared to baseline models.

## Key Results
- The DeBERTa model trained on linguistic variants achieves 91.0% accuracy on MAWPS.
- The proposed approach outperforms baseline models on SVAMP (63.5% accuracy) and PARA MAWPS (74.1% accuracy).
- Majority voting on candidate predictions improves mathematical reasoning and robustness.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method works by generating paraphrased linguistic variants of problem statements, which helps the model learn more robust representations and reduces reliance on shallow heuristics and spurious correlations. By training on these variants, the DeBERTa model becomes more resilient to linguistic variations and can generalize better to unseen problem types. The majority voting mechanism further enhances the model's performance by aggregating predictions from multiple candidates, reducing the impact of individual errors.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of the paraphrasing approach relies heavily on the quality and diversity of the generated variants, and the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the paraphrasing quality or potential biases introduced by the paraphrasers.
- The proposed approach focuses on generating paraphrased variants of problem statements but does not explicitly address the semantic preservation of the mathematical meaning, which could introduce unintended changes to the underlying mathematical relationships or ambiguity in the problem statements.
- The evaluation metrics used in the paper are based on accuracy, which provides a high-level assessment of the model's performance but lacks additional metrics such as precision, recall, and F1-score for a more comprehensive understanding of the model's strengths and weaknesses.

## Confidence
- High: The core methodology of generating paraphrased variants and using majority voting over predictions is sound and has the potential to improve MWP solving. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach on the evaluated datasets.
- Medium: The specific implementation details, such as the choice of the paraphrasers, the training process, and the majority voting mechanism, are well-described and contribute to the overall effectiveness of the method.
- Low: The generalization of the proposed approach to other MWP datasets and the robustness of the model to unseen problem types and linguistic variations require further investigation and validation.
