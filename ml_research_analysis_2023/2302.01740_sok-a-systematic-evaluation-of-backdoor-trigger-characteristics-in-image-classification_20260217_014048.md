# SoK: A Systematic Evaluation of Backdoor Trigger Characteristics in Image Classification
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01740
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01740
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents a systematic evaluation of backdoor trigger characteristics in image classification

## Executive Summary
This study systematically evaluates how different trigger characteristics affect backdoor attack effectiveness in image classification models. The authors conduct extensive experiments across three image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR10, TinyImageNet) and four DNN architectures (ResNet, VGG, AlexNet, GoogLeNet), testing various trigger sizes, positions, colors, and poisoning rates. Their findings reveal that trigger size has the most significant impact on attack success rate, outweighing poisoning rate effects, and that AlexNet demonstrates exceptional robustness against backdoor attacks compared to other architectures.

## Method Summary
The study uses transfer learning with pre-trained models (ResNet-152, VGG-19_BN, GoogLeNet, AlexNet), freezing all layers except the last fully connected layer (or up to classifier for AlexNet). Models are trained using Adam optimizer with learning rate 0.001, cross-entropy loss, and 20 epochs. Datasets are resized and converted to RGB (MNIST: 28x28→64x64, CIFAR10: 32x32→128x128, TinyImageNet: 64x64→224x224). Poisoning rates range from 0.5% to 2%, with trigger sizes of 4%, 6%, and 8%, five positions, and three colors (black, white, green). Performance is evaluated using Attack Success Rate (ASR) and Clean Accuracy Drop (CAD).

## Key Results
- Trigger size has the most significant impact on attack success rate, outweighing poisoning rate effects
- AlexNet demonstrates exceptional robustness against backdoor attacks compared to other architectures
- No single trigger configuration universally outperforms others across all settings
- Model capacity significantly influences backdoor vulnerability, with higher-capacity models being more susceptible

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Trigger size has a more significant impact on attack success rate (ASR) than poisoning rate.
- Mechanism: Larger triggers create more distinct visual patterns that the model can learn to associate with the target class, while smaller triggers may be overlooked during training.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively learn to recognize the trigger as a feature regardless of its position.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We find that the trigger size is more influential than the poisoning rate and that the performance of backdoor attacks is affected by factors such as the model architecture and the characteristics of the trigger."
  - [abstract] "Their findings reveal that trigger size has the most significant impact on attack success rate (ASR), outweighing poisoning rate effects."
- Break condition: If the trigger becomes too large relative to the input image, it may dominate the content and become detectable as an anomaly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Models with larger capacity (more parameters) are more vulnerable to backdoor attacks.
- Mechanism: High-capacity models can memorize and learn complex patterns from small subsets of data, including backdoor triggers, more effectively than smaller models.
- Core assumption: The backdoor trigger pattern is distinct enough from normal features to be learned as a separate class.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "VGG has the most neurons among the four models, leading to a larger capacity to learn the backdoor functionality."
  - [section] "We believe that models with larger capacities are more vulnerable to backdoors as they can encode more patterns in their weights even from a very small part of the dataset."
- Break condition: If regularization techniques or architectural constraints limit the model's ability to memorize specific patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: AlexNet demonstrates exceptional robustness against backdoor attacks due to its unique dropout layer placement.
- Mechanism: The dropout layers in AlexNet are positioned before the fully connected layers, which may prevent the model from learning strong associations between the trigger and target class during training.
- Core assumption: The dropout mechanism disrupts the learning of trigger-specific patterns more effectively than other architectures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "AlexNet is more robust against data poisoning backdoor attacks, and we conduct experiments to explain this finding."
  - [section] "Interestingly, the classifier modules in AlexNet and VGG are very similar... The main difference between these two is that in AlexNet, the two dropout layers precede the linear layers, but in VGG, they succeed."
- Break condition: If the dropout layers are removed or repositioned, the model may become vulnerable to backdoor attacks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does AlexNet's robustness against backdoor attacks compare to other architectures when using different trigger shapes beyond squares?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows AlexNet is more robust than VGG, ResNet, and GoogLeNet against square-shaped backdoor triggers, but only tests this single shape
- Why unresolved: The experiments only evaluate square triggers, leaving open whether AlexNet's robustness extends to other trigger shapes like sine waves, overlay patterns, or more complex designs
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing AlexNet against various trigger shapes (overlay, sine, complex patterns) with the same experimental matrix used for squares would determine if its robustness is shape-specific or general

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the precise mechanism by which dropout layers in AlexNet's classifier module contribute to its backdoor resistance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that AlexNet's dropout layers (placed before fully connected layers) may explain its backdoor resistance, but notes this requires further investigation
- Why unresolved: The authors identify a correlation between dropout placement and backdoor resistance but haven't conducted controlled experiments isolating dropout effects from other architectural differences
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing AlexNet with modified dropout configurations (removing, relocating, or adjusting dropout rates) while keeping other parameters constant would isolate the dropout contribution

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do backdoor attacks perform when using smaller poisoning rates than those tested in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses poisoning rates of 0.5%, 1%, 1.5%, and 2%, noting that larger rates are unrealistic but acknowledging that even smaller rates might be more realistic
- Why unresolved: The study doesn't test poisoning rates below 0.5%, which would be more realistic for stealthy attacks and could reveal different effectiveness patterns
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing poisoning rates in the 0.1-0.4% range would determine if AlexNet and other architectures maintain their relative robustness at more realistic attack scales

- Potential reproducibility issues due to unspecified trigger color values and poisoning implementation details
