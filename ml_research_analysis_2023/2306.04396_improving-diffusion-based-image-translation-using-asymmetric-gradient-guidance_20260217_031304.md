# Improving Diffusion-based Image Translation using Asymmetric Gradient Guidance

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.04396
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04396
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** AGG outperforms state-of-the-art models in both image and latent diffusion settings, achieving better image quality (SFID 9.64 vs 9.98), faster sampling (15s vs 40s), and strong content preservation (LPIPS 0.336 vs 0.372) on text-guided translation tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental trade-off between style transformation and content preservation in diffusion-based image translation. The authors propose Asymmetric Gradient Guidance (AGG), a hybrid method that combines one-step Manifold Constraint Gradient (MCG) with Decomposed Diffusion Sampling (DDS) optimization. This approach enables stable and efficient image translation while preserving source image structure. The method uses intermediate DDIM forward samples for structural regularization instead of computationally expensive ViT-based losses, making it more efficient while maintaining high-quality results.

## Method Summary
The proposed Asymmetric Gradient Guidance (AGG) method combines MCG and DDS updates in a hybrid manner to enable stable and efficient image translation. It applies one-step MCG gradient to compute the initial update, followed by Adam-based DDS optimization on the denoised signal using Tweedie's formula. A structural regularization loss uses intermediate DDIM forward samples instead of ViT-based losses, improving computational efficiency. The method can be implemented with both image- and latent-diffusion models, demonstrating flexibility across different architectures.

## Key Results
- AGG achieves SFID score of 9.64 compared to 9.98 for the previous best method
- Sampling time reduced from 40s to 15s per image while maintaining quality
- LPIPS score of 0.336 demonstrates strong content preservation compared to 0.372 baseline
- Outperforms state-of-the-art models in both image and latent diffusion settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: AGG enables stable, efficient image translation by combining MCG and DDS updates in a hybrid manner.
- Mechanism: AGG applies one-step MCG to compute the initial update, followed by Adam-based DDS optimization on the denoised signal using Tweedie's formula. This hybrid approach ensures the updated sample stays on the correct manifold while being computationally efficient.
- Core assumption: A single MCG gradient is sufficient to initialize the subsequent DDS optimization to ensure manifold stability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The structural regularization loss using intermediate DDIM forward samples is computationally efficient while preserving content.
- Mechanism: Instead of using expensive ViT-based losses, the method saves intermediate denoised samples during the DDIM forward process and uses them as targets for the regularization loss.
- Core assumption: Intermediate denoised samples from the forward DDIM process contain sufficient structural information to guide content preservation.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "A simpler structural regularization loss uses intermediate DDIM forward samples instead of computationally expensive ViT-based losses." [section] "Furthermore, in contrast to DiffuseIT that relies on slicing ViT based structural preservation loss, we use much simpler structural regularization term using the intermediate product stored during the forward DDIM step."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: AGG's flexibility allows implementation with both image- and latent-diffusion models.
- Mechanism: The same asymmetric gradient guidance principle applies whether working in pixel space or latent space, with appropriate modifications to the sampling equations.
- Core assumption: The mathematical formulation of AGG is invariant to the choice of diffusion model architecture.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our model's adaptability allows it to be implemented with both image- and latent-diffusion models." [section] "AGG can be applied not only to image diffusion models but also to latent diffusion models (LDM) [14]."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Score network (ϵθ) → CLIP model → DDIM forward process → Adam optimizer → Structural loss function
- **Critical path:** 1) Forward DDIM process to save intermediate denoised samples 2) Reverse sampling with AGG (MCG step followed by DDS optimization) 3) Loss calculation using style loss and structural regularization 4) Final image generation
- **Design tradeoffs:** Using MCG only once vs. at every step: computational efficiency vs. potential stability; Simple DDIM-based regularization vs. ViT-based: speed vs. potentially more powerful content preservation; Hybrid AGG vs. pure DDS or pure MCG: balance of stability and efficiency
- **Failure signatures:** Content not preserved: check if intermediate samples are being saved correctly and if regularization loss is being applied; Style not transformed: check if style loss is being computed correctly and if guidance strength is appropriate; Artifacts in output: check if DDS optimization is diverging or if there are issues with the score network
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Implement MCG step alone and verify it improves content preservation on a simple image translation task 2) Add DDS optimization after MCG and measure improvement in image quality and speed 3) Replace ViT-based regularization with DDIM intermediate samples and compare efficiency and results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does AGG perform when applied to text conditions that are semantically distant from the source image in CLIP space?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper mentions that there is a problem when a text condition has large distance from source image in CLIP space (e.g., lion -> building) as shown in Fig. 8.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper acknowledges this limitation but does not provide a solution or detailed analysis of how AGG handles such extreme cases.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative experiments showing AGG performance on text conditions with varying semantic distances in CLIP space, including edge cases like "lion -> building".

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the optimal number of DDS optimization steps per timestep for different image translation tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper uses one or two steps for image diffusion models and 10 steps for latent diffusion models, but mentions that more steps showed marginal improvement and used a resampling strategy for semantic segmentation/object detection applications.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not provide a systematic analysis of how the number of DDS steps affects performance across different tasks or provide guidelines for choosing the optimal number.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive study varying the number of DDS steps across multiple image translation tasks and analyzing the trade-off between performance improvement and computational cost.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does AGG's performance compare to methods using more computationally expensive structural preservation losses like the ViT-based loss used in DiffuseIT?
- **Basis in paper:** The paper claims to use a simpler structural regularization approach using intermediate DDIM forward samples instead of the computationally expensive ViT-based losses, but does not directly compare AGG's performance against methods using these more expensive losses.
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper shows AGG outperforms DiffuseIT overall, it doesn't specifically isolate the impact of the structural preservation loss choice on the final results.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Direct head-to-head comparisons between AGG and methods using ViT-based structural preservation losses, controlling for all other factors to isolate the impact of the loss function choice.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness on complex translation tasks with large semantic gaps (e.g., lion → building) remains unproven
- Theoretical foundations connecting asymmetric gradient guidance to existing diffusion literature are weakly established
- The optimal number of DDS optimization steps for different tasks is not systematically analyzed

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Implementation details for the hybrid MCG-DDS framework and use of intermediate DDIM samples for regularization are well-specified and reproducible
- **Medium Confidence**: Claimed improvements in SFID, LPIPS, and sampling speed are supported by experimental results, though comparison baselines could be more transparent
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about method's flexibility across image and latent diffusion models and effectiveness on complex translation tasks lack sufficient empirical validation

## Next Checks
1. **Component Ablation**: Implement and test each component of AGG (MCG, DDS, regularization) independently to verify their individual contributions to the overall performance gains claimed in the paper.

2. **Complex Task Validation**: Apply AGG to more challenging translation tasks (e.g., animal to architectural structures) to assess its effectiveness beyond the demonstrated examples and identify potential failure modes.

3. **Theoretical Grounding**: Investigate the mathematical relationship between the asymmetric gradient approach and existing diffusion model guidance techniques to establish clearer theoretical foundations for the method's design choices.
