# A Survey of Explainable AI and Proposal for a Discipline of Explanation Engineering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01750
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01750
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: Comprehensive survey of XAI techniques and proposal for 6-stage Explanation Engineering framework

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive survey of Explainable AI (XAI) techniques and proposes a new discipline called "Explanation Engineering." The authors define what constitutes an explanation in AI systems and identify key factors such as audience targeting and truthfulness. They categorize XAI methods into global vs. local and model-agnostic vs. model-specific approaches, providing examples like LIME and Shapley values. The survey examines XAI applications across four domains: finance, autonomous driving, healthcare, and manufacturing, highlighting domain-specific requirements. Finally, they propose a 6-stage framework for Explanation Engineering to systematically incorporate explainability into AI systems.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a systematic review of existing XAI techniques and applications, categorizing them based on their approach (global vs. local, model-agnostic vs. model-specific). They analyzed case studies across four domains to understand domain-specific requirements. Based on this analysis, they developed a 6-stage Explanation Engineering framework that addresses the entire lifecycle of incorporating explainability into AI systems, from initial analysis through evaluation.

## Key Results
- XAI techniques must match both audience needs and domain requirements, as different stakeholders require different types of explanations
- Explanation engineering requires systematic design trade-offs between competing goals like transparency vs performance and explanation quality vs latency
- XAI technique selection depends on both model architecture and explanation requirements, with different techniques optimal for different stages of the ML workflow

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the most effective evaluation framework for measuring the descriptive accuracy and relevancy of explanations in XAI systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Currently, most explanations are personal explanations and are evaluated by methods such as surveys... A formal explanation does not depend on surveys but can be quantitatively evaluated for adequacy... The problem we face currently is that there is no agreed upon way to measure descriptive accuracy and relevancy."
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges that while PDR (Predictive Accuracy, Descriptive Accuracy, Relevancy) framework exists, there is no agreed-upon method to measure descriptive accuracy and relevancy. The authors note this makes formal evaluation "desirable but unachievable."
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of standardized metrics that can objectively quantify how well explanations capture relationships learned by ML models (descriptive accuracy) and their usefulness to specific audiences (relevancy). This could include benchmark datasets, human subject studies, and quantitative measures that correlate with human understanding.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can explanation engineering frameworks systematically incorporate external knowledge bases to enhance the quality and context of explanations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "we would require access to extensive knowledge about the human body" for medical diagnosis explanations and discusses "Combine with External Resources" as a stage in the 6-stage framework.
- Why unresolved: While the paper acknowledges the potential value of external knowledge bases, it doesn't provide concrete methods for integrating them into XAI systems or guidelines for selecting appropriate knowledge sources for different domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of systematic approaches for identifying relevant knowledge bases, methods for extracting and representing domain knowledge, and techniques for integrating this knowledge with explanations generated from ML models. Case studies demonstrating improved explanation quality when external knowledge is incorporated.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective methods for verifying the truthfulness of explanations generated by observer models that explain other AI systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses "Truthfulness" as a key factor and warns that "When creating an observer model to explain an AI system, we need to be careful about how we train them to avoid receiving untruthful explanations."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies truthfulness as a concern but doesn't provide specific methods for verifying that explanations accurately reflect the internal workings of the models they explain, especially when second-order observer models are involved.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of verification techniques that can compare explanations against ground truth model behavior, methods for detecting when explanations are misleading or fabricated, and frameworks for testing observer models against known model behaviors to ensure explanation fidelity.

## Limitations

- The survey's framework applicability across emerging AI paradigms (such as foundation models and large language models) remains untested
- The proposed Explanation Engineering framework, while systematic, lacks empirical validation across diverse real-world implementations
- The paper may be biased toward traditional ML models, potentially limiting generalizability to newer AI approaches

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The categorization of XAI techniques (global vs. local, model-agnostic vs. model-specific) is well-established in the literature and represents consensus in the field
- **Medium Confidence**: The audience-specific explanation requirements are theoretically sound but may oversimplify the complex interactions between different stakeholder needs
- **Medium Confidence**: The 6-stage framework provides a logical structure, but its practical implementation challenges and trade-offs require field validation
