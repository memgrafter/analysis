# Benchmarking the Robustness of Quantized Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.03968
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03968
- **Reference count:** 29
- **Primary result:** Comprehensive benchmark reveals quantized models show higher adversarial robustness but lower natural corruption robustness compared to floating-point counterparts

## Executive Summary
This study establishes a benchmark for evaluating quantized model robustness against adversarial attacks, natural corruptions, and systematic noises. The research evaluates four classical architectures (ResNet, VGG, MobileNet, DenseNet) across three popular quantization methods at four different bit-widths. The benchmark reveals a critical trade-off: lower-bit quantized models demonstrate enhanced adversarial robustness compared to floating-point versions, but show increased vulnerability to natural corruptions (particularly impulse noise) and systematic noises (especially nearest neighbor interpolation). These findings provide essential insights for deploying quantized models in real-world scenarios where robustness to various perturbations is crucial.

## Method Summary
The benchmark evaluates quantized models using four classical architectures and three quantization methods across four bit-widths. The evaluation framework tests robustness against adversarial attacks, natural corruptions, and systematic noises on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets. The study compares quantized models against their floating-point counterparts to identify robustness patterns and trade-offs. The comprehensive evaluation covers 12 different model configurations per architecture, providing a systematic analysis of how quantization affects model robustness across different perturbation types.

## Key Results
- Lower-bit quantized models show higher adversarial robustness than floating-point counterparts
- Quantized models are more vulnerable to natural corruptions, especially impulse noise
- Systematic noises like nearest neighbor interpolation significantly impact quantized model performance
- A clear trade-off exists between adversarial robustness and natural corruption robustness in quantized models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind quantized models' robustness patterns stems from the inherent properties of low-precision representations. Lower-bit quantization introduces discretization effects that can actually help models resist gradient-based adversarial attacks by reducing the precision of small perturbations. However, this same discretization makes models more sensitive to noise and corruption that exploit the reduced representational capacity. The quantization process fundamentally alters the model's decision boundaries and feature representations, creating a different vulnerability profile compared to floating-point models.

## Foundational Learning
- **Quantization-aware training**: Understanding how to train models with quantized weights during the forward pass is crucial for evaluating true quantized performance
- **Adversarial attack methodologies**: Knowledge of various attack strategies (FGSM, PGD, etc.) is needed to properly assess adversarial robustness
- **Natural corruption benchmarks**: Familiarity with common image corruptions (noise types, blur, weather effects) is essential for evaluating real-world robustness
- **Systematic noise analysis**: Understanding interpolation methods and their effects on image features helps interpret robustness to geometric transformations
- **Bit-width precision trade-offs**: Recognizing how different precision levels affect model capacity and robustness is fundamental to interpreting results

## Limitations
- Limited to image classification tasks on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets
- Focuses only on 4 classical architectures, missing modern architectures like transformers
- Evaluates only 3 quantization methods, potentially missing important techniques
- Results may not generalize to other domains like object detection or NLP
