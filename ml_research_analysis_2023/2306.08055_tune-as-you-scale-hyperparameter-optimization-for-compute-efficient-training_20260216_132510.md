# Tune As You Scale: Hyperparameter Optimization For Compute Efficient Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08055
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08055
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: CARBS achieves state-of-the-art performance on ProcGen by tuning a simple PPO baseline while simultaneously discovering scaling laws for all hyperparameters

## Executive Summary
CARBS is a Bayesian optimization algorithm that performs local search around the performance-cost Pareto frontier to efficiently tune deep learning models. By learning scaling relationships between hyperparameters and model size, it can automatically discover how hyperparameters should change as models scale up, achieving compute-efficient training without manual intervention. The algorithm demonstrates state-of-the-art results on ProcGen while reproducing Chinchilla's scaling laws for language models.

## Method Summary
CARBS uses Gaussian process models to predict both performance and training cost, then performs local search around the current Pareto-optimal configurations. It defines a search distribution centered at Pareto front points and uses a cost-aware acquisition function based on expected improvement relative to the Pareto front. The algorithm includes failure prediction through a separate GP model and periodic resampling of Pareto-optimal points to maintain robustness. This approach enables automatic discovery of hyperparameter scaling relationships while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on ProcGen benchmark by tuning simple PPO baseline
- Reproduces Chinchilla's scaling laws for model size vs training tokens
- Simultaneously discovers scaling laws for all other hyperparameters across multiple tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
CARBS achieves state-of-the-art performance by leveraging local search around the Pareto frontier to avoid the curse of dimensionality in hyperparameter spaces. Instead of global exploration across exponentially growing search spaces, CARBS defines a local search distribution around current Pareto-optimal points. This focuses search effort on promising regions while still allowing escape to new optima when improvements are found.

### Mechanism 2
CARBS learns scaling relationships automatically by modeling cost-dependent performance relationships. By jointly modeling performance and cost as Gaussian processes, CARBS can extrapolate from smaller, cheaper models to larger ones. The cost-aware acquisition function naturally discovers how hyperparameters should scale with model size.

### Mechanism 3
CARBS improves robustness through Pareto front resampling and failure prediction. By resampling Pareto-optimal points and predicting failures separately, CARBS maintains a robust estimate of the performance-cost tradeoff even with noisy observations and failure-prone configurations.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Gaussian Process regression for surrogate modeling
  - Why needed here: CARBS uses GPs to model both performance and cost functions, enabling uncertainty-aware predictions and acquisition functions
  - Quick check question: What are the two key hyperparameters of a GP that control the smoothness and length scale of the learned function?

- Concept: Expected Improvement acquisition functions
  - Why needed here: CARBS modifies the standard EI to be cost-aware and Pareto-relative, requiring understanding of how EI works in Bayesian optimization
  - Quick check question: How does expected improvement differ from probability of improvement, and why is this distinction important for exploration vs exploitation?

- Concept: Pareto optimality and multi-objective optimization
  - Why needed here: CARBS explicitly searches the performance-cost Pareto frontier rather than a single objective, requiring understanding of Pareto concepts
  - Quick check question: What is the formal definition of Pareto dominance, and how does it apply when comparing configurations with different performance and cost?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Gaussian Process models (performance, cost, Pareto front) -> Local search space generator -> Acquisition function composer -> Resampling manager -> Failure predictor
- Critical path: Search space generation → GP model fitting → Candidate evaluation → Acquisition function scoring → Best candidate selection
- Design tradeoffs: Local search vs global exploration, cost-awareness vs pure performance optimization, resampling frequency vs computational budget
- Failure signatures: Poor scaling predictions, unstable Pareto fronts, failure to improve beyond initial guesses, excessive sampling of low-performance regions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run CARBS on a simple 2D synthetic benchmark with known Pareto front to verify local search behavior
  2. Tune a small neural network on MNIST with 5-10 hyperparameters to test GP modeling and acquisition functions
  3. Scale up to a medium-sized language model (few hundred million parameters) to test automatic scaling law discovery

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the performance of CARBS compare to other state-of-the-art hyperparameter optimization algorithms on problems with very high-dimensional search spaces (e.g. >50 hyperparameters)?
- Question: How does the local search strategy in CARBS compare to other local search strategies used in hyperparameter optimization, such as CMA-ES or trust region methods?
- Question: How sensitive is CARBS to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the search radius σ_search or the resampling rate N_resample?
- Question: How does CARBS scale to distributed computing environments with a large number of parallel workers?

## Limitations
- Performance on extreme model scales (>10B parameters) remains untested
- Cross-architecture generalization of scaling laws not evaluated
- Limited comparison with other local search strategies in hyperparameter optimization

## Confidence
- Scaling law discovery: Medium confidence - successfully reproduced Chinchilla results but limited cross-task validation
- High-dimensional performance: Medium confidence - claims good performance but limited testing on >10 hyperparameters
- Noise robustness: Medium confidence - failure prediction shows promise but not tested in extreme noise conditions

## Next Checks
1. **Architecture Transfer Test**: Apply CARBS-tuned hyperparameters from one architecture (e.g., ResNet) to a substantially different architecture (e.g., Vision Transformer) on the same task to assess cross-architecture generalization of discovered scaling laws.

2. **Extreme Scaling Validation**: Extend hyperparameter tuning from small models (10M parameters) to very large models (10B+ parameters) on a language modeling task, measuring whether the learned scaling relationships remain accurate across 3+ orders of magnitude.

3. **Failure Mode Analysis**: Systematically inject various types of training instability (gradient explosions, NaN losses, hardware timeouts) to evaluate whether the failure prediction GP model accurately identifies problematic configurations and whether CARBS successfully avoids them while maintaining optimization efficiency.
