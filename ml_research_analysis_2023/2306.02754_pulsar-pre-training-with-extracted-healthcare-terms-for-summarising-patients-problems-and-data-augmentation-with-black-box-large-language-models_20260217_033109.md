# PULSAR: Pre-training with Extracted Healthcare Terms for Summarising Patients' Problems and Data Augmentation with Black-box Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02754
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02754
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: PULSAR achieves 3.1-point improvement over larger models on unknown data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PULSAR, a framework for medical summarisation that combines domain-specific pre-training with data augmentation using black-box large language models. The approach addresses the challenge of summarising patients' problems from medical progress notes by leveraging healthcare-specific terminology and synthetic data generation. The framework consists of a pre-trained language model fine-tuned for the summarisation task and a data augmentation component that uses instruction following to generate paraphrases while preserving medical concepts.

## Method Summary
PULSAR employs a two-pronged approach to medical summarisation. First, it pre-trains a domain-specific language model on MIMIC-III progress notes using three concept masking objectives, where healthcare terms are identified through entity linking and NER models and then masked for the model to predict. Second, it augments training data by using large language models to generate paraphrases of source sentences while maintaining relevant healthcare terms through instruction following. This dual approach aims to overcome the scarcity of annotated healthcare data while ensuring the model learns to preserve critical medical information during summarisation.

## Key Results
- PULSAR outperforms state-of-the-art models on the BioNLP 2023 Shared Task 1A
- Achieves 3.1-point improvement over larger models on unknown data
- Data augmentation effectively alleviates lack of annotated healthcare data when training and test distributions are consistent

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by combining domain adaptation through pre-training with synthetic data generation. The pre-training objective teaches the model to understand and predict medical terminology in context, while the data augmentation creates diverse training examples that help the model generalise. By masking and predicting healthcare terms during pre-training, the model learns to recognise and preserve these critical concepts during summarisation. The LLM-based augmentation generates paraphrases that maintain semantic meaning while varying linguistic expression, effectively expanding the training distribution.

## Foundational Learning

2. **Entity Linking and NER** - Why needed: Identifies healthcare terms for masking; Quick check: Extraction accuracy of medical concepts
3. **Instruction Following for Augmentation** - Why needed: Generates paraphrases while preserving medical content; Quick check: Output paraphrases maintain original medical meaning

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Domain-specific LM (pre-trained on MIMIC-III) -> Fine-tuning on summarisation task -> LLM-based data augmentation

**Critical Path:**
1. Identify healthcare terms using entity linking/NER tools
2. Pre-train language model with concept masking objectives
3. Fine-tune on available annotated data
4. Generate synthetic data using LLMs
5. Train final summarisation model

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- External tool dependency vs. integrated concept identification
- Synthetic data quality vs. quantity trade-off
- Computational cost of pre-training vs. fine-tuning efficiency

**Failure Signatures:**
- Degradation in performance when external NER tools are unavailable
- Quality issues in LLM-generated paraphrases
- Overfitting to synthetic data distribution

**3 First Experiments:**
1. Evaluate concept prediction accuracy on masked medical terms
2. Measure paraphrase quality preservation metrics
3. Compare performance with and without data augmentation

## Limitations
- Reliance on external tools (UMLS, SciSpacy, MetaSRA) introduces potential brittleness
- Data augmentation effectiveness depends heavily on LLM output quality and consistency
- Evaluation focused primarily on one shared task dataset, limiting generalizability
