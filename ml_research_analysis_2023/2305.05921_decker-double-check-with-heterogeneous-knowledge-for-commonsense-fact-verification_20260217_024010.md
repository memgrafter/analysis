# Decker: Double Check with Heterogeneous Knowledge for Commonsense Fact Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05921
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05921
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 16
- DECKER achieves 89.5% accuracy on CREAK benchmark, outperforming previous state-of-the-art models like RACo (88.6%) and GreaseLM (77.5%)

## Executive Summary
DECKER is a novel commonsense fact verification model that bridges heterogeneous knowledge by uncovering latent relationships between structured knowledge (knowledge graphs) and unstructured knowledge (text facts). The model addresses the limitation of existing methods that focus on either type of knowledge in isolation. By retrieving relevant KG subgraphs and facts, constructing an integral graph, and using relational graph convolutional networks (R-GCN) for reasoning, DECKER achieves state-of-the-art performance on commonsense fact verification benchmarks. The approach demonstrates that integrating structured and unstructured knowledge through graph-based reasoning significantly improves verification accuracy.

## Method Summary
DECKER operates through a three-stage pipeline: knowledge retrieval, double-checking, and knowledge fusion. First, it retrieves heterogeneous knowledge including a KG subgraph and relevant facts using specialized retrievers. Second, it constructs an integral graph combining the question, retrieved facts, and KG concepts, then employs R-GCN to reason over this heterogeneous graph while filtering irrelevant information. Finally, a multi-head attention pooling mechanism refines the knowledge representation by focusing on question-relevant information, which is combined with the question representation for final prediction. The model's key innovation is the construction of bidirectional concept-to-fact edges that enable double-checking between structured and unstructured knowledge sources.

## Key Results
- Achieves 89.5% accuracy on CREAK benchmark, setting new state-of-the-art performance
- Outperforms previous methods including RACo (88.6%) and GreaseLM (77.5%)
- Ablation studies show multi-head attention pooling significantly improves performance over max/mean pooling (89.5% vs 87.5% and 86.7%)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Heterogeneous knowledge integration enables superior filtering of noisy information and enrichment of relevant knowledge.
- Mechanism: The model constructs an integral graph combining structured knowledge (knowledge graphs) and unstructured knowledge (text facts) with bidirectional edges. The relational graph convolutional networks (R-GCN) then perform reasoning over this heterogeneous graph, while multi-head attention pooling refines the knowledge representation by emphasizing question-relevant information.
- Core assumption: The complementary nature of structured and unstructured knowledge can be leveraged through graph-based reasoning to improve fact verification accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "DECKER is a commonsense fact verification model that bridges heterogeneous knowledge by uncovering latent relationships between structured knowledge (knowledge graphs) and unstructured knowledge (text facts)."
  - [section]: "Our proposed DECKER works in the following steps: (i) firstly, it retrieves heterogeneous knowledge including a KG subgraph and several relevant facts... (ii) secondly, it constructs an integral graph with encoded question and facts and then employs relational graph convolutional networks (R-GCN) to reason and filter over the heterogeneous knowledge"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers discuss heterogeneous graph reasoning but lack specific implementation details comparable to DECKER.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Double-checking between structured and unstructured knowledge through concept-to-fact edges improves filtering of irrelevant information.
- Mechanism: The model adds bidirectional concept-to-fact edges between concept nodes (from KG) and fact nodes (from text) when the concept appears in the fact. This allows the R-GCN to propagate information between these knowledge sources, enabling the model to verify facts against structured knowledge and vice versa.
- Core assumption: Facts containing concepts from the KG are more likely to be relevant for verification than those without such connections.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "it retrieves heterogeneous knowledge including a KG subgraph and several relevant facts... it constructs an integral graph with encoded question and facts and then employs relational graph convolutional networks (R-GCN) to reason and filter over the heterogeneous knowledge"
  - [section]: "For concept-to-fact edges, we use string matching and add a bidirectional edge(nc,rc2f,nf) between nc∈Vc and nf∈Vf with rc2f∈R if the concept nc can be captured in the fact nf"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - related work mentions heterogeneous graph reasoning but doesn't specifically address concept-to-fact edge construction for fact verification.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-head attention pooling with question-query interaction provides superior knowledge refinement compared to pooling methods without interaction.
- Mechanism: The model uses the initial question embedding as a query in multi-head attention pooling, computing attention weights between the question and the encoded representations of facts and concepts from the R-GCN output. This selects and combines only the most relevant information for final prediction.
- Core assumption: Question-relevant information from the heterogeneous knowledge can be effectively identified through attention mechanisms.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "it adopts a multi-head attention pooling mechanism to obtain a final refinement of enriched knowledge representation and combines it with the question representation for downstream tasks"
  - [section]: "Following the idea of Zhang et al. (2022b), we introduce a multi-head attention pooling mechanism (MHA) to ulteriorly gather the question-related information"
  - [corpus]: Strong evidence - the ablation study shows attention pooling significantly outperforms max pooling and mean pooling (89.5% vs 87.5% and 86.7% respectively).

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Knowledge Retrieval Module (KG Retriever + Fact Retriever) -> Double Check Module (Language Encoder + Graph Construction + R-GCN) -> Knowledge Fusion Module (Multi-head Attention Pooling + Predictor)

- Critical path: Question → Knowledge Retrieval → Graph Construction → R-GCN → MHA Pooling → Prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using R-GCN vs Transformer-based graph reasoning: R-GCN is more efficient for multi-relational graphs but less expressive than transformer-based approaches
  - Fixed number of retrieved facts (k=5) vs dynamic selection: Fixed number simplifies computation but may miss relevant information
  - String matching for concept-to-fact edges vs learned similarity: String matching is interpretable but may miss semantic matches

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance drops significantly when knowledge retrieval fails to find relevant information
  - Model becomes sensitive to noisy facts when concept-to-fact edges are removed
  - Attention pooling performance degrades when replaced with max/mean pooling

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replace R-GCN with standard GCN and measure performance impact to verify the importance of handling multiple relation types
  2. Remove concept-to-fact edges and evaluate how this affects the model's ability to filter noisy information
  3. Replace multi-head attention pooling with simple concatenation and compare performance to validate the effectiveness of the interaction mechanism

## Limitations
- Reliance on string matching for concept-to-fact edges may miss semantically related facts with different terminology
- Fixed retrieval of 5 facts per concept may be insufficient for complex questions or introduce noise
- R-GCN implementation may not fully capture the complexity of interactions in heterogeneous graphs compared to transformer-based approaches

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core claim that heterogeneous knowledge integration improves fact verification accuracy is strongly supported by the 89.5% CREAK accuracy outperforming prior state-of-the-art models (88.6% for RACo and 77.5% for GreaseLM).

**Medium Confidence**: The specific mechanisms of double-checking through concept-to-fact edges and the effectiveness of multi-head attention pooling are supported by ablation studies, but the exact contribution of each component is not fully isolated.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that this approach generalizes well to other fact verification domains beyond commonsense reasoning is not directly tested, as experiments focus specifically on CSQA2.0 and CREAK benchmarks.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies systematically removing each component (concept-to-fact edges, multi-head attention, R-GCN) to quantify their individual contributions to overall performance.
2. Test the model on fact verification datasets from different domains (e.g., scientific claims, news verification) to assess generalizability beyond commonsense reasoning.
3. Replace the string matching mechanism for concept-to-fact edge construction with semantic similarity measures (e.g., cosine similarity of embeddings) to evaluate whether this improves recall of relevant facts without introducing excessive noise.
