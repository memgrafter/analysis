# Are Human Explanations Always Helpful? Towards Objective Evaluation of Human Natural Language Explanations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03117
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03117
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 37
- Key outcome: A new evaluation metric (Treu) objectively measures explanation quality by assessing helpfulness to model performance during both fine-tuning and inference, consistently ranking ECQA > CoS-E v1.11 > CoS-E v1.0 > e-SNLI > ComVE across two model architectures

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of objectively evaluating human-annotated natural language explanations by proposing a new metric that measures their helpfulness to model performance. The Treu metric extends the Simulatability score by incorporating performance changes during both fine-tuning and inference phases. A unified prompt-based data format converts various NLP tasks into a consistent format to minimize structural variations. Experiments across five datasets and two model architectures demonstrate that explanations can improve model performance even when previously considered low quality, with consistent ranking across datasets.

## Method Summary
The method introduces Treu, an evaluation metric that measures explanation quality by calculating performance differences when models are trained with and without explanations (fine-tuning phase) and when they predict with and without explanations (inference phase). A unified prompt-based data format converts classification and multiple-choice tasks into a standardized multiple-choice generation format. The metric is evaluated on five datasets (CoS-E v1.0, CoS-E v1.11, ECQA, e-SNLI, ComVE) using T5-base and BART-base architectures, comparing against the baseline Simulatability score.

## Key Results
- Treu consistently ranks explanation quality across all five datasets, while Simulatability score fails to do so
- Explanations improve model performance even when previously considered low quality
- Best results observed for ECQA, worst for ComVE
- Negation and counterfactual reasoning in explanations negatively impact certain classes in e-SNLI

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The proposed Treu metric objectively evaluates explanation quality by measuring model performance changes during both fine-tuning and inference.
- Mechanism: The metric calculates the difference in prediction accuracy between models trained with and without explanations during fine-tuning, and between models predicting with and without explanations during inference.
- Core assumption: Explanations provide different types of helpfulness during training versus prediction phases, and both need to be measured for a complete evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we define a new metric that can take into consideration the helpfulness of an explanation for model performance at both fine-tuning and inference"
  - [section]: "Our evaluation metric is an extension of the Simulatability score (Doshi-Velez and Kim, 2017) and we propose a prompt-based unified data format that can convert classification or multiple choice tasks into a unified multiple choice generation task format to minimize the influence of structural variations across different tasks"
- Break condition: If explanations do not provide different types of helpfulness during training versus prediction, or if the unified format fails to minimize task differences.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The unified prompt-based data format enables consistent evaluation across different task types.
- Mechanism: By converting various NLP tasks (classification, multiple choice) into a unified multiple-choice generation format using template-based prompts, the metric controls for structural variations that could otherwise affect model performance.
- Core assumption: Task format differences significantly impact model performance and need to be controlled for objective evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "With the help of a unified dataset format, we evaluated the proposed metric on five datasets (e.g., e-SNLI) against two model architectures (T5 and BART)"
  - [section]: "we can control and minimize the influence of varying task formats on model performance while evaluating the helpfulness of explanations by leveraging a unified data format"
- Break condition: If the unified format fails to adequately control for task differences or if it introduces new biases.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Explanations with negation or counterfactual content are less helpful for model performance.
- Mechanism: The analysis shows that explanations containing negation words or counterfactual reasoning (common in explaining "neutral" or "contradiction" classes) lead to performance drops, particularly when models are trained without explanations but tested with them.
- Core assumption: Negation and counterfactual reasoning are inherently more difficult for models to interpret correctly.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We notice that the models fine-tuned on T5 and BART have more than a 40% prediction accuracy drop on data with 'neutral' labels when they are fine-tuned with Baseline and predicted with Infusion setting"
  - [section]: "We suspect human annotators behave differently while providing explanations for different categories in e-SNLI... humans tend to provide counter-examples or use negation connotations to explain why two sentences are unrelated (neutral) or contradictory"
- Break condition: If models improve at handling negation and counterfactual reasoning, or if the explanation format changes to avoid these constructions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Unified data formatter -> Model training pipelines (Baseline/Infusion) -> Evaluation module -> Treu score calculation
- Critical path: Convert dataset -> Train model with explanations -> Train model without explanations -> Evaluate both on explanation-inclusive and explanation-exclusive data -> Calculate Treu score
- Design tradeoffs: Using unified format simplifies evaluation but may lose task-specific nuances; measuring both training and inference phases provides completeness but doubles computational cost
- Failure signatures: Consistently negative Treu scores across datasets suggest explanations are generally unhelpful; wide score variation between model architectures indicates metric sensitivity to implementation details

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different model architectures (T5 vs BART) specifically influence the evaluation of explanation quality using the proposed metric?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that T5 models outperform BART models significantly across all datasets and settings.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of why T5 performs better than BART or how the architecture differences affect the metric's evaluation of explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies focusing on the architectural differences between T5 and BART and their impact on handling explanations, possibly through ablation studies or architectural modifications.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific characteristics of human-annotated explanations in e-SNLI lead to lower model performance, particularly for neutral and contradiction classes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that models fine-tuned on T5 and BART have significant prediction accuracy drops on 'neutral' and 'contradiction' labels when predicting with explanations.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that negation and counter-factual reasoning in explanations might be problematic but does not provide a detailed analysis or examples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed linguistic analysis of explanations in e-SNLI, focusing on the use of negation and counter-factuals, and their correlation with model performance drops.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the presence of negation in explanations affect model performance across different tasks, and can this be mitigated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how negation in explanations, particularly in ComVE, leads to performance issues and suggests avoiding counter-examples.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore methods to mitigate the negative impact of negation or provide strategies for annotators to create more model-friendly explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing different explanation styles (e.g., positive vs. negative framing) and their impact on model performance, along with guidelines for annotators.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the proposed metric be generalized to evaluate the quality of model-generated explanations, and what challenges might arise?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that generalizing the metric to model-generated explanations is a natural step but notes potential challenges with the Infusion setting.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results or a detailed discussion on the applicability of the metric to model-generated explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the metric to model-generated explanations and identifying specific challenges or limitations in this context.

## Limitations

- Dataset bias concerns: The metric's generalizability is limited by the five datasets used, all containing human-annotated explanations
- Model architecture sensitivity: Uncertainty about whether the metric captures fundamental explanation quality or simply reflects how well explanations align with specific model architectures' processing capabilities
- Task format conversion assumptions: The unified format may not preserve critical information for certain NLP tasks where structure is semantically important

## Confidence

- High confidence: Explanations can improve model performance even when previously considered low quality, demonstrated across multiple datasets and model architectures
- Medium confidence: The unified prompt format successfully minimizes task differences, though actual degree of control is not extensively validated
- Medium confidence: Negation and counterfactual reasoning are problematic for models, but the underlying mechanism is inferred rather than experimentally validated
