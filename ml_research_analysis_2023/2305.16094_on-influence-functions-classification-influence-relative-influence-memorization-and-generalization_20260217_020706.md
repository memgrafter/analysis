# On Influence Functions, Classification Influence, Relative Influence, Memorization and Generalization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16094
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16094
- Reference count: 6
- Key outcome: This paper studies influence functions for large-scale ML systems, focusing on simplifying their computation and linking them to memorization vs. generalization. It proposes splitting a neural network into a featurizer and a lightweight classifier, then computing influence only on classifier parameters. This reduces computation from billions to thousands of parameters. The paper also introduces "Loss Estimate Preserving" (LEP) relative influence functions, computed from small subsets of training data, which approximate full-influence values. Key findings include: (1) when classifier Hessian eigenvalues dominate, classification influence approximates full-influence accurately; (2) the sign of influence indicates memorization (negative) vs. generalization (positive); (3) LEP relative influences maintain information when using small subsets. ## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational challenge of calculating influence functions in large-scale machine learning systems. Influence functions measure how individual training examples affect model predictions, but traditional computation is prohibitively expensive for models with billions of parameters. The authors propose a novel approach that splits neural networks into a featurizer and classifier component, computing influence only on the classifier parameters. This dramatically reduces computational complexity from billions to thousands of parameters while maintaining accuracy under certain conditions. The paper also introduces a "Loss Estimate Preserving" (LEP) relative influence formulation that enables efficient computation using small subsets of training data.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes splitting neural networks into a featurizer and a lightweight classifier, then computing influence only on classifier parameters. This reduces computational complexity from billions to thousands of parameters. The authors introduce "Loss Estimate Preserving" (LEP) relative influence functions computed from small subsets of training data. They establish conditions under which classification influence approximates full-influence accurately (when classifier Hessian eigenvalues dominate) and demonstrate that influence signs indicate memorization (negative) versus generalization (positive). The approach enables scalable influence computation through efficient sampling strategies.

## Key Results
- Classification influence accurately approximates full-influence when classifier Hessian eigenvalues dominate
- Influence signs indicate memorization (negative) versus generalization (positive)
- LEP relative influences preserve information when computed from small training subsets
- The featurizer-classifier split reduces computational complexity from billions to thousands of parameters

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works because influence functions capture how training examples affect model predictions through second-order derivatives of the loss function. By separating the network into a fixed featurizer and a trainable classifier, the authors reduce the parameter space where influence is computed. The LEP formulation preserves the ranking of influences by maintaining loss estimate correlations across subsets. When classifier parameters dominate the Hessian spectrum, their influence becomes representative of the full model's behavior, enabling accurate approximations with significantly reduced computation.

## Foundational Learning
- Influence functions: Mathematical tools measuring training example impact on model predictions; needed to understand how individual data points affect model behavior
- Hessian eigenvalues: Second-order derivatives indicating parameter sensitivity; needed to characterize conditions for approximation accuracy
- Memorization vs. generalization: Concepts describing model behavior; needed to interpret influence signs as behavioral indicators
- Parameter-efficient computation: Techniques for reducing computational complexity; needed to make influence analysis scalable
- Relative influence: Normalized influence measures; needed to compare influences across different training examples

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input -> Featurizer -> Classifier -> Output
Critical path: Training data → model → prediction → influence computation → interpretation
Design tradeoffs: Computational efficiency vs. approximation accuracy; featurizer simplicity vs. classifier expressiveness
Failure signatures: Poor approximation when feature extractor parameters significantly influence predictions; unstable influences when Hessian spectra are balanced
First experiments: 1) Validate classification influence accuracy across different Hessian eigenvalue ratios, 2) Test LEP preservation across diverse dataset sizes, 3) Evaluate sign-based memorization detection across regularization strengths

## Limitations
- Approximation accuracy depends on unclear conditions about Hessian eigenvalue dominance
- Experimental validation limited to ResNet50 and CIFAR-10/100 datasets
- LEP preservation lacks theoretical guarantees across different data distributions
- Sign-based memorization interpretation requires validation across diverse model classes

## Confidence
High confidence in computational efficiency gains from featurizer-classifier split
Medium confidence in sign-based memorization-generalization interpretation
Low confidence in universality of LEP preservation claims
