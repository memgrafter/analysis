# Diffusion Models for Time Series Applications: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00624

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a systematic survey of diffusion models applied to time series tasks, covering three major formulations: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM), Score-based Generative Models (SGM), and Stochastic Differential Equations (SDE). The survey organizes existing research into three primary application areas: forecasting, imputation, and generation. For each area, the paper reviews key models, their architectures, and performance characteristics, while highlighting both the flexibility and computational challenges of diffusion approaches.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes research on diffusion models for time series by categorizing approaches based on their mathematical formulations and application domains. It examines how different noise schedules, score networks, and conditional mechanisms are adapted for temporal data. The paper systematically reviews models like TimeGrad, ScoreGrad, and D3VAE for forecasting; CSDI, DSPD, and SSSD for imputation; and TSGM for generation, providing a comprehensive taxonomy of the field's current state.

## Key Results
- Three major diffusion model formulations (DDPM, SGM, SDE) provide different frameworks for time series applications
- Diffusion models show superior flexibility and performance compared to traditional time series methods for forecasting, imputation, and generation
- Computational cost remains a significant limitation, with diffusion models generally more expensive than GAN-based alternatives
- PriSTI demonstrates effectiveness for spatial-temporal graph data but is limited to single-feature scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Diffusion models work by gradually transforming data through a Markov chain with carefully designed noise schedules. The reverse process learns to denoise corrupted samples, effectively capturing complex data distributions. For time series, this approach can model temporal dependencies and handle irregular patterns through conditional mechanisms and appropriate network architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
- Input data -> Noise injection (forward process) -> Score network -> Denoising (reverse process) -> Output

Critical Path:
The critical path involves the forward diffusion process adding Gaussian noise to data, followed by training a score network to estimate the score function at various noise levels, then using this learned score function to reverse the diffusion process and generate samples.

Design Tradeoffs:
- Forward process complexity vs. reverse process tractability
- Noise schedule selection (linear vs. cosine vs. other)
- Score network architecture complexity vs. training efficiency
- Conditional information integration vs. model flexibility

Failure Signatures:
- Mode collapse when score network fails to capture multi-modal distributions
- High reconstruction error indicating poor score function estimation
- Training instability when noise schedules are poorly designed
- Computational bottlenecks in high-dimensional time series
