# Maneuver Decision-Making For Autonomous Air Combat Through Curriculum Learning And Reinforcement Learning With Sparse Rewards

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05838
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05838
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Angle curriculum outperforms distance and hybrid curricula in air combat maneuver learning

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of autonomous air combat maneuver decision-making using reinforcement learning with sparse rewards. The authors propose integrating curriculum learning to accelerate training and improve performance. Three curriculum strategies are designed and tested: angle curriculum, distance curriculum, and hybrid curriculum. The angle curriculum demonstrates superior performance by achieving faster training speed and better handling of targets from various directions, while the hybrid curriculum fails by causing the agent to get stuck in local optima.

## Method Summary
The authors combine reinforcement learning with curriculum learning to solve the air combat maneuver decision-making problem under sparse reward conditions. Three curricula are designed based on different aspects of the air combat scenario: angle curriculum focuses on the relative angle between aircraft, distance curriculum focuses on separation distance, and hybrid curriculum combines both. The curricula progressively increase task difficulty during training, starting from easier scenarios and gradually introducing more challenging ones. The reinforcement learning algorithm learns to make optimal maneuver decisions through trial and error within each curriculum stage.

## Key Results
- Angle curriculum outperforms distance and hybrid curricula in both training speed and final performance
- Agent trained with angle curriculum can effectively handle targets approaching from different directions
- Hybrid curriculum fails by causing the agent to get stuck in local optima
- Maneuver decisions made by the angle curriculum agent are consistent with missile characteristics

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Observation Space -> State Encoder -> Policy Network -> Action Selection -> Environment -> Reward Signal -> Replay Buffer -> Training Algorithm

**Critical Path:**
Observation (aircraft states, missile info) → State Encoder (feature extraction) → Policy Network (action probability) → Action Selection (maneuver decision) → Environment (simulator) → Reward (win/loss) → Training (policy update)

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Sparse vs. dense rewards: Chosen sparse rewards for realism but added curriculum to address training difficulty
- Curriculum granularity: Angle curriculum proved more effective than distance-based approach
- Hybrid curriculum complexity: Failed to provide benefits and caused local optima

**Failure Signatures:**
- Hybrid curriculum: Agent gets stuck in local optima, failing to learn effective maneuvers
- Distance curriculum: Slower convergence and suboptimal final performance
- Sparse rewards without curriculum: Difficult or impossible training

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the angle curriculum's superiority scale with different missile characteristics and target behaviors?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows angle curriculum outperforms others but only tests with specific missile parameters and target behaviors.
- Why unresolved: The experiments used fixed missile parameters and didn't explore how curriculum effectiveness varies with different missile speeds, ranges, or target evasion patterns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying missile parameters and target behaviors while measuring curriculum performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical explanation for why hybrid curriculum gets stuck in local optima?
- Basis in paper: The paper observes hybrid curriculum causes local optimum trapping but doesn't explain why.
- Why unresolved: The paper reports the phenomenon but doesn't provide theoretical analysis of the interaction between angle and distance curricula that causes this behavior.
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical analysis or visualization of the policy landscape showing how combining both curricula creates problematic local optima.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would alternative sparse reward structures (beyond simple win/loss) improve training without sacrificing the benefits of sparse rewards?
- Basis in paper: The paper uses only binary win/loss rewards but mentions that sparse rewards cause training difficulties.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on pure sparse rewards but doesn't explore whether intermediate reward signals could help without requiring full handcrafted reward design.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing different sparse reward structures (e.g., progress rewards, survival time rewards) against the pure win/loss approach.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is based on simulation within a specific air combat scenario, not capturing real-world complexities like sensor noise and communication delays
- Hybrid curriculum failure is observed but not deeply analyzed or explained theoretically
- Comparison focuses on different curriculum strategies rather than against state-of-the-art methods without curriculum learning

## Confidence
- High confidence: The angle curriculum's superior performance and its ability to handle targets from different directions
- Medium confidence: The claim that angle curriculum performance is consistent with missile characteristics
- Low confidence: The explanation for why the hybrid curriculum failed and got stuck in local optimum
