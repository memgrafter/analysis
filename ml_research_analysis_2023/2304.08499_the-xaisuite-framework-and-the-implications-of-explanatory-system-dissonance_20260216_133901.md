# The XAISuite framework and the implications of explanatory system dissonance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08499
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08499
- Reference count: 34
- Key outcome: SHAP and LIME importance score correlations cannot predict model accuracy, and maximum importance scores do not provide more consistent comparisons

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the XAISuite framework, a unified tool for training, explaining, and analyzing machine learning models using various explanatory systems. The study compares SHAP and LIME by examining the correlation of their importance scores across 14 models and 4 tabular datasets. Results show that the correlation between SHAP and LIME importance scores cannot predict model accuracy, and using maximum importance scores per instance does not provide a more consistent explanatory comparison method. These findings highlight the limitations of current explanatory systems and emphasize the need for further research to improve their consistency and accuracy.

## Method Summary
The XAISuite framework integrates data loading, model training, explanation generation, and analysis into a unified pipeline. It uses external libraries like scikit-learn for model training and OmniXAI for explanation generation. The framework trains 14 machine learning models (7 regression, 7 classification) on 4 tabular datasets (2 regression, 2 classification) and generates explanations using both SHAP and LIME. It then analyzes and compares the importance scores from these explanatory systems, calculating correlations and creating visualizations to assess consistency and reliability.

## Key Results
- SHAP and LIME importance score correlations cannot predict model accuracy
- Maximum importance score correlations do not provide more consistent comparisons than average correlations
- XAISuite successfully implements a unified pipeline for explainable AI across multiple methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SHAP and LIME importance scores can differ widely even for the same feature in the same model
- Mechanism: The correlation between SHAP and LIME importance scores is not a stable indicator of model performance because the two methods approximate feature importance differently. SHAP uses a game-theoretic approach (Shapley values) to attribute contributions, while LIME fits a local linear surrogate model. These different approximations can lead to divergent scores, especially for features with non-linear effects or when the data distribution changes across instances
- Core assumption: The discrepancy is due to the fundamental difference in how SHAP and LIME compute importance, not because of random noise or model instability
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states that "the correlation between SHAP and LIME importance scores cannot predict model accuracy"
  - [section] shows specific examples where SHAP and LIME scores differ by wide margins (e.g., correlation less than 0.8 for a feature in the digits dataset)
- Break condition: If both methods used the exact same approximation framework (e.g., both used Shapley values), the correlation would be high and stable

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The most important feature's importance score correlation is not necessarily more reliable than the average correlation across all features
- Mechanism: Using the maximum importance score per instance does not reduce variance in correlations between explanatory systems. The study found that the variance of the correlation between maximum importance scores can be higher than the variance of the average correlation across all features. This suggests that focusing on the single most important feature does not provide a more consistent measure of explainer agreement
- Core assumption: The variance in correlations is a measure of consistency, and higher variance indicates less reliable comparisons
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states that "the correlations between SHAP and LIME importance scores for the most important features may or may not be more variable than the correlation between SHAP and LIME importance scores averaged across all features"
  - [section] presents data showing that the variance of average correlation and correlation of maximum importance scores varies across datasets, with no consistent pattern favoring maximum importance scores
- Break condition: If the most important feature was always the same across all instances and both methods agreed on its importance, the correlation of maximum importance scores would be consistently high and low variance

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The XAISuite framework provides a unified tool for training, explaining, and analyzing machine learning models, making explanations more accessible and trustworthy
- Mechanism: XAISuite integrates data loading, model training, explanation generation, and analysis into a single framework. This allows users to easily compare different explanatory systems (like SHAP and LIME) and understand their differences. By providing a consistent interface and tools for comparison, XAISuite helps users trust explanations more because they can see consensus (or lack thereof) across multiple methods
- Core assumption: A unified framework that supports multiple explanatory systems and provides tools for comparison will lead to more trustworthy explanations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states that XAISuite "unifies the process of training and explaining models" and "makes machine learning explanations more accessible and trustworthy"
  - [section] describes the three components of XAISuite: data retrieval, model creation and training, and explanation generation, emphasizing simplicity, integrability, flexibility, usability, and expandability
- Break condition: If the framework did not support multiple explanatory systems or did not provide tools for comparison, users would not be able to assess the consistency of explanations and trust would not be improved

- Component map: Data Loader -> Model Training and Explanation -> Explanation Analysis -> Additional Data
- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess data
  2. Train machine learning models
  3. Generate explanations using SHAP and LIME
  4. Compare and analyze the importance scores from different explanatory systems
  5. Visualize the results and correlations
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simplicity vs. Flexibility: XAISuite aims for simplicity in its interface but maintains flexibility by not being tied to specific external dependencies
  - Usability vs. Completeness: The framework focuses on usability by providing readable outputs but may not include every possible explanatory system or analysis method
- Failure signatures:
  - Undefined correlations: Occur when one explanatory system believes a feature is irrelevant while the other does not, indicating a significant discrepancy in how the systems interpret feature importance
  - Low or negative correlation: Suggests that the explanatory systems are not in agreement, but does not necessarily indicate that the model is inaccurate
  - High variance in correlations: Indicates that the agreement between explanatory systems is inconsistent across different features or models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the correlations between SHAP and LIME importance scores vary across different types of machine learning models (e.g., linear regression, decision trees, neural networks)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study compares SHAP and LIME correlations across 14 different models but does not specifically analyze the variation across model types
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed breakdown of correlations by model type, leaving uncertainty about whether certain model architectures inherently lead to higher or lower consistency between SHAP and LIME
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of SHAP-LIME correlations grouped by model type (e.g., linear models, tree-based models, neural networks) with statistical comparisons

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the size of the dataset or the number of features influence the consistency of SHAP and LIME explanations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study uses 4 datasets of varying sizes and complexities but does not explicitly test the effect of dataset size or feature count on explanation consistency
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore how scaling up the dataset or increasing the number of features might impact the agreement between SHAP and LIME
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying dataset size and feature count while measuring SHAP-LIME correlations to identify any patterns or thresholds

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are there specific features or feature types (e.g., categorical vs. numerical, highly correlated features) that consistently lead to discrepancies between SHAP and LIME explanations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study finds instances where SHAP and LIME disagree significantly but does not systematically analyze which feature characteristics cause these discrepancies
- Why unresolved: The paper does not categorize features or test how different feature properties affect explanation consistency
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic analysis of feature types and their impact on SHAP-LIME correlations, possibly through feature engineering or synthetic datasets

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the discrepancies between SHAP and LIME explanations be reduced or resolved by combining them into a hybrid explanation method?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study highlights the limitations of both SHAP and LIME but does not explore potential solutions like hybrid methods
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing the two methods rather than proposing or testing solutions to their inconsistencies
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of a hybrid explanation method that leverages both SHAP and LIME, with evaluation of its consistency and accuracy compared to the individual methods

## Limitations
- The study only examined tabular datasets, leaving open whether explanatory system dissonance extends to image or text data where local approximation methods may behave differently
- No statistical tests were performed to determine whether observed correlations significantly differ from random or whether differences between models are meaningful
- The correlation analysis treats SHAP and LIME as black boxes without investigating specific conditions under which their disagreement is most pronounced

## Confidence
- High Confidence: XAISuite framework successfully implements a unified pipeline for model training, explanation generation, and analysis across multiple explanatory systems
- Medium Confidence: The finding that SHAP-LIME correlation does not predict model accuracy is robust across the tested datasets and models
- Medium Confidence: The observation that maximum importance score correlations do not provide more consistent comparisons than average correlations
