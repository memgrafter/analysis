# Neural Architecture Design and Robustness: A Dataset

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06712
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06712
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces a novel dataset evaluating robustness of all 6,466 unique neural architectures from the NAS-Bench-201 search space against common adversarial attacks and corruptions.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel dataset evaluating robustness of all 6,466 unique neural architectures from the NAS-Bench-201 search space against common adversarial attacks and corruptions. The dataset facilitates research on architectural design choices for robustness and benchmarking training-free robustness measurements. Three use cases are presented: (i) evaluating Jacobian and Hessian-based measurements for robustness predictability, (ii) performing neural architecture search on robust accuracies, and (iii) analyzing how architectural design choices affect robustness. The results show that carefully crafting network topology can substantially improve robustness, with networks having the same parameter count ranging in mean adversarial robust accuracy from 20% to 41%.

## Method Summary
The dataset evaluates 6,466 unique architectures from NAS-Bench-201 on three image classification tasks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet16-120) using pretrained models from the benchmark. Each architecture is evaluated on multiple adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD, APGD, Square) with varying epsilon values and 15 common corruptions at 5 severity levels. The evaluations collect accuracy, confidence scores, and confusion matrices, stored in JSON files organized by dataset and attack/corruption type. The dataset also benchmarks training-free robustness measurements including Jacobian and Hessian-based metrics.

## Key Results
- The dataset covers all 6,466 unique architectures in NAS-Bench-201 evaluated for robustness against 4 attack types and 15 corruptions
- Networks with identical parameter counts show mean adversarial robust accuracy ranging from 20% to 41%
- Jacobian and Hessian-based measurements correlate well with robustness rankings for smaller epsilon values but not for larger ones

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Precomputing full architecture robustness evaluations eliminates expensive repeated evaluations during NAS search.
- **Mechanism:** By evaluating all 6,466 NAS-Bench-201 architectures once on multiple attacks and corruptions, the dataset provides a lookup table for robustness metrics, avoiding the need to retrain or reattack during search.
- **Core assumption:** The robustness characteristics of an architecture are invariant under the fixed training protocol used in NAS-Bench-201.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "evaluation of a network’s robustness is significantly more expensive than its evaluation for clean accuracy"
  - [section]: "the aim of this paper is to facilitate better streamlined research on architectural design choices with respect to their impact on robustness"
- **Break condition:** If robustness is highly sensitive to training hyperparameters or data augmentations not captured in the benchmark, the precomputed evaluations will mislead NAS search.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Correlation between Jacobian/Hessian-based measurements and empirical robustness enables differentiable, training-free robustness proxies in NAS.
- **Mechanism:** The dataset allows benchmarking of Frobenius norm of Jacobian and largest Hessian eigenvalue against true adversarial and corruption accuracies, validating their use as differentiable robustness signals.
- **Core assumption:** Small values of these measurements correspond to higher robustness, and the relationship holds across the full architecture space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "Jacobian-based and Hessian-based measurements correlate well for smaller ϵ values, but not for larger ϵ values"
  - [corpus]: No direct match; evidence is internal to the paper.
- **Break condition:** If adversarial attacks evolve beyond the scope of FGSM/PGD/APGD/Square, or if measurement methods change, the correlation may break.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Systematic exploration of architectural topology changes reveals robustness gains not visible in single-architecture studies.
- **Mechanism:** By evaluating the full NAS-Bench-201 search space, the paper identifies specific operation combinations (e.g., stacking convolutions) that consistently improve robustness.
- **Core assumption:** The NAS-Bench-201 search space is representative of useful architectural variations for robustness.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "networks with the same parameter count range in mean adversarial robust accuracy from 20%-41%"
  - [section]: "optimizing architecture design can have a substantial impact on the robustness of a network"
- **Break condition:** If the search space is too constrained or biased, discovered patterns may not generalize to larger or more diverse architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Dataset structure contains three main folders (cifar10, cifar100, ImageNet16-120), each containing JSON files keyed by attack/corruption and measurement type (accuracy, confidence, confusion matrix).

- **Critical path:** Load architecture parameters from NAS-Bench-201 checkpoint → Apply attack/corruption to test set → Collect accuracy, confidence, and confusion matrix → Store results in JSON under {dataset}/{key}_{measurement}.json.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Pros:** Complete coverage enables systematic analysis; precomputed results save time; multiple metrics provide rich data
  - **Cons:** Fixed training protocol may not reflect all robustness scenarios; large dataset size requires careful handling; correlations may not generalize beyond tested attacks

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Missing or inconsistent keys in JSON files
  - Epsilon values not matching metadata
  - Accuracy values outside valid [0,1] range
  - Confusion matrices not summing to dataset size

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do different architectural designs specifically impact robustness to different types of attacks and corruptions?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper shows that architectural changes can significantly affect robustness, with networks having the same parameter count ranging from 20% to 41% in mean adversarial robust accuracy. However, it does not provide detailed analysis of which specific architectural features contribute to robustness against different attack types.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper presents initial observations but does not conduct a comprehensive analysis of how specific architectural choices (e.g., types and arrangements of operations) affect robustness to different attacks and corruptions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Detailed analysis of architectural features (e.g., operation types, connectivity patterns) and their correlation with robustness against different attack types and corruptions, potentially through statistical analysis or machine learning models.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can training-free robustness measurements be improved to better predict network robustness across different attack magnitudes?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper evaluates Jacobian and Hessian-based measurements for robustness predictability but finds that these measurements correlate well with rankings for smaller epsilon values but not for larger epsilon values, especially in the case of APGD.
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper demonstrates the limitations of current training-free measurements, it does not propose or test methods to improve their accuracy for larger attack magnitudes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and validation of improved training-free measurements that maintain high correlation with robustness rankings across a wide range of attack magnitudes, potentially through incorporating additional architectural features or using more sophisticated mathematical models.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How do robustness and clean accuracy trade-offs vary across different image datasets and tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper evaluates robustness on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet16-120, showing some correlation between clean and adversarial accuracies across datasets, but the correlation becomes close to zero or even negative for multi-step attacks.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper provides initial insights into cross-dataset robustness but does not thoroughly investigate how the trade-offs between robustness and clean accuracy vary across different image datasets and tasks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comprehensive analysis of robustness-clean accuracy trade-offs across a diverse set of image datasets and tasks, potentially through systematic experiments or meta-analysis of existing results, to identify patterns and guidelines for balancing these competing objectives.

## Limitations

- The dataset's utility is fundamentally constrained by the fixed training protocol of NAS-Bench-201, which may not generalize to different hyperparameter settings or data augmentation strategies.
- The correlation between training-free measurements and robustness, while promising for smaller epsilon values, shows weaker relationships for stronger attacks, limiting their reliability as robustness proxies.
- The search space's representational capacity may not capture architectural variations that could yield even better robustness-performance tradeoffs.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The dataset's basic construction and evaluation methodology are sound and reproducible.
- **Medium confidence**: The observed correlation between Jacobian/Hessian measurements and robustness holds within the tested attack parameters but may not generalize to novel attack methods.
- **Medium confidence**: The architectural design principles identified (such as operation stacking) are meaningful within NAS-Bench-201 but require validation in larger, more diverse search spaces.
