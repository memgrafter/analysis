# Mole Recruitment: Poisoning of Image Classifiers via Selective Batch Sampling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.17080
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17080
- **Reference count:** 37
- **Primary result:** Novel data poisoning attack achieving up to 50% accuracy loss on targeted classes without modifying training data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Mole Recruitment, a novel data poisoning attack that degrades image classifier performance by selectively restructuring training batches using naturally occurring "moles" - training samples from one class that closely resemble samples from another class. Unlike traditional poisoning attacks that require modifying training data, this approach leverages the inherent ambiguity in datasets to systematically harm model accuracy. The attack was validated across multiple datasets including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and Food-101, demonstrating significant accuracy degradation for targeted classes while maintaining overall model performance on non-targeted classes.

## Method Summary
Mole Recruitment operates by first identifying "moles" - training samples that can be easily confused with other classes based on their softmax probabilities. The attack then restructures training batches by grouping these confounding samples together, exploiting their natural similarity to other classes. The method involves three main steps: mole identification through CLIP-based similarity analysis, batch restructuring by grouping similar samples, and training under this modified batch distribution. The attack is model-agnostic as it relies on naturally occurring data ambiguities rather than model-specific vulnerabilities.

## Key Results
- Up to 50% accuracy loss for individual targeted classes in offline experiments
- Up to 15% overall model accuracy degradation in offline settings
- 48% accuracy penalty for non-replay methods and 21% for replay-based methods in continual learning scenarios
- Attack successfully demonstrated on multiple datasets including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and Food-101
- Model-agnostic implementation using CLIP for mole identification across different architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The attack exploits the inherent ambiguity present in natural image datasets where certain samples from one class can be easily confused with another class. By systematically grouping these confusing samples together during training, the model is forced to develop weaker decision boundaries between the targeted classes and their confusable counterparts. The selective batch sampling creates a training distribution that emphasizes the confusion points, leading to degraded performance specifically for the targeted classes while leaving other classes relatively unaffected.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Selective batch sampling**: Strategically grouping similar/confusable samples together during training to influence model decision boundaries
   - *Why needed:* Enables targeted degradation without data modification
   - *Quick check:* Verify batch composition shows increased similarity scores

2. **Mole identification via CLIP**: Using vision-language models to find naturally ambiguous samples across classes
   - *Why needed:* Provides model-agnostic way to identify attack opportunities
   - *Quick check:* Compare mole identification consistency across different models

3. **Softmax probability analysis**: Using prediction confidence to identify samples that models find confusing
   - *Why needed:* Quantifies how easily samples can be mistaken for other classes
   - *Quick check:* Validate that identified moles have similar probabilities across multiple target classes

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map:** Data → Mole Identification → Batch Restructuring → Model Training → Performance Evaluation
- **Critical path:** Mole identification must complete before batch restructuring, which must complete before training begins
- **Design tradeoffs:** Accuracy degradation vs. detectability; model-agnostic identification vs. potentially less precise than model-specific approaches
- **Failure signatures:** Minimal accuracy degradation when moles are scarce; performance improvements when mole groups are too large; failure to maintain overall model performance
- **First 3 experiments to run:** 1) Baseline training without mole recruitment, 2) Training with randomly selected mole groups, 3) Training with progressively larger mole group sizes

## Limitations
- Heavy dependence on availability of naturally occurring moles in training data
- Assumes full access to training batches during poisoning process
- Limited evaluation in realistic deployment scenarios with varying data distributions
- Does not address potential defensive mechanisms or robustness against common training interventions

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in core mechanism and controlled experiment results
- **Medium confidence** in generalizability across different datasets and training conditions
- **Low confidence** in practical deployability without access to full training batches

## Next Checks
1. Test attack effectiveness when moles are artificially removed or scarce in training data
2. Evaluate performance under realistic constraints like distributed training or partial batch access
3. Assess attack robustness against common defensive training techniques (data augmentation, regularization, anomaly detection)
