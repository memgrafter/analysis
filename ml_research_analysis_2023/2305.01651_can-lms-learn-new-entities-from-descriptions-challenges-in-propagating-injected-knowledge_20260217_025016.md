# Can LMs Learn New Entities from Descriptions? Challenges in Propagating Injected Knowledge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01651
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01651
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 36
- Key outcome: Existing knowledge editing techniques fail to propagate new entity knowledge for complex inferences, while context prepending shows robust performance gains.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the challenge of propagating knowledge about new entities to language models given only their definitions. The authors evaluate existing knowledge editing techniques (finetuning and MEND) against a simple context prepending approach across two datasets: ECBD and Entity Inferences. Surprisingly, while editing methods succeed at reproducing injected facts when there is direct lexical overlap, they fail to generalize this knowledge for more complex inferences. Context prepending consistently outperforms these methods across all settings, suggesting that traditional knowledge editing approaches may not effectively capture the nuances needed for open-ended reasoning about new entities.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate knowledge propagation methods by first injecting entity definitions into language models using finetuning, MEND, and context prepending approaches. They then measure performance on two tasks: exact fact reproduction (ECBD dataset) and complex entity inferences (Entity Inferences dataset). The ECBD dataset contains probes for direct attribute matching, while Entity Inferences includes both explicit and implicit reasoning tasks. Models tested include Llama-1.3B, MPT-770M, and Llama-1.5B across different training configurations. Performance is measured using exact match accuracy for the probing tasks.

## Key Results
- Knowledge editing methods (finetuning and MEND) successfully reproduce injected facts when there is direct lexical overlap with the target inference
- Context prepending consistently outperforms knowledge editing methods across all settings and dataset types
- Knowledge editing approaches fail to propagate knowledge for complex inferences that require reasoning beyond exact fact matching
- Lexical overlap between injected definitions and target inferences is the primary factor determining success for editing methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Knowledge editing methods rely on modifying model parameters to encode new facts, which works well for direct fact retrieval but struggles with reasoning that requires synthesizing information. Context prepending provides the model with relevant information at inference time, allowing it to leverage its existing reasoning capabilities. The success of context prepending suggests that LMs are better at reasoning over provided context than at integrating new knowledge into their parameter space for complex inference tasks.

## Limitations
- The study relies on synthetic datasets that may not fully capture real-world entity propagation complexity
- Results are based on relatively small language models and may not generalize to larger, more capable models
- Context prepending may have limitations in scenarios with strict token budgets or verbose entity definitions

## Confidence
- Knowledge editing methods' effectiveness on direct fact reproduction: High
- Knowledge editing methods' failure on complex inferences: Medium
- Context prepending as superior approach: Medium
