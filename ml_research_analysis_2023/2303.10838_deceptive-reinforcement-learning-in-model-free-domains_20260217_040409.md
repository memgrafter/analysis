# Deceptive Reinforcement Learning in Model-Free Domains

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10838
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10838
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: DEAM achieves similar performance to optimal model-based AM, reaching real goal in 1.1× optimal path cost vs 1.6× for model-free AM.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DEAM, a deceptive reinforcement learning method for model-free and continuous action space domains. DEAM addresses the state space exploration problem of the ambiguity model (AM) by learning a deceptive policy during training rather than pre-training separate subagents. The method uses an entropy-based policy to select actions that maximize reward while keeping the true reward function private from observers. DEAM achieves state-of-the-art performance in both discrete and continuous path planning environments.

## Method Summary
DEAM learns a deceptive policy by training actor-critic subagents for each candidate reward function simultaneously. During training, DEAM uses a soft-maximum policy with decaying temperature to explore states along deceptive trajectories. Experiences are shared between all subagents, improving training efficiency compared to AM's separate pre-training approach. DEAM extends to continuous action spaces by using actor-critic networks instead of Q-learning. The method prunes actions that don't sufficiently satisfy the reward objective and selects actions that maximize the entropy of reward function probabilities.

## Key Results
- DEAM outperforms model-free AM in path cost, deceptiveness, and training efficiency
- DEAM reaches real goal in 1.1× optimal path cost compared to 1.6× for model-free AM
- DEAM is applicable in continuous action spaces, unlike AM's Q-learning approach
- DEAM explores states along deceptive trajectories more effectively than AM's separate pre-training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** DEAM's soft-maximum policy with decaying temperature enables more effective exploration than AM's separate pre-training of subagents.
- **Mechanism:** During training, DEAM uses a temperature parameter τ that starts high, allowing stochastic selection of actions. This encourages all subagents to explore different parts of the state space, leading to more accurate Q-values for states visited by the deceptive policy. As τ decays, the policy becomes more deterministic, exploiting the learned Q-values.
- **Core assumption:** The soft-maximum policy with decaying temperature provides sufficient exploration early in training to learn accurate Q-values for states visited by the deceptive policy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "DEAM, which learns using the deceptive policy during training, leading to targeted exploration of the state space."
  - [section 5] "By training with a deceptive policy, DEAM explores states along deceptive trajectories..."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** DEAM's shared experiences between subagents improve training efficiency compared to AM's separate pre-training.
- **Mechanism:** DEAM adds the experience tuple (st, a*, st+1, ri(st, a*, st+1)) to the replay buffer of every subagent, allowing them to learn from all environment interactions. This is more efficient than AM's separate pre-training, where each subagent only learns from its own experiences.
- **Core assumption:** Sharing experiences between subagents leads to more efficient learning than separate pre-training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5] "DEAM adds (st,a∗,st+1,ri(st,a∗,st+1)) to the replay buffer of every subagent (line 15) which improves policies and Q-functions at the end of each epoch (line 19)."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** DEAM's use of actor-critic subagents enables extension to continuous action spaces, unlike AM's Q-learning approach.
- **Mechanism:** DEAM uses actor-critic subagents, where the policy-based actor submits actions and the Q-learning critic calculates Q-differences. This allows DEAM to handle continuous action spaces, whereas AM's Q-learning approach requires maximization over the action space, which is intractable in continuous domains.
- **Core assumption:** Actor-critic methods can effectively handle continuous action spaces.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "DEAM is also applicable in continuous action spaces."
  - [section 5] "We make three changes to enable application in continuous action space domains. First, we use AC subagents to learn Q-functions."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - DEAM main agent -> AC subagents (one per candidate reward function) -> Replay buffers -> Observer model -> Pruning mechanism

- **Critical path:**
  1. Each subagent submits a candidate action based on its policy
  2. DEAM forms a candidate action set and prunes actions that don't meet the reward threshold
  3. DEAM calculates the entropy of the reward function probabilities for each remaining action
  4. DEAM selects the action that soft-maximizes entropy
  5. DEAM updates the environment and adds the experience to each subagent's replay buffer
  6. Subagents update their policies and Q-functions using the replay buffer

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Soft-maximum policy vs hard-maximum policy: Soft-maximum allows for more exploration but may lead to suboptimal actions; hard-maximum is more deterministic but may converge prematurely.
  - Shared experiences vs separate pre-training: Shared experiences are more efficient but may introduce instability; separate pre-training is more stable but less efficient.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - DEAM fails to reach the real goal consistently: This may indicate that the pruning threshold is too low or the temperature decay is too fast.
  - DEAM selects inefficient actions: This may indicate that the Q-values for the deceptive part of the state space are inaccurate.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Evaluate DEAM's performance on a simple discrete path planning task with known dynamics to verify that it can learn a deceptive policy.
  2. Compare DEAM's training efficiency to AM's separate pre-training approach on a discrete task to quantify the benefits of shared experiences.
  3. Test DEAM's ability to handle continuous action spaces by evaluating its performance on a simple continuous control task.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would DEAM perform with a more sophisticated observer model that is aware of being deceived?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention this as a limitation and future work direction, stating they only investigate naive observers.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates DEAM against passive and active adversaries using naive observer models. A more sophisticated observer could potentially detect and counteract DEAM's deception.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing DEAM's performance against various observer models of increasing sophistication, from naive to aware of deception.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can DEAM be extended to partially observable environments?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors only evaluate DEAM in fully observable environments and list this as a limitation.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate DEAM's performance in partially observable environments, which are more realistic in many applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating DEAM's performance in partially observable environments, comparing it to other approaches like POMDP-based methods.

### Open Question 3

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors conduct a hyperparameter study investigating the impact of pruning constant, temperature, and decay rate.
- Why unresolved: While the study provides insights, it is unclear how DEAM's performance would be affected by different hyperparameter choices in various environments and tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive hyperparameter tuning experiments across a wide range of environments and tasks, identifying the optimal hyperparameter settings for different scenarios.

## Limitations
- Paper lacks detailed experimental results comparing DEAM to other state-of-the-art deceptive RL methods
- No ablation studies are provided to quantify the individual contributions of key components
- Limited discussion of computational overhead compared to baseline methods, particularly in continuous action spaces

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: DEAM's ability to outperform model-free AM in terms of training efficiency and path cost in discrete environments
- **Medium confidence**: DEAM's extension to continuous action spaces
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of shared experiences and entropy-based exploration
