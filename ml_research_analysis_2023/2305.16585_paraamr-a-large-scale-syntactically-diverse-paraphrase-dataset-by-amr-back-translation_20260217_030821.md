# ParaAMR: A Large-Scale Syntactically Diverse Paraphrase Dataset by AMR Back-Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16585
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16585
- Reference count: 22
- Contains 15.5 million paraphrase pairs with 6.91 paraphrases per source sentence on average

## Executive Summary
ParaAMR introduces a novel method for generating large-scale, syntactically diverse paraphrase datasets using AMR back-translation. The approach encodes source sentences to AMR graphs, modifies graph focus to create new graphs, linearizes them, and decodes back to sentences. This produces paraphrases that maintain semantic similarity while increasing syntactic diversity compared to existing datasets. Quantitative and human evaluations confirm ParaAMR achieves higher syntactic diversity (TED-3 score of 5.86 vs. 3.28-4.04) while maintaining comparable semantic similarity (82.05% vs. 81.77-84.28%), demonstrating advantages across multiple downstream tasks.

## Method Summary
The ParaAMR methodology employs AMR back-translation to generate paraphrases. Starting with a source sentence, it first encodes to an AMR graph representation, then modifies the graph's focus by changing which argument or semantic role is emphasized. This modified graph is then linearized and decoded back into natural language text. The key innovation lies in using AMR graph modifications as a controllable mechanism for introducing syntactic diversity while preserving semantic content. This process is applied at scale to generate 15.5 million paraphrase pairs, achieving an average of 6.91 paraphrases per source sentence with significantly higher syntactic diversity than existing datasets.

## Key Results
- Achieved TED-3 syntactic diversity score of 5.86 compared to 3.28-4.04 for existing datasets
- Maintained semantic similarity of 82.05% compared to 81.77-84.28% for existing datasets
- Human evaluation confirmed higher syntactic diversity (1.90 vs. 1.78-1.80) while maintaining similar semantic similarity (2.00 vs. 2.03-2.04)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The AMR-based approach works by decoupling semantic content from syntactic structure. By encoding sentences into AMR graphs that capture meaning independently of surface form, the system can manipulate syntactic structure through graph modifications without altering core semantics. Changing the focus of AMR graphs (which arguments or relations are emphasized) naturally produces syntactically varied outputs while preserving meaning, as the underlying semantic relationships remain intact even as their expression changes. This mechanism provides more controlled diversity than pure language model generation while being more systematic than simple lexical substitutions.

## Foundational Learning

**AMR (Abstract Meaning Representation)**: A semantic graph representation that captures the meaning of sentences independent of syntactic form. Why needed: Provides a meaning-preserving intermediate representation that can be modified without changing semantic content. Quick check: Can verify AMR graphs preserve meaning by comparing semantic similarity scores before and after modification.

**Graph Linearization**: The process of converting graph structures into sequential text that can be processed by language models. Why needed: Enables standard sequence-to-sequence models to work with graph-structured AMR data. Quick check: Can test by ensuring linearized graphs can be accurately reconstructed back to original graph structure.

**Back-Translation**: A technique where text is translated to an intermediate representation and back to the same language, potentially generating variations. Why needed: Provides a systematic way to generate paraphrases while controlling for semantic preservation. Quick check: Can verify by measuring semantic similarity between source and generated paraphrases.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Source Sentence -> AMR Encoder -> AMR Graph Modification -> Linearization -> Decoder -> Paraphrase Sentence

**Critical Path**: The AMR encoding and graph modification stages are most critical, as errors here propagate through the entire pipeline and directly affect both diversity and semantic preservation quality.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades some computational complexity (AMR encoding/decoding) for controlled diversity generation, versus simpler but less controllable paraphrase generation methods. The use of AMR graphs enables semantic preservation but requires additional preprocessing infrastructure.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor semantic preservation manifests as low similarity scores between source and paraphrase, while insufficient diversity appears as low TED-3 scores and repetitive syntactic patterns across generated paraphrases.

**First Experiments**: 1) Generate paraphrases for a small test set and manually evaluate semantic preservation, 2) Measure syntactic diversity using automated metrics like TED-3 on initial outputs, 3) Compare graph modification strategies to determine which produces optimal diversity-preservation tradeoffs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but potential areas for future work include: characterizing biases introduced through the AMR linearization and decoding process, exploring more sophisticated graph modification strategies beyond simple focus changes, and investigating how different AMR parsing models affect the quality and diversity of generated paraphrases.

## Limitations
- Reliance on AMR graph modifications may produce surface-level rearrangements rather than meaningful linguistic variation
- Potential biases introduced through AMR linearization and decoding process are not fully characterized
- Human evaluation methodology and inter-annotator agreement details are not fully specified

## Confidence
- High confidence: Basic methodology of AMR back-translation is sound and dataset size (15.5M pairs) is verifiable
- Medium confidence: Comparative results showing increased syntactic diversity while maintaining semantic similarity
- Medium confidence: Downstream task experiments demonstrating advantages of ParaAMR
