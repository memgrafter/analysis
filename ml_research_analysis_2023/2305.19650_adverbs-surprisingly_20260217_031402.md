# Adverbs, Surprisingly

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19650
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19650
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Large language models struggle with adverb meaning, particularly speaker-oriented adverbs, and FrameNet is proposed as a framework to address this gap

## Executive Summary
This paper highlights the critical neglect of adverbs, especially speaker-oriented ones, in computational linguistics and large language models. The authors systematically evaluate natural language inference (NLI) models on probing datasets containing adverbs, revealing that these models often fail to infer meaning from adverbs and frequently ignore them entirely. FrameNet, with its frame-based semantic descriptions including semantic roles and null instantiation, is proposed as a promising framework for capturing adverb meaning. The authors suggest extending FrameNet with more adverb annotations to improve NLP models' understanding of adverbial semantics.

## Method Summary
The study creates naturalistic and synthetic probing datasets containing premise-hypothesis pairs with speaker-oriented adverbs (likelihood, unattributed-information, degree, and obviousness adverbs). The naturalistic dataset is based on FrameNet data, while the synthetic dataset uses template-based generation. Pre-trained SNLI models (RoBERTa-large) are evaluated on these datasets without fine-tuning. The models' predictions (entailment, contradiction, neutral) and their probabilities are analyzed to assess their ability to infer meaning from adverbs. Error rates are calculated for each adverb class in the naturalistic dataset and overall for the synthetic dataset.

## Key Results
- Large language models consistently struggle with adverb meaning, often ignoring adverbs entirely in semantic inference tasks
- SNLI models show high error rates on naturalistic datasets containing speaker-oriented adverbs
- FrameNet's framework, particularly its ability to describe ambiguity, semantic roles, and null instantiation, is identified as promising for adverb analysis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FrameNet captures adverb meaning through frames, frame elements, and null instantiation
- Mechanism: Frames define semantic contexts, frame elements capture semantic roles and inferences, and null instantiation records unexpressed semantic roles (e.g., speaker)
- Core assumption: FrameNet's descriptive devices for predicates generalize to adverbs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We suggest that using Frame Semantics for characterizing word meaning, as in FrameNet, provides a promising approach to adverb analysis, given its ability to describe ambiguity, semantic roles, and null instantiation."
  - [section 4.2]: "In FrameNet, frame elements are associated with (classes of) inferences...Such inferences can capture important aspects of adverb meaning..."
- Break condition: If FrameNet's framework proves insufficient for capturing the full range of adverb meanings and inferences

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Large language models struggle with adverb meaning due to training data bias
- Mechanism: Language models are primarily trained on data where main participants (subjects/objects) dominate semantic similarity, leading to neglect of adverb contributions
- Core assumption: The training data for language models lacks sufficient adverb-focused examples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SOTA language models consistently struggle with adverb meaning..."
  - [section 3.5]: "The study found that the sentences' main participants (subjects and objects) primarily determine the semantic similarity of sentence pairs, which is largely independent of adverbs."
- Break condition: If language models can be retrained on adverb-rich data to overcome this bias

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: FrameNet can be extended with adverb annotations to improve NLP models
- Mechanism: By annotating adverbs in FrameNet with appropriate frames and frame elements, a resource is created for training and evaluating NLP models on adverb meaning
- Core assumption: The effort required to extend FrameNet with adverb annotations is feasible and worthwhile
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "FrameNet, which provides frame-based semantic descriptions including semantic roles and null instantiation, is proposed as a promising framework for capturing adverb meaning."
  - [section 4.4]: "At the practical level, the time is ripe to add many more adverbs to appropriate existing frames and to create new frames for adverbs as needed."
- Break condition: If the effort required to extend FrameNet proves too resource-intensive relative to the benefits

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - FrameNet database (existing frames, frame elements, lexical units)
  - Adverb annotation pipeline (identifying adverbs, assigning frames, defining frame elements)
  - NLI model evaluation framework (probing datasets, model predictions, analysis)
  - Potential NLP applications (semantic role labeling, paraphrasing, question answering)

- Critical path:
  1. Analyze existing FrameNet frames for adverb coverage
  2. Extend FrameNet with adverb annotations (new frames, frame elements)
  3. Create probing datasets for adverb inference
  4. Evaluate NLI models on adverb inference tasks
  5. Integrate adverb annotations into NLP applications

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Expert annotation vs. crowdsourcing vs. automatic extraction for adverb annotations
  - Granularity of adverb frames (broad vs. specific)
  - Balancing coverage and accuracy in probing datasets

- Failure signatures:
  - Language models still struggle with adverb meaning despite FrameNet extensions
  - Probing datasets fail to capture the full range of adverb inferences
  - NLP applications do not benefit from adverb annotations

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate NLI models on the naturalistic and synthetic probing datasets to establish baseline performance on adverb inference
  2. Extend FrameNet with annotations for a small set of speaker-oriented adverbs and re-evaluate NLI models to assess the impact of the annotations
  3. Integrate adverb annotations into a semantic role labeling system and evaluate its performance on adverb-rich sentences

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do adverbs behave in languages other than English, particularly in typologically different languages?
- What specific mechanisms in large language models cause the observed difficulties with adverb understanding?
- How can FrameNet be effectively extended to include more adverbs and capture their nuanced meanings?

## Limitations
- The study focuses only on English, limiting generalizability to other languages
- The resource requirements for extending FrameNet with comprehensive adverb annotations are not fully explored
- The paper demonstrates the problem but does not investigate the specific architectural limitations in language models causing adverb neglect

## Confidence
- High confidence: Language models struggle with speaker-oriented adverbs
- Medium confidence: FrameNet framework is suitable for adverb representation
- Medium confidence: Extending FrameNet with adverb annotations will improve NLP models

## Next Checks

3. Compare the performance of language models trained on adverb-rich data versus standard models to isolate whether training data bias or architectural limitations drive adverb neglect
