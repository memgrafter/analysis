# Generating High Fidelity Synthetic Data via Coreset selection and Entropic Regularization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.00138
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00138
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: Improves semi-supervised learning accuracy on SVHN by filtering low-quality synthetic samples using entropic regularization and coreset selection

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of poor quality synthetic samples in semi-supervised learning by combining a generative classifier with coreset selection. The authors propose using an Energy-Based Model (EBM) as a generative classifier and apply entropic regularization to filter low-quality samples. They then use CRAIG, a coreset selection algorithm, to select a subset of high-quality samples that best approximate the full dataset gradient. The method shows significant improvement in classification accuracy on the SVHN dataset compared to using all synthetic samples or no augmentation, outperforming hand-crafted data augmentation techniques.

## Method Summary
The method combines three key components: an Energy-Based Model (EBM) serving as a generative classifier, entropic regularization for sample quality filtering, and CRAIG coreset selection for efficient subset selection. The EBM generates synthetic samples which are then filtered through entropic regularization to remove low-quality examples. The remaining high-quality samples are processed through CRAIG, which selects a subset that best approximates the gradient of the full dataset. This approach addresses the common issue in semi-supervised learning where generative models produce poor quality synthetic samples that can degrade classifier performance.

## Key Results
- Achieves significant improvement in classification accuracy on SVHN dataset compared to using all synthetic samples
- Outperforms traditional hand-crafted data augmentation techniques
- Demonstrates the effectiveness of combining entropic regularization with coreset selection for sample quality improvement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by addressing the fundamental problem of poor sample quality in generative models used for semi-supervised learning. The EBM-based generative classifier provides a principled way to generate samples with associated confidence scores. Entropic regularization acts as a filter by penalizing samples with high entropy (uncertainty), effectively removing low-quality synthetic examples. The CRAIG coreset selection then ensures computational efficiency by selecting a representative subset of the remaining high-quality samples that captures the essential information needed for training, rather than using all available synthetic data.

## Foundational Learning
- **Energy-Based Models (EBMs)**: Probabilistic models that learn an energy function to represent the data distribution; needed for generating samples with associated confidence scores
- **Entropic Regularization**: A technique that penalizes high-entropy predictions to filter uncertain samples; needed to remove low-quality synthetic examples
- **Coreset Selection**: The process of selecting a small, representative subset of data that approximates the full dataset's statistical properties; needed for computational efficiency
- **CRAIG Algorithm**: A specific coreset selection method that optimizes for gradient approximation; needed to ensure the selected subset maintains training effectiveness

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: EBM -> Entropic Regularization -> CRAIG Selection -> Classifier Training
- **Critical Path**: Sample generation → Quality filtering → Subset selection → Model training
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Quality vs quantity (filtering vs using all samples), computational efficiency vs coverage (coreset size selection), model complexity vs performance
- **Failure Signatures**: Mode collapse in EBM leading to poor sample diversity, overly aggressive filtering removing useful samples, coreset selection missing important data regions
- **First Experiments**: 1) Baseline comparison with no augmentation, 2) Comparison with standard data augmentation, 3) Ablation study removing entropic regularization

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to single dataset (SVHN), limiting generalizability
- Computational overhead of entropic regularization and coreset selection not quantified
- No discussion of scalability to larger datasets or more complex models
- Limited exploration of hyperparameter β for entropic regularization

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: Combining coreset selection with entropic regularization improves sample quality for semi-supervised learning on SVHN.

**Medium Confidence**: This approach outperforms hand-crafted augmentation methods, though comparison could be strengthened with more diverse techniques.

**Low Confidence**: Scalability claims regarding applicability to larger datasets are not empirically validated.
