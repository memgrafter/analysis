# Linear Convergence of Reshuffling Kaczmarz Methods With Sparse Constraints

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10123
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10123
- Reference count: 40
- First theoretical convergence guarantees for KZIHT showing linear convergence up to optimal statistical bias with reshuffling sampling scheme

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first theoretical convergence guarantees for KZIHT (Kaczmarz-based Iterative Hard Thresholding), a hybrid method combining Kaczmarz and IHT for compressed sensing. The authors prove that KZIHT converges linearly with reshuffling sampling scheme up to optimal statistical bias. They also propose KZPT (Kaczmarz with Periodic Thresholding), a generalization of KZIHT with periodic thresholding and two types of step sizes. The authors establish linear convergence guarantees for KZPT for randomly subsampled bounded orthonormal systems and mean-zero isotropic sub-Gaussian random matrices.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes two algorithms: KZIHT performs Kaczmarz iterations followed by hard thresholding every epoch, while KZPT performs Kaczmarz iterations with periodic thresholding every p iterations, using two different step sizes (γ for Kaczmarz inner loop, λ for IHT gradient step). The convergence analysis relies on Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) bounds for submatrices and concentration inequalities for sub-Gaussian random matrices. The methods are shown to converge linearly to the solution of sparse linear systems up to optimal statistical bias O(√(s log N/m)) for the noise level.

## Key Results
- KZIHT converges linearly with reshuffling sampling scheme up to optimal statistical bias
- KZPT with periodic thresholding achieves faster convergence than KZIHT by balancing gradient descent and thresholding steps
- KZPT maintains linear convergence up to optimal statistical bias for sparse linear regression problems
- Reshuffling sampling scheme provides better convergence than randomized sampling with replacement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: KZPT with periodic thresholding achieves faster convergence than KZIHT by balancing gradient descent and thresholding steps.
- Mechanism: The algorithm performs p KZ iterations followed by a gradient step and thresholding. This periodic approach allows the iterate to move closer to the sparse solution before thresholding, reducing the number of iterations needed to converge.
- Core assumption: The RIP constant of submatrices A(i) is bounded by δ(p), which holds with high probability for randomly subsampled BOS and sub-Gaussian matrices.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our analysis shows that KZPT with an optimal thresholding period outperforms KZIHT."
  - [section 4]: Theorem 10 provides the convergence rate of KZPT with period p and Corollary 11 shows that KZPT with optimal period outperforms KZIHT.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in related papers about KZPT's periodic thresholding advantage.
- Break condition: If the RIP constant δ(p) is too large (δ(p) ≥ 1/2), the convergence guarantee fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: KZPT maintains linear convergence up to optimal statistical bias for sparse linear regression problems.
- Mechanism: The algorithm's convergence rate is controlled by the RIP constant δ and the noise level. By choosing appropriate step sizes γ and λ, the algorithm achieves a convergence rate of (4δ)^k, which is linear in the number of epochs k.
- Core assumption: The noise vector e has sub-Gaussian components, ensuring that the residual error term is of the order O(√(s log N/m)).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our analysis shows that KZPT with an optimal thresholding period outperforms KZIHT."
  - [section 3.2]: Theorem 5 establishes the linear convergence of KZPT for sub-Gaussian random matrices up to optimal statistical bias.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in related papers about KZPT's statistical bias.
- Break condition: If the noise level is too high or the RIP constant δ is too large, the convergence guarantee may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The reshuffling sampling scheme provides better convergence than randomized sampling with replacement.
- Mechanism: By using all rows of the matrix A in each epoch without replacement, the algorithm ensures that the iterate is projected onto the solution space of each equation exactly once per epoch. This reduces the variance in the stochastic gradient estimate compared to sampling with replacement.
- Core assumption: The reshuffling scheme maintains the orthogonality of the rows, which is crucial for the convergence analysis.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We provide the first theoretical convergence guarantees for KZIHT by showing that it converges linearly to the solution of a system with sparsity constraints up to optimal statistical bias when the reshuffling data sampling scheme is used."
  - [section 5.5]: The experiments show that reshuffling KZIHT converges faster than randomized KZIHT.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in related papers about reshuffling's advantage over randomized sampling.
- Break condition: If the reshuffling scheme is not properly implemented or the matrix rows are not orthogonal, the convergence guarantee may not hold.

- Critical path: The critical path for KZPT's convergence is the interplay between the KZ iterations, the gradient step, and the thresholding operation. The algorithm's performance depends on the choice of step sizes γ and λ, the period p, and the properties of the sensing matrix A.

- Design tradeoffs: The main design tradeoff in KZPT is between the period p and the convergence rate. A larger period p reduces the frequency of thresholding, which can lead to faster convergence but may also increase the risk of overshooting the sparse solution. Conversely, a smaller period p ensures more frequent thresholding but may slow down the convergence.

- Failure signatures: KZPT may fail to converge if the RIP constant δ(p) is too large, the noise level is too high, or the step sizes γ and λ are not chosen appropriately. Additionally, if the sensing matrix A does not satisfy the required properties (e.g., randomly subsampled BOS or sub-Gaussian), the convergence guarantee may not hold.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: What is the optimal period p for KZPT that minimizes the error bound across different signal sparsity levels and measurement matrices?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives an optimal period p for KZPT in Theorem 10 and Corollary 11, showing it outperforms KZIHT for noiseless cases
- Why unresolved: The analysis assumes noiseless settings and specific matrix types (BOS and sub-Gaussian). Real-world scenarios involve noise and various matrix structures
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments comparing KZPT performance with different p values across various noise levels, matrix types (Gaussian, Bernoulli), and sparsity levels

- Why unresolved: The analysis relies heavily on specific properties of BOS and sub-Gaussian matrices (orthogonality, RIP bounds), which may not hold for other matrix types
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of new analytical techniques to handle other matrix classes (e.g., structured random matrices, deterministic matrices) or empirical evidence of KZPT performance on such matrices

## Limitations

- Theoretical analysis relies on RIP conditions with unspecified universal constants that may limit practical applicability
- Optimal thresholding period p for KZPT in the noisy setting remains an open question
- Convergence guarantees require sufficiently large sample sizes (m ≥ Cs log⁴ N for BOS, m ≥ C₁s ln(N/s)(K ln m)² for sub-Gaussian) which may be impractical
