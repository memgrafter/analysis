# Two Kinds of Recall

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.10527
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10527
- **Reference count**: 7
- **Primary result**: Neural QA models excel at d-recall (diversity) but fail at e-recall (exhaustiveness) compared to pattern-based methods

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies a critical gap in NLP evaluation by distinguishing between two types of recall: d-recall (diversity) and e-recall (exhaustiveness). While neural models trained on benchmarks like SQuAD achieve high d-recall by finding diverse answer patterns, they often miss obvious answers in structured contexts where pattern-based methods excel. The authors demonstrate this through experiments comparing a SQuAD-trained RoBERTa-large QA model against pattern matching over dependency trees, showing the model produces diverse answers but frequently fails to identify clear answers that pattern-based methods catch. The work argues that current benchmarks focus on d-recall, leaving e-recall under-evaluated and calling for evaluation methods that assess both dimensions.

## Method Summary
The authors compare recall performance between a neural QA model and pattern-based methods using PubMed and Wikipedia sentences containing specific terms. They use a pre-trained RoBERTa-large model (deepset/roberta-large-squad2) for extractive QA, applying it to sentences to answer questions like "Which molecule relates to pain?" The pattern-based method uses syntactic dependency trees with Universal Dependencies v1 to identify sentences matching specific patterns (e.g., "PERSON was educated at PLACE"). The evaluation measures both d-recall (diversity of answers found) and e-recall (exhaustiveness in finding all relevant answers), comparing the QA model's outputs against pattern-based results to highlight the differential performance.

## Key Results
- Neural QA models trained on SQuAD achieve high d-recall but significantly lower e-recall compared to pattern-based methods
- The RoBERTa-large model frequently outputs "No-Answer" for sentences where pattern matching successfully identifies clear answers
- Current benchmarks focus on d-recall evaluation, systematically neglecting e-recall assessment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism underlying this work is the fundamental difference in how neural models and pattern-based methods process information. Neural models learn statistical patterns from training data and excel at generalizing to novel phrasings (d-recall), but they lack explicit structural reasoning about syntactic relationships. Pattern-based methods directly encode syntactic dependencies and can deterministically identify specific structural relationships (e-recall), but they're limited to predefined patterns. This creates a natural trade-off where neural models find diverse answers through learned generalization while missing obvious answers in structured contexts that don't match their training distribution.

## Foundational Learning

**Syntactic dependency parsing**: Understanding how words relate to each other in sentences through labeled dependencies like subject, object, and modifier relationships. Needed to implement the pattern-based method that achieves high e-recall. Quick check: Can you identify the subject and object in a simple sentence using dependency labels?

**Extractive QA models**: Neural architectures that identify answer spans within text given questions, typically using transformer-based models like RoBERTa. Needed to demonstrate the d-recall capabilities of modern neural approaches. Quick check: Can you explain how a QA model distinguishes between answerable and unanswerable questions?

**Recall evaluation metrics**: Methods for measuring both diversity (d-recall) and exhaustiveness (e-recall) in system outputs. Needed to properly characterize the different strengths of neural versus pattern-based approaches. Quick check: Can you design a metric that captures both finding all correct answers and finding diverse correct answers?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Sentence retrieval -> Syntactic parsing -> Pattern matching (pattern-based method) OR Question answering (neural method) -> Answer comparison -> Recall evaluation

**Critical path**: The most important evaluation path is: Input sentences containing target terms → Apply both methods → Compare answer sets → Calculate d-recall and e-recall metrics

**Design tradeoffs**: Neural models offer flexibility and generalization but require large training data and may miss obvious answers; pattern-based methods are deterministic and complete for defined patterns but inflexible and require manual pattern engineering

**Failure signatures**: QA model outputs "No-Answer" for clearly answerable sentences with simple syntactic structures; pattern-based method misses answers that require semantic generalization beyond syntactic patterns

## Limitations
- The claim that benchmarks neglect e-recall is inferred rather than empirically demonstrated through benchmark analysis
- The analysis is limited to extractive QA and doesn't explore whether the d-recall/e-recall distinction applies broadly across other NLP tasks
- The reproducibility plan is constrained by missing implementation details for sentence retrieval and pattern matching specifics

## Confidence
- **High**: Experimental observations showing differential performance between neural models and pattern-based methods
- **Medium**: Broader claims about evaluation practices and general applicability of the d-recall/e-recall framework
