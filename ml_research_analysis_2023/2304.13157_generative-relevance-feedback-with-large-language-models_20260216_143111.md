# Generative Relevance Feedback with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13157
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13157
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GRF achieves 5-19% improvement in MAP and 17-24% improvement in NDCG@10 compared to RM3 expansion on document ranking benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Generative Relevance Feedback (GRF), a novel approach for query expansion that leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate relevant text independent of first-pass retrieval. Unlike traditional pseudo-relevance feedback methods, GRF builds probabilistic feedback models from long-form text generated by LLMs, tackling the vocabulary mismatch problem. Evaluations on four document ranking benchmarks show that GRF significantly outperforms previous PRF methods.

## Method Summary
GRF uses LLM-generated text as input for probabilistic query expansion models. The method generates text across 10 subtasks (keywords, entities, chain-of-thought reasoning, facts, news articles, documents, essays, etc.) and combines text from all generation subtasks. The approach is evaluated using GPT-3 (text-davinci-002) on four document ranking benchmarks (TREC Robust04, CODEC, TREC DL-19, and TREC DL-20), with effectiveness measured using MAP, NDCG@10, and Recall@1000.

## Key Results
- GRF significantly outperforms previous PRF methods, achieving 5-19% improvement in MAP and 17-24% improvement in NDCG@10 compared to RM3 expansion
- Long-form text generation in the style of the target dataset is most effective
- Combining text across all generation subtasks further improves effectiveness over any standalone generation subtask
- GRF achieves the best Recall@1k compared to state-of-the-art sparse, dense, and learned sparse PRF models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Long-form text generation in the style of the target dataset is most effective for query expansion.
- Mechanism: LLM-generated text that closely matches the target dataset's style provides more relevant context and vocabulary for query expansion.
- Core assumption: The LLM can generate text that is semantically and stylistically similar to the target dataset's relevant documents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find that long-form text generation in the style of the target dataset is most effective"
  - [section 5.1] "Furthermore, we find most effective generation subtasks are aligned with the style of the target dataset"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus analysis did not provide specific insights on style alignment effectiveness
- Break condition: If the LLM cannot generate text that matches the target dataset's style, the effectiveness of the query expansion would decrease.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Combining text across all generation subtasks improves effectiveness over any standalone generation subtask.
- Mechanism: Aggregating text from diverse generation subtasks exposes tail knowledge and uncommon synonyms, leading to a more comprehensive relevance model.
- Core assumption: Terms consistently generated across subtasks are likely useful for expansion, and diverse subtasks expose a broader range of relevant vocabulary.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Combining text across all generation subtasks further improves effectiveness"
  - [section 5.1] "Although we see significant improvements from some standalone generation subtasks... the full GRF method is consistently as good if not better than any standalone subtask"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - the corpus analysis did not specifically address the effectiveness of combining subtasks
- Break condition: If the aggregation of text from multiple subtasks introduces too much noise or irrelevant content, it could decrease the effectiveness of the query expansion.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GRF improves effectiveness without relying on first-pass retrieval effectiveness.
- Mechanism: By generating relevant text independently of the initial retrieval results, GRF avoids the vocabulary mismatch and noise introduced by non-relevant documents in the feedback set.
- Core assumption: The LLM can generate relevant text that is independent of the initial retrieval results and captures the user's information need.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Unlike traditional pseudo-relevance feedback methods, GRF builds probabilistic feedback models from long-form text generated by LLMs, tackling the vocabulary mismatch problem"
  - [section 5.1] "GRF is not reliant on first-pass retrieval effectiveness, and GRF improves NDCG@10 by +145%, MAP by +068, and R@1k by +165 (a relative improvement of +100-200% on NDCG@10 and MAP)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus analysis did not provide specific insights on the independence from first-pass retrieval
- Break condition: If the LLM-generated text does not accurately capture the user's information need or introduces irrelevant content, the effectiveness of GRF would decrease.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input query -> LLM generation -> Text aggregation -> Relevance model -> Query expansion -> Retrieval
- Critical path: LLM generation → Text aggregation → Relevance model → Query expansion → Retrieval
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Choosing the right generation subtasks and their parameters (e.g., token limits) to balance relevance and diversity
  - Tuning the interpolation between the original query and the generative expansion terms (β)
  - Deciding the number of expansion terms (θ) to include in the relevance model
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor retrieval effectiveness: The LLM-generated text may not be relevant or may not match the target dataset's style
  - Overfitting to the generated text: The expanded query may become too focused on the LLM-generated text and lose connection to the original query
  - Computational overhead: Generating and processing large amounts of text from multiple subtasks can be time-consuming and resource-intensive
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the effectiveness of individual generation subtasks (keywords, entities, facts, news articles, documents, essays) on a small subset of the target dataset.
  2. Experiment with different combinations of generation subtasks to find the most effective aggregation strategy.
  3. Tune the hyperparameters (β and θ) to optimize the balance between the original query and the generative expansion terms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of GRF vary across different domains and query types beyond the four benchmarks studied?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors evaluate GRF on four document ranking benchmarks (Robust04, CODEC, TREC DL-19, and DL-20) and observe varying effectiveness across these datasets.
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to four specific benchmarks. The effectiveness of GRF on other domains (e.g., scientific literature, legal documents) and query types (e.g., conversational queries, multi-turn dialogues) remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on a wider range of datasets representing diverse domains and query types would reveal the generalizability and limitations of GRF.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for selecting and combining generation subtasks in GRF to maximize effectiveness for a given query or dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors find that combining text across all generation subtasks improves effectiveness, but also observe that the most effective subtasks vary depending on the dataset (e.g., Facts and News for Robust04, Essay and News for CODEC).
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic approach for selecting the most relevant generation subtasks for a specific query or dataset. The effectiveness of different combination strategies (e.g., weighted averaging, ensemble methods) is also unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing different strategies for selecting and combining generation subtasks, along with an analysis of their impact on effectiveness, would provide insights into optimal GRF configurations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of LLM and its parameters (e.g., temperature, top_p) affect the effectiveness of GRF?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors use GPT-3 (text-davinci-002) with specific parameters (temperature 0.7, top_p 1.0, etc.) for text generation.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the impact of different LLMs or parameter settings on GRF effectiveness. It is unclear whether other LLMs or parameter configurations could lead to better results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing GRF effectiveness using different LLMs and parameter settings would reveal the sensitivity of GRF to these choices and identify optimal configurations.

## Limitations
- The evaluation relies on a single LLM (GPT3) and may not generalize to other language models or domains.
- The quality of LLM-generated text heavily depends on prompt engineering, which was not extensively explored in this study.
- The computational cost of generating and processing large amounts of text from multiple subtasks may be prohibitive for real-time applications.

## Confidence
- High: The core mechanism of using LLM-generated text for query expansion is sound and well-supported by the results.
- Medium: The effectiveness of combining text across all generation subtasks is supported by the results but may be sensitive to the specific tasks and datasets used.
- Low: The long-term impact of GRF on user satisfaction and task completion is uncertain and requires further investigation.
