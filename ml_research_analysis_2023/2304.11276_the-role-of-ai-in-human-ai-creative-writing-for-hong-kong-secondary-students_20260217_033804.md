# The Role of AI in Human-AI Creative Writing for Hong Kong Secondary Students

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11276

## Executive Summary
This study investigated how AI-based language models can assist secondary students in Hong Kong with creative writing ideation. Researchers developed AI sentence generation tools using autoregressive language models and conducted a creative writing competition where students used these tools to generate content for their stories. The analysis revealed that students primarily used AI-generated sentences at the beginning of paragraphs to trigger new ideas and develop story plots. The tools served dual roles: as collaborative partners helping students generate and refine ideas, and as provocateurs introducing unexpected elements that challenged students' original storylines. These findings suggest AI language models can be valuable tools for enhancing creative writing by supporting different creative processes depending on individual student needs and writing abilities.

## Method Summary
The researchers developed AI sentence generation tools using autoregressive language models and conducted a creative writing competition with Hong Kong secondary school students. The methodology involved building AI tools with Python NLP libraries, conducting a workshop on short story writing and tool usage, collecting student stories containing both original and AI-generated content, and administering post-contest surveys. Analysis focused on examining sentence placement within paragraph structure, determining which paragraphs used more AI-generated sentences, and analyzing survey responses to understand tool usage patterns and roles. The study applied Kaufman and Beghetto's "four C" model of creativity, focusing on "mini-C" creativity for learning purposes.

## Key Results
- Students primarily used AI-generated sentences at the beginning of paragraphs to trigger new ideas and develop story plots
- AI tools served dual roles as collaborative partners and provocateurs, introducing unexpected elements that challenged original storylines
- Different students utilized the text generator differently in helping them be more creative, potentially depending on their English language ability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: AI sentence generators help students overcome creative blocks by providing a starting point for story development.
- Mechanism: The AI tool generates initial sentences that serve as "head starts" for students, triggering new ideas and enabling them to build upon the generated content to develop their stories.
- Core assumption: Students benefit from external stimuli when facing creative blocks, and AI-generated sentences can provide this stimulus.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "students primarily used AI-generated sentences at the beginning of paragraphs to trigger new ideas and develop story plots"
  - [section]: "many of the students used AI-generated sentences for their opening sentence of the first paragraph... this indicates the AI text generator is used to give them a quicker or guided head start"
  - [corpus]: "Exploring AI-Generated Text in Student Writing: How Does AI Help?" suggests related work examining AI's impact on student writing quality
- Break condition: This mechanism breaks down if students become overly reliant on AI-generated content and fail to develop their own creative voice, or if the generated content consistently misaligns with the student's intended narrative direction.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: AI language models function as collaborative partners in the creative writing process.
- Mechanism: Students use AI-generated sentences as a starting point and then refine or select portions that fit their story narrative, engaging in a collaborative process with the AI tool.
- Core assumption: Students can effectively integrate AI-generated content into their own creative process without losing ownership of their work.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "tools served dual roles: as collaborative partners helping students generate and refine ideas"
  - [section]: "some students only selected the sentence or words that fit her story narrative... the AI text generator plays the role of a 'partner' in helping them generate new ideas"
  - [corpus]: Weak corpus evidence - the related papers focus on student attitudes and editing behaviors but don't directly address the collaborative partnership mechanism
- Break condition: This mechanism fails when students cannot effectively integrate AI-generated content with their own ideas, leading to disjointed or incoherent narratives.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: AI language models can act as provocateurs by introducing unexpected elements that challenge students' original storylines.
- Mechanism: The AI tool generates content that may not align with the student's planned narrative, forcing them to incorporate unexpected elements or find creative ways to integrate them.
- Core assumption: Students can benefit from creative challenges and unexpected prompts that push them beyond their comfort zones.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "provocateurs introducing unexpected elements that challenged students' original storylines"
  - [section]: "AI-based sentence generator tools may play the role of a provocateur... A student found it challenging to integrate the sentences generated by the tools into her storyline"
  - [corpus]: "Exploring EFL students' prompt engineering in human-AI story writing: an Activity Theory perspective" suggests related work on student-AI interaction during creative writing
- Break condition: This mechanism breaks when students become frustrated with the AI's suggestions and disengage from the creative process, or when the AI-generated content consistently contradicts the student's intended narrative direction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does student English proficiency level influence their preferred role of AI tools as collaborator versus provocateur?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "different students can utilize the text generator differently in helping them to be more creative in their writing, perhaps depending on the student's English language ability"
- Why unresolved: The study did not systematically analyze student proficiency levels against their AI usage patterns
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis correlating student English proficiency scores with their AI tool usage patterns and reported roles (collaborator vs provocateur)

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the long-term impacts of AI-assisted creative writing on students' independent writing skills and creativity development?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study focused on short-term effects during a single competition but didn't examine sustained impacts
- Why unresolved: The research only examined immediate effects without longitudinal tracking of student writing development
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking students' writing abilities and creative development over multiple academic terms with and without AI assistance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different AI model architectures and training approaches affect the quality and utility of generated content for creative writing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using "autoregressive language models" but doesn't compare different model types
- Why unresolved: The study used a specific type of language model without exploring how different models might perform
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies testing multiple language model architectures (transformer-based, recurrent, etc.) for creative writing assistance

### Open Question 4
- Question: What specific features or modifications to AI writing tools would maximize their educational value while minimizing potential misuse?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion discusses how teachers could "customize the type and extent of help students get" but doesn't specify optimal configurations
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the need for customization but doesn't provide specific design guidelines
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical testing of different tool configurations, restrictions, and pedagogical implementations to identify optimal balance between assistance and autonomy

## Limitations
- The study lacks detailed information about the specific autoregressive language model architecture and training parameters used
- Analysis focuses primarily on structural placement of AI-generated sentences rather than semantic quality or narrative integration
- The survey methodology and specific questions used to assess student experiences are not fully detailed
- Sample size and demographic composition of participating students are not specified

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The finding that students primarily used AI-generated sentences at the beginning of paragraphs to trigger new ideas is well-supported by the data analysis of sentence placement within student stories.
- **Medium Confidence**: The dual role of AI tools as both collaborators and provocateurs is supported by survey responses and story analysis, but the distinction between these roles could be more clearly operationalized.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that AI language models enhance creative writing by supporting different creative processes depending on individual student needs is based on limited evidence and would require more rigorous testing.
