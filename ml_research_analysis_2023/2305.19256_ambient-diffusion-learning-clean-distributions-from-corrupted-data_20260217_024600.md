# Ambient Diffusion: Learning Clean Distributions from Corrupted Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19256
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19256
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: First diffusion-based method to learn clean distributions from highly corrupted data, achieving state-of-the-art results with 90% missing pixels

## Executive Summary
Ambient Diffusion introduces a novel approach to train diffusion models on highly corrupted data by introducing additional measurement distortion during training. The method requires the model to predict the original corrupted image from a further corrupted version, enabling it to learn the conditional expectation of clean data given corrupted measurements. This approach works for various corruption processes including inpainting and compressed sensing, achieving state-of-the-art results on standard benchmarks while significantly reducing memorization compared to traditional diffusion models.

## Method Summary
Ambient Diffusion trains diffusion models on corrupted data by introducing additional measurement distortion during the diffusion process. The model learns to predict the original corrupted image from a further corrupted version, forcing it to accurately reconstruct observed pixels without exploiting missing data patterns. This enables learning the conditional expectation E[x0|Ax0] for all corruption matrices A in the support of p(A), which can be used to approximate E[x0|xt] for any noise level t and enable sampling from the clean distribution.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on CelebA, CIFAR-10, and AFHQ with up to 90% missing pixels
- Outperforms previous methods in both image generation quality and inverse problem solving
- Significantly reduces memorization when fine-tuning foundation models on small corrupted datasets
- Enables sampling from clean distributions using only a single prediction step

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Further corruption enables learning E[x0|Ax0] by preventing models from exploiting missing pixel patterns
- Core assumption: The further corruption matrix B must ensure EA|~A[AT A] remains full-rank for all ~A
- Evidence: Theoretical proof in section 3.1 shows that without further corruption, models can exploit missing pixel patterns and fail to learn the true conditional expectation

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Learned conditional expectations enable approximation of E[x0|xt] for any noise level
- Core assumption: Distribution p(A) must have sufficient support including matrices close to identity
- Evidence: Section 3.2 shows that sampling from p(A) provides a practical approximation method, though with exponentially small probability of sampling identity matrix

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Tweedie's formula connects learned conditional expectations to score functions for sampling
- Core assumption: Approximation E[x0|~Axt, ~A] ≈ E[x0|xt] must be sufficiently accurate
- Evidence: Section 2 derives the score function from Vincent's work and shows how to approximate it using learned conditional expectations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Clean data -> Random inpainting (A) -> Further corruption (B) -> Model prediction -> Loss computation -> Parameter update

- Critical path:
  1. Apply random inpainting to create corrupted training samples
  2. Apply further corruption to create additional distorted measurements
  3. Model predicts original corrupted image from further corrupted input
  4. Compute reconstruction loss on observed pixels
  5. Backpropagate and update model parameters
  6. Sample using learned model with E[x0|xt] approximation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Corruption level vs. model performance: Higher corruption reduces memorization but degrades generation quality
  - Further corruption probability vs. training stability: Too small may violate full-rank condition; too large reduces useful information
  - Fixed mask vs. reconstruction guidance sampling: Fixed mask is computationally cheaper but may have averaging effects

- Failure signatures:
  - Training divergence at high corruption levels (p ≥ 0.8)
  - Poor inpainting performance indicating insufficient further corruption
  - Memorization in fine-tuning suggesting corruption level too low
  - Degraded sample quality from broken E[x0|xt] approximation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on CIFAR-10 with 80% random inpainting and verify FID/Inception scores improve over baseline
  2. Test restoration on AFHQ with noisy inpainted measurements and compare with DDRM/DPS
  3. Fine-tune Deepfloyd IF on CelebA subset (3000 images) and measure similarity distribution to training samples

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we theoretically derive an efficient algorithm to reconstruct p0(x0) from corrupted samples without approximations?
- Basis: The paper states it's possible to recover E[x0|xt] exactly but doesn't provide an algorithm
- Why unresolved: Proves theoretical possibility but only provides approximation algorithms for sampling
- What would resolve: A concrete algorithm with theoretical guarantees for exact reconstruction

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the precise relationship between corruption level, model performance, and memorization?
- Basis: Paper demonstrates tradeoff empirically but lacks theoretical framework
- Why unresolved: Shows correlation but doesn't quantify how corruption affects both performance and memorization
- What would resolve: Mathematical model quantifying the tradeoff across datasets and architectures

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can Ambient Diffusion be extended to handle noisy measurements in the corruption process?
- Basis: Paper states it cannot handle noisy measurements and suggests this as a limitation
- Why unresolved: Current framework only works with deterministic linear transformations
- What would resolve: Extension handling both corruption and additive noise with theoretical guarantees

## Limitations

- Theoretical guarantees rely on full-rank conditions that may fail in practical scenarios
- Approximation E[x0|xt] ≈ E[x0|~Axt, ~A] degrades significantly at high corruption levels (>90%)
- Requires careful tuning of further corruption probability δ for optimal performance

## Confidence

- Theoretical guarantees: Medium (sound proof structure but relies on conditions that may not hold)
- Empirical performance: Medium-High (strong results on standard benchmarks but limited real-world validation)
- Memorization reduction: Medium (demonstrates correlation but not causal proof)
