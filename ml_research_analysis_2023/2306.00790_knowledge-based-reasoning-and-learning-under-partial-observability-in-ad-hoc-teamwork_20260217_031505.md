# Knowledge-based Reasoning and Learning under Partial Observability in Ad Hoc Teamwork

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces KAT, a knowledge-based architecture for ad hoc teamwork that combines non-monotonic logical reasoning with commonsense domain knowledge and predictive models of teammate behavior. Unlike data-driven approaches that require large labeled datasets, KAT supports online selection, adaptation, and learning of behavior prediction models while enabling collaboration under partial observability and limited communication. The system demonstrates competitive performance against state-of-the-art data-driven baselines in benchmark domains while addressing key limitations of data-intensive approaches including computational expense, lack of transparency, and poor adaptability to changing team compositions.

## Method Summary
KAT employs a hybrid approach that integrates knowledge-based reasoning with learned predictive models. The architecture uses non-monotonic logical reasoning to handle commonsense domain knowledge and uncertainty, while incorporating predictive models of teammate behavior learned from limited examples. This allows the system to reason about team dynamics, adapt to changing team compositions, and operate effectively under partial observability conditions where complete information about teammates' states is unavailable. The knowledge-based reasoning layer provides explainability and enables the system to leverage domain expertise, while the learning component allows adaptation to specific team dynamics without requiring extensive training data.

## Key Results
- KAT achieves performance comparable or superior to data-driven baselines in Fort Attack and Half Field Offense domains
- Performance advantages are particularly pronounced when training data is limited
- KAT effectively handles partial observability and team composition changes where data-driven approaches struggle

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
KAT's effectiveness stems from its hybrid approach that combines the interpretability and adaptability of knowledge-based reasoning with the learning capabilities of predictive models. The non-monotonic logical reasoning layer enables handling of commonsense domain knowledge and uncertainty, allowing the system to make reasonable inferences even with incomplete information. By learning predictive models from limited examples rather than requiring extensive datasets, KAT can adapt to specific team dynamics while maintaining transparency in decision-making. The online selection and adaptation capabilities allow continuous refinement of behavior predictions as team interactions evolve, enabling robust performance across changing team compositions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Non-monotonic logical reasoning**: Enables handling of default assumptions and exceptions in commonsense knowledge; needed because real-world domains contain exceptions to general rules; quick check: can the system revise beliefs when new contradictory information arrives
- **Predictive modeling of teammate behavior**: Learns patterns from limited examples to anticipate teammate actions; needed because ad hoc teams lack extensive historical data; quick check: can the system make reasonable predictions with minimal training examples
- **Partial observability reasoning**: Handles incomplete information about teammate states; needed because real-world communication and sensing are often limited; quick check: can the system maintain effective collaboration when key information is missing
- **Online adaptation mechanisms**: Allows continuous refinement of behavior models during interaction; needed because team compositions and dynamics change over time; quick check: can the system improve performance through ongoing interaction without retraining
- **Knowledge integration**: Combines domain expertise with learned patterns; needed because pure data-driven approaches lack interpretability and domain guidance; quick check: can the system explain its reasoning decisions

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Knowledge Base -> Non-monotonic Reasoning Engine -> Behavior Prediction Module -> Action Selection Module -> Environment Interface

**Critical Path:** Environment observations -> Knowledge Base + Reasoning Engine -> Behavior Predictions -> Action Selection -> Teammate coordination

**Design Tradeoffs:** Knowledge-based reasoning provides transparency and domain guidance but requires careful knowledge engineering; predictive models offer adaptability but need careful regularization to prevent overfitting on limited data; the hybrid approach balances these competing demands.

**Failure Signatures:** Performance degradation when domain knowledge is incomplete or incorrect; poor predictions when teammate behavior significantly deviates from learned patterns; coordination failures under extreme partial observability conditions.

**First 3 Experiments:** 1) Compare KAT performance against data-driven baselines in standard benchmark tasks; 2) Evaluate robustness to varying levels of partial observability; 3) Test adaptability to team composition changes through controlled experiments.

## Limitations
- Experimental validation limited to two specific domains (Fort Attack and Half Field Offense), constraining generalizability
- Limited comparison against data-driven baselines (only three) reduces strength of performance claims
- Lack of ablation studies prevents isolation of individual component contributions
- Computational overhead of knowledge-based reasoning layer not thoroughly characterized

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core technical contribution of integrating non-monotonic logical reasoning with learned predictive models is sound and well-described
- **Medium confidence**: Performance claims relative to data-driven baselines, as the experimental setup and comparison metrics appear reasonable but could benefit from broader baseline coverage
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about KAT's ability to handle partial observability and team composition changes, given the limited domain diversity in experiments
