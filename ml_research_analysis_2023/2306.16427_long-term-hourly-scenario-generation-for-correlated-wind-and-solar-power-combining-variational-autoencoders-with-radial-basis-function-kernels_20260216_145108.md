# Long-Term Hourly Scenario Generation for Correlated Wind and Solar Power combining Variational Autoencoders with Radial Basis Function Kernels

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16427
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16427
- Reference count: 17
- The proposed model outperforms conventional VAEs in generating accurate hourly renewable energy scenarios with better marginal distributions and spatial correlations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a two-module model for generating long-term hourly scenarios of wind and solar power generation by combining a modified Variational Autoencoder (VAE) architecture with Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernels and a similarity-based selection mechanism. The model addresses the challenge of accurately capturing the variability and intermittency of renewable energy sources while maintaining computational efficiency. When applied to a dataset of 145 renewable energy plants in Brazil, the model demonstrated superior performance compared to conventional VAE architectures, achieving better representation of marginal probability distributions and spatial correlations.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach consists of two main modules: (1) a modified VAE that uses an RBF kernel in its encoder and decoder structure to generate weekly renewable energy scenarios, and (2) a similarity-based selection mechanism in the latent space that uses Mahalanobis distance to select appropriate historical hourly profiles for disaggregating weekly scenarios into hourly values. The model was trained on aggregated weekly data from 145 renewable energy plants and evaluated based on its ability to accurately represent marginal probability distributions and spatial correlations.

## Key Results
- The proposed model outperformed conventional VAE architecture with p-values above 0.05 for all plants in representing marginal probability distributions
- Achieved mean absolute error of 0.034 for spatial correlations compared to 0.0642 for conventional VAE
- Demonstrated effectiveness in capturing complex temporal and spatial characteristics of renewable energy generation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The RBF kernel layer in the encoder extracts informative features from raw data, reducing the complexity of subsequent layers.
- Mechanism: RBF kernel maps inputs into a higher-dimensional space where relationships become linearly separable, enabling simpler transformations in later encoder layers.
- Core assumption: The RBF kernel's nonlinear mapping captures the underlying structure of renewable generation data without needing deep, complex layers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "By establishing a linear relationship between the features in the transformed space generated by the RBF kernel layer, this transformation allows subsequent encoder layers to become simpler and more concise..."
  - [abstract] "We combine the capabilities of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) with the additional benefits of incorporating the Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel in our artificial neural network architecture."
- Break condition: If the RBF kernel fails to capture the complex temporal dependencies in the data, the model's performance will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Mahalanobis distance in the latent space selects appropriate historical hourly profiles for disaggregating weekly scenarios.
- Mechanism: The Mahalanobis distance accounts for the covariance structure in the latent space, enabling accurate matching between generated weekly scenarios and historical hourly profiles.
- Core assumption: The latent space distribution learned by the VAE preserves meaningful similarity relationships that correlate with hourly generation patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To address this issue, we propose the use of Mahalanobis distance as a measure of similarity... It provides a way to calculate the distance between points and distributions, taking into consideration the variability present in the data."
  - [section] "By utilizing the neural network and the clustering characteristics of the latent space, we can develop a selection function that identifies the most suitable profiles..."
- Break condition: If the latent space doesn't preserve meaningful similarity relationships, the Mahalanobis distance will fail to select appropriate profiles.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The implicit decoder architecture with additional layers converges faster and achieves better reconstruction than explicit inverse RBF kernel approaches.
- Mechanism: Implicit definition allows the optimizer to learn the inverse transformation jointly with the decoder, rather than relying on a pre-trained, fixed inverse function.
- Core assumption: The network can learn an effective inverse transformation through additional layers during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Comparing the plots, we observe that although the architecture with the explicit definition initially shows faster decay of the loss function, after a few epochs, the performance of the network with the implicit definition becomes better..."
  - [section] "This indicates that the training process of the network with the implicit definition converges more quickly and efficiently."
- Break condition: If the additional layers cannot learn an effective inverse transformation, reconstruction quality will suffer.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input weekly data -> RBF kernel layer -> Hidden encoder layers -> Latent space -> Hidden decoder layers -> Output weekly scenarios -> Mahalanobis distance calculation -> Historical profile selection -> Hourly disaggregation

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-compute RBF kernel outputs for all training data
  2. Train encoder-decoder architecture with implicit inverse transformation
  3. Generate weekly scenarios in latent space
  4. Compute Mahalanobis distances to find best matching historical profiles
  5. Disaggregate selected profiles to hourly resolution

- Design tradeoffs:
  - RBF kernel gamma value: Higher values create more localized features but may overfit; lower values create smoother features but may miss important patterns
  - Latent space dimension: Too small loses information; too large makes similarity measurement less meaningful
  - Decoder architecture: Explicit inverse RBF is faster but less flexible; implicit approach requires more training but adapts better

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor reconstruction loss despite good training loss: RBF kernel parameters may be poorly chosen
  - Generated scenarios don't match historical patterns: Mahalanobis distance implementation may be incorrect
  - Model overfits to training data: Regularization in VAE may be insufficient

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the VAE with RBF kernel layer using different gamma values (0.1, 1, 10) and compare reconstruction loss
  2. Generate synthetic weekly scenarios and visualize their distribution compared to historical data
  3. Implement the Mahalanobis distance calculation and verify it correctly identifies similar profiles in a simple 2D latent space example

## Limitations
- The model's performance depends heavily on RBF kernel parameter selection without clear optimization methodology
- Limited validation beyond Brazilian wind and solar data, raising questions about generalizability
- Lack of ablation studies to isolate contributions of individual model components

## Confidence

*High Confidence:* The fundamental mechanism of using VAE for scenario generation and the mathematical properties of RBF kernels and Mahalanobis distance are well-established.

*Medium Confidence:* The effectiveness of combining these specific components for renewable energy scenario generation is supported by empirical results but lacks theoretical guarantees for why this particular combination works best.

*Low Confidence:* The generalizability of the model to different geographical regions or energy systems with different characteristics remains untested.
