# Ordering for Non-Replacement SGD

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15848
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15848
- Reference count: 40
- Proposes deterministic ordering for non-replacement SGD to improve convergence rates

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a deterministic ordering strategy for non-replacement stochastic gradient descent (SGD) that orders data points by decreasing gradient magnitude at the start of each epoch when using decreasing step sizes. The method provides tighter convergence bounds for strongly convex and convex functions with Lipschitz continuous gradients. Experimental results on synthetic and real datasets, as well as neural networks, demonstrate improved convergence rates and reduced training time compared to random ordering approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves computing gradient magnitudes for all data points at the beginning of each epoch and ordering them in decreasing magnitude. This deterministic ordering is then used for non-replacement SGD with decreasing step sizes. The approach is extended to mini-batch SGD and complex neural networks, maintaining the ordering principle while adapting to different optimization scenarios.

## Key Results
- Provides tighter upper bounds on distance to optimal solution for strongly convex and convex functions
- Demonstrates improved convergence on synthetic and real datasets (Iris, Boston Housing)
- Shows reduced training time and improved convergence for neural networks (MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The deterministic ordering by gradient magnitude ensures that larger gradients are processed first, potentially leading to more significant parameter updates early in the epoch. This ordering, combined with decreasing step sizes, helps maintain stability while allowing for aggressive updates when the gradient information is most informative.
