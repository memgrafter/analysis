# Deep Visual Forced Alignment: Learning to Align Transcription with Talking Face Video

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.08670
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08670
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Primary result:** Achieves 67.7ms mean absolute error and 84.2% frame-wise accuracy on LRS2 dataset for word-level visual forced alignment

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Deep Visual Forced Alignment (DVFA), a method that aligns transcription with talking face video without requiring audio input. The approach uses multi-modal attention to match text words to corresponding video frames, augmented with anomaly detection to handle transcription errors. DVFA achieves state-of-the-art performance on LRS2 and LRS3 datasets, with 67.7ms mean absolute error and 84.2% frame-wise accuracy on LRS2. The method can also detect addition, deletion, and substitution errors between predicted and ground-truth transcriptions.

## Method Summary
DVFA addresses visual forced alignment by using multi-modal attention to match text words with corresponding video frames at word level, without requiring audio. The model uses conformer-based visual and text context encoders, followed by a multi-modal attentive encoder that attends to inter-modal correspondences. Two predictors are added: Text Alignment Predictor (TAP) for word alignment and deletion error detection, and Text Presence Predictor (TPP) for word presence and addition error detection. The model predicts temporal positions of words rather than word classes, enabling handling of unseen words.

## Key Results
- Achieves 67.7ms mean absolute error on LRS2 dataset
- Achieves 84.2% frame-wise accuracy on LRS2 dataset
- Outperforms existing methods on both LRS2 and LRS3 datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Multi-modal attention enables direct matching of text words to corresponding video frames without requiring audio.
- **Mechanism:** The model stacks visual and text context representations along the time dimension and applies transformer-based attention to model inter-modal correspondences. This allows the model to "skim" through the entire video and text simultaneously, retrieving the frames that best match each word.
- **Core assumption:** Visual speech information (lip movements) is sufficiently discriminative to match specific words in the transcription without audio cues.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The proposed DVFA utilizes multi-modal attention to match transcriptions with the corresponding lip movement frames at word level."
  - [section] "Motivated by this, we design a multi-modal attentive encoder that can see the whole frames and words at once, and attend to the related parts between the two modalities."
- **Break condition:** If visual speech information is too ambiguous (e.g., homophones or similar lip movements for different words), the attention mechanism may fail to correctly match words to frames.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Augmenting alignment with anomaly detection makes the system robust to transcription errors (additions, deletions, substitutions).
- **Mechanism:** Two predictors are added: Text Alignment Predictor (TAP) predicts word alignment and deletion errors, while Text Presence Predictor (TPP) predicts word presence and addition errors. This allows the system to flag mismatches between the transcription and video.
- **Core assumption:** The model can learn to distinguish between words that are actually spoken in the video versus those that are incorrectly added to the transcription.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "by augmenting the alignment task with anomaly case detection, DVFA can detect mismatches between the input transcription and the input video while performing the alignment."
  - [section] "To handle the anomaly cases, we consider three anomaly cases, addition error, deletion, and substitution... We augment the learning problem to be able to find these three anomaly cases during performing the alignment."
- **Break condition:** If transcription errors are too frequent or severe (e.g., many substitutions), the model's performance may degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using position-based targets for alignment is more effective than word-based targets.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of predicting the actual word class for each frame, the model predicts the temporal position of each word (its index in the sentence). This avoids the need for a predefined word dictionary and allows the model to handle unseen words.
- **Core assumption:** The model can learn to map visual features to word positions without needing to recognize the actual words.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "For the prediction target, we set it to the position of each word, which enables the model to align the transcription without depending on a predefined word dictionary."
  - [section] "Compared to the results obtained using the position of each word as the prediction target (84.2%), the lower frame-wise accuracy (78.1%) is obtained by using words as the prediction target."
- **Break condition:** If the sentence structure is too complex or if words are repeated frequently, position-based prediction may become ambiguous.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Video encoder (ResNet-18 + 3D conv) → Visual context encoder (Conformer) → Multi-modal attentive encoder (Transformer) → Text Alignment Predictor (Linear) + Text Presence Predictor (Linear)
  Text encoder (learnable embedding) → Text context encoder (Conformer) → Multi-modal attentive encoder (Transformer) → Text Alignment Predictor (Linear) + Text Presence Predictor (Linear)

- **Critical path:** Video/text encoding → Context modeling → Multi-modal attention → Alignment/presence prediction

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Position-based vs word-based alignment targets (simpler, handles unseen words vs more interpretable)
  - Single multi-modal encoder vs separate encoders with fusion (simpler vs potentially more flexible)
  - Transformer layers in multi-modal encoder (6 layers chosen for best accuracy)

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High MAE but decent ACC: Model aligns words roughly but struggles with precise boundaries
  - Low ACC overall: Model fails to match words to correct frames
  - High addition/deletion detection accuracy but poor alignment: Anomaly detection works but core alignment is weak

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test position-based vs word-based alignment targets on LRS2 validation set
  2. Evaluate impact of different numbers of Conformer layers in visual/text context encoders
  3. Measure alignment performance on phoneme-level vs word-level transcriptions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out specific open questions. However, it suggests potential applications such as validating outputs of visual speech recognition models and handling videos with multiple speakers simultaneously.

## Limitations

- Performance remains untested on challenging audio-visual correspondence cases, particularly clean audio conditions where audio-based forced aligners would typically be used.
- The conformer architecture specifications are incomplete, potentially affecting reproducibility and understanding of key performance drivers.
- The evaluation only covers English speech in BBC news contexts, leaving uncertainty about performance on other languages, accents, or speaking styles.

## Confidence

**High confidence claims:**
- The multi-modal attention mechanism successfully aligns visual frames with text words at the word level (supported by 67.7ms MAE on LRS2 benchmark)
- The anomaly detection augmentation correctly identifies addition, deletion, and substitution errors (supported by detection accuracy metrics)
- Position-based alignment targets outperform word-based targets (demonstrated through ablation)

**Medium confidence claims:**
- DVFA generalizes to unseen words and sentences (supported by position-based target design but not explicitly validated)
- Visual speech information is sufficient for accurate alignment without audio (supported by performance metrics but lacks comparative validation)

**Low confidence claims:**
- DVFA can validate outputs of visual speech recognition models (proposed application but not empirically demonstrated)
- The method will perform well on diverse speaking styles and languages (no cross-lingual or cross-domain validation)
