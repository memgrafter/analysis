# Clarifying Trust of Materials Property Predictions using Neural Networks with Distribution-Specific Uncertainty Quantification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02595
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02595
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Evidential regression with recalibration provides the most accurate and tunable uncertainty estimates for catalyst property predictions, achieving R2=0.902 on OC20 dataset

## Executive Summary
This study investigates uncertainty quantification methods for machine learning predictions of catalyst properties, focusing on adsorption energy predictions using the OC20 dataset. Three UQ methods—k-fold ensembling, Monte Carlo dropout, and evidential regression—are applied to a crystal graph convolutional neural network. The work demonstrates that evidential regression with recalibration provides the most accurate and tunable uncertainty estimates, with recalibration shown to be essential for practical screening applications. The study establishes a comprehensive framework for evaluating UQ methods using five metrics: accuracy, sharpness, dispersion, calibration, and tightness.

## Method Summary
The study applies three uncertainty quantification methods (k-fold ensembling, Monte Carlo dropout, and evidential regression) to a crystal graph convolutional neural network trained on the OC20 dataset for adsorption energy predictions. Each method provides mean predictions with associated uncertainties, which are evaluated using five UQ metrics. Evidential regression uses a modified loss function with a regularization term weighted by hyperparameter λ to produce tunable uncertainty estimates. Recalibration is performed through scalar adjustment of uncertainty estimates via black-box optimization. The methods are compared based on their predictive accuracy, uncertainty sharpness and dispersion, calibration quality, and overall tightness of confidence intervals.

## Key Results
- Evidential regression achieves R2=0.902, outperforming other UQ methods for adsorption energy predictions
- Recalibration improves average calibration of all three UQ methods, with evidential regression being most accurate after recalibration
- Evidential regression provides tunable uncertainty estimates that can be adjusted via regularization weight λ
- After recalibration, evidential regression provides the most accurate and disperse uncertainty estimates for practical screening applications

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Evidential regression provides tunable, competitively trustworthy uncertainty estimates for heterogeneous catalysis applications when using neural networks.
- Mechanism: Evidential regression modifies both the loss function and model architecture to output evidential distribution parameters (γ, v, α, β) for each prediction. The regularization term in the loss function inflates the uncertainty estimate based on the absolute residual error, weighted by a scalar hyperparameter λ.
- Core assumption: The underlying distribution shape assumption (evidential distribution) accurately captures the aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties of the predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Evidential regression is demonstrated to be a powerful approach for rapidly obtaining tunable, competitively trustworthy UQ estimates for heterogeneous catalysis applications when using neural networks."
  - [section] "Evidential regression has seen use in small molecule prediction but has not yet been explored for catalysis applications to our knowledge."
- Break condition: If the evidential distribution assumption does not hold for the specific catalyst property prediction task, the uncertainty estimates may be unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Recalibration of model uncertainties is essential for practical screening applications of catalysts using uncertainties.
- Mechanism: Scalar recalibration involves multiplying all uncertainty estimates σ across all predictions by a constant chosen via black-box optimization to minimize miscalibration area. This improves the reliability of the uncertainty intervals as confidence intervals.
- Core assumption: The miscalibration area is a valid measure of how well the uncertainty estimates represent the true correctness likelihood of predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Recalibration of model uncertainties is shown to be essential in practical screening applications of catalysts using uncertainties."
  - [section] "Recalibration of model uncertainties is shown to be essential in practical screening applications of catalysts using uncertainties."
- Break condition: If the recalibration constant is poorly chosen or the optimization fails, recalibration may not improve or could worsen the calibration.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of k-fold ensembling, Monte Carlo dropout, and evidential regression provides a robust comparison framework for assessing uncertainty quantification methods in heterogeneous catalysis.
- Mechanism: Each UQ method provides a mean prediction μ with uncertainty σ, allowing for a consistent comparison of accuracy, sharpness, dispersion, calibration, and tightness metrics across methods.
- Core assumption: The five UQ metrics (accuracy, sharpness, dispersion, calibration, tightness) collectively provide a comprehensive assessment of the trustworthiness of uncertainty estimates.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We compare the UQ methods in terms of five UQ metrics: accuracy, sharpness, dispersion, calibration, and tightness."
  - [section] "These UQ metrics collectively provide a means of comparing the predictive accuracy, shape, and reliability of the uncertainty distribution between UQ methods."
- Break condition: If the chosen metrics do not adequately capture the trustworthiness of uncertainty estimates for the specific application, the comparison framework may be incomplete.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CGCNN model -> Three UQ methods (k-fold ensembling, MC dropout, evidential regression) -> Five UQ metrics (accuracy, sharpness, dispersion, calibration, tightness) -> Recalibration -> Material screening
- Critical path: 1) Train CGCNN baseline on OC20 dataset, 2) Apply each UQ method to obtain uncertainty estimates, 3) Assess UQ methods using five metrics, 4) Perform scalar recalibration if necessary, 5) Use uncertainty estimates for material enumeration and screening
- Design tradeoffs: Computational cost vs. accuracy of uncertainty estimates (e.g., MC dropout requires sampling, while evidential regression does not), flexibility vs. complexity of uncertainty tuning (e.g., evidential regression allows tuning via λ, while other methods do not), calibration vs. sharpness of uncertainty estimates (e.g., highly calibrated methods may have less sharp estimates)
- Failure signatures: Poor calibration indicated by miscalibration area or adversarial group calibration results, inflated or deflated uncertainty estimates compared to ground truth, low dispersion of uncertainty estimates, making it difficult to distinguish between confident and uncertain predictions
- First 3 experiments: 1) Compare accuracy of three UQ methods on OC20 test set using parity plots and quantitative metrics (MAE, RMSE, MDAE, MARPD, R2, R), 2) Assess dispersion and sharpness of uncertainty distributions using violin box plots and calculate IQR and Cv, 3) Evaluate calibration of each UQ method using calibration curves and calculate miscalibration area before and after scalar recalibration

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the accuracy of the CGCNN model change when trained on less sparse chemical spaces compared to the full OC20 dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that the CGCNN architecture may have better accuracy on less sparse, narrowly selected datasets.
- Why unresolved: The authors did not explore this in their experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Results showing improved test MAE when training on datasets with fewer adsorbates or narrower chemical spaces.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the UQ methods perform across different model architectures beyond CGCNN?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors acknowledge that neural networks can perform poorly under domain shift and that the choice of UQ method might be more arbitrary with effective recalibration.
- Why unresolved: The study only used CGCNN as the base model.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing UQ methods across multiple model architectures (e.g., SchNet, DimeNet++) on the same dataset.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal value of the regularization weight λ for evidential regression across different datasets and models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that λ affects calibration and dispersion, but only tested a limited range.
- Why unresolved: The study only tested λ values from 0 to 0.2.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying λ across multiple orders of magnitude for different datasets and model architectures.

## Limitations
- Computational constraints limited recalibration to only 100 hyperparameter trials rather than exhaustive optimization
- Study relies on OC20 dataset which may not fully capture all catalyst property prediction scenarios
- Choice of regularization weight λ=0.05 was selected through preliminary trials rather than systematic optimization across full hyperparameter space
