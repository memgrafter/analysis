# Adaptive Sampling for Probabilistic Forecasting under Distribution Shift

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11870
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11870
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Method improves forecasting accuracy over base model for three out of five datasets, with an average relative improvement of 8.4%

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces adaptive sampling to handle distribution shifts in time series forecasting. The key idea is to learn a discrete distribution over time steps that selects relevant training windows based on the current data regime. This is achieved by pre-training a forecasting model with uniform sampling, then fine-tuning a lightweight adaptive architecture using adaptively sampled windows, where the sampling distribution parameters are optimized via Bayesian optimization on a validation set. Experiments on synthetic and real-world data show that the method improves forecasting accuracy over the base model for three out of five datasets.

## Method Summary
The method employs a two-stage training approach for probabilistic time series forecasting under distribution shift. First, a base forecasting model is pre-trained using uniform sampling across all time steps. Then, a lightweight adaptive architecture is fine-tuned using adaptively sampled training windows, where the sampling distribution parameters are optimized through Bayesian optimization on a validation set. The adaptive sampling learns to focus on time steps that are most relevant to the current data regime, improving the model's ability to handle distribution shifts.

## Key Results
- Method improves forecasting accuracy over base model for three out of five datasets
- Average relative improvement of 8.4% across all datasets
- Demonstrated on both synthetic and real-world time series data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The adaptive sampling mechanism works by learning a discrete distribution over time steps that selects relevant training windows based on the current data regime. By focusing on the most informative time steps during fine-tuning, the model becomes more robust to distribution shifts. The two-stage training approach (pre-training with uniform sampling followed by fine-tuning with adaptive sampling) allows the model to first learn general patterns before specializing to the current regime.

## Limitations
- The method's performance is not consistent across all datasets, with improvements shown on only three out of five datasets.
- The computational overhead during fine-tuning, particularly the need for multiple Bayesian optimization iterations, is not thoroughly discussed in terms of scalability.
- The evaluation is limited to specific forecasting models (DeepAR, Autoformer, PatchTST) and may not generalize to other architectures.
