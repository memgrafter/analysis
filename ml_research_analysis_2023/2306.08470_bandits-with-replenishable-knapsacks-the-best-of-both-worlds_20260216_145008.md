# Bandits with Replenishable Knapsacks: the Best of both Worlds

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08470
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08470
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Best-of-both-worlds primal-dual template for BwRK achieves constant competitive ratio in adversarial setting and instance-independent O(√T) regret in stochastic setting.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the Bandits with Replenishable Knapsacks (BwRK) framework, extending the standard Bandits with Knapsacks model to allow for non-monotonic resource utilization through replenishment. The authors propose a primal-dual template that handles any online learning problem with replenishment, given a suitable primal regret minimizer. The template guarantees a constant competitive ratio in the adversarial setting when the budget is large enough or replenishment is positive, and an instance-independent regret bound in the stochastic setting.

## Method Summary
The method uses a primal-dual template with two regret minimizers: a bandit-feedback primal regret minimizer (AP) and a full-feedback dual regret minimizer (AD). The algorithm partitions rounds into those where budgets are sufficient (T_G) and insufficient (T_∅). In T_G, the regret minimizers are invoked; in T_∅, a void action is played. The void action assumption ensures replenishment with β ≥ 0. Regret bounds depend on the primal and dual regret minimizers used, with EXP3-SIX and Online Gradient Descent being specific instantiations.

## Key Results
- Constant competitive ratio α = ν/(1+β) in adversarial setting when B = Ω(T) or β > 0
- Instance-independent O(√T) regret bound in stochastic setting
- First positive results for BwRK under adversarial inputs
- Extension to problems with negative rewards in stochastic case via reward translation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Constant competitive ratio when B = Ω(T) or β > 0 with non-monotonic resource consumption.
- Mechanism: Replenishment allows budget accumulation before depletion, enabling dual player to set high Lagrange multipliers early and low ones later, creating a feedback loop that bounds lagrangian penalties.
- Core assumption: Void action ∅ with per-round replenishment β > 0 ensures budget never falls below zero.
- Evidence anchors:

- Break condition: If β = 0, competitive ratio degrades to 1/ρ (BwK baseline).

- Claim: Instance-independent O(√T) regret in stochastic setting.
- Mechanism: Uses weakly adaptive dual regret minimizer and primal minimizer exploiting concentration inequalities. Lagrangian regret over TG is bounded, with budget replenishment controlled by ET,δ terms.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Extension to problems with negative rewards in stochastic case via reward translation.
- Mechanism: Adding constant to all rewards makes them non-negative while maintaining multiplicative guarantees, as benchmark scales proportionally.
- Core assumption: Stochastic setting allows reward translation without affecting regret structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.2] "inventory management problem... focus on the stochastic case in which E[ct(s)]≤− β... By employing EXP-SIX as in Section 6.1, this immediately yields a ˜O(√T) regret bound"

- Break condition: In adversarial setting, translation breaks multiplicative approximation guarantees.

  - Why needed here: Dual regret minimizer must handle changing optimal actions before and after depletion (τ), requiring weakly adaptive regret bounds

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Regret minimizers (AP, AD) -> Budget vector Bt -> Round partition sets (TG, T∅) -> Action selection -> Updates -> Output
- Critical path:
  1. Check budget thresholds → determine action set
  2. Select primal action (AP.next) and dual multiplier (AD.next)
  3. Observe costs and update budgets
  4. Provide lagrangian losses to regret minimizers
  5. Iterate until T
- Design tradeoffs:
  - EXP3-SIX vs OGD for AP: EXP3-SIX gives weakly adaptive regret but requires known reward range; OGD requires bounded dual multipliers
  - Choosing AD: Generalized fixed share needs β known; OGD works without β but needs stronger primal assumptions
  - Partitioning TG vs T∅: Enables separate analysis of depletion vs replenishment phases but adds bookkeeping complexity
- Failure signatures:
  - Budget spikes above 1 after τ but AD fails to reduce λ → lagrangian penalty term blows up
  - AD's weakly adaptive regret grows faster than √T → competitive ratio or regret bounds degrade
  - AP's regret exceeds √T → additive regret term dominates
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement AP=EXP3-SIX, AD=generalized fixed share, β known; run on synthetic BwRK with constant replenishment; verify competitive ratio ≥ α
  2. Replace AD with OGD, hide β; run same synthetic problem; verify Lagrange multipliers stay bounded and regret matches theory
  3. Instantiate on inventory management (single resource, known β); run stochastic simulations; confirm O(√T) regret and correct action switching

## Limitations
- Constant competitive ratio degrades to 1/ρ baseline when both B ≠ Ω(T) and β = 0
- Stochastic regret bounds assume i.i.d. rewards and costs with unknown distributions
- Extension to negative rewards in adversarial setting fails due to multiplicative approximation constraints
- Framework requires specific regret minimizer instantiations to maintain theoretical guarantees

## Confidence
- **High**: Primal-dual template structure and constant competitive ratio when B = Ω(T) or β > 0 are well-established through lagrangian analysis
- **Medium**: Instance-independent O(√T) regret in stochastic setting depends on concentration inequalities and weakly adaptive regret bounds
- **Medium**: Extension to negative rewards in stochastic case via reward translation is straightforward but fails in adversarial setting
