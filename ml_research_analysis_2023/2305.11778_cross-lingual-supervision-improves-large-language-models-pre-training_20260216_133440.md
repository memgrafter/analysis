# Cross-Lingual Supervision improves Large Language Models Pre-training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11778
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11778
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: Cross-lingual supervision during pre-training significantly improves in-context learning abilities of large language models, especially in multilingual and low-resource settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that incorporating parallel translation data during pre-training of large language models significantly enhances their cross-lingual capabilities. By training Encoder-Decoder models on a mixture of self-supervised language modeling and supervised machine translation objectives, the authors achieve substantial improvements on tasks like question answering, summarization, and machine translation. The key innovation is using automated curriculum learning with multi-armed bandits to dynamically adjust the mixing ratio between the two objectives during training, which outperforms static baselines.

## Method Summary
The method involves training mT5-based Encoder-Decoder models using a UL2 objective on a mixture of monolingual language modeling data and parallel translation data. The critical innovation is the FAIR algorithm, a multi-armed bandit approach that dynamically adjusts the sampling ratio between MT and LM tasks during training based on intrinsic rewards measured as relative loss reduction. This automated curriculum learning approach replaces grid search for finding optimal static mixing ratios, which is computationally infeasible.

## Key Results
- Achieved 36.05 BLEU points on Flores machine translation task
- Achieved 43.39 EM on XTyDiQA question answering
- FAIR algorithm significantly outperforms static baselines and EXP3 on all evaluation tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cross-lingual supervision bridges language representations, enabling better cross-lingual transfer.
- Mechanism: By including parallel MT data during pre-training, the model learns to map between languages, improving its ability to handle open-generation tasks across languages.
- Core assumption: Parallel data provides a strong signal for learning cross-lingual mappings that self-supervised LM alone cannot capture.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "aligned cross-lingual data might enhance the abilities of LLMs across languages other than English"
  - [section 3.1] "parallel data covering the languages... always has the source or the target in English"
- Break condition: If the parallel data is noisy or not well-aligned, the bridging effect could degrade rather than improve performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Dynamic curriculum learning finds better mixing ratios than static ones.
- Mechanism: The FAIR algorithm samples tasks proportionally to their moving average rewards, allowing the model to focus on the most useful tasks dynamically.
- Core assumption: Task rewards reflect the current learning needs of the model, so adaptive sampling is more efficient than fixed ratios.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "FAIR algorithm that samples proportionally to a moving average of the rewards"
  - [table 3] "automated curriculum (either EXP3 or FAIR) outperforms all the other data-sampling strategies"
- Break condition: If reward signals are noisy or unrepresentative, the dynamic policy could overfit to short-term fluctuations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Intrinsic reward computed on training tasks avoids overfitting to specific downstream tasks.
- Mechanism: By measuring loss reduction on the same task or the other task after a gradient step, the model is rewarded for both task-specific learning and cross-task transfer.
- Core assumption: Intrinsic rewards from the training data are sufficiently representative of downstream task performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "we measure rewards intrinsically on the (pre)-training data itself"
  - [table 8] "using the non-English side of the parallel data with the LM objective significantly reduces performance"
- Break condition: If the intrinsic rewards diverge too much from actual downstream task performance, the curriculum could mislead training.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multi-task learning with mixed objectives
  - Why needed here: The model must balance language modeling and translation objectives, requiring careful scheduling to avoid catastrophic forgetting or task imbalance.
  - Quick check question: What happens if the mixing ratio is too skewed toward LM or MT? (Answer: Underperformance on the neglected task.)

- Concept: Curriculum learning and bandit algorithms
  - Why needed here: Grid search on the mixing ratio is infeasible; automated curriculum learning allows dynamic adjustment without exhaustive search.
  - Quick check question: How does the FAIR algorithm differ from EXP3 in handling task selection? (Answer: FAIR uses moving averages to avoid overcommitting to one task.)

- Concept: In-context learning evaluation
  - Why needed here: The paper evaluates models in few-shot settings, requiring understanding of how prompts and examples affect generation quality.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between closed and open generation in this context? (Answer: Closed: same language for context, question, and answer; Open: context in one language, question/answer in another.)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Encoder-Decoder mT5 architecture with separate streams for LM and MT objectives, controlled by a dynamic task selector (FAIR)
- Critical path: Data → Encoder (LM/MT) → Cross-attention → Decoder (LM/MT) → Output; with FAIR controller selecting the task at each step
- Design tradeoffs: Static vs dynamic mixing ratios; intrinsic vs downstream rewards; Encoder-Decoder vs Decoder-only architectures
- Failure signatures: Overfitting to one task, poor cross-lingual transfer, unstable training due to noisy rewards
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with LM-only baseline and measure performance on TyDiQA and Flores
  2. Train with static 50-50 LM-MT mix and compare to dynamic FAIR policy
  3. Evaluate intrinsic reward quality by comparing FAIR and EXP3 on cross-lingual tasks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the gains from cross-lingual supervision scale with model size beyond 3.8 billion parameters?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Our summarization evaluations indicate improvements with increasing the parameter count, so further experiments with larger models (say > 8B parameters) might be needed to quantify the gains of adding parallel data during pre-training more precisely."
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluated models up to 3.8 billion parameters due to computational constraints.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating models with 8+ billion parameters on the same tasks would show if gains continue to increase, plateau, or potentially decrease at larger scales.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would alternative bandit algorithms beyond FAIR and EXP3 yield better performance for curriculum learning in this context?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] "More sophisticated sampling approaches might yield better results" and the paper only tested two bandit algorithms.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compared FAIR and EXP3 algorithms for automated curriculum learning, leaving other potential algorithms unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of additional bandit algorithms (e.g., UCB, Thompson sampling, or newer approaches) on the same tasks would identify if alternative algorithms outperform FAIR.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the optimal dynamic mixing ratio between LM and MT objectives change throughout training, and what patterns emerge?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "We emphasize the importance of learning the amount of parallel data while training without resorting to a hyper-parameter search" and the paper proposes dynamic scheduling rather than static ratios.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that dynamic scheduling outperforms static approaches, it doesn't analyze the learned patterns or optimal trajectories of the mixing ratio over time.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of the λt schedules learned by FAIR across different training runs and tasks would reveal patterns in how the optimal ratio evolves during training.

## Limitations

- The approach may be conflating benefits from cross-lingual supervision with benefits from increased data diversity, making it unclear whether improvements are specifically due to cross-lingual transfer or simply more varied training data.
- The evaluation is limited to mT5 architecture with UL2 objective, limiting generalizability to other model families like decoder-only transformers or encoder-only models.
- The 15-language scope, while covering multiple language families, may not capture the full spectrum of cross-lingual transfer challenges, particularly for distant language pairs or languages with very different scripts.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The core empirical findings that combining LM and MT objectives improves downstream task performance, and that automated curriculum learning (FAIR) outperforms static mixing ratios on the tested benchmarks.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that cross-lingual supervision specifically enables better cross-lingual transfer rather than just better overall performance. The mechanism by which FAIR improves training efficiency versus alternative curriculum learning approaches.
- Low Confidence: Generalizability to other model architectures beyond mT5, and the scalability of the approach to many more languages or to extremely low-resource language pairs not covered in the current evaluation.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation on data diversity**: Run an experiment using shuffled monolingual data with similar diversity to the MT corpus (but not parallel) to determine if improvements come from cross-lingual supervision or increased linguistic variety.

2. **Architecture generalization**: Implement the same training approach on a decoder-only transformer (GPT-style) and an encoder-only model (BERT-style) to test whether FAIR-based curriculum learning transfers across architectures.

3. **Low-resource language extension**: Evaluate the approach on a truly low-resource language (e.g., one with <100k parallel sentences) not included in the current 15-language set to test robustness beyond the relatively well-resourced languages studied.
