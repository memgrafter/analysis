# Heterogeneous Federated Knowledge Graph Embedding Learning and Unlearning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02069
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02069
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: FedLU framework achieves 38.92 MRR on RotatE for link prediction on C3 dataset while supporting effective knowledge unlearning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FedLU, a framework for federated learning of knowledge graph embeddings that addresses data heterogeneity across clients while incorporating knowledge unlearning capabilities. The approach uses mutual knowledge distillation to transfer knowledge between local and global models, preserving heterogeneity while enabling effective learning. For unlearning, it employs a two-step process combining retroactive interference with passive decay to selectively forget specific knowledge. The method is evaluated on new heterogeneous datasets derived from FB15k-237, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in both link prediction and knowledge unlearning tasks.

## Method Summary
The FedLU framework addresses federated KG embedding learning through mutual knowledge distillation, where local and global embeddings reinforce each other through bidirectional knowledge transfer. For unlearning, it implements a two-step process: retroactive interference that uses both hard and soft confusion losses to erase specific knowledge, followed by passive decay that recovers model performance while suppressing activation of forgotten knowledge. The framework is evaluated on three new heterogeneous datasets (C3, C5, C10) constructed from FB15k-237 through clustering-based partitioning, with performance measured using standard link prediction metrics (Hits@1, Hits@3, Hits@10, MRR) and unlearning effectiveness.

## Key Results
- FedLU achieves 38.92 MRR for RotatE on C3 dataset in link prediction
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods on heterogeneous federated KG learning
- Demonstrates effective knowledge unlearning with significant reduction in performance on forgetting sets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Mutual knowledge distillation between local and global embeddings improves both local optimization and global generalization.
- Mechanism: The global embedding acts as a teacher for local embeddings by providing global knowledge through distillation loss, while local embeddings refine the global embedding by providing client-specific knowledge. This bidirectional flow mitigates the drift between local optimization and global convergence.
- Core assumption: Knowledge distillation can effectively transfer information between embeddings without requiring raw data sharing, and the local and global embeddings can mutually reinforce each other.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose mutual knowledge distillation to transfer local knowledge to global, and absorb global knowledge back."
  - [section 4.1]: "we maintain local and global embeddings in parallel that mutually reinforce each other but are not identical."
- Break condition: If the local and global embeddings become too dissimilar, the distillation process may fail to effectively transfer knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Retroactive interference with hard and soft confusion enables effective knowledge forgetting.
- Mechanism: Hard confusion treats the triplet to be forgotten as a negative sample, while soft confusion minimizes the score difference between the triplet and its negatives. This combined approach erases memory of the triplet while preventing overfitting to the unlearning process.
- Core assumption: The scoring function can be manipulated to "unlearn" specific triplets by treating them as negatives and minimizing their scores.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "To unlearn each triplet (ℎ𝑖, 𝑟𝑖, 𝑡𝑖) ∈ 𝐺𝑘𝑢 , we have its negative sample set 𝑁 (ℎ𝑖, 𝑟𝑖, 𝑡𝑖 ) = {(ℎ𝑖, 𝑟𝑖, 𝑡𝑖,𝑗 ) | 𝑗 = 1, . . . , 𝑛} with size 𝑛, s.t. 𝑁 (ℎ𝑖, 𝑟𝑖, 𝑡𝑖 ) ∩ 𝐺𝑘 = ∅."
  - [section 4.2]: "we design soft confusion, which reflects the distance between scores of the triplet in the forgetting set and its negative samples."
- Break condition: If the unlearning process overfits to the forgetting set, it may lead to performance degradation on other tasks.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Passive decay step recovers model performance while suppressing activation of forgotten knowledge.
- Mechanism: After retroactive interference, the model is trained on the retaining set using mutual distillation. This recovers the generalization ability lost during interference while suppressing the activation of the forgotten triplets.
- Core assumption: Training on the retaining set can recover the model's generalization ability without reactivating the forgotten knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "we perform a passive decay step afterwards by mutual knowledge distillation with 𝐺𝑘𝑐 as input."
  - [section 4.2]: "Learning on the retaining set can recover the generalization of the model. Mutual distillation suppresses the activation of the forgetting triplets."
- Break condition: If the passive decay step is too aggressive, it may lead to overfitting to the retaining set.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Server -> Global embeddings -> Clients -> Local embeddings -> Server (bidirectional knowledge flow)
- Critical path: 1) Server initializes global embedding. 2) Clients receive global embedding and update local embeddings using mutual knowledge distillation. 3) Clients upload updated global embeddings to server. 4) Server aggregates global embeddings. 5) For unlearning, clients perform retroactive interference and passive decay, then upload updated global embeddings.
- Design tradeoffs: The use of mutual knowledge distillation improves performance but increases communication overhead. The two-step unlearning process (retroactive interference + passive decay) is effective but requires additional training time.
- Failure signatures: If the mutual knowledge distillation fails, the global and local embeddings may become too dissimilar. If the unlearning process is too aggressive, it may lead to performance degradation on other tasks.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the link prediction performance of the framework on a small dataset with a few clients.
  2. Test the unlearning capability by forgetting a small set of triplets and evaluating the performance on the remaining triplets.
  3. Test the scalability of the framework by increasing the number of clients and the size of the dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of FedLU scale with increasing number of clients beyond 10?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper tests FedLU with 3, 5, and 10 clients but does not explore performance with larger numbers of clients.
- Why unresolved: The experimental results only cover a limited range of client numbers, leaving scalability for larger numbers untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with 20, 50, or 100 clients would provide insights into scalability and performance trends.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different negative sampling strategies on the effectiveness of federated KG unlearning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses a standard negative sampling approach but does not explore how alternative strategies (e.g., hard negative mining) might affect unlearning performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate the sensitivity of unlearning to negative sampling choices, which could be critical for practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing unlearning performance across multiple negative sampling strategies would clarify their impact.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does FedLU handle federated KG embedding when relations overlap between clients?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states it does not assume relation overlap between clients, leaving this scenario unexplored.
- Why unresolved: The experimental setup avoids relation overlap, so the method's behavior in overlapping relation scenarios remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing FedLU on datasets with overlapping relations would demonstrate its robustness and adaptability to this case.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the trade-off between unlearning effectiveness and computational cost in FedLU?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that unlearning is more efficient than retraining but does not quantify the computational overhead or provide detailed analysis.
- Why unresolved: The paper lacks a thorough analysis of the computational costs associated with the unlearning steps (retroactive interference and passive decay).
- What evidence would resolve it: Profiling the computational time and resource usage for unlearning versus retraining across different dataset sizes would clarify the trade-offs.

## Limitations
- Dataset construction methodology lacks detailed specifications for clustering parameters
- Effectiveness of unlearning depends heavily on unspecified hyperparameters and scoring function
- Assumption that clients can maintain both local and global embeddings may not be practical in resource-constrained environments
