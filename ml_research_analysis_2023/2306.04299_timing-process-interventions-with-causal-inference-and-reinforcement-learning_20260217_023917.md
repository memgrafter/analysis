# Timing Process Interventions with Causal Inference and Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04299
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: RL outperforms CI in synthetic process intervention timing with uplifts of 1,616 vs 1,526 (Process 1) and 1,806 vs 1,682 (Process 2)

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the optimization of business processes through precisely timed interventions, comparing causal inference (CI) and reinforcement learning (RL) methods. The research introduces synthetic data to enable genuine online RL and accurate evaluation, benchmarking both methods against a perfect policy. Experiments on two synthetic processes reveal that RL's policies outperform CI's in terms of uplift metrics and demonstrate greater robustness. RL approaches near-optimal performance, achieving within 3% of the perfect policy results. The study highlights RL's versatility for complex Prescriptive Process Monitoring (PresPM) problems while acknowledging CI's utility in settings where online learning is infeasible.

## Method Summary
The study employs a comparative framework between CI and RL for optimizing intervention timing in business processes. Synthetic process data is generated to enable online RL learning and accurate evaluation against a perfect policy baseline. CI methods use propensity score matching to estimate counterfactual outcomes, while RL methods learn optimal intervention policies through interaction with the environment. The experiments focus on two synthetic processes, measuring performance through uplift metrics and robustness. The methodology emphasizes the importance of online RL for process optimization and provides insights into the scalability and applicability of both approaches.

## Key Results
- RL policies achieve higher mean uplifts than CI: 1,616 vs 1,526 for Process 1 and 1,806 vs 1,682 for Process 2
- RL demonstrates greater robustness with lower standard deviations in performance metrics
- RL approaches within 3% of perfect policy performance, indicating near-optimal intervention timing

## Limitations
- Synthetic data may not capture the complexity and noise of real business processes
- Online RL framework assumes immediate and accurate feedback, often unrealistic in practice
- Computational costs of RL may limit scalability for large-scale processes

## Confidence
- High: Statistically significant uplifts (1,616 vs 1,526 for Process 1; 1,806 vs 1,682 for Process 2) and demonstrated robustness (lower standard deviations)
- Medium: Real-world applicability due to synthetic nature of data and controlled experimental conditions
- Low: Claims about 3% gap from optimal performance rely entirely on simulated perfect policy baselines
