# Supervised Knowledge May Hurt Novel Class Discovery Performance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03648
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03648
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Supervised knowledge can hurt NCD performance when semantic similarity is low, with up to 5% improvement possible by selectively using self-supervised knowledge.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the common assumption that supervised knowledge always benefits novel class discovery (NCD) by introducing a metric called transfer flow to measure semantic similarity between labeled and unlabeled datasets. The authors demonstrate that using supervised pretraining can actually hurt NCD performance when the labeled and unlabeled data have low semantic similarity. They propose a practical metric called pseudo transfer flow to decide whether to use supervised knowledge and develop a method that smoothly combines supervised and self-supervised knowledge. Experiments on a benchmark built using ImageNet's hierarchical structure show that transfer flow correlates with NCD performance and that supervised knowledge can hurt performance when semantic similarity is low.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces transfer flow as a metric to quantify semantic similarity between labeled and unlabeled datasets by computing MMD between conditional distributions. They construct a benchmark using ImageNet's hierarchical structure to create labeled/unlabeled splits with varying semantic similarity levels. The authors modify existing NCD methods (RS, NCL, UNO) to incorporate both supervised and self-supervised learning components. They propose pseudo transfer flow as a practical approximation that can be computed from cluster assignments on pretrained representations. The method decides whether to use supervised knowledge based on the transfer flow value and can smoothly combine both types of knowledge with an adjustable weight parameter.

## Key Results
- Transfer flow correlates with NCD performance, showing that semantic similarity predicts whether supervised knowledge helps or hurts
- Using supervised knowledge from low-similarity labeled sets leads to suboptimal results compared to using pure self-supervised knowledge
- Pseudo transfer flow provides a practical proxy for transfer flow and can guide the decision to use supervised knowledge
- The proposed method achieves up to 5% improvement on ImageNet by optimally combining supervised and self-supervised knowledge

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transfer flow quantifies how much labeled set information leaks into the unlabeled set and predicts NCD performance.
- Mechanism: Computes MMD between the conditional distributions of labeled data predictions on unlabeled samples under different unlabeled classes; larger values indicate more discriminative signal from labeled to unlabeled.
- Core assumption: The labeled conditional probability p(xu) on unlabeled data is a valid proxy for how much labeled knowledge can be transferred.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "quantifies the discriminative information in the labeled set leaked in the unlabeled set"
  - [section 3.2]: Definition 2 and Lemma 1 formalize transfer flow and its bounds.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus neighbors are NCD papers but none discuss transfer flow metric explicitly.
- Break condition: If p(xu) is not representative of true label distributions (e.g., under severe domain shift), transfer flow may misrepresent semantic similarity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using supervised pretraining hurts NCD performance when semantic similarity is low.
- Mechanism: Pretraining on labeled data biases the feature space toward labeled classes; if similarity is low, this bias conflicts with the structure of the unlabeled data, reducing clustering accuracy.
- Core assumption: The pretraining representation captures class-relevant structure and that bias from low-similarity labeled data is harmful.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "using supervised information from a low-similarity labeled set may lead to a suboptimal result as compared to using pure self-supervised knowledge"
  - [section 5.1]: Empirical results comparing Xu + Xl vs Xu + (Xl, Yl) show worse performance with supervised knowledge in low-similarity cases.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus neighbors do not discuss harm of supervised knowledge in NCD.
- Break condition: If labeled and unlabeled data share sufficient high-level features (high similarity), supervised pretraining helps rather than hurts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pseudo transfer flow is a practical proxy for transfer flow that can guide data selection.
- Mechanism: Computes MMD between cluster assignments on pretrained representations; aligns with transfer flow and NCD accuracy trends.
- Core assumption: Cluster-derived pseudo-labels approximate the true label distribution sufficiently to estimate semantic similarity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We develop a pseudo-version of the transfer flow as a practical reference to decide if supervised knowledge should be used"
  - [section 3.2]: Definition 3 formalizes pseudo transfer flow; Table 4 shows consistency with accuracy.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus neighbors do not discuss pseudo transfer flow.
- Break condition: If clustering quality is poor (e.g., high-dimensional data with unclear cluster structure), pseudo transfer flow may not reflect true semantic similarity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pretraining module -> Representation extractor -> Transfer flow calculator -> NCD clustering module -> Pseudo transfer flow estimator

- Critical path:
  1. Pretrain model on labeled data (supervised or self-supervised).
  2. Generate representations for unlabeled data.
  3. Compute transfer flow or pseudo transfer flow.
  4. Decide whether to use supervised knowledge.
  5. Run NCD clustering (with or without labeled supervision).

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Supervised pretraining: Higher bias toward labeled classes; beneficial only if similarity is high.
  - Self-supervised pretraining: More general features; better when similarity is low.
  - Pseudo transfer flow: Requires clustering quality; may fail with noisy representations.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low clustering accuracy despite high transfer flow: Cluster assignments do not reflect true label structure.
  - High pseudo transfer flow but poor accuracy: Cluster quality is poor.
  - No improvement with supervised knowledge: Labeled data bias conflicts with unlabeled structure.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compute transfer flow on CIFAR100 high-similarity split and confirm it is larger than low-similarity split.
  2. Compare NCD accuracy using supervised vs self-supervised pretraining on a low-similarity split.
  3. Compute pseudo transfer flow using K-means on ImageNet medium-similarity split and compare with accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed transfer flow metric perform with other kernel functions beyond Gaussian and Laplacian?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compare transfer flow values for Gaussian and Laplacian kernels with varying bandwidths in Appendix A.2.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests two specific kernel types and does not explore other potential kernels that might be more suitable for different data distributions or semantic relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing transfer flow across a wider range of kernel functions (e.g., polynomial, sigmoid, Matern) on diverse datasets would clarify which kernels are most effective.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical explanation for why supervised knowledge sometimes harms NCD performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors observe that supervised knowledge can hurt performance in low semantic similarity settings, and provide some intuitive explanations in Appendix D, but do not offer a rigorous theoretical framework.
- Why unresolved: The paper raises the question but does not provide a complete theoretical understanding of the mechanisms by which supervised knowledge can introduce bias or conflicting information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a formal information-theoretic analysis or empirical studies that precisely quantify the types of bias introduced by supervised knowledge in different similarity regimes.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the optimal weighting of supervised knowledge (α) vary across different semantic similarity levels and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors experiment with different α values in Section 5.2.2 and find that the optimal α varies between high, medium, and low similarity settings.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests a limited range of α values (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) on specific datasets and does not provide a general method for determining the optimal α for arbitrary NCD tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a principled method for automatically determining the optimal α based on the transfer flow or other dataset characteristics, validated across multiple datasets and similarity levels.

## Limitations
- Experiments are primarily conducted on ImageNet and CIFAR100, which may not generalize to other domains or datasets with different characteristics.
- The paper assumes that the labeled conditional probability p(xu) is a valid proxy for transfer potential, but this may not hold under severe domain shifts or when the labeled and unlabeled data have very different distributions.
- The clustering quality directly impacts pseudo transfer flow estimation, and poor clustering could lead to incorrect decisions about using supervised knowledge.

## Confidence

- Supervised knowledge hurting NCD performance at low similarity: Medium-High
- Pseudo transfer flow as a reliable proxy: Medium
