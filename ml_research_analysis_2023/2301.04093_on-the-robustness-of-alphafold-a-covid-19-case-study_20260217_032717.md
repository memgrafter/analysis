# On the Robustness of AlphaFold: A COVID-19 Case Study

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that AlphaFold, despite its high accuracy in protein structure prediction, is vulnerable to adversarial attacks. The authors show that minor perturbations to protein sequences—bounded by 20 units in BLOSUM62 distance and involving only five residue changes—can lead to significantly different predicted 3D structures. By generating adversarial sequences for 111 COVID-19 proteins, they find that the average Global Distance Test (GDT) similarity score drops to approximately 34%, indicating a substantial decrease in prediction robustness. Additionally, they prove that the problem of generating such adversarial sequences is NP-complete, establishing a theoretical foundation for the computational complexity of this task. The findings highlight the need for improved robustness in protein folding neural networks for reliable real-world applications.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a framework for generating adversarial protein sequences by optimizing for sequences that are biologically similar to the original (within BLOSUM62 distance threshold) but produce structurally divergent predictions from AlphaFold. They systematically test this approach on 111 COVID-19 proteins, generating adversarial examples and comparing the structural similarity between original and adversarial predictions using RMSD and GDT metrics. The computational complexity of finding such adversarial sequences is proven to be NP-complete through reduction from the CLIQUE problem.

## Key Results
- Minor sequence perturbations (BLOSUM62 distance ≤20, Hamming distance ≤5) cause significant structural deviations in AlphaFold predictions
- Average GDT similarity score between original and adversarial predictions is approximately 34%
- RMSD values range from 1.011Å to 49.531Å for the tested adversarial examples
- The problem of finding adversarial protein sequences is proven to be NP-complete

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: AlphaFold is vulnerable to adversarial attacks that cause large structural deviations with minimal sequence changes.
- Mechanism: Small perturbations in protein sequences, bounded by BLOSUM62 distance of 20 units and Hamming distance of 5 residues, can significantly alter the predicted 3D structure. The paper demonstrates this by generating adversarial sequences that produce RMSD values ranging from 1.011Å to 49.531Å and GDT similarity scores averaging around 34%.
- Core assumption: Minor sequence changes do not typically lead to drastic structural changes in natural proteins, but AlphaFold's predictions violate this biological expectation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The problem of finding adversarial protein sequences is computationally hard (NP-complete).
- Mechanism: The authors prove that finding minimal perturbations to fool protein folding neural networks is NP-complete by reducing the CLIQUE problem to the PFNN Adversarial Attack (PAA) problem. This establishes a theoretical foundation for why finding robust solutions is difficult.
- Core assumption: The complexity of the adversarial attack problem scales with the combinatorial nature of sequence perturbations and their structural effects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "they prove that the problem of generating such adversarial sequences is NP-complete"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: AlphaFold's prediction confidence scores do not correlate with structural robustness to adversarial perturbations.
- Mechanism: The paper shows that AlphaFold predicts adversarial structures with similar confidence values to the original sequence (e.g., average confidence around 80% for both), yet the RMSD and GDT scores indicate substantial structural differences. This suggests confidence scores are not reliable indicators of prediction trustworthiness.
- Core assumption: High confidence predictions should correspond to structurally stable outputs that are robust to small perturbations.

## Limitations
- The computational complexity analysis is theoretical and may not reflect practical attack feasibility
- The study focuses exclusively on AlphaFold without comparing robustness across different protein folding architectures
- The biological plausibility and functional impact of adversarial sequences aren't validated through experimental assays

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The experimental methodology for generating and evaluating adversarial sequences is well-defined and reproducible. The structural distance metrics (RMSD, GDT) are standard and their interpretation is clear. The observed structural deviations (GDT ~34%) are statistically significant across the tested COVID-19 protein set.

- **Medium confidence**: The NP-completeness proof provides theoretical grounding but may not reflect practical attack complexity. The biological relevance of finding sequences that preserve BLOSUM62 similarity while causing structural divergence is assumed but not experimentally validated in vivo or through functional assays.

- **Low confidence**: Claims about the broader implications for AI safety in protein engineering applications are speculative without empirical evidence of how such adversarial vulnerabilities could be exploited in real-world scenarios.
