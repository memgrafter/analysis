# $Ae^2I$: A Double Autoencoder for Imputation of Missing Values

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.06633
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06633
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Double autoencoder (Ae2I) achieves state-of-the-art RMSE performance on Movielens datasets by jointly leveraging row-row and column-column relationships

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces $Ae^2I$, a double autoencoder framework designed to impute missing values in data tables by simultaneously leveraging both row-row and column-column relationships. Unlike prior methods that rely on a single relationship type, $Ae^2I$ employs two interconnected autoencoders trained with a cross-loss penalty to ensure consistency at intersecting predictions. The model incorporates consecutive weight regularization and customizable activation functions from a logarithmic growth family. Empirical evaluations on Movielens 100K and 1M datasets demonstrate that $Ae^2I$ outperforms 12 baseline models, achieving state-of-the-art RMSE performance. The study highlights the effectiveness of consecutive weight regularization and activation function selection in improving imputation accuracy.

## Method Summary
$Ae^2I$ is a double autoencoder framework that jointly leverages row-row and column-column relationships to impute missing values in data tables. The model consists of two interconnected autoencoders trained simultaneously, with a cross-loss penalty ensuring consistency at intersecting predictions. The architecture incorporates consecutive weight regularization and customizable activation functions from a logarithmic growth family. The model is evaluated on Movielens 100K and 1M datasets, demonstrating state-of-the-art RMSE performance compared to 12 baseline models.

## Key Results
- $Ae^2I$ achieves state-of-the-art RMSE performance on Movielens datasets.
- The model outperforms 12 baseline models, including traditional and deep learning approaches.
- For Movielens 1M, $Ae^2I$ achieves an RMSE of 0.817, surpassing existing top models.
- Consecutive weight regularization and activation function selection significantly improve imputation accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
$Ae^2I$ works by leveraging both row-row and column-column relationships simultaneously, which allows it to capture more comprehensive patterns in the data. The dual autoencoder architecture ensures that the model can impute missing values more accurately by considering both dimensions. The cross-loss penalty enforces consistency at intersecting predictions, while consecutive weight regularization and customizable activation functions further enhance the model's performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input data -> Row autoencoder -> Column autoencoder -> Cross-loss penalty -> Imputed output
- **Critical path:** The row and column autoencoders are trained simultaneously, with their outputs combined through the cross-loss penalty to produce the final imputed values.
- **Design tradeoffs:** The dual autoencoder architecture allows for more comprehensive imputation but increases computational complexity. The cross-loss penalty ensures consistency but may introduce additional training challenges.
- **Failure signatures:** Poor performance may indicate issues with the cross-loss penalty or insufficient regularization. Inconsistent imputed values may suggest problems with the dual autoencoder training process.
- **First experiments:** 1) Train the row autoencoder alone to establish a baseline. 2) Train the column autoencoder alone to establish a baseline. 3) Combine both autoencoders with the cross-loss penalty to evaluate the full model.
