# The Hardware Impact of Quantization and Pruning for Weights in Spiking Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04174
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04174
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: SNNs achieve optimal energy efficiency with ternary quantization combined with pruning, maintaining accuracy down to 80% sparsity and enabling fine-grained accuracy-energy trade-offs.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically evaluates the impact of weight quantization and pruning on energy efficiency in spiking neural networks for gesture recognition. The authors examine eight quantization levels (8-bit to ternary) and seven pruning rates (0-97.5%) individually and in combination. Results demonstrate that SNNs are highly amenable to quantization, maintaining accuracy down to ternary weights, while pruning alone only preserves accuracy up to 80% sparsity. The combination of ternary quantization with pruning provides the best energy efficiency due to reduced arithmetic precision and high weight sparsity. The study also reveals that cumulative pruning-then-quantization maintains better accuracy in low-energy regimes compared to joint approaches.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates a state-of-the-art SNN model on DVS gesture recognition data, using backpropagation-through-time with arc-tangent surrogate gradients for training. Weight quantization-aware training is implemented via gradient scaling, and global unstructured pruning targets specific sparsity rates. The evaluation includes eight quantization levels (8b to ternary) and seven pruning rates (0-97.5%), tested individually and in combination using both cumulative (pruning-then-quantization) and joint approaches. Energy efficiency is estimated using a component-based model calibrated to 28nm technology, comparing different sparse data representation schemes (UBM, UOP, CP, RLE) across varying sparsity levels.

## Key Results
- SNNs maintain accuracy down to ternary weights (3-level quantization), with only 1.5% accuracy drop from 8-bit baseline
- Pruning alone preserves accuracy only up to 80% sparsity, beyond which energy costs increase by 45%
- Ternary quantization combined with pruning provides optimal energy efficiency through both reduced arithmetic precision and high weight sparsity
- Cumulative pruning-then-quantization maintains better accuracy in low-energy regimes compared to joint approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Quantization-induced sparsity provides significant energy efficiency gains when combined with sparse data representations.
- Mechanism: Ternarization forces most weights to be zero, enabling both low-precision arithmetic and high weight sparsity. This combination reduces memory accesses and compute operations in hardware.
- Core assumption: The hardware architecture can efficiently exploit weight sparsity through sparse data representations (e.g., RLE).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Ternary quantization provides the best energy efficiency due to both reduced arithmetic precision and high weight sparsity."
  - [section III] "Ternarization gains the most" among quantization methods.
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related papers discuss compression but not this specific sparsity-quantization interaction.
- Break condition: If sparse representation overhead exceeds the savings from reduced memory accesses and computations, the efficiency gain disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Cumulative pruning-then-quantization maintains better accuracy in low-energy regimes compared to joint approaches.
- Mechanism: Pruning first removes redundant weights, then quantization operates on a more compact model, preserving critical information while achieving aggressive compression.
- Core assumption: The order of operations matters because pruning can remove weights that quantization would have kept, and vice versa.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Cumulative pruning-then-quantization maintaining better accuracy in low-energy regimes."
  - [section III] "Cumulative quantization and pruning maintains accuracy in low-energy regimes"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; this appears to be a novel finding.
- Break condition: If the model cannot tolerate any accuracy loss, joint approaches might perform better by finding optimal trade-offs between pruning and quantization simultaneously.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different sparse representation schemes are optimal at different sparsity levels.
- Mechanism: For low sparsity (<25%), uncompressed offset pairs perform best. For high sparsity, run-length encoding provides superior compression and energy efficiency.
- Core assumption: The overhead of encoding/decoding metadata varies with the sparsity pattern and must be balanced against the savings from reduced data movement.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III] "For models with little sparsity (e.g. less than 25%) the UOP scheme performs best. However, models with considerable amounts of sparsity achieve better energy and EDP performance with RLE."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; this is specific to the studied SNN model and hardware.
- Break condition: If the sparsity level changes dynamically or if hardware support for different sparse formats varies, the optimal representation scheme may shift.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to one dataset (DVS gesture recognition) and one SNN model architecture, limiting generalizability
- Energy model assumes specific hardware configuration (Eyeriss-like digital SNN accelerator) without exploring alternative architectures
- Does not examine dynamic sparsity patterns or real-time adaptation of sparse representations

## Confidence
- Combined pruning-quantization claims: Medium - limited to single SNN architecture and task
- Optimal sparsity levels for different sparse representations: High - systematic evaluation across multiple sparsity ranges
- Ternary quantization providing optimal energy efficiency: Medium - assumes specific hardware characteristics that may differ in other implementations
