# Automated Identification of Disaster News For Crisis Management Using Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09896
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09896
- Reference count: 0
- Achieved 91.07% accuracy in identifying legitimate vs. illegitimate disaster news articles

## Executive Summary
This study develops a machine learning model to distinguish legitimate from illegitimate disaster news articles, focusing on Typhoon Rai coverage in the Philippines. The model combines Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Multinomial Naive Bayes classifiers using a voting ensemble approach, achieving high accuracy through text preprocessing techniques including Bag of Words, TF-IDF, and lemmatization. Trained and tested on a balanced dataset of 160 news articles, the model demonstrates strong performance with 91.07% accuracy and 91.38% F1-score, offering potential for supporting crisis management efforts by filtering unreliable information during disasters.

## Method Summary
The research employs a supervised machine learning approach using a voting ensemble classifier that combines three base models: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Multinomial Naive Bayes. Text preprocessing involves Bag of Words and TF-IDF vectorization with lemmatization to transform raw news articles into numerical features. The model is trained and evaluated on a balanced dataset of 160 articles (80 legitimate, 80 illegitimate) related to Typhoon Rai in the Philippines, with performance assessed through accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score metrics.

## Key Results
- Combined BOW model achieved 91.07% accuracy, 88.33% precision, 94.64% recall, and 91.38% F1-score
- Combined TF-IDF model reached 91.18% accuracy, 86.89% precision, 94.64% recall, and 90.60% F1-score
- Model effectively distinguishes legitimate from illegitimate disaster news articles using ensemble learning approach

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Combining multiple ML algorithms via voting classifier improves classification robustness over single models
- Mechanism: Each base classifier captures different patterns in text features; the voting ensemble aggregates predictions to reduce variance and bias
- Core assumption: Base classifiers are diverse enough that their errors are not perfectly correlated
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] States "By combining all the machine learning techniques, the Combined BOW model was able to reach an accuracy of 91.07%"
  - [section] "Additionally, a confusion matrix was created to help visualise the performance of the Machine Learning Model by displaying the true label and the predicted label"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence found in corpus neighbors about voting ensembles for disaster news classification
- Break condition: If base classifiers are highly correlated in their predictions, ensemble offers minimal improvement

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Text preprocessing (BOW, TF-IDF, lemmatization) transforms raw news text into discriminative features for ML models
- Mechanism: BOW creates word frequency vectors, TF-IDF weights words by importance, lemmatization reduces words to base forms; together they create numerical representations capturing semantic patterns between legitimate and illegitimate articles
- Core assumption: The vocabulary and structure differences between legitimate and fake disaster news are learnable from these feature representations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Bag of Words, TF-IDF and Lemmatization were implemented in the Model"
  - [section] "Additionally, the output included the identification of the top 30 frequently used words for the legitimate news articles and illegitimate ones"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors focus on different NLP approaches (CNNs, quantum-enhanced) not BOW/TF-IDF
- Break condition: If fake news uses similar vocabulary and structure as legitimate news, feature engineering cannot discriminate effectively

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Balanced dataset (1:1 ratio) prevents model bias toward the majority class and enables reliable performance metrics
- Mechanism: Equal representation of legitimate and illegitimate articles ensures the model learns both classes equally and performance metrics like precision and recall are meaningful
- Core assumption: The real-world distribution of legitimate vs. illegitimate disaster news is similar to the balanced training set
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The datasets throughout the training and testing phase were balanced with a 1:1 ratio from the legitimate and illegitimate news sources"
  - [abstract] Shows performance metrics (precision, recall, F1) calculated for both classes
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence about dataset balancing strategies
- Break condition: If real-world data is highly imbalanced, model may underperform despite balanced training

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data scraping (Scrapy) → Text preprocessing (BOW/TF-IDF, lemmatization) → Base ML models (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Multinomial Naive Bayes) → Voting ensemble → Performance evaluation (confusion matrix, F-score)
- Critical path: Preprocessing → Base model training → Ensemble prediction → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: BOW captures frequency but ignores word order; TF-IDF weights rare words higher; lemmatization reduces vocabulary but may lose some meaning
- Failure signatures: High accuracy but low precision suggests overfitting to common words; high recall but low precision indicates the model labels too many articles as illegitimate
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train each base classifier separately on the balanced dataset and compare individual performance
  2. Test ensemble performance with different voting strategies (hard vs. soft voting)
  3. Evaluate model performance when dataset is imbalanced to understand real-world robustness

## Limitations
- Balanced dataset ratio may not reflect real-world disaster news landscapes where legitimate reporting typically dominates
- Small dataset size (160 articles total) raises concerns about model generalization to different disaster types or geographic regions
- Single disaster event focus (Typhoon Rai) limits evidence for broader applicability

## Confidence
- Model performance metrics (High): The reported accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 scores are internally consistent and methodologically sound
- Ensemble method effectiveness (Medium): While the voting classifier shows improvement, the study doesn't provide ablation testing of individual base models
- Generalizability to other disasters (Low): Single-event focus and small dataset size limit confidence in broader applicability

## Next Checks

2. Evaluate model on news articles from different disaster types (earthquakes, floods, wildfires) and geographic regions to test generalization
3. Conduct time-series validation by testing on news articles from the same disaster type but different years to assess temporal stability
