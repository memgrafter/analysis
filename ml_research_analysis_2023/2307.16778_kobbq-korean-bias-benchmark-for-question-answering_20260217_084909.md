# KoBBQ: Korean Bias Benchmark for Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2307.16778
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16778
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: Culturally adapted Korean bias benchmark for QA with 268 templates and 76,048 samples across 12 bias categories

## Executive Summary
KoBBQ is a culturally adapted bias benchmark for Korean question answering systems, extending the English BBQ dataset to Korean language and culture. The authors develop a systematic framework for cultural adaptation that partitions data into Simply-Translated, Target-Modified, and Sample-Removed categories, while adding Korean-specific cultural categories. The benchmark contains 268 templates and 76,048 samples across 12 categories of social bias, validated through large-scale surveys of Korean social stereotypes. Using this dataset, the authors evaluate several multilingual language models, demonstrating significant differences in bias patterns between Korean and English models and highlighting the importance of culturally specific bias measurement.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a three-category framework for cultural adaptation: Simply-Translated (direct translation with minimal changes), Target-Modified (updating bias targets to reflect Korean social groups), and Sample-Removed (removing templates inappropriate for Korean culture). They conducted extensive surveys to collect and validate social biases and targets specific to Korean culture, then applied this framework to adapt 268 templates from the original BBQ dataset. The resulting KoBBQ dataset includes Korean-specific categories addressing cultural phenomena unique to Korean society. The authors evaluate multilingual language models using both accuracy and bias scores, comparing results with English models to demonstrate cultural differences in bias patterns.

## Key Results
- KoBBQ dataset contains 268 templates and 76,048 samples across 12 social bias categories
- Large-scale surveys successfully collected and validated Korean-specific social biases and targets
- Multilingual models show significantly different bias scores when evaluated on KoBBQ versus English BBQ
- Cultural adaptation framework demonstrates effectiveness in capturing Korean-specific stereotypes
- Results validate the need for hand-crafted, culturally specific data for accurate bias measurement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by systematically accounting for cultural differences in how social biases manifest across languages and societies. By partitioning the adaptation process into three distinct categories based on the type of modification needed, the framework ensures appropriate handling of different types of cultural content. The survey-based validation ensures that the adapted targets and categories accurately reflect real Korean social stereotypes rather than assumptions. The comprehensive dataset size and template diversity enable robust measurement of model biases across multiple dimensions of social bias.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Survey data collection -> Cultural adaptation framework -> Template modification -> Dataset assembly -> Model evaluation -> Bias analysis
**Critical path**: The survey validation ensures cultural authenticity, which is essential for meaningful bias measurement in the adapted dataset
**Design tradeoffs**: Cultural specificity vs. generalizability - the framework prioritizes Korean cultural accuracy over direct English equivalence
**Failure signatures**: Misclassification of templates between adaptation categories could lead to inappropriate cultural representation or loss of bias measurement capability

## Limitations
- Focus exclusively on Korean culture limits generalizability to other languages and cultural contexts
- Manual annotation process introduces potential subjectivity in category assignments
- Evaluation limited to multilingual models rather than specifically trained monolingual Korean models
- Demographic breakdown of survey participants not detailed, limiting understanding of representation

## Confidence
- High confidence: The cultural adaptation framework is well-defined and dataset creation is clearly documented
- Medium confidence: Survey methodology appears robust but lacks detailed demographic information
- Medium confidence: Evaluation results show differences between Korean and English models but sample size of models is small
