# Employing Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) Methodologies to Analyze the Correlation between Input Variables and Tensile Strength in Additively Manufactured Samples

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.18426
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18426
- **Reference count**: 21
- **Key outcome**: XAI techniques, particularly SHAP, were used to identify that Infill percentage and Extrusion Temperature are the dominant drivers of Tensile Strength in additively manufactured specimens, while Layer Height and Print Speed have minor effects; the relationship between parameters and strength is complex and nonlinear.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the correlation between key input variables and Tensile Strength in additively manufactured samples using Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) methodologies. The authors employ the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) framework to interpret a machine learning model trained on experimental data, providing insight into the relative influence of each parameter and the nature of their interactions. The analysis reveals that Infill percentage and Extrusion Temperature are the most significant factors affecting Tensile Strength, while Layer Height and Print Speed have comparatively minor impacts. Importantly, the relationship between process parameters and material strength is found to be complex and nonlinear, which challenges the adequacy of traditional linear models for process optimization.

## Method Summary
The authors collected experimental data on Tensile Strength from additively manufactured specimens, varying four input parameters: Infill percentage, Layer Height, Extrusion Temperature, and Print Speed. A machine learning model was trained on this dataset to predict Tensile Strength based on the input parameters. To interpret the model and understand the contribution of each parameter, the SHAP framework was applied. SHAP values quantify the impact of each feature on the model's predictions, both in terms of magnitude and direction, allowing the authors to identify which parameters most strongly influence Tensile Strength and how they interact. This approach enabled the extraction of actionable insights for process optimization.

## Key Results
- Infill percentage and Extrusion Temperature are the dominant drivers of Tensile Strength in additively manufactured specimens.
- Layer Height and Print Speed have minor effects on Tensile Strength compared to Infill percentage and Extrusion Temperature.
- The relationship between input parameters and Tensile Strength is complex and nonlinear, not adequately captured by simple linear models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
XAI methodologies, such as SHAP, work by providing a model-agnostic way to interpret complex machine learning models. SHAP assigns each feature an importance value for a particular prediction, based on game theory principles, which reflects the feature's contribution to the model's output. By aggregating SHAP values across many predictions, the relative importance of each parameter can be quantified, and the nature of their interactions can be revealed. This allows researchers to move beyond black-box predictions and gain actionable insights into which factors most strongly influence material properties, and how these factors interact in a nonlinear fashion.

## Limitations
- The limited dataset size may restrict the generalizability of the XAI-derived insights across broader material systems or printing conditions.
- The SHAP analysis assumes that the trained model adequately captures the underlying physics of the AM process, which may not hold if the model is overfit or if important process variables were omitted from the input set.
- The complex, nonlinear relationships identified are based on correlations within the experimental dataset rather than established causal mechanisms, so further validation via controlled experiments is needed to confirm these findings.
