# Harnessing the Power of LLMs in Practice: A Survey on ChatGPT and Beyond

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13712
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13712
- Reference count: 40
- This survey provides a comprehensive and practical guide for practitioners and end-users working with Large Language Models (LLMs) in their downstream natural language processing (NLP) tasks.

## Executive Summary
This survey offers a comprehensive guide for practitioners working with Large Language Models (LLMs) in downstream NLP tasks. The work examines LLMs from three perspectives: models, data, and downstream tasks, providing insights into when and how to use LLMs effectively. It discusses use and non-use cases across various NLP tasks including knowledge-intensive tasks, traditional NLU tasks, NLG tasks, and reasoning tasks. The guide aims to help researchers and practitioners successfully implement LLMs across a wide range of applications.

## Method Summary
The paper provides a comprehensive review of current GPT- and BERT-style LLMs, discussing their architectures, pre-training data, and performance across various NLP tasks. It examines the influence of pre-training data composition, training data, and test data on LLM performance. The survey synthesizes existing literature to provide practical guidance on when to use LLMs versus fine-tuned models for different task types, covering knowledge-intensive tasks, traditional NLU, NLG, and reasoning tasks. The work also addresses safety challenges and deployment considerations.

## Key Results
- LLMs generalize better than fine-tuned models on out-of-distribution data due to their diverse pre-training
- LLMs excel at knowledge-intensive tasks through memorization of real-world knowledge during pre-training
- Scaling up LLMs unlocks emergent reasoning abilities not present in smaller models
- Practical guidance provided for model selection, prompt engineering, and task-specific adaptation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs generalize better than fine-tuned models on out-of-distribution data
- Mechanism: LLMs are pre-trained on massive, diverse corpora without task-specific fine-tuning, so their representations are more robust to distribution shifts. This avoids the overfitting that occurs when fine-tuning on limited, task-specific datasets.
- Core assumption: The diversity and scale of pre-training data capture sufficient variation to handle unseen data distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "LLMs generalize better than fine-tuned models in downstream tasks facing out-of-distribution data, such as adversarial examples and domain shifts."
  - [section 3.3] "LLMs perform quite well facing such scenarios because they do not have an explicit fitting process."
  - [corpus] Weak - related surveys confirm OOD generalization is a known LLM strength, but few empirical comparisons exist.
- Break condition: If the pre-training corpus lacks diversity in the target domain, or if the task requires highly specialized patterns not present in the pre-training data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LLMs excel at knowledge-intensive tasks due to their memorization of real-world knowledge
- Mechanism: The massive scale of LLMs (billions of parameters) allows them to memorize vast amounts of factual knowledge during pre-training, which can be retrieved via prompting without external retrieval.
- Core assumption: Memorization of knowledge during pre-training is sufficient for downstream tasks, and the knowledge is retained and retrievable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "LLMs excel at knowledge-intensive tasks due to their massive real-world knowledge."
  - [section 4.3.1] "With billions of training tokens and parameters, LLMs have much more real-world knowledge than fine-tuned models."
  - [corpus] Weak - survey shows LLMs perform well on knowledge tasks, but doesn't provide direct memorization evidence.
- Break condition: If the required knowledge is not present in the pre-training corpus, or if the knowledge has changed since pre-training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Scaling up LLMs unlocks emergent reasoning abilities
- Mechanism: As model size increases, LLMs develop new capabilities (emergent abilities) that weren't present in smaller models, including arithmetic reasoning, commonsense reasoning, and logical deduction.
- Core assumption: There are discrete "thresholds" in model size where new reasoning abilities emerge, not just gradual improvement.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "With the exponential increase of model scales, LLMs become especially capable of reasoning like arithmetic reasoning and commonsense reasoning."
  - [section 4.4.2] "As defined in [113], emergent abilities of LLMs are abilities that are not present in smaller-scale models but are present in large-scale models."
  - [corpus] Moderate - surveys confirm scaling improves reasoning, but emergent ability evidence is mixed and task-dependent.
- Break condition: If the task requires reasoning patterns that don't scale with model size, or if the model hits computational/architectural limits.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Foundation models (pre-trained LLMs) → Prompt engineering → Task-specific adaptation (few-shot/zero-shot) → Evaluation

- Critical path: Model selection → Prompt engineering → Evaluation → Deployment
  - The most critical decision is choosing the right model for the task based on pre-training data and model capabilities

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Performance vs. cost: Larger models perform better but are more expensive to run
  - Generalization vs. specialization: LLMs generalize well but may underperform specialized fine-tuned models on specific tasks
  - Latency vs. quality: Larger models have higher latency but better quality outputs

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on tasks requiring specialized knowledge not in pre-training data
  - High computational costs for tasks that fine-tuned smaller models could handle
  - Hallucinations or factual errors in knowledge-intensive tasks

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test zero-shot performance on a simple classification task to assess baseline LLM capabilities
  2. Compare few-shot prompt performance vs. fine-tuned smaller model on a domain-specific task
  3. Evaluate LLM performance on an out-of-distribution test set vs. a fine-tuned model

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a comprehensive evaluation framework for LLMs that accurately reflects their performance in real-world scenarios, considering the challenges posed by noisy/unstructured input, tasks not formalized by academia, and following users' instructions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 4.6 discusses the challenges LLMs face in real-world scenarios and mentions that evaluating the effectiveness of models in the real world is still an open problem.
- Why unresolved: Real-world scenarios are complex and diverse, making it difficult to create a standardized evaluation framework that captures all the nuances and challenges.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of a standardized evaluation framework that includes diverse real-world scenarios, input types, and task definitions, along with extensive testing and validation of LLMs' performance using this framework.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we improve the interpretability of LLMs to better understand their decision-making process and reasoning abilities, especially in tasks requiring complex reasoning and emergent abilities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 4.4 discusses the reasoning abilities and emergent abilities of LLMs, while Section 4.5.2 mentions interpretability as a bonus brought by some LLM abilities.
- Why unresolved: The internal workings of LLMs are complex and not fully understood, making it challenging to interpret their decision-making process and reasoning abilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of novel techniques and methods to visualize, analyze, and interpret the internal representations and decision-making process of LLMs, along with extensive testing and validation of these techniques.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we effectively mitigate the safety challenges associated with LLMs, such as hallucinations, harmful content, and privacy concerns, while maintaining their performance and utility?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 5.3 discusses safety challenges, including hallucinations, harmful content, and privacy concerns.
- Why unresolved: Balancing the performance and utility of LLMs with safety measures is a complex task, and existing methods like human feedback and safeguards may not be sufficient to address all safety concerns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of novel safety measures and techniques that can effectively mitigate safety challenges without compromising the performance and utility of LLMs, along with extensive testing and validation of these measures.

## Limitations
- The survey's claims about LLM generalization and emergent reasoning are primarily supported by related literature reviews rather than original empirical comparisons
- The mechanism for knowledge retrieval relies heavily on the assumption that pre-training data contains relevant knowledge, but the paper doesn't provide evidence about knowledge retention or retrieval accuracy
- The effectiveness of few-shot prompting vs fine-tuning is discussed theoretically but lacks systematic benchmarking across task types

## Confidence
- **High**: Architectural distinctions between encoder-only, decoder-only, and encoder-decoder models; practical tradeoffs between performance, cost, and latency; general guidance on model selection based on pre-training data
- **Medium**: Claims about LLM superiority on out-of-distribution data; effectiveness of in-context learning for tasks with limited annotated data; knowledge retrieval capabilities without external databases
- **Low**: Specific thresholds for emergent reasoning abilities; universal applicability of zero-shot/few-shot prompting across all NLP tasks; assumption that pre-training data diversity alone ensures robust generalization
