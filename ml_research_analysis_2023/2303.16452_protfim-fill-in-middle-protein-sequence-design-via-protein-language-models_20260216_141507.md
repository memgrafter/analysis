# ProtFIM: Fill-in-Middle Protein Sequence Design via Protein Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.16452
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16452
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Key outcome**: ProtFIM outperforms existing protein language models in middle sequence design while maintaining secondary structures

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of protein sequence design in the middle of a protein, which is important for real-world protein engineering but underexplored due to the left-to-right nature of existing protein language models (pLMs). The authors propose a new benchmark called SEIFER to evaluate middle protein sequence design, and introduce a new model called ProtFIM trained via fill-in-middle (FIM) transformation. ProtFIM outperforms existing pLMs in SEIFER tasks, showing better performance in middle protein sequence design while maintaining secondary structures. Additionally, ProtFIM demonstrates comparable performance to larger models in terms of representation quality and pLDDT change. The paper concludes that ProtFIM is a promising tool for practical protein engineering.

## Method Summary
ProtFIM is a protein language model trained with fill-in-middle (FIM) transformation on the GPT2 architecture with 85M parameters. The FIM transformation moves middle spans to the end of sequences during training with special tokens [PRE], [MID], and [SUF], enabling bidirectional context utilization. The model is trained on UniRef50 protein sequences and evaluated on CASP14 proteins using the SEIFER benchmark, which measures secondary structure preservation. ProtFIM also demonstrates strong representation quality on FLIP fitness prediction benchmarks.

## Key Results
- ProtFIM outperforms ProtGPT2-C on SEIFER tasks, showing better secondary structure preservation in middle sequence design
- ProtFIM maintains comparable representation quality to larger models on FLIP fitness prediction benchmarks
- The FIM transformation enables effective bidirectional context utilization without architectural modifications

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FIM transformation enables the model to generate sequences using both prefix and suffix context, overcoming the limitation of left-to-right generation.
- Mechanism: By moving the middle span to the end and adding special tokens [PRE], [MID], and [SUF], the model learns to predict the middle span conditioned on both surrounding contexts during training.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively utilize bidirectional context during inference when the prompt is structured with [PRE], [SUF], and [MID] tokens.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "language models trained via fill-in-middle transformation, called ProtFIM, are more appropriate for protein engineering."
  - [section] "this simple and straightforward transformation successfully gives fill-in-the-middle (FIM) capability to the model without modifying model architecture"
- Break condition: If the model fails to generate coherent sequences when prompted with prefix and suffix context, or if the special tokens interfere with learning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ProtFIM outperforms existing PLMs in middle protein sequence design while maintaining secondary structures.
- Mechanism: By conditioning on both prefix and suffix during generation, ProtFIM can better preserve the surrounding structural context, leading to higher secondary structure recovery rates in the SEIFER benchmark.
- Core assumption: Secondary structure preservation is a valid proxy for maintaining functional properties in protein engineering.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ProtFIM outperforms AR-based pLM, ProtGPT2-C, in infilling protein sequence design."
  - [section] "ProtFIM performs better than ProtGPT2-C regarding retrieval" and "ProtFIM has both AR and FIM properties, resulting in efficient FIM protein engineering."
- Break condition: If SEIFER scores don't correlate with functional protein properties, or if ProtFIM fails to maintain structures in real-world applications.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: FIM modeling provides comparable representation quality to larger models while using fewer parameters.
- Mechanism: By learning from full-context during training, ProtFIM develops richer embeddings that capture protein fitness landscapes effectively, as demonstrated on FLIP benchmark tasks.
- Core assumption: Protein fitness prediction performance correlates with representation quality for downstream protein engineering tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ProtFIM exhibits comparable performance to larger models in terms of representation quality"
  - [section] "ProtFIM has strong protein representations over ProtGPT2-C, meaning that infilling modeling benefits representation learning"
- Break condition: If ProtFIM's representations don't generalize to unseen fitness landscapes or if larger models consistently outperform on protein engineering tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GPT2-base architecture (12 layers, 768 dim) with FIM input transformation during training
- Critical path: FIM transformation → training → inference with [PRE][SUF][MID] prompt → structure prediction via AlphaFold2 → secondary structure calculation via DSSP → evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: FIM transformation adds minimal computational overhead during training while enabling bidirectional context utilization, trading some perplexity performance for better engineering applicability
- Failure signatures: Poor SEIFER scores despite good perplexity, inability to generate diverse sequences, or failure to maintain secondary structures
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train ProtFIM on UniRef50 subset and verify FIM transformation works by checking if model can recover middle spans from prefix-suffix prompts.
  2. Run SEIFER evaluation on CASP14 proteins and compare retrieval/precision scores against ProtGPT2-C baseline.
  3. Test representation quality on FLIP benchmark and verify Spearman correlations meet expectations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the α-helix bias in natural protein databases affect the performance of protein language models (pLMs) on generating coils and β-sheets?
- Basis in paper: The authors found that all PLMs noticeably outperform the random generator only for the α-helix, but not for the coils and β-sheets. They hypothesize that this imbalance gives undesirable α-helix bias in existing protein sequence datasets.
- Why unresolved: While the authors hypothesize about the α-helix bias, they do not provide experimental evidence to confirm this hypothesis. Additionally, they do not explore potential solutions to mitigate this bias.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the effect of preprocessing or encoding techniques to alleviate the α-helix bias on the performance of PLMs on generating coils and β-sheets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the length of the target region in the fill-in-middle (FIM) protein engineering task affect the performance of different protein language models (pLMs)?
- Basis in paper: The authors found that the performance of the random generator drastically drops as the length of the target sites becomes longer, and it fails all predictions when the middle sites are longer than 30. Meanwhile, the performance of pLMs degrades gradually and fails at the last part, where the middle sites are longer than 40.
- Why unresolved: While the authors observe the effect of target region length on model performance, they do not explore the underlying reasons for this behavior or potential solutions to improve performance on longer target regions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the effect of different model architectures or training strategies on the performance of pLMs on longer target regions in the FIM protein engineering task.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the fill-in-middle (FIM) transformation in protein language models (pLMs) affect the representation quality compared to other training objectives like causal language modeling (CLM) and permutation language modeling (PLM)?
- Basis in paper: The authors found that ProtFIM, which uses FIM transformation, has decent performance in zero-shot fitness prediction tasks compared to other pLMs with different training objectives.
- Why unresolved: While the authors observe the effect of FIM transformation on representation quality, they do not explore the underlying reasons for this behavior or potential improvements to the FIM transformation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the effect of different variations of the FIM transformation on the representation quality of pLMs and comparisons with other training objectives.

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't provide sufficient detail on how middle span positions and lengths are sampled during FIM transformation
- SEIFER scores are based on secondary structure preservation, which may not fully capture functional protein properties or fitness
- The evaluation primarily compares against a single baseline (ProtGPT2-C), limiting the generalizability of performance claims

## Confidence

**High Confidence Claims:**
- ProtFIM successfully implements FIM transformation without architectural modifications
- The model outperforms ProtGPT2-C on SEIFER tasks with measurable improvements in secondary structure preservation
- FIM transformation provides better context utilization for middle sequence design compared to causal generation

**Medium Confidence Claims:**
- ProtFIM exhibits comparable representation quality to larger models on FLIP benchmarks
- The SEIFER benchmark provides a valid proxy for evaluating middle sequence design quality
- ProtFIM maintains both AR and FIM properties effectively

**Low Confidence Claims:**
- FIM modeling is "more appropriate for protein engineering" in general - this needs validation across diverse engineering tasks
- The specific performance improvements will generalize to all protein engineering applications

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study on FIM Transformation**: Run experiments comparing ProtFIM with and without FIM transformation (using standard CLM training) on SEIFER tasks to isolate the contribution of the FIM mechanism. This would validate whether the performance gains are specifically due to bidirectional context utilization rather than model capacity differences.

2. **Functional Validation Beyond Secondary Structure**: Test ProtFIM-generated sequences in actual protein engineering applications (e.g., binding affinity, enzyme activity) rather than relying solely on SEIFER scores. This would validate whether secondary structure preservation correlates with functional protein properties.

3. **Broader Baseline Comparison**: Evaluate ProtFIM against additional state-of-the-art protein language models beyond ProtGPT2-C, including both causal and non-causal models of similar parameter count. This would strengthen the claim that FIM modeling provides unique advantages for middle sequence design.
