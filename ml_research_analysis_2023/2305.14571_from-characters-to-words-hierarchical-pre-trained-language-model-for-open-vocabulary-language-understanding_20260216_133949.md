# From Characters to Words: Hierarchical Pre-trained Language Model for Open-vocabulary Language Understanding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14571
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14571
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: Hierarchical model outperforms subword and character-level baselines on SQuAD and GLUE benchmarks while maintaining robustness to spelling errors

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a hierarchical pre-trained language model that operates directly on character sequences with explicit awareness of word boundaries. The model uses a two-level architecture: an intra-word module that learns word representations from characters using a shallow Transformer, and an inter-word module that contextualizes word representations by attending to the full sequence. Experiments show that the model outperforms strong baselines on standard benchmarks including SQuAD and GLUE tasks. It also demonstrates robustness to spelling errors and domain shifts, with higher accuracy under various types of textual corruption compared to sub-word and character-level models. The hierarchical design enables competitive computational efficiency while avoiding reliance on fixed vocabularies.

## Method Summary
The method employs a hierarchical two-level architecture with intra-word and inter-word modules. The intra-word module uses a 4-layer Transformer to process characters within words, while the inter-word module uses a 12-layer Transformer to contextualize word-level representations. Special [WORD_CLS] tokens are inserted at word boundaries to aggregate character representations into word-level features. The model is pre-trained using whole-word masked language modeling on English Wikipedia and BookCorpus (19GB) for 3 epochs, then fine-tuned on downstream tasks including SQuAD, GLUE, and named entity recognition datasets with batch sizes of 16 and learning rates between 2e-5 and 3e-5.

## Key Results
- Outperforms subword and character-level baselines on SQuAD 1.1, SQuAD 2.0, and GLUE tasks
- Demonstrates higher accuracy under synthetic spelling errors and textual corruption
- Shows competitive computational efficiency through hierarchical decomposition of attention

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Hierarchical decomposition reduces computational complexity from O(N²) to O(N·M + N²/M²) by limiting intra-word attention to characters within the same word and inter-word attention to word-level tokens. This avoids full sequence attention over all characters. The advantage depends on word length M being much smaller than total sequence length N, and M being relatively consistent across words.

### Mechanism 2
[WORD_CLS] tokens enable adaptive word-level feature aggregation without predefined pooling windows by learning to aggregate character representations based on context rather than using fixed average/max pooling. The special tokens at word boundaries learn optimal weighting of character features through training, assuming the [WORD_CLS] token can learn optimal weighting of character features through training.

### Mechanism 3
Open-vocabulary design improves robustness to spelling errors and domain shifts by avoiding fixed subword vocabularies, allowing the model to represent unseen words as compositions of known characters rather than failing due to out-of-vocabulary tokens. This assumes character sequences contain sufficient information to reconstruct word meaning even when exact tokens are unknown.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Character sequence → Intra-word module → [WORD_CLS] aggregation → Inter-word module → Final representations
- **Critical path**: Character sequence flows through 4-layer intra-word Transformer, aggregates at [WORD_CLS] tokens, then passes through 12-layer inter-word Transformer
- **Design tradeoffs**: Shallow intra-word vs deep inter-word reflects where most computation should occur; character-level input maintains open vocabulary but requires hierarchical decomposition for efficiency; special tokens add complexity but provide adaptive aggregation
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on tasks requiring subword-level information; computational inefficiency if word boundaries are not properly detected; degraded performance when character-level representations become insufficient
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Implement basic hierarchical structure with fixed word boundaries and test on simple sequence classification; 2) Add [WORD_CLS] tokens and compare aggregation methods (average pooling vs learned); 3) Evaluate robustness by introducing synthetic spelling errors and measuring performance degradation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does the proposed hierarchical model's performance scale with increasing character vocabulary size? The paper uses a character vocabulary of size 1024 but does not explore performance changes with different vocabulary sizes.
- **Open Question 2**: What is the effect of using different word boundary detection methods on model performance across different languages? The paper mentions that spaCy is used for word boundary detection and acknowledges limitations for languages without explicit word boundaries.
- **Open Question 3**: How does the model's robustness to domain shift vary with different amounts of in-domain fine-tuning data? The paper demonstrates robustness to domain shift on biomedical and social media datasets but does not explore how performance changes with varying amounts of in-domain data.

## Limitations

- Computational complexity analysis assumes word length M remains small relative to total sequence length N, which may not hold for languages with long compound words
- Adaptive aggregation through [WORD_CLS] tokens lacks empirical validation against simpler pooling methods
- Robustness claims rely heavily on synthetic spelling error experiments that may not reflect real-world degradation patterns

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Hierarchical architecture design and computational efficiency advantages are well-supported by theoretical analysis and experimental results
- **Medium Confidence**: Effectiveness of adaptive [WORD_CLS] aggregation mechanism is demonstrated but could benefit from more direct comparisons to simpler pooling methods
- **Low Confidence**: Model's robustness to real-world spelling errors and domain shifts needs more extensive validation beyond synthetic corruption experiments

## Next Checks
