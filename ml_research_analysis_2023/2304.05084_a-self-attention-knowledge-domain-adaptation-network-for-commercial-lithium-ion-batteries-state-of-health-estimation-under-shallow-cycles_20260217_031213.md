# A Self-attention Knowledge Domain Adaptation Network for Commercial Lithium-ion Batteries State-of-health Estimation under Shallow Cycles

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05084
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05084
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: SKDAN achieves RMSE within 2% for SOH estimation under shallow-cycle conditions

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of estimating lithium-ion battery state-of-health (SOH) under shallow-cycle conditions, where batteries operate in partial state-of-charge (SOC) ranges rather than full cycles. The authors propose a novel unsupervised deep transfer learning method called the Self-attention Knowledge Domain Adaptation Network (SKDAN) to bridge the domain discrepancy between full-cycle labeled data and shallow-cycle unlabeled data. The SKDAN model uses a self-attention distillation feature extractor to automatically extract domain-invariant features from charge curves, combined with multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy (MK-MMD) for distribution alignment. The model is validated on CALCE and SNL battery datasets under various temperatures, discharge rates, and SOC ranges. The proposed SKDAN achieves a root-mean-square error within 2% and outperforms other transfer learning methods for different SOC ranges, demonstrating superior SOH estimation performance across different operating conditions and manufacturers without requiring full-cycle characteristic tests.

## Method Summary
The SKDAN method employs self-attention distillation to extract degradation-related features from battery charge curves, followed by MK-MMD domain alignment to bridge the gap between full-cycle labeled data and shallow-cycle unlabeled data. The method uses a CNN predictor with smoothness constraint to map extracted features to SOH values. The model is trained using Adam optimizer with random search for hyperparameter optimization. Input features include voltage, voltage difference, capacity difference, and incremental capacity from charge curves, with data preprocessed using sliding window approach and min-max normalization. The model is validated on CALCE and SNL battery datasets under various operating conditions.

## Key Results

- Demonstrates superior performance across different operating conditions and manufacturers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Self-attention distillation extracts domain-invariant features from charge curves, enabling transfer from full-cycle labeled data to shallow-cycle unlabeled data.
- Mechanism: The multi-head self-attention mechanism calculates weighted sums of attention weights at each position in the charge sequence, capturing degradation-related features. The distillation operation then shrinks output length by extracting dominant features through max-pooling, increasing domain-invariance.
- Core assumption: Battery charge processes contain sufficient degradation-related information that can be extracted through self-attention mechanisms, and the distillation operation preserves this information while reducing dimensionality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed method automatically extracts domain-variant features from charge curves to transfer knowledge from the large-scale labeled full cycles to the unlabeled shallow cycles."
  - [section 2.2.1]: "The self-attention mechanism excels at modeling the intrinsic relevance of time-series data. It obtains important degradation information by a weighted sum of the attention weights calculated at each position."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-kernel maximum mean discrepancy (MK-MMD) aligns the probability distributions of features between source and target domains, reducing domain discrepancy.
- Mechanism: MK-MMD measures the squared distance between distributions of features in the RKHS using multiple kernels weighted together. By minimizing this distance, samples from source and target domains are transformed into a feature space where their distributions are similar.
- Core assumption: The degradation-related features extracted by self-attention contain sufficient information to align distributions between domains when measured with MK-MMD.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed method automatically extracts domain-variant features from charge curves to transfer knowledge from the large-scale labeled full cycles to the unlabeled shallow cycles."
  - [section 2.2.3]: "The domain adaptation lossLMK-MMD is the squared distance of Fs and Ft embedded in the reproducible kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Convolutional neural network predictor with smoothness constraint maps extracted features to SOH while maintaining robustness against small perturbations.
- Mechanism: The CNN predictor uses convolutional and pooling layers to map features to SOH, while the smoothness constraint ensures the mapping relationship is uniformly continuous, preventing large output changes from small input perturbations.
- Core assumption: The degradation-related features contain sufficient information to predict SOH, and the smoothness constraint improves model generalization without losing predictive power.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed method achieves a root-mean-square error within 2% and outperforms other transfer learning methods for different SOC ranges."
  - [section 2.2.2]: "To improve the robustness and generalization of the model, we incorporate the smoothness constraint on the predictor"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input charge curve → Self-attention distillation feature extractor → MK-MMD domain alignment → CNN predictor with smoothness constraint → SOH output
- Critical path: Feature extraction (SAD) → Domain alignment (MK-MMD) → Prediction (CNN) → Optimization (overall loss)
- Design tradeoffs: SAD vs CNN-only extraction balances feature quality with computational complexity; MK-MMD vs single-kernel MMD balances alignment accuracy with training time; smoothness constraint vs prediction accuracy
- Failure signatures: High RMSE indicates feature extraction failure; poor cross-domain transfer indicates MK-MMD misalignment; overfitting indicates insufficient smoothness constraint
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Single-domain transfer from 0-100% to 0-60% SOC range to verify basic functionality
  2. Single-domain transfer from 0-100% to 20-80% SOC range to test domain adaptation
  3. Cross-domain transfer from CALCE to SNL datasets to test generalization across manufacturers

## Limitations

- The method relies heavily on charge curve information, potentially limiting applicability to scenarios where only discharge curves are available
- Computational complexity of self-attention mechanisms and multi-kernel MMD may pose challenges for real-time embedded implementations
- Performance under extreme temperature conditions (<0°C or >40°C) and very high discharge rates (>3C) remains unverified

- Domain adaptation robustness: **Medium** - Strong performance on tested conditions, but limited exploration of extreme operating scenarios
- Generalization across manufacturers: **Medium** - Validated on two manufacturers, but broader validation would strengthen claims
