# The Exploration of Knowledge-Preserving Prompts for Document Summarisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11719
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11719
- Reference count: 13
- Key outcome: Knowledge-preserving prompts via prefix-tuning improve ROUGE scores and factual consistency in document summarization

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a knowledge incorporation method for document summarization that leverages prefix-tuning with natural language prompts extracted by OpenIE. The approach adds key relations extracted from documents as prompts before the original text, then trains continuous prefix prompts to help the model recognize and utilize this structured information. Experiments on CNN/Daily Mail and XSum datasets demonstrate improvements in both ROUGE scores and factual consistency compared to baseline summarization methods.

## Method Summary
The method combines OpenIE-based relation extraction with prefix-tuning for document summarization. Natural language prompts containing key relations are extracted from source documents using OpenIE and prepended to the original text. A separate set of continuous prefix prompts is then trained to help the model better recognize and utilize this structured information. The approach aims to preserve factual knowledge from the original document while generating concise summaries, addressing the challenge of factual consistency in abstractive summarization.

## Key Results
- Improved ROUGE scores on CNN/Daily Mail and XSum benchmark datasets
- Generated summaries show better factual consistency with source document relations
- Prefix-tuning approach achieves efficiency benefits compared to full fine-tuning while maintaining performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by explicitly incorporating structured knowledge into the summarization process through knowledge-preserving prompts. By extracting key relations using OpenIE and adding them as natural language prompts, the model receives additional context about important relationships in the source document. The continuous prefix prompts are then trained to optimize how this information is processed and utilized during generation, helping the model maintain factual consistency while producing abstractive summaries.

## Foundational Learning
- **Prefix-tuning**: A parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that adds trainable prefix parameters to transformer attention layers; needed to efficiently incorporate knowledge without full model retraining
- **OpenIE relation extraction**: Extracts subject-predicate-object triples from text; needed to identify and structure key factual relationships in source documents
- **Factual consistency evaluation**: Methods to measure how well generated summaries preserve facts from source documents; needed to validate the effectiveness of knowledge preservation
- **ROUGE metrics**: Standard evaluation metrics for summarization that measure n-gram overlap between summaries and references; needed to benchmark performance against existing methods
- **Abstractive summarization**: Text generation approach that creates novel summaries rather than extracting sentences; needed as the target task for knowledge preservation
- **Continuous prompts**: Trainable prompt parameters that can be optimized end-to-end; needed to improve how the model processes and utilizes extracted knowledge

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: OpenIE extraction -> Prefix addition -> Prefix-tuning training -> Summarization generation

Critical path: Relation extraction (OpenIE) → Prompt construction → Prefix-tuning optimization → Summary generation

Design tradeoffs: The method balances efficiency (prefix-tuning vs. full fine-tuning) against performance, and explicit knowledge incorporation against potential noise from OpenIE extraction errors.

Failure signatures: Poor OpenIE extraction quality leading to noisy or incorrect prompts; insufficient prefix-tuning causing the model to ignore added knowledge; overfitting to training data that reduces generalization.

Three first experiments:
1. Evaluate prefix-tuning performance with and without knowledge-preserving prompts on CNN/Daily Mail dataset
2. Compare different relation extraction methods (OpenIE vs. semantic role labeling) for prompt construction
3. Test the impact of varying prefix lengths on summarization quality and factual consistency

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation of factual accuracy beyond ROUGE scores and manual consistency checks
- Potential noise propagation from OpenIE extraction errors to generated summaries
- Computational overhead of prefix-tuning not thoroughly analyzed relative to baselines
- Limited quantitative evidence for claimed improvements in factual consistency

## Confidence
- High confidence: The prefix-tuning approach successfully incorporates additional information into the summarization process
- Medium confidence: The method improves ROUGE scores on benchmark datasets
- Low confidence: The method consistently generates factually accurate summaries that preserve knowledge from the original document

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a comprehensive automated factuality evaluation using multiple metrics (FEQA, QAGS, FactCC) to quantify factual consistency improvements beyond ROUGE scores
2. Perform ablation studies comparing different relation extraction methods (OpenIE vs. SRL vs. semantic role labeling) to isolate the impact of extraction quality on summary performance
3. Measure and compare computational overhead including training time, memory usage, and inference latency against both full fine-tuning and baseline summarization approaches to provide a complete efficiency analysis
