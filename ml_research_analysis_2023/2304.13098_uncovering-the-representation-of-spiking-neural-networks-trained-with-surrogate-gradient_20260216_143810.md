# Uncovering the Representation of Spiking Neural Networks Trained with Surrogate Gradient

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13098
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13098
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper investigates whether spiking neural networks (SNNs) trained with surrogate gradient learn different representations from artificial neural networks (ANNs).

## Executive Summary
This study examines the representational properties of spiking neural networks trained with surrogate gradient methods compared to traditional artificial neural networks. Using centered kernel alignment (CKA) as a similarity metric, the authors analyze how network architecture, temporal dynamics, input data types, and training methods affect representation similarity. The research reveals that despite their different computational principles, SNNs trained with surrogate gradient generally develop representations similar to ANNs, particularly when residual connections are present. The study also finds that SNNs demonstrate enhanced robustness against adversarial attacks while maintaining comparable classification performance to ANNs.

## Method Summary
The study employs centered kernel alignment (CKA) to quantify representation similarity between SNNs and ANNs across various dimensions. Experiments systematically vary network width, depth, residual connections, temporal integration, and input data types (static vs event-based). The authors train matched SNN and ANN architectures on benchmark datasets, then compare their hidden layer representations using CKA scores. Additional analyses examine robustness to adversarial attacks by comparing feature representations under clean versus perturbed inputs. The temporal dimension is explored by analyzing representations across multiple time steps in SNNs, revealing how information evolves throughout the network.

## Key Results
- SNNs trained with surrogate gradient develop representations highly similar to ANNs, with similarity increasing in wider networks
- Residual connections are crucial for making SNN representations ANN-like, while spike-based computations provide limited additional representation power
- SNNs show higher robustness against adversarial attacks with greater similarity between clean and adversarial feature representations compared to ANNs
- On event-based datasets, SNNs may develop less similar representations to ANNs, suggesting domain-specific differences in representation learning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The similarity between SNN and ANN representations arises because surrogate gradient methods effectively bridge the computational gap between spiking and non-spiking networks. By providing differentiable approximations to the non-differentiable spiking nonlinearity, these methods enable SNNs to optimize similar feature representations as ANNs. The critical role of residual connections suggests they help stabilize temporal integration in SNNs, allowing them to maintain ANN-like representations across layers. The enhanced adversarial robustness likely stems from the temporal integration and spike-based computation introducing additional noise that disrupts adversarial perturbations.

## Foundational Learning

**Surrogate Gradient Methods**: Approximate gradients for the non-differentiable spiking nonlinearity during backpropagation. Needed to enable end-to-end training of SNNs through gradient-based optimization. Quick check: Verify gradient approximation accuracy across different spiking thresholds.

**Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)**: A similarity metric that measures representational alignment between neural network layers. Needed to quantify how similarly SNNs and ANNs encode information. Quick check: Confirm CKA values correlate with downstream task performance.

**Temporal Integration in SNNs**: The accumulation and processing of information across multiple time steps. Needed to understand how temporal dynamics affect representation formation. Quick check: Measure information retention across time steps in early network layers.

**Residual Connections**: Skip connections that bypass one or more layers to facilitate gradient flow. Needed to enable training of deeper networks and stabilize temporal computations. Quick check: Compare CKA similarity with and without residual connections.

**Adversarial Robustness**: The ability to maintain correct classifications under input perturbations designed to fool the network. Needed to evaluate security properties of different network architectures. Quick check: Test robustness across different attack magnitudes and methods.

## Limitations
- Focus on relatively shallow architectures may limit generalizability to deeper SNN designs
- CKA analysis captures linear relationships but may miss nuanced temporal or spike timing differences
- Small sample size of event-based datasets makes conclusions about domain-specific differences tentative
- Lack of comprehensive ablation studies to identify precise mechanisms behind adversarial robustness

## Confidence
- High: Residual connections increase ANN-like representations in SNNs
- High: General similarity between trained SNN and ANN representations
- Medium: Claims about enhanced adversarial robustness
- Medium: Temporal representation findings showing static early layers
- Low: Conclusions about SNNs being "less ANN-like" on event-based datasets
- Low: Generalizability across all SNN architectures and surrogate gradient methods
