# Class-Balancing Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00562
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00562
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Improves tail-class generation in class-imbalanced datasets using distribution adjustment regularizer

## Executive Summary
Class-Balancing Diffusion Models (CBDM) address the degradation of image quality and diversity in diffusion models when trained on class-imbalanced datasets. The method introduces a distribution adjustment regularizer during training that encourages the model to generate more balanced class distributions by adjusting the conditional transfer probability at each denoising step. CBDM adds a simple MSE-based loss that pulls the model's output toward randomly sampled target classes, improving tail-class generation without harming head-class performance. Experiments on CIFAR10/CIFAR100 and their long-tailed versions show that CBDM improves FID scores by up to 1.5 points and Recall by up to 0.08 compared to baseline DDPM, especially for tail classes.

## Method Summary
CBDM introduces a distribution adjustment regularizer that operates during the diffusion model's denoising process. At each step, the model's conditional transfer probability is modified to account for class balance by incorporating a target class distribution. The method adds an MSE-based loss term that pulls the model's predictions toward randomly sampled target classes from a balanced distribution. This regularizer is applied throughout the denoising trajectory, ensuring that the final generated samples reflect a more uniform class distribution. The approach is designed to be compatible with various diffusion model architectures and sampling methods, including DDIM.

## Key Results
- Improves FID scores by up to 1.5 points compared to baseline DDPM on CIFAR datasets
- Increases Recall by up to 0.08 for tail classes in imbalanced settings
- Maintains head-class performance while improving tail-class generation
- Compatible with DDIM sampling and data augmentation methods like ADA

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CBDM works by modifying the diffusion model's training objective to explicitly account for class distribution imbalance. During each denoising step, the model receives guidance toward generating samples from a balanced class distribution through the distribution adjustment regularizer. This regularizer adds an MSE loss between the model's current class probability predictions and target class distributions sampled from a uniform distribution. By applying this adjustment throughout the denoising process, the model learns to generate more balanced outputs without sacrificing overall generation quality.

## Foundational Learning
- Diffusion models: Understanding the denoising process and how noise is gradually removed from random noise to generate samples. Why needed: CBDM modifies the training objective within this denoising framework.
- Class imbalance in generative models: Recognizing how imbalanced training data affects generation quality and diversity. Why needed: CBDM specifically addresses this challenge.
- Conditional probability in diffusion models: Understanding how class conditioning works during generation. Why needed: CBDM modifies conditional transfer probabilities to achieve balance.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input noise -> DDPM backbone -> Denoising steps -> Distribution adjustment regularizer -> Output image

Critical path: The distribution adjustment regularizer is applied at each denoising step, making it central to the CBDM architecture. The regularizer computes MSE loss between current predictions and target class distributions, which are randomly sampled to encourage balance.

Design tradeoffs: CBDM adds computational overhead through the additional MSE loss term, but claims to maintain training efficiency. The random sampling approach for target classes is simple but may not always align with optimal balance targets.

Failure signatures: If the distribution adjustment regularizer is too strong, it may cause mode collapse or reduce overall generation quality. If too weak, it may fail to adequately address class imbalance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the limited evaluation on small-scale datasets suggests uncertainty about scalability to real-world imbalanced datasets.

## Limitations
- Experiments are conducted only on small-scale CIFAR datasets rather than large-scale real-world imbalanced datasets
- Limited validation of compatibility claims with various diffusion backbones beyond DDPM and DDIM
- Additional computational overhead from the distribution adjustment regularizer is mentioned but not thoroughly analyzed
- Effect size of improvements appears modest (up to 1.5 FID points)

## Confidence
- High confidence: The mathematical formulation of the distribution adjustment regularizer is sound and theoretically grounded
- Medium confidence: The experimental results on CIFAR datasets are reproducible and demonstrate measurable improvements
- Medium confidence: The compatibility claims with various diffusion backbones are plausible but under-validated
- Low confidence: Scalability to large-scale real-world datasets remains unproven
