# Instruction Tuning with GPT-4

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03277
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277
- Reference count: 7
- This paper introduces the first attempt to use GPT-4 to generate instruction-following data for fine-tuning large language models (LLMs).

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first attempt to use GPT-4 to generate instruction-following data for fine-tuning large language models. The authors create a 52K English and Chinese instruction-following dataset using GPT-4 as a teacher for self-instruct tuning. They also collect feedback and comparison data from GPT-4 to enable comprehensive evaluation and reward model training. The instruction-tuned LLaMA models based on the GPT-4-generated data demonstrate superior zero-shot performance on new tasks compared to models fine-tuned with data from previous state-of-the-art models. Human evaluation based on alignment criteria shows that the GPT-4 instruction-tuned LLaMA performs similarly to the original GPT-4.

## Method Summary
The paper uses GPT-4 to generate instruction-following data through a self-instruct approach, creating 52K English and Chinese instruction-output pairs. These pairs are used to fine-tune LLaMA models using supervised fine-tuning. The authors also collect comparison data from GPT-4 to train reward models. The fine-tuning process involves translating English instructions to Chinese and generating responses in both languages. The trained models are evaluated through human assessment on alignment criteria (helpfulness, honesty, harmlessness) and automatic evaluation using GPT-4 feedback and ROUGE-L scoring.

## Key Results
- GPT-4-generated instruction-following data leads to superior zero-shot performance compared to data from previous state-of-the-art models
- Instruction-tuned LLaMA models perform similarly to GPT-4 on human evaluation of alignment criteria
- GPT-4 evaluation results show consistent rankings across different models and tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-4 generates higher-quality instruction-following data than previous models
- Mechanism: GPT-4's superior language understanding and generation capabilities allow it to create more natural, diverse, and contextually appropriate instruction-output pairs
- Core assumption: GPT-4's larger model size and training data give it better generalization and instruction-following abilities than GPT-3.5

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning on GPT-4 generated data transfers the instruction-following capability to smaller models
- Mechanism: Supervised fine-tuning on high-quality instruction-output pairs teaches smaller models to follow instructions in a similar way to GPT-4
- Core assumption: The instruction-following patterns learned by GPT-4 are transferable to smaller models through supervised fine-tuning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "instruction-tuned LLaMA models based on the GPT-4-generated data demonstrate superior zero-shot performance on new tasks compared to models fine-tuned with data from previous state-of-the-art models"

  - [section 4.2] Human evaluation shows LLaMA-GPT4 performs similarly to GPT-4 on HHH alignment criteria

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data generation (GPT-4) -> Model training (Supervised fine-tuning on LLaMA) -> Evaluation (Human evaluation on HHH criteria, automatic evaluation with GPT-4 feedback, ROUGE-L scoring) -> Reward model training (using GPT-4 comparison data)

- Critical path: Data generation → Model training → Evaluation → (Optional) Reward model training → Model deployment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Data quality vs. quantity: GPT-4 generates high-quality data but in limited quantities (52K samples) compared to potential larger datasets from other sources
  - Model size vs. performance: The 7B LLaMA model shows good performance but there's a gap compared to larger commercial models like GPT-4
  - Automatic vs. human evaluation: GPT-4 evaluation is faster and cheaper but may not fully capture human preferences

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on unseen instructions despite good training performance (overfitting to generated data)
  - Reward models that don't improve alignment or even make it worse
  - Human evaluations that significantly disagree with GPT-4 evaluations

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune a small LLaMA model on a subset of the GPT-4 generated data and evaluate on a held-out test set to verify basic functionality
  2. Compare the performance of models fine-tuned on GPT-4 vs GPT-3.5 generated data on the same instruction set
  3. Train a reward model using the GPT-4 comparison data and use it to rank responses from different models, checking if rankings align with human preferences

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of GPT-4-generated instruction-tuning data scale with dataset size?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that Vicuna uses around 700K instruction-following samples compared to their 52K dataset, suggesting potential for scaling.
- Why unresolved: The authors only experimented with 52K samples and acknowledged the need to collect more data and train larger models for higher performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing performance improvements as the dataset size increases from 52K to 700K or beyond, using the same GPT-4 generation approach.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between English and Chinese instruction-following data for multilingual models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors created both English and Chinese instruction-following data using GPT-4, but didn't explore the optimal ratio for multilingual models.
- Why unresolved: The paper only created separate models for each language and studied cross-language generalization, but didn't investigate the best mix of data for truly multilingual instruction-following.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training models on various ratios of English to Chinese data (e.g., 90/10, 70/30, 50/50) and evaluating performance on both languages to find the optimal balance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does GPT-4's instruction-following ability compare to other large language models beyond GPT-3.5?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compared GPT-4-generated data to GPT-3.5 and found GPT-4 superior, but didn't benchmark against other contemporary models.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation only compared against GPT-3.5, OPT-IML, and commercial models like GPT-4 and ChatGPT, leaving out other open-source and proprietary models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive benchmarking of GPT-4 instruction-following ability against a wider range of models including BLOOM, OPT, LLaMA, and other instruction-tuned variants.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What are the limitations of using GPT-4 as a teacher for self-instruct tuning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors successfully used GPT-4 for instruction generation but didn't explore potential limitations or failure cases of this approach.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating the effectiveness of GPT-4 as a teacher but doesn't investigate scenarios where this approach might fail or produce suboptimal results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies and analyses of instruction-following tasks where GPT-4-generated data leads to poor model performance, identifying patterns or characteristics of such failure cases.

### Open Question 5
- Question: How transferable is the GPT-4 instruction-tuning approach to other large language models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors successfully applied the approach to LLaMA models but didn't explore its applicability to other LLM architectures.
- Why unresolved: The experiments were limited to LLaMA models, leaving open the question of how well this approach would work with different model architectures like OPT, BLOOM, or other transformer-based models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the GPT-4 instruction-tuning methodology to various LLM architectures and comparing the resulting performance and generalization capabilities across models.

## Limitations
- Lack of direct human evaluation comparison between GPT-4-generated data and previous state-of-the-art models' generated data
- Relatively small dataset size (52K samples) compared to other instruction tuning work
- Less detailed process for Chinese instruction-following data generation compared to English

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- GPT-4 can generate instruction-following data that, when used for fine-tuning, produces models with superior zero-shot performance compared to models fine-tuned on previous state-of-the-art generated data
- The fine-tuning methodology (supervised fine-tuning on LLaMA with GPT-4 generated data) is technically sound and reproducible
- GPT-4 can effectively evaluate and rank model responses for reward model training purposes

**Medium Confidence:**
- The GPT-4 instruction-tuned LLaMA models perform similarly to GPT-4 itself on alignment criteria, as this is based on human evaluation which may have subjective variability
- The claimed diversity improvements in GPT-4 generated data are real but the practical significance for downstream performance is not fully established
- The transfer of instruction-following capabilities from GPT-4 to smaller models is effective, though the gap to larger commercial models remains significant

**Low Confidence:**
- The specific mechanism by which GPT-4 generates higher-quality data than previous models (beyond general model capability claims)
- The long-term stability and generalization of models trained on GPT-4 generated data to truly unseen instruction distributions
- The effectiveness of GPT-4-based reward models compared to human-preference-based reward models

## Next Checks
1. **Direct data quality comparison**: Conduct human evaluations comparing instruction-output pairs generated by GPT-4 versus GPT-3.5 or other previous models across multiple dimensions (naturalness, diversity, instruction-following accuracy) to validate the claimed quality improvements.

2. **Scaling analysis**: Fine-tune LLaMA models of different sizes (3B, 13B, 33B) on the same GPT-4 generated dataset to determine if the performance gains scale predictably with model size and identify any saturation points.

3. **Out-of-distribution generalization**: Test the instruction-tuned models on instruction distributions that are intentionally different from the GPT-4 generated training data (e.g., more complex, longer instructions, different domains) to assess robustness and identify potential overfitting to the generated distribution.
