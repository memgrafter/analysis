# ChatGPT Participates in a Computer Science Exam

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09461
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09461
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: ChatGPT narrowly passes an undergraduate CS algorithms exam with 20.5/40 points, performing worse than average students (24 points)

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates ChatGPT's performance on an undergraduate computer science exam about algorithms and data structures by having it answer exam questions in 19 conversations, then hand-copying the responses for blind grading alongside 200 student exams. ChatGPT narrowly passes with 20.5 out of 40 points, performing worse than the average student score of 24 points. The model shows strength in text-based tasks like proofs and pseudo-code but struggles significantly with structured outputs like graphs, highlighting both its capabilities and limitations in technical domains.

## Method Summary
The authors posed entire exam questions to ChatGPT in 19 standardized conversations, then hand-copied the model's responses onto exam sheets. These sheets were graded blindly by teaching assistants alongside 200 student exams, with scores out of 40 points determining pass/fail (20 points minimum). The exam covered algorithms and data structures topics, and the grading was designed to be objective to minimize subjectivity in evaluation.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT scores 20.5/40 points, narrowly passing but performing below the average student score of 24 points
- GPT-4 would have achieved approximately 17% more points than GPT-3.5, reaching average student performance
- ChatGPT excels at text-based tasks (proofs, pseudo-code) but fails on structured visual outputs (graphs, diagrams)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT leverages training on well-documented algorithms and data structures found online
- Mechanism: The model retrieves and recombines patterns from its training corpus to produce valid proofs, pseudo-code, and text-based explanations
- Core assumption: The exam content is well-represented in publicly available educational materials
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the questions in our exam are structurally similar to those of other exams, solved homework problems, and teaching materials that can be found online"
  - [section 3.1] "Similar exams are being written all around the world, and lots of information about the topics covered in the exam is available on the internet"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ChatGPT excels at text-based tasks but struggles with structured visual outputs
- Mechanism: The model's architecture is optimized for language generation, not for creating valid diagrams or graphs
- Core assumption: Tasks requiring structured visual output (graphs, diagrams) are fundamentally different from text generation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "the model struggled on all exercises where that involved structured output that was not pseudo-code"
  - [section 4] "it stands out that the model hardly managed to solve any of the questions that involved drawing graphs"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 on this exam, suggesting improved reasoning capabilities
- Mechanism: Larger model size and improved training allows for better problem-solving on complex CS concepts
- Core assumption: Model performance scales with size and training improvements
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that GPT-4 would have obtained about 17% more exam points than GPT-3.5, reaching the performance of the average student"

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would ChatGPT perform on computer science exams if the topics were not well-documented online?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that "Many of the topics covered in the exam are extensively documented on the internet" and caution that ChatGPT may have seen similar exercises during training.
- Why unresolved: The experiment was conducted on an exam where the topics are well-documented online, so the performance may not reflect ChatGPT's true understanding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting the same experiment on an exam covering less common or niche computer science topics that are not well-documented online.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would ChatGPT's performance change if it was given access to a calculator or other tools?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that "an emergent literature aims to enhance the capabilities of language models, for example by giving them access to a numerical calculator."
- Why unresolved: The experiment was conducted without providing ChatGPT any additional tools beyond the exam questions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting the same experiment while allowing ChatGPT access to a calculator or other relevant tools.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would ChatGPT perform on computer science exams if the questions were posed in a more structured or step-by-step manner?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that they posed the questions in a "simple standardized format" and did not use "chain of thought reasoning" techniques that might enhance performance.
- Why unresolved: The experiment used a simple prompting strategy, so it is unclear how more structured prompts might impact performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting the same experiment while using more structured or step-by-step prompting techniques.

## Limitations

- The study's findings may be influenced by ChatGPT's training on materials similar to the exam questions, making it unclear whether poor performance reflects true limitations or lack of exposure to specific problem patterns
- The small sample size of 19 model interactions limits generalizability of findings to broader contexts
- Human grading introduces potential subjectivity and variability in evaluation despite blind grading procedures

## Confidence

**High Confidence** (Well-supported by evidence):
- ChatGPT's superior performance on text-based tasks (proofs, pseudo-code) compared to structured visual outputs (graphs)
- ChatGPT's overall performance being worse than average student performance (20.5 vs 24 points)
- GPT-4's projected improvement over GPT-3.5 (17% more points)

**Medium Confidence** (Reasonable but with caveats):
- The claim that ChatGPT's training on well-documented algorithms explains its selective success
- The conclusion that ChatGPT does not truly "understand" computer science

**Low Confidence** (Limited evidence):
- The generalizability of these findings to other CS topics beyond algorithms and data structures
- The applicability of these results to newer model versions beyond GPT-3.5

## Next Checks

1. **Training data audit**: Conduct a systematic investigation to determine whether the specific exam problems or highly similar problems appear in ChatGPT's training corpus by searching for exact problems or near-identical variants in publicly available educational materials.

2. **Cross-topic validation**: Replicate the experiment using CS exam questions from topics less likely to be well-documented online (e.g., emerging technologies, specialized domains) to determine if performance patterns hold across different content areas.

3. **Automated vs. human grading comparison**: Have ChatGPT's responses graded both by human teaching assistants and by automated assessment tools to evaluate consistency and identify potential grading biases in the evaluation process.
