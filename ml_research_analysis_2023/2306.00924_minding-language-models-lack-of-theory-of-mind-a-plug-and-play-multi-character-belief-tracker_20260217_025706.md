# Minding Language Models' (Lack of) Theory of Mind: A Plug-and-Play Multi-Character Belief Tracker

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.00924
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00924
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Primary result**: Introduces SYMBOLIC TOM, a zero-shot plug-and-play algorithm that improves language models' theory of mind performance to 92% accuracy across question types.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of improving language models' theory of mind (ToM) capabilities for multi-character reading comprehension tasks. The authors introduce SYMBOLIC TOM, a zero-shot approach that tracks characters' beliefs and their estimations of others' beliefs through explicit symbolic representations. By maintaining belief graphs for each character and leveraging off-the-shelf NLP tools, the method significantly enhances off-the-shelf neural networks' ToM performance without requiring any supervised training. Empirical results on the ToMi benchmark demonstrate dramatic improvements over baseline models, achieving up to 92% accuracy while maintaining robust out-of-distribution performance.

## Method Summary
SYMBOLIC TOM is a plug-and-play algorithm that enhances language models' theory of mind by maintaining explicit symbolic belief graphs for each character in a story. The method processes narratives sentence by sentence, using off-the-shelf NLP tools (NLI for contradiction detection, OpenIE for relationship extraction, and language models for state determination) to update belief graphs that track what each character knows and believes about the world. When answering questions, the approach filters the story context based on the relevant belief graph and uses a language model to generate answers. The zero-shot nature of the method eliminates the need for training while providing interpretable reasoning through symbolic representations.

## Key Results
- SYMBOLIC TOM achieves up to 92% accuracy across all question types on the ToMi benchmark
- The approach demonstrates superior out-of-distribution performance compared to supervised baselines
- Large language models show particular benefit from SYMBOLIC TOM's belief tracking, with accuracy improvements of up to 28% on out-of-distribution tests
- The method reveals spurious patterns in existing ToM benchmarks, highlighting the importance of out-of-distribution evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SYMBOLIC TOM improves language models' theory of mind by using explicit symbolic belief graphs that represent each character's knowledge and beliefs about the world.
- Mechanism: The approach maintains separate belief graphs for each character, tracking what each character believes about the world and what they think others believe. These graphs are updated as the story progresses, allowing the model to reason about different perspectives without needing to train or fine-tune.
- Core assumption: Language models can be guided to reason about theory of mind tasks by providing them with structured belief representations as context, even without explicit training on theory of mind.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SYMBOLIC TOM, a plug-and-play approach to reason about the belief states of multiple characters in reading comprehension tasks via explicit symbolic representation."
  - [section 3.1] "Our goal is to automatically answer reading comprehension questions given a story involving multiple characters, without requiring any supervised training or fine-tuning on this task."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The method leverages off-the-shelf models to construct and update belief graphs, avoiding the need for training while still capturing complex belief dynamics.
- Mechanism: Uses NLI models to detect contradictions and OpenIE for extracting relationships, combined with a language model to determine the resulting state after actions. This allows dynamic belief tracking without supervision.
- Core assumption: Off-the-shelf NLP tools (NLI, OpenIE, language models) are sufficiently accurate to track belief states and world changes in simple narratives.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2.1] "We represent each Bp1...pk as a graph... We construct the graphs by iterating through a story one sentence at a time, and adding both nodes and edges to the graph"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SYMBOLIC TOM's zero-shot nature and symbolic reasoning make it more robust to out-of-distribution inputs than supervised approaches.
- Mechanism: By not requiring training on the target dataset, the method avoids overfitting to spurious patterns. The symbolic reasoning approach provides interpretability and can generalize to novel story structures.
- Core assumption: The symbolic belief representation and zero-shot approach generalize better to distribution shifts than models trained on limited template-based data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Story sentences -> Global context graph + Belief graphs -> Question processing -> Filtered context -> Language model -> Answer

- Critical path:
  1. Process story sentences sequentially to build global context graph
  2. For each sentence, detect witnesses and update relevant belief graphs
  3. When answering a question, identify the target belief graph based on question semantics
  4. Filter story context to include only relevant sentences from the target belief graph
  5. Generate answer using language model with filtered context

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Symbolic vs neural: Uses symbolic graphs for explicit reasoning but relies on neural models for parsing and generation
  - Zero-shot vs fine-tuning: Avoids training overhead but may sacrifice some accuracy on in-distribution data
  - Memory vs reasoning depth: Exponential growth in belief graphs with reasoning depth (m) vs need for deeper reasoning

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on out-of-distribution inputs (indicates overfitting in supervised approaches)
  - Failure to detect correct witnesses (indicates issues with witness detection logic)
  - Incorrect belief graph updates (indicates problems with NLI or OpenIE components)
  - Language model ignoring belief graph context (indicates prompt engineering issues)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify basic belief tracking on simple stories with one character moving objects
  2. Test second-order reasoning by asking what one character thinks another character believes
  3. Evaluate robustness by modifying story structures while keeping the same primitive actions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the maximum order of theory of mind reasoning that large language models can reliably achieve using the SYMBOLIC TOM approach?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper states "Although existing NLP datasets only test up to second-order reasoning (i.e., m ≤ 2), SYMBOLIC TOM is designed to work at any depth."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests up to second-order reasoning due to limitations in existing datasets, but mentions that SYMBOLIC TOM can theoretically handle any depth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Creating and testing on datasets that require third-order or higher theory of mind reasoning to see if SYMBOLIC TOM maintains its effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of SYMBOLIC TOM scale with increasing maximum reasoning depth (m)?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper mentions "Memory requirements grow exponentially with m, the maximum order of theory of mind considered."
- Why unresolved: The paper only experiments with m = 2 due to practical constraints, but doesn't explore how performance changes with higher values of m.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing SYMBOLIC TOM with increasing values of m on appropriate datasets and measuring performance and computational costs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is SYMBOLIC TOM to variations in story structure beyond those tested in the paper?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper tests robustness to some story structure modifications but states "we would need to obtain new theory of mind datasets with significantly more types of interactions and physical commonsense in the stories."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests a limited set of story structure modifications and acknowledges the need for more diverse datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing SYMBOLIC TOM on a wide variety of story structures, including those with complex interactions, physical commonsense, and non-chronological narratives.

## Limitations

- The method's performance is bounded by the quality of off-the-shelf NLP components (NLI, OpenIE, language models) used for belief tracking
- Exponential growth in belief graphs with reasoning depth (m) creates scalability challenges for deeper theory of mind reasoning
- The approach's effectiveness on complex real-world narratives with diverse structures remains untested beyond the ToMi benchmark
