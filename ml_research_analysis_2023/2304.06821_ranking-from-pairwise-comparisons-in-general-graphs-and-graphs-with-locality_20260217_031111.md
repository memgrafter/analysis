# Ranking from Pairwise Comparisons in General Graphs and Graphs with Locality

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06821
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06821
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: Establishes tight entrywise error bounds for maximum likelihood estimation in the Bradley-Terry-Luce model across general graphs and graphs with locality

## Executive Summary
This technical report studies the problem of ranking from pairwise comparisons in the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) model, focusing on score estimation. The work establishes tight estimation error bounds for maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) in both general graphs and graphs with locality, such as 1D/2D grids. The key insight is a novel connection between statistical estimation and iterative optimization through preconditioned gradient descent, which enables matching the Cramér-Rao lower bound. The analysis identifies effective resistances as the relevant parameter governing estimation accuracy in general graphs. Additionally, the report explores divide-and-conquer algorithms that can provably achieve similar guarantees even in the sparsest regime, while offering computational advantages.

## Method Summary
The work employs maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) for score estimation in the Bradley-Terry-Luce (BTL) model, leveraging a connection between statistical estimation and iterative optimization via preconditioned gradient descent. For graphs with locality, the analysis identifies conditions under which comparing scores between far-apart items is nearly as easy as comparing nearby items, allowing MLE to achieve similar guarantees even in sparse regimes. Divide-and-conquer algorithms are proposed that provably match the Cramér-Rao lower bound while offering computational advantages. The theoretical results are validated through numerical experiments on the proposed algorithms.

## Key Results
- MLE achieves entrywise estimation error matching the Cramér-Rao lower bound in general graphs, characterized by effective resistances
- For graphs with locality (e.g., 1D/2D grids), MLE achieves similar guarantees even in the sparsest regime, provided the radius satisfies certain conditions
- Divide-and-conquer algorithms provably achieve similar guarantees while enjoying computational advantages
- The analysis identifies conditions under which locality does not hurt estimation accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The work establishes a novel connection between statistical estimation and iterative optimization through preconditioned gradient descent. This connection allows the analysis to leverage optimization techniques to derive tight statistical bounds. For graphs with locality, the key insight is that under certain conditions on the graph radius, comparing scores between distant items is nearly as easy as comparing nearby items, preventing locality from degrading estimation accuracy.
