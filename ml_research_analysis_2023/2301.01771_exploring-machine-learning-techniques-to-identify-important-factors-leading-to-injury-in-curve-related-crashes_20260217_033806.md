# Exploring Machine Learning Techniques to Identify Important Factors Leading to Injury in Curve Related Crashes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01771
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01771
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: C5.0 decision tree achieved 71.76% accuracy in predicting injury outcomes in curve-related crashes

## Executive Summary
This study investigates pre-crash events' impact on injury severity in curve-related crashes using CRSS data from NHTSA. The research develops a machine learning model using the C5.0 decision tree algorithm, which achieved 71.76% accuracy. The model identifies eight key predictors: extent of damage, critical pre-crash event, pre-impact location, trafficway description, roadway surface condition, month of crash, first harmful event, and roadway grade. Environmental and pre-crash events leading to cautious driving significantly reduce injury risk, while departing the roadway was found to be a critical factor in collisions with motor vehicles.

## Method Summary
The study uses CRSS data from NHTSA, filtering for 740 curve-related crashes after data preparation. Multiple machine learning algorithms are tested including Random Forest, C5.0, CHAID, Bayesian Network, Neural Network, C&R Tree, Quest, and Decision List. Random Forest and SHAP values identify the most effective variables, with C5.0 selected as the final model based on highest accuracy (71.76%). The eight key predictors are used to develop the injury prediction model, with 10-fold cross-validation employed for accuracy estimation.

## Key Results
- C5.0 decision tree achieved 71.76% accuracy, the highest among tested algorithms
- Eight key predictors identified: extent of damage, critical pre-crash event, pre-impact location, trafficway description, roadway surface condition, month of crash, first harmful event, and roadway grade
- Environmental and pre-crash events leading to cautious driving significantly reduce injury risk
- Departing the roadway is a critical factor in collisions with motor vehicles in curve-related crashes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
SHAP values are used to rank variable importance before training final C5.0 model. Random forest is run first to generate variable importance, then SHAP values provide local interpretability and feature selection based on ranking rather than embedded importance. Core assumption is that SHAP ranking aligns with model performance and avoids bias from random forest's embedded selection.

### Mechanism 2
C5.0 decision tree chosen over other ML methods due to highest accuracy (71.76%) and handling of multicollinearity. C5.0's recursive partitioning with entropy-based splitting avoids parametric assumptions and is robust to correlated predictors. Core assumption is that accuracy gain from C5.0 outweighs interpretability loss compared to logistic regression.

### Mechanism 3
Excluding vehicles not negotiating curves ensures focus on curve-related pre-crash events. Data filtering removes incomplete cases and non-curve scenarios, concentrating analysis on relevant pre-crash dynamics. Core assumption is that pre-crash events differ meaningfully between curve and non-curve contexts.

  - Why needed here: SHAP values provide both global and local interpretability, critical for explaining injury risk factors
  - Quick check question: What's the difference between SHAP-based and tree-based feature importance in random forest?

  - Why needed here: 10-fold cross-validation estimates accuracy without overfitting, especially important with limited 740-case sample

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data ingestion -> Filtering (curve-only, complete cases) -> Random forest -> SHAP ranking -> C5.0 training -> Model evaluation -> Interpretation
- Critical path: Data preparation -> SHAP-based feature selection -> C5.0 model training -> Accuracy validation -> Result interpretation
- Design tradeoffs: Accuracy (C5.0 71.76%) vs interpretability (complex tree structure); Sample size (740 cases) vs comprehensive variable coverage; Parametric vs non-parametric modeling for crash data
- Failure signatures: Low accuracy (<60%) indicates poor feature selection or model misfit; Highly imbalanced tree nodes suggests overfitting to specific crash types; Contradictory SHAP and C5.0 importance rankings indicates feature correlation issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run random forest with all variables, check SHAP ranking stability across multiple runs
  2. Train C5.0 with top 10 SHAP-ranked variables, compare accuracy to full-variable model
  3. Validate C5.0 predictions on held-out test set, examine terminal node distributions for injury severity patterns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do pre-crash events specifically contribute to injury severity in curve-related crashes compared to straight road crashes? The study focuses on pre-crash events in curve-related crashes but does not directly compare these to straight road crashes.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of driver age and experience on the effectiveness of pre-crash events in reducing injury severity? The study mentions driver behavior factors but does not delve into the specific impact of driver demographics like age and experience.

### Open Question 3
How do different weather conditions interact with pre-crash events to influence injury severity in curve-related crashes? The study mentions the month of the crash as a factor but does not explore the interaction between weather conditions and pre-crash events.

### Open Question 4
What role does road design play in mitigating the impact of pre-crash events on injury severity? The study acknowledges road design as a factor but does not specifically investigate its role in mitigating pre-crash event impacts.

## Limitations
- Small sample size of 740 curve-related crashes after data filtering may limit generalizability
- Exact variable definitions and coding schemes used in CRSS data are not fully specified
- Hyperparameter tuning details for C5.0 algorithm are not provided, making exact replication challenging

## Confidence
- High confidence: Overall methodology of using machine learning for crash analysis and C5.0 selection based on highest accuracy
- Medium confidence: Specific importance ranking of variables using SHAP values, as stability across different runs is not verified
- Medium confidence: Interpretation of pre-crash events' impact on injury severity, given limited sample size
