# From Adversarial Arms Race to Model-centric Evaluation: Motivating a Unified Automatic Robustness Evaluation Framework

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18503
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18503
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper presents a unified automatic robustness evaluation framework for NLP models using adversarial attacks that addresses comprehensive evaluation, practical protocol, and reliable filtering through perturbation degree-based methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RobTest, a unified automatic robustness evaluation framework for NLP models that addresses the limitations of single-attacker evaluations. The framework defines eight robustness dimensions based on model capabilities, employs multiple attack methods across these dimensions, and introduces perturbation degree-based filtering to reliably assess model robustness. By providing both average and worst performance estimation metrics, RobTest offers a comprehensive view of model vulnerabilities while maintaining practical usability through clear instructions and automated reporting.

## Method Summary
The framework implements a multi-dimensional robustness evaluation approach by defining eight robustness dimensions (typo, glyph, phonetic at char-level; synonym, contextual, inflection at word-level; syntax, distraction at sentence-level). It employs rule-based, score-based, and gradient-based adversarial attacks to generate samples at various perturbation degrees, then filters invalid samples using a perturbation degree-based method. The framework computes both average and worst performance estimation metrics and generates comprehensive reports comparing models across all dimensions. The implementation, RobTest, provides four core features: adversarial attack methods, robustness report generation, user instructions, and adversarial data augmentation.

## Key Results
- Models exhibit different capacities across all eight robustness dimensions, evidenced by substantially different robustness scores
- The perturbation degree-based filtering method better aligns with human annotation than previous heuristic rules
- RoBERTa-large consistently outperforms RoBERTa-base across all dimensions, with larger robustness gaps observed in word-level and sentence-level transformations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Model-centric evaluation captures a more comprehensive view of model robustness than single-attacker evaluation.
- Mechanism: By defining eight robustness dimensions based on model capabilities and employing multiple attack methods across these dimensions, the framework reveals different aspects of model performance that single methods miss.
- Core assumption: Different attack methods expose different vulnerabilities in models, and these vulnerabilities correlate with specific model capability weaknesses.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The framework addresses three core challenges: (1) comprehensive evaluation by defining eight robustness dimensions based on model capabilities"
  - [section]: "We observe that models exhibit different capacities across all robustness dimensions, evidenced by substantially different robustness scores"
  - [corpus]: Corpus evidence shows related work on ensemble adversarial robustness evaluation, suggesting the field recognizes the value of multi-dimensional evaluation approaches
- Break condition: If certain robustness dimensions are irrelevant to specific model architectures or tasks, or if the perturbation degree filtering method fails to properly distinguish valid from invalid samples

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Perturbation degree-based filtering of adversarial samples provides more reliable robustness evaluation than heuristic-based methods.
- Mechanism: Samples with lower perturbation degrees are more likely to be valid (preserving original meaning while maintaining the original label), while higher perturbation degrees often produce invalid samples. This allows for more accurate measurement of true model robustness.
- Core assumption: There is an inverse correlation between perturbation degree and sample validity, and this relationship is consistent across different attack methods and transformation types.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We hold a basic intuition that there is an inverse correlation between the perturbation degree and the validity of adversarial samples"
  - [section]: "Our method based on the perturbation degree better aligns with the ideal trend, while previous filtering methods show inconsistent trends"
  - [corpus]: Weak corpus evidence - only 5 related papers found, suggesting limited research on this specific filtering approach
- Break condition: If the perturbation degree validity relationship breaks down for certain types of transformations or if human annotation reveals that some high-degree perturbations are actually valid

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Employing both average and worst performance estimation metrics provides a more complete picture of model robustness than worst performance alone.
- Mechanism: Worst performance estimation reveals the minimum robustness level (how models perform under attack), while average performance estimation shows typical performance across all potential adversarial samples. Together they provide complementary information about model capabilities.
- Core assumption: The distribution of adversarial sample success rates is not uniform, and both extreme and typical cases provide valuable information about model robustness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "In our framework, we introduce the average performance estimation for a more comprehensive robustness evaluation"
  - [section]: "The average performance estimation is in complementary to the worst performance estimation, showing the models' average success rates"
  - [corpus]: Weak corpus evidence - related papers focus more on adaptive attacks and ensemble defenses rather than metric selection
- Break condition: If the average performance metric becomes uninformative due to skewed distributions or if worst performance alone proves sufficient for practical decision-making

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Robustness dimension definition (8 dimensions across 3 transformation levels) -> Attack algorithms specification (rule-based, score-based, gradient-based) -> Evaluation protocol establishment (settings and metrics) -> Invalid sample filtering implementation (perturbation degree-based) -> Report generation -> Model comparison
- Critical path: Define dimensions → Specify attack algorithms → Establish evaluation protocol → Implement filtering → Generate reports → Compare models
- Design tradeoffs: Comprehensive multi-dimensional evaluation vs computational cost, perturbation degree filtering vs potential exclusion of valid samples, average vs worst performance metrics
- Failure signatures: Inconsistent robustness scores across similar models, filtering producing too few valid samples, reports showing unrealistic performance patterns

  3. Test perturbation degree filtering by generating samples at multiple degrees and manually validating a sample to confirm the filtering correlation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How can we develop more effective and reliable methods for filtering invalid adversarial samples beyond the perturbation degree-based approach?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenge of filtering invalid adversarial samples and proposes using perturbation degree as a heuristic, but acknowledges it's an approximate suboptimal solution
  - Why unresolved: The perturbation degree approach, while effective, is heuristic-based and may not capture all nuances of sample validity across different attack methods and dimensions
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies testing multiple filtering approaches (including human annotation, grammatical correctness, semantic similarity, and novel methods) on large-scale datasets across different attack methods

- What is the optimal balance between average and worst performance estimation metrics for robustness evaluation in different real-world scenarios?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces both average and worst performance estimation metrics but notes they are complementary without specifying which is more appropriate for different risk scenarios
  - Why unresolved: Different applications have varying risk tolerances, but the paper doesn't provide guidance on when to prioritize each metric or how to weight them
  - What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies across diverse application domains showing how different weightings of average vs worst performance correlate with actual deployment success and failure rates

- How can we extend the model-centric evaluation framework to effectively incorporate task-specific robustness dimensions while maintaining its unified structure?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly notes that task-specific robustness dimensions are essential but left for future work
  - Why unresolved: While the paper establishes a general framework, it doesn't address how to systematically integrate domain-specific vulnerabilities without compromising the framework's generality
  - What evidence would resolve it: A methodology for identifying, validating, and integrating task-specific dimensions into the existing framework, along with case studies demonstrating successful application across multiple domains

## Limitations
- The framework's effectiveness relies heavily on the assumption that perturbation degree correlates with sample validity across all transformation types, which may not hold uniformly
- The framework requires significant computational resources for comprehensive evaluation across eight dimensions and multiple perturbation degrees, potentially limiting practical adoption
- The perturbation degree-based filtering method, while effective, is heuristic-based and may exclude some valid samples or include some invalid ones

## Confidence
- High confidence: The framework's multi-dimensional evaluation approach and the basic architecture of RobTest are well-supported by empirical results showing different robustness profiles across dimensions
- Medium confidence: The perturbation degree filtering method shows promising alignment with human annotation trends, but the sample size and diversity of human validation are not fully specified
- Medium confidence: The claim that average performance estimation provides complementary information to worst performance estimation is theoretically sound, but the practical significance of this distinction requires further validation
