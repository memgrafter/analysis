# Composition and Deformance: Measuring Imageability with a Text-to-Image Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.03168
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03168
- **Reference count**: 7
- **Primary result**: Proposes computational methods for measuring text imageability using DALL·E mini, showing high correlation with human judgments and sensitivity to compositional changes.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel computational approach to measuring the imageability of text using a text-to-image model. The authors propose two methods - average CLIP score and average pairwise image embedding cosine similarity - calculated from images generated by DALL·E mini. They evaluate these methods on isolated words from the MRC database and connected text from poems, captions, and news articles, comparing them to human judgments and baseline methods. The proposed methods demonstrate high correlation with human judgments of individual words and show better sensitivity to compositional changes in text compared to bag-of-words approaches. The study also explores the effects of different deformances on imageability measurements and discusses potential implications for understanding compositionality in text-to-image models.

## Method Summary
The authors measure text imageability using DALL·E mini to generate images from text prompts, then compute two metrics: average CLIP score (text-image alignment quality) and average pairwise image embedding cosine similarity (visual homogeneity of generated images). For each text input, they generate 10 images, calculate the average CLIP score across all images, and compute pairwise cosine similarities between ResNet-18 embeddings of all image pairs. These metrics are compared against human imageability ratings from the MRC database and evaluated on connected text from various corpora including poetry, captions, and news articles.

## Key Results
- Strong correlation (r=0.780) between Brysbaert concreteness ratings and MRC imageability ratings
- High correlation between proposed measures and human judgments (r=0.537 for average CLIP, r=0.566 for image similarity on isolated words)
- Bag-of-words methods fail to detect compositional changes while image-based methods succeed
- Average pairwise image embedding cosine similarity shows slightly better correlation with human judgments than average CLIP score

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Generated images' visual homogeneity correlates with human imageability ratings
- Mechanism: Words with high imageability produce images with similar visual features (low pairwise embedding cosine distance), while low imageability words produce more diverse images
- Core assumption: CLIP score and ResNet-18 embeddings capture visual similarity relevant to human imageability perception
- Evidence anchors: Strong correlation between Brysbaert concreteness ratings and MRC imageability ratings suggests visual properties are meaningful

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Deformances that disrupt compositionality reduce imageability scores
- Mechanism: Bag-of-words methods fail because they ignore word order and choice; image-based methods detect compositionality changes
- Core assumption: Human perception of imageability depends on compositional understanding, not just word presence
- Evidence anchors: Table 4 shows different methods' percent change responses to deformances

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Average CLIP score measures overall image-text alignment quality
- Mechanism: CLIP score reflects how well generated images match input text prompts, serving as proxy for imageability
- Core assumption: High imageability text produces images with strong CLIP alignment scores
- Evidence anchors: Pearson correlation r=0.537 between average CLIP score and MRC human ratings

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text prompt → DALL·E mini image generation → CLIP scoring → ResNet-18 embeddings → imageability measurements → human judgment comparison
- **Critical path**: Prompt generation → DALL-E mini image generation → CLIP score calculation → ResNet-18 embedding extraction → pairwise similarity computation → final imageability score calculation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Computational cost vs. measurement accuracy, single GPU vs. multi-GPU processing, number of generated images per prompt
- **Failure signatures**: Low correlation with human judgments, inconsistent deformance responses, high computational resource requirements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How do different text-to-image models (e.g., DALL·E 2, Stable Diffusion) compare in their ability to measure imageability and compositionality?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors mention Stable Diffusion as a potential alternative but do not use it due to issues with image generation. They also suggest that future work should compare different models' performance.
- **Why unresolved**: The study only uses DALL·E mini, so a direct comparison with other models is not possible.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conducting the same experiments with different text-to-image models and comparing their performance in measuring imageability and compositionality.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does the performance of the proposed methods vary across different languages?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors mention that DALL·E mini is trained on English-language material and their methods are limited to English isolated words and connected text.
- **Why unresolved**: The study only focuses on English text, so the performance across other languages is unknown.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Applying the proposed methods to text-to-image models trained on other languages and comparing their performance in measuring imageability.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How can the noise in AMT human judgments be reduced to obtain more reliable results?
- **Basis in paper**: The authors acknowledge that collecting a single human judgment for each text input and not asking about the device used may have introduced noise in the AMT experiments.
- **Why unresolved**: The current study only collects one judgment per text input, which may not be representative of the true imageability rating.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Collecting multiple human ratings per text input and using the average, as well as controlling for device-related factors in future AMT experiments.

## Limitations
- Computational requirements are prohibitively expensive, requiring 25 minutes per MRC word on a single GPU
- Limited validation to English text and specific deformances from Samuels and McGann (1999)
- Dependence on DALL·E mini, which may not represent capabilities of more advanced text-to-image models

## Confidence

**Confidence Level: Medium** for the core imageability measurement approach. While the correlation with human judgments (r=0.537 for aveCLIP, r=0.566 for imgSim on isolated words) is promising, the validation is primarily limited to the MRC database of individual words.

**Confidence Level: Medium** for the compositionality detection claims. The study demonstrates that bag-of-words methods fail to detect compositional changes while image-based methods succeed, but the validation relies on specific deformances from Samuels and McGann (1999).

**Confidence Level: Low** for computational efficiency claims. The paper states that image-based methods "can be prohibitively expensive," requiring 25 minutes per MRC word on a single GPU.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-model validation**: Test whether the proposed imageability measures generalize to other text-to-image models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2) and whether correlations with human judgments remain consistent across different model architectures.

2. **Extended compositional analysis**: Apply the imageability measurement methods to additional types of compositional changes beyond the current deformances, including syntactic transformations, semantic shifts, and domain-specific compositional variations.

3. **Scalability assessment**: Evaluate the computational efficiency and measurement stability when processing large-scale text corpora (e.g., entire books, social media datasets) to determine practical deployment requirements and potential bottlenecks.
