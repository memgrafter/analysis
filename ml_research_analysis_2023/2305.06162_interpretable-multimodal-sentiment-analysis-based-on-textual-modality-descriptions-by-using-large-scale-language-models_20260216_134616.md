# Interpretable multimodal sentiment analysis based on textual modality descriptions by using large-scale language models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06162
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06162
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Converting nonverbal modalities to textual descriptions enables interpretable multimodal sentiment analysis with performance comparable to or better than conventional methods

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the interpretability problem in multimodal sentiment analysis by converting nonverbal modality features (audio and facial) into textual descriptions that can be processed by large language models. The approach transforms pitch/energy patterns and facial action units into human-readable text, enabling direct interpretation of model decisions through the input descriptions. Experiments on the Hazumi dataset showed that multimodal textual descriptions maintained or improved effectiveness compared to baselines using conventional features, achieving up to 2.49% improvement in F1 score while providing interpretability.

## Method Summary
The method converts audio modality into descriptions of pitch and energy change patterns (five cases) and facial modality into action unit descriptions. These textual descriptions are combined using either separator concatenation or paragraph construction methods before being input to large language models (LLMs) for sentiment prediction. The approach was evaluated on binary classification tasks using self-reported and third-party sentiment annotations from the Hazumi dataset, comparing performance against baseline models using conventional features.

## Key Results
- Multimodal textual descriptions maintained or improved effectiveness compared to baselines using conventional features, with up to 2.49% improvement in F1 score
- Multimodal descriptions provided more helpful information than single modality descriptions, demonstrating similar characteristics to conventional fusion methods
- Paragraph construction method for combining descriptions was more effective than separator concatenation for third-party sentiment prediction, while the difference was smaller for self-reported sentiment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Converting nonverbal modality features into textual descriptions enables direct interpretability by making the model's decision process transparent through input text
- Mechanism: The approach transforms audio (pitch/energy patterns) and facial (action units) features into human-readable text descriptions. These descriptions are then fed to large language models (LLMs) which can interpret and predict sentiment based on the text, making the decision process directly traceable to the input descriptions
- Core assumption: LLMs can effectively understand and predict sentiment from textual descriptions of nonverbal modality features as well as or better than conventional feature representations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This provides an intuitive approach to directly interpret what models depend on with respect to making decisions from input texts, thus significantly improving interpretability."
  - [section]: "In this way, we constructed the input T to directly interpret what the model depends on in making decisions."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism, though related works exist on textualization of multimodal information

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multimodal textual descriptions provide more helpful information for sentiment prediction than single modality descriptions
- Mechanism: By combining descriptions from multiple modalities (audio, facial, lingual) into either separator-concatenated or paragraph-formatted text, the model receives richer contextual information that captures cross-modal interactions, leading to better sentiment predictions than using single modalities alone
- Core assumption: The combination of modality descriptions captures complementary information that enhances sentiment prediction beyond what single modalities provide
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The results also showed that multimodal descriptions have similar characteristics on fusing modalities as those of conventional fusion methods."
  - [section]: "The results in Section 5.4 showed that multimodal descriptions are generally more helpful information than those provided by single modal descriptions in sentiment predictions based on ChatGPT (API)."
  - [corpus]: Moderate - related work on multimodal fusion exists, but specific evidence for textual description combination is limited

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Constructing modality descriptions in natural language format (paragraph construction) is more effective than simple concatenation with separators for third-party sentiment prediction
- Mechanism: Paragraph construction method creates more natural text by embedding modality descriptions into a coherent narrative structure without artificial separators, which aligns better with how LLMs were trained on real text data, leading to improved performance for third-party sentiment tasks
- Core assumption: LLMs perform better when input text resembles natural language patterns they were trained on, rather than artificially structured text with separators
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The results also suggested that combining modality descriptions in a way that is close to natural languages is better than simply concatenating descriptions together for predicting third-party sentiment."
  - [section]: "Using too many separators potentially makes the input text unnatural and influences model performances."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence, though general LLM training on natural text is well-established

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Raw multimodal data -> Feature extraction (OpenFace for AUs, MATLAB for pitch/energy) -> Textual description generation -> Text combination (separator concatenation or paragraph construction) -> LLM inference (BERT/RoBERTa or ChatGPT) -> Sentiment prediction
- Critical path: Raw multimodal data → Feature extraction → Textual description generation → Text combination → LLM inference → Sentiment prediction
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Feature selection vs. information loss: Using only pitch/energy and AUs may miss important modality information but enables interpretability
  - Description granularity vs. naturalness: Detailed AU descriptions may be less natural than abstract emotional descriptions
  - Combination method vs. model compatibility: Separator concatenation is simpler but may be less effective than paragraph construction for certain tasks
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on single modalities suggests description generation is ineffective
  - Similar performance between single and multimodal inputs suggests combination method is not capturing complementary information
  - Large performance gap between discriminative and generative LLM results suggests task-model mismatch
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test description generation quality by having humans rate whether generated descriptions accurately represent the original modality features
  2. Compare performance of separator concatenation vs. paragraph construction on a small validation set before full training
  3. Evaluate whether including more detailed feature patterns (beyond the 5 cases) improves performance while maintaining interpretability

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can facial modality descriptions be made more natural and effective for large language models in sentiment analysis?
- What is the optimal way to comprehensively describe audio modality information beyond the two feature patterns used in this study?
- How do different training strategies (freezing vs. fine-tuning) affect the performance of large language models when using textual modality descriptions for sentiment analysis?

## Limitations
- The approach relies heavily on the quality of textual descriptions generated from nonverbal modalities, with limited evidence about how well these descriptions preserve critical information
- Performance gains over baseline methods are modest (up to 2.49% F1 improvement), and it's unclear whether these differences are statistically significant
- The effectiveness of the approach for third-party sentiment prediction versus self-reported sentiment shows variability that warrants further investigation

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1: Medium - The approach demonstrably makes model decisions traceable to input text
- Mechanism 2: Medium - Evidence shows multimodal descriptions are generally more helpful, but the extent varies by task
- Mechanism 3: Low - The superiority of paragraph construction appears task-dependent and needs more validation

## Next Checks
1. Conduct human evaluation studies where annotators rate the quality and accuracy of the generated textual descriptions compared to the original nonverbal modality features to validate information preservation
2. Perform ablation studies to quantify the information loss when converting nonverbal features to textual descriptions, comparing performance against models using raw features
3. Test the approach on additional multimodal sentiment datasets with different characteristics (languages, domains, annotation schemes) to assess generalizability beyond the Hazumi dataset
