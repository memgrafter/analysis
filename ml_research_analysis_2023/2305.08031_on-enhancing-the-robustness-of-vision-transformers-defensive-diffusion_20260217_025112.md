# On enhancing the robustness of Vision Transformers: Defensive Diffusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08031
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08031
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: Defensive diffusion technique using diffusion models to eliminate adversarial noise, achieving over 2% robust accuracy improvement over PGD attack on Tuberculosis X-ray dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the vulnerability of Vision Transformers (ViTs) to adversarial attacks in medical imaging applications. The authors propose a defensive diffusion technique that leverages diffusion models to remove adversarial noise through a reverse diffusion process. The approach is enhanced with knowledge distillation to create a lightweight, robust student CNN model. Experimental results on a Tuberculosis X-ray dataset demonstrate significant improvements in robustness against various adversarial attacks while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The method combines defensive diffusion with knowledge distillation to enhance ViT robustness. First, a deblurring diffusion model is trained to remove adversarial noise through forward and reverse diffusion processes. The purified images are then fed into a ViT for classification. Soft probability outputs from the ViT are used to train a lightweight student CNN model through knowledge distillation, creating a computationally efficient and robust model for gray-box attack scenarios.

## Key Results
- Defensive diffusion achieves over 2% robust accuracy improvement compared to PGD attack
- Student CNN trained via knowledge distillation maintains computational efficiency while achieving robustness
- Method outperforms state-of-the-art SEViT baseline in terms of both robustness and efficiency
- Successfully defends against multiple attack types including FGSM, PGD, and AutoPGD

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Reverse diffusion effectively removes adversarial noise from images by progressively deblurring and denoising the perturbed image.
- Mechanism: The forward diffusion phase introduces Gaussian blur to the adversarial image, mixing the adversarial noise with random blur noise. The reverse diffusion phase then uses a trained deblurring model to reconstruct the original clean image, removing both the blur and the adversarial perturbations.
- Core assumption: The deblurring diffusion model is trained on clean images and can generalize to remove adversarial noise patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By utilizing the denoising capabilities of the diffusion model, we employ a reverse diffusion process to effectively eliminate the adversarial noise from the attack sample, resulting in a cleaner image that is then fed into the ViT blocks."
  - [section] "The diffusion model comprises both forward and reverse diffusion processes. In the forward diffusion process, the model progressively introduces small amounts of Gaussian noise to the image at each time step, following a specific equation... The diffusion model is then trained to reconstruct the original image from the noise in the reverse diffusion process, leveraging knowledge from the training distribution."
- Break condition: If the adversarial noise pattern is too complex or the deblurring model is not well-trained on diverse perturbations, the reverse diffusion may fail to fully remove the noise, leaving residual artifacts.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge distillation transfers robustness from a teacher ViT to a lightweight student CNN by training the CNN on soft probability outputs from the ViT.
- Mechanism: The ViT acts as a teacher, generating soft probability distributions for each input image. The student CNN is trained to mimic these soft targets rather than hard labels, which introduces uncertainty and smooths the optimization landscape, making it harder for adversarial attacks to exploit sharp decision boundaries.
- Core assumption: Adversarial examples exhibit high transferability within the same model family, so training a CNN on ViT's soft outputs confers robustness to attacks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Additionally, we propose combining knowledge distillation with our framework to obtain a lightweight student model that is both computationally efficient and robust against gray box attacks."
  - [section] "Knowledge distillation involves training a teacher model to generate soft targets, which are then used to train a smaller student model... The distilled model is trained using soft probabilities/targets instead of hard targets... Soft targets provide valuable information that cannot be conveyed through a single hard target."
- Break condition: If the ViT itself is not robust, the soft targets may still contain adversarial vulnerabilities, and the CNN may not achieve significant robustness gains.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of defensive diffusion and knowledge distillation provides complementary robustness—diffusion purifies inputs before ViT inference, and distillation creates a lightweight, robust CNN for gray-box attack scenarios.
- Mechanism: Defensive diffusion removes adversarial noise from images before they are classified by the ViT, restoring clean accuracy. Knowledge distillation then creates a smaller CNN that is robust against gray-box attacks because it was trained on the ViT's soft outputs, which encode richer information and are less sensitive to exact input perturbations.
- Core assumption: The ViT's soft outputs are robust to adversarial noise after diffusion, and the CNN can effectively learn from these outputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Comparison of our method with a SOTA baseline method, SEViT, shows that our work is able to outperform the baseline."
  - [section] "The light weight CNN is trained on soft probabilities, input from the last layer of ViT... We conducted the same defensive diffusion experiments on the student CNN as we did on ViT and SEViT."
- Break condition: If the soft outputs from the ViT are still vulnerable after diffusion, the student CNN may inherit these vulnerabilities, reducing the overall robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input image → Defensive diffusion (forward + reverse) → Purified image → Vision Transformer (ViT) → Soft probability outputs → Knowledge distillation → Student CNN
  Parallel path: SEViT (ensemble of MLPs) for baseline comparison

- Critical path:
  1. Apply forward diffusion to adversarial image (blurring)
  2. Apply reverse diffusion (deblurring) to remove adversarial noise
  3. Feed purified image into ViT for classification
  4. Extract soft outputs from ViT
  5. Train student CNN on soft outputs for lightweight, robust model

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Defensive diffusion adds computational overhead but improves robustness; balance between purification quality and inference speed
  - SEViT uses ensemble MLPs for robustness but is computationally heavy; defensive diffusion + knowledge distillation aims for similar robustness with less compute
  - Knowledge distillation trades model size for robustness; student CNN is smaller but may have slightly lower accuracy than teacher ViT

- Failure signatures:
  - If diffusion fails: Purified image still contains adversarial noise, leading to incorrect ViT classification
  - If knowledge distillation fails: Student CNN does not achieve robustness gains, or accuracy drops significantly
  - If model transferability fails: Adversarial examples transfer poorly between ViT and CNN, reducing defense effectiveness

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test defensive diffusion on clean and adversarial images using ViT; compare accuracy before and after diffusion
  2. Train student CNN on ViT soft outputs; evaluate accuracy and robustness against FGSM/PGD attacks
  3. Compare defensive diffusion + knowledge distillation against SEViT on the same dataset and attack types

## Limitations
- Evaluation is primarily conducted on a single medical imaging dataset (Tuberculosis X-ray), limiting generalizability
- Computational overhead introduced by diffusion process requires careful consideration for deployment
- Effectiveness against more sophisticated attack methods beyond FGSM and PGD remains unclear

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using reverse diffusion to remove adversarial noise is well-established in the diffusion model literature. The knowledge distillation approach for creating robust student models is also widely validated.
- **Medium confidence**: The specific combination of defensive diffusion with knowledge distillation for ViTs, while theoretically sound, requires more extensive validation across diverse datasets and attack types.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about computational efficiency improvements relative to SEViT are difficult to verify without explicit runtime comparisons and memory usage metrics.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain robustness testing**: Evaluate the defensive diffusion approach on multiple medical imaging datasets (e.g., chest X-rays for different conditions, retinal images) and non-medical image datasets to assess generalizability beyond the Tuberculosis X-ray dataset.
