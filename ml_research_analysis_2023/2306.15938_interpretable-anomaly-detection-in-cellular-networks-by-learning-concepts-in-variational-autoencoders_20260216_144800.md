# Interpretable Anomaly Detection in Cellular Networks by Learning Concepts in Variational Autoencoders

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting anomalies in cellular networks in an interpretable way by proposing a new approach using variational autoencoders (VAEs) that learn interpretable representations of the latent space for each Key Performance Indicator (KPI) in the dataset. The core method idea involves using VAEs to model the probability distribution of the KPIs and incorporating additional information centroids using the K-means algorithm to enhance representation learning. The model learns an individual Gaussian distribution for each concept, enabling the detection of anomalies based on reconstruction loss and Z-scores. The primary results show that the proposed framework offers a faster and autonomous solution for detecting anomalies in cellular networks, with the model accurately predicting loss-based anomalies and providing interpretable insights into the detected anomalous points.

## Method Summary
The method uses VAEs with conditional priors to learn interpretable latent representations of KPI time series in cellular networks. K-means clustering is applied to KPI means to generate conceptual priors, which are then used as Gaussian priors for specific latent dimensions. The VAE is trained with a modified ELBO loss that uses these concept-specific priors, enabling the model to learn distinct, concept-aligned representations. Anomalies are detected using reconstruction loss and Z-scores computed on the learned latent dimensions, allowing for interpretable attribution to specific KPIs.

## Key Results
- The proposed framework offers a faster and autonomous solution for detecting anomalies in cellular networks.
- The model accurately predicts loss-based anomalies and provides interpretable insights into the detected anomalous points.
- The approach enables interpretable anomaly detection by learning individual Gaussian distributions for each concept and using Z-scores to identify responsible KPIs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using K-means clustering on KPI means to generate conceptual priors improves latent space interpretability.
- Mechanism: K-means groups similar KPI behaviors into clusters, each assigned a centroid. These centroids are used as mean values in the prior distribution for specific latent dimensions. This forces the VAE to learn distinct, concept-aligned representations in the latent space, making anomalies traceable to specific KPIs.
- Core assumption: Network elements with similar KPI patterns belong to the same conceptual group and should be modeled together.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We ensure the interpretability of the anomalies via additional information centroids (c) using the K-means algorithm to enhance representation learning."
  - [section 2] "we employ the k-means algorithm [15] to derive conceptual labels denoted as (c) by forming clusters based on the mean values of each KPI for an individual network element in our time series dataset."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct citation, but method is standard clustering approach.
- Break condition: If KPI distributions are too heterogeneous, K-means clusters may not capture meaningful concepts, leading to noisy priors and poor interpretability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Replacing the standard Gaussian prior with a conditional prior p(z|c) enables probabilistic anomaly attribution.
- Mechanism: In vanilla VAEs, the prior is fixed (N(0,I)). Here, each concept c has its own Gaussian prior with mean equal to the centroid and fixed variance. The KL divergence is computed against this concept-specific prior, so the latent dimensions encode concept-aligned information. This allows Z-scores to be computed per KPI.
- Core assumption: Each concept c can be represented by a single Gaussian distribution in the latent space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2] "we introduce a modification in our implementation by computing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the encoder distribution and a specific condition c, the concept, instead of using a standard Gaussian."
  - [section 2] "We incorporate the centroids into the mean of the prior for a particular latent dimension, while keeping the standard deviation constant for all latent dimensions."
  - [corpus] Assumption: No direct experimental comparison provided in corpus.
- Break condition: If a concept's data is multimodal, a single Gaussian prior will be a poor fit, leading to misleading latent representations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using Z-scores on learned latent dimensions enables interpretable anomaly detection.
- Mechanism: After training, each latent dimension corresponds to a learned Gaussian (mean and variance). For a new sample, the mean of the latent dimension is converted to a Z-score using the learned parameters. High Z-scores indicate deviation from the concept's normal behavior, pinpointing the responsible KPI.
- Core assumption: The latent dimension's learned mean is a reliable proxy for the KPI's magnitude, and the learned variance captures its typical variability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2] "we utilize the learned Gaussian distributions in the latent dimensions to interpret predicted anomaly based on a high loss. To evaluate the anomaly caused by a specific KPI, we convert the mean value of latent dimensions into the Z-scores."
  - [section 4.2] "Table 2 illustrates that the model predicts anomaly points with a high loss value for cluster 200. Since each KPI is encoded corresponding to the concept c and has its own univariate Gaussian distribution, we compute the Z-score for all five KPIs from z0 to z4 dimensions, respectively."
  - [corpus] Weak: No alternative Z-score method cited.
- Break condition: If the latent distribution is not Gaussian (e.g., due to poor training or concept misspecification), Z-scores will be invalid.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing: KPI time series → normalization → K-means clustering → centroids.
  - Encoder: 3 LSTM layers → linear layer → latent mean/log-variance (25 dims: 5 concept-specific + 20 standard).
  - Prior: For dims 0-4, N(centroid, σ²); for dims 5-24, N(0,I).
  - KL loss: D_KL(q(z|x) || p(z|c)) for concept dims, standard for others.
  - Decoder: 3 LSTM layers → linear + sigmoid → reconstruction.
  - Loss: ELBO = KL loss - log-likelihood (reconstruction probability).

- Critical path:
  1. K-means clustering on KPI means → centroids.
  2. Train VAE with conditional priors.
  3. Evaluate anomalies via reconstruction loss + Z-scores.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Centroid-based priors vs. learned priors: Centroids are interpretable but may be suboptimal if data is non-Gaussian.
  - Fixed variance for all concept dims: Simplifies model but may not capture KPI-specific variability.
  - Hard Z-score threshold vs. adaptive: Hard threshold is interpretable but may miss subtle anomalies.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor clustering: K-means centroids don't capture meaningful KPI patterns → latent dims not interpretable.
  - Overfitting: VAE reconstructs training data well but fails on test anomalies → check latent dim variance.
  - Misaligned Z-scores: High loss but low Z-scores (or vice versa) → check if concept priors match test data distribution.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline VAE (no concepts) and compare latent space visualization to proposed model.
  2. Vary number of K-means clusters (e.g., 100, 500, 1000) and measure impact on anomaly detection accuracy and interpretability.
  3. Replace Z-score threshold (+15) with adaptive threshold (e.g., 3σ) and compare false positive/negative rates.

## Limitations

- The clustering is performed on mean values of each KPI per network element, which may oversimplify the temporal dynamics and fail to capture more nuanced patterns.
- The choice of a fixed variance for all concept-specific latent dimensions is a simplification that may not adequately represent the true variability of different KPIs.
- The use of a hard Z-score threshold (+15) for anomaly detection, while interpretable, lacks justification and may be arbitrary or suboptimal for different network conditions.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The overall architecture of using VAEs with conditional priors for anomaly detection is sound and well-grounded in prior work.
- Medium confidence: The specific choice of K-means clustering on KPI means as the concept formation method is reasonable but not thoroughly validated.
- Low confidence: The interpretability of the Z-score-based anomaly attribution is asserted but not rigorously tested; it's unclear how well the latent dimensions actually align with individual KPIs in practice.

## Next Checks

1. **Concept Validation**: Visualize the latent space for different concepts using t-SNE or UMAP and check if points from the same concept cluster together and are well-separated from other concepts. This would validate that the conditional priors are learning meaningful representations.
2. **Ablation on K-means Parameters**: Systematically vary the number of K-means clusters (e.g., 100, 500, 1000) and measure the impact on both anomaly detection accuracy and the interpretability of the latent dimensions (e.g., via Z-score distribution analysis).
3. **Z-score Threshold Sensitivity**: Replace the fixed Z-score threshold (+15) with an adaptive threshold (e.g., 3 standard deviations of the training Z-scores) and compare the false positive and false negative rates on a labeled test set. This would assess whether the hard threshold is optimal or if a more adaptive approach is warranted.
