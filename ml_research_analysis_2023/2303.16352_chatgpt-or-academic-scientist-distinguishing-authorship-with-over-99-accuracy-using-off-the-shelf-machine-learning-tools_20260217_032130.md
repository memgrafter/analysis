# ChatGPT or academic scientist? Distinguishing authorship with over 99% accuracy using off-the-shelf machine learning tools

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.16352
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16352
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** Novel supervised classification method using 20 linguistic features achieves >99% accuracy in distinguishing human academic writing from ChatGPT-generated text

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of distinguishing ChatGPT-generated academic writing from human-authored text, a critical need in higher education and academic publishing. The researchers developed a novel supervised classification approach using 20 linguistic features identified through manual analysis of academic science writing. These features include paragraph complexity, sentence length diversity, punctuation usage, and specific word patterns like "however," "but," and "although." Using XGBoost classification, the method achieved over 99% accuracy in distinguishing human from AI authorship, significantly outperforming existing tools like the GPT-2 Output Detector, which misclassified 20 times more documents.

## Method Summary
The researchers manually analyzed academic science writing to identify 20 linguistic features that distinguish human authors from ChatGPT. These features capture paragraph-level complexity (sentence and word counts), stylistic patterns (use of "however," "but," "although"), and punctuation variations. They trained an XGBoost classifier using these features and evaluated it through leave-one-essay-out cross-validation. The approach achieved >99% accuracy on document-level classification, with each paragraph in a document being classified and then voting for the final document-level determination.

## Key Results
- XGBoost classifier with 20 hand-selected features achieved over 99% accuracy in distinguishing human from AI authorship
- Paragraph complexity features (sentence count, word count) showed the largest distinguishing power, with ChatGPT producing shorter, more uniform structures
- Human scientists demonstrated consistent patterns of using equivocal language with transitional phrases like "however," "but," and "although"
- Outperformed existing tools like GPT-2 Output Detector, which misclassified 20 times more documents

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Paragraph-level complexity features (sentence count, word count) effectively separate human from ChatGPT writing in academic domains.
- **Mechanism:** Human scientists produce longer, more complex paragraphs with greater sentence variation, while ChatGPT generates shorter, more uniform structures.
- **Core assumption:** Academic writing conventions in science inherently produce more complex paragraph structures than general LLM outputs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "scientists write long paragraphs and have a penchant for equivocal language, frequently using words like but, however, and although"
  - [section] "The largest distinguishing features were the number of sentences per paragraph and the number of total words per paragraph. In both cases, ChatGPT's averages were significantly lower than the human scientists"
  - [corpus] **Weak** - No corpus evidence directly supports paragraph complexity as a distinguishing factor; this appears to be an original finding
- **Break condition:** If ChatGPT training data includes more academic writing samples with longer paragraphs, or if academic writing conventions shift toward brevity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Stylistic word choice differences (use of "however," "but," "although") reliably distinguish human academic writing from ChatGPT.
- **Mechanism:** Scientists use more equivocal language and specific transitional phrases, while ChatGPT favors more general, less nuanced expressions.
- **Core assumption:** Academic scientists consistently use certain linguistic patterns that differ from LLM training data distributions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "scientists write long paragraphs and have a penchant for equivocal language, frequently using words like but, however, and although"
  - [section] "Human scientists also displayed other consistent patterns in the training data: they are more likely to use equivocal language (however, but, although)"
  - [corpus] **Missing** - No corpus comparison of word frequency distributions between human and ChatGPT academic writing
- **Break condition:** If ChatGPT training includes more academic writing with similar equivocal language patterns, or if scientists adopt more direct writing styles.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** XGBoost classification with 20 hand-selected features achieves >99% accuracy on document-level classification.
- **Mechanism:** Combining paragraph complexity, punctuation patterns, and specific word frequencies creates a robust feature space that XGBoost can effectively separate.
- **Core assumption:** The 20 features identified capture sufficient variance between human and AI writing to enable high accuracy classification.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "With a set of 20 features, including the aforementioned ones and others, we built a model that assigned the author, as human or AI, at well over 99% accuracy"
  - [section] "The model, therefore, does not rely on having any previous examples of the human writer's works whose essay is being classified"
  - [corpus] **Missing** - No external validation of feature selection or comparison to alternative feature sets
- **Break condition:** If new writing styles emerge that overlap with these features, or if feature importance shifts significantly.

## Foundational Learning

- **Supervised classification with XGBoost:**
  - Why needed here: The problem requires learning patterns from labeled examples to classify new documents; XGBoost handles complex feature interactions well.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between supervised and unsupervised learning in the context of authorship attribution?

- **Feature engineering for text classification:**
  - Why needed here: Raw text cannot be directly used by classifiers; relevant linguistic features must be extracted and quantified.
  - Quick check question: Why might simple word frequency features be insufficient for distinguishing academic writing styles?

- **Cross-validation methodology:**
  - Why needed here: Leave-one-essay-out validation prevents overfitting to individual authors and ensures model generalizes to new writers.
  - Quick check question: How does leave-one-essay-out differ from k-fold cross-validation in terms of preventing author-specific bias?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - Data collection pipeline → Text preprocessing → Feature extraction module → XGBoost classifier → Classification output
  - Feature extraction includes: paragraph statistics, sentence diversity metrics, punctuation frequency, and word pattern counts

- **Critical path:**
  - Feature extraction (20 features) → XGBoost training → Document-level voting aggregation
  - Paragraph-level predictions must be aggregated to document level for final classification

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Hand-selected features vs. deep learning approaches: Higher interpretability but potentially less generalizable
  - Document-level voting vs. paragraph-only classification: Better accuracy but requires complete documents
  - Specific to academic science vs. general applicability: Higher accuracy in domain but limited transferability

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High paragraph-level accuracy but low document-level accuracy: Voting aggregation may be problematic
  - Consistent misclassification of certain topics: Feature set may be biased toward certain writing styles
  - Performance degradation on newer ChatGPT versions: Feature patterns may have changed in newer model versions

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test feature importance ranking to identify which features contribute most to classification accuracy
  2. Compare document-level voting vs. paragraph-level classification performance on held-out test sets
  3. Evaluate performance on different academic disciplines to assess generalizability of feature set

## Limitations
- Feature selection based on manual analysis of limited academic science writing may not generalize to other disciplines
- Performance on real-world scenarios with partial documents or mixed authorship remains untested
- Assumption that linguistic patterns are stable and universal across all academic writing contexts

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The basic finding that human academic writing differs from ChatGPT output in measurable ways is well-supported by the data
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim of >99% accuracy is technically supported but may not hold across different academic domains or newer AI models
- **Low Confidence:** The assumption that these linguistic patterns are stable and universal across all academic writing contexts

## Next Checks
1. **Domain Transfer Validation:** Test the model's performance on academic writing from humanities, social sciences, and engineering disciplines to assess whether the feature set generalizes beyond the original science-focused corpus.

2. **Temporal Stability Analysis:** Evaluate the model's accuracy on documents written before and after the widespread adoption of ChatGPT to determine if writing patterns have shifted due to AI influence or changing academic conventions.

3. **Adversarial Testing:** Conduct experiments where human writers are instructed to deliberately mimic ChatGPT-style writing or where AI outputs are deliberately modified to incorporate human-like features to test the robustness of the detection method.
