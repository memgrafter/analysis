# Towards a Deeper Understanding of Concept Bottleneck Models Through End-to-End Explanation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03578
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03578
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: CBMs learn concept predictions based on entire input regions rather than distinct features, with LRP providing more interpretable saliency maps than IG

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) through end-to-end explanation techniques to understand how these models map inputs to human-defined concepts before making final classifications. Using Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) and Integrated Gradients (IG), the authors analyze whether CBMs learn distinct input regions for each concept and how concepts contribute to final classification decisions. The study reveals that CBMs generally do not map concepts to distinct input regions, instead distributing relevance across entire images, and proposes new methods for quantitatively evaluating concept importance.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates four CBM architectures (independent, sequential, joint-without-sigmoid, joint-with-sigmoid) on the CUB-200 2011 bird dataset using LRP and IG for explanation. LRP is applied with specific rule configurations (αβ for first 7 conv layers, ϵ for next 6, 0 for top 3) while IG uses SmoothGrad for noise reduction. The authors introduce a "distance pointing game" metric to evaluate saliency map alignment with ground truth concept locations and use LRP's relevance conservation property to calculate concept contribution proportions for final classification. The study compares LRP and IG saliency maps and analyzes how different CBM architectures distribute relevance from concepts to final predictions.

## Key Results
- LRP produces saliency maps with lower average distance to ground truth concept locations compared to IG, providing more interpretable feature-level groupings
- CBMs do not learn distinct input regions for individual concepts, with relevance distributed across entire bird images rather than specific features
- Sequential and joint-without-sigmoid models apply positive relevance to concepts predicted as not present and negative relevance to concepts predicted as present when making final classifications
- Concept relevance proportions enable quantitative measurement of each concept's contribution to final classification decisions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LRP attribution provides more human-aligned saliency maps than IG by grouping relevance at feature level instead of pixel-by-pixel.
- Mechanism: LRP conserves total relevance during backward propagation and redistributes it to groups of input features using composite rules (LRP-αβ, LRP-ϵ, LRP-0). This grouping reduces noisy pixel-level attributions that IG tends to produce.
- Core assumption: Grouping relevance at the feature level makes saliency maps more interpretable for concepts that occupy distinct image regions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "LRP has a lower average distance than IG" and "LRP saliency maps are filtering out noisy relevance from the input image."
  - [section] "LRP with our rule setup groups relevance to input regions, while IG applies relevance on a pixel-by-pixel basis."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence on feature-level grouping; this is inferred from LRP design.
- Break condition: If concepts do not map to distinct regions (as observed here), grouping provides no interpretability benefit over IG.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The distance pointing game quantifies whether saliency maps focus on ground truth concept locations, enabling model ranking.
- Mechanism: By measuring the average Euclidean distance between the most salient point and ground truth concept location, this metric quantifies alignment between model attention and human expectations.
- Core assumption: Ground truth concept locations are known and accurately represent human conceptual understanding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Our version, called distance pointing game, averages the distance between the most salient point of a saliency map and the ground truth point."
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence for this specific metric; this appears to be a novel evaluation method introduced in the paper.
- Break condition: If ground truth locations are inaccurate or unavailable, the metric becomes meaningless.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Concept relevance proportion enables identification of most influential concepts for final classification.
- Mechanism: Since LRP conserves total relevance, the proportion of relevance attributed to each concept directly measures its contribution to the final classification score.
- Core assumption: Relevance conservation holds throughout the propagation and concept contributions sum meaningfully.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose using the proportion of relevance as a measurement for explaining concept importance."
  - [section] "LRP enables us to calculate the contribution of each predicted concept to the final classification."
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence for this specific method; appears to be novel contribution.
- Break condition: If relevance conservation fails or if concepts interact non-linearly, proportions may misrepresent actual influence.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP)
  - Why needed here: LRP is the primary explanation technique used to trace relevance from concept predictions back to input features and from concept predictions to final classifications.
  - Quick check question: What is the fundamental rule that LRP uses to propagate relevance backwards through a neural network?

- Concept: Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs)
  - Why needed here: CBMs are the model architecture being explained, requiring understanding of how concepts map to inputs and classifications.
  - Quick check question: What are the three training methods for CBMs and how do they differ in architecture?

- Concept: Integrated Gradients (IG)
  - Why needed here: IG is used as a comparison method to LRP, requiring understanding of its pixel-level attribution approach.
  - Quick check question: How does IG differ from LRP in terms of attribution granularity and what is the main advantage of each?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input image → VGG-16 feature extractor → concept prediction layer → classification layer → output label
- Critical path: Input image → VGG-16 → concept vector → classification
- Design tradeoffs: Using pre-trained VGG-16 provides strong features but may limit concept learning flexibility. Joint training methods allow end-to-end optimization but may obscure individual component contributions.
- Failure signatures: If saliency maps show uniform relevance across entire images, concepts are not learning distinct input regions. If negative relevance appears unexpectedly in concept-to-classification paths, model has learned inverted concept relationships.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply LRP to a trained CBM and visualize concept-to-input saliency maps to verify uniform distribution.
  2. Calculate distance pointing game metric for LRP vs IG to confirm LRP's lower average distance.
  3. Compute concept relevance proportions for final classifications to identify most influential concepts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Do CBMs trained on instance-level concepts (rather than class-level concepts) learn distinct input feature mappings for each concept prediction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that class-level concepts may cause models to learn concept vectors without distinguishing visible concepts in individual samples, and suggests that instance-level concepts could constrain models to learn distinct features in the input space.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates CBMs trained on class-level concepts from the CUB dataset, which has incorrect concepts (e.g., Mallard males and females share the same concept vector despite visual differences).
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing CBMs on a dataset with instance-level concepts and comparing saliency maps to those from class-level concept models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is the phenomenon where sequential and joint-without-sigmoid models apply positive relevance to concepts predicted as not present and negative relevance to concepts predicted as present a general characteristic of CBMs or specific to the CUB dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes this pattern in two training methods but states it remains an open question whether this is general to CBMs or specific to models using the CUB dataset.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests on the CUB dataset and does not explore whether this pattern occurs with other datasets or training configurations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing CBMs on multiple datasets (e.g., CelebA) and different training methods to see if the relevance pattern persists.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How effective are the saliency maps and concept proportion contributions at improving human understanding of CBM decision boundaries and error cases?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper proposes these explanations for domain experts but does not conduct a human study to evaluate their effectiveness in improving mental models of CBM error boundaries.
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents quantitative and qualitative analysis of the explanations themselves, not their impact on human understanding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a user study where domain experts interact with CBMs using these explanations and measuring improvements in their understanding and decision-making.

## Limitations
- The findings are constrained by using class-level concepts rather than instance-level concepts, which may affect whether CBMs learn distinct input regions
- The pre-trained VGG-16 backbone may limit the flexibility of concept learning in CBMs
- Ground truth concept locations may not capture the full semantic understanding of concepts, affecting evaluation metrics

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: LRP produces more interpretable saliency maps than IG through feature-level grouping; CBM concepts generally do not map to distinct input regions
- **Medium Confidence**: The proposed distance pointing game metric effectively quantifies saliency map alignment; concept relevance proportions accurately measure classification influence
- **Low Confidence**: The finding that sequential and joint-without-sigmoid models apply positive relevance to absent concepts represents the most uncertain claim, as it requires deeper investigation of the model's learned concept relationships

## Next Checks
1. Validate the accuracy of ground truth concept locations across a subset of images with multiple annotators to ensure the pointing game metric is measuring what we intend.
2. Train CBMs using different backbone architectures (ResNet, EfficientNet) to determine if the lack of distinct concept regions is specific to VGG-16 or a more general CBM phenomenon.
3. Test with instance-level concepts rather than class-level concepts to determine if the granularity of concept definitions affects whether CBMs learn distinct input regions.
