# VSRQ: Quantitative Assessment Method for Safety Risk of Vehicle Intelligent Connected System

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01898
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01898
- Reference count: 29
- VSRQ model demonstrates 94.36% accuracy and 73.43% recall with at least 14.63% improvement over existing methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces VSRQ, a quantitative assessment method for vehicle intelligent connected system safety risk. The model combines I-FAHP (Improved Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process) with FCA (Formal Concept Analysis) clustering to identify high-risk systems by extracting safety indicators and analyzing vulnerable components. By prioritizing testing of vulnerable components, VSRQ aims to reduce overall safety risks in vehicle intelligent connected systems. The method was evaluated on the OpenPilot platform, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing approaches.

## Method Summary
VSRQ integrates I-FAHP for weighting safety indicators with FCA clustering for identifying vulnerable components in vehicle intelligent connected systems. The approach extracts safety indicators from system architecture, uses I-FAHP to determine their relative importance, and applies FCA clustering to group components based on risk factors. This combination allows for systematic identification of high-risk components that require prioritized testing and mitigation efforts. The model provides a structured framework for assessing safety risks during the concept stage of vehicle development.

## Key Results
- VSRQ achieves 94.36% accuracy in safety risk assessment
- Recall rate of 73.43% for identifying vulnerable components
- At least 14.63% improvement over existing risk assessment methods
- Effective prioritization of testing for vulnerable components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The VSRQ model works by combining multi-criteria decision making with formal concept analysis to create a comprehensive risk assessment framework. I-FAHP provides a structured approach to weighting safety indicators based on expert knowledge and fuzzy logic, while FCA clustering identifies relationships between components and their associated risks. This dual approach captures both the importance of different safety factors and the interdependencies between system components, enabling more accurate risk identification than single-method approaches.
