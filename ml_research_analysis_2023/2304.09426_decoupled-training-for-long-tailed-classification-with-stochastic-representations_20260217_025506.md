# Decoupled Training for Long-Tailed Classification With Stochastic Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09426
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09426
- Authors: [List of authors]
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes a decoupled training approach using SWA and SWAG for long-tailed classification, improving accuracy and uncertainty estimation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of long-tailed classification by proposing a decoupled training framework that leverages Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) to obtain robust feature extractors and Stochastic Weight Averaging Gaussian (SWAG) to capture parameter uncertainty. The method uses stochastic representations from the SWAG posterior to retrain classifiers through a self-distillation strategy, effectively addressing the imbalanced nature of long-tailed datasets. The approach demonstrates consistent improvements across CIFAR10/100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist-2018 benchmarks, showing enhanced both accuracy and uncertainty estimation capabilities compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of two main stages: feature extractor training and classifier retraining. First, a feature extractor is trained using SWA, which averages weights over the training trajectory to find flatter minima and better generalization. The SWA-Gaussian (SWAG) posterior is then constructed to approximate the Bayesian posterior distribution of parameters. In the second stage, the classifier is retrained using self-distillation with stochastic representations sampled from the SWAG posterior. This self-distillation process incorporates knowledge from multiple stochastic models, creating more robust classifiers that better handle class imbalance. The method integrates seamlessly with existing balancing strategies like Class-Balanced Sampling (CBS), Gradual Reweighting (GRW), and Label-Aware Smoothing (LA).

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist-2018 long-tailed classification benchmarks
- Demonstrates significant improvements in both classification accuracy and uncertainty estimation metrics
- Shows consistent performance gains across different balancing strategies (CBS, GRW, LA)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging the SWAG posterior to capture model uncertainty through stochastic representations. By sampling from this posterior during classifier retraining, the model effectively performs ensemble learning without the computational cost of training multiple models. The self-distillation process allows the classifier to learn from these diverse representations, creating a more robust decision boundary that better handles the imbalanced nature of long-tailed data. The decoupled approach ensures that the feature extractor learns general representations while the classifier can be specialized for the imbalanced distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Feature extractor training (SWA) -> SWAG posterior construction -> Stochastic representation sampling -> Classifier retraining (self-distillation)

**Critical Path**: The most critical sequence is SWA training that produces θSWA and ΣSWA, followed by SWAG sampling to generate stochastic representations, and finally self-distillation for classifier retraining. Each stage depends on the successful completion of the previous one.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades increased complexity in the training pipeline (two-stage process with Bayesian approximation) for improved performance on long-tailed data. The SWAG approximation adds computational overhead during classifier retraining but avoids the cost of full ensemble training.

**Failure Signatures**: Training may diverge if τKD is set too high during self-distillation, causing logit explosion. Insufficient performance improvement may indicate that ΣSWA doesn't capture meaningful uncertainty, suggesting the need for longer SWA training or different hyperparameter settings.

**First Experiments**: 1) Verify SWA converges to stable θSWA by monitoring training loss; 2) Check SWAG posterior quality by visualizing parameter variance distributions; 3) Test self-distillation with different τKD values to find optimal temperature scaling.

## Limitations
