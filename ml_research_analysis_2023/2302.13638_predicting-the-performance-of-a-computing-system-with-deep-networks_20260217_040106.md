# Predicting the Performance of a Computing System with Deep Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13638
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13638
- Authors: Multiple authors from various institutions
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Deep learning models predict hardware performance with R2 scores of 0.94-0.98

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a deep learning approach to predict computing hardware performance and energy consumption without exhaustive benchmarking. The authors address the challenge of evaluating all possible hardware-workload combinations by training models on MLCommons benchmark data to predict performance metrics for unseen hardware configurations. Three network architectures are evaluated: a fully-connected network, a standard CNN, and a ResNet-inspired CNN, achieving impressive R2 scores ranging from 0.94 to 0.98. The work demonstrates that deep learning can effectively capture the relationship between hardware specifications, workload characteristics, and performance outcomes, providing a practical tool for hardware evaluation in procurement and deployment scenarios.

## Method Summary
The authors develop deep learning models to predict benchmark scores for unseen hardware configurations using MLCommons benchmark data. Three different network architectures are implemented and compared: a fully-connected network, a standard CNN, and a ResNet-inspired CNN. The models take hardware specifications and workload characteristics as input and predict performance metrics and energy consumption. The evaluation uses R2 scores to measure prediction accuracy, with results showing high correlation between predicted and actual performance across all three architectures. The approach addresses the practical challenge of hardware evaluation without requiring exhaustive benchmarking of all possible hardware-workload combinations.

## Key Results
- Fully-connected network achieved R2 score of 0.96 for performance prediction
- CNN architecture achieved R2 score of 0.98 for performance prediction
- ResNet-inspired CNN achieved R2 score of 0.94 for performance prediction
- Energy consumption predictions showed similar high accuracy with R2 scores of 0.94-0.98

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works because hardware performance exhibits consistent patterns based on architectural specifications and workload characteristics. Deep learning models can capture these complex relationships through learned feature representations. The standardized nature of MLCommons benchmarks provides a consistent evaluation framework that enables model training. The hierarchical structure of CNNs particularly suits this problem as it can learn increasingly abstract representations of hardware configurations and their performance implications. The high R2 scores indicate that the relationship between hardware specifications and performance is sufficiently regular and predictable to be captured by neural networks.

Component map: Hardware specs & workload characteristics -> Network (FC/CNN/ResNet) -> Performance & energy predictions

Critical path: Data preprocessing → Model training → Prediction generation → R2 score evaluation

Design tradeoffs: Simpler models (FC) vs. complex models (CNN/ResNet) in terms of accuracy vs. training complexity; model generalization vs. dataset specificity

Failure signatures: Low R2 scores indicate poor feature engineering or insufficient training data; overfitting suggests need for regularization or more diverse training data

## Limitations
- Model generalizability to hardware configurations and workloads not represented in MLCommons dataset is uncertain
- Real-world performance variations due to power delivery, thermal conditions, and workload differences may not be captured
- Practical utility for procurement and deployment decisions with non-benchmark workloads requires validation

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Technical implementation of network architectures and comparative evaluation methodology
- Medium Confidence: Predictive performance accuracy on tested dataset distribution
- Low Confidence: Practical utility for real-world procurement and deployment decisions with diverse workloads
