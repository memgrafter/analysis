# LoSparse: Structured Compression of Large Language Models based on Low-Rank and Sparse Approximation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11222
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11222
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Combines low-rank approximation with structured pruning to achieve consistent 2-3% performance gains over iterative pruning and Movement pruning on NLU, QA, and NLG tasks at high sparsity levels

## Executive Summary
LoSparse is a structured compression method for transformer models that approximates weight matrices as the sum of a low-rank matrix and a sparse matrix. This hybrid approach aims to capture both coherent (low-rank) and incoherent (sparse) components of neural network parameters, combining the benefits of low-rank approximation and pruning while avoiding their individual limitations. The method is evaluated on natural language understanding (GLUE), question answering (SQuAD), and natural language generation (XSum, CNN/DailyMail) tasks, showing consistent performance improvements over iterative pruning and Movement pruning baselines, particularly at high sparsity levels.

## Method Summary
LoSparse decomposes weight matrices into low-rank and sparse components, where the low-rank part captures coherent neuron activations while the sparse part handles incoherent patterns. This structured compression approach alternates between low-rank approximation steps and structured pruning iterations, allowing the model to maintain important parameter relationships while achieving high compression ratios. The method can be combined with knowledge distillation and other compression techniques like CoFi for additional performance gains. The authors evaluate LoSparse across multiple NLP tasks and sparsity levels, demonstrating its effectiveness compared to standard pruning approaches.

## Key Results
- On MNLI, LoSparse achieves 2% higher accuracy than iterative pruning at 10% remaining parameters
- On SQuAD, LoSparse achieves 3% higher F1 score than iterative pruning at 5% remaining parameters
- On XSum, LoSparse achieves 2.99 higher ROUGE-1 score than iterative pruning at 30% remaining parameters

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LoSparse works by recognizing that transformer weight matrices contain both coherent and incoherent components. The low-rank approximation captures globally important patterns and relationships between neurons, while structured pruning removes redundant or task-irrelevant connections. By combining these two complementary approaches, LoSparse can achieve higher compression rates without sacrificing performance. The method is particularly effective at high sparsity levels where traditional pruning methods struggle to maintain accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: LoSparse operates on weight matrices within transformer layers, affecting attention mechanisms and feed-forward networks through its decomposition process.

**Critical Path**: Weight matrices → Low-rank + sparse decomposition → Iterative optimization → Compressed model deployment

**Design Tradeoffs**: LoSparse trades additional compression complexity for improved performance at high sparsity levels, compared to simpler pruning approaches.

**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation occurs when the low-rank component cannot adequately capture essential parameter relationships, or when excessive pruning removes critical sparse components.

**First Experiments**: 1) Apply LoSparse to a single BERT layer and measure parameter reduction vs. accuracy loss. 2) Compare low-rank only vs. sparse only compression on attention matrices. 3) Test different sparsity thresholds to find the optimal balance point.

## Limitations
- Performance gains diminish at very high sparsity levels, with relative improvements becoming less pronounced beyond 30% sparsity
- Evaluation limited to BERT-base-sized models, leaving scalability to larger architectures unverified
- Computational overhead of the structured compression process is not quantified or compared to baseline methods

## Confidence

**High confidence** in core technical contribution: The combination of low-rank approximation with structured pruning is well-motivated and theoretically sound.

**Medium confidence** in claimed advantages: While LoSparse consistently outperforms baselines, improvements are meaningful but not dramatic (2-3% gains).

**Low confidence** in generalization claims: Method not tested on decoder-only models, multilingual tasks, or non-English benchmarks.
