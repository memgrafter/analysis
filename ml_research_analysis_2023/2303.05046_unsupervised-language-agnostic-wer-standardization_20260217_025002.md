# Unsupervised Language agnostic WER Standardization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05046
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05046
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: 13.28% average WER reduction across four Indian languages

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of high Word Error Rate (WER) in ASR evaluation for languages with spelling variations, abbreviations, and compound words. The authors propose an unsupervised, language-agnostic system with two modules: spelling normalization and segmentation normalization. The system uses pronunciation, transliteration, and translation signals to identify equivalent word pairs and normalize them to a standard form. Experiments on four Indian languages show an average WER reduction of 13.28%, with high consistency between normalized WER and human perceived quality. The proposed system is scalable and effective across different language families.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes an unsupervised, language-agnostic system for Word Error Rate (WER) standardization that addresses high WER in ASR evaluation for languages with spelling variations, abbreviations, and compound words. The system consists of two modules: spelling normalization and segmentation normalization. It leverages pronunciation, transliteration, and translation signals to identify equivalent word pairs and normalize them to a standard form. The approach is evaluated on four Indian languages, demonstrating an average WER reduction of 13.28% and high consistency between normalized WER and human perceived quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Raw text -> Spelling normalization -> Segmentation normalization -> Normalized text

**Critical path**: The system processes text through spelling normalization first, then segmentation normalization, using self-supervised signals at each stage to identify and standardize equivalent forms.

**Design tradeoffs**: The unsupervised approach maximizes scalability but depends heavily on the quality and coverage of pronunciation dictionaries and translation resources. The system prioritizes language-agnostic applicability over language-specific optimization.

**Failure signatures**: False normalization of distinct words to the same canonical form, missed normalization opportunities due to incomplete pronunciation dictionaries, and incorrect segmentation of compound words leading to WER inflation.

**Three first experiments**:
1. Test normalization coverage on a held-out set of known spelling variations
2. Evaluate false normalization rate by comparing canonical forms against reference standards
3. Measure segmentation accuracy for compound words in morphologically rich languages

## Limitations
- Performance on non-Indic languages with different orthographic and morphological properties remains unproven
- Normalization quality heavily depends on pronunciation dictionary coverage and accuracy
- Potential for false normalization where distinct words are incorrectly mapped to the same canonical form

## Confidence
- Unsupervised effectiveness: Medium - Promising results but limited to four closely-related languages
- Language agnosticism: Low - Claims require validation on typologically diverse languages
- WER improvement significance: High - Substantial and consistent reductions supported by multiple evaluation methods

## Next Checks
1. Cross-linguistic validation: Test the system on diverse languages including agglutinative languages, languages with complex compounding, and logographic writing systems
2. Error analysis framework: Develop framework to quantify false normalization rates and measure semantic preservation of normalized forms
3. Resource dependency study: Conduct experiments varying quality and coverage of pronunciation dictionaries and translation resources to establish minimum requirements for different language families
