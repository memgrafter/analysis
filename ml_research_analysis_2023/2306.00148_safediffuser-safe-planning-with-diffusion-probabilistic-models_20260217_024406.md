# SafeDiffuser: Safe Planning with Diffusion Probabilistic Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00148
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00148
- Reference count: 13
- Key outcome: Diffusion models for data-driven planning face challenges in ensuring safety guarantees, limiting their application in safety-critical domains. This work proposes SafeDiffuser, a method that incorporates control barrier functions into the diffusion denoising process to guarantee safe planning trajectories. The approach introduces finite-time diffusion invariance, embedding safety specifications directly into the diffusion time steps. Three variants—robust-safe, relaxed-safe, and time-varying-safe diffusers—are developed to address local trap issues during planning. Experiments on maze navigation, robot locomotion, and manipulation tasks demonstrate that SafeDiffuser consistently satisfies safety specifications while maintaining or improving planning performance compared to baseline methods, with robust guarantees over approaches like classifier-based guidance or truncation.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion probabilistic models have shown promise in data-driven planning but lack safety guarantees critical for real-world deployment. SafeDiffuser addresses this gap by integrating control barrier functions (CBFs) directly into the diffusion denoising process, enabling safe trajectory generation with theoretical guarantees. The method introduces finite-time diffusion invariance, allowing safety specifications to be embedded at each diffusion time step. Three algorithmic variants—robust-safe, relaxed-safe, and time-varying-safe diffusers—are proposed to handle different safety scenarios and avoid local traps during planning. Experiments demonstrate that SafeDiffuser consistently satisfies safety constraints while maintaining or improving planning performance compared to baseline methods across navigation, locomotion, and manipulation tasks.

## Method Summary
SafeDiffuser incorporates control barrier functions into diffusion probabilistic models to ensure safety during planning. The core innovation is finite-time diffusion invariance, which embeds safety specifications directly into the denoising process at each diffusion time step. Three variants are developed: robust-safe diffuser for strict safety guarantees, relaxed-safe diffuser for flexible constraint satisfaction, and time-varying-safe diffuser for dynamic safety specifications. The method modifies the standard diffusion denoising process by incorporating CBF-based guidance that steers samples toward safe regions while maintaining the generative capabilities of the underlying diffusion model. This approach provides theoretical safety guarantees while addressing common issues like local trap states that can arise during planning with standard diffusion models.

## Key Results
- SafeDiffuser consistently satisfies safety specifications across maze navigation, robot locomotion, and manipulation tasks
- Planning performance matches or exceeds baseline methods while providing safety guarantees
- Robust-safe variant shows superior performance compared to classifier-based guidance and truncation approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SafeDiffuser works by integrating control barrier functions into the diffusion denoising process, creating a feedback loop that enforces safety constraints at each denoising step. The finite-time diffusion invariance ensures that safety specifications are satisfied throughout the entire denoising trajectory, not just at the final output. By modifying the denoising gradients with CBF-based guidance, the method steers the diffusion process away from unsafe regions while preserving the underlying data distribution learned by the diffusion model. The three variants provide flexibility in balancing safety strictness versus planning efficiency, with the time-varying approach enabling adaptation to dynamic safety constraints during execution.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Diffusion Model -> Denoising Process -> CBF Integration -> Safety-Guaranteed Output

**Critical Path**
Input noise → Iterative denoising steps → CBF-guided gradient modification → Safe trajectory generation

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Strict safety guarantees (robust-safe) vs. planning efficiency and flexibility (relaxed-safe)
- Computational overhead of CBF integration vs. safety performance
- Fixed safety specifications vs. dynamic adaptability (time-varying variant)

**Failure Signatures**
- Divergence in denoising process due to conflicting CBF constraints
- Local trap states where safety constraints prevent reaching goal regions
- Performance degradation when CBF approximation errors accumulate

**3 First Experiments**
1. 2D maze navigation with static obstacle constraints to validate basic safety guarantees
2. Robot locomotion with dynamic safety margins to test robustness-safe variant
3. Manipulation task with changing safety specifications to evaluate time-varying-safe approach
