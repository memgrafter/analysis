# Witscript 3: A Hybrid AI System for Improvising Jokes in a Conversation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02695
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02695
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: Witscript 3 generates jokes that human evaluators identify as jokes 44% of the time, outperforming a pure LLM baseline

## Executive Summary
Witscript 3 is a hybrid AI system that generates contextually integrated jokes in conversation by combining a large language model (GPT-3) with explicit humor algorithms. The system uses three joke production mechanisms - wordplay, common-sense knowledge, and a proprietary method - to generate candidate jokes, then selects the best one to output. Human evaluators rated Witscript 3's responses as jokes 44% of the time, significantly higher than a pure LLM baseline system (GPT-LOL at 33.8%). This demonstrates that hybrid neural-symbolic approaches can improve computational humor generation compared to end-to-end LLM approaches.

## Method Summary
Witscript 3 uses GPT-3 with prompt chaining to execute the Basic Joke-Writing Algorithm, decomposing joke creation into discrete steps: topic selection, handle extraction, association generation, punch line candidate creation, angle generation, and candidate selection. The system generates three punch line candidates using different approaches - wordplay through NLP tools, common-sense knowledge via GPT-3, and a proprietary mechanism - then selects the best candidate based on funniness scoring. The approach trades computational cost (multiple GPT-3 calls) for improved joke quality and controllability, using symbolic humor algorithms to guide the neural components.

## Key Results
- Human evaluators judged Witscript 3's responses as jokes 44% of the time versus GPT-LOL's 33.8%
- The hybrid neural-symbolic approach outperformed pure LLM-based joke generation
- The system demonstrates progress toward AI systems that can improvise jokes as effectively as non-expert humans

## Limitations

- The system's performance evaluation is based on a very small test set of only 13 sentences, limiting generalizability
- The proprietary third joke production mechanism is described but not detailed, making it impossible to fully assess its contribution
- The human evaluation involved only 8 participants rating 30 outputs, providing limited statistical power

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The system architecture and prompt chaining approach are clearly described and reproducible. The baseline comparison methodology is transparent and allows for meaningful comparison. The claim that hybrid neural-symbolic approaches can improve joke generation is supported by the experimental results.
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of the three distinct joke production mechanisms is demonstrated, but the relative contribution of each mechanism (especially the proprietary one) is unclear. The improvement over pure LLM approaches is demonstrated but could benefit from testing on larger datasets.
- **Low confidence**: The scalability of the approach to real-world conversational settings is not demonstrated, as the test set is limited to 13 carefully selected sentences. The generalizability of the 44% success rate to different domains is unknown.

## Next Checks

1. Scale up the human evaluation to at least 100 sentences from diverse conversational contexts, using 20+ evaluators to obtain statistically significant results and confidence intervals for the success rates.

2. A/B test the three mechanisms independently by running identical inputs through each mechanism separately and comparing their individual performance, then testing the complete system with and without the proprietary mechanism to quantify its contribution.

3. Benchmark against human performance by having non-expert humans attempt to generate jokes for the same 13 sentences (or a larger set) and comparing success rates, response quality, and generation time to establish whether the AI system truly matches human capabilities.
