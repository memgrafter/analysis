# On the Susceptibility and Robustness of Time Series Models through Adversarial Attack and Defense

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03703
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03703
- Reference count: 13
- All tested time series models (LSTM, GRU, RNN, CNN variants) are vulnerable to adversarial attacks

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically evaluates the robustness of seven time series models against adversarial attacks across multiple regression and classification datasets. The study compares three attack methods (FGSM, BIM, PGD) and one defense strategy (adversarial training) to understand vulnerability patterns. Results demonstrate that while all models are susceptible to attacks, certain architectures like GRU and RNN show higher vulnerability, and adversarial training can improve robustness, particularly for LSTM and GRU models.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates seven time series models (LSTM, Stacked LSTM, GRU, RNN, CNN, CNN-LSTM, ConvLSTM) on five regression datasets (Power Consumption, Wind Speed, Temperature, Humidity, Hurricane) and one classification dataset (Eye State). Three adversarial attack methods are tested: FGSM, BIM, and PGD, along with adversarial training as the defense mechanism. The experiments measure model performance degradation under attacks and improvement with defense, focusing on susceptibility patterns across different architectures.

## Key Results
- All seven time series models show vulnerability to adversarial attacks
- GRU and RNN models are the most susceptible to adversarial perturbations
- FGSM attack method proves most effective across datasets
- Adversarial training significantly improves model robustness, especially for LSTM and GRU

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: Time series models learn temporal dependencies that create specific vulnerability patterns. Sequential architectures like RNN and GRU are particularly susceptible because their hidden state accumulates perturbations over time steps, amplifying the adversarial effect. Convolutional models may be more robust due to local receptive fields that limit perturbation propagation. The effectiveness of FGSM suggests that models are highly sensitive to gradient-based perturbations in their input space.
