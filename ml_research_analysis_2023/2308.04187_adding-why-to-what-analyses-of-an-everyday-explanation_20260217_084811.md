# Adding Why to What? Analyses of an Everyday Explanation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2308.04187
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04187
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Explainers predominantly address Architecture before Relevance in naturalistic explanations of technological artifacts

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how naturalistic explanations of technological artifacts evolve, focusing on the dual nature theory (Architecture and Relevance). Using the board game Quarto! as the explanandum, the study analyzes 20 dyadic explanations through qualitative content analysis and video recall. Results show that explainers predominantly address the Architecture first, especially in the initial stages, before shifting to Relevance. This pattern is justified by the need to structure the explanation and lay out the basic components before addressing more complex aspects. The study highlights that the dual nature theory provides a useful framework for analyzing and comparing explanations, offering insights for the development of synthetic explanations in XAI.

## Method Summary
The study employed qualitative content analysis of 20 dyadic explanations of the board game Quarto!, where explainers (EX) were tasked with teaching the game without the physical artifact present. Explanations were transcribed using GAT2 and coded using the dual nature theory framework (Architecture and Relevance). Video recall sessions with EX were conducted to explore their reasoning for explanation content choices, and these justifications were analyzed using a deductive category system. Pattern analysis across explanations identified common structures and shifts between the two aspects of the dual nature.

## Key Results
- Explainers predominantly addressed Architecture aspects before shifting to Relevance, especially in early explanation stages
- The dual nature theory effectively captured the evolution of explanations, with Architecture providing structure and Relevance addressing understanding
- Shifts between Architecture and Relevance were triggered by explanation goals, emerging misunderstandings, and knowledge needs of the explainee

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Natural explanations follow a predictable dual nature pattern where Architecture is addressed first, followed by Relevance.
- Mechanism: The dual nature theory provides a structural framework that explainers intuitively use to organize information, starting with concrete components (Architecture) before moving to abstract purposes (Relevance).
- Core assumption: The dual nature theory accurately captures how humans naturally structure explanations of technological artifacts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show that explainers predominantly address the Architecture first, especially in the initial stages, before shifting to Relevance."
  - [section] "Especially in the early stages of the explanations, the aspects of the Architecture were addressed more frequently than in the middle and last third"
- Break condition: If explainers consistently start with Relevance or show no clear pattern in explaining technological artifacts, this mechanism would need revision.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Explainers use "laying out the tools" strategy - describing physical components first to create a mental framework for understanding complex functional aspects.
- Mechanism: By establishing concrete physical knowledge first, explainers create a scaffold that makes abstract Relevance information more accessible and comprehensible to explainees.
- Core assumption: Physical knowledge serves as a prerequisite foundation for understanding functional knowledge in technological artifacts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "EX regarded it as important that the explanation has a certain structure, meaning that some aspects were believed to be prerequisites that need to be explained, before explaining further details"
  - [abstract] "Reasoning in the video recalls indicated that EX regarded the focus on the Architecture as important for structuring the explanation initially by explaining the basic components before focusing on more complex, intangible aspects"
- Break condition: If research shows explainees can understand Relevance without first understanding Architecture, or if alternative explanation strategies prove equally or more effective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Shifts between Architecture and Relevance are dynamically triggered by explainee knowledge needs and explainer intentions rather than following a rigid sequence.
- Mechanism: Interactive dialogue creates emergent patterns where shifts occur based on questions, misunderstandings, and evolving understanding, rather than predetermined order.
- Core assumption: Explanation is a dynamic, interactive process responsive to real-time feedback rather than a scripted delivery.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Shifting between addressing the two sides was justified by explanation goals, emerging misunderstandings, and the knowledge needs of the explainee"
  - [abstract] "Shifting between addressing the two sides was justified by explanation goals, emerging misunderstandings, and the knowledge needs of the explainee"
- Break condition: If patterns show rigid adherence to Architecture-then-Relevance sequence regardless of explainee responses, or if shifts occur randomly without clear triggers.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do explanations of more complex technological artifacts differ from those of simpler ones like board games?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study focuses on a board game, Quarto!, and the authors suggest that explaining more complex artifacts might require different strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide evidence or analysis of explanations for more complex artifacts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of explanations for both simple and complex technological artifacts, analyzing the strategies used and the effectiveness of the explanations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the dual nature theory apply equally well to explanations of digital artifacts compared to physical ones?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that understanding the architecture of digital artifacts might not be as necessary as for physical ones, implying a potential difference in the application of the theory.
- Why unresolved: The study only examines explanations of a physical game, leaving the application to digital artifacts untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Research on explanations of digital artifacts using the dual nature theory as a framework, comparing the results to those of physical artifacts.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the level of understanding of the explainee influence the structure and content of the explanation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors discuss the knowledge needs of the explainee as a factor influencing explanation content, but do not provide specific evidence on how different levels of understanding affect the explanation.
- Why unresolved: The study does not control for or measure the explainee's prior knowledge or level of understanding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that manipulate the explainee's prior knowledge and measure the resulting changes in the explanation's structure and content.

## Limitations

- Controlled lab setting with a single board game limits ecological validity
- Absence of physical artifact may have influenced explanation strategies
- Qualitative coding approach relies on validity of dual nature theory framework

## Confidence

- Medium confidence in Architecture-first pattern finding, though generalizability is limited by single artifact type and controlled setting

## Next Checks

1. Replicate content analysis with different technological artifacts (both physical and digital) to test the robustness of the Architecture-first pattern across domains

3. Analyze the same Quarto! explanations using alternative theoretical frameworks (e.g., information gap theory or cognitive load theory) to assess whether the dual nature pattern remains salient
