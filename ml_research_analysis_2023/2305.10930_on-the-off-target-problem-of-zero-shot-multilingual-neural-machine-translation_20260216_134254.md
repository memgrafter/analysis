# On the Off-Target Problem of Zero-Shot Multilingual Neural Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10930
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10930
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 13
- This work analyzes the off-target problem in zero-shot multilingual neural machine translation, where translations are produced in the wrong target language. The study finds that failing to encode discriminative target language signals leads to off-target translations and that higher lexical similarity between languages correlates with higher off-target rates. The authors propose Language Aware Vocabulary Sharing (LA VS), a method that constructs a multilingual vocabulary by splitting shared tokens into language-specific ones, thereby increasing the KL-divergence between languages. Experiments on a benchmark with 11 languages show that LA VS reduces the off-target rate from 29% to 8% and improves the average BLEU score by 1.9 points without additional training costs or sacrificing supervised direction performance. The method is also effective on larger datasets like OPUS-100, improving English-to-many translation performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the off-target problem in zero-shot multilingual neural machine translation (MNMT), where translations are produced in the wrong target language. The authors identify that when languages share many subwords, the model struggles to distinguish between target languages during generation, especially in zero-shot scenarios. They propose Language Aware Vocabulary Sharing (LA VS), a method that splits shared tokens into language-specific ones, increasing the KL-divergence between languages and reducing off-target rates. Experiments on WMT'10 and OPUS-100 datasets show significant improvements in both off-target rates and BLEU scores without additional training costs.

## Method Summary
The authors propose Language Aware Vocabulary Sharing (LA VS), a method that constructs a multilingual vocabulary by splitting shared tokens into language-specific ones, thereby increasing the KL-divergence between languages. The method uses Sentencepiece tokenizer to learn a shared vocabulary, then applies an algorithm to add language-specific tokens by splitting shared tokens with high frequency across languages. This approach aims to improve language discrimination in the decoder by reducing token overlap between target languages. The method is evaluated on WMT'10 and OPUS-100 datasets using Transformer-Big models with target language identifiers added at input.

## Key Results
- Off-target rate reduced from 29% to 8% on Flores-101 dataset
- Average BLEU score improved by 1.9 points on 90 zero-shot translation directions
- LA VS method effective on both WMT'10 and OPUS-100 datasets
- Improvements achieved without additional training costs or sacrificing supervised direction performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Encoder fails to encode discriminative target language signal → off-target translations
- Mechanism: When lexical similarity between languages is high, the encoder outputs representations that are too similar across target languages. The decoder cannot distinguish which target language to generate because the representations overlap significantly in hidden space.
- Core assumption: The decoder relies on the encoder's output to determine the correct target language, and this signal degrades when languages share many tokens.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "failure in encoding discriminative target language signal will lead to off-target"
  - [section 3.3] "representations belonging to different directions are completely in chaos and they are also mixed"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: corpus neighbors discuss variations in zero-shot performance but don't directly validate the encoder representation degradation claim.
- Break condition: If the decoder can reliably infer target language from other signals (like language tags) without relying on encoder output, this mechanism weakens.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High lexical similarity between languages correlates with higher off-target rates
- Mechanism: When two languages share many subwords, the model treats them as more similar during training. This creates confusion about which language to generate, especially in zero-shot scenarios where supervision is absent.
- Core assumption: Token distribution similarity directly impacts model's ability to distinguish languages during generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a closer lexical distance (i.e., KL-divergence) between two languages' vocabularies is related with a higher off-target rate"
  - [section 3.4] "language pair which has higher lexical similarity from target to source may have a higher chance to encounter off-target"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence: corpus includes papers on linguistic similarity and zero-shot learning, supporting the general premise.
- Break condition: If the model can learn robust language-specific patterns despite shared vocabulary, or if other features dominate language discrimination.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Shared tokens in decoder weaken target language discrimination
- Mechanism: When multiple target languages share the same output tokens, the decoder cannot learn strong associations between specific tokens and specific target languages. This weakens the mapping from hidden states to target language generation.
- Core assumption: The decoder learns token-to-language associations during supervised training, and shared tokens dilute these associations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.5] "token overlap for different target in output space is harmful for zero-shot translation"
  - [section 3.6] "without any vocabulary sharing among languages, the model will learn a wrong correlation between input language and output language"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: corpus neighbors discuss shortcut learning and off-target issues but don't specifically validate the shared token mechanism.
- Break condition: If the model relies primarily on positional or contextual cues rather than token-to-language associations for language discrimination.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Source text + language tag → Encoder → Hidden states → Decoder + language tag → Target text
- Critical path: Source text → Encoder → Hidden states → Decoder → Target text
  - Language tag flows through both encoder and decoder
  - Vocabulary determines available tokens at each generation step
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Shared vocabulary: Fewer parameters, but higher off-target risk
  - Separate vocabularies: Lower off-target risk, but more parameters and potential loss of cross-lingual transfer
  - LA VS: Balances parameter efficiency with improved language discrimination
- Failure signatures:
  - High off-target rate (>20%) in zero-shot directions
  - Encoder representations for different targets overlap significantly in visualization
  - Decoder generates tokens that don't match target language tag
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline shared vocab model on WMT'10, measure off-target rate on Flores-101
  2. Implement separate decoder vocab, retrain, compare off-target rate and BLEU
  3. Implement LA VS with varying numbers of language-specific tokens, evaluate impact on off-target rate and BLEU

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed Language-Aware Vocabulary Sharing (LA VS) method scale to datasets with significantly more languages (e.g., beyond the 11 languages in the WMT'10 dataset or the 42 languages with 1M data in OPUS-100)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that LA VS is tested on the OPUS-100 dataset, which includes 100 languages, but the detailed performance for all language pairs is not provided.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides detailed results for a subset of languages and directions in OPUS-100, and does not explore the full scalability of LA VS to larger multilingual settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluation of LA VS on datasets with a much larger number of languages, including detailed performance metrics for all language pairs, would clarify its scalability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the number of language-specific tokens added by LA VS on the overall model performance, and is there an optimal number for different language pairs or resource levels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores the effect of adding different numbers of language-specific tokens (10k, 20k, 50k, 212k) on the off-target rate and BLEU score in the WMT'10 dataset, but does not provide a detailed analysis for different language pairs or resource levels.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate how the optimal number of language-specific tokens varies across different language pairs or resource levels, which could affect the method's effectiveness.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the performance of LA VS with different numbers of language-specific tokens for various language pairs and resource levels would provide insights into the optimal configuration.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does LA VS interact with other techniques aimed at improving zero-shot translation, such as back-translation or constrained decoding, and are there any potential trade-offs or synergies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that LA VS can be combined with back-translation and constrained decoding, but does not provide a detailed analysis of their interactions or potential trade-offs.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the interactions between LA VS and other techniques in depth, which could reveal potential synergies or trade-offs that affect the overall performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study of the interactions between LA VS and other zero-shot translation techniques, including detailed performance comparisons and analysis of trade-offs, would clarify their combined effectiveness.

## Limitations

- The paper focuses primarily on encoder representations but doesn't investigate whether decoder-side representations show similar patterns
- The analysis assumes language tags alone are sufficient supervision, but doesn't explore scenarios where tag prediction might fail
- The method's effectiveness on languages with different typological properties or truly low-resource language pairs remains untested

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The empirical finding that LA VS reduces off-target rates from 29% to 8% and improves BLEU scores by 1.9 points. The experimental methodology is sound, the datasets are well-established benchmarks, and the results are consistent across multiple language pairs and datasets.

**Medium Confidence**: The theoretical mechanism linking shared tokens to off-target problems. While the correlation is well-demonstrated and the proposed solution works, the exact causal pathway through which shared tokens cause confusion requires further investigation. The paper provides strong intuition but could benefit from more rigorous analysis of representation similarity and attention patterns.

**Medium Confidence**: The generalizability of LA VS beyond the tested language pairs and datasets. The method shows promise on WMT'10 and OPUS-100, but its effectiveness on languages with different typological properties or on truly low-resource language pairs remains untested.

## Next Checks

1. **Representation Similarity Analysis**: Compute quantitative measures of encoder representation similarity (e.g., centered kernel alignment or canonical correlation) between different target languages in both baseline and LA VS models. This would provide concrete evidence for the claim that shared vocabulary leads to overlapping representations and that LA VS increases separation.

2. **Ablation on Language Tag Dependency**: Train models with corrupted or randomized language tags during training to test whether the decoder truly relies on the tag for language discrimination or whether it learns to infer the target language from other signals. This would validate the core assumption about the decoder's dependence on explicit supervision.

3. **KL-divergence vs. Off-target Rate Correlation**: Systematically vary the number of language-specific tokens in LA VS and measure both the KL-divergence between language distributions and the resulting off-target rates. This would establish whether the relationship is monotonic and whether there's a point of diminishing returns, providing stronger evidence for the proposed mechanism.
