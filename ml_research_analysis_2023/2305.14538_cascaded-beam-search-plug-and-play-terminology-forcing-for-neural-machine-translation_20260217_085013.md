# Cascaded Beam Search: Plug-and-Play Terminology-Forcing For Neural Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14538
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14538
- Reference count: 22
- Introduces a plug-and-play approach for terminology-forcing in NMT that achieves near 100% terminology appearance without training

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Cascaded Beam Search, a novel approach for terminology-forcing in neural machine translation that requires no training or fine-tuning. The method combines logit manipulation to boost target terminology probabilities with a cascading beam structure that groups hypotheses by terminology count. Evaluated against WMT21 terminology translation task winners, the approach achieves near-perfect terminology appearance while maintaining the BLEU score of the underlying generalist multilingual model, outperforming all competitors in terminology metrics without requiring domain-specific adaptation.

## Method Summary
Cascaded Beam Search introduces a plug-and-play approach for terminology-forcing in neural machine translation. The method works by manipulating the logits of the translation model to increase the probability of target terminologies appearing in translations. It employs a cascading beam setup where beams are organized into groups based on the number of terminologies they contain. During decoding, the algorithm prioritizes beams with higher terminology counts while maintaining translation quality through strategic beam pruning and recombination. This approach eliminates the need for specialized training or fine-tuning, making it compatible with any existing NMT model.

## Key Results
- Achieves near 100% terminology appearance rates in translations
- Maintains BLEU score of underlying generalist multilingual model
- Outperforms all WMT21 terminology translation task winners in terminology appearance metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by strategically manipulating the probability distribution over target vocabulary during beam search. By increasing the logits associated with target terminologies, the method biases the translation model toward including these terms while still allowing the underlying model's knowledge to guide overall translation quality. The cascading beam structure ensures that hypotheses with more terminologies are prioritized during search, creating a systematic way to enforce terminology constraints without completely overriding the model's natural translation tendencies.

## Foundational Learning

**Logit Manipulation**: Why needed - to bias probability distributions toward target terminologies without retraining; Quick check - verify that manipulated logits still produce valid probability distributions after softmax.

**Beam Search**: Why needed - to maintain multiple translation hypotheses and explore the search space efficiently; Quick check - ensure beam width is sufficient to capture diverse translation options while controlling computational cost.

**Cascading Beam Structure**: Why needed - to systematically prioritize translations containing more terminologies; Quick check - validate that terminology count grouping effectively balances terminology enforcement with translation quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: NMT Model -> Logit Manipulator -> Cascaded Beam Searcher -> Output Translator

**Critical Path**: Input text → NMT model forward pass → Logit manipulation (α scaling) → Beam organization by terminology count → Beam pruning and recombination → Final translation output

**Design Tradeoffs**: The approach trades computational overhead during inference (multiple beam groups) for elimination of training requirements. Higher α values increase terminology appearance but may reduce translation quality. Larger beam widths improve solution quality but increase computation time.

**Failure Signatures**: Terminology dropout occurs when beams with high terminology counts are pruned early. Translation quality degradation manifests when α is set too high, causing the model to force inappropriate terminology usage. Performance bottlenecks appear when beam width is insufficient for the cascading structure.

**3 First Experiments**:
1. Vary α parameter from 0.1 to 5.0 to find optimal terminology-appearance/quality tradeoff
2. Test with different beam widths (5, 10, 20) to assess impact on terminology coverage
3. Compare against baseline beam search with no logit manipulation on terminology test sets

## Limitations
- Evaluation primarily on WMT21 benchmark limits generalizability to other domains
- Parameter sensitivity to α requires careful tuning across different models
- Focus on terminology appearance metrics may overlook other quality dimensions
