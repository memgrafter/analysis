# Learning to Defer with Limited Expert Predictions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07306
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07306
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Six expert predictions per class suffice for learning-to-defer systems to exceed both AI and human performance

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning-to-defer systems in human-AI collaboration, where AI systems must decide when to defer tasks to human experts. The core problem is that these systems typically require extensive expert predictions to accurately model expert capabilities, which becomes impractical when experts frequently change or data labeling is expensive. The authors propose a novel three-step approach that dramatically reduces expert prediction requirements by learning to generate artificial expert predictions from limited initial data. Their method enables learning-to-defer algorithms to achieve performance close to using complete expert predictions while requiring only a small fraction of the original data.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a three-step approach to reduce expert prediction requirements in learning-to-defer systems. First, they train an embedding model using ground truth labels to extract meaningful feature representations from the data. Second, they train an expertise predictor model that approximates expert capabilities using these embeddings, learning the relationship between data features and expert decision patterns. Third, they use the expertise predictor to generate artificial expert predictions for instances not labeled by human experts. These artificial predictions are combined with available human predictions to train learning-to-defer algorithms. The approach is evaluated on CIFAR-100 with synthetic human experts and NIH chest X-ray dataset with real radiologists, demonstrating that six expert predictions per class can achieve performance comparable to using complete expert data.

## Key Results
- Learning-to-defer algorithms achieve performance close to complete expert prediction sets using only six expert predictions per class
- The approach works on both synthetic CIFAR-100 dataset and real NIH chest X-ray dataset with radiologists
- Artificial expert predictions generated through embedding-based expertise modeling effectively capture expert behavior patterns
- Performance exceeds that of both individual AI and human expert components in the evaluated scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging embedding models to capture the underlying structure of expert decision-making. By training expertise predictors on these embeddings, the system learns to generalize expert behavior patterns from limited examples. This generalization enables the generation of artificial expert predictions that maintain the statistical properties of real expert decisions. The key insight is that expert expertise can be modeled as a function of input features, allowing the system to predict expert responses on unlabeled instances based on learned patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Embedding Model -> Expertise Predictor -> Artificial Prediction Generator -> Learning-to-Defer System

**Critical Path:** Ground truth labels → Embedding model training → Expertise predictor training → Artificial prediction generation → Learning-to-defer training → Performance evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs:** The approach trades off the fidelity of artificial predictions against the cost savings from reduced expert involvement. Using synthetic expert data may introduce approximation errors but enables practical deployment. The embedding-based approach requires ground truth labels but enables expertise modeling without extensive expert interaction.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance may indicate that expert behavior cannot be adequately captured through embeddings, suggesting highly idiosyncratic or context-dependent expertise. Failure may also occur when expert decision patterns are too complex to generalize from limited examples.

## Limitations
- Reliance on synthetic CIFAR-100 dataset for core methodology validation, where expert behavior is artificially generated rather than representing real human expertise patterns
- Limited real-world validation with only four radiologists in the NIH chest X-ray dataset, restricting generalizability across diverse expert populations
- Assumption that expert predictions can be approximated through embedding-based modeling may not hold for experts with highly idiosyncratic decision patterns
- Requirement for ground truth labels to train the embedding model, which may not be available in all practical settings

## Confidence

**High confidence:**
- The methodology for generating artificial expert predictions using embeddings and expertise predictors is technically sound and well-explained

**Medium confidence:**
- The performance improvements demonstrated on synthetic data translate to real-world settings with similar characteristics
- The claim that six expert predictions per class are sufficient across different datasets and expert types
