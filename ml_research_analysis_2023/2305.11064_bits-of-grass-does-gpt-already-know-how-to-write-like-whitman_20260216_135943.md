# Bits of Grass: Does GPT already know how to write like Whitman?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11064
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11064
- Reference count: 1
- Primary result: Prompt engineering (zero-shot and many-shot) cannot generate poetry in Whitman's style; fine-tuning remains necessary.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether GPT-3.5, GPT-3.5-turbo, and GPT-4 can generate poetry in Walt Whitman's style using only prompt engineering. Despite testing prompts with up to 17 examples and 8192 tokens, none of the models successfully replicated Whitman's poetic style. Binary classifiers trained to distinguish between GPT-generated poems and Whitman's originals achieved nearly 100% accuracy, indicating the models' outputs were easily distinguishable from authentic works. The findings suggest that current prompt-based approaches are insufficient for style-specific poetry generation, and fine-tuning remains the recommended method.

## Method Summary
The study generated 300 poems from each GPT model (GPT-3.5, GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4) using zero-shot prompts with Whitman poem summaries. Additionally, GPT-4 generated 300 poems using a 17-poem many-shot prompt (8192 tokens). A binary classifier (fine-tuned GPT-3 Babbage) was trained to distinguish between GPT-generated poems and Whitman's originals, with 200/100 train/validation splits per label. The classifier's accuracy served as the primary evaluation metric.

## Key Results
- GPT models consistently generated poems following 4-line stanza rhyming structures, unlike Whitman's style
- Binary classifiers achieved nearly 100% accuracy in distinguishing GPT-generated poems from Whitman's originals
- Previously fine-tuned GPT-3 models achieved 61.5% accuracy, showing more promise than prompt-based approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Zero-shot and few-shot prompting fail to capture Whitman's poetic style due to the model's reliance on statistical patterns rather than true stylistic understanding.
- Mechanism: GPT models trained on large corpora develop strong statistical associations between poetic structures and certain prompts. However, when asked to replicate a specific author's style, they default to common patterns (e.g., four-line stanzas with rhyme) because these are more prevalent in their training data.
- Core assumption: The model's training data contains more examples of generic poetic structures than of specific authorial styles.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "without fine-tuning, even when provided with the maximum number of 17 poem examples (8192 tokens) in the prompt, these models do not generate poetry in the desired style."
  - [section]: "For example, Walt Whitman's poetry does not follow the 'four lines in a stanza' structure, and does not use rhyming (Bohan 1995). The majority of poems that we generated 'in the style of Walt Whitman' do follow the 'four lines in a stanza' structure and use rhyming."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT models can retrieve original Whitman poems but struggle to generate new ones in his style, indicating a gap between memorization and generative understanding.
- Mechanism: The model has learned to recognize and reproduce exact text from its training data (retrieval), but generating new content in a specific style requires a deeper understanding of stylistic elements that isn't captured through prompting alone.
- Core assumption: Retrieval and generation rely on different aspects of the model's learned representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "GPT's ability to retrieve the text of the poems is influenced by the number of times the poem appeared in the training dataset. Regardless, GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4 are, in many cases, able to retrieve the requested poems, and therefore, we can assume that GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4 'know' the style of this poet."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning is necessary because it allows the model to adjust its weights to prioritize stylistic features over generic patterns.
- Mechanism: During fine-tuning, the model's weights are adjusted to minimize the loss on a specific dataset, allowing it to learn the unique features of Whitman's style. This process is not achievable through prompting alone, which only influences the model's output at the inference stage.
- Core assumption: Fine-tuning can effectively teach the model to prioritize stylistic features over generic patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "fine-tuning remains the recommended method."
  - [section]: "In contrast, previously fine-tuned GPT-3 models achieved 61.5% accuracy, showing more promise."

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does fine-tuning GPT-3.5-turbo or GPT-4 models produce poetry in the style of specific authors that can be distinguished from the original works with less than 50% accuracy by binary classifiers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that prompt-based approaches on GPT-3.5, GPT-3.5-turbo, and GPT-4 models fail to produce poetry indistinguishable from original works, while previously fine-tuned GPT-3 models achieved 61.5% accuracy. The paper concludes that fine-tuning remains the recommended approach.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluated prompt-based approaches on GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4, and only evaluated previously fine-tuned GPT-3 models. It did not test whether fine-tuning these newer models would improve performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing binary classifier accuracy rates below 50% when comparing poetry generated from fine-tuned GPT-3.5-turbo or GPT-4 models against original works.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific aspects of poetry style (e.g., meter, rhyme scheme, vocabulary, thematic content) are most responsible for the failure of prompt-based approaches to replicate specific authors' styles?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that generated poems consistently follow a "four lines in a stanza" structure with rhyme, which doesn't match Whitman's style. However, it doesn't systematically analyze which stylistic elements are most problematic.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides qualitative observations about structural differences but doesn't conduct a detailed analysis of which specific stylistic features distinguish generated from original poetry.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed stylistic analysis comparing generated and original poems, identifying specific features (meter, rhyme, vocabulary, themes, etc.) that differ most significantly.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would combining fine-tuning with prompt engineering produce better results than either approach alone for generating poetry in specific styles?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests both zero-shot/many-shot prompting and previously fine-tuned GPT-3 models, finding neither approach produces satisfactory results when used alone. It mentions experimenting with prompt structures but finding "no meaningful differences in output quality."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested prompt engineering on default models and previously fine-tuned models separately, but didn't test hybrid approaches combining both techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing poetry quality from models using prompt engineering alone, fine-tuning alone, and combined approaches, measured by binary classifier accuracy or human evaluation.

## Limitations
- The study focuses exclusively on one author (Walt Whitman), limiting generalizability to other authors or stylistic domains
- The binary classifier approach may not capture nuanced differences in style that human evaluators might detect
- The maximum context length used (8192 tokens with 17 examples) may still be insufficient to convey the full complexity of Whitman's style

## Confidence
- High Confidence: GPT models cannot reliably generate poetry in Whitman's style through prompt engineering alone (supported by consistent results across multiple models and strong classifier performance)
- Medium Confidence: Fine-tuning is necessary for style-specific poetry generation (limited comparison to one fine-tuned baseline, other solutions not explored)
- Low Confidence: Mechanism explanations for why prompt-based approaches fail (plausible hypotheses not empirically validated)

## Next Checks
