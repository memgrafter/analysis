# Vision Based Machine Learning Algorithms for Out-of-Distribution Generalisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06975
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06975
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: Domain generalization methods significantly outperform conventional CNNs on out-of-distribution datasets, with larger CNN models with skip connections showing better generalization than smaller models.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically compares domain generalization (DG) methods with conventional deep learning models on out-of-distribution generalization tasks using PACS and Office-Home benchmarks. The research evaluates 9 DG algorithms and 7 CNN architectures, finding that DG methods achieve superior performance on unseen domains compared to standard CNNs. Among CNNs, larger models with skip connections (ResNet50, InceptionV3) demonstrate notably better generalization capabilities. The work highlights the limitations of traditional deep learning approaches for OOD scenarios and establishes DG methods as more effective solutions for achieving robust cross-domain performance.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 9 domain generalization methods (ERM, GroupDRO, Mixup, DANN, C-DANN, Deep CORAL, MMD, IRM, RSC) and 7 conventional CNN models (AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet18/50, InceptionV3, DenseNet, SqueezeNet) on PACS and Office-Home benchmarks. Experiments use 120 epochs with batch sizes of 32 for DG methods and 64 for CNNs. The ResNet18 backbone serves as the base for DG methods, while CNNs employ fine-tuning approaches. Performance is measured through validation and target domain accuracy, with learning curves analyzed to understand generalization behavior across different model architectures and training strategies.

## Key Results
- Domain generalization methods achieve significantly higher accuracy on both validation and target domains compared to conventional CNNs
- Among CNNs, larger models with skip connections (ResNet50, InceptionV3) show better domain generalization than smaller models
- VREx emerges as the best-performing DG method across both PACS and Office-Home benchmarks
- Traditional CNNs exhibit unstable performance with domain variation, while DG methods maintain more consistent accuracy across domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Domain generalization methods outperform conventional CNNs by learning domain-invariant features through explicit minimization of domain shift during training.
- DG algorithms align feature distributions or maximize worst-case performance across domains, assuming test domains belong to a known family of related domains.
- If test domains come from fundamentally different distributions or if spurious correlations dominate, DG methods may fail to generalize effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Larger CNN models with skip connections demonstrate better OOD generalization due to enhanced gradient flow and feature reuse capabilities.
- Skip connections enable deeper networks to capture richer, more stable representations that are less sensitive to domain-specific noise.
- When domain shift is extremely large or overfitting dominates due to limited diversity, even deeper feature hierarchies may become irrelevant.

### Mechanism 3
- Domain-specific models overfit to single-domain statistics and fail to capture cross-domain invariances, learning domain-specific shortcuts that don't transfer.
- Without explicit domain generalization objectives, these models optimize for single-domain performance, masking poor generalization when validated on the same domain.
- Fine-tuning or data augmentation strategies that simulate domain shifts during training could potentially mitigate this limitation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data pipeline -> Model selection (DG or CNN) -> Training (domain-invariant objectives or standard classification) -> Validation on same domain -> Testing on unseen domain -> Accuracy/loss curves -> Analysis
- Critical path: Model training and evaluation loop; checkpointing best model based on validation accuracy; measuring gap between validation and target domain accuracy
- Design tradeoffs: DG methods require multi-domain training data and additional loss terms; CNNs are simpler but prone to overfitting; larger models generalize better but cost more compute
- Failure signatures: High validation accuracy but low target accuracy; large validation-target accuracy gaps; unstable learning curves across domains
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train ResNet50 on PACS "Art" domain, validate on same, test on "Cartoon"; compare with VREx trained on all domains
  2. Train AlexNet on Office-Home "Clipart" domain, validate on same, test on "Real World"; compare with ERM
  3. Visualize feature distributions across domains using t-SNE for both a DG method (e.g., DANN) and a vanilla CNN to qualitatively assess alignment

## Limitations
- Lack of publicly available code and precise hyperparameter settings for custom domain generalization methods (VREx, ANDMask, RSC)
- No statistical significance testing reported between methods, making performance differences potentially non-robust
- Comparison limited to accuracy metrics without exploring other generalization measures like calibration or robustness to adversarial perturbations

## Confidence

- High Confidence: CNN models generally underperform DG methods on OOD benchmarks (supported by multiple validation-target accuracy gaps)
- Medium Confidence: Larger CNNs with skip connections generalize better (based on consistent patterns across benchmarks but limited ablation)
- Medium Confidence: DG methods achieve better worst-case performance across domains (based on relative ranking, but without confidence intervals)

## Next Checks

1. Re-run experiments with statistical significance tests (e.g., paired t-tests) to verify that accuracy differences between top DG methods and CNNs are non-random
2. Implement and test the custom DG methods (VREx, ANDMask, RSC) from scratch or request source code to confirm reported performance gains
3. Extend evaluation to include robustness metrics (e.g., calibration error, adversarial accuracy) to determine if DG methods improve more than just in-distribution accuracy
