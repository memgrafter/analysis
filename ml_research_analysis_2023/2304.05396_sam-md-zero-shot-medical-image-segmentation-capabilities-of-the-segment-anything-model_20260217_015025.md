# SAM.MD: Zero-shot medical image segmentation capabilities of the Segment Anything Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05396
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05396
- Reference count: 2
- Zero-shot SAM achieves DSC of 0.857 (organs average) with 10 points, and DSC of 0.926 with bounding box prompts

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the zero-shot segmentation capabilities of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for medical image segmentation tasks using point and bounding box prompts on abdominal CT organ data. The evaluation uses 197-240 axial slices per patient from the AMOS22 dataset, with prompts generated from segmentation masks. Results show that SAM achieves competitive Dice Similarity Coefficients (DSC) compared to nnU-Net baselines, particularly with bounding box prompts. The model demonstrates strong performance even on raw CT values, indicating robustness to intensity ranges. The findings suggest SAM has significant potential as a semi-automatic segmentation tool in clinical workflows, enabling rapid expert-driven segmentation despite some accuracy decreases near class boundaries.

## Method Summary
The study performs zero-shot evaluation of SAM on the AMOS22 Abdominal CT Organ Segmentation dataset using point prompts (1, 3, 10 points) and bounding box prompts with jitter levels (0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5). CT images are clipped to -100 to 200 Hounsfield Units, and prompts are generated from segmentation masks as oracle sources. The study compares SAM's performance against 2D and 3D nnU-Net baselines using Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) as the primary metric. The evaluation covers multiple organ classes across 197-240 axial slices per patient, testing SAM's ability to generalize from natural image pre-training to medical CT data.

## Key Results
- SAM achieves DSC of 0.857 (organs average) with 10 point prompts, outperforming 1 and 3 point configurations
- Bounding box prompts with moderate jitter (0.01) achieve DSC of 0.926 (organs average), significantly outperforming point-based prompts
- SAM maintains performance on raw CT values without Hounsfield unit clipping, demonstrating robustness to high intensity ranges
- SAM shows competitive performance compared to nnU-Net baselines, suggesting potential as a semi-automatic segmentation tool

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bounding box prompts with moderate jitter outperform point-based prompts for zero-shot CT segmentation.
- Mechanism: SAM's object proposal mechanism is more robust to localization uncertainty when provided with a region-of-interest box, allowing it to capture global context before refining boundaries.
- Core assumption: The model's objectness prior generalizes across anatomical structures despite domain shift from natural images.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To the best of our knowledge, SAM does not provide a direct text prompt interface yet." → Suggests point/box are primary interactive modes.
  - [section] "Single positive bounding boxes are seen to perform considerably better than 10 positive point prompts." → Direct comparison supporting mechanism.
  - [corpus] Weak/no direct evidence; similar papers show mixed results depending on jitter level.
- Break condition: If jitter > 0.25 severely degrades accuracy, the mechanism fails as the model cannot tolerate prompt uncertainty.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SAM maintains accuracy on raw CT intensity values without Hounsfield unit clipping.
- Mechanism: The ViT encoder's attention mechanism is invariant to global intensity shifts, relying on relative pixel relationships rather than absolute Hounsfield ranges.
- Core assumption: Pre-training on LAION-5B included sufficient grayscale and medical-like intensity distributions to induce robustness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The performance does not degrade on raw CT values as well (AVG*), indicating robustness of box prompting to high intensity ranges."
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence of similar findings; this is a novel observation in the paper.
- Break condition: If segmentation fails on scans with extreme contrast or metal artifacts, the intensity invariance claim breaks.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SAM's zero-shot performance is competitive with fully supervised nnU-Net baselines.
- Mechanism: Large-scale pre-training on diverse masks transfers shape and spatial priors to unseen anatomical classes, reducing the data requirement for medical segmentation.
- Core assumption: Anatomical structures share sufficient geometric and topological features with the COCO-trained mask distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We compare the predictions of SAM to the corresponding 2D slices extracted from pre-predictions of a trained 2D and 3D nnU-Net baseline" → Direct comparison.
  - [section] Table 1 shows DSC values within ~10-15% of nnU-Net for organs with box prompts.
  - [corpus] Related papers confirm competitive zero-shot results but rarely match supervised baselines; SAM.MD is an outlier.
- Break condition: If task-specific fine-tuning on the same dataset yields >20% DSC improvement, the zero-shot competitiveness claim weakens.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - ViT image encoder → Transformer mask decoder → Output masks
  - Prompt encoder (point/box embeddings) → Decoder attention
  - Task-specific head for segmentation

- Critical path:
  1. Load CT slice → Resize to model resolution
  2. Encode prompt (point/box) → Generate mask tokens
  3. Feed image and prompt tokens → ViT encoder
  4. Transformer decoder → Soft mask prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Resolution vs. speed: ViT processes fixed 1024x1024; CT slices often require padding/cropping.
  - Prompt granularity: Points give fine control but less context; boxes give context but require accurate localization.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low DSC near organ boundaries → Prompt ambiguity or model uncertainty
  - Catastrophic failure on certain organs → Poor shape prior transfer from COCO
  - Slow inference → Memory bottleneck in ViT encoder

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run SAM on a single CT slice with 1-point and 10-point prompts; compare masks and DSC.
  2. Vary box jitter (0.01, 0.1, 0.5) on the same slice; record DSC degradation curve.
  3. Run same prompts on clipped vs. raw CT values; verify robustness claim.

## Limitations
- The study's zero-shot approach relies heavily on prompt quality, with no fine-tuning on medical data, which may limit performance on rare or ambiguous anatomical structures.
- The evaluation is constrained to abdominal CT and a single dataset (AMOS22), limiting generalizability to other imaging modalities or organs.
- The ViT encoder's resolution constraints (1024x1024) may introduce interpolation artifacts when processing CT slices that differ in aspect ratio or size.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: DSC scores for bounding box prompts with moderate jitter (0.01) are consistently higher than point-based prompts. The robustness to raw CT intensity values is clearly demonstrated. Competitive performance relative to nnU-Net baselines is well-supported by direct comparison.
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism by which SAM's attention is invariant to intensity shifts is plausible but not rigorously tested beyond the scope of this dataset. The assumption that anatomical structures share sufficient geometric priors with COCO-trained masks is reasonable but unverified for rare or pathological cases.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about SAM's utility as a semi-automatic clinical tool are aspirational and not directly validated through user studies or workflow integration tests.
