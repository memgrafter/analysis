# Causal Analysis for Robust Interpretability of Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08950
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08950
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a causal analysis approach for interpreting neural networks by using path interventions to discover causal explanatory graphs of pre-trained models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to neural network interpretability that moves beyond traditional correlation-based attribution methods by employing causal analysis. The method uses path interventions to systematically modify weights between hidden layers and measure their effects on model predictions, thereby identifying the most influential neurons and their causal relationships. By focusing on the actual causal factors underlying model performance rather than spurious correlations, the approach aims to provide more stable and faithful explanations for neural network behavior. The authors demonstrate their method across vision tasks on MNIST, CIFAR10, and ImageNet datasets, showing that it can capture robust explanations and even repair model performance by masking irrelevant weights without requiring retraining.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves conducting path interventions on pre-trained neural networks to uncover causal explanatory structures. Specifically, the approach systematically intervenes on the weights connecting hidden layers by modifying their values and observing the resulting changes in model predictions. This intervention-based analysis reveals which neurons and connections are causally important for the model's decision-making process. The method constructs causal explanatory graphs that map out the influential pathways and interactions between neurons. By testing the effects of these interventions, the approach identifies task-specific explanations that are more stable than traditional attribution methods, which often capture correlations rather than true causal relationships. The framework is designed to work with pre-trained models and can be applied to improve model performance by selectively masking out irrelevant weights.

## Key Results
- Path interventions successfully identify influential neurons and their causal interactions in neural networks
- The method provides more stable and faithful explanations compared to traditional attribution techniques
- Performance repair demonstrated by masking irrelevant weights improves classification accuracy without retraining

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by establishing causal relationships rather than relying on correlation-based attribution. Traditional interpretability methods like saliency maps or feature importance scores often identify features that are correlated with model predictions but may not be causally responsible for them. By systematically intervening on the weights between hidden layers and measuring the effects on predictions, the approach directly tests causal relationships. This intervention-based methodology reveals which neurons and connections are truly necessary for the model's decisions, rather than just being associated with them. The path interventions create a controlled experimental environment within the neural network itself, allowing researchers to distinguish between spurious correlations and genuine causal factors that drive model behavior.

Why needed: The method requires understanding which paths to intervene on and how they affect downstream predictions
Quick check: Map the network structure and identify all possible intervention points between layers

Intervention design and measurement - Skills in systematically modifying model components and quantifying effects
Why needed: The core method depends on carefully controlled interventions and precise measurement of their impacts
Quick check: Ensure intervention magnitude is appropriate and measurement captures meaningful prediction changes

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input -> Convolutional layers -> Fully connected layers -> Output predictions
Critical path: The causal pathways identified through path interventions that are most influential for predictions
Design tradeoffs: Causal accuracy vs. computational cost of interventions, interpretability vs. model fidelity
Failure signatures: Inconsistent intervention effects across similar inputs, high sensitivity to intervention magnitude
First experiments: 1) Test intervention effects on a single neuron, 2) Map causal pathways in a small network, 3) Compare intervention stability across multiple runs
