# Synthetic Health-related Longitudinal Data with Mixed-type Variables Generated using Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12281
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12281
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel DPM-based method for generating synthetic mixed-type longitudinal EHR data with high statistical fidelity and utility for downstream ML tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for generating synthetic longitudinal electronic health records (EHRs) with mixed-type variables using diffusion probabilistic models (DPMs). The method successfully synthesises realistic clinical datasets containing numeric, binary, and categorical variables, making them suitable for developing downstream machine learning models including reinforcement learning agents. The approach was validated on two clinical applications—acute hypotension and antiretroviral therapy for HIV—demonstrating that synthetic datasets closely match the statistical properties and correlations of real data while maintaining low patient disclosure risk.

The research represents the first application of DPMs for generating mixed-type longitudinal clinical data, addressing a critical gap in synthetic data generation methods that have traditionally focused on either continuous or discrete variables but not both. The DPM-based approach achieved superior performance compared to existing methods like GANs, with the synthetic datasets showing high utility for training RL agents and successfully preserving the complex temporal dependencies inherent in longitudinal clinical data.

## Method Summary
The method employs a conditional diffusion probabilistic model (DPM) to generate synthetic longitudinal EHR data with mixed variable types. The approach uses a hierarchical data representation where variables are grouped by type (numeric, binary, categorical) and temporal dependencies are preserved through sequence modelling. The DPM learns to denoise corrupted versions of real patient trajectories through a reverse diffusion process, conditioned on patient identifiers and time steps. The model architecture incorporates separate neural networks for handling different variable types, with a unified framework that can sample from the learned distribution to generate realistic synthetic patient trajectories. The conditional nature allows generation of data for specific patient cohorts or clinical scenarios.

## Key Results
- DPM-generated synthetic datasets successfully preserved statistical properties and correlations of real clinical data across mixed variable types
- Synthetic data demonstrated high utility for training reinforcement learning agents in two clinical applications (acute hypotension and HIV antiretroviral therapy)
- Patient disclosure risk was shown to be low, making the synthetic datasets safe for public access
- The method outperformed existing generative models (GANs) in preserving both distributional properties and temporal dependencies in longitudinal data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging the powerful generative capabilities of diffusion probabilistic models, which learn to reverse a gradual noising process through iterative refinement. Unlike traditional generative models that struggle with mixed data types or complex temporal dependencies, DPMs can capture the full joint distribution of longitudinal EHR data. The conditional framework allows the model to generate data conditioned on patient-specific features and temporal context, preserving the sequential nature of clinical observations. By incorporating separate handling for numeric, binary, and categorical variables within a unified architecture, the method maintains the mixed-type structure essential for realistic clinical data synthesis.

## Limitations
- Limited generalizability due to reliance on a relatively small dataset (275 patients) from a single clinical domain (surgical intensive care)
- Uncertainty about whether synthetic data captures all clinically relevant patterns, particularly rare events or complex temporal dependencies
- Evaluation focused on only two specific reinforcement learning use cases, leaving open questions about performance across broader clinical tasks
