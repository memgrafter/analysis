# Real-time Aerial Detection and Reasoning on Embedded-UAVs

- arXiv ID: 2305.12414
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12414

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a real-time embedded system pipeline for aerial pedestrian detection and activity recognition on UAVs. The core method exploits domain-specific knowledge for aerial surveillance, combining a Position and Orientation Invariant detection network (POI Net) with an ActivityNet for multi-pedestrian multi-action recognition. The system is designed to be lightweight and suitable for real-time processing on embedded devices like the NVIDIA Jetson TX2. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art accuracy on the Okutama-Action dataset while maintaining fast inference times.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a unified pipeline architecture for aerial pedestrian detection and activity recognition. The system consists of two main components: POI Net and ActivityNet. POI Net uses segmentation and regression maps to robustly detect pedestrians from various aerial orientations. ActivityNet incorporates a novel attention map approach and a BNLSTM to jointly recognize activities and refine detections temporally. The model is trained end-to-end using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1e-4. The system was successfully deployed in a real-world open-field environment, demonstrating its practicality for autonomous UAV surveying and activity reporting.

## Key Results
- Achieved mAP of 24.2% for primary actions and 13.6% for secondary actions on Okutama-Action dataset
- Maintained fast inference time of 1.43 ± 0.21 seconds on NVIDIA Jetson TX2
- Successfully deployed in real-world open-field environment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system exploits domain-specific knowledge for aerial surveillance by combining position and orientation invariant detection with temporal activity recognition. The POI Net's use of segmentation and regression maps allows for robust detection across varying aerial perspectives, while the ActivityNet's attention map approach and BNLSTM enable joint activity recognition and temporal refinement. This combination allows for accurate detection and recognition while maintaining computational efficiency suitable for real-time embedded processing.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**
- Question: How can the proposed model be adapted to detect and recognize activities of general objects beyond pedestrians in UAV imagery?
- Basis in paper: The paper focuses on pedestrian detection and activity recognition, but acknowledges limitations in handling cluttered scenarios and suggests future work on utilizing altitude or velocity information from drone sensors.
- Why unresolved: The paper primarily demonstrates the model's effectiveness on pedestrian data and does not explore its applicability to other object types or more complex environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the model's performance on datasets with diverse object classes and varying scene complexities would demonstrate its generalization capabilities.

**Open Question 2**
- Question: Can the model's accuracy be further improved by incorporating more sophisticated attention mechanisms, such as learning-based approaches, without significantly increasing computational overhead?
- Basis in paper: The paper employs a novel algorithmic attention map approach to enhance performance, but acknowledges that learning-based methods might be more effective. It also emphasizes the importance of minimizing computational overhead for real-time UAV applications.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative attention mechanisms or quantify the trade-off between accuracy and computational cost for different approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments evaluating the performance and runtime of various attention mechanisms, including both algorithmic and learning-based approaches, would provide insights into the optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency.

**Open Question 3**
- Question: How can the model be extended to handle dynamic scenes with multiple UAVs operating simultaneously, where each UAV needs to detect and recognize activities of pedestrians in its vicinity while avoiding collisions with other UAVs?
- Basis in paper: The paper focuses on a single UAV performing autonomous surveying and activity reporting. It does not address the challenges of multi-UAV coordination and collision avoidance in dynamic environments.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not discuss the integration of the proposed model with multi-UAV systems or the incorporation of collision avoidance algorithms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Simulations or real-world experiments involving multiple UAVs equipped with the proposed model, demonstrating their ability to perform coordinated surveillance, activity recognition, and collision avoidance in dynamic scenarios.
