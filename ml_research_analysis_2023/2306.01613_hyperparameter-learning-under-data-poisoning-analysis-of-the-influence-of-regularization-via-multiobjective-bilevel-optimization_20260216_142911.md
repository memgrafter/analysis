# Hyperparameter Learning under Data Poisoning: Analysis of the Influence of Regularization via Multiobjective Bilevel Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01613
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01613
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a novel poisoning attack formulation that considers the effect of attacks on model hyperparameters, formulating the problem as a multiobjective bilevel optimization.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel poisoning attack formulation that simultaneously considers the impact of attacks on both model parameters and hyperparameters through a multiobjective bilevel optimization framework. The approach enables learning optimal poisoning points and hyperparameters under worst-case scenarios, representing a significant advancement over traditional poisoning attacks that only target model parameters. The authors demonstrate that using a small trusted validation set to adaptively learn hyperparameters can substantially mitigate poisoning attacks, while also showing that regularization increases model stability. The research provides new insights into the relationship between hyperparameter selection and model robustness under adversarial conditions.

## Method Summary
The paper formulates data poisoning as a multiobjective bilevel optimization problem that simultaneously optimizes both the poisoning points and model hyperparameters. The upper level optimizes the hyperparameters and poisoning points to maximize the worst-case loss, while the lower level trains the model given these hyperparameters and poisoned data. The approach is applied to both Logistic Regression and Deep Neural Networks with L2 and L1 regularization. The framework allows for learning optimal poisoning points and hyperparameters under worst-case scenarios, with experiments demonstrating that adaptive hyperparameter learning using a small trusted validation set significantly improves robustness against poisoning attacks.

## Key Results
- Learning hyperparameters using a small trusted validation set significantly mitigates poisoning attacks
- L2 and L1 regularization provide similar benefits in terms of increasing model stability
- The poisoning attack has a more pronounced effect on later DNN layers
- Using layer-specific regularization hyperparameters is beneficial compared to global regularization
- Constant hyperparameter values a priori lead to either poor accuracy or weak robustness, while adaptive learning improves both performance and robustness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed approach works by explicitly modeling the interdependence between poisoning attacks and hyperparameter selection. Traditional poisoning attacks assume fixed hyperparameters, but this framework recognizes that an attacker can also influence the hyperparameter selection process. By formulating this as a multiobjective bilevel optimization, the attack can optimize both the poisoning points and hyperparameters simultaneously to maximize the worst-case loss. This creates a more realistic threat model where the attacker has more degrees of freedom. The use of a small trusted validation set for adaptive hyperparameter learning creates a defense mechanism that can adjust to the attack in real-time, making it significantly harder for the attacker to succeed.

## Foundational Learning
- Multiobjective Bilevel Optimization: Optimization where one problem (upper level) is constrained by the solution of another problem (lower level). Why needed: Allows modeling the hierarchical relationship between hyperparameter selection and model training under adversarial conditions. Quick check: Verify that both objectives (accuracy and robustness) are properly balanced in the optimization.
- Data Poisoning Attacks: Adversarial manipulation of training data to degrade model performance. Why needed: Understanding the threat model is crucial for developing effective defenses. Quick check: Confirm that the poisoning mechanism can actually influence the learned model parameters.
- Regularization Techniques (L1/L2): Methods to prevent overfitting by adding penalty terms to the loss function. Why needed: Regularization is shown to increase model stability under poisoning attacks. Quick check: Compare performance with and without regularization to quantify the stability benefits.
- Trusted Validation Set Assumption: The assumption that a small set of clean, unpoisoned data is available for hyperparameter selection. Why needed: This is critical for the proposed defense mechanism but may not hold in all practical scenarios. Quick check: Evaluate robustness when this assumption is violated or partially satisfied.
- Layer-wise Regularization: Applying different regularization strengths to different layers of a neural network. Why needed: The paper shows that later layers are more vulnerable to poisoning, making layer-specific regularization beneficial. Quick check: Compare global vs. layer-specific regularization performance under attacks.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Multiobjective Bilevel Optimization -> Hyperparameter Learning -> Model Training -> Performance Evaluation

Critical Path:
1. Define multiobjective bilevel optimization problem
2. Implement poisoning point optimization
3. Implement hyperparameter learning using trusted validation set
4. Train model with learned hyperparameters
5. Evaluate performance under attack scenarios

Design Tradeoffs:
- Computational complexity vs. attack strength: More complex optimization allows stronger attacks but increases computation time
- Trusted validation set size vs. defense effectiveness: Larger trusted sets provide better defense but may be harder to obtain
- Global vs. layer-specific regularization: Layer-specific offers better protection but requires more hyperparameter tuning
- Attack optimization frequency vs. training efficiency: More frequent optimization provides stronger attacks but slows training

Failure Signatures:
- If the trusted validation set is compromised, the defense mechanism fails
- When computational resources are limited, the optimization may converge to suboptimal solutions
- If the attack optimization doesn't properly balance objectives, it may focus too heavily on one aspect
- When regularization strength is too high, it may hurt legitimate model performance

First Experiments:
1. Compare model performance with fixed vs. adaptive hyperparameters under poisoning attacks
2. Evaluate the effect of trusted validation set size on defense effectiveness
3. Test layer-specific vs. global regularization under different attack scenarios

## Limitations
- Experimental scope is restricted to binary classification tasks with specific dataset sizes
- Regularization benefits shown for L1 and L2 may not generalize to other regularization types or combinations
- Computational scalability to larger models and datasets remains unverified
- The assumption of having access to a small trusted validation set may not hold in many practical scenarios

## Confidence
- Hyperparameter learning improves robustness: High
- L2/L1 regularization provides similar benefits: Medium
- Layer-specific regularization is beneficial: Medium
- Constant hyperparameters lead to poor performance: High
