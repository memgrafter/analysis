# Accelerated wind farm yaw and layout optimisation with multi-fidelity deep transfer learning wake models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16274
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16274
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: WakeNet achieves over 99.8% accuracy vs FLORIS and accelerates optimization by up to two orders of magnitude, reducing layout optimization from 36 hours to 13 minutes for 15 turbines.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces WakeNet, a machine learning framework for accurate and fast wind farm wake modeling, yaw control, and layout optimization. WakeNet employs deep learning models (FCNN, CNN) trained on FLORIS wake data to predict 2D wake velocity fields, local turbulence intensity, and power output. A key innovation is the use of multi-fidelity transfer learning: a model pre-trained on low-fidelity Gaussian wakes is fine-tuned on limited high-fidelity Curl wake data, reducing computational cost. This enables rapid, high-fidelity wake predictions suitable for real-time control and complex optimization scenarios.

## Method Summary
WakeNet uses deep learning architectures (fully connected neural networks and convolutional neural networks) trained on FLORIS-generated wake data to predict wind farm power output, wake velocity fields, and turbulence intensity. The framework leverages multi-fidelity transfer learning: a base model is first trained on low-fidelity Gaussian wake simulations and then fine-tuned on limited high-fidelity Curl wake data. This approach significantly reduces the computational burden and data requirements for achieving high accuracy. The model outputs include 2D wake velocity maps, local turbulence intensity, and aggregated power output, supporting both yaw control and layout optimization tasks.

## Key Results
- WakeNet achieves over 99.8% accuracy compared to FLORIS wake models.
- Optimization tasks (yaw and layout) are accelerated by up to two orders of magnitude; layout optimization for 15 turbines drops from 36 hours to 13 minutes.
- Multi-fidelity transfer learning reduces the need for extensive high-fidelity training data, making the approach computationally efficient.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The multi-fidelity transfer learning approach allows the model to leverage abundant low-fidelity data for initial training and then adapt to high-fidelity physics with minimal additional data, preserving accuracy while reducing computational cost. Deep learning architectures (FCNN, CNN) are well-suited for capturing complex spatial patterns in wake velocity fields and turbulence intensity, enabling accurate and fast predictions. By training on synthetic FLORIS data, the framework can rapidly evaluate many design scenarios, which is infeasible with traditional CFD or high-fidelity simulations.

## Limitations
- Reliance on synthetic FLORIS data may not fully capture real-world complexities such as terrain effects, wake meandering, or atmospheric stability variations.
- The approach is optimized for 2D rectangular layouts and may not generalize well to irregular or complex terrain configurations.
- Validation against field measurements is not demonstrated, so real-world accuracy is uncertain.

## Confidence
- High: Accuracy metrics (>99.8% vs FLORIS) and computational speedup (2 orders of magnitude) are directly measurable and well-documented.
- Medium: Multi-fidelity transfer learning benefits are sound, but validation across diverse wake scenarios is limited.
- Low: Real-world applicability claims are uncertain due to lack of field validation and potential domain gaps between FLORIS and actual wind farm behavior.
