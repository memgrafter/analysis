# Iterative Deepening Hyperband

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00511
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00511
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Iterative Deepening Hyperband (ID-HB) extends Hyperband to efficiently handle increased maximum budgets post-hoc, introducing three variants that differ in how they reuse prior evaluations while maintaining solution quality and reducing computational effort.

## Executive Summary
Iterative Deepening Hyperband (ID-HB) addresses a critical limitation of Hyperband when the maximum budget needs to be increased after an initial run. Rather than re-running the entire optimization from scratch, ID-HB continues the previous run with an increased budget, introducing three variants—discarding, preserving, and efficient—that differ in how they reuse prior evaluations. Theoretical analysis and empirical results demonstrate that ID-HB retains solution quality while significantly reducing computational effort, with the efficient variant requiring only the budget of a single run with the larger maximum budget.

## Method Summary
ID-HB extends Hyperband by continuing a previous run when the maximum budget needs to be increased post-hoc. The algorithm maintains the candidate pool from the previous run and fills it to the new required size by promoting additional configurations without revising previous promotion decisions (efficient variant), allowing previously discarded configurations to re-enter consideration (discarding variant), or reusing previously evaluated configurations to avoid redundant computations (preserving variant). The method uses Successive Halving as a subroutine and is evaluated on benchmarks including neural networks, SVMs, and XGBoost, showing significant computational savings compared to restarting Hyperband from scratch.

## Key Results
- ID-HB retains solution quality while significantly reducing computational effort compared to restarting Hyperband
- The efficient variant (eID-HB) requires only the budget of a single run with the larger maximum budget
- All three ID-HB variants—discarding, preserving, and efficient—demonstrate practical utility across diverse benchmarks
- Theoretical analysis provides convergence guarantees and bounds on cumulative regret

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: dID-HB allows revision of previous Hyperband decisions, promoting more promising configurations from the discarded set.
- Mechanism: When extending the candidate pool with newly sampled configurations, dID-HB evaluates all candidates (old discarded, old promoted, new) and promotes the top-k. This enables previously discarded configurations to re-enter consideration if they perform better at higher budgets.
- Core assumption: Some configurations discarded in earlier stages may actually be strong performers at higher budgets, but were prematurely eliminated due to insufficient training time.

  - [section 5.1]: "More specifically, when the start pool of hyperparameter configurations is extended by the new hyperparameter configurations, we allow discarding hyperparameter configurations that were promoted in the previous run and have already been evaluated on a larger budget."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found; mechanism inferred from paper description.
- Break condition: If no previously discarded configurations perform well at higher budgets, dID-HB offers no advantage over eID-HB.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: pID-HB conserves computational resources by reusing evaluations of previously discarded but already-evaluated configurations.
- Mechanism: When determining promotions, pID-HB considers both new configurations and previously evaluated-but-discarded configurations that have already been evaluated at the current budget level. This allows reusing prior evaluation results without re-running them.
- Core assumption: Previously evaluated configurations retain their evaluation results and can be compared with new configurations without re-evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "introducing three variants—discarding, preserving, and efficient—that differ in how they reuse prior evaluations."
  - [section 5.2]: "in preserving iterative deepening Hyperband (pID-HB), we promote the top-k hyperparameter configurations of a pool of candidates comprising the promoted hyperparameter configurations and all hyperparameter configurations that have already been evaluated on a larger budget in the previous Hyperband run but have not been promoted in the continued run."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found; mechanism inferred from paper description.
- Break condition: If all previously evaluated configurations are significantly worse than newly sampled ones, pID-HB provides no advantage over eID-HB.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: eID-HB maximizes computational efficiency by avoiding reconsideration of previously made decisions.
- Mechanism: eID-HB only fills up candidate pools by promoting additional configurations without revising previous promotion decisions. Once a configuration is promoted, it remains promoted regardless of new candidates' performance.
- Core assumption: Previous Hyperband decisions were reasonably good, and revising them provides minimal benefit compared to the computational overhead.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "ID-HB retains solution quality while significantly reducing computational effort, with the efficient variant requiring only the budget of the single run with the larger maximum."
  - [section 5.3]: "In principle, it may happen that some hyperparameter configurations may have been wrongly promoted as compared to starting the Hyperband run from scratch. Since already promoted hyperparameter configurations remain promoted and decisions cannot be revised, the overall performance may deteriorate."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found; mechanism inferred from paper description.
- Break condition: If previous decisions were significantly suboptimal, eID-HB may yield worse results than dID-HB or pID-HB.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - ID-HB main algorithm -> Successive Halving subroutine -> Configuration pool management -> Budget tracking system
  - Three strategy variants (dID-HB, pID-HB, eID-HB) -> Promotion decision logic -> Candidate selection mechanism

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize with previous Hyperband state (max budget, configuration pools, evaluations)
  2. Increase max budget by factor η
  3. For each bracket, fill pools to new required sizes
  4. Apply chosen strategy variant to determine promotions
  5. Continue Successive Halving with extended pools
  6. Return best configuration found

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational efficiency vs. solution quality: eID-HB is most efficient but may sacrifice quality; dID-HB potentially best quality but least efficient
  - Memory usage: Storing previous evaluation results (especially for pID-HB) requires additional memory
  - Implementation complexity: dID-HB requires most complex logic to manage configuration pools and promotion decisions

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation when previous decisions were poor (particularly for eID-HB)
  - Increased memory usage due to storing previous evaluation data
  - Potential for infinite loops if budget increases aren't managed properly

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic ID-HB with eID-HB strategy on a simple benchmark (like lcbench) to verify core functionality
  2. Compare all three strategy variants on a medium-complexity benchmark to observe performance differences
  3. Test extreme budget increase scenarios to verify theoretical guarantees about computational savings

## Limitations
- The paper acknowledges that dID-HB can potentially revise previously made promotion decisions, which could lead to suboptimal configurations being promoted
- Computational savings claims are theoretical and may not fully translate to all real-world implementations where evaluation costs vary significantly
- The analysis assumes specific configurations (η=2, Rt-1=16) that may not generalize optimally to all hyperparameter optimization problems

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core mechanism and motivation - the problem of needing to increase budgets post-hoc is well-established and the solution approach is sound
- Medium confidence in the theoretical analysis - while the proofs appear rigorous, they rely on assumptions about budget scaling that may not hold in all practical scenarios
- Medium confidence in empirical results - the benchmarks used are diverse, but the paper doesn't explore edge cases where ID-HB might underperform
