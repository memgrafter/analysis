# Sliced-Wasserstein on Symmetric Positive Definite Matrices for M/EEG Signals

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05798
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05798
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SPDSW-based kernel ridge regression outperforms state-of-the-art Riemannian methods for brain-age prediction from MEG data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Sliced-Wasserstein distance (SPDSW) for distributions of symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrices, addressing the need for efficient tools to compare such distributions in M/EEG data analysis. SPDSW is derived by averaging the Wasserstein distance between one-dimensional projections of the distributions onto geodesics passing through the identity matrix in the SPD manifold. Theoretical properties, including finite distance, weak convergence metrization, and topological equivalence to the Wasserstein distance, are established. Applied to brain-age prediction from MEG data, SPDSW-based kernel ridge regression outperforms state-of-the-art Riemannian geometry methods.

## Method Summary
The method involves preprocessing MEG signals by band-pass filtering, temporal signal-space-separation, and estimating covariance matrices from short time frames, followed by rank reduction to obtain SPD matrices. For each subject and frequency band, covariance matrices are treated as samples from a distribution. SPDSW is computed between these distributions using geodesic projections and 1D Wasserstein calculations, with a kernel matrix constructed from SPDSW values. Kernel ridge regression is then applied to predict brain age.

## Key Results
- SPDSW-based kernel ridge regression outperforms state-of-the-art Riemannian geometry methods for brain-age prediction from MEG data
- SPDSW has better computational complexity than full Wasserstein distance and is well-conditioned for regression of distributions
- SPDSW proves efficient as a surrogate for the Wasserstein distance in domain adaptation for Brain Computer Interface applications

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SPDSW is a computationally efficient alternative to Wasserstein distance for distributions of SPD matrices because it leverages one-dimensional projections onto geodesic lines.
- Mechanism: By projecting high-dimensional SPD distributions onto one-dimensional geodesics passing through the identity matrix, SPDSW reduces the computational complexity from cubic to near-linear in sample size.
- Core assumption: The geodesic projections onto lines through the identity preserve enough discriminative information for learning tasks while drastically reducing dimensionality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SPDSW is derived by averaging the Wasserstein distance between one-dimensional projections of the distributions onto geodesics passing through the identity matrix"
  - [section] "SPDSW has better complexity and is well-conditioned for regression of distributions"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence for SPD matrices; closest is "Sliced-Wasserstein Distances and Flows on Cartan-Hadamard Manifolds" which discusses SW on Riemannian manifolds but not SPD-specific
- Break condition: If the geodesic projections lose critical geometric information, or if the SPD manifold structure is too complex for 1D projections to capture

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SPDSW defines a proper Hilbertian metric enabling kernel methods for distribution regression tasks
- Mechanism: SPDSW can be embedded into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space via an explicit feature map, allowing use of kernel ridge regression on distributions
- Core assumption: The sliced Wasserstein distance is Hilbertian on the SPD manifold, which enables positive definite kernels
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SPDSW proves efficient as a surrogate for the Wasserstein distance in domain adaptation for Brain Computer Interface applications"
  - [section] "As shown in Section 2.4, SPDSW is a well-defined distance on distributions in S_d^+(R). The most straightforward way to build a kernel from this distance is to resort to well-known Gaussian kernels"
  - [corpus] "Sliced-Wasserstein Distances and Flows on Cartan-Hadamard Manifolds" discusses Hilbertian properties but not SPD-specific
- Break condition: If the SPDSW distance fails to be Hilbertian or the kernel approximation becomes too coarse

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SPDSW metrizes weak convergence, making it topologically equivalent to Wasserstein distance
- Mechanism: The averaging over all geodesic projections ensures SPDSW preserves the topological properties of Wasserstein distance, including weak convergence
- Core assumption: The collection of all geodesic projections through identity is sufficient to characterize weak convergence on the SPD manifold
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SPDSW is derived by averaging the Wasserstein distance between one-dimensional projections of the distributions onto geodesics passing through the identity matrix"
  - [section] "SPDSW metrizes the weak convergence, i.e. for µ∈ Pp(S_d^+(R)) and a sequence (µk)k in Pp(S_d^+(R)), limk→∞ SPDSWp(µk,µ) = 0 if and only if (µk)k converges weakly to µ"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence for SPD matrices; closest is "Random matrix theory improved Fréchet mean of symmetric positive definite matrices" which discusses SPD statistics but not SW convergence
- Break condition: If the projection directions don't span the space sufficiently, or if the SPD manifold topology requires more than geodesic projections for convergence characterization

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Signal → Covariance matrices → SPDSW distance → Kernel matrix → Prediction model
- Critical path: Signal → Covariance matrices → SPDSW distance → Kernel matrix → Prediction model
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Projection count vs. accuracy: More projections improve approximation but increase computation time
  - Log-Euclidean vs. Affine-Invariant metric: Log-Euclidean is computationally cheaper but may lose some geometric information
  - Time frame length: Shorter frames provide more samples but noisier covariance estimates
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor prediction performance: Check if covariance estimates are stable, if projection directions are well-sampled, or if kernel bandwidth is appropriate
  - High variance in results: Increase number of projections or check for numerical stability in distance calculations
  - Slow runtime: Profile geodesic projection computation or consider approximation methods
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare SPDSW vs. Log-Euclidean Wasserstein distance on synthetic SPD distributions with known ground truth
  2. Vary projection count (L=100, 500, 1000) on brain age prediction task to find sweet spot
  3. Test different time frame lengths on covariance estimation stability and prediction performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SPDSW perform compared to Wasserstein distance on non-M/EEG datasets involving distributions of SPD matrices?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that SPDSW is an efficient surrogate to the Wasserstein distance, but does not provide comparative results on non-M/EEG datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on M/EEG applications, so empirical comparison on other domains is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing SPDSW and Wasserstein distance on diverse datasets (e.g., medical imaging, finance) involving SPD matrix distributions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the computational complexity of SPDSW be further reduced by optimizing the sampling method for symmetric matrices?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that sampling symmetric matrices with complexity O(d^2) is possible but leaves it as an open question whether this affects theoretical bounds.
- Why unresolved: The impact of this optimization on both theoretical guarantees and practical runtime is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of theoretical bounds and runtime experiments comparing O(d^2) and O(d^3) sampling methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the indiscernible property hold for HSPDSW (horospherical SPDSW) with the Afﬁne-Invariant metric?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that whether HSPDSW satisfies the indiscernible property remains an open question.
- Why unresolved: The projection log∘πA is not a diffeomorphism, making it unclear if distinct distributions can have zero HSPDSW distance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof or counterexample demonstrating whether HSPDSW satisfies the indiscernible property.

## Limitations

- Computational Scalability: While SPDSW claims better complexity than full Wasserstein distance, the actual computational savings depend heavily on implementation details and the number of geodesic projections used.
- Theoretical Generalization: The theoretical properties (finite distance, weak convergence metrization, topological equivalence) are established under assumptions that may not hold for all SPD matrix distributions encountered in practice.
- Application-Specific Performance: The brain-age prediction results show improvement over state-of-the-art Riemannian methods, but the evaluation lacks comparison with non-Riemannian baselines that would better isolate the contribution of SPDSW.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core mechanism of computing SPDSW through geodesic projections is mathematically sound and well-defined. The implementation approach for brain-age prediction using kernel ridge regression is standard and reliable.

**Medium Confidence**: The theoretical properties (weak convergence metrization, Hilbertian nature) are proven, but their practical relevance and stability across different SPD matrix distributions remains unverified. The computational complexity claims are plausible but not empirically validated.

**Low Confidence**: The superiority of SPDSW over competing methods is demonstrated only on a single application (brain-age prediction) without ablation studies or comparisons across multiple datasets and tasks.
