# Describing and Organizing Semantic Web and Machine Learning Systems in the SWeMLS-KG

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15113
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15113
- Reference count: 35
- This work introduces the SWeMLS-KG, a knowledge graph and ontology for describing and organizing Semantic Web and Machine Learning systems (SWeMLS).

## Executive Summary
This work introduces the SWeMLS-KG, a knowledge graph and ontology for describing and organizing Semantic Web and Machine Learning systems (SWeMLS). The resource, derived from a systematic mapping study of 470 papers, provides the first machine-actionable framework for semantically describing SWeMLS, including 45 identified workflow patterns. It enables data-driven analysis of trends in this emerging field and supports tasks like searching for related work, machine-readable documentation, and improved scientific reviewing. The SWeMLS-KG is published as Linked Data and through the ORKG observatory, facilitating community contributions and long-term sustainability. Use cases demonstrate querying and embedding-based exploration of the graph, revealing insights into SWeMLS components, patterns, and their applications across domains.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed the SWeMLS-KG by converting results from a systematic mapping study (SMS) of 470 SWeMLS papers into a machine-processable format. They created the SWeMLS ontology and pattern library to formally describe SWeMLS systems and their workflow patterns, then populated a knowledge graph with system descriptions extracted from the SMS. The KG was validated against SHACL constraints to ensure consistency and conformance to the defined patterns. The resulting resource was published as Linked Data and made accessible through a SPARQL endpoint and the ORKG observatory for community use and contribution.

## Key Results
- The SWeMLS-KG provides the first machine-actionable framework for semantically describing SWeMLS systems
- 45 workflow patterns were identified and formalized in the pattern library
- The KG enables data-driven analysis of trends in SWeMLS across different domains and application areas

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The ontology bridges the gap between symbolic (Semantic Web) and sub-symbolic (ML) representations in SWeMLS systems.
- Mechanism: By formalizing SWeMLS characteristics into machine-actionable RDF, the ontology allows consistent description and reasoning over system workflows.
- Core assumption: The extracted SWeMLS characteristics during the systematic mapping study are sufficiently complete and correct.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The resource, derived from a systematic mapping study of 470 papers, provides the first machine-actionable framework for semantically describing SWeMLS"
  - [section] "we proposed a set of characteristics for describing SWeMLS and (ii) systematically collected, selected and extracted data from nearly 500 papers"
  - [corpus] "Found 25 related papers... average citations=0.0" - weak citation evidence suggests this is a novel approach
- Break condition: If key system characteristics are missing or misclassified, the ontology will fail to represent real SWeMLS accurately.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SHACL validation ensures consistency and correctness of SWeMLS-KG instances.
- Mechanism: Pattern-specific SHACL constraints are used to validate that system instances conform to the defined workflow templates.
- Core assumption: SHACL constraints accurately capture the intended structure of each pattern.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Lastly, we validated the resulting data against SHACL constraints... to ensure completeness, validity and conformance of the KG to the pattern definitions."
  - [section] "SHACL validation. We defined a set of constraints for general SWeMLS as well as instances of specific SWEMLS patterns"
- Break condition: If SHACL rules are too strict or too lenient, valid system descriptions may be rejected or invalid ones accepted.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: RDF2vec embeddings enable latent semantic exploration of the SWeMLS domain.
- Mechanism: Embeddings are computed on the KG structure, allowing similarity-based queries and visualization.
- Core assumption: The KG structure contains meaningful relationships that embeddings can capture.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we have computed RDF2vec embeddings [27] on the graph... visualizations can be generated, and additional queries, based on entity similarity in the embedding space, can be performed"
  - [section] "Fig. 5 shows scatter plots of embeddings... one can observe that the grouping is actually sensible, forming, e.g., a cluster of DBpedia and YAGO-related resources"
- Break condition: If the KG lacks sufficient connectivity or contains noise, embeddings may not capture useful semantics.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - SWeMLS ontology -> Defines classes, properties, and individuals for describing SWeMLS systems
  - SWeMLS-KG -> The populated knowledge graph containing descriptions of 470+ systems
  - SWeMLS pattern library -> Machine-actionable descriptions of 45 workflow patterns
  - OntoRefine -> Tool used for mapping spreadsheet data to RDF
  - SHACL-AF API -> Used for enriching KG with workflow connections
  - SPARQL endpoint -> Interface for querying the KG
  - ORKG observatory -> Publication platform for community access

- Critical path: Systematic mapping study → Ontology definition → KG population → Validation → Publication
- Design tradeoffs: Formal ontology vs. flexibility; comprehensive validation vs. ease of contribution
- Failure signatures: Inconsistent system descriptions, validation errors, broken links between components
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Load the SWeMLS ontology into a tool like Protege and explore its structure
  2. Execute a SPARQL query to find systems using a specific pattern (e.g., T-3)
  3. Validate a sample SWeMLS instance against its pattern's SHACL constraints

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal methodology for semi-automating the extraction of system descriptions from SWeMLS papers to populate the knowledge graph?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions plans for "semi-automatized description extraction from SWeMLS papers" as future work, indicating this is currently unresolved.
- Why unresolved: Current population of the SWeMLS-KG requires manual mapping and transformation of data from systematic mapping studies. Automating this process would require developing techniques to accurately extract relevant system details from diverse paper formats and writing styles.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of a semi-automated extraction system that can accurately identify and extract SWeMLS system descriptions from research papers with high precision and recall.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the SWeMLS-KG be extended to capture detailed machine learning experiment settings and provenance data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that existing ontologies like ML-Schema focus on ML experiment executions but the SWeMLS ontology does not currently cover detailed ML settings, hyperparameters, and evaluation results.
- Why unresolved: While the SWeMLS ontology captures system-level information, it lacks the granularity needed to fully document ML experiments and their provenance, which is important for reproducibility and comparison.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extension of the SWeMLS ontology to incorporate detailed ML experiment metadata, along with a populated knowledge graph demonstrating the capture of such data from actual SWeMLS papers.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective strategies for enabling community contributions to update and maintain the SWeMLS-KG?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses plans to make updating the SWeMLS-KG easier and mentions leveraging the ORKG observatory for community contributions, but notes this as future work.
- Why unresolved: While the infrastructure exists to accept community contributions, effective strategies for encouraging and managing these contributions at scale are not yet established.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of community contribution mechanisms, demonstrating successful community engagement and quality control in maintaining and expanding the SWeMLS-KG.

## Limitations
- The ontology may not capture emerging SWeMLS patterns as the field evolves rapidly
- Current user interface for contributing new system descriptions is limited
- Integration with existing ML ontologies (like MLS-Schema) needs further validation

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The systematic mapping study methodology is sound and the resulting corpus of 470 papers is comprehensive for the domain.
- **Medium Confidence:** The extracted SWeMLS characteristics and workflow patterns accurately represent the field, though the field is rapidly evolving.
- **Low Confidence:** The long-term sustainability and community adoption of SWeMLS-KG remain unproven.
