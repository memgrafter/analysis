# Investigating the dynamics of hand and lips in French Cued Speech using attention mechanisms and CTC-based decoding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08290
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08290
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: 58.5% word-level accuracy and 60.3% (hand) / 69.6% (lip) temporal IoU achieved using attention-based segmentation

## Executive Summary
This study presents a novel approach for automatic recognition of French Cued Speech by modeling the temporal dynamics between hand and lip movements using attention mechanisms. The authors develop a multistream neural network that integrates temporal self-attention within an RNN-based phonetic decoder, achieving 58.5% word-level accuracy on a newly recorded dataset of 1,087 sentences. A key innovation is the use of attention maps for automatic segmentation of hand and lip movements, validated through Dynamic Time Warping-based path extraction. The work provides both practical benchmarks for Cued Speech recognition and insights into the coordination patterns between manual and facial gestures.

## Method Summary
The method employs a multistream neural network architecture with Bi-GRU layers and temporal self-attention mechanisms for hand and lip feature streams. Visual features are extracted using MediaPipe (21 hand + 42 lip coordinates), reduced to 20 dimensions per stream, with hand position clustered into 8 classes. The model is trained with CTC loss and decoded using beam search with lexicon constraints, 5-gram language model scoring, and GPT-2 rescoring. Attention maps are analyzed post-hoc using DTW to extract movement segmentation boundaries, with temporal IoU used to evaluate segmentation accuracy.

## Key Results
- Achieved 58.5% word-level accuracy on French Cued Speech recognition task
- Hand movement segmentation achieved 60.3% temporal IoU, lip movement achieved 69.6% temporal IoU
- Attention maps successfully captured temporal asynchrony between hand and lip movements
- Language model rescoring improved accuracy by 25.2% compared to lexicon-only decoding

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Temporal self-attention layers enable the model to learn asynchrony between hand and lip movements in French Cued Speech.
- Mechanism: The model uses scaled-dot product attention after each Bi-GRU to weight frame contributions based on temporal relationships. By computing attention scores across all frames, the model can detect deviations from the diagonal in attention maps, which correspond to movement onsets.
- Core assumption: The model will learn to attend to movement onsets rather than maintaining diagonal attention patterns, indicating it has captured temporal relationships.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "temporal self-attention mechanisms for hand and lip feature streams are integrated within an RNN-based phonetic decoder and the resulting attention maps are analyzed a posteriori"
  - [section] "the model should pay attention to the onset of hand and/or lips movements, providing a way to segment those movements"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't provide direct evidence of attention mechanism effectiveness
- Break condition: If attention maps consistently follow the diagonal path without deviation, the model fails to capture asynchrony.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Constrained CTC decoding with lexicon and language model improves word-level recognition accuracy.
- Mechanism: Beam search decoding uses a pronunciation dictionary to constrain phoneme-to-word mapping, while an N-gram language model scores word sequences. GPT-2 rescoring further refines the top-K hypotheses by computing perplexity.
- Core assumption: The combination of lexicon constraints and language modeling will reduce recognition errors compared to phonetic-only decoding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "word-level decoding is achieved using a beam search decoder... An N-gram language model provides a likelihood score... An auto-regressive neural model based on Transformer (GPT) is finally used a posteriori to re-score"
  - [section] "the 5-gram model improves the accuracy by 25.2% compared to the lexicon-only configuration"
  - [corpus] Moderate - corpus shows performance improvements but doesn't isolate individual component contributions
- Break condition: If performance degrades when adding language model components, the decoding approach fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DTW-based attention path extraction provides accurate segmentation of hand and lip movements.
- Mechanism: Dynamic Time Warping extracts an optimal attention path from attention maps, constrained by a Sakoe-Chiba band. Movement onsets are detected when attention scores deviate from the diagonal.
- Core assumption: DTW will find consistent attention paths that align with true movement boundaries, enabling accurate segmentation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "an optimal path... along which the cumulative attention is maximum is extracted using the Dynamic Time Wrapping (DTW) algorithm"
  - [section] "the detected segmentation onsets are close to the manually annotated onset... On average, the automatically detected segmentation onset falls within 5 frames of the ground truth"
  - [corpus] Strong - corpus provides tIoU metrics (60.3% for hand, 69.6% for lips) validating segmentation accuracy
- Break condition: If tIoU values fall below acceptable thresholds, segmentation approach fails.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do speaker-specific strategies in hand-lip coordination affect the performance of automatic Cued Speech recognition systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that in future work, they plan to apply their segmentation technique to a multi-cuer database to study potential speaker-specific strategies in hand-lips coordination.
- Why unresolved: The current study only uses data from a single professional French cuer, limiting the generalizability of the findings to other speakers who may have different coordination strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Recording and analyzing data from multiple speakers with varying levels of experience and different coordination strategies would provide insights into how speaker-specific factors impact ACSR system performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal method for handling the lack of information about "liaison" in French to improve word-level decoding accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that constraining the vocabulary slightly degrades performance due to a higher number of deletion errors, mainly attributed to the lack of information about "liaison" in French.
- Why unresolved: The current study uses a pronunciation dictionary and language model, but these may not fully capture the nuances of French liaison, leading to errors in word-level decoding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating methods that explicitly account for liaison in the pronunciation dictionary and language model could potentially improve word-level decoding accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the attention-based segmentation method be further improved to better handle cases where the hand and lip attention paths overlap and are close to the diagonal?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors observe that the temporal relationships between hand and lips in CS are complex, with cases where the two attention paths overlap and are close to the diagonal, making segmentation challenging.
- Why unresolved: The current method uses a simple heuristic based on the deviation from the diagonal of the attention map, which may not be sufficient to handle all cases of overlapping hand and lip movements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Investigating alternative methods for segmenting overlapping hand and lip movements, such as incorporating additional contextual information or using more sophisticated attention mechanisms, could lead to improved segmentation accuracy.

## Limitations

- Dataset size of 1,087 sentences limits generalizability to other cuer styles and contexts
- Single-cuer data restricts evaluation of speaker-specific coordination patterns
- Attention-based segmentation sensitivity to visual feature extraction quality

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Word-level accuracy results (58.5%) and segmentation metrics (tIoU of 60.3% for hand and 69.6% for lips) are well-supported by experimental evaluation. Attention mechanism's ability to capture temporal asynchrony is validated through quantitative metrics and qualitative attention map analysis. CTC decoding framework with lexicon constraints and language modeling shows consistent performance improvements.

**Medium Confidence**: Model's generalizability to other cuer styles and languages remains uncertain due to single-cuer dataset. Segmentation method's robustness across different speech rates and content types needs further validation. Relative contribution of individual components to overall performance could be better quantified.

**Low Confidence**: Practical utility in real-world hearing-impaired communication scenarios has not been validated through user studies or field testing. Computational efficiency for real-time applications remains unclear.
