# Explanations for Automatic Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14062
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14062
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: SFL and Causal outperform LIME for ASR explanation by producing smaller and more consistent explanations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces X-ASR, a framework for explaining ASR transcriptions using adapted XAI techniques. The framework treats audio as frames and generates mutants by masking random frames to create explanations that are minimal and sufficient causes of the transcription. Evaluated on 100 Commonvoice samples across three ASR systems, SFL and Causal consistently outperformed LIME in generating smaller and more consistent explanations. The work demonstrates that causality-based approaches are more effective for ASR explanation than traditional perturbation methods.

## Method Summary
The X-ASR framework adapts three XAI techniques (SFL, Causal, LIME) from image classification to ASR explanation. It segments audio into frames, generates mutants by masking random frames at a 0.05 mutation factor, and uses similarity metrics (BERT or WER) to classify transcription correctness. SFL uses statistical fault localization to rank frame importance, Causal uses counterfactual reasoning to compute responsibility scores, and LIME uses local linear approximation. The framework then greedily selects the minimal subset of frames that maintains correct transcription classification.

## Key Results
- SFL and Causal generated significantly smaller explanations than LIME, with Causal being the most selective
- BERT similarity produced smaller but less consistent explanations than WER
- SFL with BERT achieved the best balance with 56% size and 74% consistency on average
- All three techniques showed consistent performance across Google API, Sphinx, and Deepspeech ASR systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: X-ASR explanations are effective because they use causality-based XAI techniques (SFL, Causal) that produce minimal and sufficient explanations for ASR transcriptions.
- Mechanism: The framework adapts perturbation-based methods to generate mutants of the original audio by masking random frames. It then measures how these perturbations affect transcription output using similarity metrics (BERT or WER). SFL uses statistical fault localization to rank frame importance based on failure-inducing patterns, while Causal uses counterfactual reasoning to compute responsibility scores for superframes. Both methods iteratively refine explanations until the minimal subset of frames is found that still produces a correct transcription.
- Core assumption: ASR transcriptions can be meaningfully explained by identifying minimal subsets of audio frames that are sufficient causes of the output, and that perturbation-based methods can effectively isolate these causal frames.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We provide an explanation for an ASR transcription as a subset of audio frames that is both a minimal and sufficient cause of the transcription."
  - [section] "Causal uses a framework of causality proposed by [21] which extends counterfactual reasoning by considering contingencies which are defined as changes in the current setting."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus contains related ASR quality papers but none specifically about explanation frameworks using SFL or Causal methods.
- Break condition: If the ASR model's decision boundary is highly non-linear or if transcription correctness depends on complex temporal dependencies that cannot be captured by frame-level perturbations, these methods may fail to identify truly minimal explanations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SFL and Causal outperform LIME for ASR explanation because they consider both the effect of masking frames and the number of other superframes affected by the masking.
- Mechanism: While LIME only ranks frames by their importance to the classification decision through local linear approximation, SFL and Causal incorporate additional information about how masking one frame affects other regions and the overall transcription. SFL counts mutants where frames are masked versus unmasked and output changes versus unchanged, while Causal computes responsibility based on the minimal contingency needed to create counterfactual dependence. This multi-dimensional consideration leads to more selective and consistent explanations.
- Core assumption: The effectiveness of explanations depends not just on individual frame importance but also on the interaction effects between frames and their collective impact on transcription output.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We find SFL and Causal outperform the LIME counterpart for Bert by generating significantly smaller explanations... This is because both SFL and Causal not only pay attention to the changes in the output after masking a superframe but also take the number of other superframes affected by masking."
  - [section] "Causal generates smaller explanations than SFL owing to stricter conditions from causal-theory in assigning responsibility of a superframe and the iterative refinement of superframes."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus focuses on ASR quality and domain-specific recognition but lacks papers specifically comparing explanation techniques for ASR.
- Break condition: If the ASR model treats frames largely independently or if temporal dependencies are so strong that individual frame masking is insufficient to capture causal relationships, the additional complexity of SFL and Causal may not provide meaningful benefits over simpler LIME explanations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The choice of similarity metric (BERT vs WER) significantly affects explanation quality, with BERT producing smaller but less consistent explanations than WER.
- Mechanism: BERT similarity uses semantic vector comparison which is more tolerant of minor differences in transcriptions, allowing for more selective explanations with fewer frames. WER, being a strict word-level metric with zero tolerance for differences, requires more frames to maintain correctness classification after perturbation. This tradeoff between size and consistency is inherent to the similarity metric choice rather than the explanation technique itself.
- Core assumption: The similarity metric used to classify transcription correctness after perturbation fundamentally determines the granularity and selectivity of the resulting explanations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Finally, there is a difference in size based on similarity metric. WER generates larger explanations than Bert. This is because WER has no tolerance for difference in transcription."
  - [section] "Clearly, size and consistency are conflicting metrics and we believe SFL with Bert achieves a good compromise between the two – 56% size and 74% consistency, on average."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - while the corpus contains papers on ASR quality metrics, none specifically analyze the impact of different similarity metrics on explanation quality.
- Break condition: If the application requires absolute precision in transcription matching (making WER-like strictness necessary) or if semantic similarity is sufficient regardless of exact wording (making BERT-like tolerance appropriate), the tradeoff between size and consistency may become unacceptable.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Audio -> Frame segmentation -> Mutant generation -> Classification (similarity metric) -> Explanation technique -> Frame ranking -> Greedy selection -> Output explanation
- Critical path: Audio → Frame segmentation → Mutant generation → Classification (similarity metric) → Explanation technique → Frame ranking → Greedy selection → Output explanation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Perturbation rate vs. computational cost: Higher mutation factors provide better coverage but increase runtime exponentially
  - Similarity metric choice vs. explanation quality: Strict metrics (WER) produce larger, more consistent explanations; tolerant metrics (BERT) produce smaller, less consistent explanations
  - Explanation technique choice vs. performance: SFL and Causal require more computation than LIME but produce better results
  - Frame granularity vs. temporal coherence: Individual frame analysis may miss longer-range dependencies important for transcription
- Failure signatures:
  - Empty explanations: Likely caused by similarity threshold set too high or ASR model that is insensitive to frame-level perturbations
  - Explanations covering >90% of frames: Indicates similarity threshold too low or ASR model that relies heavily on all input information
  - Inconsistent explanations across ASR systems: May indicate fundamental differences in how ASR systems process audio or inappropriate similarity metric choice
  - Explanations that don't reduce when using SFL/Causal vs LIME: Suggests the additional complexity of SFL/Causal isn't capturing meaningful frame interactions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run all three explanation techniques (SFL, Causal, LIME) on a single audio sample with both similarity metrics to observe the basic behavior and identify which technique produces the smallest explanations
  2. Vary the similarity threshold (BERT: 0.3, 0.5, 0.7; WER: 0.0, 0.05, 0.1) on the same sample to understand the tradeoff between explanation size and consistency
  3. Compare explanation consistency across two ASR systems (e.g., Google vs Sphinx) on the same sample to identify which technique and similarity metric combination provides the best balance of size and consistency

## Limitations
- The framework assumes audio frames can be meaningfully perturbed independently, which may not capture complex temporal dependencies in ASR models
- The evaluation only covers three ASR systems, limiting generalizability to other model architectures
- The similarity threshold choice (Bert=0.5, WER=0.0) appears arbitrary and may significantly impact explanation quality

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The framework successfully adapts existing XAI techniques (SFL, Causal, LIME) to ASR; SFL and Causal consistently outperform LIME in explanation quality

**Medium Confidence**: The tradeoff between explanation size and consistency is primarily determined by the similarity metric choice; Causal explanations are more selective than SFL due to stricter causal conditions

**Low Confidence**: The perturbation approach is universally effective for ASR explanation; the 100 Commonvoice samples are representative of diverse ASR scenarios
