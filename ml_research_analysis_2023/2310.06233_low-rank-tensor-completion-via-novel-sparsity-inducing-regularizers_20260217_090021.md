# Low-Rank Tensor Completion via Novel Sparsity-Inducing Regularizers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2310.06233
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06233
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Novel sparsity-inducing regularizers with closed-form thresholding functions replace tensor nuclear norm, reducing bias and outperforming state-of-the-art in low-tubal-rank tensor completion.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses low-rank tensor completion by introducing a novel framework to generate sparsity-inducing regularizers with closed-form thresholding functions. These regularizers replace the tensor nuclear norm (TNN) to alleviate the bias introduced by the ℓ1-norm. The proposed method is applied to low-tubal-rank tensor completion, and efficient ADMM-based algorithms are developed with proven convergence guarantees. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate superior restoration performance compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
The method generates sparsity-inducing regularizers via half-quadratic optimization, starting from a loss function that is quadratic for small values and concave for large values. This yields closed-form thresholding operators that are monotonically nondecreasing. These operators replace the soft-thresholding in TNN, reducing bias while maintaining sparsity. The generalized tensor singular value thresholding (GTSVT) operator is applied to each frontal slice in the Fourier domain. ADMM algorithms using GTSVT converge to a stationary point, as proven theoretically. The framework supports multiple nonconvex regularizers (e.g., ℓp-norm, Welsch, Cauchy) to further reduce bias.

## Key Results
- The proposed GTSVT operator enables efficient tensor completion with reduced bias compared to TNN.
- ADMM algorithms based on GTSVT converge provably to a stationary point.
- Experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets show superior performance in RRE, PSNR, and SSIM versus state-of-the-art methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Half-quadratic optimization produces sparsity-inducing regularizers with closed-form thresholding operators, enabling exact tensor completion without iterative thresholding.
- **Mechanism**: Starting from a loss function ϕh,λ(x) that is quadratic for |x| ≤ λ and concave for |x| > λ, half-quadratic optimization yields a regularizer φh,λ(y) such that the Moreau envelope min_y ½(x-y)² + λφh,λ(y) has a closed-form solution. The solution is given by Pφh,λ(x) = max{0, |x| - a·h'(|x|)}·sign(x), which is monotonically nondecreasing and sparsity-inducing.
- **Core assumption**: The derived regularizer's proximity operator is monotonically nondecreasing, ensuring correct and sparse solutions in tensor completion.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "We devise a framework to generate sparsity-inducing regularizers with closed-form thresholding functions." [section] Proposition 2 states that the thresholding function Pφh,λ(x) is monotonically nondecreasing. [corpus] Weak. Related work on "A framework to generate sparsity-inducing regularizers for enhanced low-rank matrix completion" supports the idea but lacks direct tensor-specific evidence.
- **Break condition**: If the concavity condition for ϕh,λ(x) is violated for x > λ, the monotonicity of Pφh,λ(x) fails, leading to incorrect solutions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Using nonconvex surrogates (e.g., ℓp-norm, Welsch, Cauchy) in place of the ℓ1-norm reduces bias in tensor completion while preserving computational tractability.
- **Mechanism**: The ℓ1-norm induces bias by shrinking all singular values uniformly. Replacing it with nonconvex regularizers like φp,λ (based on ℓp-norm with p < 1), φσ,λ (based on Welsch), or φγ,λ (based on Cauchy) creates thresholding operators that are closer to the identity function for large singular values, reducing bias. These operators still induce sparsity for small values due to the quadratic core near zero.
- **Core assumption**: The nonconvex regularizers maintain sufficient concavity to ensure the monotonicity of their thresholding operators.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "To alleviate the bias generated by the ℓ1-norm in the low-rank tensor completion problem, nonconvex surrogates/regularizers have been suggested to replace the tensor nuclear norm." [section] The section explains that nonconvex surrogates are suggested to replace TNN and alleviate bias. [corpus] Weak. While "Low-Rank Tensor Learning by Generalized Nonconvex Regularization" mentions nonconvex regularization, it does not confirm closed-form thresholding functions.
- **Break condition**: If the nonconvex regularizer is not chosen to ensure concavity for x > λ, the thresholding operator may not be monotonic, leading to incorrect or non-sparse solutions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The generalized tensor singular value thresholding (GTSVT) operator, derived via the framework, enables efficient and provably convergent ADMM-based algorithms for low-tubal-rank tensor completion.
- **Mechanism**: GTSVT replaces the soft-thresholding in TNN with the derived nonconvex thresholding operator Pφh,λ. This operator is applied to each frontal slice of the tensor in the Fourier domain, maintaining the computational efficiency of TNN while reducing bias. The ADMM framework with GTSVT converges to a stationary point as proven in Theorem 2.
- **Core assumption**: The monotonicity of Pφh,λ ensures that the ADMM updates remain stable and converge.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "efficient algorithms based on the alternating direction method of multipliers are developed. Furthermore, convergence of our methods is analyzed and it is proved that the generated sequences are bounded and any limit point is a stationary point." [section] Theorem 2 proves boundedness and convergence to a stationary point. [corpus] Weak. While "Nonconvex Robust High-Order Tensor Completion Using Randomized Low-Rank Approximation" uses nonconvex methods, it does not mention closed-form thresholding or ADMM convergence.
- **Break condition**: If the thresholding operator is not monotonic, the ADMM updates may oscillate or diverge, violating the convergence guarantees.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input tensor X^Ω → FFT along third mode → GTSVT on each frontal slice → inverse FFT → ADMM loop (M, E, P, ρ updates) → completed tensor
- **Critical path**: FFT → GTSVT on all frontal slices → inverse FFT → ADMM convergence check
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Nonconvex vs. convex: Nonconvex reduces bias but requires careful parameter tuning to maintain monotonicity.
  - Closed-form vs. iterative thresholding: Closed-form speeds up computation but limits the class of regularizers.
  - FFT-based vs. direct SVD: FFT-based is faster for large tensors but requires careful handling of Fourier coefficients.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Non-monotonic thresholding → ADMM divergence or incorrect solutions.
  - Ill-chosen parameters (e.g., σ > √2λ for Welsch) → loss of concavity → failure.
  - Numerical instability in FFT/SVD → corrupted tensor slices.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate GTSVT on a synthetic low-tubal-rank tensor with known ground truth; check if thresholding is monotonic and if bias is reduced vs. TNN.
  2. Run ADMM with GTSVT on a small image (e.g., 32×32×3) with random mask; verify convergence and compare PSNR to TNN.
  3. Test parameter sensitivity: sweep σ (for HOW) or p (for HOP) and observe effect on convergence speed and recovery quality.

## Limitations
- Theoretical foundation relies on concavity and monotonicity assumptions that may not fully account for numerical instability in high-dimensional tensors.
- Empirical validation is strong for synthetic and standard image datasets, but generalization to other tensor modalities (e.g., hyperspectral, medical) remains untested.
- Parameter tuning for nonconvex regularizers is sensitive and not fully automated, potentially limiting practical deployment.

- **Low**: Scalability to very large tensors and robustness to noise.
