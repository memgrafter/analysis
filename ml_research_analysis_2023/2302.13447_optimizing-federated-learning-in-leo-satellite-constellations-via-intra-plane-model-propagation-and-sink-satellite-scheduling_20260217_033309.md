# Optimizing Federated Learning in LEO Satellite Constellations via Intra-Plane Model Propagation and Sink Satellite Scheduling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13447
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13447

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of federated learning convergence in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite constellations, where intermittent visibility and limited bandwidth between satellites and ground stations create significant delays. The proposed FedLEO framework introduces two key innovations: intra-plane model propagation among satellites using high-speed inter-satellite links, and an optimal sink satellite scheduling algorithm to aggregate models and communicate with the ground station. By enabling satellites to exchange trained models within their orbital plane and strategically scheduling the sink satellite for ground station communication, FedLEO dramatically accelerates the learning process while maintaining high accuracy.

## Method Summary
FedLEO operates by first allowing satellites within the same orbital plane to exchange trained model parameters via inter-satellite links, enabling faster knowledge propagation than waiting for individual satellites to communicate with the ground station. The framework then implements a sink satellite scheduling algorithm that selects optimal satellites to aggregate model updates and relay them to the ground station, minimizing communication delays and maximizing bandwidth utilization. This dual approach of intra-plane model sharing combined with intelligent sink scheduling addresses the fundamental bottleneck of ground station communication in satellite-based federated learning systems.

## Key Results
- Achieves >89% accuracy on MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets within 15 hours of training
- Outperforms state-of-the-art federated learning methods by more than 10x in convergence speed
- Maintains or improves model accuracy compared to baseline approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FedLEO works by parallelizing the federated learning process across multiple satellites simultaneously rather than requiring sequential communication with a ground station. The intra-plane model propagation allows satellites to share learned parameters at high speed through dedicated inter-satellite links, effectively creating a distributed training environment within each orbital plane. The sink satellite scheduling algorithm optimizes which satellites should aggregate and forward models to the ground station, reducing the communication bottleneck that typically limits federated learning performance in satellite networks.
