# Adversarial Examples from Dimensional Invariance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06575
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06575
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: Adversarial examples arise from approximate discontinuity in neural networks that map between spaces of different dimensions, explained by topological invariance of dimension.

## Executive Summary
This work presents a novel theoretical framework explaining adversarial examples through the lens of topological invariance of dimension. The paper argues that when neural networks map from a higher-dimensional space to a lower-dimensional space (n > m), they approximate discontinuous functions due to the mathematical impossibility of continuous bijections between spaces of different dimensions. This approximate discontinuity manifests as adversarial examples - small input perturbations that cause large output changes. The framework provides insights into why certain architectures (like funneled autoencoders) and training paradigms (like unsupervised pre-training) exhibit different levels of adversarial vulnerability.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze the continuity properties of deep learning models by measuring the expansion ratio r = ea/en, where ea is the output change due to adversarial perturbation and en is the output change due to random perturbation. They train classifiers, autoencoders, and generative adversarial networks on standard datasets (Fashion MNIST, MNIST, CIFAR10, CIFAR100) and measure how this ratio changes as perturbation magnitude approaches zero. The degree of approximate discontinuity is quantified by tracking how r grows as η → 0. Additionally, the minimum pairwise output distance (dm) between inputs is measured to assess how bijective a model is after training.

## Key Results
- Classifiers and GAN discriminators exhibit high approximate discontinuity (r grows significantly as η → 0), while denoising diffusion autoencoders show little to no increase
- The degree of approximate discontinuity correlates with the ratio of input dimension to smallest hidden layer dimension
- Training increases the minimum pairwise output distance (dm), making models more approximately bijective over their training data
- Autoencoders with overcomplete architectures (input dimension = output dimension) show significantly less approximate discontinuity than funneled architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial examples arise from approximate discontinuity in neural networks that map between spaces of different dimensions.
- Mechanism: When a neural network models a bijective map f: R^n → R^m where n ≠ m, topological invariance of dimension dictates that such maps must be discontinuous. Even though neural networks are continuous by definition, training can make them approximate discontinuous functions when layer sizes compress or expand the input dimension.
- Core assumption: The network's effective behavior during inference approximates a bijective function over its training data distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "adversarial examples are approximate discontinuities resulting from models that specify approximately bijective maps f: R^n → R^m; n ≠ m"
  - [section] "Maps that are both injective and surjective (therefore bijective) are invertible... such models may be considered to be approximately bijective"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The degree of approximate discontinuity increases with the ratio of input dimension to smallest hidden layer dimension.
- Mechanism: As the compression ratio increases (e.g., 8x or 16x funneling in autoencoders), the network more closely approximates a bijective map between spaces of different dimensions, leading to greater approximate discontinuity and more pronounced adversarial examples.
- Core assumption: The model's capacity is sufficient to learn an approximately bijective mapping despite dimensional mismatch.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the larger the ratio of input to smallest model layer dimension... the more the approximate discontinuity"
  - [section] "observing the expansion ratiorη asη→ 0 for trained and untrained models, this indeed this is what is found empirically"
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct corpus support for this specific mechanism, though related work on adversarial transferability exists.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Generative models with high compression ratios (like GAN generators) suffer more from approximate discontinuity than overcomplete models (like denoising diffusion autoencoders).
- Mechanism: GAN generators map from low-dimensional latent space to high-dimensional image space (R^100 → R^768), creating a bijective-like mapping that exhibits approximate discontinuity. Denoising diffusion models maintain dimensional consistency (R^n → R^n), avoiding this issue.
- Core assumption: The dimensional relationship between input and output spaces determines the presence of approximate discontinuity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we find that both GAN generator and discriminator exhibit large growth inrη values indicating approximate discontinuity, but that the denoising diffusion autoencoder experiences very little growth"
  - [section] "the dramatic difference in approximate continuity between these models suggests that the training protocol given to these models is what is responsible for this change"

## Limitations
- The topological invariance argument assumes networks approximate bijective functions over training data, but this approximation quality is difficult to verify empirically.
- The work focuses primarily on feedforward architectures and doesn't address transformers or other modern architectures.
- The mechanism explaining why unsupervised pre-training helps classification is plausible but not rigorously proven.

- Degree of discontinuity correlates with compression ratio: High
