# ChatGPT vs State-of-the-Art Models: A Benchmarking Study in Keyphrase Generation Task

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14177
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14177
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: ChatGPT significantly outperforms state-of-the-art keyphrase generation models across scientific and news domains, including long documents, attributed to its larger token limit and multi-domain training.

## Executive Summary
This study benchmarks ChatGPT against state-of-the-art keyphrase generation models across scientific and news domains, with particular focus on long documents. Unlike previous studies, it evaluates ChatGPT's performance on full-length scientific papers and news articles, testing domain adaptation and long-document handling capabilities. Results show ChatGPT significantly outperforms existing models in all tested datasets, attributed to its larger maximum input tokens (4,096 vs. 1,024) and multi-domain training. Case studies highlight ChatGPT's ability to generate high-quality keyphrases by leveraging domain knowledge and long-term context.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) against KeyBART and other state-of-the-art keyphrase generation models using six benchmark datasets: Inspec, KP20k, NUS, SemEval2010, KPTimes, and DUC2001. Long documents are split into 2000-word chunks and processed separately, with keyphrases aggregated and deduplicated. Performance is measured using F1@5 and F1@M metrics with exact matching after preprocessing (lowercasing, stemming, punctuation removal). ChatGPT uses prompt-based learning with structured prompts, while KeyBART uses beam search (beam width 50, max length 40 tokens).

## Key Results
- ChatGPT significantly outperforms KeyBART and other state-of-the-art models across all datasets and domains
- Superior performance on long documents attributed to ChatGPT's larger maximum token limit (4,096 vs. 1,024)
- ChatGPT shows strong domain adaptation capabilities, particularly in news domain where specialized models struggle
- Multi-task and multi-domain learning through prompt-based approaches enhances keyphrase generation quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** ChatGPT's larger maximum input tokens (4,096 vs 1,024) enables it to capture more long-term word relationships, improving keyphrase generation for long documents.
- **Mechanism:** The increased context window allows the model to embed relationships between distant words that shorter context windows cannot capture, which is crucial for identifying keyphrases that may appear in later sections of long documents.
- **Core assumption:** Keyphrases in long documents often depend on context that spans the entire document rather than just local context.
- **Evidence anchors:**

- **Claim:** ChatGPT's multi-domain training enables better domain adaptation than specialized models.
- **Mechanism:** Multi-task learning across diverse domains allows ChatGPT to leverage shared knowledge from various domains when encountering new or distant domains, whereas specialized models may lose or "forget" knowledge from non-training domains during fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption:** Knowledge from multiple domains can be transferred to improve performance in related but unseen domains.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "multi-domain training" and "leverage domain knowledge and long-term context."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Document text → 2000-word chunks → ChatGPT with structured prompt → Regular expression parsing → Deduplication and concatenation → Final keyphrase set

- **Critical path:**
  1. Document text → 2000-word chunks
  2. Each chunk → ChatGPT with structured prompt
  3. ChatGPT response → Regular expression parsing
  4. Parsed keyphrases → Deduplication and concatenation
  5. Final keyphrase set

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Chunk size (2000 words) balances context preservation with API limits
  - Temperature set to 0 for deterministic output vs. more diverse but potentially inconsistent results
  - Frequency and presence penalties applied to reduce repetition and encourage diverse keyphrases

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Chunk boundaries cutting off keyphrase context → Incomplete or fragmented keyphrases
  - Over-deduplication removing valid distinct keyphrases → Loss of nuance in final output
  - Prompt not capturing document context → Irrelevant or generic keyphrases

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Vary chunk size (1000, 2000, 3000 words) and measure impact on F1@M for long documents
  2. Test different prompt structures (question vs. instruction format) and evaluate keyphrase quality
  3. Compare performance with and without frequency/presence penalties on abstractive keyphrase generation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How can keyphrase generation models be optimized to effectively capture long-term relationships in lengthy documents without compromising performance on shorter texts?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper highlights that current state-of-the-art models struggle with long document keyphrase generation due to limited maximum input tokens, leading to a loss of long-term word relationships. ChatGPT's higher token limit provides better results, suggesting a need for models that can handle both short and long documents effectively.
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper demonstrates ChatGPT's superior performance on long documents, it does not provide a definitive solution for optimizing models to balance performance across varying document lengths.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and benchmarking of a model that maintains high F1 scores on both short and long documents, with clear comparisons to existing models like KeyBART and ChatGPT.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** To what extent does ChatGPT's multi-domain training contribute to its success in keyphrase generation across diverse domains, and can this be replicated in specialized models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper notes that ChatGPT's multidomain training allows it to leverage knowledge from various tasks, enhancing its performance in keyphrase generation across different domains, including news, where specialized models like KeyBART struggle.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore whether specialized models can be trained to achieve similar cross-domain adaptability by incorporating multi-domain training or prompt-based learning.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative studies of specialized models trained with multi-domain data or prompt-based learning against ChatGPT, measuring performance across multiple domains.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the specific mechanisms by which ChatGPT's pre-training and multi-task learning enhance its ability to generate abstractive keyphrases, and how can these be incorporated into specialized keyphrase generation models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper suggests that ChatGPT's ability to generate high-quality abstractive keyphrases stems from its multi-task learning and pre-training, which allows it to apply knowledge from other tasks to the keyphrase generation task.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not detail the specific mechanisms or propose methods for integrating these capabilities into specialized models like KeyBART.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Analysis of ChatGPT's internal processes during keyphrase generation, followed by the development of specialized models that incorporate similar mechanisms, with performance evaluations on abstractive keyphrase generation tasks.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies exclusively on F1-based metrics without human validation of semantic quality
- Single prompt structure used without exploring prompt engineering variations
- Exact matching evaluation may penalize semantically equivalent but syntactically different keyphrases
- Results may not generalize to domains beyond scientific papers and news articles

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** ChatGPT's superior performance on standard keyphrase generation benchmarks (F1@5 and F1@M) is well-supported by quantitative results across multiple datasets and domains.

**Medium Confidence:** The attribution of ChatGPT's performance to its larger token limit and multi-domain training is reasonable but not definitively proven, as the study doesn't isolate these factors experimentally.

**Low Confidence:** The generalizability of results to other domains or document types beyond scientific papers and news articles remains untested, limiting the study's broader applicability.
