# Towards Bridging the Gaps between the Right to Explanation and the Right to be Forgotten

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.04288
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04288
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes ROCERF, the first algorithmic framework to generate counterfactual explanations robust to model updates from data deletion requests, achieving 100% validity for logistic regression models while maintaining low cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical tension between the right to explanation and the right to be forgotten in machine learning. When data deletion requests trigger model updates, previously provided explanations can become invalid, violating the right to explanation. The authors propose ROCERF, an algorithmic framework that generates counterfactual explanations robust to worst-case data deletion scenarios. The key innovation is formulating an optimization problem that finds explanations resistant to model updates from data removal, then developing an efficient approximation algorithm to solve it. Theoretically, ROCERF provides guarantees for linear models and certain non-linear models, while empirically it outperforms state-of-the-art methods on three real-world datasets.

## Method Summary
ROCERF generates counterfactual explanations that remain valid despite model updates from data deletion requests. The framework formulates an optimization problem to find explanations robust to worst-case data deletion scenarios, using first-order Taylor expansions to approximate model updates without exhaustive retraining. For efficient computation, ROCERF leverages leave-k-out (LKO) analysis and influence functions to estimate the effect of data removal on model parameters. The method provides theoretical bounds on validity and cost for regularized logistic regression and certain non-linear models satisfying regularity assumptions. Empirically, ROCERF achieves 100% validity for logistic regression models while maintaining low explanation costs across three real-world datasets.

## Key Results
- ROCERF achieves 100% validity for logistic regression models while maintaining low cost
- Outperforms state-of-the-art counterfactual explanation methods on three real-world datasets
- Provides theoretical guarantees with bounded costs for linear models and certain non-linear models
- Demonstrates that robust counterfactual explanations can be generated without retraining for every possible deletion combination

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: ROCERF resolves the tension between the right to explanation and the right to be forgotten by generating counterfactual explanations robust to model updates from data deletion.
- **Mechanism**: Formulates an optimization problem to find explanations resistant to worst-case data deletion scenarios, using first-order Taylor expansion and LKO analysis to avoid exhaustive retraining.
- **Core assumption**: Model updates due to data deletion can be approximated using first-order Taylor expansion, and significant deviations can be identified without retraining.
- **Break condition**: Breaks down for highly non-linear models or large deletion fractions where Taylor approximation becomes inaccurate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Provides theoretical validity and cost guarantees for linear models and certain non-linear models.
- **Mechanism**: Derives bounds showing additional cost for robust validity is O(k/n), vanishing when training set is large and removals are small.
- **Core assumption**: Models are regularized logistic regression or satisfy bounded gradients/Hessian assumptions.
- **Break condition**: Guarantees break down for highly complex models like deep neural networks or when regularity assumptions are violated.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Empirically outperforms state-of-the-art methods in validity and cost.
- **Mechanism**: Achieves 100% validity for logistic regression while maintaining low cost, and performs among best methods for neural networks.
- **Core assumption**: Evaluation metrics and experimental setup are representative of real-world scenarios.
- **Break condition**: Performance may degrade on datasets or models significantly different from those tested.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data preprocessing -> Model training -> Counterfactual explanation generation -> Model update simulation -> Evaluation

- **Critical path**:
  1. Load and preprocess dataset
  2. Split into training, validation, and test sets
  3. Train original machine learning model on training data
  4. Generate counterfactual explanations for negative test samples using ROCERF
  5. Simulate model updates by retraining on subsets of training data for different deletion fractions
  6. Evaluate validity and cost under original and updated models
  7. Compare results with baseline methods

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Computational efficiency vs. approximation accuracy: Uses Taylor expansions and LKO analysis instead of exhaustive retraining
  - Robustness vs. cost: Robustness may increase explanation costs compared to non-robust methods
  - Theoretical guarantees vs. empirical performance: Provides guarantees for certain models but empirical performance varies by dataset

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Low validity: Indicates approximation inaccuracies or excessive model updates
  - High cost: Suggests overly strict robustness constraints or ineffective optimization
  - Computational issues: Implementation inefficiency leading to long running times or memory problems

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement ROCERF for logistic regression on German Credit dataset, compare validity and cost with SCFE under small deletion fraction
  2. Vary hyperparameter k in ROCERF and evaluate impact on validity-cost tradeoff for logistic regression on German Credit
  3. Implement ROCERF for neural networks on Adult dataset, compare with baselines under different deletion fractions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can ROCERF be extended to handle high-dimensional models like neural networks without relying on local linear approximations?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses computational efficiency challenges for high-dimensional models and proposes using local linear approximations via LIME, but this may not capture full model behavior.
- Why unresolved: Theoretical guarantees assume access to full model gradients and Hessian, which are computationally expensive for deep neural networks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of efficient algorithm computing necessary quantities for neural networks without local linearization, plus theoretical analysis.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of model non-convexity on validity and cost guarantees?
- Basis in paper: Provides guarantees for linear models and certain non-linear models with strong convexity assumptions, but neural networks are non-convex.
- Why unresolved: Theoretical analysis relies on strong convexity assumptions that may not hold for complex models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies on non-convex models or theoretical extensions handling non-convexity.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is ROCERF to the choice of hyperparameter k in practice?
- Basis in paper: Authors mention method is not very sensitive to k but don't provide systematic sensitivity analysis.
- Why unresolved: Brief mention without comprehensive empirical evaluation across datasets and model types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying k across multiple orders of magnitude showing relationship with performance.

## Limitations

- Approximation accuracy may degrade for highly non-linear models or large deletion fractions where Taylor approximation becomes inaccurate.
- Theoretical guarantees depend on strong regularity assumptions that may not hold in practice, particularly for complex models.
- Empirical validation covers only three datasets and two model types, limiting generalizability claims.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The fundamental insight about the tension between explanation rights and data deletion is well-established; the core optimization framework is clearly specified.
- **Medium confidence**: Theoretical guarantees for linear models are mathematically sound but depend on regularity assumptions; empirical results are promising but limited in scope.
- **Low confidence**: Performance claims for neural networks are less substantiated with only one non-linear model type tested; efficiency claims need more rigorous benchmarking.

## Next Checks
