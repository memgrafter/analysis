# Co-Design of Approximate Multilayer Perceptron for Ultra-Resource Constrained Printed Circuits

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14576
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14576
- Reference count: 26
- One-line primary result: Achieves 6× lower area and 5.7× lower power consumption with <1% accuracy loss for printed MLPs

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a software/hardware co-design framework for approximate multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) tailored to ultra-resource constrained printed electronics. The approach combines coefficient clustering and retraining with approximate summation techniques to significantly reduce hardware footprint and power consumption while maintaining accuracy. By enabling battery-powered printed MLPs for the first time, the work opens new possibilities for low-cost, non-toxic smart printed applications.

## Method Summary
The framework clusters MLP coefficients based on their hardware efficiency and retrains models to favor more area-efficient coefficients. During inference, neuron summation is approximated by selectively discarding less significant bits. This dual approach of software optimization through retraining and hardware approximation enables substantial reductions in area and power consumption without significant accuracy loss, making complex MLPs feasible on printed circuits with large feature sizes and low integration density.

## Key Results
- 6× lower area and 5.7× lower power consumption compared to exact bespoke circuits
- Less than 1% accuracy loss achieved through approximate summation
- Enables battery-powered printed MLPs for the first time

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework leverages the inherent error tolerance in machine learning models by strategically discarding less significant bits during summation, which has minimal impact on classification accuracy. The coefficient clustering and retraining process ensures that the model adapts to use hardware-efficient parameters, optimizing the trade-off between computational efficiency and performance. This combination allows the system to exploit the specific constraints of printed electronics while maintaining functionality.

## Limitations
- Assumes continued constraints in printed electronics regarding feature sizes and integration density.
- Effectiveness primarily validated on benchmark datasets; domain-specific applications need further testing.
- Approximation techniques may introduce variability in performance across diverse environments.
- Long-term reliability and stability of battery-powered printed MLPs remain unverified.

## Confidence

- High confidence in the co-design framework's ability to reduce area and power consumption with minimal accuracy loss, based on the demonstrated results.
- Medium confidence in the framework's adaptability to various printed electronics applications, pending further validation.
- Low confidence in the scalability of the approach for future technological advancements in printed electronics.
