# Twice Regularized Markov Decision Processes: The Equivalence between Robustness and Regularization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06654
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06654
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: Regularized MDPs with both policy and value regularization can recover robust MDPs with uncertain reward and/or transition functions.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a strong connection between robust Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and twice regularized MDPs. The authors demonstrate that by incorporating both policy and value regularization into MDPs, it is possible to recover robust MDPs that account for uncertainty in both reward and transition functions. This equivalence allows for reducing the computational complexity of robust planning and learning algorithms to that of regularized methods. The proposed R2 MDP framework is shown to achieve robust performance with reduced computational cost compared to standard robust approaches through experiments on tabular and continuous domains.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a twice regularized MDP framework that incorporates both policy and value regularization terms into the Bellman operators. The key idea is to formulate the robust Bellman operators using regularization terms that depend on the uncertainty set. By carefully designing the regularization functions and their corresponding weights, the authors show that the regularized Bellman operators can be made equivalent to the robust Bellman operators for various types of uncertainty sets, including those defined by p-norms and KL-divergence. This equivalence allows for transforming robust MDP problems into regularized MDP problems, which can be solved more efficiently using existing algorithms for regularized MDPs.

## Key Results
- The proposed R2 MDP framework achieves robust performance in the presence of uncertain rewards and/or transitions.
- Experiments demonstrate reduced computational cost compared to standard robust approaches.
- The framework is applicable to both tabular and continuous domains.
