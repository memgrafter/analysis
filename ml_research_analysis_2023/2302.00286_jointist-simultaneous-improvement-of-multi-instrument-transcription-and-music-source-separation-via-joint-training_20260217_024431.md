# Jointist: Simultaneous Improvement of Multi-instrument Transcription and Music Source Separation via Joint Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00286
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00286
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Jointist outperforms existing multi-instrument transcription models on popular music and improves transcription, source separation, downbeat detection, chord recognition, and key estimation when using Jointist's transcription results.

## Executive Summary
Jointist is a multi-instrument framework that simultaneously performs automatic music transcription, instrument recognition, and source separation through joint training. The model consists of three modules - instrument recognition, transcription, and source separation - that are trained together with shared conditioning through FiLM layers. This joint training approach allows the transcription and source separation tasks to mutually improve each other's performance, while the instrument recognition module enables user-controllable transcription for specific instruments.

## Method Summary
Jointist consists of three neural network modules: an instrument recognition module (fIR) with CNN and transformer components, a transcription module (fT) based on onsets and frames with FiLM conditioning, and a source separation module (fMSS) with U-Net architecture also using FiLM. The model is trained jointly on the Slakh2100 dataset using a combined loss function. Audio is processed into log Mel spectrograms for transcription/instrument recognition and STFT spectrograms for source separation. The joint training approach allows the transcription output to guide source separation while cleaner separated audio improves transcription accuracy.

## Key Results
- Jointist outperforms existing multi-instrument transcription models on popular music
- Joint training improves both transcription (F1 scores) and source separation (SDR) compared to standalone training
- Hybrid representations combining audio spectrograms and piano rolls improve downstream MIR tasks like chord recognition and downbeat detection
- User-controllable transcription via instrument conditioning achieves competitive results with automatic selection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint training of transcription and source separation modules mutually improves both tasks
- Mechanism: The transcription module provides accurate piano roll predictions that guide the source separation module to isolate specific instruments, while the source separation task provides cleaner audio signals that improve transcription accuracy
- Core assumption: The transcription and source separation tasks are complementary and benefit from shared learning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The joint training of the transcription and source separation modules serves to improve the performance of both tasks."
  - [section 5.3] "Table 4 shows that when fMSS (TS) is jointly trained with fT, it outperforms a standalone trained fMSS (S)."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on separate architectures rather than joint training benefits
- Break condition: If the transcription output is too noisy early in training, it could degrade source separation performance

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Instrument conditioning via FiLM layers enables flexible, user-controllable transcription
- Mechanism: One-hot instrument vectors are used to condition both transcription and source separation modules through FiLM layers, allowing the model to focus on specific instruments as requested by users
- Core assumption: FiLM conditioning can effectively modulate the network behavior for different instruments
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "This module is conditioned by the one-hot instrument vectors using FiLM"
  - [section 3.3] "FiLM is added to the U-net such that the output source can be controlled by the instrument condition"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers don't explicitly discuss FiLM conditioning for this application
- Break condition: If the conditioning signal is weak or improperly scaled, the model may not effectively separate instruments

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hybrid representations combining audio spectrograms and piano rolls improve downstream MIR tasks
- Mechanism: Piano rolls provide explicit rhythmic and harmonic information that complements audio spectrograms, creating a richer representation for tasks like chord recognition and downbeat detection
- Core assumption: Symbolic information from piano rolls contains complementary information to audio spectrograms
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.1] "We observe that the model with the hybrid representation can consistently outperform the one that uses only spectrograms"
  - [section 6.2] "Using piano roll-only yields competitive results" for music classification
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers don't extensively explore hybrid audio-symbolic representations
- Break condition: If piano rolls contain errors, they could degrade rather than improve downstream task performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions but raises several implicit ones through its discussion of limitations and future work.

## Limitations
- The model is trained and evaluated on synthesized data (Slakh2100) rather than real recordings, limiting generalizability
- Subjective listening tests involved only 8 participants, providing limited statistical power
- The paper lacks detailed architectural specifications for some components, particularly the CNN and transformer configurations in the instrument recognition module

## Confidence
- High confidence: The joint training mechanism showing mutual improvement of transcription and source separation tasks
- Medium confidence: The FiLM conditioning approach for instrument-specific control
- Medium confidence: The downstream task improvements using hybrid representations

## Next Checks
