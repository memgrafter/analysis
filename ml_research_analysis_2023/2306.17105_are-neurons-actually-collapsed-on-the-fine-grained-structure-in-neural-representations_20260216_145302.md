# Are Neurons Actually Collapsed? On the Fine-Grained Structure in Neural Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17105
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17105
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Neural representations retain fine-grained structure within collapsed clusters, enabling 93% reconstruction of original labels

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the prevailing view of Neural Collapse by demonstrating that last-layer neural representations preserve fine-grained structure corresponding to original class distinctions, even after Neural Collapse occurs. Through experiments on modified CIFAR-10 datasets with coarse and fine labels, the authors show that small variations in representations faithfully capture the intrinsic structure of input distribution. They can reconstruct original 10-class labels with 93% accuracy using unsupervised clustering and linear probing on representations trained with only 5 coarse labels, nearly matching the performance of models trained on the original dataset.

## Method Summary
The authors investigate representation structure by training standard CNNs (ResNet-18, DenseNet-121, VGG-18) on modified CIFAR-10 datasets with coarse (5 classes) and fine (20 classes) labels. They use SGD optimization with various learning rates and weight decay parameters over 350 epochs. The analysis pipeline includes computing NC1/NC2 metrics to verify Neural Collapse, creating class distance matrices to measure representation structure, applying t-SNE visualization for qualitative analysis, and using a Cluster-and-Linear-Probe (CLP) test to quantify preserved class information through unsupervised clustering and linear probing.

## Key Results
- Representations trained with coarse labels can reconstruct original 10-class labels with 93% accuracy
- Fine-grained structure within collapsed clusters depends on semantic similarity between sub-classes
- Input distribution effects on representations emerge earlier in training than label-driven Neural Collapse effects
- The phenomenon is observed across ResNet and DenseNet architectures but not VGG, suggesting architectural dependence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Fine-grained Representation Preservation Despite Neural Collapse
- Claim: Last-layer representations retain fine-grained structure corresponding to original class distinctions even after Neural Collapse
- Core assumption: Fine-grained structure is not completely overwritten by Neural Collapse dynamics
- Evidence: 93% reconstruction accuracy using unsupervised clustering on coarse-label representations

### Mechanism 2: Temporal Hierarchy of Input vs. Label Effects
- Claim: Input distribution effects emerge earlier in training than label-driven Neural Collapse effects
- Core assumption: Networks first learn input structure before being fully constrained by optimization objectives
- Evidence: Class distance matrices show staggered emergence of fine-grained vs. coarse structure

### Mechanism 3: Semantic Similarity Influences Fine-grained Structure
- Claim: Fine-grained structure within super-classes depends on semantic similarity between sub-classes
- Core assumption: Network representations reflect semantic relationships that align with human perception
- Evidence: Dissimilar sub-classes show clearer separation than semantically similar ones

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What architectural or training factors cause VGG to fail to exhibit fine-grained structure while ResNet and DenseNet show it?
- Basis in paper: The authors note VGG is a "counter example" that only displays Neural Collapse without distinguishing original classes within super-classes
- Why unresolved: Requires further exploration without theoretical or empirical analysis of why VGG behaves differently
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying VGG's architectural parameters and training configurations

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the semantic similarity threshold between sub-classes affect the emergence of fine-grained representation structure?
- Basis in paper: Qualitative observations show random merging produces better separation than semantic merging in CIFAR-100
- Why unresolved: No quantitative characterization of the similarity threshold required for structure to emerge
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying semantic similarity through controlled Gaussian mixture parameters

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does fine-grained representation structure persist beyond the training distribution to unseen test data and out-of-distribution samples?
- Basis in paper: Hui et al. (2022) found Neural Collapse occurs only on training set, not test set
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on training set representations without examining generalization
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments measuring structure persistence on test sets and systematically corrupted inputs

## Limitations

- The paper's claims rely heavily on visualization quality (t-SNE) and clustering effectiveness, both sensitive to hyperparameters
- The synthetic theory section uses highly simplified assumptions that may not capture real neural network behavior
- Semantic similarity interpretation depends on aligning model representations with human judgments, introducing subjective elements

## Confidence

- High confidence: Empirical demonstration that representations retain fine-grained structure within collapsed clusters (93% reconstruction accuracy)
- Medium confidence: Theoretical framework explaining why fine-grained structure persists, given simplified assumptions
- Medium confidence: Temporal dynamics showing input effects emerge before label effects, requiring careful training curve analysis
