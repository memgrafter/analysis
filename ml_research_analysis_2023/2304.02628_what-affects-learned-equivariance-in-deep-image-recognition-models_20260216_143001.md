# What Affects Learned Equivariance in Deep Image Recognition Models?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02628
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02628
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents a new measure for learned equivariance in neural networks, based on Pearson correlation between feature maps. The authors show that this measure is consistent across network depths, unlike previous measures based on cosine similarity. Using this measure, they find that learned translation equivariance in intermediate features of CNNs correlates with ImageNet accuracy. They also investigate what factors increase learned equivariance, and find that data augmentation, reduced model capacity, and the inductive bias of convolutions lead to more learned equivariance. The authors conclude that learned equivariance is an important factor for neural network performance, and that it can be increased through careful design of the training process.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a new measure for learned equivariance in neural networks using Pearson correlation between feature maps, which demonstrates consistency across network depths unlike previous cosine similarity measures. The authors show that learned translation equivariance in intermediate features of CNNs correlates with ImageNet accuracy. Through systematic investigation, they identify three key factors that increase learned equivariance: data augmentation, reduced model capacity, and the convolutional inductive bias. The study concludes that learned equivariance is an important factor for neural network performance that can be deliberately enhanced through training process design.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a novel measure for learned equivariance based on Pearson correlation between feature maps extracted from neural networks. This measure is evaluated across different network depths and compared against traditional cosine similarity approaches. The methodology involves training various CNN architectures under different conditions (with/without data augmentation, varying model capacities) and measuring their learned equivariance properties. The correlation between learned equivariance in intermediate features and ImageNet classification accuracy is then analyzed. The experimental design systematically varies training conditions and architectural choices to identify factors that influence the development of equivariance in learned representations.

## Key Results
- Pearson correlation measure for learned equivariance shows consistency across network depths, unlike previous cosine similarity measures
- Learned translation equivariance in intermediate CNN features correlates with ImageNet accuracy
- Data augmentation, reduced model capacity, and convolutional inductive bias increase learned equivariance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Learned equivariance emerges as a beneficial inductive bias that allows networks to generalize better to transformations of input data. The Pearson correlation measure captures how consistently feature maps respond to spatial translations, providing a more stable metric than cosine similarity which can vary with network depth. When networks learn representations that maintain equivariance, they develop features that are more robust to geometric transformations, leading to improved generalization and higher accuracy on tasks like ImageNet classification. The identified factors (data augmentation, reduced capacity, convolutional bias) encourage the network to learn more translation-equivariant representations by either exposing the model to more diverse transformations during training or constraining its representational capacity to favor more general, equivariant features.

## Foundational Learning

**Pearson correlation** - A statistical measure of linear correlation between two variables, ranging from -1 to 1. Why needed: Provides the mathematical foundation for the new equivariance measure that captures linear relationships between feature maps. Quick check: Verify that correlation values are bounded between -1 and 1 in all experiments.

**Translation equivariance** - The property where a function's response to a translated input is the same as the translated response to the original input. Why needed: The core concept being measured and analyzed throughout the paper. Quick check: Confirm that convolution operations inherently satisfy translation equivariance.

**Cosine similarity** - A measure of similarity between two vectors based on the cosine of the angle between them. Why needed: Serves as the baseline comparison metric against which the new Pearson correlation measure is evaluated. Quick check: Verify that cosine similarity values are bounded between -1 and 1.

**Inductive bias** - The set of assumptions that a learning algorithm uses to predict outputs given inputs it has not encountered. Why needed: Central to understanding how architectural choices and training procedures influence learned representations. Quick check: Identify specific inductive biases in convolutional architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input images -> Convolutional layers -> Feature maps -> Pearson correlation computation -> Equivariance measurement -> ImageNet classification

**Critical path:** Data augmentation and training process -> Model architecture (convolutions, capacity) -> Learned feature representations -> Equivariance measurement -> Classification accuracy

**Design tradeoffs:** Higher model capacity vs. learned equivariance (reduced capacity increases equivariance but may limit representational power), data augmentation intensity vs. training efficiency, Pearson correlation vs. cosine similarity as measurement metrics

**Failure signatures:** Inconsistent equivariance measures across network depths, negative correlation between equivariance and accuracy, failure of Pearson correlation to capture meaningful equivariance relationships, overfitting when capacity is too low despite high equivariance

## Limitations
- The correlation between learned equivariance and ImageNet accuracy may not generalize across different architectures or datasets
- The relationship could be influenced by confounding factors not fully controlled in experiments
- Findings primarily focus on CNNs, with unclear applicability to vision transformers or modern architectures

## Confidence
- High confidence: The new Pearson correlation measure provides a more consistent assessment of equivariance across network depths
- Medium confidence: The correlation between learned equivariance and ImageNet accuracy, and the identification of factors affecting equivariance
- Medium confidence: The conclusion that learned equivariance is an important factor for neural network performance
