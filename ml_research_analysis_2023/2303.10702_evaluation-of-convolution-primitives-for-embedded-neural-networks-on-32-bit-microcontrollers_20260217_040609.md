# Evaluation of Convolution Primitives for Embedded Neural Networks on 32-bit Microcontrollers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.10702
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10702
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: Five convolution primitives evaluated on ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers, showing linear correlation between MACs and energy consumption for non-SIMD MCUs

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates five convolution primitives for embedded neural networks on ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers, implementing them using NNoM library and CMSIS-NN middleware. The research demonstrates that for MCUs without SIMD instructions, theoretical multiply-accumulate operations (MACs) linearly correlate with energy consumption, while SIMD-enabled processors show latency as a better performance indicator. The findings highlight the importance of compiler optimization and maximum MCU frequency in reducing energy consumption for embedded neural networks.

## Method Summary
The research implements and benchmarks five convolution primitives - standard, grouped, depthwise separable, shift, and add convolutions - on ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers using the NNoM library and CMSIS-NN middleware. Performance metrics including latency, energy consumption, and computational efficiency are measured across different MCU configurations with and without SIMD instructions. The study systematically compares these primitives under controlled conditions to identify optimal choices for various hardware constraints.

## Key Results
- For MCUs without SIMD instructions, theoretical MACs show linear correlation with energy consumption
- Shift convolution demonstrates high efficiency among evaluated primitives
- With SIMD instructions, latency becomes a more reliable performance indicator than MAC count due to varying data reuse efficiency
- Compiler optimization and maximum MCU frequency significantly impact energy consumption reduction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The linear relationship between MACs and energy consumption in non-SIMD MCUs occurs because each arithmetic operation consumes similar energy regardless of data reuse patterns. For SIMD-enabled processors, parallel execution and varying data reuse efficiency disrupt this linearity, making latency a better indicator as it captures pipeline utilization and memory access patterns. Compiler optimizations reduce energy by minimizing redundant operations and improving instruction scheduling.

Component map: ARM Cortex-M MCU -> CMSIS-NN Library -> NNoM Framework -> Convolution Primitives -> Performance Metrics

Critical path: Input data → Memory subsystem → Arithmetic units → Output results → Energy measurement system

Design tradeoffs: SIMDs vs. energy efficiency (parallelism increases latency but reduces energy per operation), compiler optimization vs. code size (aggressive optimization reduces energy but increases compilation time), primitive selection vs. accuracy (simpler primitives save energy but may reduce model accuracy)

Failure signatures: Unexpected energy consumption spikes (likely memory access patterns), latency variations across identical runs (potential cache interference), inconsistent MAC counts (possible implementation errors in convolution functions)

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to ARM Cortex-M architecture, limiting generalizability to other microcontroller platforms
- Energy measurements conducted in controlled laboratory settings, potentially not reflecting real-world operating conditions
- Compiler optimization impact quantified but not systematically explored across multiple optimization levels
- Linear MAC-energy correlation may not hold for more complex neural network architectures or different quantization schemes

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Comparative performance of convolution primitives on ARM Cortex-M with/without SIMD instructions
- **Medium confidence**: Linear correlation between theoretical MACs and energy consumption for non-SIMD MCUs
- **Medium confidence**: Importance of compiler optimization and maximum frequency for energy reduction
