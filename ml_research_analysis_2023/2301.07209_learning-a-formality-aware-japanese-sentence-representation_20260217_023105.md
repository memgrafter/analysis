# Learning a Formality-Aware Japanese Sentence Representation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07209
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07209
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Proposed sequence-to-sequence method with formality-aware sentence representations achieves up to 83% formality preservation and 77.6 BLEU on Japanese-to-Japanese reconstruction task.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of preserving formality in Japanese sentence representations for downstream tasks like machine translation. The authors propose a novel sequence-to-sequence approach that conditions sentence generation on both the input sentence representation and a side constraint guiding formality preservation. They introduce a learnable formality mask that can be applied additively or multiplicatively to the encoder output. To overcome the lack of formality-annotated parallel data, they adapt previous works on procedural formality classification of Japanese sentences. Experimental results demonstrate that their techniques effectively help the decoder recover input sentence formality while slightly improving semantic preservation.

## Method Summary
The method employs a Transformer encoder-decoder architecture with 1 layer each, hidden size 512, and 8 attention heads. The key innovation is a learnable formality mask applied to the encoder output, which can operate in additive or multiplicative modes. The model is trained using a joint objective combining reconstruction loss and formality classification loss, with λ weights ranging from 0.01 to 0.99. Formality labels are generated using a rule-based classifier adapted from previous work on Japanese formality detection. The model is evaluated on a Japanese sentence reconstruction task, measuring both BLEU scores for semantic preservation and formality classification accuracy for style preservation.

## Key Results
- Achieved up to 83% of sentences with correct formality preservation
- Obtained BLEU scores of up to 77.6 on Japanese-to-Japanese sentence reconstruction
- Demonstrated that formality augmentation techniques help both formality recovery and semantic preservation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The additive formality mask works by shifting the sentence embedding in a direction proportional to the formality class.
- Mechanism: The encoder output vector is adjusted by adding a learnable formality vector (r0 for informal, r1 for formal) based on the desired formality. This creates a redundancy in the embedding space that helps the decoder recover formality information.
- Core assumption: Sentence semantics and formality can be linearly separated in the embedding space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The motivation behind an additive formality augmentation is simple: both semantics and style have long been shown to be recoverable with linear vector arithmetic on the embedding space"
  - [section]: "A global shift for the encoder output within the sentence embedding space, with its magnitude and direction defined during training, creates a redundancy for the output words that are affected by formality"
  - [corpus]: Weak - the corpus provides evidence of Japanese formality markers but doesn't directly support the linear separability assumption
- Break condition: If formality information is not linearly separable from semantics in the embedding space, or if the formality markers don't consistently correspond to specific dimensions in the embedding.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The joint training objective of reconstruction + formality classification preserves formality information in the intermediate representation.
- Mechanism: By optimizing both the reconstruction quality and the correctness of formality classification simultaneously, the model learns to maintain formality information in the sentence representation while preserving semantics.
- Core assumption: The formality classifier can effectively extract formality from the sentence representation during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Experimental results suggest that our techniques not only helps the decoder recover the formality of the input sentence, but also slightly improves the preservation of input sentence semantics"
  - [section]: "Performance on the task is measured by the proportion of correctly classified sentences"
  - [section]: "We observe that our formality classifier is highly accurate (as can be seen in Table1)"
- Break condition: If the formality classifier becomes inaccurate during training, or if the joint optimization creates a conflict between reconstruction and formality preservation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using formality correctness of output sentences as a side constraint allows the model to learn formality-aware representations without overfitting to formality labels.
- Mechanism: The model is trained to minimize both reconstruction loss and the formality classification loss of the output sentences, which encourages the model to generate sentences with the correct formality while preserving meaning.
- Core assumption: The output sentence formality can be accurately evaluated during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "By letting the side constraint be the formality of output sentences rather than the output of a classifier which works with the input sentence representation, we address the potential overfitting issues"
  - [section]: "we define the formality classifier c : X → S as follows: let autoencoder g := X→ X be the composition between an encoder eg : X→ Rk and a decoder dg : Rk→ X"
  - [corpus]: Weak - the corpus provides data for training but doesn't directly support the effectiveness of this mechanism
- Break condition: If the formality classifier used during training becomes inaccurate, or if the side constraint creates too strong a bias toward formality at the expense of reconstruction quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions including how effective the techniques would be for languages other than Japanese, how Japanese and English formality classes map onto each other, and how different methods of extracting formality compare in terms of accuracy and effectiveness for Japanese formality transfer.

## Limitations
- Reliance on rule-based formality classifier that may not capture full complexity of Japanese formality markers, particularly honorifics and contextual politeness
- Experimental evaluation limited to Japanese-to-Japanese reconstruction tasks, making it difficult to assess performance on actual machine translation
- Potential bias in formality classification due to limited representation of nuanced formality markers in training data
