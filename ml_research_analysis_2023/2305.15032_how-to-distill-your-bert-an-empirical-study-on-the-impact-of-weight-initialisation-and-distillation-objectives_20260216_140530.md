# How to Distill your BERT: An Empirical Study on the Impact of Weight Initialisation and Distillation Objectives

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15032
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15032
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Attention transfer is the most effective distillation objective, especially when initializing student models with lower teacher layers in task-specific settings

## Executive Summary
This paper conducts a comprehensive empirical evaluation of knowledge distillation objectives for compressing BERT models, examining both task-specific and task-agnostic settings. The authors systematically investigate the impact of layer choice for student initialization from teacher layers and identify attention transfer as the most consistently effective objective across different initialization settings. Their efficient distillation framework reduces training time from 720 to 58 GPU hours while achieving comparable or better results. The study provides practical guidance for selecting distillation objectives and initialization strategies, recommending attention transfer for task-specific distillation and last-layer attention transfer with MSE loss for task-agnostic distillation.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates multiple knowledge distillation objectives including vanilla KD, hidden states transfer (Hid-CLS, Hid-Seq), attention transfer (Att-MSE, Att-KL), and value-relation transfer (Val-KL) on GLUE benchmark tasks. For task-specific distillation, they use RoBERTaBASE as teacher with 3-layer student models, training for 10 epochs with Fairseq implementation. Task-agnostic distillation uses BERTbase as teacher with 6-layer student models, training for 100k steps with learning rate 5e-4. The authors systematically test different initialization strategies including random initialization, every-k-th layer initialization, and first-k-layers initialization, providing comprehensive comparisons across 7 GLUE tasks (CoLA, SST-2, MRPC, QQP, MNLI, QNLI, RTE).

## Key Results
- Attention transfer consistently outperforms other objectives across different initialization settings, particularly in task-specific distillation
- Initializing students with lower teacher layers (e.g., 1,2,3) significantly improves performance for vanilla KD and hidden states transfer objectives
- Task-agnostic distillation shows less sensitivity to initialization choice compared to task-specific distillation
- The proposed efficient distillation framework achieves comparable results to previous methods while reducing training time from 720 to 58 GPU hours

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Lower teacher layer initialization significantly improves task-specific distillation performance
- Mechanism: Lower BERT layers capture more general linguistic features while higher layers encode task-specific information. Initializing student models with lower layers provides a better foundation for learning task-specific knowledge during distillation.
- Core assumption: The knowledge distribution across teacher layers follows a hierarchical pattern where lower layers contain more transferable general knowledge
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "initializing students with lower teacher layers significantly improves performance in task-specific distillation"
  - [section]: "We observe that initialization of layers has a huge impact in the task-specific setting. The performance of vanilla KD and Hidden states transfer was significantly improved when initializing from lower layers of the teacher (e.g. from 68.1% to 85.9% on QNLI for Vanilla KD)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus neighbors discussing layer initialization impact
- Break condition: If the task requires highly specialized knowledge that is encoded primarily in higher layers, or if the student architecture cannot effectively utilize lower-layer features

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Attention transfer performs consistently well regardless of initialization setting
- Mechanism: Attention matrices capture rich linguistic knowledge about word relationships and syntactic structures. MSE-based attention transfer preserves these relationships effectively during distillation, making it robust to initialization choices.
- Core assumption: Attention mechanisms encode task-agnostic linguistic knowledge that is consistently valuable across different initialization strategies
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "attention transfer performs robustly regardless of initialization choice"
  - [section]: "In the task-specific setting, attention transfer is the best choice with initialization from every-k-th teacher layer"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - related work "MST-Distill: Mixture of Specialized Teachers for Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation" suggests attention-based approaches are widely effective
- Break condition: If the target task requires attention patterns significantly different from general linguistic patterns, or if the MSE loss fails to capture the most important attention relationships

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Task-agnostic distillation benefits less from lower layer initialization compared to task-specific
- Mechanism: Task-agnostic distillation occurs during pre-training where the model learns general language representations. The extensive pre-training process allows randomly initialized students to develop effective representations regardless of initialization source.
- Core assumption: The pre-training phase provides sufficient optimization to overcome initialization differences in task-agnostic settings
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We only observe considerable improvement with the objective HidCLS, which performs poorly when randomly initialized, compared to other objectives"
  - [abstract]: "we recommend attention transfer for task-specific distillation and last-layer attention transfer with MSE loss for task-agnostic distillation"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - related work "MLKD-BERT: Multi-level Knowledge Distillation for Pre-trained Language Models" suggests initialization matters differently across distillation settings
- Break condition: If the pre-training process is significantly shortened or if the student architecture has limited capacity to learn from random initialization

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Teacher model (BERT/RoBERTa) with multiple layers
  - Student model (smaller transformer) with configurable layer count
  - Distillation framework with multiple objectives (vanilla KD, hidden states transfer, attention transfer)
  - Training pipeline supporting both task-specific and task-agnostic settings
  - Evaluation framework on GLUE benchmark

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained teacher model
  2. Configure student model architecture
  3. Select initialization strategy (random vs teacher layers)
  4. Choose distillation objective(s)
  5. Run distillation training
  6. Fine-tune on target task (for task-agnostic setting)
  7. Evaluate performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Task-specific vs task-agnostic: Task-specific allows for task-specific optimization but requires per-task training; task-agnostic is more efficient but may sacrifice some task-specific performance
  - Attention vs hidden states transfer: Attention transfer is more robust but may miss some representation-level information; hidden states transfer captures more but is initialization-sensitive
  - Lower vs higher layer initialization: Lower layers provide better foundation but may miss task-specific knowledge in higher layers

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance across all tasks: Likely issues with model architecture mismatch or incorrect hyperparameter settings
  - Good performance only on some tasks: May indicate initialization layer choice mismatch with task requirements
  - Training instability or divergence: Could be caused by learning rate issues or sequence length problems

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run vanilla KD with every-4th layer initialization on QNLI to verify basic distillation functionality
  2. Compare attention transfer vs hidden states transfer with same initialization to understand objective impact
  3. Test lower layer initialization (1,2,3) vs higher layer initialization (4,8,12) with vanilla KD to confirm initialization findings

## Limitations
- Findings primarily based on BERT/RoBERTa architectures, limiting generalization to other model families
- Focus on relatively small GLUE tasks may not reflect performance on larger, more complex benchmarks
- Computational efficiency gains come with potential tradeoffs in model capacity that weren't fully explored

## Confidence
- High confidence: The general finding that initialization layer choice matters significantly for task-specific distillation
- Medium confidence: The specific ranking of objectives (attention transfer > hidden states > vanilla KD) across all initialization settings
- Medium confidence: That the initialization benefits are primarily due to lower layers capturing more transferable linguistic features

## Next Checks
1. Test the initialization findings on a different transformer architecture (e.g., DeBERTa or T5) to verify the layer choice recommendations generalize beyond BERT
2. Conduct ablation studies on the pre-training duration for task-agnostic distillation to determine if initialization benefits persist with shorter pre-training schedules
3. Evaluate the distilled models on a larger, more challenging benchmark (e.g., SuperGLUE or BigBench) to assess whether the observed performance patterns hold for more complex tasks
