# Learning a model is paramount for sample efficiency in reinforcement learning control of PDEs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07160
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07160
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Model-based RL with learned dynamics models achieves 9× better data efficiency than model-free methods for controlling the chaotic Kuramoto-Sivashinsky PDE

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that learning a dynamics model in parallel with a reinforcement learning (RL) agent significantly improves sample efficiency for controlling partial differential equations (PDEs). The authors show that a convolutional LSTM-based surrogate model can generate synthetic experience, reducing the amount of real system data needed by nine-fold compared to model-free methods like SAC and PPO. The learned control strategies can reduce system dissipation and power consumption by over 63%, making the approach both data-efficient and effective for complex PDE control tasks.

## Method Summary
The approach combines a convolutional LSTM-based surrogate model with an RL agent (Soft Actor-Critic) for PDE control. The dynamics model learns to predict future states of the PDE system, generating synthetic experience that supplements real data collected from the system. The model is iteratively updated during training to stay aligned with the agent's current policy and avoid biases. Key architectural choices include convolutional networks for spatial structure, residual prediction for temporal smoothness, and ensemble methods for robustness. The method is tested on the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, achieving significant improvements in sample efficiency and control performance.

## Key Results
- 9× improvement in data efficiency compared to model-free SAC and PPO methods
- Over 63% reduction in system dissipation and power consumption through learned control strategies
- Ablation studies identify convolutional networks, residual prediction, and ensemble methods as critical modeling techniques

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Learning a dynamics model in parallel with the RL agent reduces the total amount of required data sampled from the real system.
- Mechanism: The surrogate model learns to predict future states of the PDE system, allowing the RL agent to generate synthetic experience through model rollouts instead of relying solely on costly real-world interactions. This synthetic data supplements the real data collected, enabling the agent to learn effective policies with fewer actual system interactions.
- Core assumption: The learned dynamics model is sufficiently accurate to generate useful synthetic experience that helps the RL agent learn better policies.
- Evidence anchors:

  - [section]: "We present a solution for the first issue using a data-driven surrogate model in the form of a convolutional LSTM with actuation. We demonstrate that learning an actuated model in parallel to training the RL agent significantly reduces the total amount of required data sampled from the real system."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Corpus papers discuss model-based RL and reduced-order modeling, but none directly support the specific claim of parallel learning of dynamics models with RL agents.
- Break condition: The dynamics model becomes too inaccurate, leading to synthetic experience that misleads the RL agent, potentially causing worse performance than model-free methods.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Iteratively updating the dynamics model during training is crucial to avoid biases in the RL training process.
- Mechanism: As the RL agent explores the environment, it visits new states and experiences new dynamics. By iteratively updating the dynamics model with this new data, the model remains aligned with the agent's current policy and the distribution of states it encounters. This prevents the model from becoming biased towards the initial state distribution or outdated dynamics.
- Core assumption: The RL agent's policy and the distribution of states it encounters change over time, necessitating continuous updates to the dynamics model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Furthermore, we show that iteratively updating the model is of major importance to avoid biases in the RL training."
  - [section]: "We found that striking a balance between regular model updates but keeping shifts in the distribution of artificial samples minor helped stabilize the optimization."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Corpus papers discuss model-based RL but do not explicitly mention the importance of iterative model updates to avoid biases.
- Break condition: The model updates become too infrequent or the agent's policy changes too rapidly, causing the model to become significantly misaligned with the current dynamics.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The convolutional LSTM architecture with residual prediction and ensemble methods is crucial for accurate and efficient learning of the PDE dynamics.
- Mechanism: Convolutional networks are well-suited to capture the spatial structure and local physics of PDEs. Residual prediction enforces temporal smoothness and helps the model focus on learning the changes in the system rather than the entire state, which improves accuracy and reduces the impact of compounding errors. Ensemble methods help mitigate model exploitation and improve the robustness of the predictions.
- Core assumption: The PDE dynamics have local spatial dependencies and can be effectively captured by convolutional networks. Residual prediction helps the model learn the dynamics more accurately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Detailed ablation studies reveal the most important ingredients of the modeling process."
  - [section]: "Convolutional models (top row) prove far more effective than fully connected networks (bottom row) in both the small as well as large data regime, learning temporal residuals or not. In terms of the comparison between residual and full-state prediction (left vs. right column), the former yields a notable advantage for forecasts over short time durations with error margins being negligible for several time units."
  - [corpus]: Weak. Corpus papers discuss convolutional networks and model-based RL, but do not specifically address the use of residual prediction or ensemble methods for PDE dynamics.
- Break condition: The PDE dynamics are not well-captured by convolutional networks, or the residual prediction approach is not effective for the specific PDE system.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - PDE system (Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation) -> Dynamics model (convolutional LSTM) -> RL agent (SAC) -> Data collection (real and synthetic experience) -> Model learning (teacher forcing, curriculum learning) -> Policy optimization (real and synthetic experience)

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize the dynamics model and RL agent
  2. Collect initial data from the PDE system using the RL agent
  3. Train the dynamics model on the collected data
  4. Generate synthetic experience using the dynamics model and the current policy
  5. Update the RL agent's policy using both real and synthetic experience
  6. Repeat steps 2-5 until the desired performance is achieved

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model complexity vs. data efficiency: More complex models may require more data but can potentially capture the dynamics more accurately.
  - Model update frequency vs. stability: Frequent model updates can keep the model aligned with the agent's policy but may also introduce instability.
  - Residual prediction vs. full-state prediction: Residual prediction can improve accuracy and reduce compounding errors but may require more careful tuning.

- Failure signatures:
  - Model exploitation: The RL agent learns to exploit inaccuracies in the dynamics model, leading to poor performance on the real system.
  - Model bias: The dynamics model becomes biased towards the initial state distribution or outdated dynamics, leading to poor generalization.
  - Overfitting: The dynamics model overfits to the training data, leading to poor performance on unseen states.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the prediction accuracy of the dynamics model on a held-out test set, comparing different architectures (e.g., convolutional vs. fully connected) and prediction methods (e.g., residual vs. full-state).
  2. Assess the sample efficiency of the model-based RL approach by comparing the performance of the RL agent trained with synthetic experience to a model-free baseline.
  3. Investigate the impact of model update frequency on the stability and performance of the RL agent by varying the number of gradient updates performed on the dynamics model per iteration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal model capacity (number of parameters) for balancing prediction accuracy and computational efficiency in PDE control tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper studies models with varying widths (β = 1.5 to 4.0) and finds that performance plateaus after β ≈ 3.0, while computation time increases exponentially.
- Why unresolved: The study only examined one PDE system (Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation), so it's unclear if these results generalize to other PDE control problems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing across multiple PDE systems with varying complexity, spatial dimensions, and chaotic behaviors would show whether the β ≈ 3.0 finding is broadly applicable or system-dependent.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different neural network architectures (e.g., CNNs vs. Koopman operator-based models) compare in terms of data efficiency and control performance for PDE systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest Koopman operator methods as promising candidates for future work, noting they can learn linear models of nonlinear systems which would be computationally efficient.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates CNN-based architectures and doesn't compare them against alternative approaches like Koopman operator methods or sparse regression techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct empirical comparisons between CNN-based models and Koopman operator or sparse regression approaches on the same PDE control tasks, measuring both sample efficiency and control performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal trade-off between model-based and model-free components in reinforcement learning for PDE control?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors use SAC as the model-free component but note that model-based RL is still inferior in situations where the model is comparatively easy to simulate, suggesting an interesting balance point exists.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a fixed architecture combining SAC with their learned surrogate but doesn't explore how varying the relative contributions of model-based vs. model-free components affects performance across different scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies varying the weight given to model-based rollouts versus direct environment interactions, across different PDE systems with varying simulation costs and control complexity.

## Limitations
- The approach is validated on a single PDE system (Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation), raising questions about generalizability to other PDEs.
- The optimal frequency and magnitude of model updates for balancing alignment with stability are not clearly established.
- The specific architectural choices are not thoroughly justified or compared against alternatives in the ablation studies.

## Confidence
- High: The overall approach of using learned dynamics models in parallel with RL agents improves sample efficiency in PDE control.
- Medium: The specific architectural choices (convolutional LSTM, residual prediction, ensemble methods) are important for accurate and efficient learning of PDE dynamics.
- Low: The generalizability of the approach to other PDE systems and the optimal frequency of model updates are well-established.
