# Use of Speech Impairment Severity for Dysarthric Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10659
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10659
- Reference count: 0
- This paper achieves 17.82% overall WER (51.25% on very low intelligibility) on UASpeech, the lowest published performance to date.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates techniques for incorporating speech impairment severity into state-of-the-art ASR systems for dysarthric speech recognition. The proposed techniques include multitask training with severity prediction, speaker-severity aware auxiliary feature adaptation, and structured LHUC transforms conditioned on both speaker-identity and severity. Experiments on the UASpeech dataset demonstrate statistically significant WER reductions up to 4.78% (14.03% relative) when incorporating severity information, with the best system achieving 17.82% overall WER through two-pass rescoring of hybrid DNN, E2E Conformer, and fine-tuned Wav2vec 2.0 systems.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three techniques for incorporating speech impairment severity into ASR systems: multitask training with severity prediction loss, speaker-severity aware auxiliary feature adaptation using spectral basis embedding (SBE) features, and structured LHUC transforms separately conditioned on speaker-identity and severity. These techniques are applied to hybrid DNN, E2E Conformer, and Wav2vec 2.0 ASR systems trained on the UASpeech dataset. The best-performing systems are combined via two-pass rescoring to achieve the lowest published WER on this challenging dysarthric speech corpus.

## Key Results
- Incorporating speech impairment severity produced statistically significant WER reductions up to 4.78% (14.03% relative)
- Best-performing system achieved 17.82% overall WER (51.25% on very low intelligibility)
- Two-pass rescoring combining hybrid DNN, E2E Conformer, and fine-tuned Wav2vec 2.0 systems achieved the lowest published performance on UASpeech

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Incorporating severity information reduces WER by addressing heterogeneity from both speaker-identity and speech impairment severity
- Mechanism: The system disentangles variability into speaker-identity and severity components, allowing separate conditioning in LHUC transforms and auxiliary features, thus improving model adaptation
- Core assumption: Speech impairment severity and speaker-identity are statistically independent sources of variability that can be modeled separately and recombined
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A key challenge in dysarthric speech recognition is the speaker-level diversity attributed to both speaker-identity associated factors such as gender, and speech impairment severity."
  - [section] "So far the majority of prior researches to address the dysarthric speaker level diversity have been focused on using speaker-identity only either in speaker-dependent (SD) data augmentation [7, 9, 13, 14, 18, 27], or in speaker adapted or dependent ASR system development [1, 3, 4, 7, 11–13, 19, 22, 25, 31–33]."
- Break condition: If severity and speaker-identity are not statistically independent, the separate conditioning may fail to capture their interaction effects

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multitask training incorporating severity prediction error improves model generalization across different severity levels
- Mechanism: By adding a severity prediction loss to the training objective, the model learns severity-invariant representations, enabling better performance on unseen speakers with varying severity
- Core assumption: Severity is a useful auxiliary task that helps the model learn more generalizable features
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a) multi-task [39] training cost interpolation between the ASR loss and speech impairment severity prediction error;"
  - [section] "incorporatingLSeve in the loss reduces the risk of overfitting to unreliable frame-level tri-states, while incorporatingLSeve increases the generalization ability of the model to test speakers with diverse severity levels."
- Break condition: If the severity labels are noisy or the severity task is too easy/hard relative to ASR, the multitask learning may not help or could even hurt performance

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Speaker-severity aware auxiliary features improve front-end adaptation to impaired speech characteristics
- Mechanism: Spectral basis embedding (SBE) features, trained to predict both speaker-identity and severity, provide richer auxiliary information than traditional iVectors or xVectors
- Core assumption: SBE features capture latent attributes of impaired speech more effectively than traditional speaker embeddings
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "b) speaker-severity aware auxiliary feature adaptation;"
  - [section] "The resulting spectral basis deep embedding (SBE) features are more effective in encoding latent attributes of impaired speech [25] than classical speaker embeddings such as iVectors [44] and xVectors [45]."
- Break condition: If the SVD decomposition or the SBE network fails to capture the relevant variability, the auxiliary features may not improve performance

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Raw speech -> Spectral Basis Embedding Network -> Auxiliary Features -> ASR System (Hybrid DNN/E2E Conformer/Wav2vec 2.0) -> WER Evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess speech data (silence stripping, data augmentation)
  2. Train SBE network to generate speaker-severity aware auxiliary features
  3. Train hybrid DNN, E2E Conformer, and Wav2vec 2.0 models with severity incorporation
  4. Combine the best-performing models via two-pass rescoring

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using severity information vs. relying solely on speaker-identity adaptation
  - Incorporating severity through auxiliary features vs. multitask learning vs. structured LHUC transforms
  - Training separate severity-dependent models vs. joint modeling with severity as an additional factor

- Failure signatures:
  - No improvement or degradation in WER when incorporating severity information
  - Overfitting to the training data, especially when using severity-dependent models
  - Poor generalization to unseen speakers with different severity levels

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a baseline hybrid DNN system without severity information and compare its performance to a system with severity incorporated through auxiliary features
  2. Train a baseline E2E Conformer system and compare its performance to a system with severity incorporated through multitask learning
  3. Fine-tune a Wav2vec 2.0 pre-trained model with and without severity prediction in the training objective and compare their performance on the UASpeech test set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does incorporating severity information compare to traditional speaker adaptation techniques like fMLLR or CMLLR in terms of effectiveness and robustness across different dysarthric speech datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper extensively compares severity-based adaptation techniques but does not directly compare against traditional speaker adaptation methods like fMLLR or CMLLR on the same dataset
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on severity-based techniques and system combination approaches rather than providing a direct comparison with classical adaptation methods
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing severity-based techniques (auxiliary features, LHUC transforms, multitask learning) against traditional adaptation methods (fMLLR, CMLLR, speaker-dependent models) on the same dysarthric speech dataset with statistical significance testing

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the severity prediction component of the multitask learning approach be further improved by incorporating additional clinical assessment features or multi-modal inputs beyond spectral basis embeddings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses spectral basis embeddings for severity prediction but mentions this as a dual-purpose solution and does not explore additional clinical features or multi-modal inputs
- Why unresolved: The current severity prediction relies solely on spectral basis embeddings derived from speech signals, without exploring whether additional clinical information or other modalities could improve severity assessment
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments incorporating clinical assessment data (e.g., intelligibility scores, dysarthria type, neurological examination results) or multi-modal inputs (video, articulatory features) into the severity prediction component and measuring improvements in ASR performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the effectiveness of severity incorporation techniques vary across different dysarthria types and severity assessment methodologies?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses the UASpeech dataset which includes speakers with various severity levels but does not analyze how different dysarthria types or severity assessment methodologies affect the performance of severity incorporation techniques
- Why unresolved: The study treats severity as a continuous variable without examining whether the effectiveness of severity incorporation varies by dysarthria type (spastic, ataxic, hypokinetic, etc.) or by different ways of assessing severity
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of how severity incorporation techniques perform across different dysarthria types and with different severity assessment methodologies, potentially requiring access to datasets with more detailed clinical information

## Limitations
- Small dataset size (only 16 dysarthric speakers) raises questions about statistical significance and generalization
- Improvements concentrated on very low intelligibility subset where baseline performance is already poor, suggesting potential overfitting
- Lack of ablation studies makes it difficult to determine which severity incorporation technique contributes most to improvements

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The overall architecture and experimental methodology are sound, following established practices in speech recognition research
- **Medium confidence**: The claimed WER reductions and relative improvements are reasonable given the techniques employed, but the small dataset size and lack of extensive ablation studies reduce confidence in the magnitude of the improvements
- **Low confidence**: The effectiveness of individual severity incorporation techniques is not well-established due to the lack of ablation studies
