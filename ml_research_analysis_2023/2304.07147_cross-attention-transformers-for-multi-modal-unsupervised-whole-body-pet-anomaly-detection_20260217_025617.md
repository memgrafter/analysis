# Cross Attention Transformers for Multi-modal Unsupervised Whole-Body PET Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07147
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07147
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: Achieves DICE score of 0.562 and AUPRC of 0.561 on hold-out test set, demonstrating robustness even when trained on data containing anomalies

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel method for unsupervised whole-body PET anomaly detection using cross-attention transformers and multi-modal conditioning with CT images. The approach leverages a VQ-GAN to encode PET and CT images into discrete latent spaces, which are then fed into a transformer with cross-attention layers to model the relationship between the modalities. The method introduces a kernel density estimation approach for anomaly map generation, replacing the classic residual-based method. The proposed approach achieves superior performance compared to state-of-the-art alternatives and demonstrates robustness even when trained on data containing anomalies.

## Method Summary
The method employs a VQ-GAN to encode PET and CT images into discrete latent spaces, followed by a transformer with cross-attention layers to model the relationship between the modalities. Anomaly maps are generated using kernel density estimation with an exponential kernel, replacing the traditional residual-based approach. The model is trained on a combination of private whole-body PET/CT datasets and the autoPET dataset, with evaluation on a hold-out test set and an external CPTAC dataset.

## Key Results
- DICE score of 0.562 and AUPRC of 0.561 on hold-out test set
- Demonstrates robustness when trained on data containing anomalies
- Achieves DICE score of 0.610 and AUPRC of 0.604 on out-of-sample CPTAC dataset
- Outperforms state-of-the-art alternatives in unsupervised whole-body PET anomaly detection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Cross-attention transformers outperform PET-only models by leveraging CT's anatomical context to disambiguate physiological from pathological uptake. Cross-attention layers enable each PET token to attend to the full CT latent sequence, providing spatial context that helps the model distinguish metabolically active but non-pathological regions from true anomalies. This works when CT and PET are well-aligned and CT provides relevant anatomical detail.

### Mechanism 2
Kernel density estimation (KDE) with exponential kernel and tuned bandwidth outperforms residual-based thresholding by modeling multimodal intensity distributions at each voxel. KDE estimates the probability density of voxel intensities across multiple stochastic reconstructions, allowing voxel-level likelihood scoring rather than a global intensity threshold. This approach is effective when voxel intensities exhibit non-Gaussian, multimodal distributions due to biological and procedural variability.

### Mechanism 3
VQ-GAN with optimized codebook size (256 vocab, 128 dim) enables discrete latent sequences that preserve fine-grained spatial patterns necessary for transformer-based anomaly detection. The codebook size controls the granularity of this representation, balancing reconstruction fidelity with sequence length for efficient transformer modeling. This works when the VQ-GAN successfully learns a useful discrete space without posterior collapse.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Transformers and self-attention
  - **Why needed here**: The paper uses transformer decoders with cross-attention to model long-range dependencies in discretized PET/CT latent sequences.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the difference between self-attention and cross-attention in the context of multi-modal medical imaging?

- **Concept**: Vector quantization and discrete latent spaces
  - **Why needed here**: VQ-GAN encodes continuous PET/CT volumes into discrete token sequences, enabling autoregressive modeling by the transformer.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the codebook size affect the granularity and computational efficiency of the latent representation?

- **Concept**: Kernel density estimation and non-parametric statistics
  - **Why needed here**: KDE replaces parametric residual thresholding by estimating voxel-wise probability density functions from stochastic reconstructions.
  - **Quick check question**: Why might a Gaussian kernel be less suitable than an exponential kernel for modeling PET uptake distributions?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: PET/CT volume → VQ-GAN encoder → discrete latent sequence → PET latent + CT latent → Transformer (self-attention + cross-attention) → likelihood scores → Resampling + dropout → VQ-GAN decoder → multiple reconstructions → KDE per voxel → anomaly score map → thresholding → segmentation

- **Critical path**: VQ-GAN → Transformer (cross-attention) → KDE → segmentation. Each stage must produce valid output for the next to function.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - VQ-GAN codebook size vs. reconstruction fidelity vs. sequence length
  - Transformer depth vs. computational cost for long sequences
  - KDE bandwidth vs. sensitivity to subtle anomalies vs. robustness to noise
  - Cross-attention vs. increased parameters and training complexity

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor VQ-GAN reconstruction → noisy transformer inputs → degraded likelihoods
  - Misaligned CT/PET → cross-attention attends to wrong anatomy → false positives/negatives
  - Suboptimal KDE bandwidth → oversmoothing (missed anomalies) or undersmoothing (false positives)
  - Over-regularized codebook → posterior collapse → transformer cannot learn

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train VQ-GAN with varying codebook sizes (64-2048 vocab, 32-256 dim) on PET only; evaluate reconstruction quality and downstream transformer DICE
  2. Add CT cross-attention to transformer; compare PET-only vs. PET+CT performance on validation set using residual maps
  3. Implement KDE with different kernels (Gaussian, exponential, etc.) and bandwidth settings; compare anomaly map quality and segmentation metrics

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the choice of kernel in KDE affect the detection of subtle anomalies in different anatomical regions? The study does not analyze performance differences across anatomical regions or anomaly types, which could be resolved by detailed analysis with statistical significance testing.

### Open Question 2
What is the optimal balance between codebook vocabulary size and vector dimension for VQ-GAN when dealing with diverse cancer types and locations? The optimal parameters may vary depending on specific characteristics of cancer types and locations, requiring testing on datasets with varying cancer types and locations.

### Open Question 3
Can the model's performance be further improved by incorporating additional clinical information, such as patient demographics or prior medical history? The study does not explore the use of additional clinical information beyond imaging data, which could be tested by including clinical metadata and evaluating impact on detection accuracy.

## Limitations
- Performance gains rely on perfect PET-CT spatial alignment; misalignment is not addressed
- KDE assumes sufficient stochastic variability in reconstructions; insufficient variability may lead to unreliable anomaly scores
- Codebook size selection is empirically tuned on a private dataset; generalizability to other imaging protocols is untested
- Out-of-sample generalization is based on a single external dataset; multi-center robustness is not established

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: KDE with exponential kernel improves upon residual-based thresholding, as supported by direct evidence of non-Gaussian, multimodal error distributions
- **Medium confidence**: Cross-attention transformers benefit from CT anatomical context, based on qualitative reasoning but without ablation studies
- **Low confidence**: The VQ-GAN codebook size (256 vocab, 128 dim) is optimal; evidence is based on internal experiments without comparison to larger public benchmarks
