# LEAPT: Learning Adaptive Prefix-to-prefix Translation For Simultaneous Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11750
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11750
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Achieves 19.6 BLEU (45% relative improvement) for Zh-En and 27.2 BLEU (35% relative improvement) for De-En in low latency compared to strong baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LEAPT, an adaptive prefix-to-prefix training policy for simultaneous machine translation that significantly outperforms existing approaches. The method is inspired by human interpreter strategies and "wait" policies, allowing the model to learn how to translate source sentence prefixes while making use of future context. The core innovation involves generating pseudo prefix pairs by detecting whether translations of source sentence prefixes are prefixes of full sentence translations, then jointly training on both original and pseudo pairs. Experiments on Chinese-English and German-English datasets demonstrate substantial improvements in translation quality while maintaining low latency.

## Method Summary
LEAPT employs a prefix-to-prefix training approach where the model learns to translate streaming source prefixes rather than full sentences. The method generates pseudo prefix pairs by identifying when a translation of a source prefix is itself a prefix of the full translation, creating valuable training data that mimics simultaneous translation scenarios. The adaptive policy dynamically segments source sentences based on contextual information, forming more flexible segments than fixed policies. A refined version adds m future words to improve translation quality and consistency between partial translations. The approach is trained jointly on original sentence pairs and pseudo prefix pairs, allowing the model to learn both full-sentence and prefix-to-prefix translation simultaneously.

## Key Results
- Achieves 19.6 BLEU score for Chinese-English (45% relative improvement over strong baseline)
- Achieves 27.2 BLEU score for German-English (35% relative improvement over strong baseline)
- Demonstrates significant performance gains in low latency scenarios
- Outperforms competitive baselines including wait-k and re-translation approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Prefix-to-prefix training enables effective learning of source prefix translation using future context
- Mechanism: Pseudo prefix pairs are generated by detecting when source prefix translations are prefixes of full translations, creating training data that mimics simultaneous translation
- Core assumption: The model can effectively learn from pseudo prefix pairs that represent realistic simultaneous translation scenarios
- Evidence: Experimental results show 45% relative improvement for Zh-En and 35% for De-En over baselines
- Break condition: Pseudo prefix pairs fail to represent realistic simultaneous scenarios or model fails to generalize

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adaptive policies outperform fixed policies in translation quality and latency
- Mechanism: Dynamic segmentation based on contextual information creates more flexible segments than fixed policies
- Core assumption: Adaptive segmentation can better balance accuracy and latency through contextual awareness
- Evidence: Results show superior performance compared to fixed policy baselines
- Break condition: Adaptive segmentation fails to effectively segment source sentences

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adding m future words improves translation quality and consistency
- Mechanism: Future words act as "conjunctions" enabling the model to consider fluency between partial translations
- Core assumption: Additional context from m future words provides sufficient information for better translations
- Evidence: Refined method demonstrates improved translation quality
- Break condition: Future words introduce noise or unnecessary complexity

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on quality of pseudo prefix pairs generated during training
- Adaptive policy effectiveness depends on quality of contextual information for segmentation
- Generalizability across different language pairs and domains remains uncertain
- Trade-off between translation quality and latency when varying m needs further exploration

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Overall framework design and experimental methodology appear sound with well-documented BLEU improvements
- **Medium Confidence**: Mechanism claims regarding prefix-to-prefix training effectiveness and impact of m future words supported by results but need more detailed ablation studies
