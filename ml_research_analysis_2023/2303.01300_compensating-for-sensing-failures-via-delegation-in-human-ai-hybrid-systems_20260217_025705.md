# Compensating for Sensing Failures via Delegation in Human-AI Hybrid Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01300
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01300
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: RL manager trained to select between human and AI agents in driving scenarios with sensing failures achieves up to 100 higher mean episode reward and eliminates up to 26 avoidable collisions compared to random delegation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a reinforcement learning-based delegation manager for hybrid human-AI systems that can compensate for sensing failures by intelligently switching control between human and AI agents. The manager observes representations of both agents' perceptions and contextual information to make delegation decisions that maximize safety and efficiency. Tested in a simplified driving simulation with various sensing failures (fog, night driving, sensor masks, color-based detection failure), the approach significantly outperforms random delegation strategies, achieving near-zero collisions while minimizing abrupt control switches.

## Method Summary
The approach trains a Deep Q-Network (DQN) manager to select between human and AI drivers in a simulated driving environment based on their perception representations and contextual information. The manager uses two CNN heads to process visual inputs from both agents, which are concatenated with contextual vectors before passing through hidden layers for decision-making. Training uses a single end-of-episode reward that penalizes collisions, steps to reach goal, and immediate delegation reversals. The key innovation is training the manager to recognize contexts where sensing deficiencies could lead to failures and delegate control to the agent most likely to succeed.

## Key Results
- Learned manager achieves up to 100 higher mean episode reward compared to random delegation strategy
- Eliminates up to 26 avoidable collisions across multiple sensing failure scenarios
- Consistently avoids collisions and minimizes sudden delegation changes in fog, night driving, sensor masks, and color-based detection failure contexts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by training an RL agent to learn the complex relationship between perception quality, environmental context, and delegation outcomes. The manager learns to associate specific patterns in perception data with the likelihood of successful task completion by each agent. When sensing failures occur, the manager can identify which agent (human or AI) is better equipped to handle the situation based on the specific type of failure and context. The use of visual perception representations allows the manager to make decisions based on actual environmental understanding rather than relying on hand-engineered rules that might not generalize across different failure modes.

## Foundational Learning
- Reinforcement Learning with Deep Q-Networks: Needed to learn optimal delegation policies from experience; check by verifying the manager learns to associate specific perception patterns with high rewards
- Multi-modal input processing: Required to handle both visual and contextual information; check by ensuring the CNN heads properly extract features from perception data
- Reward shaping for hybrid systems: Essential for balancing safety and efficiency; check by confirming the reward function properly penalizes both collisions and frequent delegation changes
- Perception representation learning: Critical for the manager to understand environmental states; check by verifying the CNN architectures can effectively process the input visual data
- Delegation policy optimization: Necessary to maximize safety while minimizing unnecessary control switches; check by monitoring delegation frequency and collision rates during training

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Simulator Environment -> Sensing Context Modifiers -> Perception Data -> CNN Heads -> Concatenation Layer -> Hidden Layers -> DQN Output -> Delegation Decision

Critical Path: The most critical path is from perception data through the CNN heads to the decision output, as the quality of feature extraction directly impacts delegation quality.

Design Tradeoffs: The paper chose DQN over other RL methods for its simplicity and effectiveness with discrete action spaces, accepting potential limitations in handling continuous state spaces. The single end-of-episode reward simplifies training but may limit the manager's ability to learn fine-grained temporal delegation patterns.

Failure Signatures: If the manager fails to learn, expect either high collision rates (reward signal not properly shaping behavior) or excessive delegation changes (penalty weights need adjustment). Poor CNN feature extraction would manifest as random or suboptimal delegation decisions regardless of perception quality.

3. Validate manager decision quality by analyzing delegation choices in scenarios with known optimal solutions

## Limitations
- Simplified CARLO environment limits real-world applicability and may not capture the full complexity of actual driving scenarios
- Absence of human-in-the-loop testing means the approach hasn't been validated with real human drivers responding to delegation decisions
- Limited testing to four specific sensing failure modes without exploring the full spectrum of potential perception failures
