# ClusTop: An unsupervised and integrated text clustering and topic extraction framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.00818
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00818
- **Reference count:** 33
- **Primary result:** Jointly clusters text and extracts topics using BERT-based embeddings and attention weights, outperforming LSA, K-means, and LDA baselines.

## Executive Summary
ClusTop introduces a joint unsupervised framework for text clustering and topic extraction. The method trains an enhanced BERT model via clustering-based pseudo-labeling, then uses self-attention weights to extract representative keywords for each cluster. Experiments on THUCNews and a social governance dataset show improved clustering metrics and more coherent topics compared to traditional approaches. The design leverages the natural cluster structure in text embeddings and BERT's attention for interpretability.

## Method Summary
ClusTop jointly optimizes text clustering and topic extraction by first training an enhanced BERT model using clustering-based pseudo-labeling. Pseudo-labels are generated from initial clustering results, which are used to fine-tune BERT embeddings to better capture cluster structure. After training, the enhanced model's self-attention weights are analyzed to extract the most representative keywords for each cluster, forming coherent topics. The approach combines embedding enhancement and attention-based keyword selection to produce interpretable and accurate clusters and topics.

## Key Results
- ClusTop significantly improves clustering metrics (ARI, AMI, F1) over LSA, K-means on vanilla BERT, and LDA baselines.
- Attention-based topic extraction yields more coherent and topic-relevant keywords compared to traditional TF-IDF methods.
- Strong performance on both THUCNews and social governance datasets, demonstrating effectiveness across domains.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ClusTop's joint training design enhances BERT embeddings by aligning them with cluster structure, improving separability and coherence. The pseudo-labeling approach provides implicit supervision without manual labels, enabling the model to learn cluster-specific patterns. By leveraging BERT's self-attention, the method extracts keywords that are semantically central to each cluster, providing interpretable topics. This dual focus on cluster optimization and attention-based interpretability distinguishes it from standard clustering pipelines.

## Foundational Learning
- **Pseudo-labeling for unsupervised learning:** Creates soft supervision from initial cluster assignments, guiding BERT to learn cluster-aware embeddings. Quick check: verify pseudo-labels improve embedding clusterability.
- **Self-attention weight interpretation:** Extracts keywords that BERT attends to most within each cluster, revealing topical focus. Quick check: inspect top attention scores for coherence.
- **Joint optimization:** Simultaneously refines embeddings and topic keywords, ensuring consistency between clusters and their descriptors. Quick check: compare joint vs. sequential training outcomes.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Text data -> BERT embedding -> Clustering -> Pseudo-labels -> Enhanced BERT training -> Attention analysis -> Keyword extraction

**Critical path:** Embedding generation -> Clustering -> Pseudo-label generation -> Enhanced BERT fine-tuning -> Attention-based keyword extraction

**Design tradeoffs:** Uses BERT for rich semantic embeddings but at higher computational cost; attention-based keyword extraction is interpretable but depends on BERT's robustness to noise and text length.

**Failure signatures:** Degraded performance on very short or noisy text; attention weights may not reflect true importance if embeddings are poorly clustered; scalability bottlenecks on large corpora.

## Limitations
- Gains may be attributable to BERT-based embeddings rather than the pseudo-labeling design; lack of comparison with modern unsupervised embedding models (e.g., Sentence-BERT) limits attribution.
- Results only evaluated on two datasets with fixed label sets, limiting generalizability.
- Heavy reliance on BERT's self-attention, which can be brittle for short or noisy text; method may not scale well without further optimization.

## Confidence
- **Core claims:** Medium - well documented and reproducible in principle, but external validation and comparison to newer baselines are needed.
- **Scalability:** Low - no ablation or runtime analysis for larger datasets.
- **Topic extraction novelty:** Medium - intuitive approach, but lacks direct comparison with attention-free keyword extraction methods.
