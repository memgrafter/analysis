# A Weak Supervision Approach for Few-Shot Aspect Based Sentiment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11979
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11979
- Reference count: 35
- One-line primary result: Proposed weakly supervised approach achieves 15.84% absolute F1 improvement in few-shot ABSA and outperforms state-of-the-art in zero-shot triplet extraction.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a weakly supervised approach to improve few-shot performance in aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA). The authors construct a noisy ABSA dataset using abundant unlabeled data through a pipeline of heuristic-based extraction, then use this dataset to pre-train a sequence-to-sequence model on multiple ABSA tasks. The pre-trained model is fine-tuned on three widely used ABSA datasets, achieving significant improvements in few-shot learning scenarios while preserving full fine-tuning performance. The method also demonstrates strong zero-shot capabilities, particularly for the harder aspect sentiment triplet extraction task.

## Method Summary
The method involves constructing a noisy ABSA dataset from 100K Amazon Electronics and 100K Yelp reviews using heuristics: frequent nouns as aspects, Hu and Liu lexicon for opinion terms, NLI models for linking, and sentiment classifiers. A T5-base model is then pre-trained on five overlapping ABSA tasks derived from this noisy data using multi-task learning and instruction tuning. Regularization techniques including tuple dropout, biased weight decay, and standard weight decay are applied to prevent overfitting and catastrophic forgetting. The pre-trained model is then fine-tuned on three downstream ABSA datasets under full, few-shot (k=5,10,20,50), and zero-shot settings.

## Key Results
- Achieves 15.84% absolute F1 improvement in few-shot learning scenarios for harder ABSA tasks
- Outperforms state-of-the-art in zero-shot aspect sentiment triplet extraction
- Maintains full fine-tuning performance while significantly improving few-shot learning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Weakly supervised dataset construction provides noisy but domain-relevant examples that improve model generalization for ABSA tasks.
- **Mechanism**: The pipeline uses heuristics to extract aspect terms (frequent nouns), opinion terms (lexicon-based), links them using NLI models, and adds sentiment using sentence-level classifiers. This creates triplets without direct ABSA supervision.
- **Core assumption**: The heuristic-based extraction (e.g., frequent nouns as aspects, lexicon-based opinion terms) is sufficiently accurate to provide useful signal for the downstream model.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "We propose a pipeline approach to construct a noisy ABSA dataset, and we use it to adapt a pre-trained sequence-to-sequence model to the ABSA tasks." [section 3.1]: Detailed description of the pipeline steps including aspect extraction using POS tagging and frequent nouns, opinion term extraction using Hu and Liu lexicon, linking using NLI model, and sentiment classification. [corpus]: Weak; the corpus only shows neighbor papers on ABSA but no direct evidence of the quality or accuracy of the noisy dataset construction.
- **Break condition**: If the heuristics produce too much noise (e.g., wrong aspect-opinion links, incorrect sentiment), the model will overfit to incorrect patterns and performance will degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Noisy ABSA Pre-Training (NAPT) aligns the model's learning objectives with the downstream ABSA tasks, improving few-shot performance.
- **Mechanism**: The model is pre-trained on five overlapping ABSA tasks (aspect extraction, opinion extraction, combined extraction, sentiment classification, and triplet extraction) using the noisy dataset. This exposes the model to task-relevant patterns before fine-tuning.
- **Core assumption**: The tasks used in NAPT are well-aligned with the final downstream ABSA tasks, and the model can transfer knowledge from these tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "We use it to adapt a pre-trained sequence-to-sequence model to the ABSA tasks." [section 3.2]: "We factorize the triplets from the noisy dataset into five separate but overlapping tasks... Note that there exists a correspondence between our NAPT tasks and classical ABSA tasks." [corpus]: No direct evidence; relies on the assumption that pre-training on related tasks improves downstream performance.
- **Break condition**: If the NAPT tasks are not sufficiently aligned with the downstream tasks, or if the model overfits to the noisy data, the benefits will not materialize.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Regularization techniques (tuple dropout, biased weight decay, standard weight decay) prevent catastrophic forgetting and overfitting during NAPT.
- **Mechanism**: These techniques ensure the model retains general pre-training knowledge while adapting to the noisy ABSA data.
- **Core assumption**: The regularization techniques are necessary and sufficient to balance between retaining pre-trained knowledge and adapting to new tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.2.1]: "First, instead of just randomly splitting the data into train/validation, we split the extracted aspect- and opinion-terms into two disjoint sets... Additionally, we invoke three types of regularization." [section 5.1]: "We analyze the importance on the downstream performance of each regularization technique used during the NAPT phase." [corpus]: No direct evidence of the effectiveness of these specific regularization techniques in this context.
- **Break condition**: If regularization is insufficient, the model may overfit to the noisy data and lose general capabilities; if too strong, it may not adapt enough to the ABSA tasks.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Heuristic-based data labeling
  - Why needed here: The method relies on extracting aspects, opinions, and sentiments without direct supervision using heuristics like frequent nouns, lexicon matching, and NLI models.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain why the method uses frequent nouns as aspect candidates instead of a learned model?

- **Concept**: Multi-task learning for structured prediction
  - Why needed here: The NAPT phase trains the model on five overlapping ABSA tasks simultaneously, requiring understanding of how to structure outputs for different but related tasks.
  - Quick check question: How does the model know which task to perform during NAPT, and how are the different output formats handled?

- **Concept**: Catastrophic forgetting prevention
  - Why needed here: Pre-training on noisy data could overwrite general knowledge from initial pre-training; regularization is needed to maintain balance.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between standard weight decay and biased weight decay in this context?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: POS tagging → frequent noun extraction → lexicon-based opinion extraction → NLI-based linking → sentiment classification → NAPT on T5-base → fine-tuning on downstream datasets
- **Critical path**: Noisy dataset construction (aspect/opinion extraction, linking, sentiment) → NAPT training on five tasks → Fine-tuning on downstream ABSA datasets → Evaluation on test sets
- **Design tradeoffs**: Noisy vs clean data: Using heuristics creates noise but enables large-scale training; trade-off between coverage and accuracy. Task complexity: Including harder tasks (e.g., triplet extraction) in NAPT helps with harder downstream tasks but may slow training. Regularization strength: Too much prevents adaptation; too little causes overfitting.
- **Failure signatures**: Low performance on downstream tasks despite good NAPT performance: likely overfitting to noisy data. Degradation on general language tasks after NAPT: catastrophic forgetting. Poor few-shot performance: NAPT tasks not well-aligned with downstream tasks.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run the noisy dataset construction pipeline on a small sample of reviews and manually verify the quality of extracted triplets
  2. Train the model on just the AE task during NAPT and evaluate on downstream AE to isolate the effect of task alignment
  3. Test the model with and without each regularization technique on a small downstream dataset to identify the most critical ones

## Limitations
- Quality of noisy dataset construction is critical but not thoroughly validated
- Method relies on English-specific resources and may not generalize to other languages
- Specific hyperparameters for NAPT training are not provided, making exact reproduction challenging

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The overall approach of using weakly supervised data for few-shot ABSA is novel and the reported performance gains on the tested datasets are likely real. The method's effectiveness on zero-shot triplet extraction is a strong claim with supporting results.
- **Medium confidence**: The specific mechanisms (tuple dropout, biased weight decay) and their relative contributions are not fully validated independently. The assumption that frequent nouns make good aspect candidates is reasonable but not empirically verified in this work.
- **Low confidence**: The quality of the noisy dataset and its impact on downstream performance is a critical unknown. Without evidence that the heuristics produce sufficiently accurate data, the foundation of the method is uncertain.

## Next Checks

1. Manually audit a sample of the noisy dataset to assess the accuracy of aspect/opinion extraction, linking, and sentiment classification. This will directly validate the quality of the data the model is trained on.
2. Perform ablation studies on the regularization techniques (tuple dropout, biased weight decay, weight decay) to determine their individual contributions to preventing overfitting and catastrophic forgetting.
3. Test the model on a held-out general language task (e.g., GLUE benchmark) after NAPT to check for catastrophic forgetting, ensuring the model retains general capabilities while adapting to ABSA tasks.
