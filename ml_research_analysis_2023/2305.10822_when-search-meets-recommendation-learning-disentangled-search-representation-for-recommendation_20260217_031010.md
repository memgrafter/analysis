# When Search Meets Recommendation: Learning Disentangled Search Representation for Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10822
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10822
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SESRec achieves significant improvements over state-of-the-art sequential recommendation models on both industrial and public datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SESRec, a search-enhanced sequential recommendation framework that disentangles similar and dissimilar user interests between search and recommendation behaviors. The method uses query-item alignment with InfoNCE loss, separate transformer encoders for S&R behaviors, and a contrastive learning task to supervise disentanglement. User interests are extracted from three perspectives: contextual representations, similar behaviors, and dissimilar behaviors, using a multi-interest extraction module. Experiments on industrial and public datasets show SESRec outperforms state-of-the-art models, achieving significant improvements in NDCG@5, NDCG@10, HIT@1, HIT@5, HIT@10, and MRR.

## Method Summary
SESRec aligns search and recommendation behaviors through query-item embeddings, then disentangles similar and dissimilar interests using a co-attention mechanism and contrastive learning. The framework employs separate transformer encoders for search and recommendation sequences, aligns query-item interactions with InfoNCE loss, partitions behaviors into similar and dissimilar sets using attention-based affinity scores, and extracts user interests from three perspectives using a multi-interest extraction module. The final prediction uses a weighted combination of contextual, similar, and dissimilar interest representations.

## Key Results
- SESRec achieves 2.36%-3.28% improvement in NDCG@5 over state-of-the-art baselines on Kuaishou dataset
- SESRec shows 1.12%-1.94% improvement in NDCG@5 on Amazon Kindle Store dataset
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of multi-interest extraction module and interest disentanglement components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Search-enhanced recommendation works because search queries and clicked items carry complementary signals about user interests that are not fully represented in browsing history alone.
- Core assumption: Users' search and recommendation interests are related but not identical, and both types of behaviors provide useful signals for predicting future interactions.

### Mechanism 2
- Disentangling similar and dissimilar interests between search and recommendation behaviors improves recommendation accuracy by preventing interference between different types of interests.
- Core assumption: User interests expressed in search and recommendation behaviors can be meaningfully separated into overlapping and non-overlapping components.

### Mechanism 3
- Multi-interest extraction from aggregated, similar, and dissimilar behaviors provides a more complete representation of user interests than using any single perspective alone.
- Core assumption: Different aspects of user behavior (contextual, similar, dissimilar) provide complementary information about user interests.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Contrastive learning and triplet loss**
  - Why needed here: To learn representations where similar interests are closer and dissimilar interests are farther apart without explicit labels
  - Quick check question: How does the triplet loss function encourage the model to separate similar and dissimilar interests?

- **Concept: Co-attention mechanisms**
  - Why needed here: To compute the affinity between search and recommendation behaviors for interest disentanglement
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between co-attention and self-attention in this context?

- **Concept: Transformer-based sequence modeling**
  - Why needed here: To capture sequential dependencies in both search and recommendation behaviors
  - Quick check question: How does the multi-head self-attention in the transformer layer help capture user interests?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Embedding layer → Bias encoding → Transformer layers → Query-item alignment → Interest contrast → Multi-interest extraction → MLP prediction
- **Critical path**: Search and recommendation behaviors → Contextual representations → Interest disentanglement → Multi-interest extraction → Prediction
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using separate transformers for search and recommendation behaviors allows capturing their distinct characteristics but increases model complexity
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on NDCG/HIT metrics may indicate issues with interest disentanglement or alignment; low improvements over baselines may suggest search behaviors are not informative
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify query-item alignment works by checking if query embeddings become more similar to their clicked items after training
  2. Test interest disentanglement by measuring JS divergence between similar and dissimilar interest sets
  3. Validate multi-interest extraction by comparing performance with and without the MIE module

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How do different disentanglement strategies (e.g., clustering-based vs. contrastive learning) compare in effectiveness for separating similar and dissimilar user interests between search and recommendation behaviors?
- **Open Question 2**: How sensitive is the disentanglement performance to the selection thresholds (γs and γr) in Equation (11) and (12), and can these thresholds be adaptively optimized?
- **Open Question 3**: How does the performance of SESRec scale with increasing sequence lengths for both search and recommendation behaviors, and what are the computational implications?

## Limitations

- The paper claims to be the first to perform interest disentanglement between search and recommendation behaviors, but provides limited external validation for this novel approach
- Automatic construction of search data for Amazon Kindle Store introduces potential artifacts that may affect generalizability of results
- The ablation study shows the multi-interest extraction module is important, but doesn't fully isolate the contribution of each component to overall performance gains

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The query-item alignment mechanism using InfoNCE loss is well-established in the literature and experimental results show consistent improvements over baselines
- **Medium confidence**: The interest disentanglement mechanism shows promise but relies on novel co-attention and triplet loss approaches that haven't been extensively validated in other contexts
- **Low confidence**: The specific hyperparameters (α=0.1, β=0.001) are not thoroughly explored across different datasets, and automatic construction of search data for Amazon Kindle Store may introduce biases

## Next Checks

1. Test SESRec on additional datasets where search behaviors are naturally occurring rather than automatically constructed to verify robustness of interest disentanglement mechanism
2. Perform a more granular ablation study that isolates the contribution of co-attention mechanism versus triplet loss in the interest disentanglement module
3. Conduct systematic hyperparameter search for α and β across both datasets to determine if suggested values are optimal or dataset-dependent
