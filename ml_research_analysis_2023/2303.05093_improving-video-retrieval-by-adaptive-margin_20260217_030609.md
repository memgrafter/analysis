# Improving Video Retrieval by Adaptive Margin

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05093
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05093
- Reference count: 40
- Improves video retrieval performance by 1.5-2.8 on R@1 for text-to-video retrieval and 2.8-2.0 on R@1 for video-to-text retrieval on MSRVTT dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitations of fixed-margin triplet ranking loss in video retrieval by proposing an adaptive margin that varies based on the similarity between positive and negative pairs. The method, called Cross-Modal Generalized Self-Distillation (CMGSD), leverages the model's own text and video encoders as "Dynamic Supervision Experts" to measure similarity in single-modal domains, which is then rescaled to produce sample-specific margins. This approach provides more accurate supervision compared to traditional fixed margins, especially for similar negative pairs that are semantically related. Experimental results demonstrate significant performance improvements over the backbone model and state-of-the-art methods across three widely used datasets.

## Method Summary
The method builds on existing video retrieval models by replacing the fixed margin in triplet ranking loss with an adaptive margin that changes based on the distance between positive and negative pairs. CMGSD uses both Dynamic Supervision Experts (the model's own text and video encoders) and Static Supervision Experts (pre-trained models) to measure similarity in single-modal domains. A rescale function maps the similarity distance (assumed to follow a Gaussian distribution) to adaptive margins that fluctuate around the hard margin based on a rescale factor β. The method adds minimal computational overhead during training and no overhead during testing, making it practical for deployment.

## Key Results
- Improves R@1 performance by 1.5-2.8 for text-to-video retrieval and 2.8-2.0 for video-to-text retrieval on MSRVTT dataset
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods by a large margin across MSRVTT, ActivityNet, and LSMDC datasets
- Achieves better performance with minimal modifications to existing video retrieval models
- Adds few computational overheads at train time and no overhead at test time

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adaptive margin reduces incorrect supervision by penalizing similar negatives less than dissimilar negatives
- Mechanism: The adaptive margin is a function of the distance between positive and negative pairs in single-modal domains (video or text). Similar negative pairs get smaller margins, reducing over-penalization, while dissimilar pairs get larger margins, enforcing stronger separation
- Core assumption: Similarity in cross-modal space can be approximated by similarity in single-modal domains (video or text)
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "negative pairs used for training are sampled randomly, which indicates that the semantics between negative pairs may be related or even equivalent, while most methods still enforce dissimilar representations to decrease their similarity"
  - [section] "Based on the discussion in section 1, the adaptive margin should be proportional to the similarity distance"
- Break condition: If the assumption that cross-modal similarity can be approximated by single-modal similarity fails, the adaptive margin would provide incorrect supervision

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Cross-Modal Generalized Self-Distillation (CMGSD) provides enhanced supervision by distilling knowledge from single-modal domains to the cross-modal domain
- Mechanism: CMGSD uses common components of the video retrieval model (e.g., text and video encoders) as "Dynamic Supervision Experts" to measure similarity in single-modal domains. This similarity is then rescaled to produce adaptive margins, effectively distilling knowledge from the model itself to improve cross-modal representation learning
- Core assumption: The text and video encoders of the model implicitly learn rich representations that can be used to measure similarity in single-modal domains
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose an adaptive margin changed with the distance between positive and negative pairs to solve the aforementioned issue"
  - [section] "To measure the similarity in the text domain and video domain, we introduce several powerful models with rich representations for text and video called 'supervision experts'"
- Break condition: If the text and video encoders do not learn rich representations early in training, the supervision from Dynamic Supervision Experts would be inaccurate

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The rescale function ensures adaptive margins fluctuate around the hard margin based on the similarity distance, providing a smooth transition between strong and weak supervision
- Mechanism: The rescale function maps the similarity distance (a Gaussian-distributed variable) to a new normal distribution with mean equal to the hard margin and variance controlled by a rescale factor β. This ensures that adaptive margins are close to the hard margin for similar negatives and farther for dissimilar negatives
- Core assumption: The similarity distance between positive and negative pairs follows a Gaussian distribution
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we assume that the similarity distance between different positive and negative pairs is Gaussian"
  - [section] "Following the above discussion, we introduce a simple yet effective rescale function, which defines the mapping relationship between similarity and adaptive margin"
- Break condition: If the similarity distance does not follow a Gaussian distribution, the rescale function may not effectively map it to appropriate adaptive margins

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Backbone model (e.g., MMT) -> Dynamic Supervision Experts (text/video encoders) -> Static Supervision Experts (pre-trained models) -> Rescale function -> Adaptive margin -> Triplet ranking loss

- Critical path:
  1. Extract features from video and text using the backbone model and pre-trained models
  2. Measure similarity in single-modal domains using Dynamic and Static Supervision Experts
  3. Apply the rescale function to map similarity distance to adaptive margins
  4. Use the adaptive margins in the triplet ranking loss to train the backbone model

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using Dynamic Supervision Experts (DSEs) vs. Static Supervision Experts (SSEs): DSEs provide adaptive margins that improve with training, but may be inaccurate early on. SSEs provide stable margins but may not adapt to the specific training data
  - The choice of pre-trained models for SSEs: Different pre-trained models may provide different levels of supervision quality and computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - If the adaptive margins do not improve performance: This could indicate that the similarity measurement in single-modal domains is not a good approximation of cross-modal similarity, or that the rescale function is not effectively mapping the similarity distance to appropriate margins
  - If the model performance degrades with CMGSD: This could suggest that the supervision from DSEs or SSEs is misleading the model, or that the balance between the hard margin and adaptive margins (controlled by λ) is not optimal

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study on MSRVTT dataset with different dataset partitions: Evaluate the impact of Dynamic Supervision Experts, Static Supervision Experts, and the rescale function on retrieval performance
  2. Comparison with state-of-the-art methods on MSRVTT, ActivityNet, and LSMDC datasets: Assess the overall effectiveness of CMGSD in improving video retrieval performance
  3. Qualitative analysis of adaptive margins: Visualize the adaptive margins produced by DSEs for different negative pairs to understand how the method assigns margins based on similarity

## Limitations

- The Gaussian assumption for similarity distance distributions is not empirically validated, which could affect the effectiveness of the adaptive margin
- The method's performance with architectures other than MMT is not explored, limiting generalizability claims
- Early training behavior of Dynamic Supervision Experts may provide misleading supervision before the model converges

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Experimental results showing consistent improvements across three datasets and the claim of minimal computational overhead
- **Medium confidence**: Mechanism claims regarding adaptive margins reducing incorrect supervision, as these rely on unproven assumptions
- **Low confidence**: Scalability claim that CMGSD works with "most" video retrieval models, as only MMT is validated
