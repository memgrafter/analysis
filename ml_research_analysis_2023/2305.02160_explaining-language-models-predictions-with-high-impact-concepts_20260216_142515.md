# Explaining Language Models' Predictions with High-Impact Concepts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02160
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02160
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes HI-Concept framework for extracting high-impact concepts from language model hidden activations using causal loss

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HI-Concept, a framework for extracting high-level concepts from large language models that cause substantial changes in output predictions. Unlike existing methods that identify correlated features, HI-Concept uses a causal loss to ensure discovered concepts have high predictive impact. The framework addresses the challenge of spurious correlations in model explanations by focusing on concepts that genuinely influence model decisions rather than those that merely correlate with outputs.

The authors evaluate HI-Concept on two text classification datasets with three different pretrained models, demonstrating consistent improvements over baseline methods in finding impactful, usable, and faithful concepts. Human studies further validate that HI-Concept identifies more causal words than baselines, suggesting its potential for model debugging and improving transparency in NLP systems.

## Method Summary
HI-Concept is a framework that extracts high-level concepts from a model's hidden activations by optimizing for those that cause substantial changes in output predictions. The key innovation is the use of a causal loss function that ensures discovered concepts have genuine predictive impact rather than merely being correlated with outputs. The framework operates by perturbing hidden activations in specific directions corresponding to potential concepts and measuring the resulting changes in model predictions. Through iterative optimization, HI-Concept identifies concepts that maximize this causal effect, distinguishing itself from methods that rely solely on correlation-based feature importance.

## Key Results
- HI-Concept consistently finds more impactful concepts compared to baselines across different datasets and models
- Human evaluation confirms HI-Concept identifies more causal words than baseline methods
- The framework demonstrates improved faithfulness in explanations compared to correlation-based approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HI-Concept works by explicitly optimizing for concepts that cause changes in model predictions rather than those that merely correlate with them. The causal loss formulation directly measures the impact of concept activation on output predictions, allowing the framework to distinguish between spurious correlations and genuine causal relationships. This approach addresses a fundamental limitation of existing explanation methods that often identify features correlated with outputs but do not necessarily influence model decisions.

## Foundational Learning

**Causal inference in ML**: Understanding how to establish cause-effect relationships rather than mere correlations
- Why needed: Most explanation methods identify correlations, but understanding causation is crucial for debugging
- Quick check: Can the method distinguish between features that correlate with predictions versus those that cause them?

**Hidden activation analysis**: Working with intermediate representations rather than just input/output spaces
- Why needed: Hidden states contain rich information about how models process inputs
- Quick check: Does the method effectively extract meaningful concepts from activation patterns?

**Concept-based explanations**: Moving beyond individual feature importance to higher-level semantic concepts
- Why needed: Humans understand models better through abstract concepts than raw features
- Quick check: Are the extracted concepts semantically meaningful and interpretable?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Input text -> Pretrained model (hidden activations) -> HI-Concept optimizer (causal loss) -> Concept vectors -> Prediction impact measurement

**Critical path**: The optimization loop that iteratively refines concept vectors by maximizing causal impact while maintaining faithfulness to the model's decision-making process

**Design tradeoffs**: HI-Concept prioritizes causal impact over correlation strength, which may miss some correlated features but ensures discovered concepts genuinely influence predictions. This represents a fundamental tradeoff between faithfulness and comprehensiveness in explanations.

**Failure signatures**: The method may struggle with concepts that have indirect or distributed effects across multiple layers, potentially missing important but subtle influences on model behavior.

**First experiments**:
1. Baseline comparison on simple linear models to establish causal vs correlational differences
2. Ablation study removing the causal loss component to demonstrate its importance
3. Testing on synthetic datasets with known causal relationships to validate the method

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation to relatively small text classification datasets and three pretrained models
- Unclear scalability to larger, more complex NLP tasks and different model architectures
- Computational overhead of causal loss optimization not thoroughly addressed
- Limited evidence of practical utility for debugging and improving model transparency

## Confidence

High confidence in:
- HI-Concept's ability to extract high-impact concepts that outperform baselines
- The causal loss formulation being well-motivated and effective
- Human evaluation supporting the identification of more causal words

Medium confidence in:
- Scalability and generalizability to larger, more complex NLP tasks
- Computational efficiency compared to baseline methods

Low confidence in:
- Practical utility for debugging and improving model transparency without concrete examples
- Demonstration of improved model performance after incorporating HI-Concept's insights
