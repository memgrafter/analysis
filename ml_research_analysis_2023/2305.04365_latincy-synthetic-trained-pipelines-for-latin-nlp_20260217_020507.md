# LatinCy: Synthetic Trained Pipelines for Latin NLP

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.04365
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04365
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Key outcome:** Introduces LatinCy, a set of trained general purpose Latin-language "core" pipelines for use with the spaCy natural language processing framework

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LatinCy, a set of trained general purpose Latin-language "core" pipelines for use with the spaCy natural language processing framework. The models are trained on a large amount of available Latin data, including all five of the Latin Universal Dependency treebanks, which have been preprocessed to be compatible with each other. The result is a set of general models for Latin with good performance on a number of natural language processing tasks (e.g. the top-performing model yields POS tagging, 97.41% accuracy; lemmatization, 94.66% accuracy; morphological tagging 92.76% accuracy). The paper describes the model training, including its training data and parameterization, and presents the advantages to Latin-language researchers of having a spaCy model available for NLP work.

## Method Summary
LatinCy is trained on five Latin Universal Dependencies treebanks (Perseus, PROIEL, ITTB, UDante, LLCT) that have been preprocessed for consistency. The pipeline uses Floret subword vectors (50k for 'md', 200k for 'lg') trained on cc100-latin corpus. The spaCy dependency pipeline includes components for POS tagging, morphological tagging, lemmatization with edit trees, dependency parsing, and named entity recognition. The models are trained separately and combined into a unified pipeline with custom tokenization rules for handling enclitic -que.

## Key Results
- POS tagging accuracy of 97.41% for the top-performing model
- Lemmatization accuracy of 94.66%
- Morphological tagging accuracy of 92.76%
- Dependency parsing UAS of 81-83% and LAS of 75-78%
- NER f-score of 89-91%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training a single spaCy pipeline on synthesized data from all five Latin UD treebanks yields better generalization than using one treebank.
- Mechanism: By remapping inconsistent POS tags, morphological features, and dependency labels across the five datasets, the model learns a unified representation that handles diverse annotation schemes.
- Core assumption: Differences in annotation style are noise that hurts performance, and removing them improves accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states the result is "a set of general models for Latin with good performance on a number of natural language processing tasks"
  - [section] notes preprocessing decisions were made "to make the model more generalizable" and cites UD for Latin as the source of annotation variation
  - [corpus] does not mention accuracy or evaluation, so cannot confirm performance improvement directly
- Break condition: If evaluation shows no accuracy gain over single-treebank models, or if preprocessing introduces harmful errors, the assumption fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using Floret subword vectors trained on a large corpus of Latin text improves downstream task performance.
- Mechanism: Subword embeddings capture morphological regularities and rare word forms, which are critical for highly inflected languages like Latin.
- Core assumption: Morphological richness in Latin means subword modeling helps more than word-level embeddings alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] states Floret vectors are "used in the training of components elsewhere in the pipeline" and that the 'lg' model uses 200k vectors
  - [abstract] reports high accuracy scores for POS tagging, lemmatization, and morphological tagging
  - [corpus] shows related papers also use subword embeddings, but does not confirm this improves accuracy for Latin
- Break condition: If evaluation shows no improvement or even degradation in accuracy compared to non-subword models, the benefit is negated.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Customizing the tokenizer to split enclitic -que and normalizing orthographic forms boosts accuracy.
- Mechanism: Handling morphological edge cases like -que splitting and u/v normalization reduces ambiguity in downstream tasks.
- Core assumption: Edge cases in tokenization and orthography are frequent enough to materially impact model performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] describes the custom tokenizer "such that all words that end with -que other than a provided list of exceptions" are split
  - [abstract] reports high accuracy scores for POS tagging, lemmatization, and morphological tagging
  - [corpus] does not mention tokenization or orthography, so cannot confirm this claim
- Break condition: If evaluation shows no improvement in accuracy after these customizations, the benefit is negligible.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Plaintext Latin
  - Tokenizer: Custom Latin tokenizer with -que splitting
  - tok2vec: Shared encoder for all components except NER
  - Components:
    - tagger: POS tagging (XPOS and UPOS)
    - morphologizer: Morphological tagging
    - trainable_lemmatizer: Lemmatization with edit trees
    - parser: Dependency parsing and sentence segmentation
    - ner: Named entity recognition (PERSON, LOC, NORP)
  - Output: Annotated Doc with tokens, POS, lemmas, morphology, dependencies, and entities
  - Vectors: Floret subword vectors (50k for 'md', 200k for 'lg')

- Critical path: Tokenizer → tok2vec → tagger/morphologizer/lemmatizer/parser → NER
  - NER uses its own tok2vec and is trained separately

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pros: Unified model from all UD treebanks, subword vectors for morphology, custom tokenizer for edge cases
  - Cons: Preprocessing decisions may lose annotation detail, subword vectors increase model size, custom components add complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy on rare or morphologically complex words
  - Incorrect tokenization of -que or orthographic normalization
  - NER misses entities not well represented in training data
  - Dependency parsing errors on sentences from underrepresented genres

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the model on a small sample of unseen Latin text and inspect tokenization, POS, lemmas, dependencies, and NER outputs.
  2. Evaluate accuracy on a held-out UD test set to confirm reported scores.
  3. Modify the que_exceptions list and retrain to see if accuracy on -que handling improves.

## Limitations

- Exact preprocessing mappings for harmonizing the five UD treebanks are not documented, making it difficult to assess what linguistic distinctions may have been lost
- Evaluation methodology is not fully detailed - unclear whether reported scores represent averages across all treebanks or performance on held-out test sets
- Paper does not address how well models handle out-of-domain text or rare morphological constructions

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The technical implementation of training Floret subword vectors and integrating them into spaCy pipelines is sound and well-established. The reported accuracy scores for POS tagging, lemmatization, and morphological tagging are plausible given the quality of the training data.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that preprocessing the five UD treebanks yields better generalization than using a single treebank. While the mechanism is logical, the paper doesn't provide direct comparison or ablation studies to confirm this improvement.

**Low confidence**: The effectiveness of the custom tokenizer's -que splitting rules and orthographic normalization. Without quantitative evaluation showing accuracy improvements from these specific customizations, their impact remains speculative.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-domain robustness test**: Evaluate LatinCy on a held-out test set of Latin text from a genre or time period not represented in the training data (e.g., medieval legal texts if training focused on classical prose). Compare performance to baseline models to assess domain generalization.

2. **Ablation study of preprocessing**: Train two versions of the model - one with the full preprocessing pipeline and one with raw, unaligned UD annotations. Measure differences in accuracy to quantify the benefit (or harm) of the harmonization process.

3. **Tokenizer customization impact**: Create a modified version of LatinCy with the custom -que tokenizer disabled and compare tokenization accuracy on a manually annotated evaluation set. This would reveal whether the custom rules provide measurable improvement or introduce errors.
