# Symmetry and Complexity in Object-Centric Deep Active Inference Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14493
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14493
- Authors: [not specified in source]
- Reference count: 35
- Key outcome: Higher β values in deep active inference models lead to lower model complexity and increased symmetry exploitation in latent space representations

## Executive Summary
This paper explores how symmetries in objects emerge in the latent state space of object-centric deep active inference models. The authors propose that minimizing model complexity through the variational free energy (VFE) objective causes the generative model to learn and exploit symmetries in object representations. By training on a subset of the YCB dataset with varying β parameters, they demonstrate that higher β values reduce model complexity and increase symmetry exploitation in the latent space. The study reveals that learned symmetries capture the physical axes of symmetry of objects and that exploiting these symmetries aids in generalizing action selection for object manipulation tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a deep active inference framework with an object-centric generative model. The model is trained on a subset of the YCB dataset while varying the β parameter, which controls the trade-off between accuracy and model complexity in the VFE objective. The model learns to infer object-centric representations in latent space, and symmetry emergence is analyzed through principal component analysis. The authors evaluate how symmetry exploitation impacts action selection generalization for object manipulation tasks, comparing performance across different β values.

## Key Results
- Higher β values lead to lower model complexity and increased symmetry exploitation in latent space
- Principal component analysis reveals that learned symmetries capture the physical axes of symmetry of objects
- Exploiting symmetries in latent space aids in generalizing action selection for object manipulation tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism underlying symmetry emergence in this model stems from the variational free energy (VFE) minimization objective. When β increases, the complexity term in VFE becomes more heavily weighted, forcing the model to find more compressed representations. Symmetries in object structure allow for such compression - objects with rotational or reflectional symmetry can be represented more efficiently by encoding only the fundamental components and symmetry operations. This creates a natural pressure toward learning symmetry-preserving representations when model complexity is prioritized over reconstruction accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Image -> Encoder -> Latent Space -> Decoder -> Reconstructed Image
Action Selection -> Manipulation Task Performance

Critical path: The model's critical path flows from image input through the encoder to latent space representation, where symmetries emerge, then through the decoder for reconstruction. The action selection component uses these latent representations to generalize manipulation tasks.

Design tradeoffs: The primary design tradeoff involves balancing reconstruction accuracy against model complexity through the β parameter. Higher β favors simpler models that exploit symmetries but may sacrifice reconstruction fidelity. Lower β allows more accurate reconstructions but potentially misses structural regularities.

Failure signatures: The model may fail to learn meaningful symmetries when objects have low inherent symmetry or when β is set too low to encourage complexity minimization. Poor action selection generalization can indicate that learned symmetries don't capture task-relevant structure.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can agents learn the optimal accuracy-complexity trade-off in view of their preferences, rather than manually varying β?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention this as future work, stating "In future work, we aim to investigate further how agents can learn the optimal accuracy-complexity trade-off, in view of an agent that needs to realize a certain set of preferences."
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on manually varying the β parameter to control the trade-off, but does not explore how agents could autonomously learn the optimal balance based on their preferences.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating an agent autonomously adjusting the complexity-accuracy trade-off based on its preferences, leading to improved performance on a task.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would incorporating equivariant architectures impact symmetry exploitation in the latent space?
- Basis in paper: The authors discuss the potential benefits of equivariant representations and mention "As an extension to the current work, we aim to investigate the impact of the optimizations introduced by Falorsi et al. in the symmetry exploitation scenario."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not experiment with equivariant architectures, which could potentially enhance the model's ability to exploit symmetries in the latent space.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments between the current model and an equivariant variant, showing the impact on symmetry exploitation and overall performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the exploitation of symmetries in the latent space relate to disentanglement?
- Basis in paper: The authors discuss the relationship between symmetry and disentanglement, stating "The type of experimentation performed in this work is similar to the one performed in the disentanglement work by Higgins et al. [12], where they showed how higher β results in better disentanglement in the latent space."
- Why unresolved: While the authors note similarities between their work and disentanglement research, they do not directly investigate the relationship between symmetry exploitation and disentanglement in their experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments explicitly measuring disentanglement in the latent space as a function of symmetry exploitation, potentially through additional regularization terms or modifications to the model architecture.

## Limitations
- Limited to a single, relatively small dataset (subset of YCB) with limited object diversity
- Focuses on static object representations without examining temporal dynamics or real-world object interactions
- β parameter analysis covers only a narrow range (0.1 to 1.0) that may not capture the full complexity-accuracy trade-off space

## Confidence
- High confidence in the theoretical framework connecting VFE minimization to symmetry exploitation
- Medium confidence in the empirical demonstration of symmetry emergence in latent space
- Low confidence in the generalization of findings to broader object manipulation scenarios
