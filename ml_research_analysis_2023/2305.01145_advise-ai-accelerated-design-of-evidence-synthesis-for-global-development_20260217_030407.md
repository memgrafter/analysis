# ADVISE: AI-accelerated Design of Evidence Synthesis for Global Development

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01145
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01145
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BERT-based AI agent reduces screening effort by 68.5% while identifying 80% of relevant documents

## Executive Summary
This study introduces ADVISE, an AI-accelerated approach to evidence synthesis for global development using BERT-based models to screen titles and abstracts. The system was integrated into human workflows to reduce manual screening effort while maintaining high recall of relevant documents. Through human-AI hybrid teaming and active learning strategies, the approach achieved significant efficiency gains in practical applications for USAID evidence gap mapping projects.

## Method Summary
The study developed a BERT-based AI agent for title and abstract screening in evidence synthesis workflows. The agent was integrated into human review processes, with active learning employed to prioritize documents for human screening. The system was validated through construction of three evidence gap maps for USAID, comparing performance against traditional SVM-based approaches and unassisted screening methods.

## Key Results
- BERT-based AI reduced human screening effort by 68.5% compared to no AI assistance
- Further reduction to 78.3% achieved through active learning with highest priority sampling
- System maintained 80% recall of relevant documents while accelerating the screening process

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The BERT-based AI agent leverages transformer architecture to capture semantic relationships in document text, enabling more accurate classification than traditional SVM approaches. Active learning prioritizes documents where the model has highest uncertainty, maximizing information gain from each human review and accelerating model improvement during the screening process.

## Foundational Learning
- **BERT transformer architecture** - why needed: captures contextual semantic relationships in text; quick check: verify model understands synonyms and polysemy in development literature
- **Active learning sampling strategies** - why needed: optimizes human effort by prioritizing uncertain or informative examples; quick check: confirm highest priority sampling consistently outperforms random selection
- **Evidence synthesis workflow** - why needed: ensures AI integration complements rather than disrupts established research processes; quick check: validate that AI assistance maintains or improves screening accuracy

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Document repository -> BERT classifier -> Active learning sampler -> Human review interface -> Feedback loop -> Updated model
Critical path: Document screening where AI predictions guide human prioritization
Design tradeoffs: Higher recall (80%) achieved at cost of some precision; active learning adds complexity but reduces overall effort
Failure signatures: Poor performance on domain-specific terminology; overfitting to training data characteristics; active learning inefficiency when document similarity is high
First experiments: 1) Test BERT vs SVM on held-out validation set; 2) Compare different active learning sampling strategies; 3) Measure human-AI team performance on progressively larger document sets

## Limitations
- Performance metrics may not generalize to domains outside global development
- Limited validation through only three evidence gap map projects
- Real-world conditions with varying reviewer expertise levels not fully explored

## Confidence
High: AI assistance reduces screening effort (68.5% reduction demonstrated)
Medium: Active learning further improves performance (directional improvement shown)
Low: AI acceleration translates to faster evidence-based decision making (process efficiency demonstrated, not decision impact)

## Next Checks
1. Test BERT-based agent on evidence synthesis tasks from other domains (healthcare, environmental science, etc.) to assess generalizability
2. Conduct field studies with multiple reviewer teams of varying expertise levels to validate performance claims under real-world conditions
3. Measure downstream impacts on actual decision-making processes and outcomes, not just screening efficiency metrics
