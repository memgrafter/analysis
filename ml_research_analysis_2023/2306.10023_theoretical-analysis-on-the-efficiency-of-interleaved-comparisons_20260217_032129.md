# Theoretical Analysis on the Efficiency of Interleaved Comparisons
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10023
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10023
- Reference count: 29
- This study presents a theoretical analysis on the efficiency of interleaving methods in online ranking evaluation.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a theoretical analysis of interleaving methods for online ranking evaluation, addressing why these methods demonstrate high efficiency compared to traditional A/B testing. While interleaving has been deployed in production systems, its theoretical efficiency advantages remained unclear in the literature. The authors develop a simple interleaving method and derive conditions under which it outperforms A/B testing, specifically when users leave rankings based on item relevance. The theoretical findings are validated through numerical analysis and user simulation experiments.

## Method Summary
The authors design a simple interleaving method similar to existing approaches and analyze its efficiency theoretically. They establish conditions under which interleaving methods are more efficient than A/B testing, particularly when user behavior follows the assumption that users abandon rankings based on item relevance - a common assumption in click models. The theoretical analysis is complemented with numerical experiments and user simulations to validate the theoretical predictions and demonstrate consistency between theoretical and empirical results.

## Key Results
- Interleaving methods are theoretically proven to be more efficient than A/B testing when users leave rankings based on item relevance
- The theoretical efficiency advantage aligns with common assumptions in click models about user behavior
- Numerical analysis and user simulation experiments validate the theoretical findings, showing consistency between theory and empirical results

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The efficiency of interleaving stems from its ability to extract comparative information from fewer users than A/B testing requires. When users abandon rankings based on item relevance, interleaving methods can simultaneously expose users to items from multiple ranking algorithms, allowing direct comparison of ranking quality with fewer user interactions. This contrasts with A/B testing, which requires separate user groups for each algorithm, leading to higher sample size requirements.

## Limitations
- The analysis focuses on a simple interleaving method that may not capture all nuances of complex real-world implementations
- Efficiency gains depend on the assumption that users abandon rankings based on item relevance, which may not hold universally
- Empirical validation relies on numerical analysis and user simulation rather than large-scale real-world deployment data

## Confidence
High: Theoretical framework demonstrating interleaving efficiency advantage under specific conditions (relevance-based user abandonment)
Medium: Empirical validation through numerical analysis and simulation showing consistency with theoretical predictions
Low: Generalization of findings to all interleaving methods and ranking scenarios given focus on simplified approach
