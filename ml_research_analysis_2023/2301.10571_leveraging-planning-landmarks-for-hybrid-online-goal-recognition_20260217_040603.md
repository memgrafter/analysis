# Leveraging Planning Landmarks for Hybrid Online Goal Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10571
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10571
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: Landmark-based goal recognition reduces computation time from seconds to milliseconds while improving accuracy on real-world cooking tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a landmark-based approach to online goal recognition that dramatically reduces computational requirements compared to traditional planning-based methods. The method extracts planning landmarks from symbolic domain models and uses them to estimate goal probabilities based on observed actions. By optimizing landmark extraction for non-STRIPS PDDL domains and ignoring trivial landmarks, the approach achieves significantly better results on the TiaGo cooking dataset than traditional planning-based goal recognition. The landmark-based method requires only milliseconds per goal after initial landmark extraction, making it suitable for real-time applications. A hybrid approach combining landmarks with a data-driven Bayesian network further improves performance, achieving better accuracy than either method alone across different observation sequence lengths.

## Method Summary
The approach leverages planning landmarks extracted from symbolic domain models to enable efficient online goal recognition. Landmarks are derived from the domain's planning problem, representing essential subgoals that must be achieved for any solution. The method extracts landmarks from non-STRIPS PDDL domains and optimizes the extraction process to avoid trivial landmarks that would slow computation. For goal probability estimation, the approach uses landmark-based heuristics that update as new actions are observed, requiring only milliseconds of computation per goal after the initial landmark extraction phase. The hybrid method combines this landmark-based approach with a data-driven Bayesian network trained on domain data, providing complementary strengths that improve overall recognition accuracy.

## Key Results
- Landmark-based goal recognition reduces computation time from seconds to milliseconds per goal after landmark extraction
- The approach achieves significantly better accuracy than planning-based methods on the TiaGo cooking dataset
- The hybrid method combining landmarks with a Bayesian network outperforms both individual approaches across different observation sequence lengths

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The landmark-based approach works by identifying essential subgoals (landmarks) that must be achieved in any valid plan. When actions are observed, the system can quickly determine which landmarks have been achieved and use this information to update goal probabilities. This is fundamentally more efficient than generating complete plans for each candidate goal, as it only tracks the achievement of key milestones rather than entire plan structures. The method exploits the fact that many planning domains have recognizable patterns in landmark achievement that correlate strongly with specific goals.

## Foundational Learning
- **Planning landmarks**: Subgoals that must be true in any valid plan for a given goal. Needed because they provide a compressed representation of plan structure that can be efficiently tracked during execution.
- **Non-STRIPS PDDL domains**: Planning domains with conditional effects and complex action structures beyond basic STRIPS. Required because many real-world domains cannot be adequately represented in simpler formalisms.
- **Online goal recognition**: The process of inferring goals from partial action sequences as they occur. Critical for applications requiring real-time goal inference.
- **Bayesian networks for goal recognition**: Probabilistic models that capture dependencies between observations and goals. Useful for combining data-driven patterns with symbolic knowledge.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Domain Model -> Landmark Extractor -> Landmark Database -> Probability Estimator -> Bayesian Network (hybrid only)

**Critical Path**: Landmark extraction happens offline once per domain. During execution, observed actions trigger landmark achievement checks, which update goal probabilities in real-time. The hybrid method adds Bayesian network inference as a parallel estimation that is combined with landmark-based estimates.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The main tradeoff is between computational efficiency and recognition accuracy. Landmark-based methods are extremely fast but may miss nuanced goal distinctions that planning-based methods capture. The hybrid approach balances these by combining fast landmark tracking with more sophisticated Bayesian inference.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor landmark extraction (too few landmarks) leads to coarse-grained recognition that cannot distinguish similar goals. Excessive landmarks (including trivial ones) cause computational slowdown. The hybrid method may suffer if the Bayesian network is poorly trained or if landmark and Bayesian estimates conflict without a proper fusion mechanism.

## Limitations

- Computational advantages assume offline landmark extraction, but preprocessing costs are not thoroughly analyzed
