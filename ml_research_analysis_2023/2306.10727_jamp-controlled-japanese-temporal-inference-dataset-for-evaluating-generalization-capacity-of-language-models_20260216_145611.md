# Jamp: Controlled Japanese Temporal Inference Dataset for Evaluating Generalization Capacity of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10727
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10727

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces JAMP, a controlled Japanese Natural Language Inference (NLI) dataset designed to evaluate temporal generalization in language models. Using a template-based approach, the dataset systematically controls inference patterns and gold labels to test how well LMs can generalize across different temporal expressions. Experiments reveal that while models show reasonable temporal generalization, they struggle with specific linguistic phenomena such as habituality, highlighting areas for improvement in NLI model development across languages.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed JAMP using a template-based approach to create a controlled Japanese NLI dataset focused on temporal inference. The methodology involves designing templates that systematically vary temporal expressions and inference patterns while maintaining control over the distribution of gold labels. This controlled construction allows for systematic evaluation of language models' ability to generalize across different temporal phenomena. The dataset is then used to evaluate multiple pre-trained Japanese language models, examining their performance on various temporal inference tasks and identifying specific areas where models struggle, such as handling habitual expressions.

## Key Results
- LMs demonstrate reasonable generalization across different temporal expressions in Japanese NLI tasks
- Models show significant difficulty with habituality phenomena, struggling to correctly infer temporal relationships involving habitual actions
- The controlled template approach successfully isolates temporal inference capabilities from other confounding linguistic factors

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The controlled template-based approach works by systematically isolating temporal inference patterns from other linguistic variables, allowing researchers to measure model generalization capacity without interference from confounding factors. By controlling the distribution of inference patterns and gold labels, the dataset creates a standardized evaluation framework that reveals specific weaknesses in how models handle temporal relationships. This methodological rigor enables clear identification of which temporal phenomena (like habituality) pose particular challenges for current language models.

## Limitations
- The dataset focuses specifically on temporal inference, representing only one dimension of NLI complexity
- The template-based approach may not capture the full range of linguistic phenomena present in naturally occurring text
- The dataset's reliance on Japanese language structures may limit direct generalizability to other languages without additional cross-linguistic validation

## Confidence

High: Claims about LMs struggling with habituality phenomena, demonstrated through controlled experimental conditions with consistent results across multiple models.

Medium: Claims about temporal generalization capacity, supported by controlled dataset but may not fully represent real-world complexity.

Low: Broader claims about NLI model development, as these extend beyond the specific temporal focus of the dataset.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct ablation studies removing template constraints to assess whether results hold in more naturalistic Japanese text.

2. Perform cross-linguistic validation by creating analogous controlled datasets in languages with different temporal expression systems (e.g., Mandarin Chinese or Turkish) to test whether observed generalization patterns are language-independent.

3. Extend evaluation to include zero-shot and few-shot prompting strategies to determine whether instruction-tuning can mitigate observed weaknesses in handling habituality and other temporal phenomena.
