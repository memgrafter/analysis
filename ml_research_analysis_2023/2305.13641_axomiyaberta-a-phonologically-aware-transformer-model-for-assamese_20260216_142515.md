# AxomiyaBERTa: A Phonologically-aware Transformer Model for Assamese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13641
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13641
- Reference count: 21
- Introduces AxomiyaBERTa, a monolingual BERT model for Assamese with phonological features and embedding dispersion

## Executive Summary
This paper presents AxomiyaBERTa, a novel monolingual BERT-based language model for Assamese that achieves state-of-the-art performance on Named Entity Recognition and strong results on longer-context tasks. The model addresses challenges in low-resource language modeling through two key innovations: an embedding disperser that mitigates anisotropy in smaller models, and the incorporation of phonological/articulatory features to enhance performance on tasks requiring longer context. AxomiyaBERTa is trained only on masked language modeling without next sentence prediction to accommodate resource-constrained settings, while still achieving competitive results across multiple downstream tasks.

## Method Summary
AxomiyaBERTa is a 66M parameter ALBERT-base-v2 model trained exclusively on masked language modeling (MLM) without next sentence prediction (NSP). The model incorporates two novel components: an embedding disperser that reduces anisotropy through a combined cosine embedding loss and binary cross-entropy loss, and a phonological attention layer that integrates articulatory features from the PanPhon system. The model is fine-tuned on four downstream tasks: Named Entity Recognition (NER), Cloze-style question answering, Wiki title prediction, and cross-document coreference resolution on a translated ECB+ corpus. Training uses Assamese datasets including Wikidumps, OSCAR, PMIndia, Common Crawl, and translated ECB+ corpus.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art F1 score of 89.91 on the AsNER dataset, surpassing baselines including XLM-R, MBERT, IndicBERT, and MuRIL
- Demonstrates strong performance on longer-context tasks: Cloze-QA (80.65% accuracy), Wiki title prediction (88.48% accuracy)
- Shows significant improvement on cross-document coreference resolution with CoNLL F1 of 36.16, particularly on mentions requiring long-distance context
- Phonological features improve disambiguation performance, especially for ambiguous tokens and semantic variations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Removing the NSP objective and adding phonological features compensates for the loss of longer-context coherence modeling.
- Mechanism: Phonological features capture articulatory patterns that correlate with semantic and morphological similarity across word boundaries, providing implicit signal for longer-range dependencies.
- Core assumption: Phonological representations contain sufficient information to model semantic coherence across sentences without explicit NSP training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Addition of phonological signals significantly improves disambiguation and performance, especially on tasks with ambiguous tokens or semantic variations."
  - [section 3.2.2] "We hypothesize that when fine-tuning a smaller model like AxomiyaBERTa in a resource-constrained setting, adding phonological signals to the latent representations of text samples allows us to achieve a balanced trade-off between possible information loss due to reduced supervision (no NSP objective) and improved task-specific performance."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No direct comparison of phonological feature effectiveness vs NSP on same dataset.
- Break condition: If phonological features fail to correlate with semantic coherence (e.g., for languages with shallow orthography or minimal phonological variation).

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Embedding anisotropy in smaller models degrades classification performance, and the embedding disperser architecture mitigates this by creating local isotropic regions.
- Mechanism: The combination of cosine embedding loss and binary cross-entropy loss pushes semantically similar embeddings closer while separating dissimilar ones, reducing global anisotropy.
- Core assumption: Local isotropy within task-relevant embedding neighborhoods is more important for classification than global isotropy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We present a novel combined loss technique to disperse AxomiyaBERTa's embeddings"
  - [section 3.2.1] "Without a meaningful objective to force embedding vectors apart during training, they trend toward an arbitrary center in Rd space. This phenomenon has also been observed by Gao et al. (2018), Ethayarajh (2019), and Demeter et al. (2020), among others."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No ablation showing performance difference between anisotropic and dispersed embeddings on held-out data.
- Break condition: If task performance does not improve despite reduced anisotropy (suggesting anisotropy isn't the limiting factor).

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Phonological awareness improves disambiguation in Named Entity Recognition by capturing morphological overlap that subword tokenization obscures.
- Mechanism: Phonological features encode articulatory information that correlates with morphological boundaries and semantic classes, providing complementary signal to surface form.
- Core assumption: Morphological information relevant to NER can be recovered from phonological features when morphological analyzers are unavailable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The addition of phonological signals significantly improves disambiguation and performance, especially on tasks with ambiguous tokens or semantic variations."
  - [section 5.1] "These results suggest phonological inputs help enrich embeddings of smaller-sized LMs to distinguish such ambiguous tokens."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No direct comparison with morphological analyzer-based approach on same dataset.
- Break condition: If phonological features provide no benefit for languages with transparent orthography or when morphological analyzers are available.

## Limitations
- The paper doesn't establish whether phonological features truly substitute for NSP's longer-context modeling or provide orthogonal signal through ablation studies
- Evaluation on cross-document coreference resolution uses translated ECB+ corpus, which may not generalize to native Assamese patterns
- The embedding disperser's contribution is not isolated - no ablation studies separate anisotropy mitigation effects from phonological feature benefits

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Technical implementation details (ALBERT-base with MLM-only training, PanPhon features, cosine embedding loss) are sound and well-documented. Evaluation methodology follows standard practices.

**Medium confidence**: Phonological features improve disambiguation is supported by results but lacks rigorous ablation studies. Baseline comparisons are reasonable but don't establish phonological features as decisive factor.

**Low confidence**: Claims about embedding anisotropy being the primary bottleneck and the disperser architecture being optimal lack sufficient empirical validation through ablation studies or alternative regularization comparisons.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on phonological features**: Train AxomiyaBERTa with NSP objective retained instead of phonological features and compare performance on same downstream tasks to establish whether phonological features compensate for NSP removal or provide independent benefits.

2. **Cross-linguistic phonological feature validation**: Test whether phonological features provide similar disambiguation benefits on another morphologically-rich low-resource language with different phonological properties (e.g., Turkish or Tamil) to assess whether improvement is phonologically general or Assamese-specific.

3. **Embedding anisotropy impact measurement**: Train two versions of AxomiyaBERTa - one with embedding disperser and one without - and measure both anisotropy metrics (cosine similarity distributions) and downstream task performance. Include baseline using alternative anisotropy mitigation techniques to determine if specific disperser architecture is optimal.
