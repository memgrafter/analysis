# Large Language Models are Versatile Decomposers: Decompose Evidence and Questions for Table-based Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13808
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13808
- Reference count: 13
- Key outcome: Introduces a novel method for table-based reasoning that leverages LLMs as versatile decomposers, achieving state-of-the-art results and outperforming human performance on the TabFact dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel method for table-based reasoning that leverages large language models (LLMs) as versatile decomposers. The approach addresses two key challenges: handling huge evidence (tables) and reasoning over complex questions. It decomposes large tables into smaller, relevant sub-tables by predicting relevant rows and columns, and breaks down complex questions into simpler sub-questions using a "parsing-execution-filling" strategy that utilizes SQL queries. The method is evaluated on three benchmark datasets (TabFact, WikiTableQuestion, and FetaQA) and achieves state-of-the-art results, outperforming human performance for the first time on the TabFact dataset. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the decomposition approach in improving table-based reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

## Method Summary
The proposed method, called Dater, leverages large language models (LLMs) as versatile decomposers to address the challenges of table-based reasoning. It consists of three main components: an evidence decomposer that breaks down huge tables into smaller, relevant sub-tables by predicting relevant rows and columns using in-context learning; a question decomposer that decomposes complex questions into simpler sub-questions using a "parsing-execution-filling" strategy with SQL queries to obtain reliable numerical values; and a reasoner that jointly leverages the decomposed evidence and reliable sub-questions to arrive at the final answer. The approach is evaluated on three benchmark datasets (TabFact, WikiTableQuestion, and FetaQA) and achieves state-of-the-art results, outperforming human performance for the first time on the TabFact dataset.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on TabFact, WikiTableQuestion, and FetaQA benchmark datasets
- Outperforms human performance on the TabFact dataset for the first time
- Demonstrates the effectiveness of decomposition approach in improving table-based reasoning capabilities of LLMs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Decomposing huge tables into smaller sub-tables reduces interference from irrelevant information and makes reasoning computationally tractable for LLMs.
- Mechanism: The evidence decomposer predicts relevant row and column indexes using in-context learning, creating a focused sub-table that contains only the information needed to answer the question.
- Core assumption: LLMs can effectively identify relevant table regions when provided with appropriate in-context examples and prompts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "decompose huge evidence (a huge table) into sub-evidence (a small table) to mitigate the interference of useless information for table reasoning"
  - [section 4.2]: "we implement sub-evidence extraction by predicting the indexes of the rows and columns with the help of a powerful LLM and a few prompts"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct experimental results showing computational tractability improvements, only claims about interference reduction
- Break condition: If the LLM cannot accurately identify relevant table regions, or if the sub-table becomes too small to contain necessary information for complex reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Decomposing complex questions into simpler sub-questions with a "parsing-execution-filling" strategy prevents hallucination and enables reliable multi-step reasoning.
- Mechanism: The question decomposer masks numerical values to create abstract logic sub-questions, converts them to SQL queries, executes them on the evidence to obtain reliable values, then fills these values back into the sub-questions.
- Core assumption: SQL execution provides reliable numerical values that prevent LLM hallucination when generating sub-questions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose a 'parsing-execution-filling' strategy to alleviate the hallucination dilemma of the chain of thought by decoupling logic and numerical computation in each step"
  - [section 4.3.1]: "we generate an abstract logic sub-question by masking the span of numerical values and then converting the abstract logic into SQL query language that is executed on the evidence for obtaining a reliable sub-question"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct comparison of hallucination rates with and without this strategy
- Break condition: If SQL execution fails to produce correct values, or if the masking-filling process introduces errors in the logical structure of questions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Joint reasoning over decomposed sub-evidence and reliable sub-questions achieves superior performance compared to direct reasoning on full tables and questions.
- Mechanism: After decomposition, the reasoner processes the focused sub-table and step-by-step sub-questions to arrive at the final answer, leveraging the reduced complexity and increased reliability.
- Core assumption: Decomposing both evidence and questions in a coordinated manner provides better information for reasoning than either approach alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "our method can effectively leverage decomposed evidence and questions and outperforms the strong baselines"
  - [section 4.4]: "the reasoner jointly leverage sub-evidence and reliable sub-questions to get a final answer"
  - [corpus]: Strong evidence - ablation study in Table 4 shows performance degradation when removing either decomposition component
- Break condition: If the decomposition process introduces errors that compound during joint reasoning, or if the reasoning task requires information spread across the entire table that gets filtered out.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: In-context learning with LLMs
  - Why needed here: The entire approach relies on LLMs performing reasoning tasks without fine-tuning, using only prompts and examples
  - Quick check question: How many in-context examples are typically provided for each decomposition step, and what is their format?

- Concept: SQL as intermediate reasoning language
  - Why needed here: SQL provides a structured way to extract reliable numerical values from tables, preventing hallucination in sub-question generation
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between using SQL versus natural language for intermediate reasoning steps?

- Concept: Table linearization and representation
  - Why needed here: Tables must be converted to text format for LLM processing, and the decomposition must work with this linearized representation
  - Quick check question: What information might be lost when converting a table to text format for LLM processing?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Question → Evidence Decomposer → Sub-table → Question Decomposer → Reliable sub-questions → Reasoner → Answer

- Critical path: Question → Evidence Decomposer → Sub-table → Question Decomposer → Reliable sub-questions → Reasoner → Answer

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using SQL vs. direct numerical computation in sub-questions
  - Number of in-context examples (4, 2, 6) vs. model performance
  - Temperature settings (0.7, 0.5, 0.5) vs. consistency of outputs
  - Self-consistency decoding vs. single inference

- Failure signatures:
  - Evidence decomposer fails: Sub-table missing critical information, performance drops significantly on complex questions
  - Question decomposer fails: Sub-questions contain hallucinated information, SQL execution errors, logical inconsistencies
  - Reasoner fails: Unable to answer even with decomposed inputs, suggests decomposition errors

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test evidence decomposition alone on TabFact test set, measure accuracy drop when removing this component
  2. Test question decomposition alone on WikiTableQuestion, measure accuracy with and without SQL-based approach
  3. Test joint reasoning with synthetic tables where ground truth decomposition is known, verify sub-tables contain correct information

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the limitations section, several open questions can be identified:

- How does the performance of Dater scale with larger tables beyond the current token limitations?
- How robust is Dater to tables with different structures or formatting, such as tables with merged cells or non-standard layouts?
- How does the performance of Dater compare to fine-tuned models on domain-specific tasks outside the benchmark datasets?

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of decomposition relies heavily on LLM performance, which is not fully characterized across different table sizes and complexities
- No ablation studies show whether the "parsing-execution-filling" strategy actually reduces hallucination compared to direct LLM reasoning
- The SQL-based approach may fail on tables with complex hierarchical structures or non-standard formats

## Confidence
- High confidence: The overall decomposition framework is technically sound and the state-of-the-art results on benchmarks are verifiable
- Medium confidence: The claim that decomposition "mitigates interference" lacks direct experimental evidence showing computational benefits
- Low confidence: The assertion that this is the "first time" outperforming human performance on TabFact needs independent verification, as human baselines can vary significantly

## Next Checks
1. Test the method on tables with non-standard formats (hierarchical headers, merged cells) to assess decomposition robustness

3. Evaluate performance degradation as table size increases beyond the typical benchmark sizes to understand practical limits
