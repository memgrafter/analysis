# Interpretability is a Kind of Safety: An Interpreter-based Ensemble for Adversary Defense

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06919
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06919
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: X-Ensemble framework using interpreter-based detection and rectification achieves robust defense against various adversarial attacks through gradient correlation and Random Forest ensemble robustness

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes X-Ensemble, an interpreter-based ensemble framework for defending deep neural networks against adversarial attacks. The framework combines multiple interpretation-based sub-detectors with a Random Forest ensemble to detect adversarial examples, then uses an interpreter-guided rectifier to recover them. The approach leverages the correlation between gradient-based interpretation methods and adversarial generation processes, while the non-differentiable RF detector provides robustness against gradient-based attacks targeting the detector itself.

## Method Summary
X-Ensemble adopts a detection-rectification pipeline featuring multiple sub-detectors (VG, IG, GBP, LRP, ORG) built upon various interpretation methods, combined via Random Forest ensemble. The framework first detects adversarial examples by comparing interpretation maps between benign and adversarial inputs, then rectifies detected adversarial examples through interpreter-guided random erasure of high-importance pixels. The non-differentiable RF model provides inherent protection against gradient-based attacks on the detector, while the ensemble handles hybrid attacks from multiple attacker types.

## Key Results
- X-Ensemble achieves higher detection AUC than baseline methods under grey-box, black-box, and white-box attack scenarios
- The interpreter-guided rectifier successfully recovers adversarial examples to benign classifications with high accuracy
- Random Forest ensemble provides superior robustness compared to CNN-based detectors against adversarial attacks targeting the detector itself

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Gradient-based sensitivity analysis of DNN interpreters correlates with adversarial example generation, enabling detection of adversarial examples by comparing interpretation maps between benign and adversarial inputs.
- Mechanism: Attackers use gradients to iteratively perturb pixels in adversarial examples, while interpreters measure pixel importance via gradients. This creates a correlation where adversarial examples exhibit different gradient distributions compared to benign examples.
- Core assumption: The sensitivity analysis-based interpreters and adversarial example generation processes share the same underlying gradient information, making the interpretation maps informative for adversarial detection.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we first reveal a gradient-based correlation between sensitivity analysis-based DNN interpreters and the generation process of adversarial examples"
  - [section]: "The gradient 𝑔𝑖 𝑗𝑙 also plays an important role in adversarial examples generation" and "pixels with larger |𝑔𝑖 𝑗𝑙 | in an adversarial example tend to be perturbed"
  - [corpus]: Weak - the corpus neighbors focus on different defense strategies without directly addressing the gradient-interpretation correlation mechanism.
- Break condition: If attackers develop methods that specifically minimize gradient differences between benign and adversarial examples, or if the gradient distributions become indistinguishable due to attack sophistication.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Random Forest (RF) ensemble detector provides robustness against adversarial attacks targeting the detector itself due to its non-differentiable nature.
- Mechanism: RF models cannot be directly attacked using gradient-based methods, making them more robust than differentiable detectors like CNNs. The ensemble combines multiple interpretation-based sub-detectors to handle hybrid attacks.
- Core assumption: The non-differentiable property of RF models provides inherent protection against gradient-based adversarial attacks that target the detector.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The non-differentiable property of RF further makes it a precious choice against the counterattack of adversaries"
  - [section]: "Compared with CNN-based models, the random forest model is non-differentiable and therefore not easy to be directly attacked"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors mention various defense strategies but don't specifically address the RF-based detector robustness mechanism.
- Break condition: If attackers develop black-box or transfer-based methods that can effectively target RF ensembles, or if the RF ensemble becomes differentiable through modifications.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Interpreter-guided random erasure of pixels in adversarial examples can recover them to benign examples by removing attacker-induced perturbations.
- Mechanism: The rectifier identifies pixels with high interpretation scores (likely perturbed by attackers) and randomly erases them with a probabilistic mechanism, then fine-tunes the classifier on the rectified examples.
- Core assumption: Pixels with high interpretation scores in adversarial examples are more likely to be the ones modified by attackers, and removing them can recover the original classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "An interpreter-based rectifier recovers adversarial examples to benign ones"
  - [section]: "For an adversarial example, its predicted label 𝑙 is unreliable, so the large 𝑔𝑖 𝑗𝑙 pixels are very likely to be modified by attackers"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors focus on different rectification or defense approaches without addressing the interpreter-guided random erasure mechanism.
- Break condition: If attackers develop methods that spread perturbations across many pixels rather than concentrating them on high-interpretation-score pixels, or if the random erasure mechanism accidentally removes important features.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Multiple interpretation sub-detectors (VG, IG, GBP, LRP, ORG) → Random Forest ensemble detector → Decision (benign/adversarial) → If adversarial → Rectifier (interpreter-guided random erasure) → Fine-tuned classifier → Output
- Critical path: Input → Detector ensemble → (If adversarial) → Rectifier → Classifier → Output
  The detector must correctly identify adversarial examples before the rectifier can recover them, and the classifier must work with rectified examples
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using multiple interpretation methods increases detection accuracy but adds computational overhead
  - Random erasure in rectification provides robustness but may reduce classification accuracy on borderline cases
  - Ensemble approach handles hybrid attacks but requires training multiple sub-detectors
- Failure signatures:
  - High false positive rate: Detector incorrectly flags benign examples as adversarial
  - Low detection rate: Detector fails to identify actual adversarial examples
  - Poor rectification: Rectifier fails to recover adversarial examples to their correct classifications
  - Slow inference: Computational overhead from multiple interpretation methods and ensemble processing
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test detector accuracy on clean examples to establish baseline false positive rate
  2. Test detector accuracy on known adversarial examples from each attack type (FGSM, PGD, Deepfool, CW, DDN)
  3. Test end-to-end classification accuracy with and without rectifier on adversarial examples

## Limitations

- The gradient-correlation mechanism may not hold against advanced adaptive attacks that minimize gradient differences between benign and adversarial examples
- The RF ensemble's non-differentiable property provides protection against gradient-based attacks but may not defend against black-box or transfer attacks
- The rectifier's effectiveness depends on specific attack characteristics and may vary significantly when attackers distribute perturbations across many pixels

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The ensemble architecture combining multiple interpretation methods is well-established and the detection pipeline is technically sound
- **Medium Confidence**: The gradient-correlation mechanism and RF robustness claims require more empirical validation against adaptive attacks
- **Medium Confidence**: The rectification mechanism's effectiveness depends on specific attack characteristics and may vary significantly across different threat models
