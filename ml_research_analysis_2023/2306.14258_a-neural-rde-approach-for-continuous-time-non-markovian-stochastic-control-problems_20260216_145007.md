# A Neural RDE approach for continuous-time non-Markovian stochastic control problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14258
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14258
- Reference count: 21
- This paper proposes a novel framework for solving continuous-time non-Markovian stochastic control problems using neural rough differential equations (Neural RDEs).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for solving continuous-time non-Markovian stochastic control problems using neural rough differential equations (Neural RDEs). The key innovation is parameterizing the control process as the solution of a Neural RDE driven by the state process, which allows for efficient Monte-Carlo estimation and memory-efficient backpropagation. The authors establish that Neural RDEs serve as universal approximators for functions of random rough paths, providing theoretical foundations for the approach. Extensive numerical experiments demonstrate superior performance compared to RNN-based methods across various non-Markovian control problems.

## Method Summary
The proposed method parameterizes the control process α_θ as the solution of a Neural RDE driven by the state process X. This formulation reformulates the control-state joint dynamics as an uncontrolled, augmented Neural RDE, enabling Monte-Carlo estimation of the value function through trajectory sampling. The method leverages rough path theory to establish universal approximation properties and demonstrates time-resolution invariance by operating continuously in time. The approach is trained using gradient descent with backpropagation through the Neural RDE dynamics.

## Key Results
- Neural RDEs serve as universal approximators for functions of random rough paths
- The framework achieves time-resolution invariance, allowing evaluation on different time grids without retraining
- Neural RDE models outperform LSTM, GRU, and RNN models in terms of final goal functional error and pathwise L2 error
- The method demonstrates better stability in irregular sampling compared to existing RNN-based approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Neural RDEs can model control processes whose solutions depend on entire historical trajectories of the state process.
- **Mechanism**: By parameterizing the control process as the solution of a Neural RDE driven by the state process, the control-state joint dynamics become governed by an uncontrolled Neural RDE. This formulation naturally captures path-dependent behavior without requiring explicit discretization of the control strategy.
- **Core assumption**: The control process can be expressed as the solution of a Neural RDE driven by the state process.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "By modelling the control process as the solution of a Neural RDE driven by the state process, we show that the control-state joint dynamics are governed by an uncontrolled, augmented Neural RDE"
  - [section 3.2]: "We parameterise controls α θ ∈A as solutions to Neural RDEs driven by X"
- **Break condition**: If the control process cannot be expressed as the solution of a Neural RDE, or if the joint dynamics cannot be reformulated as an uncontrolled RDE.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Neural RDEs provide time-resolution-invariant solutions for non-Markovian control problems.
- **Mechanism**: Neural RDEs operate continuously in time and only require the construction of an interpolated path from sampled data. This allows evaluation on different time grids without retraining, making the method robust to changes in time-resolution.
- **Core assumption**: The Neural RDE formulation is independent of the specific discretization method used.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "we demonstrate that the proposed framework is time-resolution-invariant"
  - [section 3.2]: "the sampled data enters the model only through the construction of the interpolated path, after which the RDE can be solved numerically on any desired grid"
- **Break condition**: If the control process exhibits strong dependence on the specific discretization grid used during training.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Neural RDEs serve as universal approximators for functions of random rough paths.
- **Mechanism**: The theoretical result shows that linear functionals on the signature of rough paths can approximate continuous functions on random rough paths in probability. This implies Neural RDEs can approximate optimal controls for non-Markovian problems.
- **Core assumption**: The optimal control depends continuously on the driven noise.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.4]: "Neural RDEs serve as universal approximators for functions of random rough paths"
  - [section 3.4]: "Linear functionals on the signature of rough paths... implies the universality of Neural RDEs"
- **Break condition**: If the optimal control does not depend continuously on the driven noise, or if the rough path theory assumptions are violated.
