# A Multi-Modal Neural Geometric Solver with Textual Clauses Parsed from Diagram

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11097
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11097
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: PGPSNet achieves 65.0% answer accuracy on Geometry3K, surpassing previous best results of 36.8% by Geoformer and 35.3% by NGS, and 62.7% accuracy on PGPS9K dataset

## Executive Summary
This work proposes PGPSNet, a multi-modal neural solver for geometry problem solving that converts diagrams into structured textual clauses to capture fine-grained geometric information. The model combines visual features from CNN encoders with structured text representations processed through a pre-trained language model and self-limited GRU decoder. PGPSNet achieves state-of-the-art performance on both existing Geometry3K and the new PGPS9K dataset, demonstrating that structured textual representation of diagrams significantly improves geometric reasoning capabilities.

## Method Summary
PGPSNet converts geometry diagrams into structured textual clauses that describe geometric primitives and their relations, then processes these through a multi-modal architecture combining CNN visual features with language model processing. The model employs structural and semantic pre-training using Masked Language Modeling on the textual clauses, applies data augmentation through five strategies including token replacement and diagram flipping, and uses self-limited decoding to constrain the search space. The solution program format uses 34 geometric theorem operators to generate interpretable step-by-step solutions.

## Key Results
- Achieves 65.0% answer accuracy on Geometry3K dataset, surpassing previous state-of-the-art of 36.8%
- Achieves 62.7% answer accuracy on PGPS9K dataset, outperforming InterGPS (59.8%) and NGS (34.1%)
- Demonstrates effectiveness of structured textual clause representation for geometric reasoning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Textual clauses capture fine-grained structural and semantic information more effectively than raw diagram pixels by converting geometric primitives and relations into structured text. This enables the language model to leverage its inherent syntactic parsing capabilities and attention mechanisms for relational reasoning. The approach assumes language models excel at processing structured text with clear syntax compared to unstructured visual features.

### Mechanism 2
Structural and semantic pre-training on textual clauses improves geometric understanding by forcing the model to learn geometric relationships and patterns through Masked Language Modeling. This builds geometric cognition before solving, based on the assumption that pre-training on task-specific structured text is more effective than general language pre-training for specialized reasoning tasks.

### Mechanism 3
Self-limited decoding constrains the search space and improves reasoning efficiency by limiting output candidates to variables appearing in the problem text and copying embeddings from encoder output. This reduces the combinatorial explosion of possible solution paths, assuming that constraining the search space preserves valid solutions while eliminating unlikely paths.

## Foundational Learning

- **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)**: Pre-training on structured geometric clauses teaches the model to understand relationships between geometric entities before attempting problem solving. Quick check: If the clause is "line A B C" and "⊙A lieson E B D", what should fill the mask in "BD⊥EA on [M]"?

- **Autoregressive sequence generation**: The solution program is generated step-by-step as a sequence of theorem applications, requiring the model to maintain state across multiple reasoning steps. Quick check: How does the model decide when to introduce a new process variable versus reuse an existing one?

- **Geometric theorem knowledge representation**: The solution program uses 34 operators corresponding to geometric theorems, requiring the model to learn which theorems apply in which contexts. Quick check: What geometric knowledge is required to correctly generate "Gougu N1 N5 N3 Get y" from the problem conditions?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Diagram → CNN → Visual tokens → GRU encoder → Fused representation → GRU decoder → Solution program → Program executor → Numerical answer
- **Critical path**: The model processes visual features through CNN, combines with structured textual clauses through GRU encoder, generates solution program through self-limited GRU decoder, and executes the program to produce numerical answers
- **Design tradeoffs**: Textual clauses vs. visual features (clauses are more structured but may lose spatial information), pre-training vs. direct fine-tuning (pre-training builds geometric knowledge but requires more computation), self-limited decoding vs. full search (faster but may miss creative solutions)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor pre-training leads to struggles with basic geometric relationships, insufficient data augmentation causes overfitting to training problem types, over-constrained decoding prevents generation of valid solutions requiring novel variables
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Ablation study removing structural clauses to measure their contribution to performance, 2) Comparison of different pre-training strategies (no pre-training, MLM, CLM) on PGPS9K, 3) Evaluation of data augmentation impact by varying augmentation probability from 0 to 1.0

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper identifies several open questions including how to improve the efficiency of parsing geometry diagrams into textual clauses, whether more advanced pre-training tasks beyond Masked Language Modeling could enhance geometric understanding, and how to extend the current solution program format to handle more complex geometric theorems and operations. These questions highlight areas where further research could improve geometric problem solving capabilities.

## Limitations

The core innovation of textual clause representation has limited empirical validation in isolation, making it unclear whether performance improvements come primarily from clause-based representation or the combined effect of multiple components. The self-limited decoding mechanism's effectiveness depends heavily on the assumption that all necessary variables will be present in the problem text, which may artificially limit solution space for complex geometry problems requiring auxiliary constructions.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The general architecture combining visual and textual modalities works effectively for geometry problem solving
- **Medium confidence**: The specific claim that textual clauses capture fine-grained structural information better than raw visual features
- **Low confidence**: The isolated contribution of self-limited decoding versus the overall model improvements

## Next Checks

1. **Clause representation ablation study**: Train a baseline model using only CNN-extracted visual features without textual clauses, keeping all other components constant to isolate the contribution of the textual clause mechanism.

2. **Variable constraint stress test**: Create a benchmark subset of geometry problems requiring auxiliary constructions that introduce variables not present in the original problem text, and compare PGPSNet performance on this subset versus a baseline without self-limited constraints.

3. **Clause complexity scaling analysis**: Systematically vary the granularity of textual clause representation and measure performance degradation to establish the minimum viable clause complexity for effective geometric reasoning.
