# $β$-DARTS++: Bi-level Regularization for Proxy-robust Differentiable Architecture Search

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.06393
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06393
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** β-DARTS introduces Beta-Decay regularization to improve stability and generalization in DARTS, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks while maintaining robustness under reduced proxy conditions.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses three critical challenges in differentiable neural architecture search (DARTS): stability, generalization, and proxy robustness. The authors propose β-DARTS, which introduces Beta-Decay regularization to constrain activated architecture parameters, preventing skip connections from dominating and improving both stability and generalization. The method also incorporates weight flooding regularization to enhance proxy robustness. Extensive experiments demonstrate that β-DARTS achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple search spaces and datasets, with the searched architectures generalizing well across datasets and maintaining robustness under various proxy settings.

## Method Summary
β-DARTS introduces two key regularization techniques to DARTS: Beta-Decay regularization and weight flooding. Beta-Decay applies a softmax-normalized penalty on activated architecture parameters (β) to constrain their magnitude and variance, ensuring fair competition among operations and minimizing Lipschitz constraints for better generalization. Weight flooding regularization prevents the model from learning until zero loss by alternating between gradient descent and ascent once a flood level is reached, pushing weights to more robust areas with smoother parameter landscapes. These techniques are integrated into the standard DARTS bi-level optimization framework without requiring significant architectural changes.

## Key Results
- β-DARTS achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple search spaces including NAS-Bench-201, DARTS, MobileNet, and NAS-Bench-1Shot1
- Searched architectures demonstrate excellent generalization across datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet16-120, ImageNet-1K)
- β-DARTS maintains robust performance under various proxy reduction settings (data, channels, layers, epochs)
- The search process converges efficiently, reaching optimal points early in training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Beta-Decay regularization constrains activated architecture parameters to prevent domination by parameter-free operations like skip connections. The regularization applies a softmax-normalized penalty on the activated parameters β after softmax, reducing the variance and forcing them closer to their mean. This ensures fair competition among operations and prevents skip connections from dominating. Core assumption: The softmax-activated parameters β directly reflect operation importance, and constraining their variance improves stability and generalization. Break condition: If the assumption that softmax-activated parameters accurately represent operation importance is invalid, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
Beta-Decay regularization minimizes Lipschitz constraints, improving generalization ability of the searched architecture. By constraining the magnitude of β parameters, the method reduces the sensitivity of the supernet to input perturbations, effectively minimizing the Lipschitz constant. This leads to better generalization across datasets. Core assumption: Smaller Lipschitz constants correlate with better generalization in neural networks. Break condition: If the relationship between Lipschitz constants and generalization is not valid for architecture parameters, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 3
Weight flooding regularization improves proxy robustness by preventing the model from learning until zero loss. Once the training loss reaches a flood level, the optimization alternates between gradient descent and ascent, pushing weights to a more robust area with a smoother parameter landscape. This makes the search robust to various proxy settings. Core assumption: Learning until zero loss causes model overconfidence and test performance degradation, and preventing this improves robustness. Break condition: If the flood level is set incorrectly (too high or too low), the mechanism may not improve robustness or may degrade performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does Beta-Decay regularization compare to other architecture regularization methods like FairDARTS or DARTS+ in terms of stability and generalization? Basis in paper: The paper mentions that previous works like FairDARTS [21] and DARTS+ [20] address stability issues but use different approaches (limiting skip connections, changing search process). The paper claims Beta-Decay regularization is more generic and doesn't require extra changes. Why unresolved: The paper only compares Beta-Decay regularization to L2/weight decay regularization and doesn't provide direct comparisons with other state-of-the-art regularization methods in the literature. What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive ablation study comparing Beta-Decay regularization to other architecture regularization methods on multiple benchmarks and search spaces.

### Open Question 2
What is the theoretical relationship between the Lipschitz constant (C(w)) minimized by Beta-Decay regularization and the actual generalization gap observed in practice? Basis in paper: The paper provides theoretical analysis showing that Beta-Decay regularization minimizes the Lipschitz constant, which is related to generalization. However, it doesn't establish a direct quantitative relationship between the Lipschitz constant and generalization gap. Why unresolved: While the theoretical analysis suggests a connection, the paper doesn't empirically validate how changes in the Lipschitz constant translate to improvements in generalization performance. What evidence would resolve it: Experiments measuring the Lipschitz constant of searched architectures and correlating it with their generalization performance across multiple datasets.

### Open Question 3
How does the effectiveness of Beta-Decay regularization vary across different search spaces and datasets? Basis in paper: The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of Beta-Decay regularization on NAS-Bench-201, DARTS, MobileNet, and NAS-Bench-1Shot1 search spaces, and CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet16-120, and ImageNet-1K datasets. However, it doesn't provide a systematic analysis of how its performance varies across these different settings. Why unresolved: The paper shows that Beta-Decay regularization works well across different search spaces and datasets, but doesn't investigate whether its effectiveness is consistent or varies depending on the specific characteristics of the search space or dataset. What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of Beta-Decay regularization's performance across a wider range of search spaces and datasets, potentially revealing patterns or insights about its effectiveness in different scenarios.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis connecting Beta-Decay to Lipschitz constraints is primarily conceptual rather than rigorously proven
- Flooding regularization lacks extensive ablation studies to determine optimal flood level settings across different datasets and search spaces
- Proxy robustness benchmarks only evaluate final architecture performance rather than search process stability under varying proxy conditions

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Beta-Decay regularization effectively prevents skip connection domination and improves stability in DARTS
- **Medium confidence**: The theoretical connection between Beta-Decay and Lipschitz constraints improving generalization is plausible but not fully proven
- **Medium confidence**: Weight flooding regularization improves proxy robustness, though optimal parameter settings require further study
- **High confidence**: The empirical results showing state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks are robust
