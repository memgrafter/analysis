# Planning for Attacker Entrapment in Adversarial Settings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00822
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00822
- Reference count: 8
- A covert defender manipulates environment to guide attacker into trap states while concealing presence

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the attacker entrapment problem, where a covert defender manipulates the environment to guide an attacker into trap states while concealing its presence. The authors formulate this as an infinite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the defender chooses between "no-op" actions and outcome-selection actions to lead the attacker toward traps or lower-value states. A key contribution is a method to compute a "budget" K, representing the maximum number of defender actions possible before the attacker suspects manipulation. Experiments on grid-world and related domains show that the defender's optimal policy significantly reduces the attacker's expected reward in most cases, with planning times remaining reasonable for small-to-medium problems.

## Method Summary
The defender's problem is encoded as an infinite-horizon discounted MDP with states representing the attacker's state, action, and remaining budget. The defender chooses between "noop" actions (allowing environment to proceed normally) and outcome-selection actions (forcing transitions toward traps or low-value states). Budget K is computed as the shortest trace more likely under a high-entropy alternative model than the attacker's belief. The defender's MDP is solved using value iteration to obtain the optimal entrapment strategy. The approach guarantees the attacker's value is never increased and is often significantly reduced.

## Key Results
- Defender's optimal policy significantly reduces attacker's expected reward in most cases
- Budget K successfully bounds defender actions to avoid detection
- Planning times remain reasonable for small-to-medium grid-world domains
- Attacker's value is guaranteed never to increase under optimal defender policy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The defender can guide the attacker to trap states without detection by limiting its intervention to K steps.
- **Mechanism**: The defender constructs an MDP where states include the current state, attacker's action, and remaining budget. The defender chooses "noop" actions or outcome-selection actions to minimize attacker's cumulative reward. Budget K is computed as the shortest trace that is more likely under a high-entropy alternative model than the attacker's belief.
- **Core assumption**: The attacker maintains uncertainty about the environment model and updates beliefs using Bayesian reasoning with two hypotheses (correct model vs. high-entropy alternative).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "the defender is constrained to achieve its goal within K number of steps, where K is calculated as a pessimistic lower bound within which the attacker is unlikely to suspect a threat"
  - [section]: "we will limit the defender's actions to the first K steps... the budget represents in the worst case the minimal number of transitions that can occur from the initial state wherein the attacker's beliefs may tip over to M0"
  - [corpus]: Weak correlation with adversarial planning and MDP formulations; specific entrapment mechanism not covered in neighbors
- **Break condition**: If the attacker's belief update mechanism differs from the assumed Bayesian model, or if the attacker detects environmental manipulation beyond the K-step budget.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The defender's MDP formulation guarantees the attacker's value is never increased and is often significantly reduced.
- **Mechanism**: The defender's MDP has states (s,a,k) where s is attacker's state, a is attacker's action, k is remaining budget. Rewards are negative of attacker's rewards, and absorbing states have rewards based on attacker's optimal Q-value. This creates incentives to reach trap states or low-value states for the attacker.
- **Core assumption**: The defender knows the attacker's model and can accurately predict the attacker's action distribution using a noisy-rational model.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "the optimal policy for the MDP gives the defender's strategy against the actions of the attacker"
  - [section]: "By allowing the reward value to be equal to the negative of the optimal Q value of the agent, we allow for the fact that once the defender stops acting in the world, the attacker is free to achieve the maximum possible value"
  - [corpus]: Some neighbors discuss MDP-based planning and adversarial strategies, but specific formulation with budget constraint not found
- **Break condition**: If the attacker's actual action distribution significantly deviates from the noisy-rational model, or if the defender's model of the attacker's MDP is inaccurate.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The budget K can be efficiently computed using uniform-cost search on traces.
- **Mechanism**: Search starts from initial state, expanding prefixes of increasing length until finding a trace that is more likely under the high-entropy alternative model M0 than under the attacker's model MA. The search stops when such a trace is found or when a predefined step limit is reached.
- **Core assumption**: There exists a finite trace length within which the attacker's beliefs might tip, and this can be bounded by the defender's MDP solution without budget restrictions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section]: "we can stop the search if we know the K is going to be above some prespecified limit... we can solve the defender MDP without budget restrictions... and look at the optimal policy"
  - [section]: "A simple way to do this is to turn it into a uniform-cost search problem"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; neighbors discuss search and MDP planning but not specific budget computation method
- **Break condition**: If the attacker's belief update is not captured by the two-hypothesis model, or if the uniform-cost search becomes intractable for large state spaces.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: MDP models MA (attacker) and MD (defender) -> Budget computation via uniform-cost search -> Value iteration on defender MDP -> Defender policy extraction

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Parse attacker's MDP specification
  2. Compute budget K using uniform-cost search
  3. Construct defender's MDP with budget dimension
  4. Solve defender's MDP using value iteration
  5. Extract defender's policy from optimal value function

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - State space explosion: Defender's MDP state space grows as |SA| × |AA| × (K+1), which can become intractable for large problems
  - Budget computation cost: Uniform-cost search may be expensive, but can be bounded by solving the unconstrained defender MDP
  - Model accuracy: Solution quality depends on accurate attacker model and prediction of action distribution

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Planning timeouts: May indicate state space too large or budget too high
  - Zero budget: May indicate attacker's model is already inconsistent with high-entropy alternative
  - No improvement in attacker value: May indicate poor attacker model prediction or insufficient budget

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement and test budget computation on small grid world domains with known optimal solutions
  2. Verify defender MDP construction by checking value bounds (Proposition 1) on simple domains
  3. Run complete system on 4×4 grid world with varying trap positions and slip probabilities to validate effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How effective are online and approximate planners compared to the value iteration method used in the paper for generating defender strategies in attacker entrapment problems?
2. How can the defender's strategy be adapted to handle evolving attacker beliefs, especially if the attacker becomes aware of the defender's presence?
3. Can cognitive biases be effectively represented and exploited by the defender to deceive human attackers?

## Limitations
- Assumes attacker updates beliefs using specific two-hypothesis Bayesian model
- Computational complexity grows exponentially with budget K and state space size
- Solution quality depends on accurate modeling of attacker's noisy-rational action selection

## Confidence
- High: Theoretical MDP formulation and Proposition 1 (budget ensures attacker never benefits)
- Medium: Budget computation method and practical effectiveness across domains
- Medium: Assumptions about attacker belief updating and action selection models
