# Testing Causal Models of Word Meaning in GPT-3 and -4

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14630
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14630
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GPT-4 encodes causal structure of artifact representations matching human performance on HIPE tasks, while GPT-3 does not despite competent artifact term usage

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates whether large language models encode human-like causal representations of word meanings using the HIPE theory. The authors test GPT-3 and GPT-4 on human-validated stimuli concerning judgments about causal outcomes, object function, and object naming for artifacts like mops, pencils, and whistles. Results show that GPT-4's responses closely match human data, exhibiting the predicted causal structure (e.g., screening off of distal factors). In contrast, GPT-3 fails to encode this structure across multiple prompt variations, despite appearing to use artifact-related terms competently. The findings highlight the need for rigorous evaluation of LLMs using theory-driven psycholinguistic tests, as positive results on behavioral tests alone may not reliably indicate human-like processing.

## Method Summary
The study tests GPT-3 and GPT-4 using scenarios adapted from Chaigneau et al. (2004) involving artifacts (mop, pencil, whistle) with varied object function, history, structure, and user goal. The experimental design manipulates different factors of artifact scenarios and measures their effects on naming, function, and causal outcome judgments. GPT-3 and GPT-4 are prompted with these scenarios using different formats (Likert scale, Yes/No) and system messages, then their responses are compared against human data using Spearman correlation.

## Key Results
- GPT-4's responses closely match human data patterns and exhibit the predicted causal structure with screening-off effects
- GPT-3 fails to encode the causal structure hypothesized by HIPE across multiple experimental variations
- The experimental design successfully probes for causal representations by manipulating different artifact factors and measuring their effects

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-4 successfully encodes causal structure of artifact representations that match human performance in HIPE tasks.
- Mechanism: GPT-4's representations allow it to distinguish between proximal and distal causes of artifact function, correctly predicting that structure and action are more critical than history and goal when evaluating object naming, function, and causal outcomes.
- Core assumption: GPT-4 has developed internal representations that capture human-like causal reasoning about physical artifacts through its training on text data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find no evidence that GPT-3 encodes the causal structure hypothesized by HIPE, but do find evidence that GPT-4 encodes such structure."
  - [section 5.1] "The results from GPT-4 bear a striking resemblance to the human data... In the causality and function heatmaps, we see a green vertical on the right and a red horizontal on the bottom, lightening towards the top right and bottom left respectively."
- Break condition: If GPT-4 fails to show the expected screening-off pattern across multiple object types and question types, or if results are inconsistent with human data patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-3 fails to encode human-like causal structure despite appearing competent at artifact-related language tasks.
- Mechanism: GPT-3 can generate coherent text about artifacts but lacks the deeper causal representations needed to correctly weight different factors (history, structure, goal, action) when making judgments about artifacts.
- Core assumption: GPT-3's representations are based on surface-level associations rather than grounded causal models of physical objects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find no evidence that GPT-3 encodes the causal structure hypothesized by HIPE"
  - [section 5.1] "The results from GPT-3 are much less consistent with the predictions based on the CM and with the human data... GPT-3's responses only agree with the CM predictions in 3 out of 6 cases."
- Break condition: If GPT-3 shows consistent patterns matching HIPE predictions across multiple experimental variations, or if it demonstrates similar causal reasoning to GPT-4.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The experimental design successfully probes for causal representations by manipulating different factors of artifact scenarios and measuring their effects.
- Mechanism: By creating scenarios where one factor (history, structure, goal, or action) is compromised while others remain baseline, and measuring the effect on naming, function, and causal outcome judgments, the experiment tests whether models encode the predicted causal structure.
- Core assumption: The HIPE theory's predictions about which factors should have stronger/weaker effects on judgments are valid and can be tested through this experimental paradigm.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "The CM predicts that history should have a less significant effect on outcome judgements than structure, and that goal should have a less significant effect than action."
  - [section 5.1] "The CM predicts that compromising any factor should have a negative effect on outcome judgements relative to baseline, but with distal factors yielding a smaller negative effect than proximal factors."
- Break condition: If the experimental manipulation fails to produce the predicted patterns in human data, or if alternative explanations can account for the results.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Scenario generation -> Prompt construction -> Model API calls (GPT-3/GPT-4) -> Response collection -> Probability extraction/Statistical analysis -> Comparison with human data
- Critical path: Generate scenarios → Construct prompts with proper context → Collect model responses → Extract probabilities or binary judgments → Compare against HIPE predictions and human data → Analyze correlations
- Design tradeoffs: Using Yes/No responses vs. Likert scales (simpler but less nuanced vs. more detailed but potentially harder for models), in-context learning vs. standalone prompts (better context but more complex), direct probability access vs. repeated sampling (more accurate but resource-intensive vs. less accurate but feasible)
- Failure signatures: GPT-3 showing inconsistent patterns across experimental variations, GPT-4 failing to show expected screening-off patterns, poor correlation with human data, or models failing to use response scales appropriately

  2. Run the same experiment on GPT-3 to confirm negative results and test robustness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What additional psycholinguistic tests are needed to diagnose whether LLMs have truly human-like conceptual representations?
- Basis in paper: The authors explicitly raise this question, noting that success on behavioral tests alone is not sufficient evidence of human-like processing and asking what additional testing is required.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that while GPT-4 matches human performance on HIPE tests, this doesn't necessarily indicate human-like causal reasoning, as GPT-3 performs poorly despite competent artifact term usage. This suggests current tests may not be sufficiently diagnostic.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of new psycholinguistic tests that can distinguish between surface-level pattern matching and genuine causal reasoning about concepts, perhaps by introducing novel artifacts or impossible scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Do LLMs possess genuine causal models of concepts, or are they performing sophisticated pattern matching?
- Basis in paper: The authors note that HIPE theory assumes concepts are embedded in causal models grounded in the physical world, but GPT-4's success on these tests might indicate that such grounding is not necessary for the behavior.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows GPT-4 matches human performance on HIPE tests but cannot determine whether this reflects genuine causal understanding or sophisticated pattern matching from text alone.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with novel artifact concepts or impossible scenarios that would require genuine causal reasoning rather than pattern matching, or ablation studies showing what happens when key associative patterns are disrupted.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What explains the qualitative difference in performance between GPT-3 and GPT-4 on HIPE tests?
- Basis in paper: The authors note a "marked difference" between GPT-3 and GPT-4, with GPT-3 failing to encode the causal structure despite competent artifact term usage, while GPT-4 performs well.
- Why unresolved: The paper documents this difference but does not investigate its source - whether it's due to scale, training data differences, architectural changes, or other factors.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of model components, training procedures, and performance on related tasks to isolate what enables GPT-4's success, or experiments varying model scale and architecture while controlling other factors.

## Limitations

- The study focuses on only three artifact types (mop, pencil, whistle), limiting generalizability to other domains
- GPT-3's failure to encode causal structure may reflect different problem-solving strategies rather than absence of understanding
- The interpretation that GPT-4's success indicates human-like causal representations is not definitively proven and could reflect sophisticated pattern matching

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The comparative results between GPT-3 and GPT-4 are robust and well-supported by multiple experimental variations
- **Medium confidence**: The interpretation that GPT-4's success indicates human-like causal representations is reasonable but not definitively proven
- **Low confidence**: Generalizing these results to broader claims about LLM capabilities or the relationship between behavioral success and conceptual understanding requires caution

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-domain generalization test**: Replicate the experiment with non-artifact domains (natural kinds like animals, or abstract concepts) to determine whether GPT-4's success is specific to artifacts or reflects broader causal reasoning capabilities

2. **Intervention analysis**: Systematically manipulate specific factors in GPT-4's responses (e.g., by fine-tuning or prompt engineering) to determine whether its causal representations can be selectively disrupted, providing stronger evidence for genuine causal understanding rather than surface-level pattern matching

3. **Latent space investigation**: Use interpretability tools to examine whether GPT-4's internal representations exhibit the predicted causal structure (e.g., through causal mediation analysis or intervention testing in the model's latent space), providing direct evidence for the existence of the hypothesized causal representations
