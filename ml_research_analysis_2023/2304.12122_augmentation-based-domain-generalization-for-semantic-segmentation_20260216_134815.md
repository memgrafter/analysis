# Augmentation-based Domain Generalization for Semantic Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12122
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12122
- Reference count: 40
- This paper systematically evaluates image augmentations for improving domain generalization in semantic segmentation

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of image augmentations for domain generalization in semantic segmentation tasks. The authors employ a full factorial design of experiments to systematically evaluate 5 augmentations (Elastic Transform, Gaussian Blur, CLAHE, Random Rain, Random Resized Crop) and their combinations. They demonstrate that random cropping is essential for both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization, and that combining augmentations can significantly improve performance. The study achieves competitive results with state-of-the-art methods, showing that simple augmentations can be effective for domain generalization.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct experiments using DeeplabV2 with ResNet-101 on Synthia dataset (source) and evaluate on Cityscapes and BDD (targets). They employ a two-step approach: first evaluating individual augmentations combined with random cropping, then using full factorial design to test all combinations of the top 5 augmentations. The study uses Albumentations library with default parameters and analyzes results using quadratic regression to understand main effects and interactions. Learning rate sensitivity analysis reveals strong dependency of target domain performance on this hyperparameter.

## Key Results
- Random cropping is essential for both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization
- Combinations of augmentations (e.g., elastic transform + random resized cropping) provide significant improvements over single augmentations
- Interactions between augmentations are mostly negative, reducing overall performance gains
- The best augmentation settings achieve 39.5% mIoU on Cityscapes, competitive with state-of-the-art (40.9% for DAFormer)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Random cropping improves both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization.
- Mechanism: By randomly cropping images, the model is forced to learn features that are invariant to spatial context, reducing overfitting to specific scene layouts and object arrangements in the source domain.
- Core assumption: The semantic content of objects remains recognizable even when cropped to smaller patches.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Random cropping is essential for both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization"
  - [section]: "We consider resizing as the baseline where no augmentation are applied. Therefore, we select random crops as the basis augmentation which will be applied in all further experiments"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence on random cropping's effect.
- Break condition: If objects in the dataset are often too small or fragmented by cropping, the model may fail to learn meaningful features.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Combinations of augmentations generally outperform single augmentations.
- Mechanism: Combining multiple augmentations introduces diverse perturbations that the model must learn to handle, leading to more robust feature representations.
- Core assumption: Each augmentation introduces a different type of domain shift, and their combination covers a wider range of possible shifts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Expected, because our experiments confirm the common scientific standard that combination of multiple different augmentations out-performs single augmentations"
  - [section]: "We observe that the combination of the best performing augmentations realizes significant improvements for both in- and out-of-domain generalization"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence on augmentation combinations.
- Break condition: If augmentations interfere with each other (e.g., one augmentation destroys information needed by another), the combined effect may be worse than individual effects.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Augmentations work differently for in-domain and out-of-domain generalization.
- Mechanism: Augmentations that improve in-domain generalization may not translate to out-of-domain generalization if they don't address the specific types of domain shifts present between source and target domains.
- Core assumption: The types of domain shifts affecting in-domain performance differ from those affecting out-of-domain performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Augmentations work differently for in-domain and out-of-domain generalization"
  - [section]: "Overall, it becomes clear that the augmentations work dissimilar for in- and out-of-domain generalization"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence on different effects for in-domain vs out-of-domain.
- Break condition: If the source and target domains are very similar, augmentations may improve both types of generalization equally.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - DeeplabV2 with ResNet-101 backbone -> Albumentations library -> Synthia/Cityscapes/BDD datasets

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained ImageNet weights
  2. Apply random cropping (baseline)
  3. Apply additional augmentations based on experimental design
  4. Train for specified epochs
  5. Evaluate on validation sets

- Design tradeoffs:
  - ResNet-101 is more established but potentially less efficient than newer architectures like DAFormer
  - Random cropping is computationally efficient but may lose context for small objects
  - Using all augmentations simultaneously may lead to diminishing returns due to negative interactions

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor out-of-domain performance despite good in-domain performance suggests augmentations aren't addressing target domain shifts
  - Very low performance on both domains suggests augmentations are too aggressive or damaging
  - High variance in results suggests instability in augmentation application

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: Random crop only (no other augmentations)
  2. Single augmentation: Gaussian blur + random crop
  3. Two-augmentation combination: Elastic transform + random resized crop

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal combination of augmentations for maximizing both in-domain and out-of-domain generalization simultaneously?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors found that augmentations work differently for in-domain and out-of-domain generalization, and that the best combination for one may not be optimal for the other.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that there is a trade-off between in-domain and out-of-domain performance, but does not provide a clear solution for balancing these objectives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of a multi-objective optimization framework that can find the Pareto-optimal set of augmentations balancing in-domain and out-of-domain performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of learning rate interact with augmentation strategies to affect domain generalization performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observed a strong dependency of target domain performance on the learning rate, suggesting a complex interaction with augmentations.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies this dependency, it does not systematically investigate how different learning rates interact with different augmentation strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments varying both learning rates and augmentation strategies to map out the interaction landscape and identify optimal combinations.

## Limitations

- Findings are based on experiments with a single source-target domain pair (Synthia → Cityscapes/BDD), limiting generalizability
- Use of Albumentations' default parameters introduces potential variability as optimal parameters may differ across datasets
- Strong dependency of target domain performance on learning rate suggests results may be sensitive to hyperparameter choices
