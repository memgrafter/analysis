# Considering Layerwise Importance in the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11244
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11244
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Different layerwise importance metrics produce distinct performant lottery tickets with little overlapping connections, showing lottery tickets are not unique

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether considering layerwise importance in the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) procedure affects the resulting sparse networks. The authors propose using different importance metrics (L1, L2, Softmax, Min-Max) to calculate weight importance values within each layer, rather than using global weight magnitudes. Experiments on various network architectures and datasets demonstrate that different importance metrics lead to distinct performant lottery tickets with minimal connection overlap. The results suggest that lottery tickets are not unique and that layerwise importance can provide additional context for pruning decisions.

## Method Summary
The paper modifies the standard Lottery Ticket Hypothesis procedure by replacing global magnitude pruning with layerwise importance metrics. For each pruning iteration, connections within each layer are scored using different metrics (L1, L2, Softmax, Min-Max normalization), then the lowest-scoring connections are pruned. The remaining weights are rewound to an early training iteration, and the process repeats. This approach aims to provide better context for pruning decisions by comparing connections within their layer distribution rather than globally across the entire network.

## Key Results
- Different importance metrics (L1, L2, Softmax, Min-Max) produce distinct lottery tickets with minimal overlapping connections
- Lottery tickets found with different metrics show similar performance despite structural differences
- Some connections demonstrate stability across different tickets, surviving multiple pruning strategies and showing lower variance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Layerwise importance metrics provide a more calibrated pruning criterion by accounting for weight distributions within layers.
- Mechanism: Instead of comparing raw weight magnitudes globally, the proposed methods rescale or normalize weights within each layer, ensuring connections are compared relative to their layer's distribution rather than across the entire network.
- Core assumption: Weight distributions vary significantly between layers due to different layer sizes and initialization schemes, making global comparisons suboptimal.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "This global comparison removes context information between connections within a layer." [section 2] "Taking these issues into account, we propose a modification of the LTR procedure where the importance of connection within a given layer is represented by an importance value assigned to it."
- Break condition: If weight distributions within layers are similar across the network, or if global magnitude pruning already captures the most important connections regardless of layer context.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multiple distinct lottery tickets can be extracted from the same initialization using different importance metrics.
- Mechanism: The LTR procedure becomes deterministic when using fixed seeds, meaning the only variable affecting which connections are pruned is the choice of importance metric. Different metrics prioritize different connections, leading to structurally different but equally performant sparse networks.
- Core assumption: The training process is repeatable and deterministic given fixed seeds, and the only source of variation between runs is the importance metric.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We find that given a repeatable training procedure, applying different importance metrics leads to distinct performant lottery tickets with little overlapping connections." [section 5] "We consider the LTs found by the following importance measures: magnitude, L1 and L2... We find that the resulting tickets share little amount of connections."
- Break condition: If the training process introduces sufficient stochasticity that the importance metric becomes a secondary factor, or if certain connections are universally critical regardless of metric.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Some connections are more "stable" across different lottery tickets and survive reinitialization.
- Mechanism: Connections that appear in multiple tickets generated with different importance metrics tend to have lower variance in their weight evolution and fewer sign flips during training. These stable connections can guide the search for lottery tickets more efficiently.
- Core assumption: Connections that consistently appear across different pruning strategies have intrinsic importance to the network's function.
- Evidence anchors: [section 5.1] "We find that on average, for three random runs, the amount of remaining connections is higher than the expected value, assuming that each connection has the same chance to be in the ticket." [section 5] "Looking at the overlapping connections in the first convolutional layer... we can notice that the overlapping connections on average have a larger difference between the initial and final weight value."
- Break condition: If the apparent stability is due to architectural constraints rather than intrinsic importance, or if reinitialization destroys the pattern.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Initialize model with Kaiming Normal -> Pre-train for k iterations -> Iteratively prune lowest-scoring connections using layerwise importance -> Rewind to iteration k -> Evaluate sparse network

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize model with Kaiming Normal
  2. Pre-train for k iterations (rewind point)
  3. For each pruning iteration:
     - Train for m-k iterations
     - Calculate layerwise importance scores
     - Prune lowest-scoring connections
     - Rewind to iteration k
  4. Evaluate resulting sparse network

- Design tradeoffs:
  - L1/L2 vs. Softmax vs. Min-Max: L1/L2 preserve more weight information but can be sensitive to scale; Softmax normalizes but may collapse distributions; Min-Max ensures coverage but can be too aggressive.
  - Layerwise vs. global: Layerwise provides context but adds complexity; global is simpler but may miss layer-specific patterns.

- Failure signatures:
  - Layer-collapse: Some layers become completely pruned, breaking information flow
  - Accuracy degradation: Sparse networks perform significantly worse than dense counterparts
  - Inconsistent results: Different runs produce very different tickets without clear pattern

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reproduce baseline LTR results on ResNet-18 + TinyImageNet to establish performance floor
  2. Implement and test L1 normalization on same setup to verify improved layer balance
  3. Run multiple trials with different seeds to measure overlap and stability of connections

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: Are there fundamental structural differences between lottery tickets found using different importance metrics that could be exploited to improve pruning efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that different importance metrics lead to distinct performant lottery tickets with little overlapping connections, and that some layers have noticeably larger fractions of overlapping connections across tickets.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies these differences, it doesn't investigate whether these structural differences could be leveraged to develop more efficient pruning strategies or to understand the fundamental mechanisms behind lottery ticket formation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the effectiveness of different importance metrics in various pruning scenarios, and analysis of whether the structural differences between tickets lead to different generalization properties or training dynamics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the overlapping connections between lottery tickets found with different importance metrics contribute to the network's performance, and can they be used to accelerate the search for lottery tickets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies overlapping connections between tickets found with different importance metrics and observes that these connections have lower variance and survive partial reinitialization tests.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't fully explore the functional significance of these overlapping connections or investigate whether they could be used as a starting point for finding lottery tickets more efficiently.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing that initializing with these overlapping connections leads to faster convergence or better performance in finding lottery tickets, and analysis of the specific roles these connections play in the network's functionality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of importance metric affect the distribution of weights in the final lottery ticket, and what implications does this have for the network's ability to generalize?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses different importance metrics and their effects on pruning decisions, but doesn't thoroughly investigate how these choices impact the final weight distribution or generalization performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper compares the performance of tickets found with different metrics, it doesn't delve into the deeper implications of how these choices affect the network's internal representations and ability to generalize to new data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of the weight distributions in tickets found with different metrics, and experiments comparing their generalization performance on unseen data or under distribution shifts.

## Limitations
- Computational overhead: Layerwise normalization adds complexity and potential computational cost compared to simple global magnitude pruning
- Sensitivity to hyperparameters: Results may depend heavily on specific pruning ratios and training durations
- Limited exploration of failure cases: The paper doesn't thoroughly investigate scenarios where layerwise methods might underperform global magnitude pruning

## Confidence

The core claim that layerwise importance metrics produce distinct but performant lottery tickets has Medium confidence based on experimental evidence across multiple architectures and datasets. The assertion that lottery tickets are not unique is supported by quantitative overlap analysis, though the practical significance of different ticket structures remains unclear. The identification of "stable" connections across tickets represents a Medium confidence extrapolation, as the stability analysis relies on limited sampling and doesn't establish causal importance.
