# Interactive-Chain-Prompting: Ambiguity Resolution for Crosslingual Conditional Generation with Interaction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10309
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10309
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: INTER CP T outperforms strong baselines on crosslingual translation ambiguity resolution using question-answer interaction without requiring full context access

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Interactive-Chain-Prompting (INTER CP T), a novel method for resolving ambiguities in crosslingual conditional generation tasks such as machine translation. INTER CP T breaks down the translation task into a sequence of subproblems—detecting ambiguities, resolving them via question-answer interactions with a simulated user, and generating the final translation. To evaluate the method, the authors created AMBIG MT, a new dataset covering five types of ambiguities (polysemy, formality, "it" resolution, gender-neutral names, and neutral professions) across four language pairs. Experiments with PaLM models show that INTER CP T consistently outperforms strong baselines, including methods with direct access to context, achieving significant improvements in BLEU, BLEURT, and ambiguity resolution accuracy. The results demonstrate that INTER CP T is an emergent ability at larger model scales and can generalize to unseen ambiguities, while also mitigating gender and formality biases in generated translations.

## Method Summary
The Interactive-Chain-Prompting method resolves translation ambiguities through a three-step reasoning chain: first detecting ambiguities in the source text, then resolving them via question-answer interaction between a Translator model and a User model, and finally generating the final translation. The approach uses few-shot prompting with exemplars and does not require fine-tuning. The Translator model detects ambiguities and asks clarifying questions, while the User model provides context-based answers. The method is evaluated on the AMBIG MT dataset covering five ambiguity types across four language pairs using PaLM models of varying sizes.

## Key Results
- INTER CP T consistently outperforms baselines (PaLM-with-context and PaLM-no-extras) across all five ambiguity types and four language pairs
- The method shows emergent properties at larger model scales, with scaling curves flattening for baselines but continuing to improve for INTER CP T
- INTER CP T achieves significant improvements in BLEU (up to 5.8 points), BLEURT (up to 5.6 points), and task-specific metrics like gender accuracy (6-10 points improvement)
- The approach successfully generalizes to unseen ambiguities and mitigates gender and formality biases in generated translations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Breaking down translation into ambiguity detection, question-answering, and generation steps improves accuracy.
- Mechanism: The method decomposes the main task into subproblems that are easier for the model to handle sequentially, reducing cognitive load.
- Core assumption: Sequential resolution of subproblems leads to better final output than direct generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Interactive-Chain-Prompting (INTER CPT) breaks down the translation task into a sequence of subproblems—detecting ambiguities, resolving them via question-answer interactions with a simulated user, and generating the final translation."
  - [section]: "INTER CPT departs from other prompt-based techniques that sequentially solve subproblems in two fundamental ways: (1) the subproblems are related but considerably different to the main task and (2) the solutions to subproblems requires interaction with another LLM."
- Break condition: If subproblems become too complex or the interaction model fails to provide useful answers, the sequential approach may degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Interaction with a simulated user allows disambiguation without requiring full context.
- Mechanism: The translator model asks clarifying questions, and the user model provides answers based on additional context, mimicking real-world clarification.
- Core assumption: Questions generated by the translator model can effectively narrow down the ambiguity space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "INTER CPT breaks down the translation task into a sequence of subproblems—detecting ambiguities, resolving them via question-answer interactions with a simulated user, and generating the final translation."
  - [section]: "We argue that a type of question-answer interaction with a 'user' is necessary to resolve ambiguous queries, especially when a user (1) is unfamiliar with the main task and may not possess the skills to choose from many model prediction options; (2) knows how to answer simple pointed questions about a query but may not be able or willing to decide and add appropriate context on the fly."
- Break condition: If the simulated user provides incorrect or irrelevant answers, the disambiguation process fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Larger model scales enable emergent ambiguity resolution capabilities.
- Mechanism: As model parameters increase, the ability to ask relevant questions and incorporate answers improves, leading to better translations.
- Core assumption: Scale directly correlates with the model's ability to handle complex reasoning tasks like ambiguity resolution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The results demonstrate that INTER CPT is an emergent ability at larger model scales and can generalize to unseen ambiguities."
  - [section]: "At this stage, baselines scaling trend decelerates, with scaling curves flattening, compared to INTER CPT. It shows that INTER CPT is an emergent ability of model scale (Wei et al., 2022a)."
- Break condition: If scale does not continue to improve question quality or answer incorporation, the emergent property may plateau.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Translator Model -> User Model -> Final Translation Generator

- Critical path:
  1. Detect ambiguity in source text
  2. Generate clarifying question
  3. Provide context to user model
  4. Generate answer from user model
  5. Incorporate answer into translation prompt
  6. Generate final translation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of exemplars vs. model performance
  - Specialist vs. generalist prompt templates
  - Translator model size vs. user model size
  - Direct context access vs. interactive clarification

- Failure signatures:
  - Translator asks irrelevant questions
  - User model provides incorrect answers
  - Final translation ignores provided context
  - Performance plateaus despite scale increases

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test INTER CPT with 8-shot generalist template on formality and "it" resolution datasets
  2. Compare specialist vs. generalist prompt performance on polysemy dataset
  3. Evaluate scale effects by running INTER CPT with different model sizes on all ambiguity types

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the quality of the simulated User LM's answers impact the overall translation performance of INTER CPT, and could human-generated answers further improve results?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Fig. 5 shows that, when the Translator LM is a 62B PaLM model, a higher parameter User LM improve overall performance. It is therefore possible that answer quality has a significant impact on translation performance and that human-generated answers can further improve overall performance."
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that scaling the User LM parameters improves performance, it does not directly compare the quality of answers from different User LM sizes or directly test human-generated answers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing INTER CPT performance using different User LM sizes (e.g., 8B, 62B, 540B) on the same test set, and a direct comparison between simulated User LM answers and human-generated answers for the same ambiguities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the specific error types that INTER CPT struggles with most, and how can these be addressed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper provides an error analysis in Section 6.6, identifying five types of errors: wrong question, wrong answer, many ambiguities, limited context, and style or other.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies error types, it does not provide solutions or strategies for mitigating these errors.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing different strategies for addressing each error type, such as improving the User LM's ability to answer questions, providing more context, or refining the Translator LM's ability to ask clarifying questions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does INTER CPT perform on ambiguities beyond the five types studied in the paper, and can it generalize to new ambiguity types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "We observe that on the Gender Neutral Names dataset INTER CPT performs best on BLEU and BLEURT and is much more able to resolve ambiguities with 6 to 10 points G-Acc improvements over Palm-with-context. On the Neutral Professions data, where test samples are taken from a different domain (Wikipedia biographies instead movie scripts), Palm-with-context and INTER CPT have similar performances."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests INTER CPT on five specific ambiguity types and does not explore its performance on other ambiguity types or its ability to generalize to unseen ambiguities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing INTER CPT on a wider range of ambiguity types, including those not explicitly addressed in the paper, and evaluating its ability to generalize to new ambiguity types in zero-shot settings.

## Limitations

- The method relies on simulated user interactions rather than human feedback, which may not capture all real-world ambiguity scenarios
- Evaluation is constrained to five predefined ambiguity types, limiting understanding of performance on novel or more complex ambiguities
- The approach requires multiple LLM calls, potentially limiting efficiency in production settings

## Confidence

- High confidence in the general framework effectiveness: The method demonstrates consistent improvements across multiple ambiguity types and language pairs, with statistically significant results
- Medium confidence in emergent scale effects: While the scaling trends are promising, the paper only tests three model sizes, and the plateauing of baseline performance needs further validation
- Medium confidence in generalization claims: The method shows promise on unseen ambiguities in the evaluation, but the extent of generalization to entirely new ambiguity types requires more extensive testing
