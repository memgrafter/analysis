# Measuring and Modifying Factual Knowledge in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06264
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06264
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Information-theory-based metrics (entropy, KL-divergence) outperform ranking methods by 35% in measuring factual knowledge

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces information-theory-based metrics for measuring factual knowledge in large language models, addressing limitations of existing ranking-based approaches. The authors propose using entropy and KL-divergence to quantify knowledge by analyzing probability distributions before and after instilling target facts. Through synthetic experiments, they demonstrate that these metrics outperform traditional ranking methods by over 35% in accuracy. The study also examines explicit (prompt-based) versus implicit (fine-tuning) knowledge instillation methods, finding that explicit approaches are inadequate for location and language-related facts, while showing promising applications in hallucination detection and factual alignment tasks.

## Method Summary
The method measures knowledge by computing entropy and KL-divergence between a language model's probability distribution over vocabulary tokens before and after instilling a target fact. For explicit instillation, facts are added through prompt modification; for implicit instillation, models are fine-tuned on modified prompts to create gold labels. The approach uses information theory to capture knowledge as uncertainty reduction in the model's predictions. The framework is validated through synthetic experiments comparing these metrics against ranking-based methods on BERT and T5 models using the LAMA benchmark, and extended to real-world applications including in-context learning tasks and hallucination detection.

## Key Results
- Entropy and KL-divergence metrics outperform ranking-based methods by over 35% in synthetic experiments
- Explicit knowledge instillation fails for location and language-related facts, requiring implicit fine-tuning instead
- Most relations involving location or language objects cannot be reliably differentiated across appeared, hallucinated, and missing facts in in-context learning
- Administrative territory, occupation, and employer relations show lower need for explicit instillation
- Headquarters location and employer relations demonstrate higher resistance to hallucination

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Entropy and KL-divergence metrics capture knowledge by measuring changes in probability distributions before and after fact instillation
- Mechanism: The approach measures knowledge by computing entropy/KL-divergence between the LLM's probability distribution over vocabulary tokens before and after instilling a target fact, capturing uncertainty reduction
- Core assumption: A model "knows" a fact when the uncertainty (entropy) of its predictions remains unchanged after fact instillation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "measure knowledge by analyzing the LLM's prediction probability distribution before and after instilling the target knowledge, employing metrics such as entropy and KL-divergence"
  - [section 2] "we use entropy to define the prompt's uncertainty as: H(prompt) = −ΣPklog(Pk)" and "we can measure the knowledge contained in the LLM about f by computing H(prompt) − H(prompt|instilling f into LLM)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - only general relation to knowledge editing but no direct mechanism validation
- Break condition: If probability distributions remain identical before and after instillation despite the model lacking knowledge, or if order of probabilities matters but entropy cannot capture it

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Information theory metrics outperform ranking-based methods by capturing full probability distributions rather than just top predictions
- Mechanism: Unlike ranking methods that only consider gold label position, entropy and KL-divergence use complete probability distributions, providing more nuanced knowledge measurement
- Core assumption: Knowledge is not binary and cannot be fully captured by ranking metrics alone
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "surpassing them by over 35% in a synthetic experiment" and "entropy considers the complete probability distribution, we can assess knowledge more comprehensively"
  - [section 2] "entropy cannot account for the order in the predicted probabilities of words in the vocabulary" (acknowledging limitation) and "KL-divergence is defined as KL(P ||Q)"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - related works on knowledge editing suggest ranking methods have limitations but don't directly validate distribution-based metrics
- Break condition: When vocabulary is truncated or out-of-vocabulary tokens significantly affect the distribution, or when prompt sensitivity overwhelms the distribution differences

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Explicit knowledge instillation works well except for location and language-related facts where implicit fine-tuning is required
- Mechanism: The method compares explicit (prompt-based) vs implicit (fine-tuning) knowledge instillation and finds that certain relation types (location, language) require parameter modification rather than prompt engineering
- Core assumption: Explicit instillation can approximate implicit instillation for most relations, but fails for specific types
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "discovering that LLMs exhibit limitations in capturing new knowledge under specific circumstances for one of these methods" and "explicit methods are inadequate for location and language-related facts"
  - [section 5.2] "the majority of them are samples with labels related to location...or language...for both BERT and T5" and "we cannot approximate implicit instillation with the explicit approach for these types of relations"
  - [corpus] Strong evidence - related work on knowledge editing confirms entity-centric approaches have limitations for certain relation types
- Break condition: When the cost of fine-tuning is prohibitive (as with GPT-3/4) and the relation type falls into the inadequate categories, or when explicit instillation partially works but creates hallucinations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Knowledge measurement (entropy/KL-divergence) -> Knowledge instillation (explicit/implicit) -> Application modules (factual alignment, hallucination detection)
- Critical path: For measuring knowledge: (1) Generate prompt with masked fact, (2) Obtain probability distribution before instillation, (3) Instill fact explicitly or implicitly, (4) Obtain probability distribution after instillation, (5) Compute entropy/KL-divergence difference. For instillation: (1) Select relation type, (2) Determine if explicit method is adequate, (3) Apply appropriate instillation method
- Design tradeoffs: Explicit instillation is cheaper but fails for location/language relations; implicit instillation is more reliable but costly. Using full vocabulary vs top-k approximations affects metric accuracy. The system trades computational cost for measurement accuracy
- Failure signatures: Explicit instillation failures manifest as unchanged KL-divergence despite missing knowledge, or hallucinations in generated text. Measurement failures show high entropy differences for known facts or low differences for unknown facts

## Limitations

- Vocabulary approximation method for models without full vocabulary access may introduce systematic biases
- Inability to fine-tune GPT-3/4 limits generalizability of explicit vs implicit instillation findings across model families
- Metrics struggle to reliably differentiate between hallucinated and genuinely missing facts in real-world scenarios

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The information-theoretic framework using entropy and KL-divergence is mathematically sound and the synthetic experiments clearly demonstrate superiority over ranking-based methods.

**Medium Confidence**: The practical implications for in-context learning and hallucination detection are suggestive but not definitive. The vocabulary approximation method introduces uncertainty.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that explicit instillation is "inadequate" for location and language facts may be overstated, as the metrics only show reduced correlation rather than complete failure.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset validation**: Replicate the synthetic experiments using alternative knowledge datasets (e.g., Google RE, ConceptNet) to verify that the 35% improvement over ranking methods is consistent across different knowledge domains.

2. **Vocabulary approximation stress test**: Systematically evaluate the impact of vocabulary truncation on metric accuracy by comparing full-vocabulary results (BERT, T5) against progressively truncated vocabularies for the same facts.

3. **Real-world hallucination detection benchmark**: Apply the metrics to a human-annotated dataset of LLM-generated text containing known hallucinations, measuring precision and recall in detecting hallucinated facts versus genuinely missing knowledge.
