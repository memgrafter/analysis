# Unsupervised Deep Unfolded PGD for Transmit Power Allocation in Wireless Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11865
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11865
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: DUPGD achieves up to 49% improvement in mean rate relative to maximum power baseline with 2x fewer iterations than iterative PGD

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for transmit power control (TPC) in wireless networks using deep unfolding of the projected gradient descent (PGD) algorithm. The proposed Deep Unfolded PGD (DUPGD) method transforms the iterative PGD optimization into a deep neural network structure, where step-size parameters are learned through unsupervised training. By leveraging the structure of the original PGD algorithm while learning optimal parameters, DUPGD achieves significant performance improvements over both traditional iterative PGD and maximum power baseline methods, particularly in dense device-to-device (D2D) communication scenarios.

The key innovation lies in the unsupervised learning framework that optimizes step-size parameters without requiring labeled data, making it practical for real-world deployment. Performance evaluation demonstrates that DUPGD can achieve up to 49% improvement in mean rate compared to maximum power baseline while requiring more than twice as few iterations as the iterative PGD algorithm. The method shows strong generalization capabilities across different deployment densities and maintains robustness under varying wireless channel conditions.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a transmit power control algorithm based on deep unfolding of the projected gradient descent method. The approach involves converting the iterative PGD algorithm into a deep neural network structure where each layer corresponds to one iteration of PGD, but with learned step-size parameters δk₁ and δk₂. The method uses unsupervised learning with the Adam optimizer, where the loss function is defined as the negative sum rate. The training process learns optimal step-size parameters while preserving the convergence properties of the original PGD algorithm.

The implementation includes two variants: DUPGD-online and DUPGD-offline, trained on simulated D2D communication scenarios with varying numbers of transmitter-receiver pairs. The neural network architecture consists of two hidden layers with 64 neurons each and ReLU activation functions. Performance is evaluated against both the iterative PGD algorithm and maximum power baseline in terms of convergence speed and achieved sum rate.

## Key Results
- DUPGD achieves up to 49% improvement in mean rate compared to maximum power baseline
- DUPGD requires more than 2x fewer iterations than iterative PGD while achieving better performance
- The method demonstrates strong generalization across different deployment densities (10-20 transmitter-receiver pairs)
- DUPGD maintains robustness to varying wireless channel conditions and noise levels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The deep unfolding approach works by exploiting the iterative structure of PGD while learning optimal step-size parameters through unsupervised training. By transforming the optimization problem into a neural network architecture, DUPGD can leverage powerful gradient-based optimization techniques to find better step-size parameters than traditional fixed or hand-tuned values. The unsupervised learning framework eliminates the need for labeled data, making it practical for real-world deployment where obtaining optimal power allocations is computationally expensive.

The method's effectiveness stems from its ability to learn problem-specific step-sizes that adapt to the wireless channel conditions while maintaining the convergence guarantees of the original PGD algorithm. This hybrid approach combines the interpretability and convergence properties of traditional optimization methods with the flexibility and performance of deep learning techniques.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Channel gains, noise variance, max power constraints -> DUPGD neural network (with learned δk₁, δk₂ parameters) -> Power allocation decisions -> Sum rate calculation -> Loss (negative sum rate) -> Parameter updates via Adam

Critical Path:
Input data (channel gains, noise) → Neural network layers (with learned step-sizes) → Power allocation output → Sum rate computation → Backpropagation through time for parameter updates

Design Tradeoffs:
- Number of layers vs. convergence speed: More layers allow finer optimization but increase computational complexity
- Learning rate vs. stability: Higher learning rates speed up training but may cause instability
- Network size vs. generalization: Larger networks may overfit to specific scenarios
- Unsupervised vs. supervised learning: Avoids need for labeled data but may converge to suboptimal local minima

Failure Signatures:
- Slow convergence or divergence of training loss indicates improper learning rate or initialization
- Overfitting to training scenarios shows poor generalization to new deployment densities
- Power allocations exceeding maximum constraints indicate incorrect projection implementation
- Performance worse than maximum power baseline suggests learned step-sizes are ineffective
