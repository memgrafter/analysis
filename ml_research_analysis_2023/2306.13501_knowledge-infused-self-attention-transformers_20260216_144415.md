# Knowledge-Infused Self Attention Transformers
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13501
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13501
- Reference count: 33
- Introduces systematic knowledge infusion method for transformers with accuracy improvements from 74.79% to 80.9% on QQP and 72.3% to 88.53% on MNLI

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses limitations in transformer-based models like hallucinations and alignment issues through systematic knowledge infusion. The authors categorize transformer components into inductive biases and latent representations, then propose three categories of knowledge infusion: shallow (embedding-level), semi-deep (encoder-level), and deep (interleaved across all transformer blocks). Deep infusion, which incorporates knowledge at both latent representations and inductive biases across all layers, demonstrates superior performance on GLUE benchmark tasks compared to other methods.

## Method Summary
The authors systematically categorize transformer components into inductive biases and latent representations, then define three categories of knowledge infusion: shallow (at embedding level), semi-deep (at encoder level), and deep (interleaved across all transformer blocks at both representation and bias levels). They evaluate these approaches on GLUE benchmark tasks using both standard accuracy metrics and newly proposed evaluation metrics including Combined Graph Encoder and KSAT model Accuracy (CGKA) and Data Efficiency at K (DE@k). The deep infusion approach interleaves knowledge incorporation throughout the model architecture rather than limiting it to specific layers.

## Key Results
- Deep knowledge infusion improved QQP accuracy from 74.79% to 80.9% and MNLI from 72.3% to 88.53%
- DE@50 metric achieved 80-83% accuracy using only 50% of training data
- Deep infusion outperformed shallow and semi-deep approaches across GLUE benchmark tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness stems from addressing transformer limitations through structured knowledge incorporation at multiple architectural levels. By interleaving knowledge at both latent representations and inductive biases across all transformer blocks rather than limiting infusion to specific layers, the model can leverage external knowledge more effectively throughout the reasoning process. This systematic approach helps mitigate hallucinations and alignment issues by grounding representations in factual knowledge while maintaining the model's ability to generalize.

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Input Embeddings -> Knowledge Graph Encoder -> Transformer Blocks -> Output Classifier
- Critical path: Knowledge injection points at embedding layer (shallow), encoder layer (semi-deep), or interleaved across all blocks (deep)
- Design tradeoffs: Depth of infusion vs computational overhead and training complexity
- Failure signatures: Performance degradation on tasks requiring reasoning vs factual recall
- Three first experiments: 1) Compare shallow vs deep infusion on single GLUE task, 2) Measure computational overhead differences, 3) Test knowledge retention after fine-tuning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions regarding the generalizability of knowledge infusion approaches across diverse domains beyond the GLUE benchmark tasks evaluated. Key uncertainties include whether similar gains would transfer to domains requiring different knowledge types or structural relationships, and how the computational overhead introduced by deep knowledge infusion methods affects practical deployment feasibility.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation to GLUE benchmark tasks without domain-specific validation
- New evaluation metrics lack comparative validation against established benchmarks
- Computational overhead of deep knowledge infusion methods not quantified

## Confidence
- High confidence in comparative effectiveness of deep vs shallow/semi-deep infusion within GLUE framework
- Medium confidence in generalizability of three-category framework across domains
- Low confidence in new evaluation metrics (CGKA, DE@k) due to lack of comparative validation

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate knowledge infusion framework across domain-specific datasets (biomedical, legal, scientific) to assess cross-domain generalization
2. Conduct ablation studies measuring computational overhead (FLOPs, memory usage, inference time) for each knowledge infusion category
3. Validate new metrics (CGKA, DE@k) against established evaluation frameworks across multiple model architectures to establish discriminative power and reliability
