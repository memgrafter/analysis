# Autoencoders for a manifold learning problem with a Jacobian rank constraint

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14194

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the manifold learning problem by introducing a novel autoencoder training approach that combines architectural constraints with a soft rank constraint using the Ky-Fan k-antinorm of the Jacobian. The method aims to find an operator that maps points to close neighbors on a hidden k-dimensional manifold while preserving local geometry. The key innovation is the addition of a curvature term and a soft rank constraint term to the autoencoder's objective function, allowing for more flexible handling of heterogeneous datasets where different parts may have different intrinsic dimensions.

## Method Summary
The proposed method formulates manifold learning as an optimization problem that combines architectural constraints with a soft rank constraint. The autoencoder's objective function includes a reconstruction term, a curvature term that preserves local geometry, and a soft rank constraint term using the Ky-Fan k-antinorm of the Jacobian. An alternating algorithm is developed to minimize this objective, alternating between gradient descent and updates of tangent spaces. The soft rank constraint allows the method to handle datasets where different parts may have varying intrinsic dimensions, providing flexibility over hard constraints.

## Key Results
- The proposed method outperforms the CAE+H method in terms of manifold learning quality and classification accuracy on synthetic and real-world datasets
- The soft rank constraint allows for more flexible handling of heterogeneous datasets where different parts may have different intrinsic dimensions
- Experimental results on MNIST demonstrate classification improvements, though direct validation of manifold learning quality remains limited

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by constraining the autoencoder to learn mappings that preserve the local manifold structure while enforcing a low-rank Jacobian constraint. The curvature term ensures that nearby points on the manifold map to nearby points in the latent space, maintaining local geometry. The soft rank constraint using the Ky-Fan k-antinorm provides a differentiable approximation to enforcing low-dimensional structure, allowing the optimization to find representations that are close to but not strictly confined to a k-dimensional manifold.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input Data -> Encoder -> Latent Space -> Decoder -> Output Data -> Jacobian Constraint -> Objective Function

**Critical Path**: The optimization algorithm alternates between updating the autoencoder parameters (encoder and decoder) and updating the tangent spaces that define the local manifold structure.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Soft rank constraint vs hard rank constraint - soft constraints provide better convergence properties and flexibility but may allow some deviation from strict low-dimensional structure. Curvature term vs reconstruction-only objective - adding curvature improves manifold preservation but increases computational complexity.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor convergence or suboptimal solutions may occur when the soft rank constraint is too loose (allowing too much dimensionality) or too tight (preventing adequate reconstruction). The method may struggle with datasets that have complex, non-uniform manifold structures.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees for the alternating optimization algorithm remain incompletely characterized, with no formal convergence proofs provided
- The method assumes data lies on or near a k-dimensional manifold, but real-world data often exhibits complex structure including multiple manifolds and varying dimensionality
- Experimental evaluation is limited in scope, using only three datasets with relatively small sample sizes
