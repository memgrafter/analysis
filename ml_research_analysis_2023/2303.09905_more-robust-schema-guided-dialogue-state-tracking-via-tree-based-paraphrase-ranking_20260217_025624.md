# More Robust Schema-Guided Dialogue State Tracking via Tree-Based Paraphrase Ranking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09905
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09905
- Reference count: 40
- This work proposes a framework for improving the robustness of schema-guided dialogue state tracking models to prompt style and vocabulary variations.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of improving schema-guided dialogue state tracking (DST) robustness to variations in schema descriptions. The authors propose a tree-based paraphrase ranking algorithm that generates synthetic schemas with controlled lexical diversity while preserving semantic faithfulness. By augmenting training data with these generated schemas, the method improves both average joint goal accuracy and schema sensitivity across seen and unseen slots in the SGD-X benchmark.

## Method Summary
The proposed method generates synthetic schemas by first creating a large pool of paraphrase candidates using Pegasus and T5 models with different decoding settings. These candidates undergo heuristic filtering to remove toxic or hallucinated content, followed by entailment filtering using a BART model to ensure semantic faithfulness. A tree-based ranking algorithm then selects diverse paraphrases by traversing a 3-level tree structure organized by Jaccard distance, entailment scores, and string similarity metrics. The selected paraphrases augment training data 6x, and models (D3ST and T5DST) are trained on this augmented data to improve robustness to schema variations.

## Key Results
- Tree-based ranking improves lexical diversity (Jaccard distance) and semantic faithfulness (entailment scores) compared to baseline augmentation methods
- Training with synthetic schemas improves average joint goal accuracy on SGD-X benchmark for both seen and unseen slots
- Models trained with augmented data show reduced schema sensitivity, indicating less overfitting to training schema patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Tree-based ranking improves lexical diversity while preserving semantic faithfulness.
- Mechanism: Candidates are filtered and ranked by a sequence of metrics (Jaccard, entailment, string similarity) in a tree structure, enabling controlled traversal for diversity vs. faithfulness trade-offs.
- Core assumption: A tree traversal guided by metrics ensures higher diversity without breaking semantic constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "tree-based ranking to jointly optimise lexical diversity and semantic faithfulness"
  - [section] "Each unique list of metric values describing the distance between the input and a candidate generates a path in the tree"
- Break condition: If entailment metric is weak or poorly calibrated, diversity may come at the cost of semantic drift.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Synthetic schema augmentation improves generalization and robustness in DST.
- Mechanism: Training data is augmented with diverse paraphrases of schema descriptions, forcing the model to learn invariant slot semantics rather than surface forms.
- Core assumption: Model overfits to stylistic cues in training schemas; diverse synthetic schemas reduce this bias.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "augmenting training data with these generated schemas improves the average joint goal accuracy and schema sensitivity"
  - [section] "the models trained with our augmentation are less likely to overfit to the linguistic patterns of the training schemas"
- Break condition: If synthetic schemas contain too much noise or hallucination, robustness gains may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Entailment filtering improves semantic faithfulness of synthetic schemas.
- Mechanism: BART-based entailment model filters out paraphrases that do not semantically overlap with original schema descriptions.
- Core assumption: Entailment scores correlate with human judgments of semantic fidelity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "entailment model to increase semantic faithfulness"
  - [section] "We implement our entailment filter using BART"
- Break condition: Entailment model fails to capture nuanced semantic differences (e.g., "departure time" → "arrival time").

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Paraphrase generation → Heuristic + entailment filtering → Tree construction → Tree ranking → Schema variant generation → DST training
- Critical path: Paraphrase generation → Tree-based ranking → Augmented data → DST training
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Diversity vs. semantic faithfulness (Jaccard vs. entailment)
  - Computation cost vs. ranking accuracy (large candidate pool vs. efficient filtering)
  - Heuristic vs. learned filtering (speed vs. precision)
- Failure signatures:
  - Low entailment scores across variants → semantic drift
  - High self-BLEU among variants → insufficient diversity
  - No improvement in SSJGA → overfitting to style still present
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate 500 paraphrases per schema with Pegasus, apply heuristic + entailment filtering, rank with tree, and inspect Jaccard/Entailment scores.
  2. Augment D3ST training with 2x and 6x synthetic schemas, evaluate JGAorig, JGAv1-5, and SSJGA.
  3. Compare Pegasus + BART vs. EDA vs. Backtranslation on diversity metrics and DST performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of entailment model affect the quality of generated schemas?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention using BART for entailment but also note that correlations with human judgment are not perfect.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative entailment models or their impact on schema quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing schema generation using different entailment models (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa) and their impact on lexical diversity, semantic faithfulness, and downstream DST performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the tree-based ranking algorithm be extended to optimize for additional criteria beyond lexical diversity and semantic faithfulness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state their framework can build arbitrary-depth trees with custom user metrics.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates optimization for two criteria and does not explore other potential objectives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the framework to optimize for additional criteria such as syntactic complexity, domain specificity, or task-related features, and evaluating their impact on schema quality and DST performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size of the candidate paraphrase pool affect the quality and diversity of generated schemas?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors generate 500 candidates per input but do not systematically vary this number.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the relationship between candidate pool size and schema quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the number of generated candidates (e.g., 100, 500, 1000) and evaluating their impact on lexical diversity, semantic faithfulness, and downstream DST performance.

## Limitations
- Synthetic schema fidelity: The extent to which generated paraphrases preserve domain-specific meaning is unclear, with no qualitative examples of filtered paraphrases.
- Generalization beyond SGD-X: All experiments are conducted on SGD-X benchmark with only 5 schema variants, not validated on truly out-of-domain schema variations.
- Computational overhead: Tree-based ranking processes up to 500 paraphrase candidates per schema description, likely introducing significant computational overhead.

## Confidence
- High confidence: Tree-based ranking algorithm successfully improves both lexical diversity and semantic faithfulness compared to baseline augmentation methods.
- Medium confidence: Augmented training improves robustness to schema variations is supported by SGD-X experiments, but results are less compelling for seen slots.
- Medium confidence: The method reduces overfitting to training schema patterns is supported by schema sensitivity metrics, but lacks direct evidence on truly unseen schema styles.

## Next Checks
1. Test the augmentation method on DST datasets with different domains (e.g., MultiWOZ) and schemas with completely different slot types to verify generalization claims beyond SGD-X.
2. Remove the tree-based ranking and use simpler filtering (e.g., only entailment threshold) to quantify the specific contribution of the tree structure to diversity-faithfulness trade-off.
3. Manually inspect a sample of generated paraphrases to verify that semantic faithfulness is maintained, particularly for complex slot descriptions that involve multiple entities or relationships.
