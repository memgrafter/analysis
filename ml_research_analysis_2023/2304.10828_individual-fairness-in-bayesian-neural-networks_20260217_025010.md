# Individual Fairness in Bayesian Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10828

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates individual fairness in Bayesian neural networks using epsilon-delta individual fairness (IF). The authors adapt gradient-based adversarial attacks (FGSM/PGD) to create fairness-aware attacks (Fair-FGSM/Fair-PGD) that use task-specific similarity metrics like weighted L_p and Mahalanobis distance. They provide statistical guarantees for worst-case expected fairness over the input space using concentration inequalities. Experiments show BNNs trained with approximate Bayesian inference (VI and HMC) consistently outperform deterministic neural networks and deep ensembles on fairness metrics, with improvements attributed to Bayesian training and averaging reducing gradient magnitudes.

## Method Summary
The method involves three key components: first, implementing Fair-FGSM and Fair-PGD attacks by modifying standard adversarial attacks with task-specific similarity metrics for gradient projection; second, training BNNs using variational inference (VI) and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) alongside deterministic neural networks and deep ensembles on Adult and Folktables datasets; and third, evaluating individual fairness by sampling test points, applying fairness attacks, and computing empirical probability that predictive differences stay within tolerance delta. The approach leverages concentration inequalities to provide statistical guarantees for worst-case expected fairness over the entire input space.

## Key Results
- BNNs trained with VI and HMC show significantly better individual fairness than deterministic NNs and deep ensembles
- Fairness improvements are consistent across both Adult and Folktables datasets using gender as the sensitive attribute
- Bayesian training and averaging reduce gradient magnitudes, leading to improved fairness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Bayesian neural networks achieve better individual fairness through their inherent uncertainty quantification. During Bayesian training, the posterior distribution over weights captures uncertainty, and during prediction, averaging over this posterior smooths predictions. This averaging effect reduces the magnitude of gradients with respect to inputs, making the model's predictions less sensitive to small perturbations in similar individuals. The Fair-FGSM/Fair-PGD attacks exploit this by using task-specific similarity metrics, and BNNs show more robustness to these attacks due to their smoother decision boundaries.

## Foundational Learning
- **Individual Fairness (ε-δ definition)**: Measures whether similar individuals receive similar predictions; needed to quantify fairness in BNNs and requires understanding of similarity metrics and tolerance thresholds.
- **Concentration Inequalities**: Mathematical tools providing statistical guarantees about worst-case fairness; needed for theoretical bounds and verified through checking inequality conditions hold.
- **Variational Inference (VI)**: Approximate Bayesian inference method for training BNNs; needed for scalable posterior approximation and checked via ELBO convergence.
- **Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC)**: MCMC method for sampling from posterior distributions; needed for accurate posterior sampling and validated through trace plots and effective sample size.
- **Mahalanobis Distance**: Similarity metric accounting for feature correlations; needed for task-specific fairness measurement and verified through distance matrix computation.
- **Weighted L_p Distance**: Task-specific similarity metric with feature importance weighting; needed for incorporating domain knowledge into fairness and checked through weight validation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Data Preprocessing -> Model Training (Deterministic NN, Deep Ensemble, BNN-VI, BNN-HMC) -> Fairness Attack (Fair-FGSM, Fair-PGD) -> Evaluation Metrics

**Critical Path:** Model training must complete before fairness attacks can be applied, and attack results feed directly into fairness evaluation metrics.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Shallow architectures (3 layers, 16-32 neurons) are chosen for computational efficiency but may limit representation power. VI offers faster training than HMC but potentially less accurate posterior approximation.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor fairness results indicate incorrect implementation of Mahalanobis distance projection during attacks. BNNs not showing improved fairness suggests issues with posterior sampling or VI/HMC implementation.

## Limitations
- Empirical evaluation limited to binary gender as sole sensitive attribute, potentially missing intersectional fairness concerns
- Computational cost of Fair-PGD attacks may limit scalability to larger datasets or models
- Comparison focuses on shallow architectures, leaving uncertainty about fairness improvements in deeper networks

## Confidence
- High confidence in theoretical framework and concentration inequality guarantees
- Medium confidence in empirical demonstration across datasets, though limited to specific metrics and model sizes
- Medium confidence in attribution of fairness improvements to Bayesian averaging versus other training aspects
