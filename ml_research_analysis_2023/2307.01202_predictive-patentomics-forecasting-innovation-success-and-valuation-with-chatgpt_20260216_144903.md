# Predictive Patentomics: Forecasting Innovation Success and Valuation with ChatGPT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2307.01202
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01202
- Reference count: 35
- A deep neural network using ChatGPT embeddings predicts patent acceptance with 86% F1 score and patent value with 42% adjusted R², outperforming traditional methods and revealing market underreaction to patent applications.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to predicting patent success and value using OpenAI's ChatGPT embeddings, achieving significant improvements over traditional structural variables alone. A deep neural network trained on the ChatGPT embedding and firm-level features predicts patent acceptance with an F1 score of 86% and patent value with an adjusted R² of 42%. The embedding alone drives a 24% incremental improvement in value prediction and helps identify worst- and best-case applications with ~10% higher accuracy. These results suggest that LLM-based embeddings capture nuanced textual quality information that correlates with patent examiner decisions and market valuation.

## Method Summary
The method combines USPTO patent application and grant data (2001-2020) with firm-level financial data from CRSP/Compustat, generating ChatGPT Ada-002 embeddings for patent titles and abstracts. A 3-layer feed-forward neural network with Mish activation function is trained on these embeddings plus structural features (CPC classifications, firm characteristics, etc.) to predict patent acceptance probability and value. The model uses a rolling 3-year training window to prevent look-ahead bias, with performance evaluated through benchmark comparisons and economic applications including adjusted patent valuation and long-short portfolio construction that yields 3.3% annual abnormal returns.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT embedding + deep neural network achieves 86% F1 score for patent acceptance prediction and 42% adjusted R² for patent value prediction
- Embedding alone drives 24% incremental improvement in value prediction over structural variables
- Long-short portfolio based on predicted acceptance rates generates 3.3% annual abnormal returns, indicating market underreaction to patent applications

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT embedding captures nuanced textual quality signals that correlate with patent success
- Mechanism: The embedding vector encodes multiple dimensions of text quality (e.g., sentiment, writing clarity, technicality) which are relevant to human patent examiners' decisions
- Core assumption: Patent examiners' subjective judgments of application quality correlate with application success
- Evidence anchors: "OpenAI's state-of-the-art textual embedding accesses complex information about the quality and impact of each invention"; "Ada-002...transforms an entire body of text into a vector of 1536 real numbers"
- Break condition: If examiner decisions are based primarily on objective technical criteria rather than text quality signals

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Deep neural networks with Mish activation function better capture non-linear relationships between embedding features and patent value
- Mechanism: Mish activation handles negative inputs and captures highly non-linear relationships that simpler activation functions like ReLU cannot effectively model
- Core assumption: The relationship between patent text quality and economic value is non-linear and complex
- Evidence anchors: "Mish perform significantly better in cases with large sample sizes and feature count"; "considerable increase proves that the ChatGPT textual embedding vector is a powerful and non-trivial predictor for value"
- Break condition: If the relationship between embedding features and patent value is actually linear or simpler than assumed

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The market underreacts to patent application information, creating arbitrage opportunities
- Mechanism: Investors focus on granted patents rather than applications, missing valuable signals about future innovation that the model can extract
- Core assumption: Patent applications contain valuable information about future firm innovation that the market does not fully incorporate
- Evidence anchors: "a long-short portfolio based on predicted acceptance rates achieves significant abnormal returns of 3.3% annually"; "the market does not monitor applications in particular as closely as patents"
- Break condition: If the market efficiently incorporates all available patent application information

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: USPTO patent/application data → text preprocessing → ChatGPT embedding generation → Feature engineering (embedding + structural variables) → Deep learning model (3-layer MLP with Mish activation) → Prediction outputs (acceptance probability, value estimate) → Economic applications (valuation adjustment, portfolio construction)
- Critical path: Embedding generation → Feature matrix creation → Model training and prediction → Portfolio performance calculation
- Design tradeoffs: Using complex neural networks vs. simpler models (better performance but less interpretability), Mish activation vs. traditional activations (better non-linear modeling but newer, less tested), focusing on textual features vs. structural features (captures new signals but may miss other important information)
- Failure signatures: Poor out-of-sample performance indicating overfitting, lack of improvement over benchmarks suggesting embeddings don't add value, inability to achieve significant abnormal returns indicating market efficiency
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train benchmark model without embeddings to establish baseline performance
  2. Test different activation functions (ReLU, ELU, Mish) to verify Mish's superiority
  3. Construct long-short portfolio based on model predictions to test economic significance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the ChatGPT embedding outperform human reviewers in predicting patent acceptance?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that the embedding captures information about the quality and impact of each invention and improves prediction accuracy by 24% over traditional models
- Why unresolved: The paper does not directly compare the embedding's performance to that of human patent examiners or industry experts
- What evidence would resolve it: A head-to-head comparison of the embedding's predictions against those of human reviewers on a common dataset of patent applications

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the predictive model perform on patent applications from industries with different technical complexities?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions using CPC classifications and AI classifications but does not analyze performance across different technical domains
- Why unresolved: The paper does not report results broken down by industry or technical complexity
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance metrics (AUC, F1 score, etc.) for the predictive model on patent applications from various industries or technical domains

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the predictive model be used to identify and prevent the grant of low-quality patents?
- Basis in paper: The paper demonstrates the model's ability to identify the "worst" applications with a 26% acceptance rate, suggesting potential for screening
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test whether the model's predictions influence the USPTO's decision-making process
- What evidence would resolve it: A study tracking the outcomes of patent applications flagged by the model as low-quality and comparing them to a control group

## Limitations
- Reliance on ChatGPT's Ada-002 embeddings may limit reproducibility as embedding quality could vary with different LLM versions or providers
- 3-year rolling training window may not capture longer-term structural changes in patent examination patterns or market behavior
- Major claims about market underreaction warrant medium confidence and should be validated across different time periods and market conditions

## Next Checks

1. **Out-of-sample robustness test**: Replicate the model performance using a completely independent dataset or time period not used in training to verify that results are not overfit to the specific sample.

2. **Feature importance analysis**: Conduct ablation studies to isolate which components (embedding dimensions, structural variables, activation function) contribute most to predictive power, helping identify potential redundancy or overfitting.

3. **Economic significance stress test**: Construct portfolios using different formation periods, holding periods, and risk adjustment methods to confirm that the abnormal returns are robust to various portfolio construction choices and not an artifact of specific methodological decisions.
