# Variational Inference for Bayesian Neural Networks under Model and Parameter Uncertainty

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00934
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00934
- Authors: [List of authors]
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Scalable VI approach for BNNs with structural and parameter uncertainty using spike-and-slab priors and Concrete distributions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a scalable variational inference method for Bayesian neural networks that addresses both model structure and parameter uncertainty. The approach uses latent binary variables to represent model structures and spike-and-slab priors on weights, enabling automatic network sparsification. The authors develop a doubly stochastic VI algorithm that employs reparametrization of marginal inclusion probabilities and Concrete distributions to handle the discrete nature of model indicators, allowing for efficient gradient-based optimization.

## Method Summary
The method extends variational inference to Bayesian neural networks by introducing latent binary variables that determine whether each weight is included in the model. Spike-and-slab priors are placed on the weights, with the spike component (point mass at zero) inducing sparsity. The variational approximation uses Concrete distributions to relax the discrete inclusion indicators to continuous surrogates, enabling the reparametrization trick for gradient-based optimization. The approach supports different variational families including mean-field, multivariate normal, and low-rank approximations for modeling dependencies between inclusion probabilities.

## Key Results
- Achieved 98.1% accuracy on MNIST with only 7.9% of weights active using the median probability model
- Demonstrated near-perfect accuracy (98.8%) on MNIST when applying a 95% posterior probability threshold for classification
- Showed good performance on out-of-domain data, indicating effective uncertainty quantification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Variational inference with reparametrization of marginal inclusion probabilities enables tractable posterior approximation over both model structures and parameters.
- Mechanism: By introducing latent binary variables for weight inclusion and applying Concrete distributions, the discrete model space is relaxed to a continuous surrogate that can be optimized using gradient-based methods. The reparametrization trick allows backpropagating through stochastic sampling steps, making the ELBO differentiable with respect to variational parameters.
- Core assumption: The variational family (Bernoulli for indicators, Gaussian for weights) can approximate the true posterior well enough that optimizing the ELBO yields meaningful posterior uncertainty estimates.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The median probability model yields high accuracy while enforcing strong sparsity by selecting weights with inclusion probability above 0.5.
- Mechanism: By thresholding marginal inclusion probabilities at 0.5, only the most probable weights are retained, effectively pruning the network without sacrificing much predictive performance. This works because the variational inference provides calibrated posterior inclusion probabilities that reflect the data's support for each weight.
- Core assumption: The variational posterior approximates the true marginal inclusion probabilities sufficiently well that the 0.5 threshold captures the "essential" weights for accurate prediction.

- Core assumption: The true posterior has non-trivial correlations between inclusion indicators, and the added flexibility of a full or low-rank covariance matrix captures these correlations better than independent Bernoulli assumptions.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Variational inference and the evidence lower bound (ELBO)
  - Why needed here: The paper's entire inference procedure hinges on maximizing the ELBO to approximate the intractable posterior over both model structures and parameters.
  - Quick check question: Can you derive the ELBO from the KL divergence between the variational distribution and the true posterior?

- Concept: Reparametrization trick for stochastic gradients
  - Why needed here: The discrete nature of model indicators requires relaxing them to continuous surrogates (Concrete distributions) so that gradients can flow through the sampling process.
  - Quick check question: How does the Concrete distribution approximate a Bernoulli variable, and what role does the temperature parameter play?

- Concept: Spike-and-slab priors and their role in model selection
  - Why needed here: The prior structure (Gaussian slab + point mass spike) induces sparsity by allowing weights to be exactly zero with positive probability, which the variational approximation learns to select.
  - Quick check question: In the context of neural networks, what is the effect of placing a spike-and-slab prior on a weight versus a simple Gaussian prior?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prior layer -> Variational layer -> Optimization layer -> Prediction layer
- Critical path: Prior → Variational family → ELBO computation → Stochastic gradient estimation → Parameter update → Prediction
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Mean-field vs. MVN vs. low-rank MVN: Simpler, faster, but less expressive vs. more accurate posterior approximation at higher computational cost
  - Concrete temperature: Lower temperature → closer to true Bernoulli but harder optimization; higher temperature → smoother but less discrete
  - Prior variance of slab: Larger variance → more flexible models but risk of overfitting; smaller variance → stronger shrinkage
- Failure signatures:
  - Over-sparsity: Median probability model accuracy drops sharply → check marginal inclusion probabilities and prior variances
  - Under-sparsity: Model remains dense despite intended sparsity → verify thresholding and check for degenerate Concrete sampling
  - Poor uncertainty calibration: Out-of-domain performance degrades → inspect posterior variance estimates and correlation structures
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a small BNN (e.g., 1 hidden layer, 50 units) on MNIST with mean-field variational approximation; compare median probability model sparsity vs. full model accuracy
  2. Replace mean-field with MVN structure; observe changes in inclusion probability correlations and predictive performance
  3. Vary the Concrete temperature parameter; analyze the effect on optimization stability and sparsity patterns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the biases of variational inference in Bayesian neural networks with spike-and-slab priors compare to the biases of other approximate inference methods (e.g., MCMC) for this class of models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that "variational inference within these complex nonlinear models should be performed" and references prior work showing that "results can be strongly biased" for variational inference in linear models with spike-and-slab priors. It also mentions that "various approaches for reducing the bias in variational inference are developed" but does not evaluate or compare these approaches.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any empirical comparison of variational inference bias to other methods like MCMC for BNNs with spike-and-slab priors. The theoretical analysis of posterior consistency by Bhattacharya and Maiti (2021) and Chérief-Abdellatif (2020) is mentioned but not directly connected to bias evaluation.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic empirical study comparing the bias of variational inference to MCMC or other exact methods on benchmark BNN problems with known ground truth. This could include analyzing posterior distributions of parameters and predictive accuracy across different approximation techniques.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal way to incorporate prior knowledge about correlations between weights or neurons in Bayesian neural networks, and how does this affect the trade-off between sparsity and predictive performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses "dilution priors" that down-weight models with highly correlated variables and mentions that "parameter and model priors can incorporate joint-dependent structures" to improve sparsification. It also notes that "more research should be done on the choice of priors" and that "studies of the accuracy of variational inference within these complex nonlinear models should be performed."
- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests that incorporating prior knowledge about correlations could improve performance, it does not provide any empirical evaluation of different correlation structures or demonstrate how this affects the sparsity-predictive accuracy trade-off in BNNs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental comparisons of different prior structures (e.g., dilution priors, hierarchical priors, group sparsity priors) on BNN architectures, measuring both the achieved sparsity levels and predictive performance on benchmark datasets. This would include ablation studies showing the impact of removing correlation structures.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of Bayesian model averaging with spike-and-slab priors scale to deeper and wider neural network architectures, and what are the computational bottlenecks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "our approach does not currently aim at interpretable inference in most cases" and notes that "the number of parameters to optimize" affects computational time. It mentions that "studies of the accuracy of variational inference within these complex nonlinear models should be performed" and that "addressing depth and width selection more explicitly" is future work.
- Why unresolved: The experimental evaluation is limited to networks with 2 hidden layers (400 and 600 neurons). The paper mentions computational time increases with parameter count but does not analyze how this scales with depth or width, nor does it identify specific computational bottlenecks in the variational inference algorithm.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic scalability analysis varying network depth (e.g., 2-10 hidden layers) and width (e.g., 100-1000 neurons per layer), measuring training time, memory usage, and predictive performance. This should identify which components of the algorithm (e.g., covariance matrix computations, sampling procedures) become bottlenecks at different scales.

## Limitations

- Limited evaluation to three benchmark datasets (MNIST, FMNIST, PHONEME) without testing on larger-scale problems
- Computational overhead of structured variational approximations (MVN, low-rank MVN) not extensively analyzed
- Claims about uncertainty quantification in out-of-domain settings based on limited ablation studies
