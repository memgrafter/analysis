# Injecting knowledge into language generation: a case study in auto-charting after-visit care instructions from medical dialogue

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of under-generation of rare but important tokens in sequence-to-sequence models, which can lead to factual errors in high-stakes domains like healthcare. The authors propose a method to identify and uplift the conditional probability of these rare tokens during training. They introduce the concept of "utilization rate" to measure the propensity of a rare token to appear in both the source and reference sequences. The method involves injecting external knowledge to compute utilization rates and using a regularization term in the training objective to uplift the marginal probability of important rare tokens. The approach is evaluated on the task of generating after-visit care instructions from medical dialogues.

## Method Summary
The proposed method identifies high utilization concepts using external knowledge and a regularization term in the training objective to uplift the marginal probability of these concepts. The training uses a transformer architecture with FairSeq, Adam optimizer, and early stopping based on validation performance. The utilization rate is computed using an equivalence class map provided externally, allowing for the identification of important rare tokens even when dataset frequency estimates are poor. The approach involves weighted utilization loss, where the weight for each token is determined by its utilization rate.

## Key Results
- Proposed method improves model's utilization rate, reducing under-generation of important rare tokens
- Enhances relevance and usability of generated instructions without negatively impacting fluency
- Achieves improved concept overlap and reduced uncertainty in generated instructions

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing: Tokenization, concept recognition using UMLS ontologies
  - Model: Transformer-based sequence-to-sequence architecture
  - Training: Standard cross-entropy loss + utilization loss (unweighted or weighted)
  - Evaluation: BERTScore, GPT-2 perplexity, concept overlap, human evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Identify medical concepts in source and reference sequences
  2. Compute utilization rates using external knowledge and dataset values
  3. Train model with utilization loss regularization
  4. Evaluate model performance on test set

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Unweighted vs. weighted utilization loss: Weighted loss may be more effective but requires accurate utilization rate computation
  - External knowledge dependency: Model performance relies on the quality of the external knowledge source
  - Computational cost: Computing utilization rates and applying regularization increases training time

- Failure signatures:
  - Model performance does not improve despite regularization: Could indicate inaccurate utilization rate computation or ineffective regularization
  - Degenerate outputs: May result from over-regularization or inappropriate utilization rate weighting
  - Low concept overlap: Suggests the model is not effectively generating important rare tokens

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train model with unweighted utilization loss (α = 0.5) and evaluate utilization rate estimation
  2. Train model with weighted utilization loss using identity mapping and compare performance to unweighted loss
  3. Train model with weighted utilization loss using semantic type mapping and compare performance to other approaches

## Limitations

- The proposed method's effectiveness relies heavily on the quality of the external knowledge source and the accuracy of the utilization rate computation.
- The impact of the weighted utilization loss compared to the unweighted loss is not thoroughly explored.
- The human evaluation results are limited to a small sample of 50 examples, which may not be representative of the entire dataset.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The mechanism of using utilization rates to identify important rare tokens and uplifting their marginal probability through regularization is sound and well-motivated.
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of the weighted utilization loss compared to the unweighted loss is supported by the results but requires further validation.
- Low confidence: The generalizability of the proposed method to other domains or tasks beyond healthcare is uncertain due to the reliance on domain-specific external knowledge.
