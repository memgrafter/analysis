# Visual Question Answering: A Survey on Techniques and Common Trends in Recent Literature

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
This survey systematically analyzes 25 recent papers on Visual Question Answering (VQA), covering the period from 2020 to 2022. The study examines VQA methodologies, datasets, evaluation metrics, and emerging trends in the field. Key focus areas include bias mitigation techniques, external knowledge incorporation, and graph-based reasoning approaches. The survey identifies CNN, RNN, and transformer architectures as the most widely used techniques, with accuracy remaining the dominant evaluation metric. The work highlights critical gaps in standardization and calls for expanded research beyond current trends.

## Method Summary
The survey methodology involved systematically reviewing 25 papers published between 2020 and 2022 on VQA techniques and trends. Papers were analyzed across multiple dimensions including architectural approaches, dataset usage, evaluation metrics, and emerging research directions. The study particularly focused on recent developments in bias reduction, knowledge incorporation, and reasoning capabilities. ## Key Results
- CNN, RNN, and transformer architectures remain the dominant VQA techniques
- Accuracy is the primary evaluation metric across surveyed papers
- Emerging trends include bias mitigation, multilingual scene-text VQA, and graph-based reasoning
- Critical need identified for standardized datasets and expanded research beyond current trends

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VQA systems work by combining visual understanding with natural language processing to answer questions about images. The survey identifies that modern VQA approaches leverage multimodal architectures that process visual features (via CNNs) and textual information (via RNNs or transformers) in parallel, then fuse these representations for reasoning. The effectiveness stems from the complementary strengths of these architectures: CNNs excel at spatial feature extraction, RNNs capture sequential text patterns, and transformers enable complex cross-modal attention mechanisms.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Image -> CNN Backbone -> Visual Features -> Fusion Module -> Reasoning Module -> Answer Generator -> Output

Critical path: Image input flows through CNN backbone for feature extraction, then fuses with processed question embeddings, passes through reasoning module, and generates answer output.

Design tradeoffs: CNN vs transformer backbones (accuracy vs computational cost), fusion strategies (early vs late fusion), reasoning approaches (end-to-end vs modular), and knowledge integration methods (implicit vs explicit).

Failure signatures: Overfitting to dataset biases, poor generalization to novel question types, sensitivity to image quality variations, and degradation with complex reasoning requirements.

First experiments: 1) Baseline accuracy on established VQA benchmarks, 2) Bias detection performance on VQA-CP dataset, 3) Ablation study of different fusion strategies

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The survey identifies several open research directions: the need for more standardized and comprehensive common databases, exploration of underrepresented research domains within VQA, development of more robust evaluation metrics beyond accuracy, and investigation into fairness and accessibility considerations in VQA systems.
