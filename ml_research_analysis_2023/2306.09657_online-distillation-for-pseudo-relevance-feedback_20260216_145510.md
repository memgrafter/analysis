# Online Distillation for Pseudo-Relevance Feedback

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09657
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09657
- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Online Distillation (ODIS), a method for pseudo-relevance feedback that distills a lightweight single-query model from the re-ranking results of a neural model during query processing. The distilled model is designed to approximate the relevance scores of the neural model and can be executed efficiently over an index to retrieve documents missed in the first retrieval stage. ODIS is evaluated on TREC Deep Learning passage ranking datasets and demonstrates favorable performance compared to established pseudo-relevance feedback techniques, dense retrieval methods, and sparse-dense ensemble approaches. Specifically, ODIS improves recall by up to 10.2% while maintaining ranking quality close to the exhaustive neural model search.

## Method Summary
ODIS trains a linear model over TF-IDF token weights to minimize rank-biased score differences from a cross-encoder (CE) re-ranker. The process involves re-ranking top-1000 results from a first-stage retriever with CE, then training a linear model (50 token weights, ℓ1 regularization, ReLU) to mimic CE scores using pairwise preference loss based on reciprocal rank differences. The distilled model is then executed as a query over the full corpus to retrieve new documents, with the union of initial and retrieved results finally re-ranked by CE. The method is evaluated on TREC Deep Learning 2019 and 2020 passage ranking datasets across various retrieval pipelines.

## Key Results
- ODIS improves recall by up to 10.2% compared to baseline methods
- Maintains ranking quality close to exhaustive neural model search with nDCG@1000 scores comparable to baselines
- Outperforms established PRF techniques (RM3, Bo1) and dense retrieval methods in terms of both recall and ranking quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: A linear combination of TF-IDF-weighted terms can effectively approximate the ranking behavior of a complex neural cross-encoder model.
- Mechanism: The distilled model learns term weights that replicate the pairwise preference ordering produced by the cross-encoder. By minimizing rank-biased score differences, the linear model captures the essential relevance signals without the computational cost of the neural model.
- Core assumption: The neural model's ranking decisions can be represented as a linear function of term weights for each query, even though the original model uses complex attention mechanisms.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Indeed, we find that a lexical model distilled online can reasonably replicate the re-ranking of a neural model."
  - [section] "We observe that ODIS has a strong ability to approximate various leading retrieval pipelines in ranking relevant documents."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss PRF techniques but don't specifically address linear approximation of neural rankings.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The online distilled model can identify new relevant documents that were missed in the initial retrieval stage.
- Mechanism: By executing the distilled model over the entire corpus, documents that were ranked highly by the neural model but missed by the initial retrieval can be retrieved and added to the re-ranking pool, improving recall.
- Core assumption: The distilled model generalizes beyond the specific documents it was trained on to identify other relevant documents in the corpus.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "These models can be used as queries that execute efficiently on indexes. This second retrieval stage can enrich the pool of documents for re-ranking by identifying documents that were missed in the first retrieval stage."
  - [section] "We observe consistent and significant performance improvements, especially with respect to the recall of missing relevant documents."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss PRF but don't specifically address the ability to retrieve new relevant documents beyond the initial pool.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The sparsity regularization ensures the distilled model remains executable as an efficient query against the index.
- Mechanism: ℓ1 regularization pushes term weights toward zero, limiting the number of non-zero terms and ensuring the model can be executed efficiently. The model automatically increases regularization if convergence occurs without achieving target sparsity.
- Core assumption: A sparse model with limited non-zero term weights can still capture the essential relevance signals needed for effective retrieval.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we add a ℓ1 regularisation component to the loss, which pushes parameter weights towards zero. The weight of this component with respect to the data loss is controlled by hyperparameter 𝑟."
  - [section] "if the model converges without the desired sparsity, the model will continue to train, but with a higher regularisation rate"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Cross-encoder (teacher model) → Distillation process → Linear TF-IDF model (student) → Index execution → Final re-ranking with cross-encoder
- Critical path: Initial retrieval → Cross-encoder re-ranking → Online distillation training → Secondary retrieval with distilled model → Final cross-encoder scoring
- Design tradeoffs: Simplicity and efficiency of linear model vs. potential loss of complex relevance signals; online training overhead vs. improved recall; sparsity constraints vs. model expressiveness
- Failure signatures: Poor RBO scores between distilled and original rankings; negligible improvement in recall; excessive training time per query; failure to achieve target sparsity
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test distillation quality by comparing RBO between original cross-encoder rankings and distilled model rankings on a held-out query set

  3. Evaluate computational overhead by measuring average training time per query and ensuring it remains practical for real-time use

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can ODIS effectively distill dense query vectors or non-linear student models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that ODIS could be applied over dense document embedding features to find a strong query vector for dense retrieval, but leaves this exploration to future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates ODIS using token-level TF-IDF features and linear combinations, not exploring dense or non-linear models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing ODIS performance when distilling dense vectors or using non-linear student models versus the current linear approach.

### Open Question 2

- Why unresolved: This is proposed as a potential future direction but not investigated in the current work.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing improved performance of first-stage query rewriting when using ODIS-generated models as training data compared to other methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What techniques can reduce the computational overhead of ODIS distillation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that ODIS adds computational overhead compared to baselines in the query rewriting process, taking 98ms per query on average.
- Why unresolved: While the overhead is quantified, the paper does not propose or test methods to reduce it.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of optimization techniques that significantly reduce ODIS distillation time while maintaining effectiveness.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of ODIS depends critically on the assumption that a linear combination of TF-IDF terms can approximate complex neural ranking decisions, with limited analysis of performance across different query types.
- The sparsity constraint may limit the model's ability to capture all relevant signals, potentially creating a trade-off between execution efficiency and ranking quality that isn't fully explored.
- Computational overhead of online training per query, while mentioned as practical, lacks detailed benchmarking across different hardware configurations.

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Linear approximation of neural rankings): Medium - The paper demonstrates reasonable RBO scores but doesn't provide extensive analysis of when the linear approximation succeeds or fails.
- Mechanism 2 (Improved recall through additional retrieval): High - Clear quantitative improvements in recall metrics are shown across multiple baselines.
- Mechanism 3 (Sparsity regularization effectiveness): Low - The sparsity mechanism is described but lacks empirical validation of its necessity or optimal settings.
