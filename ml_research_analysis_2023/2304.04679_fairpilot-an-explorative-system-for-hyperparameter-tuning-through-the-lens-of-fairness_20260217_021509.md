# FairPilot: An Explorative System for Hyperparameter Tuning through the Lens of Fairness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04679
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04679
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FairPilot reveals trade-offs between fairness and accuracy through interactive Pareto frontier visualizations

## Executive Summary
FairPilot is a system that addresses the challenge of selecting machine learning models in high-risk decision-making domains while considering fairness. The system uses a grid-search approach to explore all combinations of model and hyperparameter settings, then displays the Pareto frontier of fairness-performance trade-offs as an interactive visualization map. Users can explore individual model Pareto frontiers, multi-model comparisons, and superimposed frontiers across different fairness metrics, with real-time hyperparameter impact visualization. In a case study using the ELS dataset for college degree prediction with race as a sensitive attribute, FairPilot revealed that achieving a 25% increase in fairness (Predictive Equality) required sacrificing 10% accuracy, while for Equal Opportunity, a 7% fairness gain required only 3% accuracy loss.

## Method Summary
FairPilot employs an exhaustive grid-search approach during preprocessing to train and evaluate every combination of model and hyperparameter settings. The system computes multiple fairness metrics alongside accuracy for each configuration, then constructs Pareto frontiers to identify optimal trade-offs between competing objectives. Results are indexed and stored for rapid interactive exploration through a web interface. The preprocessing phase systematically explores the full factorial space of models (Decision Tree, Random Forest, Logistic Regression, SVC) and their hyperparameters, with cross-validation to ensure robust evaluation. The interactive visualization allows users to explore individual model Pareto frontiers, compare multiple models simultaneously, and superimpose different fairness metrics to understand how various definitions of fairness affect model selection.

## Key Results
- FairPilot revealed that achieving a 25% increase in Predictive Equality required sacrificing 10% accuracy
- For Equal Opportunity, a 7% fairness gain required only 3% accuracy loss
- The system successfully visualized Pareto frontiers showing optimal trade-offs between different fairness metrics and accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FairPilot reduces cognitive load in model selection by precomputing all hyperparameter combinations and storing them for interactive exploration.
- Mechanism: The system uses a grid-search approach during preprocessing to train and evaluate every combination of model and hyperparameter settings, then indexes these results for rapid Pareto frontier visualization.
- Core assumption: The space of model/hyperparameter combinations is independent of the space of evaluation criteria (fairness and accuracy metrics).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The core method uses a grid-search approach to explore the full factorial of model and hyperparameter combinations"
  - [section 3] "the space of the model/hyperparameter combinations is independent of the space of model evaluation criteria"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in corpus about grid-search preprocessing
- Break condition: If the hyperparameter space is too large for exhaustive search, the preprocessing becomes computationally infeasible and the approach fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: FairPilot enables responsible model selection by making trade-offs between fairness and accuracy transparent and interactive.
- Mechanism: By displaying Pareto frontiers that show the optimal trade-offs between different fairness metrics and accuracy, users can visually compare models and choose based on their specific application needs and fairness priorities.
- Core assumption: Users can effectively interpret Pareto frontiers and make informed decisions based on visual trade-off representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "displays the Pareto frontier of fairness-performance trade-offs as an interactive visualization map"
  - [section 5.2] "users can select groups of points on the legend to remove them from the visualization" and "modify the hyperparameters that determine the size, color, and shape of the points in real time"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence about Pareto frontier visualization effectiveness
- Break condition: If users lack understanding of Pareto optimality concepts, they may misinterpret the visualizations and make suboptimal choices.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: FairPilot democratizes ML by making complex model selection accessible to practitioners without deep ML expertise.
- Mechanism: The interactive web interface with dropdown menus, default hyperparameter values, and real-time visualization allows non-experts to explore model options without needing to understand underlying algorithms.
- Core assumption: Practitioners can effectively use the interface to explore and understand model trade-offs without deep technical knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "democratizing ML tools and fairness consideration are crucial" and "FairPilot is the first system to combine these features"
  - [section 5.1] "The user is then asked to specify the sensitive attributes" and "Our system provides default values for each"
  - [section 2] "make machine learning more accessible to non-experts" as a research goal
- Break condition: If the interface complexity exceeds the user's technical background, the democratization goal fails and users may make poor model selections.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Web interface (Flask + Plotly) -> User interaction and visualization
  - Preprocessing engine (Python 3.10.9) -> Grid search over hyperparameters
  - Model evaluation pipeline -> Trains and evaluates models with cross-validation
  - Data storage layer -> Indexes model results for interactive querying
  - Fairness metrics module -> Computes various fairness definitions

- Critical path:
  1. User uploads dataset and configures model/hyperparameter settings
  2. Preprocessing engine performs exhaustive grid search training
  3. Results are indexed and stored in database
  4. User explores Pareto frontiers through interactive visualizations
  5. User selects optimal model based on their trade-off preferences

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Exhaustive grid search vs. more efficient optimization (Bayesian optimization) - comprehensiveness vs. scalability
  - Real-time interactivity vs. preprocessing time - immediate exploration vs. computational cost
  - Multiple fairness metrics vs. simplicity - comprehensive fairness evaluation vs. user cognitive load

- Failure signatures:
  - Long preprocessing times indicating too large a hyperparameter space
  - Poor model performance suggesting suboptimal hyperparameter ranges
  - Visualizations that don't clearly show trade-offs indicating evaluation metric issues
  - System crashes during grid search suggesting memory constraints

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test with a small synthetic dataset and one model type to verify grid search functionality and basic Pareto frontier visualization
  2. Test with a real dataset but limit to 2-3 hyperparameter combinations to verify the full pipeline works before scaling up
  3. Test the interactive visualization features with mock data to ensure the UI components (hover, selection, color coding) function correctly

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the relative performance differences between Pareto frontier optimization and other hyperparameter tuning methods (Bayesian optimization, random search) when considering fairness metrics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions MOBO as a future direction and compares to random search in the introduction, but doesn't evaluate different optimization strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses grid search and doesn't benchmark against other optimization methods that might be more efficient for fairness-aware hyperparameter tuning.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing grid search, random search, and Bayesian optimization methods on the same datasets and fairness metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the computational cost scale with dataset size and number of hyperparameters in FairPilot?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that training ML models is resource-hungry and that the exponential space of hyperparameters makes model selection overwhelming, but doesn't provide complexity analysis.
- Why unresolved: No analysis of time/space complexity or scaling behavior with respect to dataset size, number of models, or hyperparameter combinations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical runtime analysis across different dataset sizes and hyperparameter grid resolutions, plus theoretical complexity analysis.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How transferable are the identified Pareto optimal hyperparameters across different datasets or domains?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on a single ELS dataset case study without testing generalizability or transferability of results.
- Why unresolved: No experiments showing whether optimal hyperparameters found for one dataset/domain transfer to others, which would impact practical deployment.
- What evidence would resolve it: Cross-domain validation experiments showing performance when applying hyperparameters optimized on one dataset to others with similar characteristics.

## Limitations
- The grid-search approach may become computationally prohibitive with large hyperparameter spaces or datasets
- The paper lacks user study validation showing whether practitioners actually make better decisions using the system
- The system requires 9.6 hours of preprocessing for 7,700 model configurations, suggesting scalability concerns

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The technical implementation of Pareto frontier computation and visualization is sound and follows established methods in multi-objective optimization
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that FairPilot enables responsible model selection by making trade-offs transparent is supported by the visualization design but lacks user study validation
- **Low Confidence**: The democratization claim that non-experts can effectively use the system without deep ML expertise is stated but not empirically tested
