# Robust Path Following on Rivers Using Bootstrapped Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.15178
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15178
- **Reference count**: 14
- **Primary result**: DRL agent achieves cross-track errors up to 4.36m versus 26.30m for PID controller

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a Deep Reinforcement Learning agent for autonomous surface vessel navigation on inland waterways. The approach uses a bootstrapped Q-learning algorithm (KEBDQN) with 10 ensemble heads and kernel-based target selection to handle environmental uncertainty in river currents and depth variations. The agent demonstrates superior performance compared to PID controllers on real-world river data from the lower and middle Rhine, achieving up to 6x reduction in cross-track error while maintaining robust performance across varying vessel speeds and propeller revolutions.

## Method Summary
The method employs a bootstrapped DQN-based algorithm (KEBDQN) with a versatile training environment generator to create diverse river scenarios. The state space includes vessel dynamics (position, course, speed) and environmental factors (water depth, current attack angle), with discrete rudder angle actions limited to ±2° changes. The agent is trained for 3 million steps on generated rivers and evaluated against a PID controller tuned via Particle Swarm Optimization on real-world Rhine river data. The reward structure penalizes deviations from the path and course errors while encouraging efficient navigation.

## Key Results
- DRL agent achieves cross-track errors up to 4.36 meters compared to 26.30 meters for PID controller
- The agent successfully adapts to varying propeller revolutions and vessel speeds during testing
- Demonstrated robustness across real-world river scenarios from the lower and middle Rhine

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bootstrapped DQN provides higher robustness by sampling Q-value distributions instead of point estimates, reducing overestimation bias in uncertain river environments.
- Mechanism: The ensemble of 10 Q-network heads with kernel-based target selection creates multiple estimates of Q-values for each state-action pair, allowing the agent to capture epistemic uncertainty in river current and depth variations.
- Core assumption: River environments have sufficient variability during training that ensemble disagreement reflects meaningful uncertainty about navigation decisions.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "state-of-the-art bootstrapped Q-learning algorithm to generate robust and generalizable policies" and [section] "Waltz and Okhrin (2022) propose to replace the maximum over all possible actions in the target with a kernel-based testing procedure"
- Break condition: If river training scenarios lack sufficient diversity, ensemble disagreement becomes meaningless noise rather than useful uncertainty signal.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Kernel-based target selection (KEBDQN) outperforms standard DQN by using distributional statistics rather than max-operator for bootstrapping targets.
- Mechanism: Instead of selecting max Q-value across actions, KEBDQN computes a kernel-weighted average of Q-values where weights depend on statistical tests comparing each head's estimate to the maximum.
- Core assumption: The river navigation problem has sufficient noise and non-stationarity that overestimation bias significantly impacts learning efficiency and final policy quality.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "Waltz and Okhrin (2022) propose to replace the maximum over all possible actions in the target with a kernel-based testing procedure"
- Break condition: In deterministic or low-noise environments, the additional complexity of kernel-based selection provides no benefit over simpler max-operator.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: State representation including water depth and current attack angle enables effective generalization across diverse river conditions.
- Mechanism: By encoding normalized relative water depth and current attack angle in the state vector, the agent learns to anticipate how environmental factors affect vessel dynamics and adjust control strategy accordingly.
- Core assumption: The relative water depth normalized by maximum depth and current attack angle are sufficient statistics for capturing environmental influence on maneuvering.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "The second part encodes information about the surroundings of the agent: 𝑠𝑒 = (̃ 𝑦𝑒,𝑡, ̃ 𝑦𝑒,𝑡−1, 𝜒 𝑒,𝑡 𝜒𝑒,𝑡−1, ℎ𝑡−𝑑/max(ℎ), 𝛾 𝑟𝑒𝑙)⊤"
- Break condition: If current direction and speed information is incomplete or if depth variations don't significantly impact vessel dynamics in the tested scenarios.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Environment generator → State preprocessor → KEBDQN agent (shared core + 10 heads) → Action limiter → Vessel simulator → Reward calculator
- Critical path: State observation → KEBDQN forward pass → Action selection → Environment step → Reward calculation → Replay buffer → Training update
- Design tradeoffs: Discrete actions (limited to ±2° changes) vs. continuous actions (risk of physically impossible rudder commands); ensemble size (10 heads) vs. computational cost; state dimensionality vs. learning efficiency
- Failure signatures: High variance in Q-value estimates across heads suggests insufficient training diversity; consistently large cross-track errors indicate poor generalization; rudder jittering suggests action space discretization issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run KEBDQN and DQN side-by-side on identical training rivers, compare learning curves and final cross-track error distributions
  2. Test robustness by evaluating trained policies on rivers with different current profiles and depth distributions than training data
  3. Perform ablation study removing water depth and current attack angle from state representation to measure importance of environmental awareness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions but leaves several important areas unexplored, including comprehensive comparisons to other state-of-the-art RL algorithms and systematic evaluation of performance scaling with environmental complexity.

## Limitations

- Lacks direct comparisons to alternative uncertainty-aware RL methods (e.g., ensemble-DQN, Bayesian RL) to definitively establish bootstrapped KEBDQN's superiority for river navigation
- Claims about generalization to "never-seen scenarios" are supported by real-world validation but lack quantitative comparison to alternative approaches
- No ablation studies on the importance of individual state features or comparison of performance scaling with environmental complexity

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The DRL agent outperforms the PID controller on tested river scenarios, with demonstrated cross-track error reduction from 26.30m to 4.36m
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism by which bootstrapped Q-learning provides robustness (through ensemble disagreement capturing epistemic uncertainty) is plausible but not definitively proven without comparison to non-bootstrapped variants
- **Medium confidence**: The generalization claims are supported by real-world validation but lack comprehensive testing across diverse environmental conditions beyond the Rhine river data

## Next Checks

1. Compare KEBDQN performance against standard DQN and ensemble-DQN on identical training environments and metrics to quantify the specific benefit of kernel-based target selection
2. Conduct systematic ablation studies removing individual state features (water depth, current attack angle) to quantify their contribution to policy performance and generalization
3. Test the trained policies on river scenarios with environmental conditions (current speeds, depth profiles) that differ significantly from the training distribution to rigorously evaluate true generalization capabilities
