# JEIT: Joint End-to-End Model and Internal Language Model Training for Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08583
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08583
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: JEIT achieves up to 10.2% relative WER reduction and CJJT achieves up to 16.4% relative WER reduction on rare-word test sets compared to baseline models

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes JEIT (Joint End-to-End model and Internal Language Model training), a method that injects unpaired text into the internal language model during E2E training to improve rare-word speech recognition. The approach computes E2E loss on audio-transcript pairs while the ILM estimates cross-entropy loss on unpaired text, training the model to minimize a weighted sum of both losses. Experiments on Google's large-scale streaming production task demonstrate that JEIT and combined JEIT/JOIST training (CJJT) achieve significant improvements on rare-word test sets while maintaining source-domain performance.

## Method Summary
JEIT trains an E2E model and its internal language model jointly by minimizing a weighted sum of E2E loss (computed on audio-transcript pairs) and ILM loss (computed as cross-entropy on unpaired text). The method uses Modular Hybrid Autoregressive Transducer (MHAT) architecture, which introduces a blank decoder for more robust text-only adaptation compared to standard HAT. For maximum effectiveness, JEIT is combined with JOIST (which injects unpaired text through the encoder), creating CJJT that benefits from both encoder and decoder text injection approaches.

## Key Results
- JEIT achieves up to 10.2% relative WER reduction on rare-word test sets compared to baseline models
- CJJT (combined JEIT and JOIST) achieves up to 16.4% relative WER reduction on rare-word test sets
- MHAT architecture outperforms HAT in the JEIT framework and shows greater robustness during ILM adaptation
- Models maintain source-domain WER performance while improving rare-word recognition

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint training of E2E model and ILM (JEIT) improves rare-word recognition by injecting unpaired text into ILM during E2E training.
- Mechanism: The E2E model computes E2E loss on audio-transcript pairs while its ILM estimates a cross-entropy loss on unpaired text. The model is trained to minimize a weighted sum of both losses, allowing ILM to absorb knowledge from unpaired text while E2E training serves as regularization.
- Core assumption: The ILM can effectively learn from unpaired text during joint training without degrading the overall E2E ASR performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "With JEIT, the E2E model computes an E2E loss on audio-transcript pairs while its ILM estimates a cross-entropy loss on unpaired text."
  - [section 3]: "Our goal is to improve the ASR accuracy on rare-word test sets by making use of large-scale unpaired text while maintaining WER on source-domain task... JEIT minimizes a weighted sum of the E2E loss and ILM loss below"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus provides related papers but lacks direct experimental validation of JEIT's mechanism.
- Break condition: If the ILM becomes too strong relative to the E2E model, it may degrade overall ASR performance, especially in HAT architecture without structural factorization.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: MHAT architecture outperforms HAT in the JEIT framework due to structural separation of ILM score prediction.
- Mechanism: MHAT introduces a blank decoder that generates label embeddings for blank posterior calculation, while the label decoder generates embeddings for label posterior. This separation allows MHAT to work with increasingly stronger ILMs without losing E2E ASR functionality.
- Core assumption: Structural separation of ILM score prediction from acoustic model score predictions is crucial for robust text-only adaptation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We also show that modular hybrid autoregressive transducer (MHAT) performs better than HAT in the JEIT framework, and is much more robust than HAT during ILM adaptation."
  - [section 2.2]: "To achieve more robust text-only adaptation, we proposed MHAT... introduces a blank decoder that takes in the same previous labels as the label decoder to generate the current label embeddings"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers discuss similar architectures but don't specifically validate MHAT's superiority in JEIT context.
- Break condition: If the blank decoder and label decoder become too coupled or if the projection matrices are not properly initialized, MHAT may not maintain its advantage over HAT.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining JEIT with JOIST (CJJT) achieves additive improvements by injecting unpaired text through both encoder and decoder.
- Mechanism: JEIT injects unpaired text into the label decoder to minimize E2E and ILM losses, while JOIST injects it through the encoder to minimize E2E losses. Combining them allows both approaches to contribute to improved ASR performance.
- Core assumption: Text injection into encoder and decoder are complementary and their improvements are additive.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "To push the limit of unpaired text injection, we further propose a combined JEIT and JOIST training (CJJT) that benefits from modality matching, encoder text injection and ILM training."
  - [section 3]: "To improve rare-word recognition, JEIT injects unpaired text into the label decoder... while JOIST injects it through the encoder... We show in the experiments that JEIT and JOIST are complementary to each other and CJJT achieves better ASR performance than either method alone."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers discuss text injection methods but lack experimental validation of their combination.
- Break condition: If the weighting between JEIT and JOIST losses is not properly tuned, or if the encoder and decoder text injection interfere with each other, CJJT may not achieve the expected additive improvements.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Cross-entropy loss minimization
  - Why needed here: The ILM loss is computed as the summed negative log probability of all label sequences predicted by ILM on unpaired text.
  - Quick check question: How does minimizing cross-entropy loss help the ILM learn from unpaired text?

- Concept: Kullback-Leibler divergence regularization
  - Why needed here: KLD regularization is used in ILM adaptation methods to maintain source-domain ASR performance, but JEIT avoids this by combining adaptation into single-stage training.
  - Quick check question: What role does KLD regularization play in ILM adaptation, and why can JEIT avoid it?

- Concept: Modality matching
  - Why needed here: JOIST uses modality matching to map unpaired text to a latent space shared by speech and text, allowing text data to benefit the entire E2E model.
  - Quick check question: How does modality matching enable unpaired text to improve ASR performance through the encoder?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - HAT/MHAT: E2E model with encoder, decoder(s), and joint network
  - ILM: Internal language model within E2E model
  - JOIST: Method for encoder text injection
  - JEIT: Method for decoder text injection
  - CJJT: Combined JEIT and JOIST training

- Critical path:
  1. Joint training of E2E model and ILM with unpaired text
  2. Computation of E2E loss on audio-transcript pairs
  3. Computation of ILM loss on unpaired text
  4. Minimization of weighted sum of E2E and ILM losses
  5. (Optional) Combining with JOIST for encoder text injection

- Design tradeoffs:
  - JEIT vs. ILMA: JEIT simplifies two-stage ILMA into single-stage training but requires careful tuning of ILM loss weight
  - HAT vs. MHAT: MHAT provides better robustness during ILM adaptation but introduces additional complexity with blank decoder
  - JOIST vs. JEIT: JOIST injects text through encoder while JEIT uses decoder; combining them (CJJT) may provide additive benefits but increases training complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - Degradation in source-domain WER while improving rare-word WER
  - Overfitting to unpaired text, leading to poor generalization
  - Instability during training, especially when ILM loss weight is not properly tuned

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement JEIT with HAT and compare WER on rare-word test sets vs. baseline
  2. Switch to MHAT and evaluate robustness during ILM adaptation
  3. Combine JEIT with JOIST (CJJT) and measure additive improvements on rare-word recognition

- Question: How does JEIT performance scale with different amounts of unpaired text data beyond 100B sentences?

- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't test performance with more than 100B unpaired sentences to determine if improvements continue to scale
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing WER improvements on rare-word test sets when training with 200B, 500B, or 1T unpaired sentences

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the exact mechanism by which MHAT's modular structure enables better ILM adaptation compared to HAT?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows MHAT outperforms HAT during ILMA and JEIT, attributing it to "structural factorization" that allows "structurally independent ILM"
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the empirical advantage but doesn't provide detailed analysis of why the structural separation specifically enables better adaptation
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies comparing intermediate representations, ILM behavior analysis, or theoretical explanation of how modularization prevents interference

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of JEIT and CJJT compare when applied to non-streaming ASR architectures?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on streaming HAT and MHAT architectures with cascaded encoders, but doesn't test non-streaming alternatives
- Why unresolved: All experiments use streaming models, so it's unclear if the benefits transfer to non-streaming architectures that might have different encoder-decoder interactions
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing JEIT/CJJT performance on non-streaming RNN-T or transformer-based ASR models with different architectural constraints

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to a single large-scale production system, restricting generalizability to other domains or smaller deployments
- Requires 100B unpaired text tokens, raising practical concerns for domains with limited text resources
- Computational overhead implications of joint training approach are not addressed

## Confidence

**High Confidence Claims:**
- JEIT effectively improves rare-word recognition with consistent WER reductions across multiple test sets
- CJJT provides additive improvements over either method alone, with 16.4% relative WER reduction on rare-word test sets
- MHAT architecture demonstrates superior performance and robustness compared to HAT in JEIT framework

**Medium Confidence Claims:**
- JEIT avoids the need for KLD regularization used in ILM adaptation methods through single-stage training
- JEIT simplifies the two-stage ILMA process into single-stage training, though training efficiency analysis is limited

**Low Confidence Claims:**
- Specific mechanisms by which MHAT's blank decoder contributes to robustness are not fully elucidated
- Claim of complementary improvements assumes independence between encoder and decoder injection methods without thorough exploration of potential interactions
