# Robustness Benchmark of Road User Trajectory Prediction Models for Automated Driving

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01895
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01895
- Reference count: 3
- Key outcome: Training models with perturbations reduces performance degradation for known perturbations, but data augmentation cannot guarantee robustness to unforeseen perturbations.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a robustness benchmark for road user trajectory prediction models in automated driving. The study evaluates four models (Constant Velocity, LSTM, MotionCNN, and MultiPath++) against three realistic perturbations: missing road information, late detections, and noisy heading angles. Results show significant performance degradation under these perturbations, with minADE increases up to +1444.8%. Training models with similar perturbations effectively reduces degradation, with error increases of up to +87.5%. However, the study argues that data augmentation through perturbations during training cannot guarantee robustness towards unforeseen perturbations. The work also highlights cases where degraded inputs lead to more accurate predictions, suggesting that models may not learn the correct relationships in the data. Future work will focus on analyzing the impact of perturbation severity, evaluating in-vehicle robustness enhancement, and further examining cases with improved predictions using degraded inputs.

## Method Summary
The study benchmarks four trajectory prediction models (Constant Velocity, LSTM, MotionCNN, and MultiPath++) using the Waymo Open Motion Dataset. The models are evaluated on their ability to predict vehicle, pedestrian, and cyclist trajectories under three realistic perturbations: missing road information, late detections, and noisy heading angles. Performance is measured using minimum average displacement error (minADE). Models are first trained and tested on the original dataset to establish baselines. Then, perturbations are applied to the test data to assess degradation. Finally, models are retrained with perturbed data to evaluate the effectiveness of robustness training.

## Key Results
- Performance degradation of up to +1444.8% in minADE when models are evaluated on perturbed data without robustness training.
- Training with perturbations effectively reduces performance degradation, with error increases of up to +87.5%.
- Noisy heading angles cause the most severe degradation, with minADE increases of +560.8% to +1444.8%.
- Some cases show improved predictions with degraded inputs, suggesting models may not learn correct data relationships.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training with perturbations reduces prediction errors for known perturbations.
- Mechanism: Data augmentation through perturbed samples exposes models to variations they might encounter during deployment, enabling them to learn to generalize over these variations.
- Core assumption: Perturbations applied during training are representative of real-world variations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Training the models with similar perturbations effectively reduces performance degradation, with error increases of up to +87.5%."
  - [section] "Training with perturbed data for the identified perturbations effectively mitigates performance degradation"
- Break condition: If perturbations do not cover the full range of real-world variations, or if they introduce unrealistic data distributions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Missing road information sometimes improves predictions.
- Mechanism: Models may be over-reliant on road information and learn spurious correlations. Removing road information forces models to exploit other available features, leading to better predictions when road data is noisy or misleading.
- Core assumption: Road information is not always the most informative feature for trajectory prediction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Furthermore, degrading the inputs sometimes leads to more accurate predictions, suggesting that the models are unable to learn the true relationships between the different elements in the data."
  - [section] "There is sufficient information to make significantly better predictions even without any road information"
- Break condition: If road information is the primary determinant of trajectory, removing it will consistently degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Noisy heading angles cause severe prediction errors.
- Mechanism: Models transform inputs to the target's coordinate frame using the last observed heading angle. A highly noisy heading angle causes the entire scene to be rotated incorrectly, leading to invalid predictions.
- Core assumption: Models are not trained to handle heading angle errors beyond a certain threshold.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "If the entire scene is rotated with a wrong angle, they are unable to produce sensible predictions."
  - [section] "highly noisy heading angle measurements, which could be caused by faulty sensors or adverse weather conditions"
- Break condition: If models are explicitly trained to be robust to heading angle errors, or if heading angle is not used for coordinate frame transformation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input data -> Preprocessing (coordinate frame transformation, scaling) -> Model prediction -> Perturbation application -> Evaluation
- Critical path: Input data → Preprocessing → Model prediction → Perturbation application → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model complexity vs. robustness: Simpler models (CV, LSTM) are less affected by some perturbations but less accurate overall.
  - Training data vs. generalization: Training with perturbations improves robustness to known perturbations but may not generalize to unforeseen ones.
- Failure signatures:
  - Off-road predictions when road information is missing or historical data is limited
  - Erratic predictions when heading angle is noisy
  - Degraded performance when models are not trained with similar perturbations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate each model on the original dataset to establish baseline performance.
  2. Apply each perturbation to the test data and evaluate model performance to quantify degradation.
  3. Retrain each model with perturbed data and evaluate performance on both original and perturbed data to assess robustness improvement.

## Limitations
- The study relies on a single dataset (Waymo Open Motion Dataset), which may limit generalizability to other driving environments.
- Only extreme perturbations are evaluated, without exploring a range of perturbation severities.
- The paper identifies cases where degraded inputs improve predictions but does not provide a complete theoretical explanation for this phenomenon.

- Identification of noisy heading angles as problematic: High
