# A Data-centric Framework for Improving Domain-specific Machine Reading Comprehension Datasets

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.00483
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00483
- **Reference count**: 37
- **Primary result**: Data-centric framework achieves up to 33% and 40% relative improvements in recall@1 and exact match scores for biomedical MRC datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a data-centric framework designed to enhance the quality of domain-specific Machine Reading Comprehension (MRC) datasets, with a focus on biomedical domains. The framework employs three key techniques: negative passage selection, question reformulation via paraphrasing, word substitution, and back translation. When applied to four biomedical MRC datasets, the framework demonstrates substantial improvements in retrieval and reader model performance. The results show that back translation is particularly effective, yielding the highest relative improvements, especially for smaller datasets like BioASQ.

## Method Summary
The framework proposes three main components to improve MRC datasets: (1) negative passage selection to better filter irrelevant passages, (2) question reformulation through paraphrasing to increase diversity, (3) word substitution to enrich vocabulary, and (4) back translation to generate linguistically diverse questions. These techniques are applied to domain-specific datasets, particularly in the biomedical field, to enhance the quality and diversity of training data. The framework is evaluated on retrieval and reader models, with a focus on recall@1 and exact match scores.

## Key Results
- Up to 33% relative improvement in recall@1 scores for retrieval models
- Up to 40% relative improvement in exact match scores for reader models
- Best improvements observed for the smallest dataset (BioASQ) using back translation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework improves MRC datasets by enhancing the quality and diversity of training data through targeted data augmentation techniques. Negative passage selection reduces noise by filtering irrelevant passages, while question reformulation techniques (paraphrasing, word substitution, back translation) increase linguistic diversity and robustness. These improvements lead to better generalization and performance of MRC models, particularly in domain-specific contexts where data scarcity and domain-specific terminology pose challenges.

## Foundational Learning

**Negative Passage Selection**: Filtering out irrelevant passages to reduce noise in training data. Why needed: Improves model focus on relevant information. Quick check: Measure reduction in irrelevant passages post-filtering.

**Question Reformulation via Paraphrasing**: Generating semantically equivalent questions with varied phrasing. Why needed: Increases model robustness to linguistic variations. Quick check: Evaluate semantic similarity between original and paraphrased questions.

**Word Substitution**: Replacing words with synonyms or domain-specific terms. Why needed: Enriches vocabulary and handles domain-specific terminology. Quick check: Verify semantic preservation after substitution.

**Back Translation**: Translating questions to another language and back to the original. Why needed: Generates diverse linguistic structures. Quick check: Assess linguistic diversity metrics pre- and post-translation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data → Negative Passage Selection → Question Reformulation (Paraphrasing/Word Substitution/Back Translation) → Enhanced Dataset → Model Training → Evaluation

**Critical Path**: The core pipeline involves filtering passages, reformulating questions, and training models on the enhanced dataset. The critical path is: Data → Negative Passage Selection → Question Reformulation → Model Training.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The framework balances data quality (via negative passage selection) with diversity (via question reformulation). Tradeoffs include computational cost of back translation versus simpler paraphrasing techniques.

**Failure Signatures**: Over-optimization to specific datasets, potential introduction of artifacts via back translation, and reduced generalizability to non-biomedical domains.

## Limitations
- Potential over-optimization to biomedical datasets, raising generalizability concerns.
- Substantial relative improvements, but absolute performance gains and practical significance are unclear.
- Possible introduction of biases or artifacts through back translation and other augmentation techniques.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Framework's ability to improve dataset quality through negative passage selection and question reformulation is well-supported.
- **Medium confidence**: Back translation yields best improvements, but magnitude may be dataset-dependent.
- **Low confidence**: Generalizability to non-biomedical domains is not sufficiently demonstrated.

## Next Checks
1. Apply the framework to MRC datasets from non-biomedical domains (e.g., news, social media, legal documents) to assess generalizability.
2. Conduct ablation studies to isolate the contribution of each data augmentation technique to observed improvements.
3. Evaluate the impact of enhanced datasets on downstream tasks and user studies to measure practical improvements in MRC system performance and user satisfaction.
