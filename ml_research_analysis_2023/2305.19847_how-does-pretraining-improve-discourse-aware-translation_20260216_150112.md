# How Does Pretraining Improve Discourse-Aware Translation?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19847
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19847
- Authors: Not specified
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Probing tasks show discourse relation knowledge varies by PLM architecture and layer, affecting discourse-aware translation performance

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how discourse knowledge embedded in pretrained language models (PLMs) impacts their effectiveness in spoken language translation. The authors introduce a novel probing task to interpret PLMs' ability to capture discourse relation knowledge and validate three state-of-the-art PLMs (BERT, BART, GPT-2) across different architectures. Through experiments on the IWSLT2017 Chinese-English dataset, they demonstrate that discourse elements create varying learning difficulties for PLMs and that NMT models initialized from different PLM layers exhibit consistent trends with probing task results. The findings provide insights into how and when discourse knowledge in PLMs should be leveraged for downstream translation tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a two-stage methodology: first, they use probing tasks with frozen PLMs and trainable MLPs to evaluate discourse relation classification accuracy across different layers and architectures using the PDTB2.0 dataset. Second, they leverage these PLMs in NMT models by initializing encoders and decoders with different PLM layers, comparing architectures (BERT, GPT-2, BART) and language versions (English, Chinese, multilingual). Translation performance is evaluated using BLEU, TER, and METEOR scores on the IWSLT2017 Chinese-English dataset, with fine-grained analysis of how individual layers affect discourse-aware translation quality.

## Key Results
- Discourse modeling ability varies significantly across PLM architectures and layers, with encoder-decoder models performing best on higher layers
- Explicit discourse relations are easier for PLMs to capture than implicit ones, with explicit relations achieving much higher accuracy
- Source language PLMs provide more significant benefits to discourse-aware translation than target language PLMs, with multilingual-BERT showing substantial improvements

## Limitations

- The analysis relies on probing tasks as a proxy for discourse knowledge, but the relationship between probing accuracy and actual translation quality remains correlational rather than causal.
- The study focuses on a single language pair (Chinese-English) and a specific domain (spoken language translation), limiting generalizability to other translation tasks.
- The mechanism by which discourse knowledge from PLMs actually improves translation quality is not directly demonstrated.

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The observation that different PLM architectures and layers exhibit varying discourse relation classification accuracy is well-supported by empirical results. The finding that explicit discourse relations are easier to capture than implicit ones is also strongly evidenced.

**Medium Confidence:** The claim that source language PLMs provide more significant benefits than target language PLMs for discourse-aware translation is supported by experimental results but lacks theoretical justification for why this should be the case.

**Low Confidence:** The mechanism by which discourse knowledge from PLMs actually improves translation quality is not directly demonstrated. The paper shows correlations between probing accuracy and translation performance but does not provide evidence that discourse knowledge specifically causes these improvements.

## Next Checks

1. **Causal Validation Test:** Design an ablation study that systematically removes discourse-relevant phenomena from the test set and measures whether translation performance changes correlate with the discourse knowledge levels indicated by probing tasks.

2. **Cross-Lingual Generalization:** Replicate the probing and translation experiments across multiple language pairs (e.g., English-French, English-German) to determine if the observed patterns of PLM effectiveness generalize beyond the Chinese-English pair.

3. **Discourse-Specific Evaluation:** Implement targeted evaluation metrics that specifically measure translation quality of discourse phenomena (anaphora resolution, coherence markers, discourse connectives) rather than general translation quality metrics to establish a clearer link between discourse knowledge and discourse-aware translation performance.
