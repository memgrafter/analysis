# Defining and Explorting the Intelligence Space

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06499
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06499
- Authors: Not specified
- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This article presents a trilevel definition of intelligence—immediate, cumulative, and full-spectrum—to address the challenge of defining intelligence across natural and artificial forms. The framework constructs a hierarchy of intelligence concepts mapped onto an "intelligence space" that includes approximations to these levels. The analysis identifies regions corresponding to human intelligence, AI, and humanlike intelligence, exploring implications for the singularity, generative AI, ethics, and intellectual property. The framework reveals that LLMs approximate immediate and cumulative intelligence but fall short of full-spectrum intelligence due to limitations in awareness, collaboration, and socialization. The article argues that current AI systems cannot be considered creators of intellectual property due to lack of intentionality.

## Method Summary
The article develops a trilevel intelligence framework by constructing a hierarchy of intelligence concepts and mapping them onto a broader intelligence space. This involves defining immediate intelligence (real-time processing), cumulative intelligence (knowledge accumulation), and full-spectrum intelligence (complete awareness, collaboration, and socialization). The framework then identifies regions within this space that correspond to different forms of intelligence, using qualitative analysis to compare human intelligence, AI systems, and humanlike intelligence approximations.

## Key Results
- LLMs approximate immediate and cumulative intelligence but lack full-spectrum capabilities
- Intelligence space framework identifies distinct regions for human, artificial, and humanlike intelligence
- Current AI systems cannot be considered intellectual property creators due to lack of intentionality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by providing a structured hierarchy that captures different dimensions of intelligence rather than attempting a single, all-encompassing definition. By decomposing intelligence into immediate, cumulative, and full-spectrum levels, it creates a navigable space where different entities (humans, AI systems, hypothetical constructs) can be positioned based on their capabilities. This hierarchical approach allows for meaningful comparisons between natural and artificial intelligence while acknowledging their distinct characteristics and limitations.
