# Investigating model performance in language identification: beyond simple error statistics

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the performance of language identification (LID) systems on the MERLIon CCS Challenge dataset, which features accented English-Mandarin code-switched child-directed speech. The study goes beyond simple error statistics to analyze how well LID systems perform on individual recordings and speech units with different linguistic properties. The authors find that LID systems perform better on files with larger proportions of English than on files with larger proportions of Mandarin, and particularly struggle with identifying the language in segments shorter than 2 seconds. The presence of vernacular words impacts LID accuracy differently for English and Mandarin, with systems tending to struggle with English segments mixed with vernacular words. The study recommends adopting additional metrics during system evaluation to ascertain the individual variation in error rates for automatic speech processing systems.

## Method Summary
The study analyzes the performance of 8 LID systems (7 submitted systems and 2 baselines) on the MERLIon CCS Challenge dataset. The authors evaluate system performance on individual recordings and speech units with different linguistic properties, such as proportion of Mandarin, presence of vernacular words, degree of child-directed speech, duration of language segments, and rate of code-switching. The authors use various metrics to assess the LID systems' performance, including utterance-level accuracy, per-language error rates, and F1 scores. The study also investigates the impact of different linguistic properties on LID accuracy and provides recommendations for adopting additional metrics during system evaluation.

## Key Results
- LID systems perform better on files with larger proportions of English than on files with larger proportions of Mandarin.
- LID systems struggle with identifying the language in segments shorter than 2 seconds.
- The presence of vernacular words impacts LID accuracy differently for English and Mandarin, with systems tending to struggle with English segments mixed with vernacular words.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study's findings suggest that LID systems' performance is influenced by various linguistic properties of the input speech, such as the proportion of languages, presence of vernacular words, and segment duration. The authors argue that understanding these factors is crucial for improving LID systems' performance and developing more robust models. By analyzing the performance of LID systems on individual recordings and speech units with different linguistic properties, the study provides insights into the challenges faced by current LID systems and highlights the need for adopting additional metrics during system evaluation.
