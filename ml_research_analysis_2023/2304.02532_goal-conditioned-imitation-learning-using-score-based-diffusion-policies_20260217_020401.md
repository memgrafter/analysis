# Goal-Conditioned Imitation Learning using Score-based Diffusion Policies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02532
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02532
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Achieves 0.94/3.69 reward/result on Relay Kitchen vs 0.86/2.69 for Diffusion-X with 3 denoising steps

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces BESO, a score-based diffusion policy for goal-conditioned imitation learning from uncurated play data. BESO leverages the expressiveness of score-based diffusion models to learn multimodal action distributions without requiring hierarchical structures or additional clustering. It uses a decoupled SDM architecture with a transformer backbone, enabling fast action generation in just 3 denoising steps. The method also supports learning a goal-independent policy via classifier-free guidance.

## Method Summary
BESO employs a score-based diffusion model as a policy that learns from uncurated play data containing state-action trajectories. The model uses a transformer backbone to encode state sequences, actions, goals, and noise levels, with skip connections to handle different noise levels. Training employs denoising score matching with an exponential noise scheduler. For fast inference, BESO uses deterministic ODE solvers (DDIM) requiring only 3 denoising steps. Classifier-free guidance is implemented by randomly dropping out the goal during training, enabling simultaneous learning of goal-dependent and goal-independent policies.

## Key Results
- Achieves 0.94/3.69 reward/result on Relay Kitchen benchmark vs 0.86/2.69 for Diffusion-X with 3 denoising steps
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods on Block-Push and CALVIN benchmarks
- Demonstrates fast inference (3 steps) compared to 30+ steps required by other diffusion-based policies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Score-based diffusion models can learn multimodal action distributions from uncurated play data without requiring hierarchical architectures.
- Mechanism: The diffusion process gradually corrupts actions with Gaussian noise, and the score model learns to reverse this corruption. This naturally captures the multimodality in the data because the model learns to denoise multiple plausible action paths leading to the same goal state.
- Core assumption: The uncurated play data contains sufficient diversity of solutions for the same tasks, and the score model can represent these distributions effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "BESO leverages a generative, score-based diffusion model as its policy. BESO decouples the learning of the score model from the inference sampling process, and, hence allows for fast sampling strategies to generate goal-specified behavior in just 3 denoising steps, compared to 30+ steps of other diffusion based policies. Furthermore, BESO is highly expressive and can effectively capture multi-modality present in the solution space of the play data."
  - [section III-B] "The diffusion process of both models can be described using stochastic-differential equations (SDEs) [41]. These models achieved state-of-the-art results in various tasks such as text-based image synthesis [7, 33], human motion generation [42] and offline reinforcement learning (Offline-RL) [15, 16, 45]."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Classifier-free guidance enables simultaneous learning of goal-dependent and goal-independent policies.
- Mechanism: By randomly dropping out the goal during training, the score model learns to generate actions conditioned on the state alone (goal-independent) while also learning the conditional distribution given both state and goal. At inference, a weighted combination of these gradients allows control over the influence of the goal.
- Core assumption: The dropout rate is appropriate and the model can learn distinct representations for goal-conditioned and unconditioned policies.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Finally, we show how BESO can even be used to learn a goal-independent policy from play-data using classifier-free guidance."
  - [section IV] "We train a goal-conditioned diffusion policy π(a|s,g) by applying a dropout rate of 0.1 to the goal g, which also trains an implicit goal-independent policy π(a|s) within our goal-conditioned model."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Deterministic ODE solvers enable fast action generation (3 steps) without sacrificing performance.
- Mechanism: The probability flow ODE has the same marginal distributions as the stochastic SDE but without noise injection. This allows using larger step sizes and fast numerical solvers to generate actions efficiently.
- Core assumption: The learned score model is accurate enough that deterministic sampling can produce good actions without the noise injection that typically helps stochastic samplers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "BESO decouples the learning of the score model from the inference sampling process, and, hence allows for fast sampling strategies to generate goal-specified behavior in just 3 denoising steps, compared to 30+ steps of other diffusion based policies."
  - [section IV-B] "ODE solvers do not add noise during the inference process, which can reduce the number of function evaluations and accelerate sampling [20]."

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) and Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)
  - Why needed here: The diffusion process is mathematically described using SDEs, while the probability flow used for fast sampling is an ODE. Understanding both is crucial for grasping how the model learns and generates actions.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between the SDE and the probability flow ODE in the context of diffusion models?

- Concept: Score Matching and Denoising Score Matching
  - Why needed here: The score model is trained using denoising score matching, which involves learning to predict the gradient of the log-density of the data. This is the core learning objective for the diffusion policy.
  - Quick check question: How does denoising score matching differ from standard score matching, and why is it used in diffusion models?

- Concept: Classifier-Free Guidance
  - Why needed here: This technique allows the model to learn both goal-conditioned and goal-independent policies simultaneously by randomly dropping out the conditioning information during training.
  - Quick check question: What is the purpose of the guidance scale λ in classifier-free guidance, and how does it affect the generated actions?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - State Encoder -> Action Encoder -> Noise Encoder -> Transformer Backbone -> Skip Connections -> Output

- Critical path:
  1. Sample initial action from prior distribution.
  2. Iteratively denoise the action using the score model and numerical ODE solver.
  3. Return the final denoised action as the policy output.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model Capacity vs. Inference Speed: Larger models may capture more complex distributions but are slower to sample from.
  - Noise Scheduler Range: A wider range of noise levels may improve learning but requires more careful tuning.
  - Number of Inference Steps: More steps generally improve accuracy but reduce inference speed.

- Failure signatures:
  - Model Collapse: If the model fails to capture the multimodal nature of the data, it may produce only one type of solution.
  - Instability with CFG: If the guidance scale λ is not tuned properly, the model may produce unstable or undesirable actions.
  - Slow Inference: If the number of inference steps is too high or the model is too large, the policy may be too slow for real-time applications.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train BESO on a simple goal-conditioned task (e.g., block push) and evaluate its ability to reach different goal configurations.
  2. Compare the performance of BESO with and without classifier-free guidance to understand its impact on learning both goal-dependent and goal-independent policies.
  3. Evaluate the effect of different ODE solvers (e.g., Euler, DDIM) and noise levels on the quality and speed of action generation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper mentions plans to extend BESO for language-guided behavior generation and collect a large set of play data on real robots to demonstrate real-world applications. However, no specific open questions are explicitly called out in the provided text.

## Limitations
- The claims about multimodal learning capability are primarily supported by qualitative observations rather than quantitative measures of diversity.
- The computational efficiency gains from 30+ to 3 denoising steps are impressive but lack comparison against other acceleration techniques like distillation or hierarchical approaches on the same backbone.
- The paper relies heavily on ablation studies rather than controlled comparisons of individual mechanisms.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using score-based diffusion models for goal-conditioned imitation learning, supported by established diffusion model theory and successful training procedures
- **Medium confidence**: The specific architectural choices (transformer backbone, decoupled SDM) and their contribution to performance, as these are not extensively compared against alternatives
- **Medium confidence**: The claims about capturing multimodality from uncurated data, which are demonstrated empirically but lack rigorous quantification of diversity or comparison to specialized multimodal approaches

## Next Checks
