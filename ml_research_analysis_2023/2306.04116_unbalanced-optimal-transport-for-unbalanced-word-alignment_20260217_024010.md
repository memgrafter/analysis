# Unbalanced Optimal Transport for Unbalanced Word Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04116
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04116
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: Generic OT-based methods achieve competitive performance against state-of-the-art models in monolingual word alignment

## Executive Summary
This study explores optimal transport (OT) as a natural approach for monolingual word alignment, examining balanced, partial, and unbalanced formulations. The authors demonstrate that OT-based methods can effectively model semantic interactions between sentences without requiring specialized techniques. The research shows that the choice of OT problem formulation significantly impacts performance, particularly regarding null alignment ratios. The study achieves competitive results against state-of-the-art alignment models while providing insights into when different OT formulations excel.

## Method Summary
The authors investigate three variants of optimal transport problems for word alignment: balanced OT, partial OT, and unbalanced OT. They apply these formulations to both supervised and unsupervised monolingual alignment tasks, using semantic similarity between words as the transport cost. The methods leverage the Sinkhorn algorithm for efficient computation of regularized OT solutions. For producing unbalanced alignments from balanced OT, they introduce a thresholding approach. The framework is evaluated across multiple datasets with varying null alignment ratios to understand the strengths and limitations of each OT formulation in different scenarios.

## Key Results
- Generic OT-based methods achieve competitive performance against state-of-the-art alignment models without specialized techniques
- Partial OT excels in high null alignment scenarios, particularly in unsupervised settings
- Thresholding on regularized balanced OT effectively produces unbalanced alignments
- Supervised OT-based methods show strong performance in datasets with high null alignment frequencies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Optimal transport provides a principled mathematical framework for measuring the "cost" of transforming one distribution into another. In word alignment, this translates to finding optimal correspondences between words in two sentences based on their semantic similarity. The different OT formulations (balanced, partial, unbalanced) naturally capture varying assumptions about alignment constraints. Balanced OT assumes perfect matching between all elements, partial OT allows for partial matching when distributions differ in mass, and unbalanced OT explicitly handles insertions and deletions. This flexibility makes OT particularly well-suited for the varying null alignment ratios encountered in real-world alignment tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Word Embeddings -> Transport Cost Matrix -> OT Solver (Sinkhorn) -> Alignment Matrix -> Thresholding (if needed) -> Final Alignment

**Critical Path**: The most critical computational path is from the transport cost matrix computation through the Sinkhorn algorithm to produce the alignment matrix. This represents the core OT solution process and determines both computational efficiency and alignment quality.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The primary tradeoff involves regularization strength in OT - higher regularization enables faster computation but may reduce alignment precision. The choice between balanced, partial, and unbalanced formulations involves tradeoffs between computational complexity and alignment flexibility. Partial and unbalanced formulations better handle null alignments but require more sophisticated solvers.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor alignment quality typically manifests as either overly sparse alignments (excessive thresholding or insufficient regularization) or overly dense alignments (insufficient thresholding or excessive regularization). Performance degradation in high null alignment scenarios may indicate the need for partial or unbalanced formulations.

**First Experiments**: 1) Run balanced OT on a simple sentence pair with known alignment to verify basic functionality; 2) Compare alignment quality across different regularization parameters; 3) Test thresholding approach on balanced OT output to produce sparse alignments
