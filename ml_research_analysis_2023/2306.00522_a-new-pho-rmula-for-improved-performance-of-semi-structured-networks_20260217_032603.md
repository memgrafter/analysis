# A New PHO-rmula for Improved Performance of Semi-Structured Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00522
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00522
- Reference count: 26
- One-line primary result: Post-hoc orthogonalization (PHO) solves identifiability issues in semi-structured neural networks (SSNs) while improving prediction performance and convergence speed compared to online orthogonalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses identifiability issues in semi-structured neural networks (SSNs) that combine interpretable structured linear models with deep neural networks. The authors show that existing orthogonalization techniques to ensure identifiability lead to suboptimal network estimation, slower convergence, and degraded prediction performance. To solve these problems, they propose a non-invasive post-hoc orthogonalization (PHO) method that guarantees identifiability of model components while improving estimation and prediction quality. PHO works by first training an unconstrained SSN, then post-hoc orthogonalizing the unstructured predictor and adjusting the structured weights. Experiments demonstrate that PHO achieves better prediction performance than constrained methods, converges faster, and provides meaningful interpretability measures. The approach is also generalizable beyond SSNs to other additive combinations with learnable inputs.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a post-hoc orthogonalization (PHO) method to address identifiability issues in semi-structured neural networks (SSNs). The approach involves first training an unconstrained SSN, then orthogonalizing the unstructured predictor with respect to the structured features and adjusting the structured weights accordingly. This reformulation moves all linear effects from the DNN part to the structured model part without changing the overall prediction, ensuring identifiability of the structured effects. The method is non-invasive, meaning it doesn't require changes to the training procedure or network architecture. PHO can be applied to various semi-structured models, including those with learnable structured inputs like neural additive models (NAMs).

## Key Results
- PHO achieves better prediction performance than constrained methods while guaranteeing identifiability of model components.
- PHO converges faster than online orthogonalization (ONO) approaches during training.
- PHO generalizes to semi-structured models with learnable inputs (e.g., NAMs), extending identifiability guarantees beyond fixed structured features.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Post-hoc orthogonalization preserves model predictions while making structured effects interpretable
- Mechanism: The PHO method first trains an unconstrained SSN, then post-hoc orthogonalizes the unstructured predictor and adjusts structured weights. This reformulation moves all linear effects from the DNN part to the structured model part without changing the overall prediction, ensuring identifiability of the structured effects.
- Core assumption: The unconstrained SSN can learn the same optimal prediction as the constrained SSN; the structured and unstructured predictors are orthogonal after training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "propose a non-invasive post-hoc orthogonalization (PHO) that guarantees identifiability of model components and provides better estimation and prediction quality"
  - [section] "As a consequence, predictions of an SSN with ONO in expectation (and in the limit) are given by X ∗ ˆβ + ˆU ∗ˆγ"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations on orthogonalization preserving predictions
- Break condition: If the DNN fails to learn orthogonal features during training, or if the structured features X are highly collinear, the post-hoc orthogonalization may fail to correctly separate the effects.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PHO removes batch-size-dependent prediction errors present in online orthogonalization
- Mechanism: In online orthogonalization (ONO), predictions depend on the batch size due to the projection matrix P⊥X being batch-dependent. PHO applies orthogonalization only once after training, using the full data, so predictions are independent of batch size and have lower variance.
- Core assumption: The model has converged and the learned unstructured features are approximately orthogonal to the structured features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Lemma 3.6 (Error rate ONO prediction)...σ2E is O(1/b)" and "predictions of an SSN with ONO in expectation (and in the limit) are given by X ∗ ˆβ + ˆU ∗ˆγ"
  - [section] "Due to the orthogonalization, the prediction of an orthogonalized network depends on the batch size used for prediction"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations on batch-size prediction errors
- Break condition: If the model is not fully trained or the unstructured features are not truly orthogonal to the structured features, the assumption that predictions are batch-independent may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PHO generalizes to semi-structured models with learnable inputs (e.g., NAMs)
- Mechanism: Since PHO only requires the final model output and not the intermediate orthogonalization, it can be applied to models where the structured inputs are themselves learned features, such as in neural additive models. This extends identifiability guarantees beyond fixed structured features.
- Core assumption: The model has a separable additive structure that can be orthogonally decomposed post-hoc.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "PHO – in contrast to ONO – can be applied to additive combinations with learnable inputs... This effectively generalizes the approach... to a much larger model class"
  - [section] "Our suggested solutions guarantee correct interpretability, are applicable to most of the methods and models discussed in the literature"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations on generalization to NAMs or learnable inputs
- Break condition: If the learned features are highly correlated or the additive structure is not well-separated, the post-hoc orthogonalization may not yield clean identifiability.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input (X, Z) -> SSN (DNN with Z, learns U; structured part Xβ; additive fusion Xβ + Uγ) -> PHO (orthogonalize Uγ, adjust β) -> Output (eβ, eUγ)

- Critical path:
  1. Forward pass through SSN to get ˆβ, ˆγ, and ˆU
  2. Compute orthogonalization matrix P⊥X (or use mini-batch algorithm)
  3. Update structured weights and orthogonalize unstructured features
  4. Return adjusted model for interpretation and prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - PHO vs ONO: PHO is computationally cheaper at training time, avoids batch-size-dependent errors, and generalizes to learnable inputs; ONO enforces orthogonality during training but is slower and less flexible.
  - Mini-batch vs full-data PHO: Mini-batch PHO is more scalable for large n; full-data PHO is simpler but requires O(n) storage.

- Failure signatures:
  - If β estimates are unstable or large, the orthogonalization may not have worked properly (possibly due to collinearity in X or poor DNN training).
  - If predictions degrade after PHO, likely the model was not well-trained or the orthogonality assumption is violated.
  - If structured effects look implausible, check for over-regularization or insufficient model capacity.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train SSN with ONO and PHO on a small synthetic dataset (n=100, p=3, q=3), compare convergence speed and structured effect estimates.
  2. Evaluate batch-size impact: predict with batch sizes b=1, 10, 100 on test data, compare RMSE for ONO vs PHO.
  3. Apply PHO to a NAM on a tabular dataset, compare structured effect estimates to the original NAM and to a GAM baseline.

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that PHO can be applied to NAMs by adjusting the structured features post-hoc, but notes that additional regularization may be needed when the learned features exhibit high correlation.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the application of PHO to NAMs, the specific regularization techniques required to handle correlated learned features are not fully explored or validated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing different regularization approaches for PHO applied to NAMs, along with theoretical analysis of their effectiveness in handling correlated features.

### Open Question 2

- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the convergence speed of ONO compared to PHO and mentions that PHO is more scalable, but does not provide a detailed analysis of computational trade-offs for very large datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides some insights into the computational aspects of PHO, but a comprehensive comparison with ONO in terms of training time and memory usage for very large datasets is missing.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed benchmarking study comparing the training time and memory usage of ONO and PHO on datasets of varying sizes, including very large datasets.

### Open Question 3

- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the application of PHO to both linear and non-linear structured models, a comprehensive comparison of its performance across different types of structured models is lacking.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance of PHO when applied to different types of structured models, including GAMs with non-linear effects and linear models, on various benchmark datasets.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks direct empirical or theoretical validation for the generalization of PHO to learnable inputs (e.g., NAMs).
- No ablation studies are provided to isolate the effect of orthogonalization from other training choices.
- The paper does not provide rigorous proofs for all claims about batch-size-independent predictions under PHO.

## Confidence

- Mechanism 3 (Generalization to NAMs and learnable inputs): **Low** - Claimed but not empirically validated; no theoretical bounds given.
