# Morph-SSL: Self-Supervision with Longitudinal Morphing to Predict AMD Progression from OCT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08439
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08439

## Executive Summary
Morph-SSL is a self-supervised learning framework that leverages longitudinal OCT scans to predict AMD progression from intermediate to neovascular stages. The method uses morphing operations between paired OCT scans from different visits to create synthetic training samples, enabling the model to learn temporal progression patterns without requiring manual annotations. By training on unlabelled longitudinal OCT data from 399 eyes, Morph-SSL learns features that capture subtle morphological changes indicative of disease progression.

The approach addresses the challenge of limited annotated medical imaging data by utilizing the temporal structure inherent in longitudinal OCT scans. The morphing-based self-supervision captures the continuous nature of AMD progression, allowing the model to learn representations that are predictive of future disease states. When evaluated on conversion prediction from intermediate to neovascular AMD within 6 months, Morph-SSL demonstrates superior performance compared to traditional supervised training and other self-supervised learning approaches.

## Method Summary
Morph-SSL employs a self-supervised learning strategy that leverages the temporal relationship between paired OCT scans from different patient visits. The core innovation involves creating morphed samples by interpolating between OCT volumes from sequential visits, generating synthetic intermediate states that represent plausible disease progression. These morphed samples, along with their corresponding optical flow fields capturing the spatial transformations, serve as input to a Siamese network architecture. The network learns to predict the temporal distance between morphed samples and their original counterparts, effectively learning representations that encode progression-related features. A classifier is then trained on these learned features to predict the risk of conversion to neovascular AMD within a specified time window.

## Key Results
- Achieves AUC of 0.766 for predicting conversion from intermediate to neovascular AMD within 6 months
- Outperforms models trained from scratch and other popular SSL methods on the same task
- Demonstrates ability to stratify patients into low, moderate, and high-risk groups for clinical decision support

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The morphing-based self-supervision works by exploiting the continuous nature of AMD progression captured in longitudinal OCT scans. By interpolating between scans from different visits, the method generates synthetic intermediate states that represent plausible disease trajectories. The model learns to recognize patterns of change across these morphed samples, developing representations that are sensitive to subtle morphological alterations associated with disease progression. This approach effectively utilizes the temporal structure in the data without requiring manual annotations, allowing the model to learn from the full longitudinal dataset rather than just labeled conversion events.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Raw OCT volumes → Preprocessing → Siamese network (morphing encoder + temporal predictor) → Learned features → Classifier → Conversion prediction

**Critical Path:**
The critical path flows from paired OCT volumes through the morphing operation to generate synthetic intermediate states, which are then processed by the Siamese network to learn progression-sensitive features. The quality of the morphing operation and the temporal prediction task directly impact the learned representations' ability to predict conversion.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Morphing interpolation vs. more complex generative models: simpler morphing is computationally efficient but may not capture non-linear progression patterns
- Temporal distance prediction vs. contrastive learning: temporal prediction directly models progression but may be sensitive to irregular visit intervals
- Siamese architecture vs. single-stream: Siamese allows direct comparison of morphed pairs but doubles computational requirements

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor morphing quality leading to unrealistic intermediate states that confuse the feature learning process
- Temporal prediction task failure when visit intervals are highly irregular or missing
- Overfitting to specific morphing patterns that don't generalize to real progression

## Limitations
- Limited dataset size (399 eyes) raises concerns about generalizability to broader AMD populations
- Focus on 6-month prediction window may not capture full spectrum of AMD progression patterns
- Assumption that linear morphing is meaningful may not hold for all disease progression types
- Risk stratification proposed without clinical validation or comparison to existing assessment tools
- Dependence on paired longitudinal scans limits applicability where follow-up data is unavailable

## Confidence
