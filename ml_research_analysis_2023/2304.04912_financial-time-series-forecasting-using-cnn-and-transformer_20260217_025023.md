# Financial Time Series Forecasting using CNN and Transformer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04912
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: CNN-Transformer hybrid achieves 56.7% 2-class accuracy for stock price direction prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a hybrid neural network architecture that combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers for financial time series forecasting. The model addresses the challenge of predicting whether stock prices will move up, down, or remain flat by leveraging CNNs to capture short-term local patterns and Transformers to model long-term dependencies. Experiments on intraday S&P 500 data demonstrate that this CTTS (CNN-Transformer Time Series) approach outperforms both traditional statistical methods (ARIMA, EMA) and deep learning baselines (DeepAR) in sign prediction accuracy.

## Method Summary
The proposed CTTS architecture processes financial time series data through a dual-pathway approach. First, 1D convolutional layers extract local temporal features from the input sequence, capturing short-term patterns and local dependencies. These CNN-extracted features are then combined with the original time series data and fed into a Transformer encoder, which learns global contextual relationships and long-term dependencies. The model outputs probability distributions across three classes (up, down, flat) using a classification head. This hybrid design aims to leverage the strengths of both architectures: CNNs' efficiency in capturing local patterns and Transformers' ability to model long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms.

## Key Results
- CTTS achieves 56.7% accuracy for binary (up/down) stock price direction prediction
- CTTS achieves 44.1% accuracy for ternary (up/down/flat) classification
- Outperforms DeepAR by 3.5% in 2-class accuracy and 4.6% in 3-class accuracy
- High-confidence predictions (>70% probability threshold) show improved reliability with accuracy gains of 5-8 percentage points

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of the CTTS model stems from its ability to capture both short-term and long-term dependencies in financial time series data. CNNs efficiently extract local patterns and temporal features that represent immediate price movements and short-term trends. These features, combined with the raw time series data, are then processed by the Transformer encoder, which uses self-attention mechanisms to identify long-range dependencies and contextual relationships across the entire sequence. This dual approach addresses the limitations of using either architecture alone, as pure CNNs may miss global context while pure Transformers may struggle with local pattern recognition in high-frequency financial data.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Raw Time Series -> CNN Layers -> CNN Features -> Concatenate with Original Series -> Transformer Encoder -> Classification Head -> Probability Output

**Critical Path**: The most important data flow is from raw time series through CNN feature extraction, then combined with original series into the Transformer encoder, which produces the final classification.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Using 1D CNNs balances computational efficiency with feature extraction capability. The choice of 3-class classification adds complexity but provides more granular predictions. Combining CNN features with original series preserves both local patterns and global context.

**Failure Signatures**: Model may overfit to short-term noise if CNN layers are too deep. Transformer may struggle with very long sequences due to quadratic attention complexity. Poor performance on "flat" class suggests difficulty distinguishing between minor fluctuations.

## Limitations
- Sign prediction (up/down/flat) provides less actionable information than price level forecasting
- 3-class classification complexity may not align with practical binary trading needs
- Modest performance improvements (3.5% over DeepAR) may not translate to substantial trading profits after costs
- Limited generalizability to markets beyond S&P 500 intraday data
