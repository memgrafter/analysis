# Towards Robust Metrics for Concept Representation Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.10367
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10367
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Introduces Oracle Impurity Score (OIS) and Niche Impurity Score (NIS) to measure concept representation purity in concept learning models, addressing limitations of existing disentanglement metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces two novel metrics—Oracle Impurity Score (OIS) and Niche Impurity Score (NIS)—to evaluate the purity of concept representations in concept learning (CL) models. Unlike existing disentanglement metrics that assume independence and scalar representations, these metrics can handle correlated concepts and distributed impurities across multiple representations. The authors demonstrate that their metrics can detect impurities caused by model capacity, spurious correlations, and representation types. Through extensive benchmarking, they show that explicit supervision doesn't always yield purer concepts, challenging common assumptions in the field.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two complementary metrics: OIS compares the predictive power of learned concepts against ground truth concepts to detect localized impurities, while NIS measures how information about ground truth concepts is distributed across multiple learned representations. Both metrics use helper MLPs to estimate mutual information via AUC, with OIS constructing a purity matrix and NIS using a concept nicher to identify entangled concepts. The methods are evaluated on synthetic tabular data and image datasets (dSprites, 3dshapes) with controlled concept correlations, comparing CL methods (CBM, CW) against disentanglement learning approaches (VAE variants, CCD, SENN).

## Key Results
- OIS and NIS successfully detect impurities from model capacity, spurious correlations, and representation types
- Explicit supervision does not guarantee purer concepts, challenging common assumptions
- Metrics are robust to concept correlations, providing reliable evaluation even with correlated ground truth concepts
- NIS identifies distributed impurities that OIS misses, demonstrating complementary utility

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The Oracle Impurity Score (OIS) can detect impurities localized within individual concept representations by comparing predictive power to ground truth concepts.
- **Mechanism**: OIS constructs a purity matrix measuring how well each learned concept predicts other concepts compared to ground truth concepts. The deviation from the oracle matrix quantifies impurities.
- **Core assumption**: The predictive power of a learned concept for other concepts should match what would be expected from their ground truth labels if the representation is pure.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "OIS compares the predictability of ground truth concepts with that of learned concepts"
  - [section]: "The (i, j)-th entry of this matrix as a proxy of the mutual information between the i-th concept representation and the j-th ground truth concept"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; neighboring work focuses on robustness and representation evaluation but not specifically on purity metrics
- **Break condition**: If concept correlations are non-linear or higher-order, the linear correlation-based nicher may fail to capture all impurities

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Niche Impurity Score (NIS) captures impurities distributed across multiple concept representations by analyzing subsets of concepts.
- **Mechanism**: NIS uses a concept nicher to rank concepts by entanglement, then measures how predictive a niche complement is for each ground truth concept. Higher predictability indicates distributed impurities.
- **Core assumption**: Information about a ground truth concept spread across multiple learned concepts indicates impurity.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "NIS captures impurities distributed across multiple representations"
  - [section]: "The NIS looks at the predictive power of subsets of disentangled concepts"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; neighboring work discusses representation alignment but not niche-based impurity detection
- **Break condition**: If the concept nicher threshold is poorly chosen, NIS may miss impurities in very sparse or very dense correlation structures

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Both metrics are robust to inter-concept correlations, allowing meaningful evaluation even when ground truth concepts are correlated.
- **Mechanism**: OIS measures deviation from oracle matrix (which includes ground truth correlations), while NIS evaluates niche complements which naturally account for correlations through the nicher.
- **Core assumption**: Metrics should not penalize for correlations present in ground truth, only for additional impurities.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Our metrics are robust to concept correlations"
  - [section]: "Our metrics are robust to inter-concept correlations"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; neighboring work discusses robustness but not specifically correlation robustness in purity metrics
- **Break condition**: If concept correlations change dramatically between training and evaluation, the metrics may produce inconsistent results

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data → Concept representations → Purity matrix computation → OIS/NIS calculation → Evaluation
- **Critical path**: Data → Concept representations → Purity matrix computation → OIS/NIS calculation → Evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - OIS vs NIS: OIS captures localized impurities, NIS captures distributed impurities
  - Correlation measure: Pearson correlation is efficient but may miss non-linear relationships
  - Helper model capacity: Too small → poor impurity detection; too large → overfitting to noise
- **Failure signatures**:
  - OIS consistently low across all models → Possible issue with concept alignment or helper model capacity
  - NIS high but OIS low → Impurities are distributed rather than localized
  - Both metrics inconsistent across runs → Random seed sensitivity or data preprocessing issues
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compute OIS and NIS on a simple synthetic dataset with known impurities to verify metric behavior
  2. Compare OIS/NIS on two CBMs with different bottleneck activations (sigmoid vs logits) to validate sensitivity to representation type
  3. Test metric robustness by varying helper model capacity and observing stability of OIS/NIS scores

## Limitations
- OIS relies on linear correlation assumptions that may not capture complex, non-linear concept dependencies
- Metric performance on high-dimensional concept spaces beyond tested datasets is unknown
- Sensitivity to helper model architecture choices beyond tested configurations is not fully explored

## Confidence
- **OIS confidence**: Medium - relies heavily on linear correlation assumptions
- **NIS confidence**: High - demonstrates robust theoretical grounding through niche-based approach
