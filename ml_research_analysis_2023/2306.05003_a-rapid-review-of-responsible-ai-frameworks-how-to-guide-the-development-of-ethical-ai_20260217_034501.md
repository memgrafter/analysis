# A Rapid Review of Responsible AI frameworks: How to guide the development of ethical AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05003
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05003
- Reference count: 9
- Only 5.79% of frameworks cover all SDLC phases, with most focusing on requirements elicitation

## Executive Summary
This rapid review analyzed 148 Responsible AI frameworks to assess their coverage of AI ethical principles and software development life cycle phases. The study found significant gaps in practical implementation tools, with 80% of frameworks lacking supporting tools. While most frameworks address the four key principles of transparency, fairness, robustness, and privacy, they predominantly focus on the requirements elicitation phase of development. Non-profit organizations propose the majority of frameworks, though companies provide more practical tools for implementation. The research highlights the need for comprehensive, standardized frameworks that support all development phases and both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

## Method Summary
The study employed a rapid review methodology to systematically search academic databases (Scopus), grey literature (Google Scholar, Algorithm Watch, OECD), and company repositories to identify Responsible AI frameworks. The researchers applied eligibility criteria to filter collected studies and extract relevant data on framework type, coverage of AI ethical principles, SDLC phases, and supporting tools. The analysis synthesized findings to identify gaps in framework coverage and tool availability, focusing on how well frameworks support practical implementation across the entire development lifecycle.

## Key Results
- 5.79% of frameworks cover all SDLC phases, with most focusing on requirements elicitation
- 80% of frameworks lack supporting implementation tools
- Non-profit organizations propose 50.7% of frameworks, followed by companies (31.9%) and universities (17.4%)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Mapping RAI principles to system requirements creates actionable development constraints.
- Mechanism: By translating high-level ethical principles into concrete system requirements like explainability, bias mitigation, and data governance, the framework provides developers with measurable targets.
- Core assumption: All RAI principles can be expressed as verifiable system requirements without losing ethical nuance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This principle includes and can be directly linked with system requirements such as traceability and explainability."
  - [section] "This principle can be directly mapped with system requirements such as avoidance of unfair bias, accessibility and universal design and include stakeholder participation."
- Break condition: If a principle cannot be mapped to any verifiable system requirement, the mechanism fails to provide actionable guidance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Stakeholder diversity improves framework comprehensiveness.
- Mechanism: Including both technical (developers, testers) and non-technical (policy makers, end users) stakeholders in framework development ensures coverage across the entire SDLC and addresses varied implementation contexts.
- Core assumption: Different stakeholder groups have complementary knowledge that, when combined, cover the full development spectrum.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "There is not a 'catching-all' framework supporting both technical and non-technical stakeholders in the implementation of real-world projects."
  - [section] "more than half of the tools can be used by Non-technical stakeholders (57.4%, 27/47). Nevertheless, a significant amount of tools for supporting the Technical stakeholders is also provided (29.8%, 14/47)."
- Break condition: If stakeholder involvement creates conflicting requirements that cannot be reconciled, the mechanism produces incoherent frameworks.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Rapid review methodology efficiently identifies gaps in existing frameworks.
- Mechanism: By systematically searching multiple sources and applying consistent filtering criteria, rapid reviews quickly reveal coverage gaps across SDLC phases and principle domains.
- Core assumption: The search strategy captures a representative sample of existing frameworks, including both published and industry-specific ones.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We conducted a rapid review of several frameworks providing principles, guidelines, and/or tools to help practitioners in the development and deployment of Responsible AI (RAI) applications."
  - [section] "The search in Google Scholar did not produce any useful results. Summing up the resources selected from the identified data sources in the last step, we ended up with 148 unique resources (without duplicates)."
- Break condition: If the search methodology systematically excludes certain types of frameworks, the identified gaps may be incomplete.

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: The review maps frameworks to specific SDLC phases to assess coverage gaps, so understanding these phases is essential for interpreting results.
  - Quick check question: Can you list the five main phases of the SDLC and briefly describe what activities occur in each?

- Concept: Responsible AI (RAI) principles
  - Why needed here: The review focuses on four specific RAI principles, so understanding what each principle entails is crucial for evaluating framework coverage.
  - Quick check question: How would you explain the difference between "transparency" and "fairness" in the context of AI systems?

- Concept: Framework categorization (Principle, Guideline, Tool)
  - Why needed here: The review categorizes frameworks into these types to analyze their practical utility, so understanding this distinction helps interpret the findings about tool scarcity.
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes a "principle" framework from a "tool" framework in terms of what they provide to practitioners?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Search strategy (database queries) -> Screening process (title/abstract/full-text review) -> Classification system (framework type, SDLC phase, stakeholder type) -> Synthesis/analysis (statistical aggregation and gap identification)
- Critical path: Search → Screening → Classification → Synthesis → Gap Analysis → Recommendations
- Design tradeoffs: The rapid review sacrifices comprehensiveness for speed, potentially missing niche frameworks but efficiently identifying major gaps in coverage.
- Failure signatures: Inconsistent classification between reviewers, incomplete search coverage, or misclassification of framework types would compromise the validity of gap analysis.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replicate the search strategy on a smaller scale with a single database to verify the search string captures relevant frameworks.
  2. Test the classification system by having two reviewers independently classify a sample of frameworks to measure inter-rater reliability.
  3. Validate the SDLC phase mapping by checking if classified frameworks actually address the claimed development phases through document analysis.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the most effective approach to develop a comprehensive framework that encompasses all RAI principles and all SDLC phases?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the lack of a "catching-all" framework supporting both technical and non-technical stakeholders in the implementation of real-world projects.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a clear solution or approach to develop such a comprehensive framework.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed methodology or framework that addresses all RAI principles and SDLC phases, with practical tools for both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the principle proliferation problem be addressed to ensure a global consensus on RAI principles?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the problem of principle proliferation, where different organizations propose varying definitions of RAI principles.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not propose a solution to standardize the definitions of RAI principles.
- What evidence would resolve it: A unified set of RAI principles with clear definitions and a methodology for achieving global consensus.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the key factors that influence the adoption of RAI practices in organizations, and how can these be addressed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the lack of comprehensive frameworks and tools that can support the adoption of RAI practices in real-world projects.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not identify the specific factors that influence the adoption of RAI practices or propose strategies to address them.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study or survey that identifies the key factors influencing the adoption of RAI practices and provides strategies to address these factors.

## Limitations
- The rapid review methodology may have missed proprietary or emerging frameworks not publicly available or indexed in searched databases.
- Framework classification into SDLC phases and principle coverage relies on manual coding, introducing potential subjectivity.
- The study focuses on frameworks rather than their actual implementation or effectiveness in practice, limiting conclusions about real-world impact.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Identification of coverage gaps across SDLC phases and statistical distribution of framework providers are well-supported by data collection and analysis methods.
- **Medium confidence**: Mapping of RAI principles to system requirements is conceptually sound but may oversimplify complex ethical considerations.
- **Low confidence**: Conclusion that frameworks fail to support both technical and non-technical stakeholders equally is based on tool availability counts rather than actual usability assessments.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct stakeholder interviews with both technical developers and non-technical decision-makers to assess the actual usability and comprehensiveness of the frameworks identified in the review.
2. Perform a follow-up search specifically targeting proprietary company frameworks and recent publications from 2024-2025 to evaluate if coverage gaps have been addressed since the initial review.
3. Implement a small sample of frameworks in actual AI development projects to measure their effectiveness in addressing the ethical principles they claim to support, particularly focusing on the less-covered SDLC phases like deployment and maintenance.
