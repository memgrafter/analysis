# Towards Understanding How Self-training Tolerates Data Backdoor Poisoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08751
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08751
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Self-training with strong data augmentations can significantly mitigate backdoor attacks, even when unlabeled data is poisoned, though standard accuracy may drop.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how self-training with unlabeled data can defend against backdoor attacks when the defender has no knowledge of the attack. The authors demonstrate that vanilla self-training is ineffective against backdoor poisoning and propose a new scheme that leverages strong data augmentations during the pseudo-labeling stage. By applying random cropping of 0.5 portion of image combined with vertical flipping, the method disrupts the backdoor trigger's feature correlation with the target class, reducing attack success rate while recovering standard accuracy. The approach is validated on CIFAR-10 with various backdoor attacks and shows promise for practical deployment.

## Method Summary
The method involves pretraining a model on a small portion of labeled (potentially poisoned) data, then performing self-training with strong data augmentations (random crop 0.5 + vertical flip) during pseudo-labeling of unlabeled data. The process iterates for several rounds, adding confident pseudo-labeled samples to the training set. Optionally, the method can integrate self-supervised representation learning (SimCLR) to learn robust feature representations before self-training. The final model is trained from scratch on the refined dataset. This approach disrupts backdoor triggers during pseudo-labeling while leveraging unlabeled data to recover standard accuracy.

## Key Results
- Vanilla self-training is ineffective against backdoor attacks, achieving high attack success rates
- Strong data augmentations (RCS0.5+VFlip) during pseudo-labeling significantly reduce attack success rates
- The method recovers standard accuracy on clean samples while maintaining backdoor defense
- Combining self-supervised representation learning (SimCLR) with self-training provides additional improvements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Strong data augmentations applied during pseudo-labeling disrupt the backdoor trigger's feature correlation with the target class.
- Mechanism: The backdoor trigger exploits a shortcut feature—the trigger pattern itself—that correlates strongly with the target label during training. Data augmentation (e.g., random cropping and vertical flipping) modifies the input image such that the trigger is either removed, repositioned, or otherwise distorted. This forces the model to rely on other features for classification, breaking the trigger-label association.
- Core assumption: The backdoor trigger is localized and detectable by the augmentation transformation. Augmentations must be strong enough to remove or sufficiently distort the trigger, yet not overly degrade the clean data's features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose to defend the backdoor attacks by leveraging strong but proper data augmentations in the self-training pseudo-labeling stage."
  - [section]: "we explore the landscape of data augmentations as 'feature manipulation' mechanism to alleviate the effect of backdoor trigger. Data augmentations not only help in augmenting the dataset with additional data, but they also make it harder for the model to overfit to 'easy to learn but bad' features [52]."
- Break condition: If the backdoor trigger is distributed across the entire image (e.g., global perturbations) or if augmentations are too weak to significantly alter the trigger, the defense fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Self-training with unlabeled data enables recovery of standard accuracy while maintaining backdoor defense.
- Mechanism: The self-training process iteratively refines pseudo-labels on unlabeled data. By initially applying strong augmentations only to the labeled (poisoned) data during pseudo-labeling, the model is trained to reduce backdoor correlation. The unlabeled data, if mostly clean, provides additional feature diversity and helps the model recover classification accuracy on benign samples. The iterative nature allows the model to gradually unlearn the backdoor while reinforcing clean patterns.
- Core assumption: A sufficient portion of the unlabeled data is clean or only lightly poisoned, and the model can leverage this data to recover accuracy without reintroducing backdoor correlations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We begin by making a pilot study to show that vanilla self-training is not effective in backdoor mitigation. Spurred by that, we propose to defend the backdoor attacks by leveraging strong but proper data augmentations in the self-training pseudo-labeling stage."
- Break condition: If the unlabeled data is heavily poisoned (high poison ratio), the self-training process may reinforce backdoor correlations, leading to high ASR.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining self-supervised representation learning (SimCLR) with self-training provides stronger backdoor defense by learning robust feature representations.
- Mechanism: SimCLR learns contrastive representations using stochastic data augmentations, which inherently disrupt backdoor triggers during representation learning. These robust representations are then used in the self-training process, where K-means clustering assigns pseudo-labels based on the learned feature space. This decouples the trigger from the label in the representation space, making it harder for the model to learn backdoor correlations.
- Core assumption: The contrastive learning process with stochastic augmentations sufficiently disrupts the backdoor trigger in the learned representations, and the clustering step accurately assigns pseudo-labels that are not influenced by the trigger.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Finally, we explore the direction of combining self-supervised representation learning with self-training for further improvement in backdoor defense."
- Break condition: If the backdoor trigger significantly influences the learned representations despite augmentations, or if the clustering step misassigns labels due to representation overlap, the defense fails.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Labeled dataset (𝒟𝐿) -> Pretrained model -> Data augmentation module -> Self-training loop -> Final model
  Unlabeled dataset (𝒟𝑈) -> Data augmentation module -> Self-training loop -> Final model
  (Optional) SimCLR encoder -> K-means clustering -> Self-training loop -> Final model

- Critical path:
  1. Pretrain model on labeled (poisoned) data
  2. Apply strong data augmentations to labeled data during pseudo-labeling
  3. Generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled data
  4. Iteratively retrain model using confident pseudo-labels
  5. (Optional) Use SimCLR to learn robust representations and assign pseudo-labels via clustering
  6. Train final model from scratch on refined dataset

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Strong vs. weak augmentations: Stronger augmentations better disrupt backdoor triggers but may reduce standard accuracy if not balanced
  - Labeled vs. unlabeled data ratio: More unlabeled data can improve accuracy but may introduce more poisoned samples
  - Iterative vs. one-shot self-training: Iterative refinement allows gradual learning but increases training time

- Failure signatures:
  - High ASR with low SA: Indicates the backdoor trigger is not sufficiently disrupted by augmentations or the unlabeled data is too poisoned
  - Low SA with moderate ASR: Suggests over-aggressive augmentations or insufficient recovery from unlabeled data
  - No improvement over baseline: Implies the augmentations are not effective or the self-training process is not properly configured

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: Self-training without data augmentation on CIFAR-10 with 10% poison ratio in labeled data
  2. Strong augmentation test: Apply RCS0.5+VFlip during pseudo-labeling on labeled data and evaluate ASR reduction
  3. SimCLR integration: Train SimCLR on combined labeled and unlabeled data, cluster representations, and perform self-training with pseudo-labels from clustering

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can self-training with unlabeled data effectively mitigate backdoor attacks when the defender has no knowledge of the attack and no access to clean samples?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper poses this question in the Problem Setup section and aims to address it throughout the study.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents a method that shows promise but still leads to a drop in standard accuracy, indicating that a more optimal solution is needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: A method that significantly reduces backdoor attack success rates without compromising standard accuracy would resolve this question.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective data augmentation techniques for mitigating backdoor attacks in self-training schemes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores various data augmentation techniques and finds that random cropping of 0.5 part of the image combined with vertical flipping is particularly effective.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies a strong augmentation technique, it also notes that different attacks may require different augmentations, and no single augmentation is universally effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of various data augmentation techniques across different types of backdoor attacks to identify the most universally effective methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can self-supervised representation learning, such as SimCLR, be optimally combined with self-training to enhance backdoor defense?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores this direction and presents preliminary results, but notes a trade-off between backdoor defense and standard accuracy.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides initial findings but does not fully explore the potential of this approach or resolve the trade-off issue.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experimentation and refinement of the combination of self-supervised learning and self-training to achieve both high backdoor defense and standard accuracy.

## Limitations

- The defense's effectiveness is heavily dependent on the availability of sufficient clean unlabeled data
- The choice of strong augmentations (RCS0.5+VFlip) is heuristic and may not generalize to all backdoor trigger types
- The paper does not extensively explore scenarios with high poison ratios in unlabeled data

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The paper's demonstration that vanilla self-training is ineffective against backdoor attacks is well-supported by empirical results
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism by which strong augmentations disrupt backdoor triggers is plausible and supported by evidence
- **Low Confidence**: The integration of SimCLR with self-training is promising but less validated

## Next Checks

1. Evaluate unlabeled data poison ratio impact: Test the defense with varying poison ratios (e.g., 10%, 30%, 50%) in the unlabeled data to determine the threshold at which the defense fails
2. Test on adaptive backdoor triggers: Assess the defense's robustness against backdoor triggers that are designed to be robust to common augmentations (e.g., global perturbations or triggers distributed across the entire image)
3. Compare augmentation strategies: Conduct a systematic study of different augmentation strengths and types (e.g., random erasing, color jitter) to identify the optimal configuration for backdoor mitigation
