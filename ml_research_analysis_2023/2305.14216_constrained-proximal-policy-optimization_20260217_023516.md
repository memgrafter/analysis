# Constrained Proximal Policy Optimization

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to constrained reinforcement learning (CRL) by treating the problem as probabilistic inference and employing an Expectation-Maximization (EM) framework. The method, called Constrained Proximal Policy Optimization (CPPO), iteratively optimizes the policy through an E-step that finds an optimal policy distribution within the feasible region and an M-step that performs first-order updates to minimize KL divergence. A heuristic algorithm is developed to solve the resulting convex optimization problem, and a conservative update mechanism is introduced to improve constraint satisfaction. The approach is evaluated on Safety Gym benchmark environments, demonstrating comparable or superior performance to existing baselines in terms of reward return and constraint satisfaction.

## Method Summary
The Constrained Proximal Policy Optimization (CPPO) algorithm addresses constrained reinforcement learning by leveraging an EM framework. The E-step calculates an optimal policy distribution in the feasible region by solving a convex optimization problem, while the M-step performs a first-order update to reduce the KL divergence between the current policy and the optimal policy. A recovery update mechanism is introduced to enhance constraint satisfaction. The method avoids second-order optimization and dual variables, making it computationally efficient. The algorithm is evaluated on Safety Gym benchmark environments with varying batch sizes, rollout lengths, and cost constraints, using episodic return and episodic cost as performance metrics.

## Key Results
- CPPO achieves comparable or superior performance to existing baselines (CPO, PPO-Lagrangian, TRPO-Lagrangian) on Safety Gym benchmarks.
- The method demonstrates effective constraint satisfaction while maintaining competitive reward returns.
- CPPO avoids second-order optimization and dual variables, offering computational efficiency compared to traditional CRL approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism of CPPO relies on treating constrained reinforcement learning as a probabilistic inference problem. By using an EM framework, the algorithm iteratively refines the policy through two key steps: the E-step identifies an optimal policy distribution within the feasible region, and the M-step updates the policy to minimize KL divergence from this optimal distribution. This approach ensures that the policy remains feasible while optimizing performance. The heuristic algorithm for solving the convex optimization problem in the E-step and the conservative update mechanism further enhance constraint satisfaction and stability.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **First-Order vs. Second-Order Optimization**: CPPO avoids second-order optimization for computational efficiency but may sacrifice some precision in constraint handling.
- **Heuristic Algorithm vs. Exact Solution**: The heuristic algorithm for solving the convex optimization problem in the E-step trades exactness for speed and scalability.
- **Conservative Updates**: The recovery update mechanism ensures constraint satisfaction but may slow down learning if triggered frequently.

### Failure Signatures
- **Constraint Violations**: If the policy frequently violates cost constraints, it may indicate issues with the E-step or recovery update mechanism.
- **Slow Convergence**: Improper setting of the KL divergence constraint δ' can lead to slow convergence or unstable training.
- **Suboptimal Performance**: Inaccurate cost evaluation or network architecture choices may result in suboptimal reward returns.
