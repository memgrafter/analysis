# On Sensitivity and Robustness of Normalization Schemes to Input Distribution Shifts in Automatic MR Image Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13276
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13276
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Batch normalization causes significant performance degradation with MR artifact distribution shifts, while GN and LN maintain robustness with up to 15% AUROC improvement

## Executive Summary
This work investigates how different normalization schemes affect deep learning model robustness to input distribution shifts in automatic MR image diagnosis. The authors identify batch normalization (BN) as a key factor causing performance degradation when test-time data distributions differ from training distributions due to MR artifacts. Through systematic evaluation on the fastMRI dataset with simulated artifacts (herringbone, Rician noise, bias field, and motion), they demonstrate that Group Normalization (GN) and Layer Normalization (LN) provide significantly better robustness than BN, with up to 15% improvement in AUROC for certain artifacts. The findings suggest that spatial-dimension normalization techniques like GN and LN are more suitable for medical imaging applications where input distribution shifts are common.

## Method Summary
The authors train PreactResNet-18 with different normalization schemes (BN, GN, LN, AdaBN) on clean fastMRI data and evaluate performance on images corrupted with simulated MR artifacts. They systematically vary artifact intensity and measure AUROC and balanced accuracy across three knee pathologies (ACL tear, Meniscus tear, Cartilage). The study compares normalization statistics between training and test data using ℓ2 distance to quantify BN's sensitivity to distribution shifts. The experimental design includes a grid search over learning rates and weight decay parameters, with artifact simulations covering herringbone patterns, Rician noise, bias field distortions, and rigid body motion.

## Key Results
- BN performance degrades significantly with increasing artifact intensity, showing up to 15% lower AUROC compared to GN and LN
- GN and LN maintain consistent performance across all artifact types and intensities, demonstrating superior robustness to distribution shifts
- The ℓ2 distance between training and test normalization statistics correlates with BN performance degradation
- AdaBN partially mitigates distribution shift effects but remains less robust than GN and LN for severe artifacts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Batch normalization (BN) performance degrades with input distribution shifts because its training-time statistics become misaligned with test-time data distributions.
- Mechanism: BN computes mean and variance statistics during training across the batch dimension. When input data distributions shift (due to artifacts), these pre-computed statistics no longer accurately represent the test data, leading to normalization errors that propagate through the network.
- Core assumption: The mismatch between training and test-time normalization statistics directly correlates with model performance degradation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We observe that Batch Normalization (BN), a widely used technique during the training of DL models for medical image analysis, is a significant cause of performance degradation in these changing environments."
  - [section 4.2]: "We compare the statistics calculated during training with the input statistics during testing... the ℓ2 distance between the mean and variance of the test data and BN statistics."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus focuses on data shift detection but doesn't specifically address BN's role in MR image artifacts.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Group normalization (GN) and layer normalization (LN) are more robust to distribution shifts because they compute statistics across spatial dimensions rather than batch dimensions.
- Mechanism: GN and LN calculate mean and variance across all spatial locations within a layer (or groups of channels), making them independent of batch composition and therefore less sensitive to input distribution shifts that affect individual samples differently.
- Core assumption: Spatial-dimension normalization provides more stable statistics across varying input conditions than batch-dimension normalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "As a solution, we propose to use other normalization techniques, such as Group Normalization and Layer Normalization (LN), to inject robustness into model performance against varying image artifacts."
  - [section 3.4]: "Layer normalization (LN)... calculates the mean and standard deviation across all features in a layer instead of only using the batch dimension... This allows LN to be applied consistently in both the training and testing phases."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus mentions time series normalization but doesn't specifically address GN/LN robustness to image artifacts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adaptive batch normalization (AdaBN) partially mitigates distribution shift effects but is insufficient for severe shifts because it only updates statistics without changing the normalization framework.
- Mechanism: AdaBN recomputes BN statistics using an exponential moving average across test distribution, providing some adaptation capability, but still relies on the batch normalization framework which is inherently sensitive to distribution shifts.
- Core assumption: Updating statistics alone is insufficient when the underlying normalization framework is fundamentally mismatched to the shifted data distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4]: "Adaptive Batch normalization (AdaBN) is an extension of the standard Batch Normalization technique, which recomputes the BN statistics using an exponential moving average across the test distribution to improve model generalization."
  - [section 4.2]: "Interestingly, BN performs similarly to GN and LN when dealing with rigid-motion artifact" - suggesting AdaBN's limitations become apparent with more severe shifts.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus doesn't specifically address AdaBN's limitations with severe distribution shifts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do normalization techniques affect model robustness to input distribution shifts across different deep learning architectures beyond PreactResNet-18?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates normalization techniques using PreactResNet-18 architecture, but does not explore how these findings generalize to other architectures like Transformers or U-Net.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on a single architecture, leaving uncertainty about whether normalization scheme effectiveness generalizes across different model types commonly used in medical imaging.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing BN, GN, and LN across multiple architectures (CNNs, Transformers, U-Net) on the same MR artifact dataset would clarify architecture-specific normalization requirements.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between normalization layer position (early vs. late layers) and model robustness to different types of MR artifacts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "intermediate layers" when discussing normalization schemes but does not investigate whether different layers respond differently to artifacts.
- Why unresolved: The study applies the same normalization across all layers, but different MR artifacts may affect normalization statistics differently depending on layer depth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Layer-wise ablation studies comparing different normalization schemes at different depths would reveal whether artifact sensitivity varies by layer position.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do adaptive normalization techniques compare to architectural modifications in improving model robustness to MR artifacts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares BN, GN, and LN but does not explore whether architectural modifications (e.g., residual connections, attention mechanisms) could provide similar robustness benefits.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses solely on normalization as a solution, without considering whether architectural changes could address the same robustness challenges.
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparisons between normalization-based and architecture-based approaches for handling MR artifacts would clarify the relative contributions of each strategy.

## Limitations

- Artifact simulation fidelity is uncertain, with incomplete specification of the herringbone artifact generation method and potential mismatch between simulated and real-world clinical artifact severity
- Results are demonstrated only on fastMRI knee data for three specific pathologies, limiting generalizability to other body parts, pathologies, or medical imaging modalities
- The study relies on ℓ2 distance metrics between training and test statistics without establishing direct causal relationships between normalization statistic misalignment and performance degradation

## Confidence

- **BN degradation with distribution shifts:** Medium confidence - supported by theoretical reasoning and observed performance drops, but causal mechanisms need stronger validation
- **GN/LN superiority over BN:** Medium confidence - empirical results show consistent improvements, but the magnitude may vary with different architectures and datasets
- **ℓ2 distance as proxy for BN sensitivity:** Low confidence - this metric correlates with performance degradation but hasn't been validated as a predictive measure of BN failure

## Next Checks
