# Generating symbolic music using diffusion models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08385
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08385
- Reference count: 0
- This paper proposes a denoising diffusion probabilistic model that uses a binomial prior distribution to generate piano rolls, with an efficient forward process and improved sampling algorithm.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel denoising diffusion probabilistic model for generating symbolic music in the form of piano rolls. The key innovation is using a binomial prior distribution that allows for an efficient forward process where noisy samples can be generated on-the-fly without precomputing the entire sequence. The model also proposes an improved sampling algorithm that selectively preserves parts of the denoised estimate across steps, reducing wasted computation and improving sample quality. The UNet architecture with stride-2 downsampling preserves pitch and time information while enabling hierarchical feature learning. Experiments demonstrate the model can generate coherent piano rolls up to 16 beats in length and perform conditional tasks like harmonization and completion.

## Method Summary
The method uses a binomial diffusion kernel q(xt|x0) = B(xt; αtx0 + (1-αt)0.5) that depends only on the input x0, enabling efficient on-the-fly generation of noisy piano-rolls during training. A UNet architecture is trained to denoise these noisy samples, with stride-2 convolutions for downsampling that preserve information better than decimation. The improved sampling algorithm computes δ = xT ⊕ ˆx0 and uses a mask sampled from B(δβt) to selectively preserve parts of the noise sample xT while keeping other parts from the denoised estimate ˆx0. The model is trained on 2,044 16-beat piano-roll segments from the Maestro dataset for 50 epochs with 100 diffusion steps per segment.

## Key Results
- The model can generate piano rolls with coherence at time scales up to 16 beats (the length of training segments)
- An improved sampling algorithm produces higher quality samples by preserving parts of the denoised estimate across steps
- The model successfully performs conditional generation tasks including harmonization, completion, and variation of given piano rolls

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The binomial kernel with dependency only on the input x0 allows on-the-fly generation of noisy piano-rolls without precomputing the entire sequence.
- Mechanism: By deriving the forward process kernel q(xt|x0) = B(xt; αtx0 + (1-αt)0.5), each noisy sample xt can be computed directly from x0 without iterating through all prior time steps. This enables random access to any step t during training.
- Core assumption: The binomial diffusion kernel can be reformulated to depend only on the initial state x0 while preserving the probabilistic properties of the diffusion process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "By repeating the success probability substitution from the prior step until t = 0, the binomial kernel that depends only on the input x0 is proved to be q(xt|x0) = B(xt; αtx0 + (1-αt)0.5)."
  - [abstract]: "The model uses an efficient forward process that allows the model to be trained on-the-fly without generating all the noisy piano rolls."
- Break condition: If the reformulated kernel fails to preserve the correct marginal distribution of xt at each step, the diffusion process would not converge to the prior distribution.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The improved sampling algorithm reduces wasted computation by preserving parts of the denoised estimate across steps.
- Mechanism: Instead of fully denoising to x0 and adding independent noise at each step, the algorithm computes δ = xT ⊕ ˆx0 and uses a mask sampled from B(δβt) to selectively preserve parts of the noise sample xT while keeping other parts from the denoised estimate ˆx0.
- Core assumption: Preserving parts of the noise sample across steps maintains continuity in the latent space and improves sample quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "An improved sampling method in each step would partially add back the noise from xT to ˆx0... As parts of the reconstructed piano-roll are preserved in each step, no computation is wasted, and the generated sample quality is improved."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct citation, but follows from diffusion sampling theory.
- Break condition: If the neural network cannot accurately predict x0 from xt, the preserved noise components may introduce artifacts rather than improving coherence.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The UNet architecture with downsampling preserves pitch and time information while enabling hierarchical feature learning.
- Mechanism: Using convolution filters with stride of 2 for downsampling (instead of decimation) allows learnable filters to preserve information while reducing spatial dimensions. The skip connections then route this information to the corresponding upsampling layers.
- Core assumption: Conv layers with stride 2 can preserve information better than simple decimation while still providing the computational benefits of downsampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Using a conv layer with stride of 2 to downsample feature-maps has an advantage over just dropping or decimating every other column and row... Activation Maximization... reveals the convolution filters have a high resolution view of the input piano-roll, Figure 3."
  - [abstract]: "An improved sampling algorithm is also proposed that can more consistently produce high quality samples."
- Break condition: If the downsampling loses critical temporal or pitch information, the model cannot learn long-range dependencies necessary for coherent music generation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Forward process (binomial kernel) -> UNet (denoising) -> Improved sampling algorithm (iterative denoising) -> Conditional generation
- Critical path: Training UNet → Sampling algorithm using trained UNet → Conditional generation/modification of piano-rolls
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Binomial vs Gaussian noise: Binomial preserves binary nature but may limit expressiveness compared to continuous models
  - Direct generation vs latent space: Direct generation allows conditioning on any part of piano-roll but may require more computational resources
  - Downsampling strategy: Conv stride-2 preserves information but adds parameters vs decimation
- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse: Generated piano-rolls lack diversity or always follow similar patterns
  - Low quality samples: Generated music lacks coherence or contains artifacts
  - Training instability: Loss doesn't converge or fluctuates significantly during training
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Unconditional generation: Generate piano-rolls without conditioning to verify basic functionality
  2. Inpainting: Condition on first half of piano-roll and generate second half to test coherence
  3. Harmonization: Provide melody and generate accompanying harmony to test conditional generation

## Limitations
- The model is trained and evaluated only on 78 MIDI files from the Maestro dataset, limiting generalizability
- The 16-beat segment length constrains the temporal coherence that can be evaluated
- The discrete nature of the binomial kernel may limit the model's ability to capture expressive nuances like varying note velocities

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The binomial kernel formulation enables efficient on-the-fly training; UNet architecture effectively learns hierarchical features; model generates coherent piano rolls up to 16 beats
- Medium Confidence: The improved sampling algorithm consistently produces higher quality samples; the model successfully performs conditional tasks; direct generation approach is superior for this application
- Low Confidence: The model's performance generalizes to piano music outside the Maestro dataset; binomial diffusion approach is superior to other discrete methods; computational efficiency gains are significant
