# ONE-PEACE: Exploring One General Representation Model Toward Unlimited Modalities

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.11172
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11172
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: 4B parameter multimodal model achieves SotA on 10+ tasks without external vision/language pretrained models

## Executive Summary
ONE-PEACE introduces a scalable general representation model designed to handle vision, audio, and language modalities within a unified framework. The architecture employs modality adapters, shared self-attention layers, and modality-specific feed-forward networks (FFNs) to enable flexible multi-modal fusion and easy extension to new modalities. Through two modality-agnostic pretraining tasks—cross-modal contrastive learning for semantic alignment and intra-modal denoising contrastive learning for fine-grained detail capture—ONE-PEACE achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse downstream tasks including image classification, audio classification, audio-text retrieval, and zero-shot multimodal retrieval, all without relying on external pretrained vision or language models.

## Method Summary
ONE-PEACE is a 4B parameter multimodal model that uses modality adapters (V-Adapter for vision, A-Adapter for audio, L-Adapter for language) to process inputs from different modalities. These adapters feed into shared self-attention layers that enable cross-modal interaction, followed by modality-specific FFNs for extracting modality-specific details. The model is pretrained in two stages: first on vision-language pairs, then on audio-language pairs. Two pretraining tasks are used: cross-modal contrastive learning aligns semantic spaces across modalities, while intra-modal denoising contrastive learning captures fine-grained details within each modality through masked prediction and contrastive objectives. The model achieves strong performance across diverse downstream tasks and demonstrates emergent zero-shot retrieval capabilities between modalities that were never directly paired during training.

## Key Results
- Achieves 89.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet without external vision pretrained models
- Obtains 63.0% mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation
- Demonstrates emergent zero-shot retrieval capability, achieving 35.4% R@1 on audio-to-image retrieval and 40.9% R@1 on image-to-audio retrieval

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Sharing self-attention layers across modalities enables effective cross-modal alignment while keeping modality-specific processing separate. Cross-modal alignment is most effective when interaction happens at the attention layer, not at the feature extraction layer. Evidence includes the abstract statement about shared self-attention enabling multi-modal fusion and corpus focus on other multimodal models but not ONE-PEACE's specific architecture. Break condition: If modality-specific information extraction becomes critical for performance, or if cross-modal interaction at the attention layer proves insufficient for alignment.

### Mechanism 2
Intra-modal denoising contrastive learning captures fine-grained modality-specific details while cross-modal contrastive learning aligns semantic spaces. Combining within-modality detail capture with cross-modality alignment produces better representations than either alone. Evidence includes the abstract describing the two modality-agnostic pretraining tasks and section details on intra-modal denoising contrastive learning as a combination of masked prediction and contrastive learning. Break condition: If either component alone proves sufficient for downstream task performance, or if the combination creates optimization conflicts.

### Mechanism 3
Using language as an intermediary modality enables emergent zero-shot retrieval across unpaired modalities. Language serves as a sufficiently expressive and universal intermediary for representing concepts across different modalities. Evidence includes the abstract statement about emergent zero-shot retrieval capability and section explanation of language playing an intermediary role during pretraining. Break condition: If language proves insufficient as an intermediary for certain modality pairs, or if direct modality-to-modality alignment becomes necessary for performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Modality adapters (V-Adapter, A-Adapter, L-Adapter) → Shared self-attention layers → Modality-specific FFNs (V-FFN, A-FFN, L-FFN) → Lightweight decoder for masked prediction
- **Critical path**: Input → Modality Adapter → Shared Self-Attention → Modality FFN → Output (for standard processing); Input → Modality Adapter → Shared Self-Attention → Lightweight Decoder → Output (for masked prediction)
- **Design tradeoffs**: Shared self-attention enables cross-modal alignment but may limit modality-specific specialization; modality-specific FFNs enable detailed processing but may reduce cross-modal interaction
- **Failure signatures**: Poor cross-modal retrieval suggests attention layer issues; poor fine-tuning suggests FFN layer issues; training instability suggests normalization or activation function problems
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test cross-modal retrieval performance with different temperature values in contrastive loss
  2. Evaluate impact of sharing vs. separating self-attention and FFN layers on downstream tasks
  3. Measure emergent zero-shot retrieval capability by testing retrieval between unpaired modalities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does ONE-PEACE's performance scale with larger model sizes and more extensive pretraining datasets?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper mentions that ONE-PEACE is a 4B parameter model and achieves state-of-the-art results, but doesn't explore scaling beyond this size or using larger pretraining datasets
- **Why unresolved**: The paper focuses on the current 4B parameter model and its performance. Scaling experiments would require significant computational resources and time
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments training ONE-PEACE with 10B+ parameters on datasets with 100B+ image-text pairs, comparing performance on downstream tasks

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can ONE-PEACE effectively handle more than three modalities (vision, audio, language) in a unified manner?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper mentions ONE-PEACE's potential to expand to unlimited modalities but only experiments with vision, audio, and language
- **Why unresolved**: The current architecture and experiments are limited to three modalities. Adding more modalities would require architectural changes and new pretraining tasks
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extending ONE-PEACE to handle video, 3D point clouds, and other modalities, demonstrating competitive performance on relevant downstream tasks

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does ONE-PEACE's emergent zero-shot retrieval capability generalize to more diverse and complex multimodal scenarios?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper demonstrates emergent zero-shot retrieval between audio and image modalities but doesn't explore more complex scenarios or diverse datasets
- **Why unresolved**: The current experiments are limited to simple audio-image retrieval tasks. More complex scenarios would require diverse datasets and evaluation metrics
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments on datasets with more diverse multimodal relationships, such as video-language retrieval or audio-language-video retrieval, comparing performance to specialized models

## Limitations
- The 4B parameter size creates significant computational barriers for practical deployment and reproducibility
- Emergent zero-shot retrieval capability lacks detailed analysis of its limitations and failure modes across different modality pairs
- Ablation studies are incomplete, particularly missing comparisons with alternative architectural choices like sharing FFN layers

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Architectural design choices (shared self-attention with modality-specific FFNs) are well-supported by experiments showing superior performance over alternatives that share both attention and FFN layers
- **Medium Confidence**: Language serving as an effective intermediary for emergent zero-shot retrieval is supported by experimental results, but underlying mechanism could be more thoroughly analyzed
- **Low Confidence**: Scalability claims for adding new modalities are based on architectural design rather than empirical validation with actual new modalities beyond the three tested

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation of Architectural Components**: Systematically test models where FFN layers are shared versus attention layers, and evaluate whether claimed benefits of current design hold across all downstream tasks
2. **Emergent Retrieval Robustness Analysis**: Conduct comprehensive testing of zero-shot retrieval across all possible modality pairs and analyze failure cases to understand limitations of language as intermediary
3. **Scalability Validation**: Implement and evaluate model with at least one additional modality (e.g., tactile or thermal data) to empirically verify claimed ease of extension through modality adapters and FFNs
