# Practical Conformer: Optimizing size, speed and flops of Conformer for on-Device and cloud ASR

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00171
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00171
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Practical Conformer achieves 6.8x speedup on cloud TPU with 16% relative WER degradation, reducible to 5% with cascaded second-pass encoder

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational bottleneck of conformer models in on-device and cloud ASR by proposing a practical design that optimizes for size, speed, and FLOPs. The authors demonstrate that by strategically replacing early self-attention layers with convolution-only blocks, downsizing the architecture, and utilizing RNNAttention-Performer layers, they can reduce model size by 2.1x and FLOPs by 2.7x while achieving a 6.8x speedup. The optimized model can serve as both a standalone encoder for on-device applications and as the first part of a high-performance cascaded-encoder ASR pipeline.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three key optimizations for conformer models: replacing the first few conformer blocks with convolution-only blocks, strategically downsizing the model architecture by tuning the feed-forward module expansion factor, number of convolution-only blocks, and total number of conformer blocks, and utilizing RNNAttention-Performer layers to replace standard self-attention. These optimizations are designed to meet specific constraints (model size <50M parameters, latency <5ms on TPU, FLOPs <100M) while maintaining acceptable accuracy. The model is trained on large-scale Voice Search data with various augmentations and can be cascaded with a second-pass encoder to recover accuracy.

## Key Results
- 2.1x reduction in model size and 2.7x reduction in FLOPs
- 6.8x speedup on cloud TPU with 16% relative WER degradation
- Cascaded second-pass encoder fully recovers recognition accuracy
- Practical Conformer achieves best accuracy among models <50M parameters

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Replacing self-attention layers with RNNAttention-Performer layers reduces memory bandwidth usage during inference.
- Mechanism: RNNAttention-Performer avoids explicitly materializing the full attention tensor, instead computing attention scores incrementally via prefix-sum algorithms. This reduces the number of internal states from quadratic to linear in sequence length, directly addressing the memory-bandwidth bottleneck where "vast majority of [states] are associated with self-attention layers."
- Core assumption: The RNNAttention-Performer maintains comparable accuracy to standard self-attention while reducing memory overhead.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "utilizing an RNNAttention-Performer. Our optimized conformer can be readily incorporated into a cascaded-encoder [1] setting..."
  - [section]: "Another way to improve computational efficiency and reduce memory footprint of the conformer is to apply a recently introduced class of the linear attention techniques that avoid explicitly materializing the attention tensor, effectively replacing quadratic space and time complexity of the attention module by a more manageable linear one..."
- Break condition: If the RNNAttention-Performer cannot maintain long-range dependency modeling capability, accuracy will degrade beyond acceptable levels.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Removing self-attention layers from the first few conformer blocks and replacing them with convolution-only blocks reduces computational cost without significant accuracy loss.
- Mechanism: Early conformer blocks primarily learn low-level features that can be captured by convolutional layers, which have fewer internal states and lower computational complexity than self-attention. This is supported by [section] which states "we hypothesize that low-level features learned by the first few conformer blocks can be captured by simpler layers."
- Core assumption: The first few layers of a conformer do not require the global context modeling capability of self-attention.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "including replacing lower conformer blocks with convolution-only blocks"
  - [section]: "First, we hypothesize that low-level features learned by the first few conformer blocks can be captured by simpler layers. Thus, to reduce computation and size, we remove self-attention layers from the lowest blocks, relying just on the convolution and feed-forward modules in Figure 1."
- Break condition: If low-level feature extraction requires global context, accuracy will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Strategic resizing of model dimensions (FFM, NCB, TB) optimizes the tradeoff between model size, computational cost, and accuracy within given constraints.
- Mechanism: By carefully tuning the feed-forward module expansion factor (FFM), number of convolution-only blocks (NCB), and total number of conformer blocks (TB), the model can be compressed to meet size (<50M parameters) and flop (<100M) constraints while maintaining acceptable accuracy. This is demonstrated in Table 3 where various configurations are tested.
- Core assumption: There exists an optimal configuration of FFM, NCB, and TB that balances the competing objectives.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "strategically downsizing the architecture"
  - [section]: "Given the approximate constraints discussed in Section 1, specifically with TPU-latency less than around 5ms and flops below 100M, we carefully chose the widths and depths of our layers to get the highest accuracy from a 50M-parameter encoder."
- Break condition: If the optimal configuration cannot be found within the given constraints, either accuracy or speed requirements will not be met.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input (128D log-mel + 16D domain-id) -> Encoder (series of conformer blocks with optimized attention) -> Decoder (RNN-T) -> (Optional) Cascaded second-pass encoder

- Critical path:
  1. Feature extraction from audio input
  2. Encoding through first-pass conformer (optimized)
  3. Decoding with RNN-T
  4. (Optional) Second-pass encoding and re-decoding

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. accuracy: Smaller models are faster but less accurate
  - Number of self-attention layers vs. speed: Fewer self-attention layers are faster but may lose some modeling capability
  - Type of attention mechanism (standard vs. RNNAttention-Performer) vs. memory usage: RNNAttention-Performer uses less memory but may have different accuracy characteristics

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy degradation when removing self-attention layers from early blocks
  - Memory errors or crashes when using standard self-attention on long sequences
  - TPU latency exceeding 5ms when model is too large or has too many self-attention layers

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replace self-attention with RNNAttention-Performer in a small subset of layers and measure TPU latency and WER
  2. Remove self-attention from the first N blocks and measure impact on TPU latency and WER
  3. Tune FFM, NCB, and TB hyperparameters systematically to find optimal configuration within size and flop constraints

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the RNNAttention-Performer compare to other efficient attention mechanisms like Emformer or Linear Transformers in terms of quality-speed trade-offs for streaming ASR?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that Emformer is not compared due to the importance of immediate textual output for their application, and discusses RNNAttention-Performer as a replacement for self-attention.
- Why unresolved: The paper only benchmarks RNNAttention-Performer against the baseline and convolution-only variants, without exploring other efficient attention mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: A direct comparison of RNNAttention-Performer against Emformer and Linear Transformers on the same ASR task, measuring both WER and latency.

## Limitations

- The RNNAttention-Performer implementation lacks detailed specifications and external validation
- The optimal configuration of architectural parameters (FFM, NCB, TB) is not rigorously demonstrated through systematic exploration
- The paper does not explore alternative efficient attention mechanisms or sparsity techniques that might offer better tradeoffs

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The general principle that self-attention layers are computationally expensive and contribute to slow inference speeds is well-established in the literature. The cascaded-encoder approach for recovering accuracy after first-pass optimization is a proven technique with existing citations.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific claim that replacing early conformer blocks with convolution-only layers maintains acceptable accuracy while reducing computation is supported by the paper's results but lacks external validation. The RNNAttention-Performer implementation details are unclear, though the general concept of linear attention mechanisms is sound.

**Low Confidence**: The optimal configuration of FFM, NCB, and TB parameters within the given constraints is claimed but not rigorously demonstrated. The paper shows one successful configuration but does not explore the parameter space systematically or provide sensitivity analysis.
