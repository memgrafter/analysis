# Deep Learning-based Bio-Medical Image Segmentation using UNet Architecture and Transfer Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14841
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14841
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Transfer learning with pre-trained encoders achieved 67%, 76%, and 82% Dice coefficients versus 46%, 41%, and 34% for from-scratch UNet on HeLa, PhC-U373, and DRIVE datasets respectively

## Executive Summary
This study demonstrates that transfer learning significantly outperforms training biomedical image segmentation models from scratch. The authors implement UNet from scratch in PyTorch and apply transfer learning using pre-trained ResNet encoders. On three biomedical datasets (HeLa cells, PhC-U373 cells, and DRIVE retinal vessels), transfer learning achieved 67-82% Dice coefficients compared to 34-46% for from-scratch implementations. The combined focal loss and dice score loss function proved crucial for handling class imbalance and optimizing segmentation overlap. The results highlight that pre-trained encoders and improved loss functions are essential for achieving high accuracy when training data is limited in biomedical imaging applications.

## Method Summary
The study implements UNet architecture for biomedical image segmentation using PyTorch, comparing from-scratch training against transfer learning with pre-trained ResNet encoders. The approach uses the segmentation models library to leverage ImageNet pre-trained weights, applies horizontal flip and rotation augmentation due to limited training samples, and combines focal loss with dice score loss to handle class imbalance. The model is trained on three biomedical datasets: HeLa cells (84 images), PhC-U373 cells (114 images), and DRIVE retinal vessels (40 images), with performance evaluated using Dice coefficient metric.

## Key Results
- Transfer learning with pre-trained ResNet encoders improved Dice scores from 46% to 67% on HeLa cells dataset
- PhC-U373 cells segmentation accuracy increased from 41% to 76% using transfer learning approach
- DRIVE retinal vessel segmentation achieved 82% Dice coefficient with transfer learning versus 34% for from-scratch UNet
- Combined focal loss and dice score loss function significantly outperformed traditional cross-entropy loss

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transfer learning with pre-trained encoders (e.g., ResNet) significantly improves biomedical image segmentation performance compared to training from scratch.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained encoders capture rich hierarchical features from large-scale datasets (ImageNet) that generalize well to biomedical images, providing better feature representations for segmentation tasks.
- Core assumption: Features learned from natural images transfer effectively to biomedical image domains.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Transfer learning with pre-trained encoders (e.g., ResNet) and improved loss functions (focal loss + dice score) significantly outperformed the from-scratch implementation"
  - [section 3.1]: "We implement it by using Segmentation models from segmentation models library [19]. This library enabled us to use ResNet weights as the encoder, which has weights for training on ImageNet, semi-supervised learning (SSL) and semi-weakly supervised learning"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in corpus papers for this specific mechanism, though related papers discuss UNet variants and improvements

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Combining focal loss with dice score loss improves segmentation performance on imbalanced biomedical datasets.
- Mechanism: Focal loss addresses class imbalance by down-weighting easy examples (background) while dice score optimizes the overlap between predicted and ground truth masks, creating a balanced optimization objective.
- Core assumption: Biomedical images typically have severe class imbalance (small objects on large backgrounds).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "We combined focal loss and dice score as follows: α(Focal Loss) - log(Dice Score). Focal loss is an improved version of Cross-Entropy loss (CE) that tries to handle the class imbalance problem by assigning more weights to hard or easily misclassified examples and to down-weight easy examples (i.e. Background objects)"
  - [abstract]: "improved loss functions (focal loss + dice score) significantly outperformed the from-scratch implementation"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in corpus papers for this specific loss combination mechanism

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Image augmentation and hyper-parameter tuning significantly improve model performance when training data is limited.
- Mechanism: Augmentation artificially expands the training dataset by creating variations of existing samples, while hyper-parameter tuning optimizes the learning process for the specific dataset characteristics.
- Core assumption: Limited training data in biomedical imaging requires data augmentation and careful optimization to prevent overfitting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "Image augmentation: Since the number of samples are low in segmentation tasks, image augmentation plays a key role in improving accuracy of the model. We applied horizontal flip and rotation to generate new samples"

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the optimal depth of UNet architecture vary across different biomedical image segmentation tasks and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that UNet has two main limitations: "First its optimal depth (number of blocks for down-sampling) is unknown, and second, its skip connection imposes restriction to only aggregate the same-scale feature map of encoder and decoder sub-networks."
- Why unresolved: While the paper acknowledges this limitation, it does not systematically investigate how UNet depth should be optimized for different datasets or whether deeper architectures (like UNet++) provide consistent improvements across biomedical domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies comparing different UNet depths (3-7 levels) across multiple biomedical datasets with quantitative metrics and analysis of when additional depth becomes detrimental.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different pre-trained encoder architectures (beyond ResNet) impact biomedical image segmentation performance when used with transfer learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention using "ResNet weights as the encoder" but note "we implement it by using Segmentation models from segmentation models library" without comparing to other encoders like EfficientNet, MobileNet, or Vision Transformers.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluates one encoder type, leaving open questions about whether certain architectures are better suited for biomedical segmentation or whether performance gains are encoder-specific or general across transfer learning approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using multiple pre-trained encoders (ResNet, EfficientNet, MobileNet, ViT) on the same biomedical datasets with identical fine-tuning protocols.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between training dataset size and the effectiveness of transfer learning versus training from scratch in biomedical image segmentation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors observe that "using Resnet encoder significantly outperforms traditional UNet performance" and "shows weight initialization based on transfer learning from Imagenet dataset is critical in improvement of segmentation as we have access to limited number of images in our datasets," suggesting transfer learning is crucial for small datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses datasets of similar small sizes (84-114 samples) but does not systematically vary training data quantity to determine at what point transfer learning becomes more beneficial than training from scratch.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments systematically reducing training set sizes across multiple datasets to identify the data threshold where transfer learning transitions from beneficial to essential for acceptable performance.

## Limitations
- The study does not isolate individual contributions of pre-trained encoders versus combined loss functions through ablation studies
- Performance comparison lacks consideration of training time and computational resource requirements
- Only one pre-trained encoder architecture (ResNet) is evaluated, limiting generalizability to other model families

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The observed performance differences between from-scratch and transfer learning approaches are substantial and consistent across multiple datasets (67% vs 46%, 76% vs 41%, 82% vs 34% Dice scores).
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific contribution of each component (pre-trained encoder vs focal loss + dice score combination) to the overall improvement is not isolated through ablation studies.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of these findings to other biomedical imaging domains or different pre-trained architectures beyond ResNet.
