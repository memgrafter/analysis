# TANGOS: Regularizing Tabular Neural Networks through Gradient Orthogonalization and Specialization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05506
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05506
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel regularization technique that improves tabular neural network generalization through gradient orthogonalization and specialization

## Executive Summary
TANGOS introduces a novel regularization approach for tabular neural networks that encourages neurons to specialize in sparse, non-overlapping input features through gradient orthogonalization. Unlike traditional regularizers that primarily constrain weight norms or activation distributions, TANGOS promotes unique characteristics in model weights and improves diversity among latent units. The method demonstrates improved out-of-sample generalization performance across various real-world datasets and can be combined with existing regularization methods for enhanced results.

## Method Summary
TANGOS operates by encouraging latent unit attributions to be both specialized (focusing on specific input features) and orthogonalized (minimizing overlap between units). The regularization technique modifies the gradient updates during training to promote these properties, effectively pushing neurons to develop distinct roles within the network. This approach is distinct from conventional regularization methods like dropout or weight decay, as it directly shapes the functional specialization of neurons rather than simply constraining their parameters or activations.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates improved out-of-sample generalization compared to popular regularization methods
- Shows effectiveness in both regression and classification tasks across real-world datasets
- Can be applied jointly with existing regularization techniques for even greater performance gains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind TANGOS's effectiveness lies in its ability to create diverse, specialized representations within the neural network. By encouraging latent units to focus on non-overlapping input features, the network develops a richer, more distributed representation of the input space. This specialization reduces redundancy between neurons and allows the network to capture more nuanced patterns in the data, leading to better generalization performance on unseen examples.
