# Quantifying the Dissimilarity of Texts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02457
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02457
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Explores text dissimilarity measures using vocabularies, word frequencies, and vector embeddings, finding Jensen-Shannon divergence performs strongly while measure choice depends on task and text length

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically evaluates different approaches for quantifying text dissimilarity, comparing measures based on vocabulary overlap, word frequency distributions, and vector embeddings. The authors conduct both analytical and numerical investigations to understand how these measures perform across various tasks and how their behavior changes with text length. Their analysis reveals important trade-offs between different approaches and identifies conditions under which each method performs best.

The study demonstrates that no single measure universally outperforms others across all scenarios, with the Jensen-Shannon divergence showing strong overall performance while vector embedding methods excel for shorter texts. The authors also highlight critical limitations of some common measures, such as the inconsistency of Jaccard distance for vocabulary comparison.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate text dissimilarity measures through three complementary approaches. First, they develop analytical expressions to characterize the behavior of different measures, particularly focusing on how they depend on text length. Second, they conduct numerical experiments using synthetic data to systematically vary text characteristics and measure performance. Third, they validate findings on benchmark datasets to assess real-world applicability. The measures are evaluated across three text representations: vocabulary overlap (using Jaccard distance), word frequency distributions (using Jensen-Shannon divergence), and vector embeddings (using cosine similarity between text embeddings). Performance is assessed across multiple tasks including classification, clustering, and retrieval.

## Key Results
- Jensen-Shannon divergence applied to word frequencies performs strongly across all tasks
- Vector embedding-based approaches are better for smaller texts
- The optimal measure is task-dependent
- Jaccard distance between vocabularies is inconsistent across text lengths
- Jensen-Shannon divergence and embedding-based approaches are robust to changes in text length

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of different dissimilarity measures stems from how they capture different aspects of textual information. Vocabulary-based measures like Jaccard distance only consider presence/absence of words, missing frequency information. Word frequency distributions captured by Jensen-Shannon divergence preserve information about word usage patterns while being mathematically well-behaved. Vector embeddings encode semantic relationships between words, allowing measures to capture meaning beyond surface form. The performance differences across text lengths occur because shorter texts provide less statistical information, making semantic embeddings more valuable when word frequency statistics are unreliable.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Jensen-Shannon divergence** - A symmetric, smoothed version of Kullback-Leibler divergence that measures similarity between probability distributions. Why needed: Provides a principled way to compare word frequency distributions. Quick check: Verify it satisfies properties like symmetry and boundedness.

2. **Jaccard distance** - Measures dissimilarity between sets as 1 minus the size of intersection divided by union. Why needed: Simple baseline for vocabulary comparison. Quick check: Test consistency across different text lengths.

3. **Vector embeddings** - Dense numerical representations of text that capture semantic meaning. Why needed: Enable semantic comparison beyond exact word matching. Quick check: Verify embeddings capture expected semantic relationships.

4. **Cosine similarity** - Measures angle between vectors, commonly used for comparing embeddings. Why needed: Provides scale-invariant similarity measure for embeddings. Quick check: Test on known similar/dissimilar pairs.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Text inputs -> Text representation (vocabulary/word frequencies/embededdings) -> Dissimilarity measure (Jaccard/JSD/cosine) -> Output score

Critical path: The transformation from raw text to appropriate representation is critical, as poor representations will yield poor dissimilarity scores regardless of the measure used. The choice of measure should align with the representation type.

Design tradeoffs: Vocabulary-based measures are simple but ignore frequency and semantics. Frequency-based measures capture usage patterns but miss semantic relationships. Embedding-based measures capture semantics but require pre-trained models and may be less interpretable.

Failure signatures: Jaccard distance fails on texts with different lengths but similar content. Frequency-based measures struggle with very short texts where statistics are unreliable. Embedding measures can fail when semantic relationships don't align with task requirements.

First experiments: 1) Compare Jaccard vs JSD on texts with same vocabulary but different frequencies. 2) Test embedding-based measures on short vs long texts. 3) Evaluate all measures on texts with synonym substitution.

## Limitations

- Analysis limited to GloVe embeddings, performance may vary with different architectures
- Strong performance claims for Jensen-Shannon divergence may not generalize to specialized domains
