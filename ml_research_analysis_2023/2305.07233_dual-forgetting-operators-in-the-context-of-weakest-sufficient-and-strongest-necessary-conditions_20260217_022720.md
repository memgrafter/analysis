# Dual Forgetting Operators in the Context of Weakest Sufficient and Strongest Necessary Conditions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07233
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07233

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces weak forgetting, a novel operator in knowledge representation that serves as a dual to standard forgetting. While standard forgetting preserves entailment of necessary conditions, weak forgetting preserves entailment of sufficient conditions. The authors characterize both operators inferentially using quantifier elimination based on Ackermann's Lemma and its fixpoint generalization. The approach is developed for propositional logic and extended to first-order and fixpoint logics. The paper demonstrates that weak forgetting can be more informative than standard forgetting in many cases and often offers computational advantages.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a theoretical characterization of weak forgetting using quantifier elimination techniques. The approach builds on Ackermann's Lemma and its fixpoint generalization to define and characterize the forgetting operators inferentially rather than semantically. The method is first developed for propositional logic and then extended to first-order and fixpoint logics. The authors establish relationships between the forgetting operators and strongest necessary/weakest sufficient conditions, providing a comprehensive theoretical framework for understanding these dual operators.

## Key Results
- Weak forgetting is formally defined as the dual operator to standard forgetting
- Both operators are characterized inferentially using quantifier elimination
- Weak forgetting is shown to be more informative than standard forgetting in many cases
- Computational advantages of weak forgetting are demonstrated in several contexts
- The approach is successfully extended from propositional to first-order and fixpoint logics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's approach works by establishing a formal duality between two forgetting operators through quantifier elimination. Standard forgetting removes information while preserving all necessary conditions for entailment, whereas weak forgetting removes information while preserving all sufficient conditions. This duality is made precise through Ackermann's Lemma and its fixpoint generalization, which provide the mathematical foundation for characterizing when formulas can be eliminated while preserving the desired entailment properties. The inferential characterization allows for a unified treatment across different logical frameworks.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Formulas -> Quantifier elimination (Ackermann's Lemma) -> Weakest sufficient/strongest necessary conditions -> Weak forgetting/Standard forgetting

Critical path: The core computational step involves applying quantifier elimination to remove specified formulas while preserving either sufficient or necessary conditions for entailment. This process depends critically on correctly identifying the logical relationships between the formulas to be forgotten and the target conditions.

Design tradeoffs: The choice between standard and weak forgetting involves a tradeoff between preserving necessary versus sufficient conditions. Weak forgetting tends to retain more information but may be computationally more expensive in some cases, particularly for complex fixpoint formulas.

Failure signatures: The forgetting process may fail if the conditions for quantifier elimination are not met, or if the preserved conditions become too complex to be useful. In fixpoint logics, failure may occur if the fixpoint structure cannot be maintained through the forgetting process.
