# Diversity is Definitely Needed: Improving Model-Agnostic Zero-shot Classification via Stable Diffusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03298
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03298
- Reference count: 0
- This paper demonstrates that improving synthetic image diversity is more important than reducing domain gap for zero-shot classification, achieving results comparable to CLIP on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and EuroSAT.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates Model-Agnostic Zero-shot Classification (MA-ZSC), where standard classification architectures are trained to classify real images without using real images during training. Recent work showed that synthetic training images generated by diffusion models can address MA-ZSC, but performance still falls short of large-scale vision-language models. The authors propose that the main issue is not a domain gap between synthetic and real images, but rather a lack of diversity in the synthetic dataset. They introduce a "bag of tricks" to improve diversity in the image generation process using a pre-trained diffusion model. Their approach significantly improves zero-shot classification performance across various architectures, achieving results comparable to state-of-the-art models like CLIP.

## Method Summary
The authors use Stable Diffusion V1.4 with CLIP text encoder to generate synthetic training images from class labels. They create datasets for CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and EuroSAT using modified prompts to increase diversity: class name only, multi-domain prompts (photo, drawing, painting, etc.), and random unconditional guidance scales. Five classification architectures (ResNet50, ResNet101, ViT, MobileNetV3, ConvNeXt) are trained from scratch on these synthetic datasets and evaluated on real test sets for zero-shot classification accuracy.

## Key Results
- Improving synthetic dataset diversity through prompt variations significantly boosts zero-shot classification accuracy across multiple architectures.
- Their approach achieves CIFAR-10 accuracy of 81.4%, comparable to CLIP-ResNet50's 75.6%.
- Multi-domain prompts and random guidance scales together provide the largest accuracy improvements.
- Results demonstrate that diversity, not domain alignment, is the key bottleneck in synthetic-data-based zero-shot learning.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Synthetic image diversity, not domain gap, is the primary bottleneck in MA-ZSC.
- Mechanism: The diffusion model's text encoder maps prompts to CLIP embeddings. Low diversity in generated samples means the classifier sees limited variations of each class, so it cannot learn robust class boundaries. Increasing diversity via prompt variations exposes the classifier to richer intra-class variance.
- Core assumption: All synthetic images share the same underlying latent manifold as real images, so more diverse synthetic samples cover the same space as real ones.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "... we find that diversity in the synthetic dataset is more important. We propose a bag of tricks to improve diversity..."
  - [section 3.2] "We instead hypothesise a lack of diversity in the synthetic image dataset is the cause of poor zero-shot performance."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-domain prompts improve zero-shot accuracy by forcing the generator to produce out-of-domain examples.
- Mechanism: Adding domain qualifiers (photo, drawing, painting, etc.) changes the CLIP embedding space to include stylistic attributes. The diffusion model then generates images with varied visual styles, increasing the feature diversity seen by the classifier.
- Core assumption: Stylistic variations are beneficial for learning class-specific features, even if some styles are unrealistic.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "Multi-Domain - Next, we directly influence the diversity by providing a list of domains..."
  - [section 4.3] "Despite approximately 90% of the images being generated under this setting not being realistic; we see the most significant improvement for the CIFAR datasets..."
  - [corpus] Weak: No neighbor paper discusses domain-prompt diversification.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Random unconditional guidance scale injects stochastic diversity into the denoising process.
- Mechanism: The guidance scale modulates the balance between fidelity to the text prompt and sampling diversity. Randomly sampling between 1 and 5 during generation produces a mix of high-fidelity and diverse, possibly noisy samples, increasing intra-class variation.
- Core assumption: The classifier benefits from seeing both tightly aligned and loosely aligned samples, learning to focus on core class features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "Random Unconditional Guidance - We use the base class prompt and randomly set the unconditional guidance scale between values of 1 and 5."
  - [section 4.3] "Random unconditional guidance resulting in an accuracy improvement further supports our hypothesis that increasing diversity is more important than reducing a domain gap..."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14, frozen) → Latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion V1.4) → Synthetic image generator → Synthetic image dataset → Classifier (e.g., ResNet50) → Zero-shot evaluation
- Critical path: Prompt generation → Image synthesis → Dataset assembly → Model training → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More DDIM steps → Higher image quality but slower generation
  - Higher guidance scale → More prompt fidelity, less diversity
  - Larger synthetic dataset → More diversity but higher storage/compute cost
- Failure signatures:
  - Low diversity → Overfitting to synthetic patterns, poor real-world transfer
  - Excessive guidance → Mode collapse, lack of intra-class variance
  - Prompt ambiguity → Misaligned generations (e.g., "Apple" yielding logo instead of fruit)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate base class dataset with "an image of a {class}" prompt; train ResNet50; record baseline accuracy.
  2. Generate multi-domain dataset with "a {domain} of a {class}" prompt; train and compare accuracy.
  3. Generate random guidance dataset; train and compare accuracy; analyze qualitative diversity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the diversity of synthetic datasets affect zero-shot learning performance across different model architectures and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose that diversity in synthetic datasets is more important than reducing domain gaps between synthetic and real images for improving zero-shot learning capabilities.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides initial insights but does not exhaustively explore all possible combinations of model architectures, datasets, and diversity-increasing techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments testing various model architectures and datasets with different diversity-increasing techniques would provide a clearer understanding of the relationship between synthetic dataset diversity and zero-shot learning performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between diversity and realism in synthetic training data for zero-shot classification?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors use a "bag of tricks" to improve diversity in synthetic images but do not explicitly discuss the trade-off between diversity and realism in their generated data.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on improving diversity but does not investigate how much realism can be sacrificed for increased diversity without negatively impacting zero-shot performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the level of diversity and realism in synthetic datasets and measuring their impact on zero-shot classification performance would help determine the optimal balance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size of the synthetic training dataset affect zero-shot learning performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors generate synthetic datasets with the same number of images per class as their real counterparts but do not explore the impact of varying dataset sizes on zero-shot performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate whether increasing or decreasing the size of synthetic training datasets could lead to improvements in zero-shot learning capabilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training models on synthetic datasets of varying sizes and measuring their zero-shot classification performance would provide insights into the relationship between dataset size and zero-shot learning capabilities.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the proposed "bag of tricks" for improving synthetic dataset diversity be applied to other types of generative models beyond latent diffusion models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose a set of modifications to the text-to-image generation process using a pre-trained diffusion model to enhance diversity.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on applying these tricks specifically to latent diffusion models but does not explore their potential applicability to other generative models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the proposed diversity-increasing techniques to other generative models (e.g., GANs, autoregressive models) and evaluating their impact on zero-shot learning performance would determine the broader applicability of these methods.

## Limitations
- The study assumes synthetic and real images share compatible feature manifolds without formal validation.
- Results are limited to CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and EuroSAT datasets, which may not generalize to more complex real-world tasks.
- The mechanisms by which diversity improvements translate to accuracy gains are not fully explained or quantified.

## Confidence
- Medium: The empirical results are compelling and reproducible, but the lack of formal analysis of synthetic-real domain alignment and the limited scope of experiments leave room for doubt about broader applicability.
