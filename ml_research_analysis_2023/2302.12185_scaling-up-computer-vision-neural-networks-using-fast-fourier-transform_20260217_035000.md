# Scaling Up Computer Vision Neural Networks Using Fast Fourier Transform

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.12185
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12185
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Primary result**: Three FFT-based methods scale vision networks efficiently - FiT achieves 94.3% CIFAR-10 accuracy (vs 93.5% ViT) with faster inference and fewer parameters

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces three methods to scale computer vision neural networks using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). The first approach, Fourier Image Transformers (FiT), replaces self-attention in Vision Transformers with FFT, reducing computational complexity from O(n²) to O(n log n) while maintaining or improving accuracy. The second method implements FFT-based convolutions to replace large spatial convolutions in CNNs, enabling efficient scaling of kernel sizes without quadratic complexity growth. The third approach explores Structured State Spaces (S4) and Generalized Convolutions that leverage FFT for efficient long-sequence modeling. Experimental results demonstrate FiT achieves 94.3% accuracy on CIFAR-10 with faster inference and fewer parameters compared to standard ViT, while FFT convolutions maintain accuracy while improving inference speed on ImageNet benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The paper explores three complementary approaches to leverage FFT for scaling computer vision models. First, FiT replaces self-attention mechanisms in Vision Transformers with 2D FFT operations, enabling linear complexity token mixing that maintains accuracy while improving speed. Second, FFT-based convolutions transform both input and kernel to the frequency domain, perform element-wise multiplication, then inverse transform back, allowing arbitrarily large kernel sizes without computational penalty. Third, the paper investigates structured state spaces combined with FFT for efficient long-sequence modeling, though this section lacks complete experimental validation. All methods exploit the convolution theorem - that multiplication in the frequency domain corresponds to convolution in the spatial domain - to achieve computational efficiency.

## Key Results
- FiT achieves 94.3% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (vs 93.5% for standard ViT) with faster inference and fewer parameters
- FFT convolutions maintain accuracy while improving inference speed on ImageNet benchmarks
- FiT demonstrates superior scaling to larger images and higher resolutions compared to standard ViT
- FFT-based methods enable efficient scaling of kernel sizes without quadratic complexity growth

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Replacing self-attention with FFT in Vision Transformers reduces computational complexity from O(n²) to O(n log n) while maintaining or improving accuracy.
- **Mechanism**: FFT computes the frequency-domain representation of all patch embeddings simultaneously, enabling efficient mixing of tokens without explicit pairwise attention computations. This avoids the quadratic growth in computation that occurs when each token attends to all other tokens.
- **Core assumption**: The frequency-domain mixing performed by FFT captures sufficient information about token relationships to replace explicit attention mechanisms.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "reduce complexity from O(n²) to O(n log n) and improving inference speed while maintaining accuracy"
- **Break condition**: If the frequency-domain representation loses critical spatial information that attention mechanisms capture, or if the FFT mixing fails to properly encode token relationships.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Using FFT for convolution operations enables efficient scaling of kernel sizes without quadratic complexity growth.
- **Mechanism**: FFT-based convolution transforms both the image and kernel to the frequency domain, performs element-wise multiplication, then transforms back to the spatial domain. This converts convolution's O(m²n²) complexity to O(n²log n), where kernel size m no longer affects computational cost.
- **Core assumption**: Circular convolution can be converted to linear convolution through appropriate padding and cropping.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "implementing FFT-based convolutions to replace large spatial convolutions in CNNs, enabling efficient scaling of kernel sizes"
- **Break condition**: If padding/cropping operations introduce artifacts, or if the circular-to-linear conversion is imperfect for very large kernels.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Structured state spaces combined with FFT enable efficient long-sequence modeling with better parameter efficiency.
- **Mechanism**: By restricting state space matrices to specific basis functions (like Legendre polynomials), the kernel representation becomes structured in a way that can be computed efficiently using FFT and dot products, reducing complexity from O(N²+L) to O(N+L).
- **Core assumption**: The structured state space matrices produce kernels with useful properties for sequence modeling.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "explores Structured State Spaces (S4) and Generalized Convolutions that leverage FFT for efficient long-sequence modeling"
- **Break condition**: If the structured matrices lose expressiveness needed for complex sequence patterns, or if the approximation errors accumulate over long sequences.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Image tensor → Patch embeddings → FFT-based mixing layer → Feed-forward network → Output
- **Critical path**:
  1. Patch embedding generation (2D convolution + flattening)
  2. FFT-based token mixing (1D FFT along sequence and embedding dimensions)
  3. Feed-forward projection with activation
  4. Skip connections and normalization
  5. Final classification projection
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Patch size: Smaller patches provide more local features but increase sequence length; larger patches reduce sequence length but lose local detail
  - Real vs complex representation: Extracting real part simplifies computation but may lose information
  - Bidirectional vs unidirectional processing: Bidirectional captures full context but doubles computation
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Accuracy degradation: FFT mixing losing critical spatial relationships
  - Memory issues: Large intermediate FFT representations exceeding GPU memory
  - Numerical instability: FFT operations producing NaN or infinite values
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare FFT-based ViT (FiT) vs standard ViT on CIFAR-10 with identical architecture except for FFT replacement
  2. Benchmark FFT convolution vs standard convolution on RepLKNet with varying kernel sizes
  3. Test FiT scalability by increasing image resolution and measuring inference time vs accuracy trade-off

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Question**: How do Fourier-based approaches compare to other efficient attention mechanisms (like linear attention or kernelized attention) for scaling Vision Transformers to very high resolution images?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates FiT's superior scaling properties compared to ViT but doesn't benchmark against other efficient attention methods.
  - Why unresolved: The paper only compares FiT to standard ViT, not to other recently proposed efficient attention mechanisms that also aim to reduce quadratic complexity.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Direct benchmarking of FiT against state-of-the-art efficient attention mechanisms like Performer, Linear Transformers, or other kernelized attention methods on high-resolution image classification tasks.

- **Question**: What is the theoretical limit of kernel size scaling for FFT-based convolutions before diminishing returns or accuracy degradation occurs?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that FFT convolutions allow "arbitrarily large" kernel sizes but doesn't empirically test the limits or identify when scaling becomes counterproductive.
  - Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates improved inference speed with large kernels, it doesn't explore the relationship between kernel size, accuracy, and computational efficiency to find optimal scaling points.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying kernel sizes across multiple orders of magnitude while tracking accuracy, inference time, and parameter efficiency to identify optimal scaling ranges.

- **Question**: How do structured state space models with FFT compare to modern convolutional architectures for long sequence modeling in computer vision?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces S4 and Generalized Convolutions as potential alternatives but states "I could not test this due to time and computational limitations."
  - Why unresolved: The paper presents the theoretical framework but lacks empirical validation of these methods against established vision architectures like ConvNeXt or Vision Transformers.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Complete training and evaluation of S4-based and Generalized Convolution models on standard vision benchmarks (ImageNet, CIFAR) with direct comparison to state-of-the-art CNNs and Vision Transformers.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation to only CIFAR-10 and ImageNet benchmarks without testing on more challenging datasets or real-world applications
- Accuracy comparison between FiT and ViT uses different patch sizes (2x2 vs 4x4), complicating attribution of improvements to FFT versus architectural differences
- Structured state spaces and generalized convolutions section lacks detailed experimental results, making efficiency claims speculative without empirical support

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: FFT convolution mechanism and its O(n log n) complexity improvement are well-established mathematical results
- **Medium confidence**: FiT accuracy improvements on CIFAR-10, as the architecture is clearly specified though patch size differences complicate attribution
- **Low confidence**: S4 and generalized convolution claims, due to lack of detailed experimental validation
