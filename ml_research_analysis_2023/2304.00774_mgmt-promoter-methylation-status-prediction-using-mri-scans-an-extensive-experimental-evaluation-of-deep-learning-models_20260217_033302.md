# MGMT promoter methylation status prediction using MRI scans? An extensive experimental evaluation of deep learning models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00774
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00774
- Reference count: 18
- Despite extensive experimentation with state-of-the-art deep learning models, MGMT promoter methylation status cannot be reliably predicted from MRI scans alone, with AUC scores plateauing around 0.62

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether deep learning models can predict MGMT promoter methylation status in glioblastoma patients using brain MRI scans. The authors conduct extensive experiments using various state-of-the-art architectures including ResNets, DenseNets, EfficientNets, ViTs, and Swin transformers on one of the largest public MRI datasets (585 patients). Despite rigorous evaluation using multiple interpretability techniques and cross-validation, the models consistently fail to achieve reliable performance, with AUC scores plateauing around 0.62. The study concludes that MGMT methylation status cannot be accurately predicted from MRI scans alone, highlighting the need for external validation cohorts and standardized evaluation protocols for deep learning models in cancer diagnosis.

## Method Summary
The study trains and evaluates deep learning models to predict MGMT promoter methylation status from brain MRI scans. The authors use two datasets: the BRaTS2021 challenge dataset (585 patients with multi-parametric MRI scans) and the NoduleMNIST3D dataset for comparison. Multiple state-of-the-art architectures are tested including ResNet variants (10, 18, 34), DenseNet variants (121, 169, 201), EfficientNet variants (b0, b1, b3), ViT, and Swin transformers. Models are evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation with AUC as the primary metric. Extensive interpretability analyses are performed including Grad-CAM, occlusion sensitivity, feature visualizations (t-SNE, PCA), and loss landscape analysis to understand why models fail to achieve reliable performance.

## Key Results
- AUC scores plateau around 0.62 across all tested architectures, indicating poor predictive performance
- Loss landscapes show flat curvature around 0.7, suggesting models are in a random state even after extensive training
- Feature visualizations (t-SNE, PCA) show entangled classes throughout the network, indicating inability to extract discriminative features
- Models can localize tumors effectively but fail to identify features correlating with methylation status

## Limitations
- The study is limited to one specific molecular marker (MGMT methylation) and may not generalize to other radiogenomic predictions
- Results are based on a single dataset (BRaTS2021) and require validation on independent cohorts
- The paper does not explore whether additional MRI modalities or multimodal data could improve prediction performance

## Confidence
- High confidence: The inability to achieve better than AUC 0.62 is well-supported by extensive experimental evidence across multiple architectures and interpretability analyses.
- Medium confidence: The conclusion that MRI cannot predict MGMT status may be dataset-specific and requires validation on independent cohorts.
- Low confidence: The specific mechanisms underlying the failure (e.g., whether it's truly impossible vs. requiring different approaches) remain speculative.
