# Keep Your Self-supervised Learning: A Survey on Image-based Generative and Discriminative Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13689
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13689
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comprehensive survey of 100+ image-based SSL frameworks covering both generative and discriminative approaches

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive review of self-supervised learning frameworks for image-based tasks, covering over 100 unique approaches. The authors systematically categorize methods into generative and discriminative approaches, discussing various pretext tasks like image colorization, inpainting, and masked image modeling. They analyze the resurgence of generative SSL with masked image modeling and provide benchmarks on ImageNet and COCO datasets, making it a valuable resource for researchers entering this rapidly evolving field.

## Method Summary
The survey reviews existing literature on image-based self-supervised learning by categorizing frameworks into generative and discriminative approaches. It examines over 100 SSL frameworks, analyzing their pretext tasks, technical concepts like Siamese networks and contrastive learning, and performance on benchmarks. The authors provide a structured overview of the field's evolution, highlighting key innovations and current trends.

## Key Results
- Generative SSL with masked image modeling (MIM) has achieved results that match or surpass instance discrimination methods
- Vision transformers are particularly well-suited for MIM tasks due to their patch-based structure
- Discriminative SSL frameworks using contrastive learning remain highly effective, with instance discrimination as the dominant approach
- The field has evolved from handcrafted pretext tasks to more sophisticated approaches leveraging large-scale training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Self-supervised learning achieves strong feature extraction without human labels by designing pretext tasks that use image properties themselves as supervisory signals
- Mechanism: The paper defines SSL as a learning paradigm where the supervisory signal is provided by the data itself through pretext tasks (e.g., image colorization, inpainting, geometric transformations, jigsaw puzzles, instance discrimination, masked image modeling). These tasks force DNNs to learn useful representations as a by-product of solving the pretext task.
- Core assumption: The pretext tasks are chosen such that solving them requires learning semantically meaningful and robust features that transfer to downstream tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states: "self-supervised learning in computer vision emerged as a method for extracting robust features from unlabeled data using the properties of images themselves"
  - [section] states: "The idea behind SSL is straightforward: devise an experimental setting in which the task that provides the supervisory signal can be solved without human annotation and then train DNNs to solve it."
- Break condition: If pretext tasks do not require learning semantically meaningful features, or if the learned representations do not transfer well to downstream tasks, SSL performance will degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Discriminative SSL methods achieve strong performance by optimizing for representation learning directly through contrastive or clustering-based objectives
- Mechanism: Discriminative SSL frameworks use losses like InfoNCE to maximize similarity between representations of different views of the same image while minimizing similarity between representations of different images (instance discrimination). Clustering-based methods assign pseudo-labels via clustering and use cross-entropy loss. These methods optimize directly for good representations.
- Core assumption: The contrastive or clustering objectives effectively guide the network to learn discriminative and invariant features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] states: "instance discrimination was established as the dominant pretext task for image-based discriminative SSL, thanks to the superb results achieved using this type of self-supervision"
  - [section] describes InfoNCE loss: "maximizing the similarity between representations of two augmentations of the same image, while minimizing the same metric across different images"
- Break condition: If the contrastive or clustering objectives are poorly designed or if the negative samples are uninformative, the learned representations may not be useful.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Generative SSL methods, particularly masked image modeling (MIM), achieve strong performance by learning to reconstruct or predict missing image information, leveraging the patch-based structure of vision transformers
- Mechanism: MIM divides an image into patches, masks some patches, and tasks the model with generating their corresponding pattern (pixel values or tokens). This forces the model to learn rich, contextual representations. MIM is particularly effective with vision transformers due to their patch-based input.
- Core assumption: The MIM task requires learning semantically meaningful features to accurately reconstruct or predict the masked patches.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] states: "MIM has been recognized as a tremendously powerful pretext task that enables generative SSL to reach and even surpass the results obtained with instance discrimination"
  - [section] describes MIM: "divide an image into a collection of equal-sized patches, mask some of the patches, and task the model with generating their corresponding pattern"
- Break condition: If the masking strategy or reconstruction target is not informative, or if the model overfits to the MIM task without learning useful representations, performance will suffer.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Backbone network -> SSL framework -> Linear evaluation head -> Augmentation function

- Critical path:
  1. Prepare image dataset (unlabeled)
  2. Define SSL framework and pretext task
  3. Initialize backbone network
  4. Train backbone with SSL framework using pretext task and loss function
  5. Freeze backbone weights
  6. Add linear evaluation head
  7. Train linear head on labeled data (if available) or use for downstream tasks
  8. Evaluate performance on downstream tasks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Backbone architecture: CNNs (e.g., ResNet) vs. ViTs. ViTs are better suited for MIM, while CNNs are traditionally used for discriminative SSL
  - Pretext task: Choice of pretext task (e.g., instance discrimination, MIM, colorization) impacts the type of features learned and downstream performance
  - Loss function: Choice of loss function (e.g., InfoNCE, MSE, cross-entropy) impacts the optimization objective and learned representations
  - Augmentation strategy: Choice of augmentations impacts the invariances learned and the quality of contrastive pairs or MIM targets

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor downstream performance: Indicates that the learned representations are not useful or do not transfer well
  - Network collapse: In discriminative SSL, the network may collapse to a trivial solution (e.g., all outputs the same). This can be prevented by using negative samples, stop-grad, or other techniques
  - Overfitting to pretext task: The model may overfit to the pretext task without learning useful features. This can be mitigated by using stronger augmentations or more diverse pretext tasks

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a simple contrastive learning framework (e.g., SimCLR) on a small image dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10) to understand the basic mechanics of contrastive SSL
  2. Implement a masked image modeling framework (e.g., MAE) on a small dataset to understand how MIM works with ViTs
  3. Compare the performance of different SSL frameworks (e.g., MoCo vs. SimCLR) on a downstream task (e.g., image classification) to understand the impact of different design choices

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal evaluation protocol for self-supervised learning models - fine-tuning, linear probing, or K-nearest neighbors?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that generative SSL frameworks prefer fine-tuning while discriminative ones prefer linear probing, and that linear probing misses the opportunity to utilize strong but non-linear features.
- Why unresolved: There is conflicting evidence about which evaluation method best correlates with downstream task performance, and no clear theoretical explanation for these differences.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic studies comparing the correlation between different evaluation methods and actual downstream task performance across diverse datasets and tasks, along with theoretical analysis explaining the observed differences.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the fundamental mechanism that prevents collapse in self-supervised learning frameworks, particularly in non-contrastive methods like BYOL and SimSiam?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that "a definite answer to the way distillation-based SSL methods avoid collapse is not yet found" and mention various hypotheses that have been proposed and refuted.
- Why unresolved: Multiple theories have been proposed but none have been conclusively proven, and the phenomenon appears to be sensitive to model architecture and training details.
- What evidence would resolve it: Rigorous theoretical analysis combined with controlled experiments that systematically vary architectural components and training procedures to isolate the key factors preventing collapse.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are vision transformers truly superior to convolutional neural networks for generative self-supervised learning tasks like masked image modeling?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "ViTs, which have been considered a prerequisite for MIM, are not irreplaceable and that CNNs can still compete with ViTs in generative SSL" based on recent results.
- Why unresolved: The field has largely assumed ViTs are necessary for MIM-based methods, but recent work suggests CNNs may be competitive with the right architecture and training procedure.
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparisons of CNNs and ViTs on the same MIM-based tasks using equivalent computational resources and carefully controlled experimental conditions.

## Limitations
- The survey provides a comprehensive overview but does not conduct original experiments or benchmarks to validate the claims about individual framework performance
- The analysis is primarily qualitative, relying on reported results from original papers rather than standardized evaluations
- Rapid evolution of the field means some recent developments may not be fully captured

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Basic mechanisms of contrastive learning and InfoNCE loss are well-established and experimentally validated across multiple frameworks
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about masked image modeling resurgence are supported by recent literature but require careful interpretation as the field is rapidly evolving
- **Medium confidence**: The categorization of frameworks and their relationships is logical but depends on subjective interpretation of architectural similarities
