# San-BERT: Extractive Summarization for Sanskrit Documents using BERT and it's variants

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01894
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01894
- Reference count: 30
- BERT-based models outperform TF-IDF for Sanskrit summarization

## Executive Summary
This work develops BERT-based language models for Sanskrit and uses them for extractive summarization. The authors train three variants—San-BERT, San-ALBERT, and San-RoBERTa—on a newly released 800 MB Devanagari Sanskrit corpus. Extractive summaries are generated by extracting sentence embeddings from these models, applying dimensionality reduction, and clustering sentences via k-means to identify key topics. Sentences are ranked by cosine distance to cluster centroids and selected as the summary. The approach is evaluated using ROUGE (unigram, bigram, and longest common subsequence) and BERTScore on both short and long documents. San-ALBERT and San-BERT yield the highest ROUGE and BERTScores, outperforming TF-IDF and San-RoBERTa. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of contextualized embeddings for Sanskrit summarization, with San-ALBERT achieving the best overall performance.

## Method Summary
The approach uses three Sanskrit BERT variants trained on 800 MB of Devanagari Sanskrit text. For summarization, sentences are embedded using these models, reduced in dimensionality, and clustered via k-means. Sentences closest to cluster centroids are selected as summary sentences. The method is evaluated using ROUGE (unigram, bigram, longest common subsequence) and BERTScore on both short and long documents, comparing against TF-IDF and San-RoBERTa baselines.

## Key Results
- San-ALBERT and San-BERT achieve highest ROUGE and BERTScores on Sanskrit summarization
- Contextualized embeddings from BERT variants outperform TF-IDF baselines
- San-ALBERT demonstrates best overall performance across evaluation metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BERT's masked language modeling pre-training enables it to capture deep semantic relationships in Sanskrit text. By learning contextualized embeddings, the model can distinguish between sentences with similar surface forms but different meanings, enabling more accurate sentence clustering and summary extraction. The fine-tuning process adapts these pre-trained representations to the specific characteristics of Sanskrit documents, improving the quality of extracted summaries.
