# Sparsifying Bayesian neural networks with latent binary variables and normalizing flows

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03395
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03395
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: LBBNN-FLOW method achieves superior predictive accuracy and sparsity on MNIST variants while providing better uncertainty estimates than dropout and dense BNNs

## Executive Summary
This paper extends latent binary Bayesian neural networks (LBBNNs) by incorporating normalizing flows and the local reparameterization trick (LRT) to improve both predictive performance and network sparsity. The authors demonstrate that by using normalizing flows on the variational posterior distribution, the network learns a more flexible posterior than the mean-field Gaussian, leading to better accuracy and increased sparsity. The method is evaluated on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and Kuzushiji-MNIST datasets, showing improved performance over baseline LBBNN and Monte Carlo dropout methods. Additionally, the approach is shown to be effective for variable selection in logistic regression with highly correlated features.

## Method Summary
The method extends LBBNNs by applying normalizing flows to the variational posterior distribution, allowing for a more flexible approximation than mean-field Gaussian. The local reparameterization trick is used to efficiently sample pre-activations instead of weights, reducing computational cost and variance in gradient estimates. The spike-and-slab prior combined with latent binary variables induces sparsity by allowing weights to be turned on or off during inference. The approach uses inverse autoregressive flows (IAF) in activation space to transform the variational posterior, with the number of flow layers and neural network architecture within flows being key design choices.

## Key Results
- LBBNN-FLOW achieves higher predictive accuracy and sparsity compared to LBBNN-LRT and Monte Carlo dropout on MNIST, FMNIST, and KMNIST datasets
- The method demonstrates superior variable selection capabilities in logistic regression with correlated features, achieving higher true positive rates while maintaining low false positive rates
- LBBNN-FLOW provides more realistic estimates of predictive uncertainty, especially in low-data regimes, compared to dropout and dense BNNs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using normalizing flows on the variational posterior distribution increases flexibility and improves predictive performance while maintaining network sparsity.
- Mechanism: Normalizing flows transform a simple initial distribution through invertible transformations, allowing the variational posterior to capture complex dependencies between weights.
- Core assumption: The true posterior distribution has dependencies between weights that can be approximated by invertible transformations.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section mentioning improved flexibility of variational posterior with normalizing flows.
- Break Condition: If transformations are not flexible enough or computational cost outweighs performance gains.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The local reparameterization trick reduces computational cost and variance in gradient estimates compared to sampling weights directly.
- Mechanism: LRT samples pre-activations directly using mean and variance of weight distribution, avoiding sampling large weight matrices.
- Core assumption: Sums of independent Gaussian random variables are Gaussian, making central limit theorem applicable.
- Evidence anchors: Section explaining sampling of activations as independent Gaussian variables and variance reduction in gradient estimates.
- Break Condition: If weight distributions are too skewed or have heavy tails, Gaussian approximation may break down.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sparsity is induced by spike-and-slab prior combined with latent binary variables for structural uncertainty inference.
- Mechanism: Each weight has associated binary inclusion variable, learning which weights to include or exclude based on posterior inclusion probabilities.
- Core assumption: Many weights are redundant and can be set to zero without significant loss in predictive performance.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentioning structural uncertainty and inference through posterior predictive distribution.
- Break Condition: If prior inclusion probability is too high/low, desired sparsity level may not be achieved or important weights may be pruned.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (x) -> Hidden layers (semi-affine transformations with weights w, binary inclusion variables γ, biases b, activation functions σ) -> Output layer (y) -> Spike-and-slab prior p(w|γ) = γN(0,σ²) + (1-γ)δ(w) -> Variational posterior (mean-field Gaussian extended with latent binary variables and normalizing flows) -> Inference (local reparameterization trick and stochastic variational inference)

- Critical path:
  1. Define neural network architecture and prior distributions
  2. Choose variational family (mean-field Gaussian extended with latent binary variables and normalizing flows)
  3. Compute ELBO and gradients using local reparameterization trick
  4. Optimize variational parameters using stochastic gradient ascent
  5. Perform inference on new data using posterior predictive distribution

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Flexibility vs. computational cost: Using normalizing flows increases flexibility but also increases computational cost
  - Sparsity vs. predictive performance: Spike-and-slab prior induces sparsity but may decrease performance if important weights are pruned
  - Number of normalizing flow layers: Increasing layers improves approximation but increases computational cost and overfitting risk

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor predictive performance: May indicate variational posterior is not flexible enough or prior is inappropriate
  - Insufficient sparsity: May indicate prior inclusion probability is too high or spike-and-slab prior is ineffective
  - High computational cost: May indicate too many flow layers or overly complex neural networks in flows

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement LBBNN-LRT method and compare performance to baseline LBBNN on simple classification task (MNIST)
  2. Add normalizing flows to variational posterior and evaluate impact on predictive performance and sparsity compared to LBBNN-LRT
  3. Test variable selection capabilities of LBBNN-FLOW on logistic regression with correlated features and compare to other methods

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of prior inclusion probability (α) affect sparsity and predictive performance of LBBNN-FLOW compared to other methods?
- Basis in paper: Paper mentions using α=0.10 for classification experiments and α=0.25 for logistic regression simulation study
- Why unresolved: Paper does not systematically explore effect of varying α on sparsity and performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive study varying α across range of values for different datasets and architectures, comparing sparsity levels and predictive accuracy

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do LBBNN-FLOW and other Bayesian neural network methods handle out-of-distribution (OOD) data compared to non-Bayesian methods?
- Basis in paper: Paper shows LBBNN methods can detect OOD data using pre-activation values but does not compare to other methods
- Why unresolved: Paper only demonstrates OOD detection with LBBNN methods and does not benchmark against state-of-the-art OOD detection techniques
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments evaluating OOD detection performance against other methods on various datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does performance of LBBNN-FLOW scale with number of normalizing flow layers and complexity of neural network used in flows?
- Basis in paper: Paper uses normalizing flows of length two with neural networks having two hidden layers of 250 neurons each
- Why unresolved: Paper does not explore impact of varying number of flow layers or complexity of neural networks within flows on performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying number of flow layers and size of neural networks within flows, measuring trade-off between performance and computational cost

## Limitations

- Analysis focuses on specific datasets (MNIST variants, synthetic logistic regression) without testing on more complex or real-world sparse regression problems
- Computational overhead of normalizing flows is not thoroughly analyzed, making it unclear whether performance gains justify additional complexity
- Spike-and-slab prior's effectiveness for very deep networks remains untested

## Confidence

- Sparsity results: Medium
- Uncertainty quantification advantages: Medium (needs validation on more diverse datasets)
- Extension from LBBNN to LBBNN-FLOW: Medium (technically sound but requires more extensive ablation studies)
