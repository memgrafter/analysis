# Automatic Discrimination of Human and Neural Machine Translation in Multilingual Scenarios
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19757
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19757
- Reference count: 18
- Multilingual classifiers incorporating source text improve accuracy and robustness in MT vs HT discrimination

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method for discriminating between human and machine translations in multilingual scenarios using fine-tuned language models. The key innovation is incorporating source text alongside target translations in multilingual classifiers, which improves both accuracy and cross-system robustness compared to monolingual approaches. The study systematically evaluates different model architectures across multiple language pairs and demonstrates that training on multiple source languages and longer document-level sequences further enhances performance.

## Method Summary
The approach uses pre-trained language models (DEBERTA-V3 for monolingual, M-BERT/M-BART/XLM-R/M-DEBERTA for multilingual) fine-tuned on balanced datasets of human and machine translations. Two strategies are employed: target-only (English translation only) and source+target (concatenated source and English texts). The models are trained on WMT test sets from 2018-2019, with machine translations generated using Google Translate and DeepL. Evaluation includes accuracy on test sets, cross-system robustness testing, and comparison of sentence-level vs document-level performance across seven language pairs.

## Key Results
- Multilingual classifiers incorporating source text achieve 88.7% accuracy on document-level German-English classification
- Training on multiple source languages (German, Russian, Chinese) improves performance across both monolingual and multilingual classifiers
- Document-level training provides significant accuracy gains over sentence-level approaches
- Source+target models demonstrate superior cross-system robustness compared to target-only models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multilingual classifiers that incorporate source text improve accuracy and robustness compared to monolingual classifiers.
- Mechanism: The classifier leverages both source and target language representations to better distinguish between human and machine translations by capturing translation artifacts that may not be apparent in the target language alone.
- Core assumption: The source language provides discriminative information that complements the target language for classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we demonstrate that incorporating the source text in the input of a multilingual classifier improves (i) its accuracy and (ii) its robustness on cross-system evaluation, compared to a monolingual classifier."
  - [section]: "Using a multilingual LM and therefore having access also to the source sentence results overall in better performance than an equivalent LM that only has access to the target sentence."
- Break condition: If the source language does not provide discriminative information, the benefit of including source text may diminish or disappear.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training on multiple source languages improves classifier performance.
- Mechanism: Exposure to diverse translation patterns across multiple source languages helps the classifier learn more generalizable features for distinguishing human and machine translations.
- Core assumption: Translation patterns and artifacts vary across source languages, and exposure to this diversity improves generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we find that using training data from multiple source languages (German, Russian, and Chinese) tends to improve the accuracy of both monolingual and multilingual classifiers."
  - [section]: "Having additional training data from different source languages clearly helps, even for the multilingual source + target model."
- Break condition: If translation patterns across source languages are too similar or if the classifier overfits to language-specific artifacts rather than general translation quality indicators.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Document-level training improves classification accuracy compared to sentence-level training.
- Mechanism: Longer context provides more information about translation consistency, style, and coherence, which helps distinguish human from machine translations.
- Core assumption: Translation quality indicators manifest more clearly at the document level than at the sentence level.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we show that bilingual classifiers and classifiers trained on multiple source languages benefit from being trained on longer text sequences, rather than on sentences."
  - [section]: "we confirm our two previous findings: the models can improve performance when training on texts from a different source language... and the models clearly benefit from having access to the source text itself during training and evaluation."
- Break condition: If document-level context introduces noise or if the classifier cannot effectively process long sequences, the benefit of document-level training may diminish or disappear.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preparation: WMT test sets, Google Translate/DeepL translations
  - Models: Monolingual (DEBERTA-V3) and multilingual (M-BERT, M-BART, XLM-R, M-DEBERTA)
  - Training pipeline: Fine-tuning on sentence-level and document-level data
  - Evaluation: Accuracy on test sets, cross-system evaluation, BLEU/COMET scores

- Critical path:
  1. Prepare balanced dataset with human and machine translations
  2. Fine-tune monolingual and multilingual models on training data
  3. Evaluate models on test sets and cross-system scenarios
  4. Analyze results and draw conclusions about classifier performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Monolingual vs multilingual models: Tradeoff between potential performance gains and increased model complexity
  - Sentence-level vs document-level training: Tradeoff between computational efficiency and potential performance gains
  - Number of source languages: Tradeoff between improved generalization and increased data requirements

- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy across all test scenarios
  - Significant drop in performance when testing on translations from different source languages or MT systems
  - Overfitting to specific translation patterns or source languages

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune monolingual and multilingual models on German-English data and evaluate on German test set
  2. Fine-tune models on data from multiple source languages and evaluate on German test set
  3. Train and evaluate models on document-level data instead of sentence-level data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does training on high-quality machine translations (e.g., Google/DeepL) cause classifiers to perform worse on low-quality translations, and if so, how can this be mitigated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly shows that classifiers trained on high-quality MT perform worse on low-quality MT systems (WMT3/WMT4), with accuracy dropping below chance level.
- Why unresolved: The paper only observes this phenomenon but does not explore solutions or investigate why this happens.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing classifier performance on varying MT quality levels, including techniques like domain adaptation, data augmentation with low-quality translations, or training on a mix of high/low-quality translations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal way to incorporate source text information into multilingual classifiers for MT vs HT discrimination?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests different approaches (concatenation, order variations) but notes that adding language tags was not experimented with, and concludes that incorporating source text improves performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one method of incorporating source text (simple concatenation) and does not explore other potential approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of different source text incorporation methods (language tags, separate input streams, attention mechanisms) across multiple language pairs and MT systems.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does training on document-level data compare to sentence-level data in terms of computational efficiency and practical deployment considerations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows document-level training improves accuracy but doesn't discuss computational costs or practical deployment implications.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on accuracy improvements but doesn't address the trade-offs between improved performance and increased computational requirements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of training time, memory usage, and inference speed between sentence-level and document-level approaches, along with benchmarks on different hardware configurations.

## Limitations
- Primary evaluation focuses on news domain data from WMT tasks, limiting generalizability to other domains
- Study uses only two MT systems (Google Translate and DeepL) for training, potentially limiting robustness findings
- Document-level truncation may prevent full realization of document context benefits

## Confidence
- Multilingual source+target classifiers: Medium confidence - clear performance improvements but limited to specific MT systems and domains
- Multiple source language training benefits: Medium confidence - consistent improvements but no exploration of optimal number of languages or language family diversity
- Document-level training advantages: Medium confidence - demonstrated accuracy gains but truncation issues raise questions about full context utilization

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain validation**: Evaluate the classifiers on non-news domains (e.g., literature, technical documentation, social media) to assess generalization beyond the WMT news domain used in the study.

2. **Additional MT system robustness**: Test the classifiers against a broader range of MT systems including academic systems, different commercial APIs, and specialized domain MT systems to verify the cross-system robustness claims.

3. **Language family diversity**: Conduct experiments using source languages from different families (e.g., Semitic, Dravidian, Uralic) to determine whether the multilingual benefits extend beyond Indo-European languages and identify potential limitations.
