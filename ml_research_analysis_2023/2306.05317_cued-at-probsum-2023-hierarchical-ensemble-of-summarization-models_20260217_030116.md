# CUED at ProbSum 2023: Hierarchical Ensemble of Summarization Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05317
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05317
- Authors: Multiple authors
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Achieved ROUGE-L score of 32.77 on held-out challenge data, best result in BioNLP 2023 shared task

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a hierarchical ensemble method for summarizing medical progress notes in a low-resource setting. The approach combines token-level ensembles of diverse fine-tuned Clinical-T5 models with Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding to improve summarization performance. Using only 765 training examples from MIMIC-III, the method achieves state-of-the-art results in the BioNLP 2023 shared task, demonstrating that ensembling can significantly boost performance when training data is scarce.

## Method Summary
The method employs a hierarchical ensemble of summarization models (HESM) that first creates token-level ensembles by averaging predictions from multiple fine-tuned Clinical-T5 models, then applies MBR decoding to select the most representative summary. The approach fine-tunes Clinical-T5-Large on different combinations of medical note fields (assessment, objective, subjective) and uses different random seeds to create diverse model variants. Token-level ensemble averages conditional probabilities across models for each output token, while MBR decoding uses ROUGE-L as a reward metric to select the optimal sequence from the ensemble candidates.

## Key Results
- Achieved ROUGE-L score of 32.77 on held-out test data, ranking first in the BioNLP 2023 shared task
- {A} (assessment field) and {A}+{S} (assessment + subjective) field combinations yielded the best performance
- Fine-tuned Clinical-T5 models significantly outperformed extractive, abstractive, and zero-shot baselines
- Hierarchical ensemble approach demonstrated effectiveness in low-resource medical summarization (765 training examples)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Token-level ensemble reduces the variance of predictions by averaging probabilities across diverse models.
- Mechanism: For each output token, the method averages the conditional probabilities from multiple fine-tuned Clinical-T5 models, producing smoother, more stable predictions.
- Core assumption: The models are sufficiently diverse so that their errors are not perfectly correlated.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "token-level ensembles of diverse fine-tuned Clinical-T5 models"
  - [section] "In token ensembling, each token's probability is the average probability of the individual models"
  - [corpus] Weak—corpus lacks direct ensemble diversity or performance comparisons.
- Break condition: If all models make correlated mistakes, averaging does not improve robustness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: MBR decoding improves output quality by selecting the most representative sequence according to a task-specific metric.
- Mechanism: MBR picks the sequence that maximizes the expected reward under the distribution of candidate outputs, approximated by averaging rewards over sampled sequences.
- Core assumption: The reward function (ROUGE-L) aligns with human judgment of quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Minimum Bayes Risk (MBR) decoding"
  - [section] "we use ROUGE-L as the reward metric R"
  - [corpus] Weak—no corpus data showing MBR specifically outperforms alternatives.
- Break condition: If the reward metric poorly correlates with actual usefulness, MBR selection may choose poor outputs.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hierarchical ensemble (token-level ensemble + MBR) leverages both per-token robustness and global sequence coherence.
- Mechanism: First layer creates diverse candidate sequences via token-level ensemble; second layer selects the best candidate via MBR.
- Core assumption: Diversity in first layer leads to meaningful variation in MBR candidates.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Hierarchical Ensemble of Summarization Models (HESM)"
  - [section] "Hierarchical ensemble is a method that aims to combine the above two approaches"
  - [corpus] Weak—corpus does not quantify how much MBR benefits from token-level ensemble diversity.
- Break condition: If first-layer candidates are too similar, MBR gains diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data → Preprocessing → Fine-tune Clinical-T5 (θA, θAS) → Token-level ensemble → MBR decoding → Output
- Critical path: Fine-tune → Token ensemble → MBR; all downstream quality depends on diversity of fine-tuned models
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More models → higher diversity but higher inference cost
  - Larger input fields → better coverage but more noise
  - MBR vs. beam search → better metric alignment but slower
- Failure signatures:
  - Low ROUGE-L with diverse inputs → token ensemble not effective (models may be too similar)
  - High training ROUGE but low test → overfitting or metric misalignment
  - MBR picks poor outputs → reward metric misalignment
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run token-level ensemble over 2 fine-tuned θA models, compare ROUGE-L to single model
  2. Add MBR decoding to the ensemble from (1), measure gain
  3. Vary input fields {A}, {A}+{S}, {A}+{S}+{O}, observe impact on ensemble performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the HESM approach perform with larger training datasets, and would the relative improvement from ensembling remain consistent?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper emphasizes low-resource setting (765 examples) and demonstrates significant improvements through ensembling, but does not explore scaling to larger datasets.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses exclusively on the low-resource scenario and does not provide analysis of how ensemble methods perform as data increases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing HESM performance on progressively larger training datasets, showing how ROUGE-L scores and ensemble benefits scale with data size.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal combination of input fields ({A}, {S}, {O}) for medical note summarization across different clinical contexts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper systematically tests different field combinations and finds {A} and {A}+{S} work best, but suggests {A}+{S}+{O} might yield further improvements.
- Why unresolved: The study only tests combinations on this specific dataset and does not explore whether optimal field combinations vary by clinical specialty or note type.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis across multiple medical specialties and note types, identifying consistent patterns in which field combinations yield optimal performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do alternative evaluation metrics compare to ROUGE-L for assessing medical summarization quality, particularly regarding clinical accuracy and completeness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge ROUGE-L limitations and note that n-gram overlap metrics are "notably not optimal for abstractive summarization assessment."
- Why unresolved: The study relies solely on ROUGE-L despite recognizing its limitations, without exploring clinical expert evaluation or alternative metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Side-by-side comparison of ROUGE-L with clinical expert ratings and other evaluation frameworks like BERTScore or task-specific quality metrics on the same summaries.

## Limitations

- The study lacks ablation experiments to isolate the contribution of token-level ensemble versus MBR decoding components
- Evaluation relies solely on ROUGE-L metric without clinical expert assessment of summary quality
- Small training set (765 examples) raises concerns about overfitting and generalizability to other medical domains
- No reported diversity metrics for ensemble members to verify the diversity assumption underlying the approach

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The fundamental mechanism of token-level ensemble averaging is well-established and mathematically sound. The use of Clinical-T5 as a base model is appropriate given its domain-specific pretraining on MIMIC-III/IV data.

**Medium confidence**: The reported performance improvement (32.77 ROUGE-L) is credible given the competitive setting and the logical combination of ensemble methods with MBR decoding. However, without baseline comparisons from individual models or alternative ensemble strategies, the exact contribution of the hierarchical approach remains uncertain.

**Low confidence**: Claims about clinical utility and the superiority of this specific hierarchical ensemble configuration over other possible architectures lack empirical support. The paper does not provide statistical significance testing or error analysis of the generated summaries.

## Next Checks

1. **Ensemble diversity quantification**: Measure pairwise ROUGE scores between individual ensemble members to empirically verify that diversity assumptions hold. Calculate the correlation between ensemble diversity metrics and final performance improvements.

2. **Ablation study**: Remove MBR decoding while keeping token-level ensemble, and vice versa, to isolate the contribution of each hierarchical layer. Compare against a simple ensemble of beam-search outputs without MBR.

3. **Clinical expert evaluation**: Conduct a small-scale qualitative assessment with medical professionals to evaluate whether ROUGE-L improvements translate to clinically meaningful summary quality, focusing on accuracy of diagnoses and medical terminology.
