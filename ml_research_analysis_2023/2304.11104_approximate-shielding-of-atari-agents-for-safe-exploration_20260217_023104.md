# Approximate Shielding of Atari Agents for Safe Exploration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11104
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11104

- Key outcome: This paper proposes a principled algorithm for safe exploration in reinforcement learning using shielding. The authors address the challenge of balancing exploration and conservatism in constrained settings, which is crucial for real-world applications. Their approach builds on latent shielding, leveraging world models to verify policy roll-outs in the latent space of a learned dynamics model. The key innovations include using safety critics to improve stability and farsightedness, and incorporating twin delayed target critics to reduce overestimation of expected costs and overly conservative behavior. The algorithm is grounded in probabilistic computation tree logic (PCTL) and provides PAC-style bounds on the probability of accurately estimating constraint violations. ## Executive Summary
This paper introduces approximate shielding, a method for safe exploration in reinforcement learning that leverages learned world models to verify policy roll-outs in latent space. The approach addresses the challenge of balancing exploration and conservatism in constrained settings by using safety critics to improve stability and farsightedness, and incorporating twin delayed target critics to reduce overestimation of expected costs and overly conservative behavior. The algorithm is grounded in probabilistic computation tree logic (PCTL) and provides PAC-style bounds on the probability of accurately estimating constraint violations. Experimental results on Atari games demonstrate that the approximate shielding algorithm effectively reduces safety violations and, in some cases, improves convergence speed and final policy quality.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a method for safe exploration in reinforcement learning using shielding, which leverages learned world models to verify policy roll-outs in latent space. The approach is based on latent shielding, where a DreamerV2 world model is trained to predict future states and costs. Safety verification is performed by rolling out the task policy in the learned model and checking if the expected costs exceed a threshold. The method uses safety critics to estimate expected costs under the task policy and bootstrap the value at the end of imagined trajectories, allowing for farsighted shielding without increasing the imagination horizon. Twin delayed target critics are used to reduce overestimation of expected costs and overly conservative behavior. The algorithm provides PAC-style bounds on the probability of accurately estimating constraint violations and is evaluated on Atari games, demonstrating reduced safety violations while maintaining or improving performance compared to standard DreamerV2.

## Key Results
- The approximate shielding algorithm effectively reduces safety violations on Atari games compared to DreamerV2 baseline.
- In some cases, the method improves convergence speed and final policy quality while maintaining safety.
- The approach provides PAC-style bounds on the probability of accurately estimating constraint violations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Safety critics provide farsighted shielding without increasing the imagination horizon
- Mechanism: The two safety critics estimate expected costs under the task policy and are used to bootstrap the value at the end of imagined trajectories. This allows the algorithm to look further into the future than the fixed imagination horizon without additional model rollouts.
- Core assumption: The safety critics accurately estimate expected costs from states at the imagination horizon
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We use safety critics to improve the stability and farsightedness of the algorithm"
  - [section 3.4]: "Safety critics estimate the expected costs under the task policy πtask. They give us an idea of how safe specific states are under the task policy state distribution. Additionally, we can use them to bootstrap the end of 'imagined' trajectories for further look-ahead capabilities."
- Break condition: If the safety critics become poorly trained or suffer from overestimation bias, the farsighted estimates will be unreliable

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Twin delayed target critics reduce overestimation and overly conservative behavior
- Mechanism: Two safety critics are trained with a TD3-style algorithm where the minimum of the two target critics is used for TD-λ targets. This prevents overestimation of expected costs and reduces overly conservative behavior.
- Core assumption: The two critics learn complementary value estimates that can be combined through minimization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4]: "Each of the two safety critics vC1 and vC2, has its own target critic vC1' and vC2', that are updated periodically with slow updates. The TD-λ targets are constructed by taking a minimum of the two target critics vC1' and vC2' as follows..."
  - [corpus]: "Model-based Dynamic Shielding for Safe and Efficient Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning" (related work suggests TD3-style methods are effective for reducing overestimation)
- Break condition: If both critics consistently overestimate or underestimate, taking the minimum will not correct the bias

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Latent shielding with world models enables safe exploration without requiring safety-relevant abstractions
- Mechanism: The algorithm learns a world model (DreamerV2) that can be used to imagine trajectories in latent space. Safety verification is performed by rolling out the task policy in this learned model and checking if the expected costs exceed a threshold.
- Core assumption: The learned world model accurately captures the relevant dynamics for safety verification
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Previous approaches to shielding assume access to a safety-relevant abstraction of the environment or a high-fidelity simulator. Instead, our work is based on latent shielding - another approach that leverages world models to verify policy roll-outs in the latent space of a learned dynamics model."
  - [section 3.5]: "To estimate μs|=φ we roll-out the world model pθ with the task policy πtask to generate a batch of m sequences of compact latent states"
- Break condition: If the world model has significant prediction errors in safety-critical states, the shielding will fail to detect violations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Environment interface (Atari emulator)
  - World model (DreamerV2 with cost and safety-discount predictors)
  - Task policy and critic
  - Safe policy and critic
  - Safety critics (twin delayed target critics)
  - Shielding module (decides between task and safe policies)

- Critical path:
  1. Collect experience from environment using shielded policy
  2. Train world model on collected data
  3. Train task policy using imagined trajectories
  4. Train safe policy to minimize expected costs
  5. Train safety critics to estimate expected costs under task policy
  6. Use safety critics for shielding decision

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Imagination horizon (H=15) vs. computation cost
  - Number of safety critic samples (m=512) vs. estimation accuracy
  - Bounded safety parameter (ε=0.1) vs. exploration vs. safety
  - Using minimum of two critics vs. single critic with other regularization

- Failure signatures:
  - High safety violations despite shielding: world model or safety critics poorly trained
  - Poor task performance: overly conservative shielding or safe policy not aligned with task
  - Training instability: learning rate issues or insufficient regularization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify shielding reduces violations on simple environments with known safety constraints
  2. Test sensitivity to safety critic sample count (m) on performance and safety
  3. Evaluate impact of imagination horizon (H) on shielding effectiveness vs. computation cost

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the authors derive tighter bounds on the total variation (TV) distance between the approximate transition system and the true transition system to improve the accuracy of safety guarantees?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge the need for tighter bounds on TV distance in Section 3.1, stating "To get similar bounds for the approximate transition system bT we can try to get a bound on the total variation (TV) distance between bT and T. However, this is left for future work."
- Why unresolved: Deriving such bounds is non-trivial and requires further theoretical analysis of the learned dynamics model's approximation error.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof or empirical demonstration showing the relationship between model error and safety violation probability estimates.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the approximate shielding algorithm maintain safety guarantees when the world model's dynamics are not perfect, and how does model error propagate to safety violation estimates?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors use a learned dynamics model for shielding, which introduces approximation errors, but they don't fully characterize how these errors affect safety guarantees.
- Why unresolved: The relationship between model approximation error and safety violation probability estimates is not fully explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies showing the sensitivity of safety guarantees to world model accuracy, or theoretical bounds on the impact of model error on safety violation estimates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of approximate shielding scale with more complex environments beyond the Atari benchmark, particularly in continuous control tasks or real-world robotics applications?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors test their algorithm on a small set of Atari games, which are relatively simple compared to real-world applications.
- Why unresolved: The algorithm's effectiveness in more complex, high-dimensional environments is not demonstrated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the algorithm's performance on more challenging continuous control tasks or real-world robotics applications.

## Limitations

- The method assumes access to state-dependent safety labels, which may not be available in many real-world applications.
- The twin delayed target critics add computational overhead, and their effectiveness relative to simpler alternatives needs further validation.
- The approach is tested on relatively simple Atari environments, and its effectiveness in more complex, high-dimensional environments is not demonstrated.
