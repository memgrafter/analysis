# Evaluating LLMs on Document-Based QA: Exact Answer Selection and Numerical Extraction using Cogtale dataset

## Quick Facts

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07878

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the performance of large language models (GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4) on document-based question-answering tasks using the Cogtale dataset. The Cogtale dataset contains questions requiring exact answer selection from predefined options and numerical extraction from research papers on interventions for older adults. The authors develop a framework that retrieves relevant passages from documents based on the question and uses an LLM to select the correct answer from the options. They evaluate the models on single-select, yes-no, multiple-select, and number extraction question types. GPT-4 achieved an overall accuracy of 41.84% while GPT-3.5-turbo achieved 31.45%. Both models performed better on single-select and yes-no questions but struggled with multiple-select and number extraction.

## Method Summary
The study uses the Cogtale dataset, which consists of research papers on interventions for older adults with questions categorized into single-select, multiple-select, yes-no, and number extraction types. The evaluation framework retrieves relevant passages from documents based on question similarity, then passes the question and retrieved context to either GPT-3.5-turbo or GPT-4 with simple prompts. The models select answers from provided options in a zero-shot setting. Performance is measured by overall accuracy and accuracy per question type, with human expert-tagged responses serving as ground truth.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieved 41.84% accuracy while GPT-3.5-turbo achieved 31.45% on the Cogtale dataset
- Both models performed better on single-select and yes-no questions compared to multiple-select and number extraction
- GPT-4 consistently outperformed GPT-3.5-turbo across all question types

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Retriever -> LLM (GPT-3.5-turbo or GPT-4) -> Prompt -> Evaluation
- Critical path:
  1. Question input
  2. Retriever extracts relevant document chunks
  3. LLM processes question and chunks with prompt
  4. LLM generates answer
  5. Answer is compared to ground truth for evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Retriever complexity vs. accuracy: More sophisticated retrieval methods may improve accuracy but increase computational cost
  - Model choice (GPT-3.5-turbo vs. GPT-4): Higher accuracy with GPT-4 comes at increased cost
  - Prompt simplicity vs. effectiveness: More detailed prompts may guide better responses but increase token usage
- Failure signatures:
  - Low overall accuracy across all question types: Potential issues with retriever or LLM comprehension
  - High accuracy on some question types but low on others: Indicates format-specific challenges for the LLM
  - Consistently incorrect answers for specific options: May suggest bias or misunderstanding in the LLM's reasoning

  2. Test the impact of varying the number of retrieved document chunks on LLM accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different retriever mechanisms affect the performance of LLMs on document-based QA tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "these findings hinge on the assumption that the retrievers furnish pertinent context necessary for accurate responses, emphasizing the need for further research on the efficacy of retriever mechanisms in enhancing question-answering performance."
- Why unresolved: The study assumes that the retriever provides relevant context but does not explore how different retriever mechanisms might impact the accuracy of the LLM's responses.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of different retriever mechanisms (e.g., BM25, dense retrieval) using the same LLM models and question types.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can few-shot prompting strategies significantly improve the performance of LLMs on document-based QA tasks compared to zero-shot approaches?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "Our experiments were performed in the zero-shot setting. Few-shot setting may help to improve the performance of the LLM."
- Why unresolved: The study only used zero-shot prompting, leaving the potential impact of few-shot learning unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting the same experiments with few-shot prompts and comparing the results to zero-shot performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do newer versions of GPT models (e.g., GPT-4-turbo, GPT-5) perform on document-based QA tasks compared to GPT-4?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "The language models exhibit dynamic behavior, and a model update has the potential to enhance or diminish its performance on the dataset."
- Why unresolved: The study uses GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4, but does not evaluate newer or future iterations of these models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the latest GPT models on the same Cogtale dataset and comparing their performance to GPT-4.

## Limitations

- Retriever specification uncertainty: The paper lacks detail on the exact retriever mechanism used, creating a significant gap in understanding how context is provided to the LLMs.
- Prompt design ambiguity: While the paper mentions using simple prompts, it does not specify the exact prompt structure or provide examples.
- Dataset specificity: The Cogtale dataset focuses on interventions for older adults, which may limit generalizability of the findings to other domains or document types.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The overall performance comparison between GPT-3.5-turbo (31.45%) and GPT-4 (41.84%) is well-supported by the presented results and aligns with expectations based on known differences between these models.
- Medium confidence: The claim that single-select and yes-no questions are easier for LLMs than multiple-select and number extraction is supported by the results but could benefit from deeper analysis of failure cases to confirm the underlying reasons.
- Low confidence: The assertion that the retriever's output is the primary determinant of LLM performance is mentioned but not empirically validated in the paper, as there's no analysis of how different retriever qualities affect final answer accuracy.
