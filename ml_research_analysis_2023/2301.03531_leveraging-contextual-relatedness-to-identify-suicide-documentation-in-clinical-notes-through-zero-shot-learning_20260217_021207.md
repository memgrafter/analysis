# Leveraging Contextual Relatedness to Identify Suicide Documentation in Clinical Notes through Zero Shot Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03531
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03531
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Zero-shot learning method achieved AUC of 0.946 in identifying suicide documentation in clinical notes

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a zero-shot learning approach to identify suicide documentation in clinical notes, addressing the challenge of data sparsity in suicide-related training samples. Using U.S. Veterans Affairs clinical notes, the method maps document contents to a semantic space via a deep neural network, leveraging word embeddings and contextual features. The approach successfully identified suicide-related content with 94% accuracy at a 0.90 probability threshold, even in notes lacking relevant ICD-10-CM codes, without requiring manual annotation.

## Method Summary
The study employs a zero-shot learning (ZSL) framework that maps clinical note contents to a semantic space using a deep neural network. The approach utilizes word embeddings and contextual features to capture the semantic relatedness of suicide-related content. By avoiding traditional supervised learning approaches, the method can identify suicide documentation without requiring extensive labeled training data, instead leveraging the contextual relationships between words and concepts within the clinical text.

## Key Results
- ZSL DNN achieved AUC of 0.946 compared to baseline bag-of-words DNN with AUC of 0.47
- At 0.90 probability threshold, method identified suicide-related notes with 94% accuracy
- Successfully identified suicidality in notes lacking relevant ICD-10-CM codes
- Method requires no manual annotation for training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging semantic space mapping through deep neural networks to capture contextual relationships between words and concepts. By using word embeddings that encode semantic similarity, the model can identify suicide-related content even when specific suicide-related terms are absent. The zero-shot learning framework allows the model to generalize from semantic understanding rather than relying on explicit suicide-related keywords or phrases.

## Foundational Learning
- **Zero-shot learning**: Learning paradigm where models can classify examples from classes not seen during training, needed to handle sparse suicide documentation without manual annotation
- **Semantic space mapping**: Process of converting text into high-dimensional vector representations that capture meaning relationships, needed to understand contextual relationships
- **Word embeddings**: Dense vector representations of words that capture semantic similarity, needed to encode meaning relationships between suicide-related and related concepts
- **Contextual features**: Information about word usage patterns and surrounding text, needed to understand suicide-related content in clinical context
- **Deep neural networks**: Multi-layer neural architectures capable of learning complex patterns, needed to model intricate relationships in clinical text
- **Semantic relatedness**: Measure of conceptual similarity between words or documents, needed to identify suicide content through indirect associations

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Clinical notes -> Text preprocessing -> Word embeddings -> DNN layers -> Semantic space mapping -> Probability score

Critical path: The model processes clinical text through embedding layers to capture semantic meaning, then uses deep neural network layers to learn complex patterns of suicide-related content, ultimately producing probability scores indicating likelihood of suicide documentation.

Design tradeoffs: The zero-shot approach sacrifices the precision of supervised learning for the ability to identify suicide content without extensive manual annotation. This trade-off enables broader application but may miss nuanced suicide-related expressions that don't fit the learned semantic patterns.

Failure signatures: The model may fail when clinical notes use highly specialized suicide-related terminology not captured in the training embeddings, or when suicide risk is communicated through non-verbal clinical observations rather than textual descriptions. Notes with significant spelling errors or non-standard abbreviations may also challenge the semantic mapping process.

## Limitations
- Single clinical system (VA notes) limits generalizability across different healthcare settings
- Method depends on quality and coverage of word embeddings, which may miss domain-specific terminology variations
- 0.90 probability threshold may sacrifice sensitivity for specificity, potentially missing some suicide cases
