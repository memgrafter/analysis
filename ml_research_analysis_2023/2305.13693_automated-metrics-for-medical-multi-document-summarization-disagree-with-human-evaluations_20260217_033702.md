# Automated Metrics for Medical Multi-Document Summarization Disagree with Human Evaluations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13693
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13693
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Commonly used automated metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore) poorly align with human judgments in medical multi-document summarization

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the alignment between automated metrics and human assessments of quality in medical multi-document summarization for literature reviews. The researchers conducted human evaluations of 6 systems across 8 quality facets and pairwise preferences, then compared these judgments against automated metrics including ROUGE, BERTScore, Delta-EI, PIO-Overlap, and NLI/STS/ClaimVer. The results reveal a significant disconnect between commonly reported metrics and human-assessed system rankings, with ROUGE and BERTScore showing poor or even anti-correlations with human evaluations. Novel metrics like PIO-Overlap and Delta-EI demonstrate stronger correlations with human judgments, suggesting the need for improved evaluation methods in this domain.

## Method Summary
The researchers evaluated multi-document summarization quality for biomedical literature reviews using submissions from the MSLR-Cochrane shared task. They conducted human evaluations of 6 systems on 8 quality facets (fluency, PIO alignment, effect direction, strength of claim) plus pairwise preferences, using 100 reviews per system. Automated metrics including ROUGE, BERTScore, Delta-EI, PIO-Overlap, and NLI/STS/ClaimVer were computed for each generated summary. The study then analyzed correlations between automated metrics and human judgments to assess alignment, identifying which metrics best capture human-assessed quality in this domain.

## Key Results
- ROUGE and BERTScore showed poor correlation or anti-correlation with human-assessed system rankings
- PIO-Overlap and Delta-EI metrics demonstrated stronger alignment with human evaluations
- The study provides a dataset of human-assessed summary quality to support future metric development
- Results highlight the need for improved automated evaluation methods in medical multi-document summarization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study reveals that traditional n-gram overlap metrics (ROUGE) and embedding-based similarity measures (BERTScore) fail to capture the semantic and structural requirements of medical literature reviews. These metrics are designed for general text similarity and do not account for the specific quality dimensions important in medical summarization, such as Population-Intervention-Outcome (PIO) alignment and effect interpretation. The proposed metrics (PIO-Overlap, Delta-EI) are explicitly designed to evaluate these domain-specific aspects, leading to better alignment with human judgments.

## Foundational Learning
- MSLR-Cochrane shared task: A benchmark for medical multi-document summarization focusing on systematic literature reviews - needed for understanding the specific domain and task context
- PIO framework (Population-Intervention-Outcome): A structured approach for representing medical evidence - needed to evaluate how well summaries capture key medical information
- Pairwise preference annotation: A human evaluation method where annotators choose between two system outputs - needed for robust quality assessment beyond absolute ratings

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: MSLR-Cochrane Submissions -> Human Evaluation (8 facets + pairwise) -> Automated Metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore, Delta-EI, PIO-Overlap, NLI/STS/ClaimVer) -> Correlation Analysis

Critical Path: Human evaluations serve as ground truth for assessing automated metric quality, with correlation analysis revealing which metrics best capture human-assessed quality

Design Tradeoffs: The study prioritizes comprehensive human evaluation over larger system coverage, focusing on depth of analysis rather than breadth of systems tested

Failure Signatures: Anti-correlation between metrics and human rankings suggests metrics may be capturing irrelevant features (e.g., lexical overlap without semantic accuracy)

## Limitations
- Small scale human evaluation (6 systems, 100 reviews per system) may not capture full task diversity
- Focus on MSLR-Cochrane domain limits generalizability to other medical summarization tasks
- PIO-Overlap metric performance depends on underlying PIO extraction model whose characteristics are not fully characterized

## Confidence
- High confidence: Automated metrics (ROUGE, BERTScore) show poor correlation with human judgments in this domain
- Medium confidence: Novel metrics (Delta-EI, PIO-Overlap) demonstrate improved alignment with human evaluations
- Low confidence: Generalizability of findings to other medical summarization tasks beyond MSLR-Cochrane

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a larger-scale human evaluation with more systems and diverse medical domains to verify the robustness of findings
2. Perform ablation studies on the PIO-Overlap metric to quantify the impact of the underlying PIO extraction model's performance
3. Test the proposed metrics on other biomedical summarization tasks (e.g., radiology report summarization) to assess domain transferability
