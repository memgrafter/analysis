# Mining bias-target Alignment from Voronoi Cells

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03691
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03691
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed bias-agnostic method achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art supervised approaches even with multiple biases

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a bias-agnostic approach to mitigate biases in deep neural networks by mining bias-target alignment from Voronoi cells. The method identifies when a model has learned bias features by monitoring misclassified samples' distances to Voronoi hyperplanes, then uses this information to discourage bias propagation through reweighting and information removal. Experiments on Biased MNIST, Multi-Color MNIST, CelebA, and ImageNet datasets show the approach achieves competitive performance against both supervised and bias-specific debiasing methods.

## Method Summary
The method extracts bias-target alignment information from misclassified samples' distances to Voronoi hyperplanes at the bottleneck layer, using peaks in these distances to identify when bias features have been learned. This alignment information is then used in two ways: reweighting the loss function to emphasize bias-misaligned samples, and adding an information removal head that minimizes mutual information between bias predictions and true bias labels. The approach requires only the target labels and dataset, making it bias-agnostic and applicable without explicit bias annotations.

## Key Results
- Achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art supervised approaches on Biased MNIST with various correlation levels
- Successfully handles multiple biases in Multi-Color MNIST where samples have dual-color backgrounds
- Demonstrates effectiveness on real-world CelebA dataset for gender-biased hair color classification
- Shows consistent improvement across different architectures including ResNet-18 and custom CNNs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Identifying bias-target alignment through Voronoi cell geometry allows bias-agnostic debiasing without needing explicit bias labels. The method monitors misclassified samples' distances to the Voronoi hyperplane of the correct class at the bottleneck layer. When this distance peaks, the model has learned bias features, and the corresponding bias-target alignment can be extracted. This works because at some point during training, the model will adapt to bias and misclassified samples will be attracted toward the wrong class centroid, creating a measurable geometric signature.

### Mechanism 2
Reweighting loss based on bias-target alignment improves generalization by emphasizing bias-misaligned samples. Samples are assigned weights inversely proportional to their bias alignment (aligned samples get lower weight, misaligned get higher weight), making the model learn more from samples that contain useful generalization information. This works because bias-misaligned samples contain more task-relevant information than bias-aligned ones, and emphasizing them during training leads to better generalization.

### Mechanism 3
Removing bias alignment information at the bottleneck through mutual information minimization prevents the model from learning biased representations. An auxiliary Information Removal Head (IRH) is trained to predict bias labels, and the mutual information between its predictions and the true bias labels (conditioned on bias-target misalignment) is minimized, effectively removing the link between bias and target class representations. This works because bias alignment information is extractable at the bottleneck layer and can be explicitly minimized without harming task performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Vanilla model -> Bias extraction and alignment detection -> Debiasing model with weighted loss and IRH
- **Critical path:** 1) Train vanilla model to convergence 2) Monitor misclassified samples' distances to Voronoi hyperplanes to find e* (peak distance epoch) 3) Extract bias-target alignment labels from vanilla model at e* 4) Train debiasing model with weighted loss and IRH minimization
- **Design tradeoffs:** Using same architecture for vanilla and debiasing models simplifies implementation but may limit flexibility; monitoring every epoch vs. every few iterations balances accuracy of e* detection with computational cost; weighting scheme assumes balanced classes
- **Failure signatures:** Extremely high standard deviation in test accuracy indicates noisy bias extraction; vanilla model never converges suggests poor initialization or learning rate issues; IRH fails to learn indicates bias information is not present at bottleneck
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Reproduce Biased MNIST results with ρ = 0.99 to validate basic pipeline works 2) Test Multi-Color MNIST with both biases to verify multi-bias handling 3) Compare vanilla vs. debiased model on bias-conflicting subset of CelebA to measure debiasing effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal learning rate for the vanilla model in extreme bias-target alignment scenarios (e.g., ρ = 0.999 in Biased MNIST)?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention that tuning the learning rate is crucial in extreme scenarios, but do not provide specific guidance.
- Why unresolved: The paper only briefly mentions the importance of the learning rate without exploring different values or providing a methodology for determining the optimal rate.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the learning rate across a range of values for different bias levels would identify optimal rates and provide guidelines for practitioners.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed method perform when the bias-target alignment is 100% (ρ = 1), where no bias-contrary samples exist?
- Basis in paper: The authors state that their method would fail in this scenario as there would be no bias-misaligned samples to extract information from.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore this edge case or propose any modifications to handle it.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on synthetic datasets with ρ = 1 and comparing performance to other debiasing approaches would validate the failure mode and potentially inspire adaptations for this scenario.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the bias-target alignment extraction technique be generalized to other machine learning tasks beyond image classification?
- Basis in paper: The method relies on Voronoi cells and latent representations, which are applicable to many ML tasks, but the paper only demonstrates results on image classification datasets.
- Why unresolved: The authors do not test or discuss the method's applicability to other domains like NLP, tabular data, or reinforcement learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the technique to diverse ML tasks (e.g., text classification, fraud detection) and measuring performance relative to task-specific debiasing methods would establish its generalizability.

## Limitations
- The method may fail when biases are extremely strong (ρ=0.999) due to noisy distance metrics
- Performance depends on bias being extractable at the bottleneck layer, which may not hold for all bias types
- The reweighting scheme assumes bias-misaligned samples are representative of true data distribution, which may not be true in highly skewed datasets

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Basic pipeline of extracting bias information from misclassified samples' distances to Voronoi cells and using this for reweighting loss
- **Medium confidence**: Information removal head's ability to eliminate bias information without harming task performance
- **Low confidence**: Method's scalability to complex real-world biases and multiple interacting biases

## Next Checks

1. **Robustness to extreme bias**: Test the method on Biased MNIST with ρ=0.999 and measure whether the distance metric becomes too noisy to extract reliable bias labels, as suggested by the high standard deviation in reported results.

2. **Multi-bias interaction**: Evaluate on datasets with three or more interacting biases to verify that the Voronoi-based extraction can disentangle multiple bias sources, extending beyond the dual-color MNIST experiment.

3. **Real-world generalization**: Apply the method to a real-world dataset with known spurious correlations (e.g., medical imaging with demographic biases) and compare against domain-specific debiasing approaches to assess practical utility.
