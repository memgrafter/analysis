# The Second Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge

## Executive Summary
The Second Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge assessed the state-of-the-art in monocular depth estimation using the diverse SYNS-Patches dataset. Eight teams outperformed the provided SotA baseline across various supervision strategies, including fully-supervised, self-supervised, multi-task, and proxy depth methods. The top supervised submission improved relative F-Score by 27.62%, while the top self-supervised submission improved it by 16.61%. Supervised methods generally leveraged large collections of diverse datasets, while self-supervised methods focused on network architecture improvements and pretrained backbones. The results highlight the need for more varied training data, particularly for self-supervised methods, and the ongoing challenge of accurate depth boundary prediction.

## Method Summary
The challenge focused on monocular depth estimation (MDE) using the SYNS-Patches dataset, which contains diverse images from urban, residential, industrial, agricultural, natural, and indoor scenes with high-quality dense LiDAR ground truth. Eight unique submissions used various supervision strategies including fully-supervised (ground-truth depth), self-supervised (photometric reconstruction loss), multi-task, and proxy depth methods. Methods ranged from ConvNeXt-B/ResNet encoders with Monodepth decoders to transformer-based architectures like BEiT384-L and MonoViT. All methods were median aligned w.r.t. the ground-truth, regardless of the supervision used, to ensure identical evaluations and fair comparisons. Performance was evaluated using image-based (MAE, RMSE, AbsRel), pointcloud-based (F-Score, IoU, Chamfer distance), and edge-based metrics (edge accuracy/completion) computed at depth boundary pixels.

## Key Results
- The top supervised submission improved relative F-Score by 27.62% compared to the baseline.
- The top self-supervised submission improved relative F-Score by 16.61% compared to the baseline.
- Supervised methods generally outperformed self-supervised methods, but self-supervised techniques showed significant improvements over the baseline, especially in pointcloud reconstructions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods be improved to generalize effectively to indoor environments, where current approaches trained on automotive data show significant performance gaps?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that self-supervised methods "show significantly inferior performance in Indoor settings, as they lack the data diversity to generalize," and supervised methods using indoor datasets outperformed self-supervised approaches by a large margin in indoor scenes.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies the data diversity gap, it does not propose specific architectural or training modifications that would enable self-supervised methods to generalize to indoor scenes without requiring additional labeled datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results demonstrating improved indoor performance using self-supervised methods with modified architectures, synthetic data augmentation, or novel proxy depth supervision techniques.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What causes the "halos" or interpolation artifacts at depth boundaries in monocular depth estimation, and how can they be effectively reduced or eliminated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "accurate depth boundary prediction is still a highly challenging problem" and that most methods frequently predict "halos, representative of interpolation artifacts between the foreground and background."
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies this as a challenge, it does not analyze the root causes of these artifacts or propose specific solutions to address them.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies showing the effectiveness of different architectural designs, loss functions, or post-processing techniques in reducing boundary artifacts, along with analysis of why certain approaches work better than others.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can monocular depth estimation methods handle transparent and specular surfaces (e.g., glass) that are not captured by LiDAR or photometric constraints?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how "transparent surfaces, such as the glass, are not captured when using LiDAR or photometric constraints" and notes that "it is unclear how an accurate supervision signal could be generated for these cases."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a limitation but does not propose solutions for handling transparent surfaces in monocular depth estimation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstration of depth estimation methods that can accurately predict depth for transparent surfaces, either through multi-modal sensing, synthetic data generation, or novel training strategies that account for these challenging cases.

## Limitations

- The SYNS-Patches dataset, while diverse, may not fully represent all real-world scenarios, potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings.
- The evaluation protocol relies on median alignment, which may not fully capture absolute depth accuracy differences between methods.
- The report focuses on submissions that outperformed the baseline, potentially introducing selection bias in the analysis of successful approaches.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Claims regarding the superior performance of supervised methods over self-supervised ones, supported by quantitative metrics across all evaluation categories. The observation that large-scale dataset diversity improves generalization is well-supported by the results.

**Medium Confidence**: Claims about specific architectural improvements (e.g., ConvNeXt, transformer-based encoders) benefiting self-supervised methods are supported by performance gains but lack detailed ablation studies. The assertion that depth boundary prediction remains challenging is based on edge-based metrics but could benefit from qualitative analysis.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about the optimal dataset composition for supervised methods are speculative, as the report doesn't provide detailed analysis of which specific datasets contributed most to performance gains. The relative importance of various supervision strategies (multi-task, proxy depth) compared to pure supervised or self-supervised approaches is not thoroughly explored.

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset Coverage Analysis**: Conduct a systematic analysis of the training datasets used by top-performing supervised methods to identify which specific scenes and depth distributions contributed most to generalization performance on SYNS-Patches.

2. **Architectural Ablation Studies**: Perform controlled experiments isolating the impact of specific architectural changes (e.g., deformable convolutions, transformer-based encoders) on self-supervised depth estimation performance, separating architectural improvements from dataset effects.

3. **Cross-Environment Generalization Test**: Evaluate the best-performing models on an entirely new dataset (not used in training or validation) with different environmental characteristics to assess true generalization capabilities beyond the SYNS-Patches test set.
