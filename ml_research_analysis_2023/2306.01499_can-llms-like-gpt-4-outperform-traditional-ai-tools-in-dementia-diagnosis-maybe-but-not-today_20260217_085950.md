# Can LLMs like GPT-4 outperform traditional AI tools in dementia diagnosis? Maybe, but not today

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01499
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01499
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: GPT-4 shows promising performance in dementia diagnosis but currently does not surpass traditional AI tools like RRL in accuracy

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether large language models like GPT-4 can outperform traditional AI tools in dementia diagnosis. The authors design prompt templates to convert diagnostic tasks into multiple-choice questions, enabling GPT-4 to generate diagnoses on real clinical datasets (ADNI and PUMCH). While GPT-4 demonstrates competitive performance and can match or exceed simple traditional models in certain scenarios, it currently falls short of traditional AI tools' accuracy, particularly in complex multi-class classification tasks. The study highlights GPT-4's advantages in leveraging existing medical knowledge and providing interpretable explanations, while also identifying key limitations including the inability to fine-tune and high input quality requirements.

## Method Summary
The researchers designed prompt templates to convert dementia diagnosis into multiple-choice questions, enabling GPT-4 to generate diagnostic results. They conducted experiments on two real clinical datasets (ADNI and PUMCH) comparing GPT-4 against traditional AI tools (CART, Logistic Regression, RRL, Random Forest, XGBoost) and doctors. The study employed both zero-shot and few-shot learning settings, with comprehensive evaluation across binary and ternary classification tasks. Data preprocessing included feature extraction and train/test splitting based on roster ID to prevent data leakage.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 matches or outperforms simple traditional models (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree) in some binary classification scenarios
- GPT-4 struggles with complex multi-class classification (CN vs MCI vs Dementia) compared to traditional AI tools
- GPT-4's explanations are readable but may not faithfully reflect its actual decision-making process

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-4 can match or outperform simple traditional models in some dementia diagnosis scenarios under zero-shot or few-shot settings.
- Mechanism: GPT-4 leverages its pre-trained knowledge from massive cross-disciplinary corpora to generate diagnostic results without requiring specialized training datasets.
- Core assumption: The medical knowledge embedded in GPT-4's training corpus is sufficiently relevant and accurate for dementia diagnosis tasks.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states GPT-4 "can match or outperform supervised learning models like Logistic Regression and Decision Tree in some scenarios"
- Break condition: When required medical domain knowledge exceeds what was covered in GPT-4's training corpus

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-4 can provide interpretable explanations for its diagnostic decisions that are readable and understandable.
- Mechanism: GPT-4's natural language generation capabilities allow it to articulate the reasoning behind its diagnoses using coherent, contextually relevant text.
- Core assumption: The ability to generate coherent text translates to generating medically meaningful explanations.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states GPT-4 "has the ability to provide interpretable explanations for its decisions"
- Break condition: When explanations are inconsistent with actual decision-making process or medically incorrect

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-4 can utilize existing medical expertise for diagnosis by recognizing cognitive tests and their corresponding functions.
- Mechanism: GPT-4's pre-training enables it to associate specific test names with their corresponding cognitive functions.
- Core assumption: GPT-4 has been exposed to sufficient medical literature during training to develop these associations.
- Evidence anchors: Paper states GPT-4 "can utilize existing medical expertise for diagnosis" by recognizing cognitive test functions
- Break condition: When encountering novel or highly specialized tests not well-represented in training data

  - Why needed here: Study shows specific prompt templates significantly improve GPT-4's performance by converting diagnostic problems into multiple-choice format
  - Quick check question: What are the key components of an effective prompt template for converting medical diagnosis into LLM-friendly formats?

- Concept: Zero-shot vs few-shot learning differences
  - Why needed here: Study demonstrates few-shot learning can improve GPT-4's performance compared to zero-shot learning in some cases
  - Quick check question: How does providing a few examples in the prompt affect GPT-4's performance on specialized medical tasks?

- Concept: Interpretability vs faithfulness in AI explanations
  - Why needed here: Study distinguishes between GPT-4's ability to provide readable explanations (interpretability) and whether those explanations accurately reflect its decision-making (faithfulness)
  - Quick check question: What's the difference between a model being interpretable versus being faithful to its actual reasoning?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing pipeline → Prompt template generation module → GPT-4 API interface → Evaluation framework comparing against traditional ML models and doctors
- Critical path: Data preprocessing → Prompt template generation → GPT-4 inference → Result interpretation → Comparison with baseline models
- Design tradeoffs: Using prompt templates instead of direct table input trades off input efficiency for better LLM comprehension; limiting max_tokens to 1 improves response precision but may lose context; temperature adjustment affects determinism vs creativity
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on PUMCH-T dataset suggests model struggles with complex multi-class problems; sensitivity to input quality indicates robustness issues; inconsistency between explanations and conclusions reveals faithfulness problems
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test different prompt template variations (with/without few-shot examples, different formatting) to identify optimal configuration
  2. Compare zero-shot vs few-shot performance across different dataset sizes to quantify context value
  3. Conduct ablation study on input features to determine which cognitive test results are most influential in GPT-4's decisions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of fine-tuning GPT-4 on its performance in dementia diagnosis tasks?
- Basis in paper: Authors discuss the current inability to fine-tune GPT-4 as a limitation affecting its performance
- Why unresolved: Paper does not provide experimental results comparing GPT-4's performance with and without fine-tuning
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing GPT-4's performance before and after fine-tuning using labeled dementia cases

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does GPT-4's performance compare to traditional AI tools when handling different types of dementia cases (early vs. late-stage)?
- Basis in paper: Authors note their tasks involve early diagnosis of dementia, which is more challenging than general diagnosis
- Why unresolved: Paper does not provide detailed breakdown of performance across different dementia stages
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive analysis of GPT-4's performance on various dementia cases categorized by stage or severity

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the specific reasons for inconsistencies between GPT-4's explanations and its final diagnostic conclusions?
- Basis in paper: Authors mention GPT-4's reasons and conclusions might be inconsistent, indicating faithfulness cannot be guaranteed
- Why unresolved: Paper does not provide detailed analysis of factors contributing to these inconsistencies
- What evidence would resolve it: Qualitative and quantitative analysis identifying patterns that lead to inconsistencies and proposing improvement methods

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does GPT-4's performance in dementia diagnosis tasks generalize to other medical domains and tasks?
- Basis in paper: Authors suggest future research should investigate GPT-4's performance in other medical domains
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses solely on dementia diagnosis tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of GPT-4's performance across various medical domains and tasks

## Limitations
- Study findings based on relatively small clinical datasets (253-375 subjects), raising generalizability concerns
- GPT-4's explanations, while readable, may not faithfully reflect its actual reasoning process, limiting clinical utility
- Inability to fine-tune GPT-4 for specific medical domains represents a fundamental constraint compared to traditional AI tools

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: GPT-4 can provide readable explanations for its diagnostic decisions, though their faithfulness remains uncertain
- **Medium confidence**: GPT-4 matches or outperforms simple traditional models in certain scenarios, particularly with binary classification tasks
- **Medium confidence**: GPT-4 struggles with complex multi-class classification and shows inconsistent performance across different datasets

## Next Checks
1. Conduct external validation on larger, more diverse clinical datasets with at least 1,000 subjects across multiple clinical centers to assess generalizability
2. Perform ablation studies comparing GPT-4's diagnostic performance with and without its natural language explanations to quantify interpretability value
3. Test GPT-4's sensitivity to input quality by systematically introducing noise and missing values into clinical data to establish robustness thresholds
