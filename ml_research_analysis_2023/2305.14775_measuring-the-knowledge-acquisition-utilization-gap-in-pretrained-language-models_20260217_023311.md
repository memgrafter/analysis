# Measuring the Knowledge Acquisition-Utilization Gap in Pretrained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14775
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14775
- Authors:
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: Introduces XTEVAL framework revealing significant gaps between acquired and utilized knowledge in PLMs across scales

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the XTEVAL framework to systematically measure parametric knowledge utilization in pretrained language models. The framework extracts knowledge from model parameters, constructs downstream tasks from this extracted knowledge, and measures how well models can utilize their possessed knowledge. When applied to encyclopedic knowledge across PLM sizes from 125M to 13B parameters, the study finds a significant gap between acquired and utilized knowledge, limited robustness under distribution shifts, and persistent utilization gaps even as model size increases.

## Method Summary
The XTEVAL framework operates in three stages: (1) knowledge extraction using soft-prompting to identify facts the model can predict correctly from a diagnostic dataset (LAMA), (2) downstream task construction by converting extracted knowledge into document retrieval instances, and (3) evaluation through fine-tuning and testing on this constructed task. The framework uses contrastive learning (InfoNCE loss) to train models to retrieve relevant documents, measuring the gap between what knowledge a model possesses versus what it can actually utilize.

## Key Results
- Significant gap exists between knowledge acquired during pretraining and knowledge that can be utilized in downstream tasks
- Larger models close the acquired knowledge gap but the utilized knowledge gap persists
- Knowledge utilization shows limited robustness under distribution shifts (disjoint relation types)
- Results hold across multiple PLM architectures (BERT, RoBERTa, GPT-Neo, OPT) and sizes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Models can extract parametric knowledge from their parameters without fine-tuning
- Mechanism: Soft-prompting techniques allow models to generate structured predictions based on learned parameter representations
- Core assumption: Knowledge is encoded in parameters in an accessible format
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "parametric knowledge resides reliably within a subset of trained parameters in pre-trained models (PLMs)"
  - [section 2.1]: "soft-prompting (Qin and Eisner, 2021) to obtain the model's predictions, as it enhances prediction consistency"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence, only indirect mentions of soft-prompting approaches
- Break condition: If knowledge is not encoded in accessible format or soft-prompting fails

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Downstream task performance reflects knowledge utilization
- Mechanism: Tasks constructed exclusively from extracted knowledge ensure performance measures knowledge utilization
- Core assumption: Task success requires utilizing parametric knowledge
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Performance on this task thus depends exclusively on utilizing the model's possessed knowledge"
  - [section 2.1]: "By constructing the task based on the model's pre-existing knowledge, we ensure that the model is evaluated solely on its possessed knowledge"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence, no direct corpus support for this evaluation paradigm
- Break condition: If model solves tasks through heuristics without utilizing parametric knowledge

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Scaling model size reduces acquired knowledge gap but not utilization gap
- Mechanism: Larger models encode more knowledge, reducing difference between total and identifiable knowledge
- Core assumption: Scaling increases knowledge capacity rather than utilization capability
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "larger models close the acquired knowledge gap but the utilized knowledge gap remains"
  - [section 5]: "We observe the following trends: First, we confirm that a greater fraction of knowledge triples in D can be identified in larger models"
  - [corpus]: No corpus evidence for scaling effects on utilization gaps
- Break condition: If scaling affects both gaps proportionally or larger models show improved utilization

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Knowledge extraction from PLMs
  - Why needed here: Framework relies on extracting knowledge triples from model parameters as diagnostic dataset
  - Quick check question: Can you explain how soft-prompting differs from discrete prompting in extracting knowledge from PLMs?

- Concept: Contrastive learning objectives
  - Why needed here: Downstream task uses InfoNCE loss to train model to retrieve relevant documents
  - Quick check question: What is the purpose of using multiple negative samples in the contrastive loss formulation?

- Concept: Distributional shift in evaluation
  - Why needed here: Tests robustness by creating train/test splits with disjoint relation types
  - Quick check question: Why would having disjoint relations between train and test sets create a distributional shift?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Knowledge extraction module (soft-prompting) -> Document generation module (stochastic template filling) -> Cross-encoder retrieval module (BERT-style architecture) -> Training loop with InfoNCE loss

- Critical path: Knowledge extraction → Downstream task creation → Fine-tuning → Evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using soft-prompts vs. discrete prompts for knowledge extraction
  - Number of negative samples in contrastive learning
  - Train/test split strategy for downstream task

- Failure signatures:
  - High variance in knowledge extraction across seeds
  - Downstream task performance not improving with more negatives
  - Performance drop under distributional shift

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Extract knowledge from small PLM (125M) and verify it can predict at least 30% of LAMA facts
  2. Create downstream task with 4 negatives per type and measure baseline performance
  3. Test distributional shift by creating disjoint relation train/test splits and measure performance degradation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do larger language models perform on tasks requiring knowledge utilization compared to smaller models, beyond encyclopedic knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the effect of scaling laws on knowledge utilization gaps in larger models, specifically OPT models ranging from 125M to 13B parameters
- Why unresolved: The study primarily focuses on encyclopedic knowledge and does not explore the performance of larger models on other types of knowledge, such as procedural or task knowledge
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments using XTEVAL on various types of knowledge, such as procedural and task knowledge, across different model sizes to compare their performance and knowledge utilization gaps

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the underlying factors that contribute to the gap between identifiable and usable knowledge in language models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies a gap between identifiable and usable knowledge in language models but does not explore the underlying factors contributing to this gap
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on measuring the gap and its persistence across different model sizes but does not delve into the reasons behind the gap's existence
- What evidence would resolve it: Investigate the role of model architecture, training data, and fine-tuning methods in influencing the gap between identifiable and usable knowledge in language models

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of language models on downstream tasks change when exposed to a more diverse range of relation types during fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that language models struggle to utilize their knowledge on examples with unseen relation types during fine-tuning, indicating a limitation in their generalization capabilities
- Why unresolved: The study only examines the effect of a distributional shift in relation types on knowledge utilization but does not explore the impact of a more diverse range of relation types during fine-tuning
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments where language models are fine-tuned on a more diverse set of relation types and evaluate their performance on downstream tasks to determine if exposure to a wider range of relation types improves their knowledge utilization and generalization capabilities

## Limitations
- Knowledge extraction reliability: Soft-prompting approach may introduce significant variability, with extracted knowledge sets varying across random seeds
- Downstream task validity: Artificially constructed queries and documents differ substantially from real-world usage patterns
- Scaling effects interpretation: Causal mechanism for why larger models close acquired gap but not utilization gap remains unclear

## Confidence
- High confidence: Knowledge acquisition-utilization gap exists across all tested PLM sizes
- Medium confidence: Larger models close acquired knowledge gap but not utilized knowledge gap
- Low confidence: Knowledge utilization is not robust to distributional shifts based on limited experiments

## Next Checks
1. **Stability analysis of knowledge extraction**: Run knowledge extraction with 10+ random seeds on multiple PLM architectures and report variance in extracted knowledge sets and downstream task performance
2. **Alternative downstream tasks**: Evaluate the same extracted knowledge on a more realistic downstream task, such as relation classification or open-domain question answering, to validate whether the utilization gap generalizes beyond document retrieval
3. **Fine-tuning stability**: Investigate whether the knowledge utilization gap persists when models are fine-tuned on natural language instructions rather than the artificial document retrieval task, and whether few-shot learning capabilities improve with model scale
