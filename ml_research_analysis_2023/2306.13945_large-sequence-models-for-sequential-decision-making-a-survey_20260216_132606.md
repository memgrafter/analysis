# Large Sequence Models for Sequential Decision-Making: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13945
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13945
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Comprehensive survey examining Transformer-based sequence modeling approaches for addressing key challenges in reinforcement learning

## Executive Summary
This survey comprehensively examines recent works leveraging large sequence models, particularly the Transformer, for sequential decision-making tasks. It highlights the Transformer's advantages in addressing key challenges in reinforcement learning, including sample efficiency, credit assignment, and partial observability. By treating sequential decision-making as sequence modeling, the Transformer enables effective handling of long sequences and mitigates distribution shifts. The survey categorizes approaches based on their utilization of the Transformer, including offline RL, model-based RL, meta RL, and multi-agent RL. It also explores the potential for building large decision models and discusses future research directions, such as theoretical foundations, network architectures, algorithms, and training systems.

## Method Summary
The survey analyzes recent approaches that leverage Transformers for sequential decision-making by framing RL problems as sequence modeling tasks. These methods take trajectories of states, actions, and rewards as input sequences and use supervised learning to predict actions or next states/rewards. The core approach involves pre-training on large datasets of diverse trajectories followed by fine-tuning on specific tasks, leveraging the Transformer's attention mechanism to handle long-range dependencies and partial observability.

## Key Results
- Transformers can address key RL challenges including sample efficiency, credit assignment, and partial observability
- Sequence modeling approaches can leverage supervised learning techniques on pre-collected data to bypass trial-and-error exploration
- The survey identifies four main categories of Transformer-based approaches: offline RL, model-based RL, meta RL, and multi-agent RL
- Building large decision models similar to language models in NLP is identified as a promising future direction

- **Claim:** Treating sequential decision-making as sequence modeling mitigates long-standing RL challenges like sample efficiency, credit assignment, and partial observability.
- **Mechanism:** By modeling trajectories as sequences of states, actions, and rewards, sequence models (especially Transformers) can leverage supervised learning techniques to learn policies, bypassing the need for trial-and-error exploration.
- **Core assumption:** The sequential structure of decision-making data can be effectively captured by sequence models, and the distribution shifts inherent in RL can be managed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Transformers excel at modeling long sequences, which is crucial for addressing the partial observability problem in RL.
- **Mechanism:** Transformers use attention mechanisms to selectively focus on relevant parts of the input sequence, allowing them to build long-term dependencies and recover missing information from historical observations.
- **Core assumption:** The attention mechanism in Transformers can effectively model the dependencies between different parts of the input sequence, and the model has sufficient capacity to capture long-term dependencies.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input trajectories (states, actions, rewards) -> Transformer layers with attention -> Predicted actions/next states
- **Critical path:** Pre-training on large datasets → Fine-tuning on specific tasks → Online interaction for further improvement
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Model size vs. computational cost: Larger models may offer better performance but require more resources
  - Sequence length vs. attention complexity: Longer sequences allow for better modeling of long-term dependencies but increase computational cost
  - Modality integration: Incorporating multiple modalities (e.g., vision, language) can enhance performance but adds complexity
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor performance on tasks with long-term dependencies
  - Difficulty generalizing to new tasks or environments
  - High computational cost or memory requirements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can the sequence modeling approach effectively address the distribution shift problem in reinforcement learning?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper discusses the distribution shift problem in reinforcement learning and mentions that sequence models like the Transformer can potentially mitigate this issue by leveraging previous experience from offline data.
- **Why unresolved:** The effectiveness of sequence models in addressing distribution shift has not been fully validated through extensive experiments and theoretical analysis.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comprehensive empirical studies comparing the performance of sequence models with traditional RL methods in various environments with different levels of distribution shift, along with theoretical analysis of the generalization guarantees provided by sequence models.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How can large decision models be trained efficiently and effectively to achieve strong generalization across diverse tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper discusses the potential of building large decision models and highlights the need for efficient training systems to support their development.
- **Why unresolved:** The training of large decision models involves challenges such as massive memory and computational requirements, data distribution, and coordination between inference and training modes.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Research on novel training algorithms, parallel computing techniques, and resource management strategies specifically designed for large decision models, along with empirical results demonstrating their effectiveness in training and generalization.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the theoretical foundations of sequence modeling for sequential decision-making, and how can they be integrated with traditional RL methods?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper mentions the lack of theoretical guarantees for policy optimization in sequence modeling approaches compared to traditional RL methods.
- **Why unresolved:** The theoretical analysis of sequence modeling for sequential decision-making is still in its early stages, and the integration of sequence modeling with traditional RL methods that offer theoretical guarantees is an open problem.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development of theoretical frameworks that establish the convergence and optimality guarantees of sequence modeling approaches, along with research on hybrid methods that combine the strengths of sequence modeling and traditional RL in terms of both empirical performance and theoretical foundations.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness heavily depends on quality and diversity of pre-training datasets
- Computational cost of Transformers for long sequences remains significant for real-time applications
- Theoretical foundations connecting sequence modeling to optimal decision-making are still underdeveloped

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The survey's categorization of different Transformer-based approaches (offline RL, model-based RL, meta RL, multi-agent RL) is well-supported by the literature and provides a clear taxonomy
- **Medium Confidence:** Claims about Transformers addressing partial observability and long-term dependencies are supported by empirical results but lack rigorous theoretical guarantees
- **Low Confidence:** Predictions about building general-purpose "large decision models" similar to GPT-3 are highly speculative, with limited empirical evidence beyond a few pioneering works like Gato
