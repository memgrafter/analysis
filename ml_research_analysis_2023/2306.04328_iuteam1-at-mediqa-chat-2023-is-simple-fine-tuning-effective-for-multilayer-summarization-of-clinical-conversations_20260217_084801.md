# IUTEAM1 at MEDIQA-Chat 2023: Is simple fine tuning effective for multilayer summarization of clinical conversations?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04328
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04328
- Reference count: 2
- Ensemble of section-wise models achieves highest ROUGE scores for clinical conversation summarization

## Executive Summary
This study explores ensemble approaches for summarizing clinical conversations into medical chart notes. The authors evaluate three methods: a single summarization model, an ensemble of models trained on separate chart note sections, and a multi-layer approach where ensemble results are further summarized. Using the LSG BART model for all experiments, they find that the section-wise ensemble approach outperforms the single model baseline on ROUGE metrics. However, the multi-layer approach fails to improve accuracy, suggesting that simple fine-tuning of multi-layer ensembles doesn't enhance coherency. The findings indicate that section-wise ensemble models are a promising strategy for clinical conversation summarization.

## Method Summary
The study employs three approaches for clinical conversation summarization: 1) a single LSG BART model trained on full chart notes, 2) an ensemble of LSG BART models each trained on separate chart note sections (HPI, PE, Assessment & Plan), and 3) a multi-layer approach where section-wise ensemble outputs are passed through another LSG BART model. The LSG BART model handles long documents using local, sparse, and global attention mechanisms. Models are trained for 20 epochs with decaying learning rate starting at 5e-5, using mixed precision training. The dataset consists of doctor-patient conversations and corresponding chart notes from MEDIQA-Chat 2023 Task B (67 training, 20 validation, 40 hidden test conversations).

## Key Results
- Section-wise ensemble models outperform single model baseline on ROUGE scores
- Multi-layer approach with second LSG BART model fails to improve accuracy
- Simple fine-tuning of multi-layer ensembles does not enhance coherency
- PubMed fine-tuning degrades performance due to domain mismatch

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Section-wise ensemble models outperform single models because different chart note sections have distinct content and style, allowing specialized models to capture section-specific coherence.
- Mechanism: By training separate LSG BART models on individual chart note sections (e.g., HPI, PE, Assessment & Plan), each model learns the internal coherence and vocabulary of its section, leading to higher ROUGE scores when results are concatenated.
- Core assumption: The internal coherence of a single section is higher than the coherence of a full chart note, making section specialization beneficial.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The ensemble approach trained on separate sections outperforms the single model baseline and achieves the highest ROUGE scores."
  - [section] "The primary motivation for using an ensemble of models is that text internal to a particular section of the chart note is much more coherent than external to it."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: Corpus analysis shows only 0.0 average citations for related papers, suggesting limited validation of this mechanism in the broader literature.
- Break condition: If the section boundaries are not well-defined or the sections are too heterogeneous, the benefit of specialization may be lost.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Simple fine-tuning of a multi-layer ensemble does not improve accuracy because the second layer model cannot effectively integrate section-specific outputs into a coherent whole.
- Mechanism: The second LSG BART model in the multi-layer approach fails to improve coherency because it receives already-section-specific outputs that may not align well when combined, leading to lower ROUGE scores in assessment and plan sections.
- Core assumption: A single summarization model can effectively integrate specialized section outputs into a coherent chart note.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "However, the multi-layer approach does not improve accuracy, indicating that simple fine-tuning of multi-layer ensembles does not enhance coherency."
  - [section] "However, attempting to pass the results to another LSG BART model as in approach 3 fails to generate better summaries evidenced by the extensive drop in accuracy for assessment and plan and exam sections."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No strong corpus support for multi-layer ensemble effectiveness in clinical summarization.
- Break condition: If the second layer model is trained specifically to handle section integration or if the sections are pre-processed to ensure alignment, the approach might improve.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Domain adaptation through fine-tuning on PubMed data does not improve accuracy because the data distribution (medical literature vs. clinical conversations) is too different.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning on PubMed data, which consists of medical literature, introduces token embeddings and summarization patterns that are not suitable for clinical conversations, resulting in lower ROUGE scores.
- Core assumption: The summarization patterns in medical literature are transferable to clinical conversation summarization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The primary reason behind these lower scores is probably that PubMed data is based on medical literature rather than medical conversations."
  - [section] "Thus we did not pursue this model further."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No corpus support for PubMed fine-tuning effectiveness in clinical conversation summarization.
- Break condition: If the fine-tuning data is more closely aligned with the target domain (e.g., clinical conversations) or if the fine-tuning process includes domain-specific adaptation techniques, the approach might improve.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: LSG BART model with local, sparse, and global attention -> Section extraction script -> Ensemble of LSG BART models (one per section) -> (Optional) Second LSG BART model for multi-layer ensemble -> ROUGE, BLEURT, and BERTScore evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess input data to extract and group chart note sections
  2. Train individual LSG BART models on each section
  3. Concatenate section-specific outputs to form the final chart note
  4. (Optional) Pass concatenated outputs through a second LSG BART model for multi-layer ensemble
  5. Evaluate the generated chart notes using ROUGE, BLEURT, and BERTScore metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single model vs. ensemble: Single model is simpler but may lack section-specific coherence; ensemble is more complex but can capture section-specific nuances.
  - Multi-layer ensemble vs. single-layer: Multi-layer may improve coherency but can introduce integration challenges; single-layer is simpler but may not fully integrate section-specific outputs.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low ROUGE scores in specific sections (e.g., Assessment & Plan) may indicate poor integration in multi-layer ensembles.
  - Inconsistent section boundaries or poor section extraction may lead to lower overall accuracy.
  - Overfitting to PubMed data may result in lower scores when fine-tuning on non-clinical literature.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a single LSG BART model on the full chart note and evaluate using ROUGE scores.
  2. Train an ensemble of LSG BART models on separate sections and concatenate outputs; compare ROUGE scores to the single model.
  3. Implement a multi-layer ensemble by passing section-specific outputs through a second LSG BART model; evaluate the impact on ROUGE scores.

## Limitations

- Small dataset size (67 training, 20 validation conversations) raises concerns about overfitting and model robustness
- Evaluation relies solely on ROUGE metrics without human evaluation of clinical accuracy or coherence
- Study only tested one approach to multi-layer ensembles, limiting conclusions about multi-layer effectiveness
- Domain mismatch between PubMed literature and clinical conversations limits generalizability of fine-tuning results

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The finding that section-wise ensemble models outperform single models on ROUGE metrics is well-supported by the experimental results. The ablation showing the multi-layer approach degrades performance is also clearly demonstrated.

**Medium Confidence:** The interpretation that domain shift between PubMed literature and clinical conversations explains the poor performance of PubMed fine-tuning is plausible but not definitively proven. Alternative explanations (e.g., data quality, fine-tuning methodology) weren't systematically explored.

**Low Confidence:** The claim that simple fine-tuning of multi-layer ensembles cannot enhance coherency is overstated. The study only tested one approach to multi-layer ensembles; more sophisticated integration methods or different architectures might yield different results.

## Next Checks

1. **Human Evaluation Study:** Conduct a blinded human evaluation where clinical experts assess the generated chart notes for accuracy, completeness, and clinical coherence, comparing the single model, section-wise ensemble, and any multi-layer variants.

2. **Cross-Institutional Validation:** Test the trained models on clinical conversation datasets from different institutions or healthcare systems to assess generalizability and identify whether the section-wise approach remains effective across different clinical contexts and documentation styles.

3. **Alternative Multi-layer Architectures:** Implement and evaluate more sophisticated multi-layer approaches, such as weighted averaging of section outputs, hierarchical attention mechanisms, or transformer-based integration layers specifically designed to reconcile section-specific outputs into coherent whole notes.
