# Identifying Adversarially Attackable and Robust Samples

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12896
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12896
- Reference count: 34
- This work introduces the concept of sample attackability and robustness in the context of adversarial attacks on deep learning models

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a deep-learning-based method to detect the most attackable and robust samples in unseen datasets for unseen target models. The approach leverages encoding representations from multiple pre-trained models to create a model-agnostic attackability detector that outperforms simple uncertainty-based measures. The detector is evaluated on Cifar10 and Cifar100 using various model architectures and attack methods, achieving F1 scores up to 0.893 for robust sample detection and 0.907 for attackable sample detection on Cifar100.

## Method Summary
The method trains a deep attackability detector using encoding representations from multiple seen models (vgg, resnext, densenet) and evaluates it on an unseen target model (wrn). The detector uses single hidden-layer fully connected networks to classify samples as attackable or robust based on their vulnerability to adversarial attacks. The approach leverages FGSM attack sizes during training and evaluates performance using PGD attacks on unseen models, with thresholds set to define attackable and robust samples.

## Key Results
- Deep attackability detector outperforms uncertainty-based methods (F1 scores up to 0.893 for robust detection vs 0.7-0.8 for uncertainty methods)
- Strong rank correlation between FGSM and PGD attack effectiveness (Spearman correlations near 1.0) indicates attackability is data-dependent rather than attack-method-specific
- The detector generalizes to unseen models and attack methods, demonstrating model-agnostic attackability detection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deep learning attackability detectors outperform uncertainty-based methods because they capture model-agnostic sample vulnerability features that are not well-represented by confidence alone.
- Mechanism: The detector learns to identify attackable/robust samples by training on encodings from multiple seen models, creating a model-agnostic representation that generalizes to unseen models.
- Core assumption: The encoding representations learned by different architectures contain transferable features that correlate with sample attackability across models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The deep attackability detector performs better than simple model uncertainty-based measures for identifying the attackable/robust samples"
  - [section] "it is useful and efficient to exploit the encoding representation of input images, hn,k, already learnt by each model"
- Break condition: If the encoding spaces of different architectures are fundamentally incompatible or if attackability is purely model-specific rather than data-dependent.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Universal attackability detection works because attackable samples show consistent behavior across different model architectures.
- Mechanism: The detector identifies samples that are attackable for multiple models by learning patterns that persist across architectures, making it effective for unseen models.
- Core assumption: Samples that are easy to attack share common characteristics that manifest similarly across different model architectures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "A sample can be defined as robust if its adversarial perturbation is larger than another set threshold" and "We can evaluate the performance of attackability detectors on the unseen target model, Ft /∈ M"
- Break condition: If different architectures have fundamentally different decision boundaries that make attackability architecture-specific rather than data-dependent.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The strong correlation between FGSM and PGD attack effectiveness indicates that attackability is a fundamental property of the data rather than specific to attack method.
- Mechanism: Since different attack methods rank samples similarly in terms of vulnerability, detectors can generalize across attack types by focusing on data properties.
- Core assumption: The fundamental vulnerability of a sample is independent of the specific attack algorithm used to measure it.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Experiments in Section 6 demonstrate that the rank correlation between different attack methods (FGSM and PGD) is extremely high"
- Break condition: If certain attack methods reveal vulnerabilities that other methods cannot detect, making attackability method-dependent.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image samples → Multiple model encoders (vgg, resnext, densenet) → Concatenated features → FCN classifier → Attackability probability
- Critical path: Sample → Multiple model encodings → Concatenated features → FCN classifier → Attackability probability
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single hidden layer vs deeper networks: Simplicity and generalization vs potential for better performance
  - Number of seen models: More models for better generalization but increased computational cost
  - Binary vs multi-class classification: Binary is simpler but loses granularity about attack severity
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on unseen models suggests encoding features aren't transferable
  - High false positives/negatives indicate threshold calibration issues
  - Degradation on matched vs unmatched settings suggests overfitting to attack method
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test detector performance on seen models (should show high accuracy) to verify training
  2. Evaluate on unseen model with same attack method as training (matched setting)
  3. Evaluate on unseen model with different attack method (unmatched setting) to test generalization

## Limitations
- The approach relies on the assumption that attackability is a data-dependent property that generalizes across models and attack methods
- The binary classification framework may miss nuanced gradations in attackability
- The choice of thresholds for attackable/robust samples could significantly impact results

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** The empirical methodology is sound, the experimental design is rigorous, and the comparison against uncertainty-based baselines is valid
- **Medium confidence:** The proposed mechanism of model-agnostic attackability detection through encoding feature aggregation is plausible but not thoroughly validated
- **Low confidence:** The generalizability of the approach to non-image domains or very different architectures (e.g., transformers) is speculative without additional experiments
