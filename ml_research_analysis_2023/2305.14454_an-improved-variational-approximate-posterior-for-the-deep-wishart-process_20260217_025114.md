# An Improved Variational Approximate Posterior for the Deep Wishart Process

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14454
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14454
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces A-generalised and AB-generalised Wishart distributions that improve DWP performance while maintaining negligible computational overhead

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel improvement to variational approximate posteriors for deep Wishart processes by introducing A-generalised (singular) Wishart and AB-generalised (singular) Wishart distributions. These new distributions extend the previous generalised Wishart framework by allowing linear combinations of rows and columns in the Bartlett decomposition, providing significantly greater flexibility. The authors demonstrate both analytically and empirically that these new distributions form proper supersets of the previous GW distribution. Through experiments on UCI datasets, DWPs using the new approximate posteriors consistently match or outperform deep Gaussian processes and previous DWP implementations across all evaluation metrics, while adding negligible computational cost. This work represents the first instance of DWPs achieving equal-or-better predictive performance than comparable DGPs on standard UCI benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The authors extend the variational approximate posterior framework for deep Wishart processes by introducing two new distributions: A-generalised (singular) Wishart and AB-generalised (singular) Wishart. These distributions build upon the previous generalised (singular) Wishart distribution by incorporating linear combinations of rows and columns in the Bartlett decomposition, which provides greater flexibility in modeling the covariance structure. The key innovation lies in allowing these linear combinations, which enables the new distributions to capture more complex dependencies while maintaining the theoretical properties required for variational inference. The authors provide analytical proofs demonstrating that these new distributions are proper supersets of the previous GW distribution, and validate their approach through extensive experiments on UCI datasets, showing consistent improvements over existing methods.

## Key Results
- DWPs using A-GW or AB-GW approximate posteriors match or outperform DGPs and previous DWPs across all metrics on UCI datasets
- The new distributions are analytically proven to be proper supersets of the previous GW distribution
- Computational overhead is described as "negligible" while providing significantly improved flexibility
- This represents the first DWP work to achieve equal-or-better predictive performance than comparable DGPs on standard UCI benchmarks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The improved performance stems from the increased flexibility provided by allowing linear combinations of rows and columns in the Bartlett decomposition. This additional flexibility enables the approximate posterior to better capture the complex covariance structures present in real-world data, particularly when dealing with non-Gaussian noise patterns or heavy-tailed distributions. By expanding the family of distributions available for variational inference, the model can better approximate the true posterior, leading to improved uncertainty quantification and predictive accuracy. The AB-GW distribution, which allows combinations in both rows and columns, provides the maximum flexibility while maintaining computational tractability through its structured parameterization.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input features -> Linear layers -> Non-linear transformations -> Wishart process layers -> A-GW/AB-GW approximate posterior -> Predictive distribution

Critical path: The inference pipeline flows from input through multiple layers of non-linear transformations, each contributing to the Wishart process structure, ultimately producing predictions through the improved approximate posterior. The key computational bottleneck lies in the matrix operations required for the Bartlett decomposition with linear combinations.

Design tradeoffs: The authors chose to add flexibility through linear combinations in the Bartlett decomposition rather than more complex parameterizations, balancing expressiveness with computational efficiency. This approach maintains the structured nature of Wishart processes while avoiding the computational explosion that might come from more general parameterizations.

Failure signatures: Potential failure modes include numerical instability when the linear combination parameters lead to near-singular matrices, or when the increased flexibility causes overfitting on smaller datasets. The model may also struggle with extremely high-dimensional data where the Wishart process assumptions become less appropriate.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out specific open questions beyond suggesting that the new distributions could be applied to other Bayesian models that use Wishart processes. The authors focus primarily on demonstrating the effectiveness of their approach within the deep Wishart process framework.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to UCI datasets without testing on larger-scale or more complex datasets that might reveal limitations
- The "negligible" computational cost claim needs more rigorous quantification across different hardware and dataset sizes
- Theoretical analysis could benefit from more extensive mathematical proofs, particularly regarding distribution properties under various parameter regimes
- No discussion of scalability to very high-dimensional data or comparison with modern deep learning approaches beyond DGPs
