# Take a Break in the Middle: Investigating Subgoals towards Hierarchical Script Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10907
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10907
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Incorporating subgoals improves hierarchical script generation quality in terms of soundness, diversity, and alignment with human intuition

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces hierarchical script generation as a new task, where given a goal, a model generates subgoals and steps organized in a two-level hierarchy. The authors contribute a new Instructables dataset and extend the existing wikiHow dataset with subgoal annotations. They propose several baseline methods and evaluation metrics, and conduct extensive experiments to investigate the effects of subgoals both qualitatively and quantitatively. The results demonstrate that hierarchical decomposition through subgoals significantly improves script generation quality compared to flat generation approaches.

## Method Summary
The method involves fine-tuning T5-base with prompt-based fine-tuning using special tokens to preserve hierarchy. The pipeline includes automatic step segmentation using one of four methods (Next Sentence Prediction, perplexity, agglomerative clustering, or topic detection), self-supervised subgoal labeling through dual learning (generating goals from steps), and hierarchical generation using either top-down or interleaving approaches. The model is trained to generate subgoals first, then steps for each subgoal, with the interleaving approach generating subgoal-step pairs sequentially rather than all subgoals first.

## Key Results
- Incorporating subgoals improves script generation quality in terms of soundness, diversity, and alignment with human intuition
- The interleaving generation approach outperforms top-down generation across most metrics
- HSG with gold segmentation and subgoals establishes an upper bound, showing significant room for improvement in automatic methods
- Performance differences between models are more pronounced on the more challenging Instructables dataset compared to wikiHow

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using subgoals as intermediate representations improves script generation quality by reducing the step generation burden and providing structured guidance
- Mechanism: The model first generates subgoals, then generates steps for each subgoal, breaking the problem into smaller, more manageable pieces
- Core assumption: Subgoals capture meaningful task decomposition that helps organize the step generation process
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "incorporating subgoals improves the quality of generated scripts in terms of soundness, diversity, and alignment with human intuition"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The interleaving generation approach produces better results than top-down because subgoals are closer to their corresponding steps during generation
- Mechanism: The model generates a subgoal, then its steps, then the next subgoal, etc., keeping relevant information temporally close
- Core assumption: Temporal proximity of related information improves generation quality
- Evidence anchors: [section] "Between the top-down and interleaving approaches, the latter achieves slightly better or tied scores among almost all metrics"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Self-supervised subgoal labeling through dual learning enables effective subgoal prediction without explicit annotations
- Mechanism: Train a model to generate the goal from steps, then use the learned representations to generate subgoals from steps
- Core assumption: The relationship between steps and their parent goal can be learned and transferred to the steps-to-subgoal relationship
- Evidence anchors: [section] "we perform the labeling in a self-supervised manner. That is to say, we regard it as a dual problem of script generation"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Segmentation module (unsupervised) → Subgoal labeling module (self-supervised) → Hierarchical generation module (T5-base fine-tuned) → Evaluation module

- Critical path:
  1. Segment steps using one of four methods (NSP, perplexity, clustering, topic detection)
  2. Generate subgoals for each segment using dual learning approach
  3. Fine-tune T5-base with hierarchical prompts
  4. Generate scripts at inference time using interleaving or top-down approach

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using T5-base instead of larger models for efficiency vs. potential quality loss
  - Self-supervised subgoal labeling vs. requiring expensive annotations
  - Interleaving vs. top-down generation approaches

- Failure signatures:
  - Repetitive subgoals indicate poor segmentation or learning issues
  - Low-quality steps suggest insufficient subgoal guidance
  - High perplexity on Instructables vs. wikiHow indicates dataset difficulty differences

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare baseline GOSC with HSG using gold segmentation and subgoals to establish upper bound
  2. Evaluate different segmentation methods on a subset of data
  3. Test interleaving vs. top-down generation approaches on the full test set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the performance of hierarchical script generation change when extending beyond two levels of hierarchy?
- Would incorporating additional modalities (e.g., images, videos) alongside text improve the quality of generated subgoals and steps?
- How would different decoding strategies affect the quality of generated hierarchical scripts, particularly in terms of avoiding repetitive subgoals and ensuring global coherence?

## Limitations
- Reliance on self-supervised subgoal labeling may introduce noise and inaccuracies in the hierarchical structure
- Segmentation methods, while showing reasonable performance, are not perfect and can lead to suboptimal subgoal generation
- Performance gap between models and human-level generation remains significant, particularly for complex tasks

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The effectiveness of hierarchical decomposition in improving script generation quality
- **Medium Confidence**: The self-supervised subgoal labeling mechanism shows promise but requires more rigorous validation
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the approach to domains beyond how-to instructions remains unclear
