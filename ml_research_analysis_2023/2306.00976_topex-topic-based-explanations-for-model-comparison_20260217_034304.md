# TopEx: Topic-based Explanations for Model Comparison

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00976
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00976
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: Topic-based explanations that compress feature attributions into model-independent, human-interpretable topics for comparing language models

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of comparing language model behavior through explanations by proposing TopEx, a method that transforms feature attributions into topic-based explanations using topic modeling. Traditional explanation methods are either overwhelming due to large vocabularies or incomparable across models. TopEx condenses these explanations into a smaller set of model-independent topics, making model comparisons more interpretable and actionable. The method is demonstrated on DistilRoBERTa and GPT-2 fine-tuned on Yelp Reviews and GoEmotions datasets, revealing meaningful differences in how the models process text for sentiment and emotion classification.

## Method Summary
TopEx works by first obtaining feature attributions (importance scores) for each token in a dataset using standard attribution methods like integrated gradients. These token-level attributions are then aggregated by topic using a pre-defined or learned topic model (such as LDA or LIWC). Each topic's importance score is computed as a weighted sum of token attributions, where weights are determined by the topic distribution of each token. The final output is a set of topic importance scores for each model, enabling direct comparison of model behavior at the topic level rather than the token level. This approach reduces dimensionality and aligns explanations across models by mapping them to shared topics.

## Key Results
- TopEx successfully identifies differences in model behavior: DistilRoBERTa focuses more on dining descriptions for 5-star reviews, while GPT-2 looks more at negativity
- Topic-based explanations are more interpretable and actionable than raw feature attributions for model comparison
- The method works across different architectures (DistilRoBERTa and GPT-2) and tasks (sentiment and emotion classification)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TopEx leverages the intuition that language models use different feature representations, making direct comparison of token-level attributions difficult. By mapping token attributions to a shared topic space, the method creates a common vocabulary for comparing model behavior. Topic modeling acts as a dimensionality reduction step that preserves the most salient information for comparison while filtering noise. The weighting scheme accounts for token importance across the dataset, ensuring that the resulting topic scores reflect genuine model behavior rather than artifacts.

## Foundational Learning
- **Feature attribution methods** (e.g., integrated gradients, LIME): Used to quantify the importance of each input token for a model's prediction
  - Why needed: Provide the raw importance scores that TopEx transforms into topics
  - Quick check: Verify attributions are stable across similar inputs and capture known model behaviors
- **Topic modeling (LDA, LIWC)**: Groups words into semantically coherent topics and provides topic distributions for each word
  - Why needed: Enables compression of high-dimensional token attributions into interpretable, comparable topics
  - Quick check: Ensure topics are coherent and distinct; validate with topic coherence metrics
- **Model comparison via explanations**: The practice of comparing models based on what they attend to or consider important
  - Why needed: TopEx is specifically designed for this purpose; understanding its goals clarifies the design
  - Quick check: Confirm explanations are consistent with known differences in model architectures or training

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input text → Feature attribution method (e.g., integrated gradients) → Token importance scores → Topic model (LDA/LIWC) → Topic importance scores → Model comparison
- **Critical path**: The most important flow is from token attributions through topic aggregation to final comparison scores; each step must preserve interpretability and comparability
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using topic modeling simplifies comparison but may lose fine-grained differences; the choice of topic model affects granularity and interpretability
- **Failure signatures**: Poor topic coherence leads to meaningless comparisons; unstable attributions cause noisy topic scores; mismatched vocabularies across models may hide differences
- **First experiments**:
  1. Compare two fine-tuned versions of the same model to establish baseline variation
  2. Apply TopEx to a dataset with known topic structure to verify correct topic recovery
  3. Test sensitivity to the number of topics used in LDA

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness on more diverse or general-purpose corpora is unclear; topic quality may degrade with domain diversity
- No analysis of topic stability across fine-tuned checkpoints, which could conflate architectural differences with training artifacts
- The choice of 10 topics per model is arbitrary and lacks sensitivity analysis

## Confidence
High confidence: The core idea that topic modeling can compress feature attributions is sound and the method works as described on the tested datasets.
Medium confidence: The claim that TopEx reveals "fundamental differences" in model behavior is supported but relies heavily on qualitative inspection rather than quantitative validation.
Low confidence: The generalizability of TopEx to other model types, tasks, or larger vocabularies is not established.

## Next Checks
1. Test topic stability by comparing explanations from multiple checkpoints of the same fine-tuned model to establish baseline variation.
2. Evaluate TopEx on a more diverse corpus (e.g., multi-domain or long-form text) to assess robustness when topics are less clean.
3. Conduct a user study comparing human ability to distinguish model behavior using TopEx versus raw feature attributions to quantify the claimed accessibility advantage.
