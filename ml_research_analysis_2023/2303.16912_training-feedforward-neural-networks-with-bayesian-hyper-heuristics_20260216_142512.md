# Training Feedforward Neural Networks with Bayesian Hyper-Heuristics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16912
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16912
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: The Bayesian hyper-heuristic (BHH) achieved competitive performance and low variance across fourteen datasets, ranking fourth among thirteen methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Bayesian hyper-heuristic (BHH) that dynamically selects from a pool of low-level heuristics—including gradient-based and meta-heuristic methods—to train feedforward neural networks. The BHH uses Bayesian statistical concepts to learn the probability that each heuristic will perform well at each stage of training, based on past performance tracked in a performance log. The method was evaluated across fourteen classification and regression datasets using shallow neural networks, comparing the BHH against ten individual heuristics. The BHH configuration with only gradient-based heuristics (bhh gd) achieved an overall average rank of fourth among thirteen methods, showing competitive performance and low variance across datasets, indicating good generalization. The results demonstrate that the BHH can effectively automate heuristic selection in neural network training, though it did not outperform the best individual heuristic overall.

## Method Summary
The Bayesian hyper-heuristic (BHH) automates the selection of training heuristics for feedforward neural networks by maintaining a population of candidate solutions and dynamically choosing the most appropriate heuristic at each training step. The BHH implements a probabilistic predictive model based on Naïve Bayes to compute the probability of each heuristic performing well given the current entity and performance credit. A performance log stores historical heuristic-entity-loss tuples in a sliding window, while credit assignment strategies assign discrete credits to top-performing combinations. The BHH updates concentration parameters using MAP estimation and maintains state continuity across heuristic switches through proxy state update operations. The method was implemented using TensorFlow 2.7 and TensorFlow Probability 0.15.0, evaluating fourteen UCI datasets with shallow FFNNs containing one hidden layer.

## Key Results
- The BHH configuration with only gradient-based heuristics (bhh gd) achieved an overall average rank of fourth among thirteen methods
- The BHH demonstrated low variance across datasets, indicating good generalization
- The method showed competitive performance compared to ten individual heuristics across fourteen classification and regression datasets
- The BHH effectively automates heuristic selection in neural network training without outperforming the best individual heuristic overall

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Bayesian hyper-heuristic (BHH) dynamically selects the most effective training heuristic for each entity in the population by maintaining and updating probability distributions over heuristic performance.
- Mechanism: At each training step, the BHH uses a Naïve Bayes classifier to compute P(H|E,C), where H is the heuristic, E is the entity, and C is the performance credit. This probability guides heuristic selection, with higher probabilities assigned to heuristics that historically perform well on similar entities.
- Core assumption: Heuristic performance is conditionally independent given the entity and credit assignment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The BHH uses Bayesian statistical concepts to learn the probability that each heuristic will perform well at each stage of training"
  - [section 6.6] "The BHH implements a probabilistic predictive model based on the fundamentals of the Naïve Bayes algorithm"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors focus on hyper-heuristics and Bayesian methods but not this specific Bayesian heuristic selection mechanism
- Break condition: If the independence assumption is violated (e.g., heuristics have correlated performance patterns), the Naïve Bayes approximation degrades and selection accuracy drops.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The BHH generalizes well across diverse datasets by learning entity-specific heuristic performance patterns through a performance log with sliding window.
- Mechanism: The performance log stores heuristic-entity-loss tuples over a replay window. Credit assignment strategies (ibest, pbest, gbest, rbest) assign discrete credits to top-performing combinations, which update concentration parameters α, β, γ1, γ2 via MAP estimation. This updates prior beliefs about which heuristics work best for which entities.
- Core assumption: Performance patterns observed in the replay window are representative of current training dynamics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method was evaluated across fourteen classification and regression datasets... showing competitive performance and low variance across datasets"
  - [section 6.4] "Historical heuristic-entity performance outcomes are stored in a performance log... only a sliding window of the performance history is maintained"
  - [section 6.5] "Credit assignment strategy... assigns a discrete credit indicator to heuristics that perform well, based on their performance metrics"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't provide evidence about sliding window generalization mechanisms
- Break condition: If training dynamics change rapidly, the fixed-size replay window may contain outdated information, leading to poor heuristic selection.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The BHH maintains state continuity across heuristic switches through proxy state update operations.
- Mechanism: Different heuristics maintain different state parameters (position, velocity, gradients, etc.). The BHH maps these through proxy operations so that when switching heuristics, required state parameters are computed by outsourcing to another heuristic that maintains them, ensuring all heuristics have consistent state.
- Core assumption: State parameters can be computed or approximated by other heuristics without loss of essential information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.2] "State is then maintained in two parts: primary and proxied state... A solution to state indifference is to proxy heuristic state update operations"
  - [section 7.3] "The mapping of proxied heuristic state update operations implemented by the BHH in the empirical process is given in Figure 2"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors don't discuss state management in hyper-heuristics
- Break condition: If proxy operations introduce significant approximation error or if heuristics require fundamentally incompatible state representations, performance degrades.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Entity Pool -> Apply Heuristic -> Compute Loss -> Performance Log -> Credit Assignment -> Bayesian Updater -> Selection Mechanism -> Proxy State Manager -> Next Step
- Critical path: Entity → Apply Heuristic → Compute Loss → Log Performance → Credit Assignment → Bayesian Update → Heuristic Selection → Next Step
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Replay window size vs. memory usage vs. learning quality (larger windows provide more context but increase memory and computation)
  - Credit assignment strategy choice (ibest focuses on iteration best, pbest on personal best, etc.) affects selection pressure
  - Burn-in period length vs. exploration vs. exploitation balance
  - Normalizing vs. non-normalizing credit values affects credit assignment scaling
- Failure signatures:
  - If the BHH selects heuristics randomly without clear pattern, the performance log may be too small or the Bayesian update interval too infrequent
  - If the BHH gets stuck using only one heuristic, the credit assignment may be too aggressive or the diversity in the heuristic pool too low
  - If training performance degrades after heuristic switches, proxy state operations may be introducing errors
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a simplified BHH with only two heuristics (SGD and Adam) on a small dataset to verify the basic selection mechanism works
  2. Add proxy state mapping for position and gradient parameters, test heuristic switching with state continuity
  3. Expand to the full heuristic pool and test on a classification dataset, verify performance log and credit assignment update concentration parameters correctly

## Limitations

- The performance of the BHH is evaluated against individual heuristics but not against ensemble methods or adaptive learning rate schedulers, leaving open questions about relative effectiveness
- The Naïve Bayes assumption of conditional independence may not hold in practice, potentially degrading selection accuracy
- The paper does not report computational overhead costs of the BHH compared to individual heuristics, making cost-effectiveness unclear
- State proxy operations may introduce approximation errors that aren't quantified

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The BHH achieves competitive average rankings and demonstrates low variance across datasets (claims supported by experimental results across 14 datasets and 30 runs)

- **Medium confidence**: The claim of good generalization across diverse datasets (supported by results but could be strengthened with additional datasets or analysis of failure cases)
