# Improving Hyperparameter Learning under Approximate Inference in Gaussian Process Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04201
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04201
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Hybrid VI-EP training achieves more reliable hyperparameter learning and better generalization for non-conjugate GP models

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in Gaussian process models with non-conjugate likelihoods: the entanglement between approximate inference and hyperparameter optimization. The authors propose a hybrid training procedure that uses variational inference (VI) for efficient posterior inference and an Expectation Propagation (EP)-like marginal likelihood approximation for hyperparameter learning. By leveraging the dual parameterization framework of Khan & Lin (2017), they can extract EP-style site parameters directly from the VI posterior without added computational cost. The method is evaluated on binary classification and robust regression tasks, showing superior performance in terms of log predictive density compared to standard VI, Laplace approximation, and EP.

## Method Summary
The hybrid training procedure separates the E-step (inference) from the M-step (learning) using different objectives. During the E-step, natural gradient descent optimizes the VI ELBO to find a good posterior representation. The M-step then uses gradient descent on an EP-like marginal likelihood approximation extracted from the VI posterior through dual parameterization. This approach avoids the bias of optimizing hyperparameters directly with the VI ELBO while maintaining computational efficiency by reusing the VI computations. For large datasets, the method extends to sparse GP models using inducing points with tied dual parameterization to avoid storing all site parameters.

## Key Results
- The hybrid approach achieves more reliable and stable hyperparameter learning compared to standard VI, LA, and EP methods
- Experiments show superior log predictive density on test sets across binary classification and robust regression tasks
- The method demonstrates good generalization ability on both full and sparse GP models without added computational cost

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The EP-like marginal likelihood approximation from the VI posterior improves hyperparameter learning quality
- Mechanism: By leveraging the dual parameterization, the VI posterior structure exactly matches the EP posterior structure, allowing direct extraction of EP-style site parameters from VI inference. These site parameters then enable accurate marginal likelihood approximation without additional computation.
- Core assumption: The approximate posterior obtained through VI has the same structure as the EP approximate posterior when using the dual parameterization (Khan & Lin, 2017)
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that a direct approximation of the marginal likelihood as in Expectation Propagation (EP) is a better learning objective for hyperparameter optimization"
  - [section] "Crucially, the approximate posterior (10) has the same form as its EP counterpart (6)"
  - [corpus] "Likelihood approximations via Gaussian approximate inference" - suggests EP-style approximations are effective for non-Gaussian likelihoods
- Break condition: If the dual parameterization does not accurately capture the true posterior structure, or if the VI posterior variance is severely underestimated, the extracted EP parameters may lead to poor hyperparameter estimates

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Separating E-step (inference) and M-step (learning) with different objectives improves optimization stability and performance
- Mechanism: The E-step uses VI's ELBO to find a good posterior representation via natural gradient descent, while the M-step uses the EP-like marginal likelihood approximation for hyperparameter optimization. This separation prevents the ELBO bias from contaminating hyperparameter learning.
- Core assumption: The ELBO is a suitable objective for inference but not for hyperparameter learning, while the EP marginal likelihood is better for hyperparameter optimization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that a direct approximation of the marginal likelihood as in Expectation Propagation (EP) is a better learning objective for hyperparameter optimization"
  - [section] "We strongly advocate against the common practice of jointly optimizing variational and hyperparameters using the ELBO, as the training target is only representative for the variational parameters"
  - [corpus] "Towards Improved Variational Inference for Deep Bayesian Models" - suggests VI objectives may not be optimal for all purposes
- Break condition: If the E-step and M-step objectives become too conflicting (as shown in Fig. 5), the optimization may become unstable and require early stopping

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The hybrid training procedure achieves better generalization without added computational cost
- Mechanism: By using the same VI posterior for both objectives, the method maintains computational efficiency while improving the quality of hyperparameter learning. The EP-like marginal likelihood estimate from the VI posterior provides a better proxy for test performance.
- Core assumption: The EP-like marginal likelihood estimate correlates better with test performance than the VI ELBO
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our hybrid approach also demonstrates good generalization ability on both full and sparse GP models"
  - [section] "To investigate whether the improved marginal likelihood estimation also leads to better generalization, we select the optimal hyperparameter location across the grid values and compare the log predictive density on the test set"
  - [corpus] "Enhancing Gaussian Process Surrogates for Optimization and Posterior Approximation via Random Exploration" - suggests exploration can improve surrogate quality
- Break condition: If the correlation between the EP-like marginal likelihood and test performance breaks down (e.g., with extreme sparsification), the method loses its advantage

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Conjugate-computation variational inference and dual parameterization
  - Why needed here: The method relies on Khan & Lin (2017)'s framework that links VI and EP through dual parameterization, enabling the EP-like marginal likelihood extraction from VI
  - Quick check question: How does the dual parameterization connect the VI and EP posterior structures?

- Concept: Natural gradient descent for variational parameter optimization
  - Why needed here: The E-step uses natural gradient descent in the dual parameterization space, which is computationally efficient and appropriate for exponential family distributions
  - Quick check question: Why is natural gradient descent preferred over standard gradient descent for optimizing the VI parameters?

- Concept: Expectation Propagation and its marginal likelihood approximation
  - Why needed here: The M-step objective is based on EP's marginal likelihood approximation, so understanding EP's mechanics is crucial for implementing the hybrid training procedure
  - Quick check question: How does EP approximate the marginal likelihood differently from VI's ELBO approach?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - E-step: VI inference with natural gradient descent in dual parameterization space
  - M-step: Hyperparameter optimization using EP-like marginal likelihood approximation
  - Dual parameterization layer: Converts between standard and natural parameter spaces
  - Sparse approximation module: Extends the approach to large datasets with inducing points
  - Convergence monitoring: Detects when EP-like objective starts decreasing

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize variational parameters λ(0) and hyperparameters θ(0)
  2. E-step: Optimize λ using natural gradient descent on LVI
  3. M-step: Optimize θ using gradient descent on LEP
  4. Check convergence criteria and repeat until termination

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Separation of inference and learning objectives vs. computational simplicity of joint optimization
  - Accuracy of EP-like approximation vs. simplicity of VI ELBO
  - Memory usage for storing dual parameters vs. computational efficiency

- Failure signatures:
  - LEP starts decreasing during M-step iterations (indicates overshooting)
  - Poor correlation between EP-like marginal likelihood and test performance
  - Instability in natural gradient descent for E-step

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the basic hybrid training procedure on a small binary classification dataset (e.g., IONOSPHERE) and verify it converges
  2. Compare the EP-like marginal likelihood surface with MCMC baseline on a grid of hyperparameters
  3. Test the sparse extension on a larger dataset with inducing points and verify computational efficiency gains

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed hybrid training procedure scale to very large datasets beyond the sparse GP experiments presented?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that sparse approximation is used to handle large datasets due to O(n³) computational complexity, but the sparse experiments only cover datasets up to 19,000 points.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore performance on truly massive datasets (millions of points) where even sparse methods may become computationally prohibitive.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on benchmark datasets with millions of points, or theoretical analysis of computational complexity scaling, would clarify the practical limits of the approach.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is the proposed method to the initialization of hyperparameters and variational parameters?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that EP can sometimes overshoot the optimum and become unstable, requiring careful stopping criteria, but does not systematically explore initialization sensitivity.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a single initialization strategy (unit lengthscale and magnitude) across all experiments without exploring how different initializations affect convergence and final performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying initialization strategies, or theoretical analysis of the optimization landscape, would reveal the robustness of the method to initialization choices.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the proposed hybrid training procedure be extended to other types of likelihoods beyond Bernoulli and Student-t?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates the method on binary classification with Bernoulli likelihood and robust regression with Student-t likelihood, but does not explore other likelihood types.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on specific likelihood types without exploring the generality of the approach to other common likelihoods like Poisson, Gamma, or skewed distributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the method to different likelihood types, or theoretical analysis of the requirements for the method to work, would establish its broader applicability.

## Limitations

- The method's effectiveness depends critically on the assumption that the dual parameterization accurately captures the relationship between VI and EP posteriors, which may break down with highly non-Gaussian likelihoods
- The hybrid training procedure requires careful monitoring of the EP-like objective during training, as the conflicting nature of the two optimization targets can lead to overshooting and instability
- Performance may degrade with extreme sparsification when too few inducing points are used, leading to biased marginal likelihood estimates

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The hybrid training procedure's computational efficiency claim, as it leverages existing VI computations without added cost
- **Medium confidence**: The superiority of EP-like marginal likelihood over VI ELBO for hyperparameter learning, supported by experimental results but dependent on problem structure
- **Medium confidence**: The general applicability to both full and sparse GP models, though performance may degrade with extreme sparsification
