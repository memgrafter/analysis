# Transcendental Idealism of Planner: Evaluating Perception from Planning Perspective for Autonomous Driving

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.07276
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07276
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces a principled framework for evaluating perception errors' impact on autonomous vehicle planning through geometric interpretation in Hilbert space.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the "Transcendental Idealism of Planner" (TIP) framework for evaluating perception errors in autonomous driving systems. TIP approaches this problem by formulating planning as expected utility maximization and interpreting it geometrically in a Hilbert space. The framework decomposes perception errors into planning-critical and planning-invariant components, providing a universal metric that quantifies the consequence of perception errors on the planning process. Unlike conventional metrics that focus on final behavior, TIP evaluates errors based on their impact on the planner's decision-making process.

## Method Summary
TIP leverages geometric interpretation of planning as utility maximization in Hilbert space, where perception errors can be decomposed into orthogonal components affecting planning decisions versus those that don't. The framework formulates perception evaluation as computing the projection of error vectors onto the planning-critical subspace, effectively measuring how perception errors propagate through the planning pipeline. This approach provides a principled way to identify which perception errors truly matter for safe and effective autonomous driving.

## Key Results
- TIP provides a universal metric for quantifying perception error impact on planning decisions
- Outperforms conventional metrics by identifying critical perception errors missed by behavior-focused approaches
- Validated effectiveness on both synthetic and real-world autonomous driving datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
TIP works by recognizing that not all perception errors equally impact planning decisions. Through Hilbert space formulation, it geometrically separates perception errors into those that influence planning (critical) and those that don't (invariant). This decomposition allows for more nuanced evaluation of perception system performance, focusing on errors that actually matter for safe driving decisions rather than just observable behaviors.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Perception System → Hilbert Space Projection → Planning-Critical Error Measurement → TIP Score
- **Critical Path**: Raw perception data → Error vector computation → Hilbert space projection → Critical component extraction → Impact quantification
- **Design Tradeoffs**: TIP prioritizes theoretical rigor and planning-focused evaluation over computational efficiency, potentially increasing computational overhead but providing more meaningful error analysis
- **Failure Signatures**: TIP may miss errors that don't directly affect planning decisions but could have indirect consequences, such as edge cases in rare scenarios

## Limitations
- Geometric interpretation may not fully capture real-world planning complexity
- Linearity assumption in planning might not hold in highly dynamic environments
- Utility function formulation may not account for all relevant factors in complex driving scenarios
