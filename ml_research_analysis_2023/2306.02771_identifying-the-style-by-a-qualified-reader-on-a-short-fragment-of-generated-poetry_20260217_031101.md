# Identifying the style by a qualified reader on a short fragment of generated poetry

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02771
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02771
- Reference count: 6
- Key outcome: LSTM models reproduce poetic style with ≥0.7 macro-average accuracy; expert evaluators outperform naive readers

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether LSTM models can effectively reproduce the style of famous Russian poets in generated poetry. Three character-based LSTM models were trained on corpora of texts by Nekrasov, Mandelshtam, and early Pasternak. Generated 4-line samples were shown to 94 literature students, who identified the poet's style from a closed list of 4 candidates. Each model achieved at least 0.7 macro-average accuracy in style reproduction. Accuracy increased significantly (to 0.87 for Nekrasov) when respondents could quote the poet by heart, indicating that deep familiarity with an author's work improves style recognition. The experiment confirms that LSTM models effectively reproduce poetic style even with limited training data, and that expert evaluators are better suited than naive readers for assessing style in poetry generation tasks.

## Method Summary
The study trained three character-based LSTM models on texts by Nikolay Nekrasov (1.2M characters), Osip Mandelshtam (265K characters), and early Boris Pasternak (316K characters). Each model generated 4-line poetry samples, which were presented to 94 literature students. Assessors identified the poet's style from a closed list of 4 candidate poets and reported whether they could quote the poet by heart. Macro-average accuracy was used as the primary metric to evaluate style reproduction, with results compared between respondents who could quote the poet and those who could not.

## Key Results
- Each LSTM model achieved at least 0.7 macro-average accuracy in reproducing poetic style
- Nekrasov model reached 0.87 macro-average accuracy when respondents could quote by heart
- Expert evaluators demonstrated significantly better style recognition than would be expected from naive readers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LSTM models can reproduce poetic style effectively with limited training data.
- Mechanism: Character-based LSTM learns n-gram and distributional patterns that encode authorial style markers, enabling generation that preserves those markers.
- Core assumption: Style is encoded in character-level distributions and can be captured without semantic understanding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Each model showed at least 0.7 macro-average accuracy. The experiment showed that it is better to involve a professional rather than a naive reader in the evaluation of style in the tasks of poetry generation, while lstm models are good at reproducing the style of Russian poets even on a limited training corpus."
  - [section 3.1] "I've trained three character-based LSTM neural network models... All three models were trained on the corpus of texts by famous Russian-speaking poets; Nikolay Nekrasov (1,200,000 characters... Osip Mandelshtam (265,000 characters... early works of Boris Pasternak (316,000 characters...)"
  - [corpus] Weak: No explicit analysis of what character patterns correspond to style; assumed from "good at reproducing" claim.
- Break condition: If style depends critically on meaning or larger syntactic structures beyond character n-grams, LSTM reproduction fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Expert familiarity with a poet's work strongly improves style recognition accuracy.
- Mechanism: Deep memorization of an author's complete corpus creates mental templates that experts use to detect stylistic deviations or matches in short fragments.
- Core assumption: Style recognition is template-matching rather than probabilistic inference from fragments alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It has appeared that accuracy of definition of style increases if the assessor can quote the poet by heart."
  - [section 4] "People who can quote the poet's lines by heart correctly, define Nekrasov's style in 80.6% of cases. That gives us 0.87 macro-average accuracy."
  - [corpus] Weak: No psychological or cognitive modeling of template-matching; only empirical correlation.
- Break condition: If short fragments are too ambiguous even for experts, or if style cues are not represented in memorized material.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Closed-list multiple-choice format with contextual distractors effectively tests style reproduction without revealing the target author.
- Mechanism: By forcing choice among plausible contemporaries, the test isolates stylistic recognition from recognition of famous lines or semantic content.
- Core assumption: Expert judgment is based on style, not on content familiarity or guessing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2] "it is necessary for the evaluator not to know what style the machine is trying to reproduce... The compromise will be a closed list of names, one of which belongs to the very poet, on whose texts the model was trained."
  - [section 3.2-3.4] "4 possible candidates for authorship... Nekrasov and Vyazemsky are 19th century poets... The choice between these two options should be hard."
  - [corpus] Weak: No validation that distractors are truly plausible; relies on author's literary judgment.
- Break condition: If distractor authors are too dissimilar, or if experts can guess without reading the fragment.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Macro-average accuracy in multi-class classification
  - Why needed here: Standard accuracy misleads when classes are imbalanced; macro-average gives equal weight to each class's performance.
  - Quick check question: If class A has 90% correct and class B has 10% correct, what is macro-average accuracy vs. overall accuracy?

- Concept: Character-level language modeling
  - Why needed here: LSTM is trained at character level, so understanding what character distributions encode is key to interpreting style reproduction.
  - Quick check question: Why might a character-level model produce non-existent words like "novodorodnyj" in the Pasternak sample?

- Concept: Expertise calibration in subjective evaluation tasks
  - Why needed here: The study hinges on whether experts outperform novices; knowing how to measure and control for expertise is critical.
  - Quick check question: How does asking "can you quote by heart" function as a proxy for expertise in this experimental design?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data ingestion → character tokenization → LSTM sequence modeling → text generation → human evaluation pipeline
  - Human evaluation includes closed-list questionnaire + expertise self-assessment

- Critical path:
  1. Train LSTM on full poet corpus (characters)
  2. Generate 4-line samples per model
  3. Present to experts with 4-option authorship list
  4. Record responses + familiarity rating
  5. Compute macro-average accuracy per model
  6. Segment by "can quote by heart" vs. "cannot" for deeper insight

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Character-level vs. word-level: More data needed but captures subtler stylistic features
  - Short fragments vs. longer passages: Faster evaluation but risk of insufficient stylistic signal
  - Closed list vs. open response: Controls for guessing but limits generalizability

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy near chance level → model fails to capture style
  - High variance across experts → expertise measurement unreliable
  - Systematic bias toward one distractor → distractors not plausible

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Retrain same LSTM architecture on shuffled character order to confirm style capture is not random
  2. Test word-level LSTM on same corpora to compare character vs. word effectiveness
  3. Run same evaluation with naive readers to quantify expertise effect size

## Limitations
- Character-level modeling captures style patterns but doesn't identify specific stylistic features
- Expertise measure (quoting by heart) may not fully capture literary expertise
- Closed-list format limits generalizability of results
- Study focuses on Russian poetry, limiting cross-linguistic applicability

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: LSTM models achieve at least 0.7 macro-average accuracy in style reproduction; expert familiarity improves recognition accuracy significantly
- **Medium confidence**: Character-level LSTM is better suited than word-level for capturing subtle stylistic features in poetry; closed-list format effectively isolates stylistic recognition
- **Low confidence**: Specific character patterns that encode style are identified; distractor poets are equally plausible to all respondents

## Next Checks
1. Analyze generated samples to identify specific character n-gram patterns that correlate with authorial style recognition
2. Validate the plausibility of distractor poets by having independent experts rank similarity to target authors
3. Compare character-level and word-level LSTM performance on the same corpora to quantify the advantage of character modeling for style reproduction
