# Teacher-Student Knowledge Distillation for Radar Perception on Embedded Accelerators

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07586
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07586
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: 100x speedup on embedded hardware with 0.91 R1 recall and 0.99 specificity for stationary object detection

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a teacher-student knowledge distillation approach for low-level radar perception tasks, specifically stationary object detection using range-azimuth spectrum data. The teacher model combines traditional radar signal processing with deep learning, while the student model is a lightweight CNN optimized for embedded hardware accelerators. The approach achieves a 100x speedup compared to the teacher while maintaining high detection accuracy (R1=0.91, P1=0.93, specificity=0.99). The student model also extends detection range by an average of 47 meters and demonstrates constant runtime independent of vehicle speed.

## Method Summary
The method employs knowledge distillation to transfer detection capabilities from a hybrid teacher model (combining traditional radar signal processing with deep learning) to a lightweight student CNN. The student learns directly from low-level radar spectrum data to predict detection outputs, bypassing computationally expensive intermediate signal processing stages. Training uses a weighted mean squared error loss to handle class imbalance, and CoordConv layers enable learning geometric lane-width functions. The student is specifically designed for efficient deployment on MMA-accelerated embedded hardware.

## Key Results
- Student achieves 100x speedup compared to teacher model on embedded hardware
- Detection accuracy: R1 recall=0.91, P1 precision=0.93, specificity=0.99
- Extends maximum detection range by 47 meters on average
- Runtime remains constant regardless of host vehicle speed

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The hybrid teacher model's non-trainable blocks perform computationally intensive traditional radar signal processing tasks that cannot leverage hardware acceleration
- Mechanism: Traditional signal processing steps like interpolation, convolution-based filtering, and feature extraction are inherently sequential and branch-heavy, making them incompatible with matrix-multiplication-focused accelerators (MMAs)
- Core assumption: MMA cores are optimized for dense linear algebra operations and cannot efficiently execute conditional logic, variable-length loops, or irregular memory access patterns common in classical DSP
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Unfortunately, these signal processing algorithms are often poorly suited to run on embedded hardware accelerators"
  - [section] "The non-trainable algorithms cannot benefit from the MMA core on an embedded devices, designed to run fast matrix multiplication calculations"
- Break condition: If MMA cores evolve to efficiently handle irregular control flow or if DSP kernels are rewritten in matrix form, this advantage disappears

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge distillation transfers the teacher's detection capability to a lightweight student that can fully exploit MMA acceleration
- Mechanism: The student learns a mapping from low-level radar spectra directly to detection outputs via convolutional layers, bypassing expensive intermediate signal processing stages. CoordConv layers allow learning geometric lane-width functions without explicit computation
- Core assumption: The student can approximate the composite function of the teacher's trainable and non-trainable components using only end-to-end learning from labeled data
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "knowledge distillation to transfer detection capabilities from the teacher to the student model, enabling faster inference on resource-constrained devices"
  - [section] "CoordConv also allows the student to better model the apparent difference in signal strength between near and far objects"
- Break condition: If the teacher's detection function is too complex to be approximated by the student's capacity, distillation accuracy degrades

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Selective training on teacher-positive samples allows the student to extend detection range without penalizing false positives
- Mechanism: By training only on samples where the teacher confidently detects debris, the student learns to extrapolate beyond the teacher's maximum range without being penalized for novel detections in evaluation
- Core assumption: The student's convolutional features learned from high-confidence teacher examples generalize to unseen ranges and conditions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The student can extend the maximum distance at which debris can be detected... By selective training, the student can take what it learns from the teacher's higher-speed examples and apply it to the low-speed samples"
- Break condition: If the training set lacks sufficient diversity in speed and range conditions, the student fails to generalize

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Low-level radar spectrum representation (range-azimuth maps)
  - Why needed here: The student and teacher both operate directly on raw range-azimuth maps rather than processed point clouds, requiring understanding of radar data geometry and resolution
  - Quick check question: What do the dimensions (464x256) of the range-azimuth map represent in terms of radar resolution and field of view?

- Concept: Knowledge distillation training objective (weighted MSE)
  - Why needed here: The imbalanced dataset (few positive detections) requires weighted loss to prevent the student from ignoring rare debris detections
  - Quick check question: How does the weighting factor wi in the WMSE formula change the loss contribution from positive vs. negative samples?

- Concept: CoordConv for geometric learning
  - Why needed here: Standard convolutions are translationally invariant, but detecting in-lane debris requires learning the ego-lane geometry as a function of range
  - Quick check question: Why does appending range and azimuth coordinate channels to the input allow the student to learn the lane-width function?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Raw radar data -> Range-azimuth map generation -> Teacher model inference -> Student training -> Embedded deployment

- Critical path:
  1. Raw radar data acquisition and range-azimuth map generation
  2. Teacher model inference for labeling (offline)
  3. Student model training with weighted MSE loss
  4. Student model deployment on MMA-enabled embedded hardware

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. speed: Student achieves 100x speedup at slight recall degradation (0.91 R1 vs. teacher's implicit performance)
  - Range extension vs. precision: Selective training allows range extension but requires modified P-score evaluation
  - Memory vs. receptive field: 3x1 final conv balances range-direction context with embedded memory constraints

- Failure signatures:
  - Student underfits: High WMSE loss, poor R1 recall (<0.8)
  - Student overfits: Perfect training R1 but poor validation R1
  - Hardware mismatch: Student runs slower than expected on target MMA due to layer configurations incompatible with MMA tiling

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: Train student without CoordConv layers; measure degradation in lane-geometry learning and overall recall
  2. Ablation: Train student with uniform MSE loss (no weighting); observe impact on debris detection in imbalanced data
  3. Range test: Evaluate student on teacher-negative samples to quantify range extension capability and false positive rate

## Limitations
- The computational advantage of MMA cores over traditional DSP is asserted but not empirically validated on representative hardware
- The selective training methodology for range extension lacks ablation studies to quantify its specific contribution
- Cross-dataset generalization of the distilled student model is not evaluated

## Confidence
- **High**: Performance metrics (100x speedup, 0.91 R1 recall, 0.99 specificity) are directly measurable and well-documented
- **Medium**: The teacher-student architecture design and CoordConv implementation are clearly described and justified
- **Low**: The comparative advantage of MMA hardware over traditional DSP and the generalization of selective training across datasets are asserted without direct evidence

## Next Checks
1. **Hardware validation**: Implement and benchmark both teacher and student models on the target embedded MMA accelerator to verify the 100x speedup claim and identify any bottlenecks
2. **Ablation study**: Compare student performance with and without selective training to isolate its contribution to range extension
3. **Cross-dataset testing**: Evaluate the distilled student model on an independent radar dataset to assess generalization beyond the training conditions
