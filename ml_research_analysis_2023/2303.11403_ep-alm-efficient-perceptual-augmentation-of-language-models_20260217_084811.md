# eP-ALM: Efficient Perceptual Augmentation of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11403
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11403
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: eP-ALM achieves state-of-the-art performance on VQA and captioning tasks while freezing >99% of parameters and training only a linear projection layer plus soft prompts

## Executive Summary
Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities at scale, and vision transformers are following similar trends. This paper challenges the need for large-scale multimodal pretraining by proposing a parameter-efficient approach to augment language models with perceptual capabilities. The eP-ALM method achieves this by freezing pretrained unimodal models and training only a linear projection layer and soft prompts, significantly outperforming baselines on VQA and captioning tasks across image, video, and audio modalities.

## Method Summary
eP-ALM adapts pretrained unimodal models for multimodal tasks by extracting [CLS] tokens from several layers of a frozen perceptual encoder (ViT, TimeSformer, or AST), linearly projecting them to the language model's embedding space, and injecting them at corresponding layers of a frozen language model (OPT). The approach trains only the linear projection layers and optionally a soft prompt (10 learnable tokens), achieving parameter efficiency while maintaining strong performance. The model is trained using cross-entropy loss with AdamW optimizer (lr=1e-5, warmup to 2e-5, then decay to 1e-6), batch size 64, for 8 epochs on standard benchmarks including VQA v2, GQA, and COCO Caption.

## Key Results
- Significantly outperforms baselines on VQA and captioning tasks across image, video, and audio modalities
- Achieves parameter efficiency by freezing >99% of total parameters while training only one linear projection layer
- Shows consistent improvement when adding more trainable parameters (30.72% for eP-ALMlin to 34.34% for eP-ALMada with adapters)
- Demonstrates strong performance with limited training data, outperforming methods requiring large-scale multimodal pretraining

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training only one linear projection layer plus soft prompts achieves strong multimodal performance while freezing >99% of parameters.
- Mechanism: The linear projection maps [CLS] tokens from the perceptual encoder into the language model's embedding space, while soft prompts provide task-specific context to guide the LM's output generation.
- Core assumption: The perceptual encoder and language model produce representations that are sufficiently aligned in semantic space such that a simple linear transformation can bridge them.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "by freezing more than 99% of total parameters, training only one linear projection layer, and prepending only one trainable token, our approach (dubbed eP-ALM) significantly outperforms other baselines"
  - [section 3.1]: "We base our approach on this constraint and train only one linear projection layer (single connection) to connect both models"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus focuses on related multimodal fusion work but doesn't directly address parameter-efficient adaptation through linear projections
- Break condition: If the semantic gap between vision and language representations becomes too large, the linear projection will be insufficient and performance will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Hierarchical injection of [CLS] tokens from multiple encoder layers improves performance by leveraging multi-level semantic abstractions.
- Mechanism: Instead of using only the final [CLS] token, tokens from intermediate layers are projected and injected at corresponding positions in the language model, allowing the model to access both detailed and abstract visual information.
- Core assumption: Different layers in the perceptual encoder capture different levels of abstraction, and injecting these at corresponding levels in the language model allows for richer cross-modal interactions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "we take the [CLS] tokens from several layers of the perceptual model, and we inject these tokens into several layers of the LM"
  - [section 4.2]: "we can notice a consistent improvement of eP-ALM when adding more trainable parameters. The most parameter-efficient model is eP-ALMlin which has 30.72%, while the best has 34.34% (with Adapters eP-ALMada)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains related work on multimodal fusion but not specifically on hierarchical token injection
- Break condition: If the injection levels don't correspond well to the abstraction levels in the language model, or if the model becomes too complex to train effectively.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Casting VQA as open-ended generation rather than classification allows for more flexible and semantically rich responses.
- Mechanism: The model generates free-form text answers conditioned on both the question and visual input, evaluated by strict character-wise comparison with ground truth.
- Core assumption: Open-ended generation can capture semantic nuances that classification-based approaches miss, even though it's more challenging to evaluate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1.1]: "we cast the problem as open-ended generation and compute the accuracy after a strict comparison between the output text (without truncation) and the ground truth one"
  - [section 4.1.2]: "However, this is more challenging and not in favor of our approach" (acknowledging the difficulty of this approach)
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains related work on VQA but not specifically on open-ended generation approaches
- Break condition: If the evaluation metric doesn't align well with semantic correctness, or if the model generates plausible but factually incorrect answers.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Perceptual encoder (ViT/TimeSformer/AST) - frozen
  - Linear projection layer(s) - trainable
  - Soft prompt - trainable
  - Language model (OPT) - frozen
  - Cross-entropy loss for training
  - Token injection mechanism at specific layers

- Critical path:
  1. Extract [CLS] tokens from perceptual encoder layers
  2. Project tokens to language model embedding space
  3. Prepend projected tokens and soft prompt to text tokens
  4. Pass through frozen language model
  5. Compute loss and backpropagate to trainable components only

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Parameter efficiency vs. performance: Training fewer parameters saves resources but may limit performance
  - Linear projection vs. adapters: Linear projection is more efficient but adapters may capture more complex relationships
  - Open-ended generation vs. classification: More flexible but harder to evaluate
  - Hierarchical injection vs. single token: Richer information but more complex to implement

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degrades significantly when scaling to larger models
  - The model generates plausible but incorrect answers
  - Training becomes unstable with certain layer configurations
  - Evaluation metrics don't capture semantic correctness

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement eP-ALMlin variant and test on VQA v2 to verify basic functionality
  2. Add soft prompts to create eP-ALMpt and measure performance improvement
  3. Experiment with different numbers of [CLS] tokens and injection layers to find optimal configuration

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different sampling strategies for the visual tokens (e.g., [CLS] vs average pooling) affect the performance of eP-ALM across different modalities?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions using [CLS] tokens from several layers of the perceptual encoders and compares this to using only the last [CLS] token. It also notes that averaging [CLS] tokens from all processed frames is worse than using a single [CLS] token for video captioning.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not systematically explore different sampling strategies for visual tokens or compare their impact on performance across modalities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments comparing eP-ALM with different visual token sampling strategies (e.g., [CLS] from specific layers, average pooling, attention-weighted pooling) across image, video, and audio tasks, and analyze the results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of scaling the visual encoder on the performance of eP-ALM, and how does this interact with the size of the language model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that scaling the visual model (ViT) improves performance and that the best results are obtained with ViT-L/16. It also shows that scaling the language model (OPT) improves performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the interaction between the size of the visual encoder and the language model, or provide a comprehensive analysis of how scaling the visual encoder affects performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conduct experiments scaling the visual encoder (e.g., ViT-S, ViT-B, ViT-L) while keeping the language model size fixed, and vice versa. Analyze the results to understand the interaction between the two model sizes and their impact on performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does eP-ALM perform on tasks beyond VQA, captioning, and audio captioning, such as visual reasoning, image generation, or multimodal sentiment analysis?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on evaluating eP-ALM on VQA, captioning, and audio captioning tasks. While it mentions that the approach can be extended to other modalities, it does not provide evidence of its performance on other tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the applicability of eP-ALM to a wider range of multimodal tasks beyond the ones explicitly evaluated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Apply eP-ALM to other multimodal tasks, such as visual reasoning (e.g., NLVR2), image generation (e.g., DALL-E), or multimodal sentiment analysis (e.g., MOSI), and evaluate its performance on these tasks.

## Limitations

- The approach's performance on diverse, real-world multimodal data remains untested beyond established benchmarks
- Data efficiency claims need more comprehensive validation across different training set sizes
- Open-ended generation evaluation methodology may not fairly compare with classification-based approaches
- Performance scaling with larger models and different perceptual encoders requires further validation

## Confidence

- High confidence: The core mechanism of using linear projections and soft prompts to bridge frozen unimodal models is technically sound and well-explained
- Medium confidence: The performance claims relative to baselines, while impressive, rely on comparisons that may not be directly comparable
- Low confidence: The open-ended generation evaluation methodology and its ability to fairly compare with classification-based approaches is questionable

## Next Checks

1. Scale-up validation: Test the approach with larger language models (e.g., OPT-13B or larger) and different perceptual encoders to verify parameter efficiency claims hold across model scales.

2. Cross-dataset generalization test: Evaluate the trained models on out-of-distribution data (e.g., using VQA v2 trained models on VizWiz or other specialized datasets) to verify the generalization claims and identify potential domain-specific limitations.

3. Ablation study on projection depth: Systematically vary the number of [CLS] tokens injected and their corresponding layers to create a more comprehensive understanding of the optimal configuration.
