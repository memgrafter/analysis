# MuAViC: A Multilingual Audio-Visual Corpus for Robust Speech Recognition and Robust Speech-to-Text Translation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MuAViC, a large multilingual audio-visual corpus for speech recognition and speech-to-text translation. The corpus contains 1200 hours of transcribed audio-visual speech in 9 languages, covering both speech recognition and 6 English-to-X and 6 X-to-English translation directions. Built from TED and TEDx talks, MuAViC provides text translations for 6 of the 9 languages and demonstrates effectiveness for building noise-robust speech recognition and translation models.

## Method Summary
MuAViC was constructed from TED and TEDx talks, automatically processed to align audio, visual, and text modalities. The corpus covers 9 languages with transcribed speech data and includes translations for 6 languages. Automatic speech recognition and translation systems were used to process the source material, creating parallel audio-visual-text datasets for both speech recognition and translation tasks. The corpus specifically targets noise-robustness by incorporating challenging acoustic conditions. Assumption: The automatic processing pipeline likely includes speech segmentation, alignment with transcripts, and noise injection for robustness training.

## Key Results
- Audio-visual speech recognition models achieve 53.2 average WER across 9 languages in noisy environments
- Audio-visual speech-to-text translation models achieve 20.8 average BLEU score for English-to-X directions in noisy conditions
- Audio-visual speech-to-text translation models achieve 11.2 average BLEU score for X-to-English directions in noisy conditions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Audio-visual integration works by leveraging visual cues (lip movements, facial expressions) to disambiguate speech in noisy conditions where audio signals are degraded. The visual modality provides complementary information that helps speech recognition systems maintain performance when acoustic signals are corrupted by background noise. This multimodal fusion approach is particularly effective because human speech perception naturally integrates both audio and visual information, and neural models can learn to exploit these cross-modal correlations for improved robustness.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Raw audio/visual streams -> Feature extraction -> Multimodal fusion -> Recognition/Translation model -> Output predictions

**Critical path**: The most compute-intensive operations occur during feature extraction (spectrograms, face embeddings) and multimodal fusion layers where audio and visual representations are combined

**Design tradeoffs**: MuAViC prioritizes coverage across many languages over depth in any single language pair, trading potential monolingual performance gains for broader multilingual applicability

**Failure signatures**: Performance degradation occurs primarily under extreme noise conditions where visual cues become unreliable, and for language pairs with limited parallel training data

## Limitations
- Corpus construction relies on automatic processing which may propagate errors through the dataset
- Evaluation metrics (WER and BLEU) have known limitations in capturing semantic equivalence
- Simulated noise conditions may not fully represent real-world acoustic environments

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Corpus construction process and basic statistics are well-documented and verifiable
- **Medium confidence**: Performance metrics are methodologically sound but sensitive to unreported hyperparameters
- **Medium confidence**: Noise-robustness claims rely on simulated rather than real-world noise conditions
