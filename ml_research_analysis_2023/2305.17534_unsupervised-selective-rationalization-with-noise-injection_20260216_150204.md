# Unsupervised Selective Rationalization with Noise Injection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17534
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17534
- Reference count: 12
- Authors: None provided
- Key outcome: Introduces noise injection to improve unsupervised rationale generation, achieving up to 21% relative improvement in F1 score and enabling use of high-capacity generators like BERT

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of generating plausible rationales in unsupervised selective rationalization, where existing methods often produce rationales that lack convincing explanations for predictions. The authors propose a novel training strategy that uses online noise injection to probabilistically replace unimportant words in the generated rationale with noise during training. This approach disrupts the generator's ability to exploit biases in the predictor, encouraging more plausible rationale generation. The method is evaluated across multiple datasets including a new benchmark combining movie review datasets, ERASER Movie Review, MultiRC, and FEVER tasks, showing significant improvements in both rationale plausibility and task accuracy.

## Method Summary
The proposed method introduces online noise injection during training of the rationale generator. The generator creates rationales that are expected to explain the predictor's decisions, but without supervision it can learn to exploit biases in the predictor rather than generating truly explanatory content. The noise injection mechanism addresses this by probabilistically replacing unimportant words in the generated rationale with noise during training. This disruption prevents the generator from learning to game the predictor through spurious correlations, forcing it to produce more meaningful and plausible rationales. The approach is particularly effective at enabling the use of powerful high-capacity generators like BERT, which previously degraded performance without noise injection.

## Key Results
- Achieved up to 21% relative improvement in F1 score for rationale plausibility
- Demonstrated significant improvements in task accuracy across multiple datasets
- Enabled use of high-capacity rationale generators (BERT) without performance degradation
- Maintained or improved model faithfulness while improving plausibility

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The noise injection mechanism works by introducing stochastic perturbations during training that prevent the generator from learning to exploit predictable patterns in the predictor. When unimportant words are randomly replaced with noise, the generator cannot rely on spurious correlations to produce rationales that appear plausible but lack true explanatory power. This forces the generator to focus on genuinely important words that consistently contribute to accurate predictions. The probabilistic nature of the noise injection means the generator must learn robust patterns rather than memorizing specific strategies for gaming the predictor. This approach effectively regularizes the generator, encouraging it to produce rationales that are both plausible to humans and genuinely explanatory of the model's predictions.

**Model Faithfulness**: The degree to which generated rationales actually explain the model's predictions. Why needed: Ensures interpretability is meaningful rather than superficial. Quick check: Do rationale omissions affect prediction accuracy?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input Text -> Generator -> Rationale + Noise -> Predictor -> Prediction

**Critical Path**: The generator produces a rationale by selecting important words from the input text. This rationale is then passed through the noise injection mechanism where unimportant words are probabilistically replaced with noise tokens. The modified rationale is fed to the predictor, which generates the final prediction. The generator is trained to maximize predictor accuracy while the noise injection prevents it from exploiting predictor biases.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Higher noise injection rates provide stronger regularization but may make training unstable or prevent learning entirely. Lower rates may not sufficiently disrupt bias exploitation. The choice of noise distribution (uniform vs. targeted) affects which types of spurious correlations are disrupted. Using high-capacity generators like BERT provides better rationale quality but requires careful noise injection to prevent performance degradation.

**Failure Signatures**: If noise injection is too aggressive, the generator may fail to learn any meaningful patterns and produce random or empty rationales. If too weak, the generator will continue exploiting predictor biases, producing rationales that look plausible but don't truly explain predictions. Poor predictor architecture can limit the effectiveness of any rationale, regardless of generator quality.

**First Experiments**: 1) Test different noise injection probabilities (0.1, 0.3, 0.5) to find optimal balance between regularization and learning. 2) Compare rationale quality with and without noise injection using human evaluation. 3) Evaluate whether high-capacity generators (BERT vs. LSTM) benefit differently from noise injection.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness heavily depends on careful tuning of noise injection probability parameter
- Generalization to non-text domains (medical, legal, scientific) remains untested
- Predictor accuracy as proxy for rationale quality doesn't guarantee true faithfulness
- Evaluation limited to specific benchmark datasets, limiting broader applicability assessment

## Confidence
- High confidence in plausibility improvement claims (supported by quantitative F1 score improvements)
- Medium confidence in high-capacity generator claims (empirical but may depend on architectural choices)
- Low confidence in broader applicability across diverse NLP tasks (limited evaluation scope)
