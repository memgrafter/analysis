# Boosting classification reliability of NLP transformer models in the long run

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10016
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10016
- Reference count: 28
- Key outcome: Fine-tuning BERT-based models with all available unlabeled data best maintains classification performance over time, though regular re-annotation is still necessary as fine-tuning alone only slows performance degradation.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how to maintain the performance of BERT-based classification models over time as language and topics evolve. Using a large corpus of Hungarian social media comments about COVID-19 vaccination from 2020-2021, the authors compare different approaches to fine-tune the model over time. They find that using all available unlabeled data for fine-tuning is most effective, but fine-tuning alone does not prevent performance degradation - it only slows it down. Regular re-annotation is necessary to maintain high performance as language changes rapidly.

## Method Summary
The authors use huBERT (Hungarian BERT) and fine-tune it using unlabeled Hungarian social media comments from three different time periods: Sep 2020-Jan 2021 (1.3M comments), Oct-Nov 2021 (777k comments), and Sep 2020-Dec 2021 (5.5M comments). They train classifiers on manually annotated comments (10k from first round, 4.5k from second round) and evaluate F1-scores for anti-vax and non-anti-vax classes. The study compares performance when using different time periods for fine-tuning and tests sampling strategies (random vs. weirdness-based) for selecting new annotations.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning with all available unlabeled data from all time periods maintains the best classification performance
- Fine-tuning alone does not prevent performance degradation but only slows it down
- Randomly sampling new annotations is more effective than selecting based on word "weirdness" metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuning with all available unlabeled data from all time periods is the most effective approach for maintaining classification performance over time.
- Mechanism: Using the complete corpus for fine-tuning allows the model to learn the full distribution of language patterns, including emerging terminology and contextual shifts, rather than being constrained to a limited temporal window.
- Core assumption: Language change in the domain is continuous and affects the underlying data distribution, requiring exposure to the full temporal spectrum for effective adaptation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our results show that the best solution is using all available unlabeled comments to fine-tune a model"
  - [section] "The model with the full unlabeled data and the model with the combination of swallow and deep learning had the same level of performance"
- Break condition: If the cost of processing the full corpus becomes prohibitive or if temporal drift is so extreme that older data becomes irrelevant noise.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Random sampling of new annotations is more effective than targeted sampling based on word "weirdness" metrics.
- Mechanism: Random sampling provides a more representative sample of the evolving language landscape, capturing unexpected patterns and contexts that targeted sampling might miss, leading to better generalization.
- Core assumption: The distribution of important linguistic changes is not easily predictable by simple frequency metrics and requires broader coverage.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "randomly sampling new annotations is more effective than selecting based on word 'weirdness' metrics"
  - [section] "The random sampling approach worked better than the specific weirdness-based sampling approach, even for classifying comments with high weirdness values"
- Break condition: If weirdness metrics could be improved to capture more sophisticated semantic or contextual shifts rather than just frequency changes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning slows down but does not prevent performance degradation in rapidly changing linguistic environments.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning adapts the model to recent patterns, providing a buffer against immediate performance drops, but cannot fully compensate for fundamental shifts in language use that require new annotations to capture.
- Core assumption: Language evolution in the domain is too rapid and complex for fine-tuning alone to maintain performance indefinitely.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "fine-tuning alone does not prevent performance degradation but only slows it down"
  - [section] "fine-tuning does not protect against model performance loss, but at least slows down the performance loss"
- Break condition: If the rate of language change slows significantly or if fine-tuning techniques become more sophisticated in capturing temporal dynamics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the BERT model degrade over time, and what is the rate of degradation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that fine-tuning does not prevent the model from losing performance but merely slows it down, and that in a rapidly changing linguistic environment, it is not possible to maintain model performance without regularly annotating new text.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific data on the rate of performance degradation over time or how quickly the model's performance drops without re-annotation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking model performance over extended periods with regular re-annotation intervals would provide concrete data on degradation rates.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Are there alternative sampling strategies that could be more effective than random sampling for selecting comments to re-annotate?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that randomly sampling new annotations is more effective than selecting based on word "weirdness" metrics, but it does not explore other potential sampling strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares random sampling with weirdness-based sampling, leaving open the possibility that other methods might be more effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing various sampling strategies (e.g., active learning, uncertainty sampling) against random sampling would determine if more effective alternatives exist.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of the model vary across different topics or domains beyond COVID-19 vaccination?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on COVID-19 vaccination comments, but the authors discuss the broader issue of maintaining model performance over time as language and topics evolve.
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to a single domain (COVID-19 vaccination), so it is unclear whether the findings generalize to other topics or domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the same methodology to different domains (e.g., politics, sports, entertainment) and comparing results would indicate whether the findings are domain-specific or generalizable.

## Limitations
- The study focuses exclusively on Hungarian social media comments about COVID-19 vaccination, limiting generalizability to other languages and domains.
- The time period covered (Sep 2020-Dec 2021) represents a particularly dynamic context with rapidly evolving terminology, potentially exaggerating observed performance degradation.
- Computational requirements for processing the full corpus of unlabeled data (5.5M comments) may not be feasible for all organizations.
