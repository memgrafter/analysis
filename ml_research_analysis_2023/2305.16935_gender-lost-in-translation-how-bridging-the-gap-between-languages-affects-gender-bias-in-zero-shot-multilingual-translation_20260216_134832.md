# Gender Lost In Translation: How Bridging The Gap Between Languages Affects Gender Bias in Zero-Shot Multilingual Translation

## Quick Facts

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16935

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates gender bias in zero-shot multilingual neural machine translation, specifically examining how different bridging strategies and bridge languages affect gender preservation. The study compares zero-shot translation (direct translation between unseen language pairs) with pivot-based translation (translating through an intermediate language) across different bridge languages with varying levels of gender inflection. The authors also explore how encouraging language-agnostic hidden representations in transformer models can reduce masculine bias. Using the MuST-SHE corpus for evaluation, they find that zero-shot translation performs better than pivoting when bridging via low-inflection languages like English, but the advantage reverses with richer gender-inflected bridge languages like Spanish or German.

## Method Summary
The study trains transformer-based multilingual NMT models on various parallel corpora, comparing baseline models with modifications that encourage language-agnostic representations (residual connection removal, auxiliary similarity loss, adversarial training). Models are evaluated using zero-shot translation and pivot-based translation across different bridge languages. The MuST-SHE corpus provides gender-annotated evaluation data for French-Italian translations, with accuracy metrics weighted by sample size to measure gender preservation performance. BLEU scores assess translation quality alongside gender-specific accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot translation outperforms pivot-based translation in preserving feminine gender when bridging via low-inflection languages like English
- Language-agnostic representations significantly reduce masculine bias in zero-shot models, leading to more balanced gender preservation
- With increased levels of gender inflection in the bridge language, pivoting surpasses zero-shot translation regarding fairer gender preservation for speaker-related gender agreement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Zero-shot translation reduces masculine bias compared to pivot-based translation when bridging via low-inflection languages like English.
- Mechanism: Zero-shot translation relies on continuous semantic representations rather than discrete pivot language representations. This preserves gender information that would otherwise be lost when translating through a genderless bridge language like English.
- Core assumption: Gender information in source sentences is not fully recoverable after translating through a genderless bridge language.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show that zero-shot translation outperforms pivot-based translation in preserving feminine gender when bridging via low-inflection languages like English"
  - [section] "For English, a language limited to pronominal gender... we find that zero-shot translation performs better than pivoting regarding a more fairly balanced preservation of feminine and masculine gender"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence linking English's gender properties to bias mitigation in zero-shot translation
- Break condition: If bridge language has rich gender inflection, this advantage disappears as pivoting can preserve gender information through the bridge.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Language-agnostic hidden representations reduce masculine bias in zero-shot translation.
- Mechanism: Modifications that encourage language-agnostic representations (removing residual connections, auxiliary similarity loss, adversarial training) help the model learn gender-independent semantic mappings, reducing reliance on language-specific gender patterns that may be biased.
- Core assumption: Language-specific representations encode gender biases that impair gender preservation in unseen language pairs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Additionally, language-agnostic representations significantly reduce masculine bias in zero-shot models, leading to more balanced gender preservation"
  - [section] "Modifying our baseline B to encourage language-agnostic representations improves the poor gender preservation performance of B noticeably when performing zero-shot translation"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence directly linking language-agnostic representations to reduced bias
- Break condition: If modifications are too aggressive, they may degrade overall translation quality, potentially reintroducing bias through poor translations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Bridge language gender inflection level affects whether zero-shot or pivot-based translation is more gender-fair.
- Mechanism: With high-inflection bridge languages like Spanish or German, pivoting can preserve gender information through the bridge language, while zero-shot translation may lose this information in continuous representations. With low-inflection languages like English, pivoting loses gender information while zero-shot translation can preserve it.
- Core assumption: The gender inflection level of the bridge language determines whether discrete (pivoting) or continuous (zero-shot) representations better preserve gender information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "with increased levels of gender inflection in the bridge language, pivoting surpasses zero-shot translation regarding fairer gender preservation for speaker-related gender agreement"
  - [section] "Using two richer gender-inflected bridge languages, Spanish and German, revealed that with an increased level of gender inflection in the bridge language, pivoting surpasses zero-shot translation regarding fewer gender-biased outputs for utterances with speaker-related gender agreement"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence linking specific bridge languages to gender preservation differences
- Break condition: If bridge language has moderate inflection, the difference between approaches may be negligible.
