# Think Rationally about What You See: Continuous Rationale Extraction for Relation Extraction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03503
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03503
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: RE2 improves relation extraction F1 performance by leveraging continuity and sparsity factors for rationale extraction, especially in low-resource settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RE2, a continuous rationale extraction framework for relation extraction that addresses the challenge of lacking gold rationales. The core innovation is using continuity and sparsity factors to extract relevant and coherent rationales from sentences, combined with an optimizable binary mask that adjusts rationales based on relation labels. Experiments on four datasets demonstrate that RE2 surpasses baseline models, achieving significant F1 improvements, particularly in low-resource settings.

## Method Summary
RE2 employs a continuous rationale extraction approach where a binary mask selects relevant tokens from sentences, guided by continuity and sparsity factors. The model uses marginal inference with Lagrange multipliers to reformulate hard constraints into continuous functions, enabling end-to-end optimization. The relation classifier takes the selected rationales along with entity information to predict relation labels. The framework is trained jointly without requiring gold rationales, using the relation label as a pseudo-label to guide rationale extraction.

## Key Results
- RE2 surpasses baseline models on four relation extraction datasets
- Significant F1 performance improvement, especially in low-resource settings
- Extracted rationales show better continuity and coherence compared to baseline approaches
- The model effectively removes irrelevant content while maintaining semantic coherence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Continuity and sparsity factors enforce selection of coherent, noise-free segments that improve relation extraction accuracy
- Mechanism: Binary mask with continuity (CON) and sparsity (SPA) factors optimize token selection. Continuity ensures consecutive tokens are chosen together, maintaining semantic coherence. Sparsity limits token count, reducing noise. Together they produce relevant, fluent rationales.
- Core assumption: Semantic coherence in extracted rationales directly correlates with improved relation extraction performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "leverages two continuity and sparsity factors to obtain relevant and coherent rationales from sentences"
  - [section] "The soft constraint is inherited from the CON factors, encouraging to select consecutive tokens that are relevant to the RE task"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: corpus shows related work on relation extraction but no direct evidence of continuity/sparsity impact on F1 performance
- Break condition: If continuity and sparsity factors are removed, F1 performance drops (as shown in ablation study)

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: End-to-end training with optimizable binary mask allows model to adjust rationales according to relation labels without gold rationales
- Mechanism: Binary mask vector selects tokens and updates based on relation label during training through marginal inference with Lagrange multiplier, reformulating hard constraints into continuous functions for gradient-based optimization
- Core assumption: Relation label provides sufficient signal to guide rationale extraction in absence of gold rationales
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "adjust the rationales that need to be selected according to the relation label"
  - [section] "we employ an adjustable binary mask for rationale selection and modify the rationale tokens necessary for the relation extraction task using relation labels"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: corpus does not mention end-to-end training or binary mask optimization for relation extraction
- Break condition: If relation labels are uninformative or noisy, rationale extraction may not align with true relevant content

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Incorporating entity information with continuity and sparsity constraints leads to more accurate relation extraction, especially in low-resource settings
- Mechanism: Model combines entity embeddings with rationale tokens for relation classifier input. Continuity and sparsity factors ensure selected rationales are coherent and focused, particularly beneficial when training data is limited
- Core assumption: Entity information provides strong signal for relation extraction, and combination with well-chosen rationales amplifies this signal
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments on four datasets show that RE2 surpasses baselines"
  - [section] "Removing the added entities has minimal effect on F1 performance (0.1%)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: corpus mentions entity-based methods but does not discuss combination with continuity/sparsity factors
- Break condition: If entity information is not informative for the relation, adding entities may not improve performance

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Sentence → BERT encoding → Importance matrix → Factor graph (CON+SPA) → Binary mask → Rationale selection → Relation classification → Loss → Backpropagation

- Critical path:
  Sentence → BERT encoding → Importance matrix → Factor graph (CON+SPA) → Binary mask → Rationale selection → Relation classification → Loss → Backpropagation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sparsity factor vs. completeness: Higher sparsity reduces noise but may omit relevant information; lower sparsity includes more content but risks noise
  - Continuity factor vs. flexibility: Stronger continuity enforces coherence but may miss discontinuous but relevant segments
  - End-to-end training vs. modularity: Joint training allows adaptation but may be harder to debug than separate modules

- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 performance: Could indicate poor rationale selection (check continuity/sparsity balance) or weak relation classifier
  - High perplexity in extracted rationales: Suggests rationales are not semantically coherent (check continuity factor)
  - Overfitting on training data: May occur if sparsity is too low or if model relies too heavily on entity information

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary the sparsity parameter K and measure F1 performance and rationale length to find optimal balance between noise reduction and information retention
  2. Remove the continuity factor and compare F1 performance and rationale coherence (e.g., using perplexity) to quantify its impact
  3. Train the model without adding entity information to the relation classifier and measure change in F1 performance to assess contribution of entities
