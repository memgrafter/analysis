# Combo of Thinking and Observing for Outside-Knowledge VQA

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06407
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06407
- Reference count: 35
- Key outcome: Proposed framework achieves 6.17% accuracy improvement on OK-VQA dataset by combining conventional VQA and language-centric paradigms

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for outside-knowledge visual question answering (OK-VQA) that bridges the gap between conventional VQA approaches and language-centric methods. The core innovation lies in constraining the cross-modality space into the same space as natural-language space, enabling direct decoding of both text and multimodal embeddings. By introducing four types of knowledge (explicit/implicit textual and multimodal), the framework significantly outperforms existing methods on the OK-VQA dataset. The approach addresses the fundamental challenge of OK-VQA where questions require external knowledge beyond the visual content.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework combines multimodal and textual encoders in a unified architecture. A multimodal encoder (LXMERT/VisualBERT) processes visual features while a textual encoder (T5) handles knowledge sources including Wikipedia passages, GPT-3 outputs, and OFA responses. The answer decoder combines outputs from both encoders in a shared latent space. The model is pre-trained on VQAv2 to accumulate multimodal knowledge, then fine-tuned on OK-VQA with additional knowledge retrieval. This architecture allows the introduction of explicit and implicit multimodal and textual knowledge while preserving visual information through the unified space constraint.

## Key Results
- Achieves 6.17% accuracy improvement over state-of-the-art on OK-VQA dataset
- Ablation studies show significant performance drops when removing any of the four knowledge types
- Pre-training on VQAv2 proves essential, with "w/o pre-train" condition showing substantial accuracy decline
- The model maintains competitive performance even without fine-tuning, demonstrating strong knowledge integration capabilities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Constraining cross-modality space into the same space as natural-language space allows the model to decode both text and multimodal embeddings directly. By using a multimodal encoder for visual features and a textual encoder for knowledge sources, the framework preserves visual information while leveraging the vast textual knowledge space. The answer decoder combines outputs from both encoders in a unified latent space. Core assumption: Visual features can be effectively mapped into the same dimensional space as textual embeddings without losing critical information.

### Mechanism 2
Introducing four types of knowledge (explicit/implicit textual and multimodal) provides complementary benefits that improve OK-VQA performance. The framework retrieves explicit knowledge from Wikipedia, accumulates multimodal knowledge from VQAv2 pre-training, and retrieves implicit knowledge from GPT-3 and OFA. Each knowledge type addresses different aspects of the question-answering task. Core assumption: Different knowledge sources capture different aspects of world knowledge that are complementary for answering complex visual questions.

### Mechanism 3
Pre-training on VQAv2 enables the model to accumulate multimodal knowledge that enhances performance on OK-VQA. The model is pre-trained on VQAv2 to learn visual question answering patterns, then fine-tuned on OK-VQA with additional knowledge sources. This pre-training helps the answer decoder handle outputs from both multimodal and textual encoders. Core assumption: Multimodal knowledge learned from VQAv2 is transferable and beneficial for the more challenging OK-VQA task.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image → Multimodal Encoder → Feature Mapping → Answer Decoder ← Textual Encoder ← Knowledge Retrieval ← Question
- Critical path: Image → Multimodal Encoder → Feature Mapping → Answer Decoder ← Textual Encoder ← Knowledge Retrieval ← Question
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using parallel encoders (vs. serial) preserves visual features but requires careful fusion
  - Four knowledge types provide coverage but increase complexity and computational cost
  - Pre-training on VQAv2 improves performance but requires additional training resources
- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy despite high knowledge hit rate: suggests poor knowledge-to-answer conversion
  - Significant performance drop without pre-training: indicates over-reliance on pre-learned patterns
  - Inconsistent results across different knowledge types: suggests imbalance in knowledge integration
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate individual encoder performance: Test multimodal encoder on VQA accuracy, textual encoder on knowledge retrieval accuracy
  2. Test knowledge integration: Run ablation study removing each knowledge type to measure impact
  3. Validate pre-training benefit: Compare performance with and without VQAv2 pre-training on a validation subset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can the model effectively combine the output of the two encoders (multimodal and textual) to maximize the benefits of both natural-language and cross-modality spaces? The paper acknowledges the need for further exploration in this area but does not provide specific methods or approaches to achieve this combination effectively.

### Open Question 2
How can the model effectively handle the introduction of multiple offline knowledge retrieval processes, such as retrieving relevant Wikipedia passages, while maintaining online deployment capabilities? The paper mentions that the proposed method involves multiple offline knowledge retrieval processes, which may make it difficult to deploy the model as an online model.

### Open Question 3
How can the model effectively address the potential risks of dataset bias, such as language bias, multimodal shortcut, and harmful stereotypes, in the OK-VQA and VQAv2 training sets? The paper acknowledges the potential risks of dataset bias in VQA models but does not provide specific methods or strategies to address these biases.

## Limitations

- Scalability concerns due to heavy reliance on multiple external knowledge sources (Wikipedia, GPT-3, OFA) that may become unavailable or change APIs
- Computational cost of maintaining and querying four different knowledge types may limit practical deployment in resource-constrained environments
- Uncertainty about how well the approach generalizes to domains beyond OK-VQA, particularly for languages other than English or specialized knowledge domains

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the core mechanism of mapping multimodal and textual spaces to a unified representation, evidenced by the 6.17% accuracy improvement over SOTA
- **Medium confidence** in the specific contributions of each knowledge type, as ablation studies show performance drops but individual contributions aren't fully isolated
- **Low confidence** in the long-term sustainability of the approach, given its heavy dependence on external APIs and lack of discussion about handling knowledge source failures

## Next Checks

1. **Knowledge Source Redundancy Test**: Systematically test the framework's performance when each knowledge source is individually disabled or provides noisy/inaccurate information, to quantify the system's robustness to knowledge source failures.

2. **Cross-Domain Generalization Study**: Evaluate the model's performance on related tasks such as science question answering or medical visual question answering to assess whether the learned knowledge integration generalizes beyond the OK-VQA domain.

3. **Computational Efficiency Analysis**: Measure the end-to-end inference time and memory usage with all four knowledge types active, then compare against simpler baselines to quantify the practical cost-benefit tradeoff of the approach.
