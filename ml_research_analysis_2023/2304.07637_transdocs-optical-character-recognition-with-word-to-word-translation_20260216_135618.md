# TransDocs: Optical Character Recognition with word to word translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07637
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07637
- Reference count: 17
- One-line primary result: Data augmentation with synthetic OCR errors improves BLEU-4 from 0.73 to 0.81 in attention-based translation.

## Executive Summary
This work presents TransDocs, an end-to-end OCR-to-translation pipeline designed to correct OCR output errors before translation. By generating synthetic distorted English images, running them through EasyOCR, and pairing predictions with Spanish ground truth, the model learns to map common OCR errors (e.g., “c0de” → “code”) to correct translations. Two LSTM-based seq2seq models are trained: a standard model and a Luong attention model. Data augmentation significantly improves BLEU-4 scores and reduces validation loss, demonstrating the value of error-aware training for OCR-generated text.

## Method Summary
The pipeline uses TextRecognitionDataGenerator to create synthetic distorted images, which are processed by EasyOCR to simulate OCR errors. These error-prone predictions are paired with correct English words and their Spanish translations from the ANKI corpus. Two LSTM-based seq2seq models—standard and Luong attention—are trained on both original and augmented data. The models use learned embeddings and are evaluated using BLEU-4 score and validation loss.

## Key Results
- Data augmentation reduced validation loss by ~0.4.
- Attention model BLEU-4 improved from 0.73 to 0.81.
- Attention-based seq2seq outperformed standard seq2seq on augmented data.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Synthetic data augmentation improves OCR robustness to distortions.
- Mechanism: TextRecognitionDataGenerator creates images with randomized fonts, backgrounds, skew, and noise; EasyOCR processes them to produce error-prone predictions mapped to correct translations.
- Core assumption: Distorted OCR outputs can be mapped to correct translations if ground-truth is available.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract, section on data generation, and OCR prediction mapping.
- Break condition: If OCR model fails to produce recognizable output even with augmented data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Attention-based seq2seq models outperform standard seq2seq models.
- Mechanism: Luong attention computes context vectors from encoder/decoder hidden states, allowing the decoder to focus on relevant source words.
- Core assumption: Attention helps the model focus on relevant source words, improving translation of distorted OCR output.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section on attention model.
- Break condition: If attention model cannot learn to attend correctly due to insufficient data or noise.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Lower validation loss correlates with higher BLEU-4 scores.
- Mechanism: Training monitors Sparse Categorical Cross-Entropy loss; lower validation loss indicates better generalization, reflected in BLEU-4.
- Core assumption: Validation loss is a reliable proxy for translation quality.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and BLEU-4 results table.
- Break condition: If validation loss decreases but BLEU-4 does not improve.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models with LSTM
  - Why needed here: OCR outputs must be translated word-by-word; LSTM cells capture sequential dependencies and context.
  - Quick check question: How does an LSTM cell maintain memory across time steps?

- **Concept**: Attention mechanisms in neural translation
  - Why needed here: OCR errors can scramble word order; attention allows decoder to focus on relevant source words regardless of position.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between Bahdanau and Luong attention?

- **Concept**: BLEU score computation
  - Why needed here: Quantifies translation quality; higher BLEU indicates closer match to reference translation.
  - Quick check question: What does BLEU-4 measure and how is it calculated?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: TextRecognitionDataGenerator → EasyOCR → Error-to-translation mapping → Seq2seq LSTM models (standard/attention) → BLEU-4 evaluation
- **Critical path**: Generate augmented OCR outputs → pair with ground-truth translations → train seq2seq model → evaluate BLEU-4.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Synthetic data vs real OCR errors: Synthetic may not capture all real-world noise patterns.
  - Attention vs no attention: Attention improves quality but increases complexity and training time.
  - EasyOCR vs Tesseract: EasyOCR handles distortion better but may be slower.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - BLEU-4 plateaus despite training: Possible overfitting or insufficient data diversity.
  - Validation loss diverges from training loss: Overfitting or learning rate too high.
  - OCR predictions are too corrupted: Augmentation strategy insufficient.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train standard seq2seq on clean data, measure BLEU-4 baseline.
  2. Train attention seq2seq on augmented data, compare BLEU-4 and validation loss.
  3. Vary n_units and learning rate to find optimal hyperparameters for BLEU-4.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does using different OCR models (e.g., Tesseract) in the pipeline improve overall translation performance compared to EasyOCR, particularly for skewed or noisy text?
- Basis in paper: Authors compared EasyOCR and Tesseract, found EasyOCR better for distorted text, but did not test how Tesseract affects the end-to-end translation pipeline.
- Why unresolved: The study only tested EasyOCR in the final pipeline and did not explore whether Tesseract’s strengths in oriented or high-detail text could benefit the translation model.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing both OCR models end-to-end and comparing BLEU-4 scores for translated output when OCR errors are introduced.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would incorporating more diverse and realistic OCR error types (e.g., real-world scanning artifacts) in data augmentation lead to further improvements in translation accuracy?
- Basis in paper: The authors used synthetic distortions via TextRecognitionDataGenerator but noted that real-world documents have varied artifacts like uneven lighting, smudges, or complex backgrounds not fully simulated.
- Why unresolved: The augmentation focused on controlled distortions; real-world error distributions may differ and impact model robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the trained model on real scanned documents with varied artifacts and comparing BLEU-4 scores with models trained on synthetic-only errors.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Would replacing LSTM cells with GRU cells in the seq2seq architecture improve translation quality for OCR error correction, as suggested by prior literature?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention GRU could be better for short sentences/words but did not implement or test it.
- Why unresolved: The study used LSTM without comparing against GRU variants, despite acknowledging GRU’s potential advantages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating GRU-based seq2seq models on the same datasets and comparing BLEU-4 scores and validation loss with LSTM models.

## Limitations
- Performance improvements are based on synthetic data, not validated on real-world OCR errors.
- BLEU-4 scores are modest (0.73 to 0.81) and may not generalize to more complex or noisy document images.
- The ANKI dataset may not fully represent real-world document translation needs.
- Runtime efficiency and memory usage are not reported, limiting practical deployment insights.

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the methodology of using data augmentation to train OCR-aware translation models.
- **Medium confidence** in the reported BLEU-4 improvements due to limited dataset and lack of real-world validation.
- **Low confidence** in the robustness of the pipeline to diverse OCR error types not captured by synthetic augmentation.

## Next Checks
1. Test the TransDocs pipeline on real-world OCR outputs from varied document types (e.g., scanned books, receipts) to assess robustness to actual OCR errors.
2. Compare performance against a baseline that uses post-processing OCR correction before translation, to determine if end-to-end training offers tangible benefits.
3. Evaluate model generalization by testing on a different language pair or out-of-domain text to ensure the approach is not overfit to the ANKI dataset.
