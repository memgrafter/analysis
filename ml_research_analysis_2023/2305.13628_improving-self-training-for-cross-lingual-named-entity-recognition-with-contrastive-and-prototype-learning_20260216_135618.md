# Improving Self-training for Cross-lingual Named Entity Recognition with Contrastive and Prototype Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13628
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13628
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: +1.03 micro-F1 improvement on CoNLL and +3.16 on WikiAnn over previous SOTA methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ContProto, a self-training framework that addresses noisy pseudo labels in cross-lingual named entity recognition (NER) through a combination of contrastive learning and prototype-based pseudo-labeling. The method constructs pseudo-positive pairs for supervised contrastive learning between source and target language entities, then refines pseudo labels using moving-average prototypes as cluster centroids. ContProto achieves state-of-the-art results on six cross-lingual transfer pairs from CoNLL and WikiAnn datasets, demonstrating that contrastive learning creates reliable entity clusters while prototype learning improves pseudo-label accuracy.

## Method Summary
ContProto combines contrastive self-training with prototype-based pseudo-labeling to improve cross-lingual NER. The framework uses a span-based NER model with XLM-RoBERTa backbone to process source-language labeled data and target-language unlabeled data. During training, it constructs pseudo-positive pairs by combining source-language entities with target-language spans predicted as the same entity type, then applies supervised contrastive learning to align representations while separating different entity classes. Prototype learning dynamically refines pseudo labels by computing class-specific centroids and updating target-language span labels based on proximity to these prototypes. The two components work synergistically - contrastive learning creates separated clusters that make prototype learning effective, while refined pseudo labels from prototypes improve contrastive pair quality.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on six cross-lingual transfer pairs
- Improves micro-F1 by +1.03 over previous best method on CoNLL dataset
- Improves micro-F1 by +3.16 over previous best method on WikiAnn dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contrastive self-training improves cross-lingual NER by aligning source and target language entity representations while separating clusters of different entity types.
- Mechanism: Constructs pseudo-positive pairs by combining a labeled source-language entity with a target-language span predicted as the same entity type, and applies supervised contrastive learning to pull these pairs together while pushing apart different entity classes.
- Core assumption: The model's current predictions on target-language spans are sufficiently accurate to serve as pseudo labels for constructing valid positive pairs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our contrastive self-training facilitates span classification by separating clusters of different classes, and enhances cross-lingual transferability by producing closely-aligned representations between the source and target language."
  - [section 3.1] "we further construct pseudo-positive pairs comprising of a labeled source-language entity and a target-language span predicted as the same entity type by the current model."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prototype-based pseudo-labeling dynamically refines noisy pseudo labels during training by leveraging class-specific representation clusters.
- Mechanism: Uses moving-average prototypes as cluster centroids, then updates target-language span pseudo labels toward the closest prototype, with a margin-based criterion to prevent false positives for non-entity spans.
- Core assumption: Entity representations form tight clusters after contrastive learning, making prototypes reliable indicators of true entity types.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "prototype-based pseudo-labeling effectively improves the accuracy of pseudo labels during training."
  - [section 3.2] "By ranking the distances between the representation of an unlabeled span and each prototype, we gradually shift its soft pseudo label towards the closest class."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The two components are mutually beneficial - contrastive learning creates reliable clusters that make prototype learning effective, while prototype learning improves pseudo labels that make contrastive learning more accurate.
- Mechanism: Creates a feedback loop where better pseudo labels from prototype learning lead to better pseudo-positive pairs for contrastive learning, which in turn creates tighter clusters for more accurate prototype assignment.
- Core assumption: The iterative improvement between components is stable and doesn't amplify errors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our contrastive self-training and prototype-based pseudo-labeling are mutually beneficial."
  - [section 3.2] "By virtue of the clustering effect from contrastive learning, the resulting representations and prototypes act as guidance for refining pseudo labels. In turn, the model trained with refined pseudo-labels predicts unlabeled spans more accurately, and ensures the validity of pseudo-positive spans for contrastive learning."

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Supervised contrastive learning (SupCon)
  - Why needed here: Extends standard contrastive learning to leverage label information, creating positive pairs from same-class entities which is essential for separating entity types
  - Quick check question: How does SupCon differ from standard contrastive learning in terms of positive pair construction?

- Concept: Prototype learning
  - Why needed here: Provides a way to represent each entity class as a centroid in representation space, enabling dynamic pseudo-label refinement based on cluster proximity
  - Quick check question: What is the advantage of using prototypes over fixed class centroids computed from the entire dataset?

- Concept: Cross-lingual representation alignment
  - Why needed here: Enables transfer of knowledge from source to target languages by ensuring entities of the same type have similar representations across languages
  - Quick check question: Why is explicit cross-lingual alignment important for zero-shot transfer in NER?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: XLM-RoBERTa backbone -> Span representation -> Contrastive learning module (MLP projection, positive pair construction) -> Prototype learning module (moving-average prototypes, pseudo-label refinement) -> Consistency regularization module (KL divergence between dropout views)

- Critical path: Forward pass → span representation → contrastive loss computation → prototype update → pseudo-label refinement → backward pass

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using pseudo predictions as labels for positive pairs trades off some noise for scalability (vs. requiring gold labels)
  - Moving-average updates provide stability but may lag behind rapid representation changes
  - Margin-based criterion reduces false positives but may miss edge cases

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor contrastive clustering → prototypes won't be meaningful → pseudo-label quality won't improve
  - Too aggressive pseudo-label updates → error amplification → contrastive pairs become invalid
  - Incompatible margin settings → either too many false positives or too many missed entities

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify contrastive learning creates separated clusters by visualizing t-SNE of span representations
  2. Test prototype-based pseudo-label refinement by measuring oracle F1 of pseudo labels across training epochs
  3. Evaluate the impact of removing either contrastive learning or prototype learning (ablation studies) on final performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Question**: How does the performance of ContProto scale when applied to languages with larger linguistic gaps beyond those tested (e.g., languages from different families like Japanese or Finnish)?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates six cross-lingual transfer pairs from CoNLL and WikiAnn datasets, but does not test languages from entirely different families or scripts.
  - Why unresolved: The authors acknowledge that larger linguistic gaps may pose challenges, but do not empirically test these scenarios.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing ContProto's performance on transfer pairs involving languages from different families or scripts, such as Japanese, Finnish, or Arabic to English.

- **Question**: What is the impact of domain shift between the source-language labeled data and target-language unlabeled data on ContProto's effectiveness?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that domain shift might pose a question on the effectiveness of their method, but do not provide empirical evidence or mitigation strategies.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not address how ContProto handles domain shift, which is a common challenge in cross-lingual NLP.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing ContProto's performance on in-domain versus out-of-domain target-language data, and analysis of its robustness to domain shift.

- **Question**: How does ContProto perform on datasets with a larger number of entity types and imbalanced class distributions?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that the datasets used have few entity types and relatively balanced classes, and suggest that performance may vary with larger class sets and long-tailed distributions.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not test ContProto on datasets with more entity types or significant class imbalance, leaving its scalability and robustness in such scenarios unclear.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results on datasets with many entity types (e.g., 50+) and significant class imbalance, along with analysis of prototype quality and pseudo-label accuracy in these settings.

## Limitations

- The method's success heavily depends on the accuracy of initial pseudo labels for constructing contrastive pairs, with potential for error amplification if target-language predictions are too noisy
- Performance may degrade when applied to languages with larger linguistic gaps beyond those tested in the paper
- The method hasn't been tested on datasets with many entity types or significant class imbalance, leaving scalability questions unanswered

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall experimental results showing +1.03 F1 improvement on CoNLL and +3.16 on WikiAnn compared to previous SOTA methods. These are well-established datasets with clear baselines.
- **Medium Confidence**: The mutual benefit claim between contrastive learning and prototype-based pseudo-labeling. While the paper argues they're mutually beneficial, the ablation studies only show individual component contributions without demonstrating the iterative feedback loop's effectiveness.
- **Low Confidence**: The exact mechanism by which the margin-based criterion prevents false positives without missing true entities. The mathematical formulation is provided but empirical validation of this specific aspect is limited.

## Next Checks

1. **Pseudo-Label Quality Analysis**: Track and visualize the oracle F1 score of pseudo labels throughout training epochs to verify that prototype-based refinement actually improves label quality and doesn't introduce error amplification.

2. **Contrastive Learning Effectiveness**: Create t-SNE visualizations of span representations at different training epochs to empirically verify that contrastive learning successfully separates entity clusters and whether prototype quality correlates with cluster separation quality.

3. **Ablation of Cross-Lingual Transfer**: Repeat the main experiments using a weaker multilingual encoder (e.g., mBERT instead of XLM-RoBERTa) to determine whether the method's success depends on strong cross-lingual alignment or if it can compensate for weaker transfer through its training mechanisms.
