# Multi-label Video Classification for Underwater Ship Inspection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17338
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17338
- Reference count: 32
- This paper proposes a multi-label video classification model for underwater ship hull inspection using spatiotemporal transformers, achieving an F1-score of 0.72 on the LIACI dataset validation set.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of automatic video analysis for underwater ship hull inspection, a process currently performed manually and labor-intensively. The authors propose a multi-label video classification model that leverages the self-attention mechanism of transformers to capture spatiotemporal attention in consecutive video frames, improving upon existing methods that only consider spatial information on individual frames. The model is evaluated on the LIACI dataset and demonstrates promising results, achieving an F1-score of 0.72 on the validation set. The authors provide a detailed analysis of the benefits and limitations of using image-based classifiers and explore the advantages of incorporating temporal information into the model.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a multi-label video classification model for underwater ship hull inspection that exploits the self-attention mechanism of transformers to capture spatiotemporal attention in consecutive video frames. The model divides each video frame into fixed-size patches, applies self-attention over both spatial (within-frame) and temporal (across consecutive frames) dimensions, and aggregates contextual features for improved multi-label classification stability. The model is pre-trained on large-scale datasets (ImageNet, COCO) and fine-tuned on the smaller LIACI dataset, leveraging learned spatial feature hierarchies.

## Key Results
- The proposed model achieves an F1-score of 0.72 on the LIACI dataset validation set.
- COCO pre-trained ViT outperforms ImageNet pre-trained ViT in most multi-label metrics, except precision.
- Temporal consistency improves prediction confidence stability compared to frame-only classification methods.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Self-attention in transformers enables spatiotemporal context aggregation better than frame-wise CNNs.
- Mechanism: The model divides each video frame into fixed-size patches, applies self-attention over both spatial (within-frame) and temporal (across consecutive frames) dimensions, and aggregates contextual features for improved multi-label classification stability.
- Core assumption: Static visual content across frames contains enough shared information to be linked by attention without motion-based cues.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "exploits the self-attention mechanism of transformers to capture spatiotemporal attention in consecutive video frames"
  - [section II.D] "TimeSformer [21] ... utilizes self-attention over both spatial and temporal dimensions"
- Break condition: If consecutive frames are too dissimilar (e.g., sudden lighting changes, large camera motion), the attention links may not capture useful correlations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Temporal consistency improves prediction confidence stability compared to frame-only classification.
- Mechanism: By processing multiple consecutive frames jointly, the model learns to smooth predictions over time, reducing abrupt confidence fluctuations observed in image-only classifiers.
- Core assumption: Consecutive frames in underwater inspection videos change minimally, allowing temporal smoothing without losing discriminative power.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section IV.A] "COCO ViT outperformed IMAGENET ViT by a small margin in all metrics except precision" and "Fig. 10 demonstrates that both models improved the stability of temporal confidence"
  - [section III.C] "utilize OpenCV to observe the temporal aspect of the model's confidence for different labels during an inspection"
- Break condition: If the dataset contains abrupt environmental changes (e.g., occlusion, lighting variation), temporal smoothing may blur important distinctions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pre-training on large datasets (ImageNet, COCO) provides transferable feature representations for underwater domain adaptation.
- Mechanism: Vision Transformer models pre-trained on large-scale natural image datasets are fine-tuned on the smaller LIACI dataset, leveraging learned spatial feature hierarchies.
- Core assumption: Underwater imagery shares enough low-level visual patterns (edges, textures) with natural images for pre-training benefits to transfer.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II.B] "ViT needs to be pre-trained on large-scale datasets and then fine-tuned on smaller datasets"
  - [section IV.A] "COCO ViT outperformed IMAGENET ViT by a small margin in all metrics except precision"
- Break condition: If the underwater imagery is too domain-shifted (e.g., color casts, low contrast), pre-training benefits may be minimal or even harmful.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: 7 consecutive video frames → Patch embedding (32x32 patches) → Spatial Transformer (ViT encoder) → Temporal Transformer (self-attention over time) → MLP head → Multi-label outputs
  - Image-only variant: Single frame → ViT encoder → MLP head → Multi-label outputs
- Critical path: Frame preprocessing → Patch embedding → Spatial feature extraction → Temporal aggregation → Classification head → Loss computation (BCEWithLogitsLoss)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Frame sampling vs. tubelet embedding: Sampling is simpler but may miss motion cues; tubelet embedding captures motion but needs extra 3D CNN.
  - Pre-training dataset choice: COCO vs. ImageNet affects multi-label capability and domain transfer.
  - Transformer depth vs. dataset size: Deeper transformers need more data; freezing spatial transformer speeds training but may limit adaptation.
- Failure signatures:
  - Loss plateauing early → Model capacity mismatch or data insufficient for spatiotemporal learning.
  - High variance in temporal predictions → Temporal transformer not learning consistent features.
  - Low multi-label F1 → Class imbalance or weak discriminative features.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train frame-only ViT (ImageNet pre-trained) on LIACI validation set; evaluate multi-label metrics and temporal stability on a sample clip.
  2. Implement uniform frame sampling tokenization; train spatiotemporal ViT with frozen spatial transformer; compare validation loss and temporal consistency to frame-only.
  3. Replace uniform sampling with tubelet embedding using pre-trained 3D ResNet; evaluate if temporal performance improves and training stability increases.

## Limitations
- The LIACI dataset is relatively small for transformer training, raising questions about the model's robustness when scaled to larger, more diverse underwater inspection datasets or different underwater environments.
- The paper uses uniform frame sampling rather than tubelet embedding, which may miss important motion cues or temporal patterns that tubelet embedding could capture.
- The pre-training domain transfer from natural images to underwater imagery may be limited due to the unique visual challenges of underwater environments.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The fundamental approach of using transformers for multi-label video classification, the implementation of patch embedding and self-attention mechanisms, and the general superiority of temporal models over frame-only methods.
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific architectural choices (7-frame sampling, frozen spatial transformer, BCEWithLogitsLoss), the comparative advantage of COCO pre-training, and the interpretation of temporal stability improvements.
- **Low Confidence**: Generalization claims beyond the LIACI dataset, the optimality of the sampling strategy versus alternatives, and the extent of domain transfer from natural image pre-training to underwater imagery.
