# Record Deduplication for Entity Distribution Modeling in ASR Transcripts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06246
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06246
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: 95% retrieval of misrecognized entities, 5% relative WER reduction via contextual biasing

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of recognizing and handling misrecognized entities in ASR transcripts, particularly for trending search queries in voice digital assistants. The authors propose a method based on record deduplication, a technique from entity resolution, to model the true entity distribution from ASR output. The method involves clustering references to the same entity, including misrecognitions, and using this information to improve contextual biasing. The approach successfully retrieves 95% of misrecognized entities and shows an estimated 5% relative word error rate reduction when used for contextual biasing.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a record deduplication approach to model entity distribution from ASR outputs, particularly for trending search queries in voice digital assistants. The method involves clustering references to the same entity, including misrecognitions, by employing blocking, comparison, and clustering steps. This clustered entity distribution is then used to improve contextual biasing in the runtime ASR model, achieving a 5% relative word error rate reduction.

## Key Results
- Successfully retrieves 95% of misrecognized entities from ASR outputs
- Achieves 5% relative word error rate reduction when used for contextual biasing
- Demonstrates effectiveness of record deduplication technique from entity resolution for ASR entity modeling

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method leverages record deduplication techniques from entity resolution to cluster references to the same entity, including misrecognitions, from ASR outputs. By modeling the true entity distribution through clustering, the approach can better identify and correct misrecognized entities during runtime ASR processing, leading to improved contextual biasing and reduced word error rates.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** ASR Outputs -> Blocking -> Comparison (LR/DT/SVM) -> Clustering -> Entity Distribution -> Contextual Biasing -> Improved ASR

**Critical Path:** The comparison and clustering steps are critical, as they directly impact the quality of the entity distribution model used for contextual biasing.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The choice of comparison model (logistic regression, decision tree, SVM) affects the precision of entity clustering. More complex models may improve accuracy but at the cost of increased computational overhead.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor clustering of references due to noise in ASR outputs or insufficient co-occurrence data
- Incorrect identification of coreferent pairs, leading to false positives or negatives
- Ineffective contextual biasing due to an inaccurate entity distribution model

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on a proprietary in-house RNN-T model, making benchmarking against standard ASR systems difficult
- Lacks detailed hyperparameter configurations for comparison models used in the deduplication framework
- Synthetic voice generation process using Amazon Polly is not fully specified, raising questions about representativeness
- 5% relative WER reduction presented without confidence intervals or statistical significance testing

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The conceptual framework of using record deduplication for entity resolution in ASR contexts is sound and well-established
- **Medium confidence**: The reported 95% retrieval rate for misrecognized entities, as this depends heavily on specific implementation details
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of the 5% relative WER reduction across different ASR systems and domains
