# OMS-DPM: Optimizing the Model Schedule for Diffusion Probabilistic Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08860
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08860
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: OMS-DPM achieves 2× sampling speedup on Stable Diffusion while maintaining generation quality

## Executive Summary
OMS-DPM addresses the challenge of optimizing model schedules in diffusion probabilistic models (DPMs) to improve the trade-off between generation quality and speed. The key insight is that different denoising steps benefit from different model capacities, allowing small models to outperform large models at certain timesteps. The proposed predictor-based search algorithm efficiently explores the exponential space of model schedules to find optimal combinations that achieve better quality-speed trade-offs than single-model approaches.

## Method Summary
OMS-DPM proposes a predictor-based search algorithm to find optimal model schedules for DPMs. Given a model zoo of pre-trained models with varying capacities and a time budget constraint, the method trains a performance predictor that maps model schedules to predicted FID scores. An evolutionary algorithm then uses this predictor to explore the space of model schedules, iteratively refining candidates to find schedules that maximize generation quality within the time budget. The approach leverages the observation that different models excel at different denoising steps, enabling simultaneous improvements in both quality and speed through intelligent model combination.

## Key Results
- Achieves 2× sampling speedup on Stable Diffusion while maintaining generation quality
- Significantly improves the quality-speed trade-off compared to single-model baselines across multiple datasets
- Successfully applies to various datasets including CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet-64, and LSUN-Church

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Different denoising steps in DPMs benefit from different model capacities, enabling quality-speed trade-offs.
- Mechanism: Small models outperform large models at certain timesteps due to better fit to local denoising dynamics.
- Core assumption: Model capacity and denoising task difficulty are inversely correlated at specific timesteps.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we observe that small models, though having worse generation quality when used alone, could outperform large models in certain generation steps"
  - [section 3] "the ranking of denoising ability is not consistent across the whole time axis"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; related work focuses on solver optimization not model scheduling.
- Break condition: If model capacity perfectly correlates with denoising performance across all timesteps.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Predictor-based search can efficiently explore the exponential model schedule space.
- Mechanism: Trained predictor generalizes from sampled schedules to predict FID of unseen combinations.
- Core assumption: FID scores are sufficiently smooth in the model schedule space for generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We design OMS-DPM, a predictor-based search algorithm"
  - [section 4.2.1] "The predictor is trained with a small amount of data and can generalize to unseen model schedules"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related works focus on architecture search but not specifically for DPM model scheduling.
- Break condition: If FID landscape is too noisy or discontinuous for meaningful prediction.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Evolutionary algorithms effectively navigate the model schedule optimization space.
- Mechanism: Iteratively mutates and evaluates model schedules using predictor scores to converge to high-performing solutions.
- Core assumption: The search space has exploitable structure rather than being purely random.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2.1] "we employ an evolutionary algorithm to quickly explore the space"
  - [section 5] "a simple evolutionary algorithm (Real et al., 2019) is sufficient for providing significant improvement"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; assumes effectiveness of evolutionary methods in this specific context.
- Break condition: If evolutionary search gets trapped in local optima without finding better schedules.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Model zoo -> Predictor -> Search algorithm -> Sampler

- Critical path:
  1. Prepare schedule-FID training data by sampling random schedules
  2. Train predictor on schedule-FID pairs
  3. Run evolutionary search with predictor to find optimal schedule
  4. Execute sampling using discovered schedule

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model zoo size vs search space complexity
  - Predictor training data quantity vs prediction accuracy
  - Search algorithm iterations vs computational cost
  - Sampler choice (DDIM vs DPM-Solver) affecting discretization

- Failure signatures:
  - Predictor fails to generalize (high Kendall's Tau on validation data)
  - Search converges to poor schedules (similar to random schedules)
  - Discovered schedules violate time budget constraint
  - Quality degradation when using null models in schedule

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train predictor with small dataset and validate on held-out schedules
  2. Run evolutionary search with synthetic data to verify convergence
  3. Test discovered schedule on CIFAR-10 with DDIM sampler to validate improvement over single-model baseline

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the underlying reason for the observed phenomenon where smaller models can outperform larger models at certain denoising steps in diffusion probabilistic models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly mentions this observation in Section 3 and Fig. 2, noting that the ranking of denoising ability is not consistent across the whole time axis.
- Why unresolved: While the paper observes this phenomenon, it does not provide a theoretical explanation for why smaller models might perform better at specific steps.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis or empirical study that identifies the specific characteristics of denoising steps where smaller models excel, and explains why these steps favor smaller models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of model schedule affect the convergence properties of diffusion probabilistic models, particularly in terms of generation quality as the number of function evaluations increases?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the trade-off between generation quality and speed, and shows that OMS-DPM can lower the converging FID of DPM-Solver. However, it does not deeply explore how different model schedules affect convergence properties.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on optimizing the model schedule for a given budget but does not extensively investigate how different schedules impact the long-term convergence behavior of the model.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the convergence curves of different model schedules, analyzing how the choice of schedule affects the rate of improvement in generation quality as more function evaluations are used.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the insights gained from optimizing model schedules in diffusion probabilistic models be generalized to other generative models or machine learning tasks that involve a sequence of operations or stages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes by suggesting that the idea of combining off-the-shelf models to improve performance-efficiency trade-offs could support a wider range of applications beyond DPMs.
- Why unresolved: While the paper hints at broader applications, it does not provide concrete examples or experiments demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach in other domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful application of the model schedule optimization approach to other generative models (e.g., GANs, VAEs) or sequential decision-making tasks, showing improved performance-efficiency trade-offs compared to using a single model.

## Limitations
- Computational overhead of evolutionary search may be substantial for larger model zoos
- Method effectiveness depends on having access to multiple pre-trained models of varying capacities
- Predictor's assumption of smooth FID landscape may not hold for all dataset characteristics

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core observation that different models perform differently at different timesteps is well-supported by experimental evidence across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet-64, LSUN-Church).
- **Medium Confidence**: The predictor-based search algorithm's effectiveness is demonstrated but could benefit from additional ablation studies on predictor architecture choices and training data size requirements.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim of 2× speedup on Stable Diffusion is promising but based on a single experiment with limited details on the specific Stable Diffusion variant used.
