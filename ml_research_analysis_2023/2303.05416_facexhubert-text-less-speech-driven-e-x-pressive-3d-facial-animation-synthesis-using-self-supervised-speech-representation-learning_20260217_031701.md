# FaceXHuBERT: Text-less Speech-driven E(X)pressive 3D Facial Animation Synthesis Using Self-Supervised Speech Representation Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05416
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05416
- Reference count: 40
- 22% improvement in perceptual realism over state-of-the-art on BIWI dataset

## Executive Summary
FaceXHuBERT is a text-less speech-driven 3D facial animation system that uses self-supervised speech representation learning to capture personalized and subtle facial motion cues. The approach leverages the pretrained HuBERT model to extract both lexical and non-lexical audio information, combined with a GRU-based decoder and binary emotion conditions. The method achieves realistic lip-syncing and emotional expressiveness while being computationally efficient and robust to background noise.

## Method Summary
The FaceXHuBERT method uses a pretrained HuBERT encoder to extract self-supervised speech representations from audio input. These representations are combined with speaker identity embeddings and binary emotion conditions (happy/sad) before being processed by a GRU-based decoder to generate 3D facial animation parameters. The system operates in a text-less manner, eliminating the need for transcription and enabling more natural capture of prosodic and emotional features. The architecture is designed to distinguish subtle facial motions through the integration of multiple conditioning signals while maintaining computational efficiency compared to transformer-based alternatives.

## Key Results
- Achieves 4.80 mm mean face vertex error on BIWI dataset
- Outperforms state-of-the-art by 22% in perceptual realism (78% preference rate)
- 4× faster training compared to transformer-based approaches
- Maintains performance under various audio conditions and background noise

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system captures expressive facial animation by leveraging HuBERT's ability to encode both lexical content and paralinguistic features from speech. The self-supervised pretraining on large speech datasets allows the model to learn rich representations that include emotional and speaker-specific characteristics. By conditioning on both speaker identity and emotion states, the model can disambiguate between similar phonetic content that may require different facial expressions. The GRU decoder efficiently processes these multi-modal inputs to generate temporally coherent facial animations that align with both the speech content and emotional intent.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Audio -> HuBERT Encoder -> Feature Concatenation -> GRU Decoder -> 3D Face Parameters

**Critical path**: The most important processing flow is Audio → HuBERT → [Speaker Embedding + Emotion Condition] → GRU → Animation Output. This path transforms raw speech into expressive facial animations while incorporating personalization and emotional context.

**Design tradeoffs**: The system trades off the expressiveness of transformer architectures for the computational efficiency of GRUs, achieving 4× faster training. It uses binary emotion conditions rather than continuous emotion representations to simplify the learning task while maintaining expressiveness. The text-less approach eliminates transcription requirements but may miss some lexical-level synchronization cues.

**Failure signatures**: Poor lip-sync accuracy when speech contains non-standard pronunciations or accents not well-represented in the HuBERT pretraining data. Emotional expressions may appear generic or mismatched when the binary emotion condition doesn't capture nuanced emotional states. Background noise may degrade performance if it significantly corrupts the HuBERT feature extraction process.

## Limitations
- Performance evaluation limited to BIWI dataset, which may not represent diverse speech patterns and accents
- Binary emotion conditions oversimplify the continuous nature of emotional expression
- HuBERT pretraining on LibriSpeech may not capture conversational speech nuances
- Limited validation of noise robustness across diverse noise types and SNR conditions
