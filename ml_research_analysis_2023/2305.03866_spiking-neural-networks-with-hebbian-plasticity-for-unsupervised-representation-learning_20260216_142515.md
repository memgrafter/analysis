# Spiking neural networks with Hebbian plasticity for unsupervised representation learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03866
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03866
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: Achieved 97.7% accuracy on MNIST and 83.8% on F-MNIST using spiking BCPNN with Hebbian plasticity

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a spiking neural network model that transforms the non-spiking Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN) with Hebbian plasticity into a spiking variant. The key innovation is interpreting spikes as Poisson-distributed samples from underlying firing rates computed via BCPNN probabilities, enabling online correlation-based Hebbian-Bayesian learning and rewiring. The model achieves sparse firing rates (~50 spikes/s) matching cortical pyramidal neurons while maintaining competitive classification accuracy on standard benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The method converts a non-spiking BCPNN with Hebbian-Bayesian learning into a spiking network through Poisson spike generation from activation probabilities. The architecture consists of spiking neurons organized in hypercolumns with minicolumns, using short-term Z-traces for coincidence detection and long-term P-traces for synaptic updates. Structural rewiring adapts connectivity based on usage scores derived from P-traces. The model is trained unsupervised on MNIST and F-MNIST with 10 epochs, using pattern presentation times of 200ms and gaps of 100ms, achieving competitive accuracy while maintaining biologically realistic firing rates.

## Key Results
- Achieved 97.7% accuracy on MNIST and 83.8% on F-MNIST benchmarks
- Maintained sparse firing rates (~50 spikes/s) matching cortical pyramidal neurons
- Short-term filtering (τz ~50ms) was crucial for reliable learning in spiking regime
- Performance approaches that of non-spiking BCPNN and matches other Hebbian-based spiking networks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Poisson spike generation preserves the underlying firing rate statistics of the non-spiking BCPNN model while achieving biologically realistic firing rates.
- Mechanism: Spikes are sampled independently from a Poisson distribution where the probability of a spike in each time step is proportional to the BCPNN activation probability. This creates sparse, irregular firing patterns matching cortical pyramidal neurons.
- Core assumption: The Poisson sampling process accurately approximates the underlying rate-based activation when the maximum firing rate is set appropriately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We interpret spikes as stochastic independent samples from a Poisson distribution, where the underlying firing rates are computed as probabilities from the BCPNN model."
  - [section]: "Our Poisson spike generation mechanism is simpler than integrate-and-fire models, but it still recapitulates in vivo irregular cortical pyramidal spiking patterns with realistic firing rates."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers discussing Poisson sampling for BCPNN conversion.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Short-term filtering with Z-traces enables reliable Hebbian learning despite irregular spike timing.
- Mechanism: Z-traces accumulate pre- and post-synaptic activity over a short time window (τz ~50ms), providing a coincidence detection mechanism that bridges the gap between irregular spike timing and the need for temporal correlation in Hebbian learning.
- Core assumption: The time constant τz must be long enough to capture meaningful spike coincidences but short enough to avoid cross-pattern interference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We found that short-term filtering (Z-traces) was crucial for this process. The time constants we found to work best (τz ~50ms) roughly match the dendritic depolarization time constant."
  - [section]: "For biologically realistic values of fmax, like 50 spikes/s, performance with τz ≤ 10ms is very low... However, for τz ~50ms, the performance closely approximates the non-spiking model."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers discussing Z-trace filtering for BCPNN spiking conversion.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The Hebbian-Bayesian learning framework preserves representation learning capability when converted to spiking.
- Mechanism: The same correlation-based Hebbian-Bayesian synaptic plasticity that works in the non-spiking BCPNN model continues to function in the spiking version, with P-traces accumulating evidence from the Z-traces to update weights and biases.
- Core assumption: The probabilistic interpretation of neural activity remains valid in the spiking regime, allowing the same learning rules to apply.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We achieved this by transforming the non-spiking feedforward Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN) model, employing an online correlation-based Hebbian-Bayesian learning and rewiring mechanism... into a spiking neural network."
  - [section]: "The learning mechanism is local, online, and correlation-based Hebbian-Bayesian synaptic plasticity where each synapse accumulates short and long-term traces of pre-, post-, and joint activities."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus papers discussing Hebbian-Bayesian learning preservation in spiking conversion.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input layer (spiking neurons) -> Z-trace filtering -> Neuronal activation -> Poisson spike generation -> Hidden layer (spiking neurons with minicolumns) -> Z-trace accumulation -> P-trace updates -> Weight/bias updates -> Structural rewiring

- Critical path:
  1. Pattern presentation → spike generation in input layer
  2. Synaptic integration with Z-trace filtering → neuronal activation
  3. Poisson spike generation from activation probabilities
  4. Z-trace accumulation from spike coincidences
  5. P-trace updates from Z-traces
  6. Weight and bias parameter updates
  7. Rewiring mechanism updates connectivity matrix

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Firing rate vs. spike irregularity: Lower maximum firing rates produce more realistic spike statistics but may require longer presentation times for reliable learning.
  - Time constant τz vs. learning reliability: Longer τz improves coincidence detection but increases risk of cross-pattern interference.
  - Sparse connectivity vs. representational capacity: Higher connectivity allows more complex representations but increases computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor classification accuracy with τz < 10ms or τz > 100ms
  - Unrealistic firing rates (>100 spikes/s) indicating incorrect scaling
  - Lack of learning with completely random initial connectivity
  - Overfitting with too high connectivity percentage

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify Poisson spike statistics: Record spike trains from a single neuron during repeated presentations of the same pattern and analyze inter-spike interval distribution.
  2. Test Z-trace filtering: Train with different τz values (1ms, 10ms, 50ms, 100ms) and measure classification accuracy on MNIST.
  3. Validate rewiring mechanism: Visualize receptive fields before and after training to confirm that minicolumns learn meaningful feature detectors.

## Limitations
- The model's competitive performance relative to other SNN approaches relies heavily on the specific BCPNN conversion methodology, but direct comparisons with non-BCPNN spiking models are limited in the corpus.
- While the Poisson sampling approach is claimed to produce biologically realistic firing rates, the evidence is primarily from the authors' own analysis rather than independent validation.
- The structural rewiring mechanism's contribution to performance is demonstrated through ablation, but the exact mechanisms of how sparse connectivity emerges during learning remain underspecified.

## Confidence
- **High**: The model achieves the reported classification accuracies on MNIST (97.7%) and F-MNIST (83.8%), as these are directly measurable outcomes with clear evaluation protocols.
- **Medium**: The claim that short-term filtering (τz ~50ms) is crucial for learning in the spiking regime is supported by systematic parameter sweeps showing performance degradation outside this range.
- **Medium**: The assertion that the model maintains biologically realistic firing rates (~50 spikes/s) is supported by analysis, though the connection to cortical pyramidal neuron data is indirect.

## Next Checks
1. **Reproduce the parameter sweep**: Replicate the systematic variation of τz values (1ms, 10ms, 50ms, 100ms) to independently verify that performance peaks around 50ms and degrades at extremes.
2. **Analyze spike statistics independently**: Record spike trains from trained neurons and perform independent analysis of inter-spike interval distributions to confirm Poisson-like statistics and ~50 spikes/s average rate.
3. **Compare with alternative SNN baselines**: Implement and train a non-BCPNN spiking network (e.g., with STDP or calcium-based plasticity) on the same datasets to establish whether the BCPNN conversion provides unique advantages.
