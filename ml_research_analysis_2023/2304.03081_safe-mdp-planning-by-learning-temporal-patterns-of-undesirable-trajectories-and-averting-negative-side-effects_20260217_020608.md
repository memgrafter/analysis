# Safe MDP Planning by Learning Temporal Patterns of Undesirable Trajectories and Averting Negative Side Effects

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03081
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03081
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: A method that learns non-Markovian safety patterns from trajectory labels via RNN classification and integrates with MDP planning using Lagrange multipliers, achieving superior performance on both Markovian and non-Markovian negative side effects compared to prior methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses safe MDP planning in the presence of negative side effects (NSEs) that arise from incomplete environment models. The key innovation is learning non-Markovian safety patterns from trajectory-level categorical labels using a recurrent neural network, rather than assuming known Markovian cost functions. The authors integrate this classifier with a model-based gradient estimation method using a Lagrange multiplier framework to jointly optimize rewards and safety constraints. Empirical results on discrete and continuous domains show the approach satisfies complex safety constraints while maximizing returns, outperforming previous methods on both Markovian and non-Markovian NSEs.

## Method Summary
The method learns non-Markovian safety patterns from trajectory-level categorical labels using a recurrent neural network (RNN), then integrates this classifier with a model-based gradient estimation method using a Lagrange multiplier framework to jointly optimize rewards and safety constraints. The approach requires trajectory data labeled with NSE categories, which is used to train an RNN classifier (GRU for continuous tasks, MLP for discrete). This classifier is then incorporated into a Lagrangian relaxation framework that handles the trade-off between reward maximization and safety constraint satisfaction. The method uses model-based gradient estimation via the reparameterization trick to provide lower-variance updates compared to model-free approaches.

## Key Results
- The proposed method satisfies complex safety constraints while maximizing returns on both discrete and continuous domains
- Outperforms previous methods on both Markovian and non-Markovian negative side effects
- Model-based gradient estimation shows significantly better performance than model-free alternatives
- The approach is highly scalable and modular, enabling continual safety learning as more NSE data is collected

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Trajectory-Level Safety Classification
Learning non-Markovian safety patterns via RNN trajectory classification allows capturing safety constraints that depend on entire sequences rather than just immediate state-action pairs. The RNN processes entire trajectories and outputs a probability of belonging to each NSE category, enabling detection of complex safety patterns missed by Markovian cost functions. This assumes NSEs can be effectively modeled as trajectory-level patterns that are learnable from labeled trajectory data.

### Mechanism 2: Lagrange Multiplier Integration
Integrating the learned safety model with MDP planning via Lagrange multipliers enables joint optimization of rewards and safety constraints within a single computation graph. The Lagrange multiplier method incorporates both the reward gradient and the safety constraint gradient (derived from the RNN classifier) into a unified optimization framework. This assumes the safety constraint gradient can be effectively estimated and used alongside the reward gradient for policy optimization.

### Mechanism 3: Model-Based Gradient Estimation
Model-based gradient estimation using the reparameterization trick provides lower variance and more targeted updates compared to model-free methods. By reparameterizing both the policy and transition model, the method computes pathwise derivatives of the classifier output with respect to policy parameters, enabling precise gradient-based updates. This assumes the classifier is differentiable and the MDP model is known, allowing effective use of the reparameterization trick.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MDP Environment -> Policy Network -> Trajectory Classifier (GRU) -> Lagrange Multiplier System -> Gradient Estimation Module

- Critical path:
  1. Collect trajectory data from initial policy
  2. Label trajectories with NSE categories
  3. Train GRU classifier on labeled data
  4. Use classifier within Lagrange multiplier framework
  5. Compute gradients via model-based estimation
  6. Update policy parameters

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model-based vs model-free gradient estimation: Model-based provides lower variance but requires known MDP model
  - RNN complexity vs accuracy: More complex RNNs may capture better patterns but increase training time
  - Lagrange multiplier learning rate: Too fast causes instability, too slow prevents constraint satisfaction

- Failure signatures:
  - High NSE rates despite training: Classifier not capturing true safety patterns
  - Policy collapse: Lagrange multiplier updates causing instability
  - Slow convergence: Model-based gradients not providing sufficient signal

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train classifier on synthetic trajectory data with known NSE patterns
  2. Run baseline PPO without safety constraints to generate initial trajectory data
  3. Test model-based gradient estimation on a simple CMDP with known analytical gradients

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper doesn't explicitly call out open questions, but several important questions emerge from the work:

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed method handle the trade-off between reward optimization and safety constraints when safety data is limited or noisy?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the method learns from labeled trajectory data, but doesn't discuss the impact of limited or noisy data on performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating the method's effectiveness with sufficient labeled data but doesn't explore scenarios with limited or noisy safety data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing the method's performance with varying amounts of labeled data and different levels of noise in the safety labels would provide insights into its robustness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the proposed method be extended to handle safety constraints that evolve over time or are context-dependent?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses learning non-Markovian safety patterns but doesn't explicitly address dynamic or context-dependent safety constraints.
- Why unresolved: The current formulation assumes static safety constraints, but real-world scenarios might require handling evolving or context-dependent safety requirements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the method to incorporate dynamic or context-dependent safety constraints and evaluating its performance in such scenarios would demonstrate its applicability to more complex real-world settings.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of RNN architecture (e.g., GRU vs. LSTM) affect the performance of the trajectory classifier and the overall safety performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper uses GRU networks for trajectory classification but doesn't compare its performance with other RNN architectures like LSTM.
- Why unresolved: While GRU is chosen for its balance between accuracy and network size, the impact of different RNN architectures on the method's performance remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the performance of the proposed method using different RNN architectures (e.g., GRU vs. LSTM) would provide insights into the importance of the choice of RNN for trajectory classification and safety performance.

## Limitations
- Assumes known MDP models, limiting applicability to real-world scenarios where transition dynamics are unknown
- Trajectory-level labeling requirement may be impractical in many domains, creating a data collection bottleneck
- Limited empirical validation across diverse environments, with most experiments on synthetic domains
- No ablation studies comparing RNN vs simpler classifiers, leaving the necessity of sequence modeling unclear

## Confidence
- **High**: The Lagrange multiplier framework for integrating safety constraints is theoretically sound and well-established
- **Medium**: RNN-based trajectory classification can capture non-Markovian safety patterns, though empirical validation is limited
- **Medium**: Model-based gradient estimation provides lower variance than model-free approaches, but requires accurate MDP models
