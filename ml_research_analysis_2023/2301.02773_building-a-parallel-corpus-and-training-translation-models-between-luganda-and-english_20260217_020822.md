# Building a Parallel Corpus and Training Translation Models Between Luganda and English

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02773
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02773
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: First NMT system for Luganda-English with BLEU scores of 21.28 (Luganda→English) and 17.47 (English→Luganda)

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of building machine translation systems for low-resource languages by developing the first neural machine translation (NMT) system for Luganda and English. The authors constructed a bilingual parallel corpus of 41,070 sentence pairs by combining three open-source datasets and trained Transformer models with hyperparameter optimization. The resulting system achieved BLEU scores of 21.28 for Luganda-to-English and 17.47 for English-to-Luganda translations, demonstrating the viability of NMT approaches for this language pair. The trained models and dataset will be publicly released to support further research and development.

## Method Summary
The authors developed a bilingual parallel corpus for Luganda-English by combining three open-source datasets to create 41,070 pairwise sentences. They applied the Transformer model architecture with hyperparameter optimization to train translation models in both directions. The experimental setup focused on addressing the data scarcity challenge typical of low-resource language pairs, using standard NMT training procedures with careful parameter tuning to maximize performance given the limited training data available.

## Key Results
- Achieved BLEU score of 21.28 for Luganda-to-English translation
- Achieved BLEU score of 17.47 for English-to-Luganda translation
- First published NMT system for the Luganda-English language pair
- Successfully built and released a parallel corpus of 41,070 sentence pairs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Transformer model's self-attention mechanism effectively captures long-range dependencies in text, which is crucial for handling the grammatical and syntactic differences between Luganda and English. The combination of multiple open-source datasets helped overcome the data scarcity challenge typical of low-resource languages. Hyperparameter optimization ensured optimal model configuration for the specific characteristics of the Luganda-English language pair, maximizing translation quality within the constraints of limited training data.
