# STREAMLINE: Streaming Active Learning for Realistic Multi-Distributional Settings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10643
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10643
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: STREAMLINE improves rare slice accuracy by up to 5% for image classification and up to 8% mAP for object detection in multi-distributional streaming settings

## Executive Summary
STREAMLINE is a streaming active learning framework designed to address scenario-driven slice imbalance in multi-distributional settings. The framework targets rare but critical slices in streaming data (such as foggy weather conditions in autonomous driving) by intelligently allocating labeling budget and selecting diverse, novel instances. Using submodular information measures, STREAMLINE implements a three-step process: slice identification, slice-aware budgeting, and data selection. The framework was evaluated across five real-world datasets for both image classification and object detection tasks, demonstrating significant improvements in rare slice performance compared to baseline methods.

## Method Summary
STREAMLINE operates on episodic streaming data where each episode contains unlabeled data from potentially different slice categories. The framework employs a three-step approach: first, it identifies the slice category of incoming data using normalized submodular mutual information measures; second, it allocates labeling budget intelligently, providing more budget to rare slices while being conservative with common ones; and third, it selects diverse and novel instances from the unlabeled stream using submodular conditional gain. The method uses Facility Location Variant Mutual Information (FLQMI) for slice identification and Facility Location Conditional Gain (FLCG) for instance selection, with similarity kernels adapted from cosine similarity for classification and a specialized reduction approach for object detection.

## Key Results
- STREAMLINE improved rare slice accuracy by up to 5% for image classification tasks compared to baseline methods
- For object detection, STREAMLINE achieved up to 8% improvement in mAP on rare slices
- The framework demonstrated consistent performance improvements across five real-world datasets (iWildCam, PovertyMap, BDD100K, Cityscapes, and KITTI)
- STREAMLINE showed superior labeling efficiency compared to random sampling and traditional active learning baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The three-step process (slice identification, slice-aware budgeting, and data selection) enables STREAMLINE to effectively address scenario-driven slice imbalance in streaming data.
- **Mechanism:** By first identifying the slice using submodular mutual information measures, STREAMLINE can allocate more labeling budget to rare slices and use submodular conditional gain to select diverse, novel instances for the identified slice.
- **Core assumption:** The incoming data stream can be reliably categorized into distinct slices based on semantic similarity, and these slices have varying frequencies of occurrence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "STREAMLINE's key idea is a three-step process: 1) Slice identification using submodular mutual information measures, 2) Slice-aware budgeting that allocates more labeling budget to rare slices, and 3) Data selection using submodular conditional gain to choose diverse, novel instances for the identified slice."
  - [section] "The first step identiﬁes the slice category utilizing a normalized submodular mutual information measure [10]. The second step distributes the labeling budget in an intelligent manner to target rare slices while being more relaxed on common slices. The third step selects a subset from the unlabeled stream that is semantically dissimilar to the identiﬁed slice’s existing labeled data by maximizing the submodular conditional gain [10]"
- **Break condition:** The mechanism breaks down if the data stream cannot be reliably categorized into distinct slices, or if the semantic similarity measures used for identification are not effective.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The use of submodular information measures allows for efficient and effective selection of representative instances from the unlabeled stream.
- **Mechanism:** Submodular functions, such as Facility Location Variant Mutual Information (FLQMI) and Facility Location Conditional Gain (FLCG), are used to measure semantic similarity and select diverse, novel instances.
- **Core assumption:** Submodular functions can effectively model the diminishing returns property of information gain, making them suitable for selecting representative instances.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Using submodular information measures, we propose STREAMLINE, a novel streaming active learning framework that mitigates scenario-driven slice imbalance in the working labeled data via a three-step procedure of slice identification, slice-aware budgeting, and data selection."
  - [section] "We draw upon the modeling power of submodular functions and the recently proposed submodular information measures [9]."
- **Break condition:** The mechanism breaks down if the submodular functions do not accurately model the information gain, or if the similarity kernels used in these functions are not effective.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The slice-aware budgeting scheme allows STREAMLINE to effectively target rare slices by allocating more budget to them.
- **Mechanism:** The budgeting scheme calculates the selection budget by incorporating a base selection budget and a saved budget parameter that accumulates excess budget when encountering common slices and dissipates this excess when encountering rare slices.
- **Core assumption:** Rare slices require more attention in the labeling process to ensure robust model performance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "STREAMLINE's key idea is a three-step process: 1) Slice identification using submodular mutual information measures, 2) Slice-aware budgeting that allocates more labeling budget to rare slices, and 3) Data selection using submodular conditional gain to choose diverse, novel instances for the identified slice."
  - [section] "While traditional pool-based methods select a ﬁxed amount of unlabeled data to label (for example, BADGE [4]), this aspect of these strategies does not translate effectively to the episodic streaming setting studied here, where AL methods can beneﬁt greatly on rare slices by apportioning their budget according to the type of data arriving in U."
- **Break condition:** The mechanism breaks down if the budgeting scheme does not accurately reflect the importance of rare slices, or if the accumulation and dissipation of the saved budget parameter is not effective.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Slice Identification -> Slice-Aware Budgeting -> Data Selection
- **Critical path:** The critical path in STREAMLINE is the three-step process: slice identification, slice-aware budgeting, and data selection. Each step depends on the successful completion of the previous step.
- **Design tradeoffs:** STREAMLINE trades off the complexity of managing multiple components and submodular functions for improved performance on rare slices. The use of submodular functions also introduces additional computational overhead compared to simpler active learning methods.
- **Failure signatures:** Failure in STREAMLINE could manifest as poor performance on rare slices, misattribution of data to incorrect slices, or inefficient use of the labeling budget. These failures could be caused by issues with the submodular functions, the slice identification process, or the budgeting scheme.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Experiment 1:** Test the slice identification component in isolation using a dataset with known slice categories. Measure the accuracy of slice attribution.
  2. **Experiment 2:** Test the slice-aware budgeting component in isolation using a simulated data stream with known slice frequencies. Measure the effectiveness of budget allocation to rare slices.
  3. **Experiment 3:** Test the data selection component in isolation using a dataset with known representative instances. Measure the diversity and novelty of the selected instances.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results or theoretical analysis of STREAMLINE's performance in non-episodic streaming settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing STREAMLINE's performance on episodic vs. non-episodic streams, or theoretical analysis of how the identification and selection mechanisms behave with mixed-slice data.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using slice summaries (via submodular optimization) on STREAMLINE's scalability and performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that slice summaries could be used to reduce the dependency on labeled slice sizes, but notes this would reduce the information available for slice identification and selection, potentially affecting reliability.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not implement or evaluate this approach, leaving the trade-off between scalability and performance unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing STREAMLINE with and without slice summaries, measuring both scalability improvements and any degradation in identification/selection accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does STREAMLINE handle the emergence of new, previously unseen slices in the data stream?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that STREAMLINE assumes slice categories are known a priori and suggests adding a similarity threshold to create new slices, but does not implement or evaluate this modification.
- Why unresolved: The proposed modification for handling new slices is theoretical and untested in the paper.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments introducing new slices during STREAMLINE's operation to test the proposed similarity threshold approach, or analysis of how the current implementation fails when encountering unknown slices.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the effect of different minimum budget fractions (ρ) on STREAMLINE's performance across various datasets and tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses different ρ values for different datasets (0.825 for PovertyMap, 0.5 for iWildCam and object detection tasks) but does not provide a systematic analysis of how varying ρ affects performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the sensitivity of STREAMLINE to the choice of ρ parameter or provide guidance on selecting optimal values.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying ρ across a range of values for each dataset, showing how rare slice accuracy and overall performance change with different budget allocation strategies.

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of slice identification, which may be challenging in scenarios with ambiguous or overlapping slice categories
- Computational overhead from submodular function evaluations may limit scalability to very large streaming datasets
- The budgeting scheme assumes known or estimable slice frequencies, which may not hold in truly open-world settings

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The three-step framework design and its effectiveness in addressing slice imbalance (supported by multiple experimental results across different datasets and tasks)
- **Medium confidence**: The scalability claims, as computational costs for submodular functions on large datasets are not extensively evaluated
- **Medium confidence**: The robustness across different slice granularity levels, as the paper focuses primarily on predefined slice categories
