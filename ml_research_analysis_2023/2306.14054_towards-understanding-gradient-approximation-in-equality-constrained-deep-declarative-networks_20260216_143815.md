# Towards Understanding Gradient Approximation in Equality Constrained Deep Declarative Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14054
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14054
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 23
- This paper analyzes when approximate gradients can be used for equality-constrained deep declarative networks, providing conditions under which ignoring constraint terms yields descent directions for the global loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates gradient approximation techniques for equality-constrained deep declarative networks, where the backward pass typically requires solving a linear system involving the Hessian of the declarative node. The authors develop theoretical conditions under which ignoring constraint terms in this backward pass still yields descent directions for the global loss. They prove that for linear equality constraints with full-rank constraint matrices, the approximate gradient is a descent direction in expectation, while for normalization constraints, the approximation works when the Lagrange multiplier is smaller than the smallest Hessian eigenvalue. Experimental validation on three problems confirms these theoretical insights, showing that approximation works well on average for linear constraints but has more nuanced behavior for normalization constraints.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes deep declarative networks with equality constraints where the backward pass typically requires solving a linear system Ah = -v involving the Hessian H of the declarative node. The authors propose approximating this by ignoring constraint terms, yielding bA^T h = -v. They analyze when this approximation yields descent directions by computing the expected value of g^T b g (where g is the true gradient and bg is the approximate gradient). For linear equality constraints, they prove E[g^T b g] = m - p ≥ 0 when the constraint matrix A has full rank. For normalization constraints, they show E[g^T b g] ≥ 0 when the Lagrange multiplier λ is smaller than the smallest Hessian eigenvalue. Experiments use a three-layer MLP with ReLU activation, AdamW optimizer (learning rate 1e-3), and 500 iterations on three problems: Euclidean projection onto L2-sphere, optimal transport, and eigen decomposition.

## Key Results
- For linear equality constrained problems, the approximate gradient is a descent direction in expectation when the constraint matrix A has full rank
- For normalization constraints, the approximation is a descent direction when λ < λ_1 (smallest Hessian eigenvalue)
- Experimental results confirm approximation works well on average for linear constraints but has more nuanced behavior for normalization constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: For linear equality constraints, ignoring constraint terms yields a descent direction on average when the constraint matrix has full rank.
- Mechanism: The approximate gradient becomes -v^T H^(-1), which is a descent direction when g^T b g ≥ 0. For linear constraints, E[g^T b g] = m - p ≥ 0.
- Core assumption: Hessian H is non-singular and incoming gradient v is isotropically distributed.
- Break condition: When H has high condition number or constraints are nearly linearly dependent.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: For normalization constraints, approximation is a descent direction when λ < λ_1 (smallest Hessian eigenvalue).
- Mechanism: E[g^T b g] contains term involving λ_i - λ. When λ < λ_1, this term is positive, making expected value non-negative.
- Core assumption: Hessian is positive definite and gradient distribution remains isotropic.
- Break condition: When λ exceeds the smallest eigenvalue.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Approximation works particularly well during early training stages due to more isotropic gradient distributions.
- Mechanism: Early training produces more uniformly distributed gradient directions across parameter space, satisfying isotropy assumption.
- Core assumption: Training dynamics naturally produce isotropic gradients early in optimization.
- Break condition: As training progresses and gradients become more aligned with descent direction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can theoretical bounds for gradient approximation be extended to problems with non-linear constraints beyond normalization?
- Basis in paper: Analysis for other non-linear constraints "is not as straightforward" and leaves open possibility of extending theory
- Why unresolved: Paper provides only partial analysis for normalization constraints without developing general framework
- What evidence would resolve it: Unified theoretical framework characterizing when approximation works for arbitrary non-linear constraints

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does effectiveness of gradient approximation vary with different optimization problem formulations and loss functions?
- Basis in paper: Shows approximation works well early in training but less well later, and differently for different problems
- Why unresolved: Only tests few specific problem formulations without systematic study
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive empirical study across diverse optimization problems and loss functions

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can computational efficiency gains of gradient approximation be quantified and optimized for practical deep learning applications?
- Basis in paper: States approximation is "computationally much more efficient" but does not quantify speedup
- Why unresolved: No timing experiments or systematic comparison of computational costs
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical benchmarks showing speed-accuracy tradeoffs and optimization techniques

### Open Question 4
- Question: How can gradient approximation be improved for eigen decomposition problems to work reliably beyond special case of targeting largest eigenvalue with all others negative?
- Basis in paper: Shows approximation "rarely a descent direction" for general eigen decomposition cases
- Why unresolved: Identifies limitation but does not propose solutions or modified approximation schemes
- What evidence would resolve it: New approximation methods extending reliable performance to general eigen decomposition scenarios

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis relies on idealized assumptions about gradient distributions being isotropic
- Approximation can produce non-descent directions in certain cases, particularly for normalization constraints
- Empirical validation limited to small-scale problems with specific architectures

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Theoretical framework for linear equality constraints and proof of descent directions in expectation
- **Medium confidence**: Experimental results on three test problems, though small scale suggests caution
- **Low confidence**: Claim about early training dynamics being favorable for approximation
