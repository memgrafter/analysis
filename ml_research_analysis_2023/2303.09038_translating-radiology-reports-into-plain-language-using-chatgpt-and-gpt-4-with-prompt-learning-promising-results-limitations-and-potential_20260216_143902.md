# Translating Radiology Reports into Plain Language using ChatGPT and GPT-4 with Prompt Learning: Promising Results, Limitations, and Potential

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09038
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09038
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: ChatGPT achieved 4.1/5 quality score in translating radiology reports to plain language with minimal information loss

## Executive Summary
This study evaluated ChatGPT's ability to translate complex radiology reports into plain language for patient education. Using 62 chest CT and 76 brain MRI reports, the model achieved an average quality score of 4.1/5 with only 0.07 information points missing and 0.11 points misinterpreted per report. The translated reports were typically 20-30% shorter than originals while maintaining clarity. About 37% of responses included specific patient recommendations based on report findings. Results varied significantly with different prompts, but performance improved with detailed instructions. Comparison with GPT-4 showed significantly better quality. The study demonstrates ChatGPT's potential for clinical education while highlighting the need for prompt optimization and further validation.

## Method Summary
The study used 62 chest CT and 76 brain MRI reports from single institutions, translated using ChatGPT with various prompt formulations. Quality was assessed by radiologists who evaluated completeness, accuracy, and clarity of translations. Translations were compared to original reports for length and content retention. GPT-4 was used as a comparison model. The study focused on prompt engineering to optimize translation quality, testing different instruction styles and detail levels.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieved 4.1/5 average quality score with minimal information loss (0.07 points missing, 0.11 misinterpreted per report)
- Translated reports were 20-30% shorter than originals while maintaining clarity
- 37% of translations included specific patient recommendations
- GPT-4 showed significantly better performance than ChatGPT

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ChatGPT's transformer architecture enables effective pattern recognition in medical language, allowing it to identify key clinical findings and translate them into accessible terminology. The model's training on diverse medical literature provides contextual understanding of radiological terminology, while its attention mechanisms help preserve critical information relationships during translation. GPT-4's enhanced architecture likely contributes to its superior performance through improved context understanding and more sophisticated language generation capabilities.

## Foundational Learning
- Prompt engineering optimization - why needed: Translation quality varied significantly with different prompts, requiring systematic optimization
- Quick check: Test multiple prompt variations to identify optimal formulations

- Radiology report structure understanding - why needed: Models must recognize and preserve critical clinical information while simplifying language
- Quick check: Verify preservation of key findings across multiple report sections

- Patient communication standards - why needed: Ensure translations meet clinical communication requirements and avoid liability
- Quick check: Validate translations against established patient education guidelines

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Radiology reports -> ChatGPT/GPT-4 -> Plain language translations -> Radiologist quality assessment

Critical path: Input report → Prompt processing → Content extraction → Language simplification → Quality validation

Design tradeoffs: Model sophistication vs. prompt engineering effort; translation accuracy vs. readability; automation vs. clinical oversight

Failure signatures: Missing critical findings, misinterpreting clinical terminology, generating inappropriate patient recommendations

First experiments:
1. Test multiple prompt variations on identical reports to establish baseline performance
2. Compare radiologist vs. patient comprehension of original vs. translated reports
3. Validate translation accuracy across different imaging modalities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can prompt engineering be standardized across different report types and institutions?
- What is the optimal balance between translation accuracy and readability for patient comprehension?
- How should liability be addressed when AI systems include patient recommendations in medical translations?
- What are the long-term effects of AI-translated reports on patient understanding and healthcare outcomes?

## Limitations
- Small sample size (62 chest CT and 76 brain MRI reports) from single institutions limits generalizability
- Reliance on radiologist assessments rather than actual patient comprehension testing
- Performance highly dependent on prompt engineering rather than inherent model capabilities
- 37% inclusion of patient recommendations raises liability concerns requiring clinical validation
- Limited validation across different imaging modalities and clinical contexts

## Confidence
High confidence in ChatGPT's ability to produce patient-friendly translations with good quality scores and minimal information loss

## Next Checks
