# Day-Ahead PV Power Forecasting Based on MSTL-TFT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05911
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05911
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces MSTL-TFT, a novel approach for day-ahead PV power forecasting that combines MSTL decomposition with Temporal Fusion Transformer to achieve superior accuracy over traditional methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel MSTL-TFT approach for day-ahead photovoltaic power forecasting. The method combines Multiple Seasonal-Trend Decomposition using LOESS (MSTL) to extract meaningful temporal patterns from historical PV power generation data with a Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) model to forecast future power output. The approach leverages an attention mechanism to effectively handle temporal dependencies and is evaluated on the Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre dataset, demonstrating significant improvements over traditional decomposition and prediction methods.

## Method Summary
The MSTL-TFT approach consists of two main components: MSTL decomposition and TFT prediction. MSTL is used to decompose historical PV power generation data into multiple seasonal components and trend components, extracting meaningful patterns and seasonality. The decomposed components are then fed into a Temporal Fusion Transformer model, which employs an attention mechanism to effectively capture temporal dependencies and forecast PV power output for the next day. The method is compared against other common decomposition algorithms (VMD, EEMD, VMD-EEMD) and prediction models (BP, LSTM, XGBoost) on the DKASC dataset.

## Key Results
- MSTL-TFT achieves superior performance with significant improvements in NMAE and NRMSE compared to other approaches
- The method demonstrates effectiveness in handling temporal dependencies through its attention mechanism
- Investigation reveals the impact of meteorological data availability and weather conditions on forecasting accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of MSTL-TFT stems from its ability to capture both long-term trends and multiple seasonal patterns in PV power generation data through MSTL decomposition. This decomposition allows the TFT model to focus on learning the temporal dependencies within each component separately, improving its ability to forecast future values. The attention mechanism in TFT enables the model to selectively focus on relevant historical patterns when making predictions, enhancing its accuracy in capturing complex temporal relationships in PV power generation.

   - Quick check: Validate attention distribution aligns with known seasonal patterns in PV data

4. **Decomposition-based Forecasting Approaches**
   - Why needed: Separates complex time series into more manageable components for individual modeling
   - Quick check: Ensure component-wise predictions can be effectively recombined

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
MSTL Decomposition -> TFT Prediction -> Forecast Output

**Critical Path:**
Raw PV data -> MSTL decomposition (multiple seasonal components + trend) -> TFT model (attention-based temporal modeling) -> Day-ahead forecast

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The decomposition-first approach trades computational overhead for potentially better handling of complex seasonal patterns. MSTL's multiple seasonal decomposition capability is particularly suited for PV data with both daily and yearly cycles, though it may be overkill for simpler datasets. The attention mechanism in TFT provides interpretability benefits but increases model complexity compared to simpler RNN-based approaches.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor decomposition quality leading to residual patterns in components
- Attention weights that fail to capture known seasonal patterns
- Performance degradation when meteorological data is incomplete or delayed
- Overfitting to training dataset's specific weather patterns

## Limitations
- Evaluation based on a single dataset (Desert Knowledge Australia Solar Centre), limiting generalizability across diverse geographical locations and climate conditions
- Statistical significance of performance improvements over baselines not demonstrated
- Specific thresholds for meteorological data quality and quantity required for maintaining acceptable forecasting accuracy are not defined
