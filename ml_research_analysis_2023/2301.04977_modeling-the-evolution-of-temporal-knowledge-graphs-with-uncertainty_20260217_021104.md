# Modeling the evolution of temporal knowledge graphs with uncertainty

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04977
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04977
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: WGP-NN achieves state-of-the-art results on temporal knowledge graph link prediction with MRR of 33.16 and 64.97 on ICEWS18 and YAGO datasets respectively

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces WGP-NN, a novel graph neural network architecture that employs weighted Gaussian processes to model the temporal evolution of event probabilities and their uncertainties in temporal knowledge graphs. Unlike existing methods that only express uncertainties in entity representations, WGP-NN captures both the occurrence probability and its time-dependent uncertainty through the mean and variance of Gaussian processes. The model operates in continuous time, handles complex temporal dynamics without parametric assumptions, and naturally models concurrent events through its neighborhood aggregation module.

## Method Summary
WGP-NN consists of three main modules: a neighborhood aggregation module that computes mean embeddings for connected objects at each timestamp, an RNN encoder that processes interaction history into hidden states, and a weighted Gaussian process module that maps these states to continuous-time probability and uncertainty estimates via pseudo-points. The model uses uncertainty-adapted cross-entropy loss with 2nd order approximation and regularization terms to push variance to a target value ν.

## Key Results
- Achieves 33.16 MRR and 35.21 H@3 on ICEWS18 dataset
- Achieves 64.97 MRR and 67.25 H@3 on YAGO dataset
- Outperforms nine baseline models including TComplEx, Know-Evolve, and CyGNet
- Demonstrates superior uncertainty modeling with naturally increasing variance for predictions further from observed data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: WGP-NN achieves superior temporal knowledge graph link prediction by modeling both occurrence probability and uncertainty through Gaussian process variance.
- Mechanism: The model encodes temporal dependencies into pseudo-points via RNN, then uses weighted Gaussian processes to map these logits to continuous-time probability and uncertainty estimates, avoiding discretized time slices.
- Core assumption: Gaussian processes can effectively represent the temporal evolution of event probabilities and their uncertainties in continuous time.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "WGP-NN captures both the occurrence probability and its time-dependent uncertainty through the mean and variance of Gaussian processes."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: WGP-NN models concurrent events natively through its neighborhood aggregation module.
- Mechanism: By aggregating embeddings from all connected objects at each time slice and feeding this into the RNN, the model captures the joint effect of multiple events happening simultaneously.
- Core assumption: Event concurrence can be represented by averaging neighbor embeddings at each timestamp.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "WGP-NN natively models the concurrence of events via its Neighborhood aggregation module."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: WGP-NN satisfies the locality property where uncertainty grows as time moves away from observed data.
- Mechanism: The Gaussian process variance naturally increases for query times far from the pseudo-points' time range, reflecting decreasing confidence in predictions.
- Core assumption: The GP kernel and pseudo-point distribution ensure that variance is lowest near observed times and increases outward.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "the model also satisfies the locality property, namely that the uncertainty outside the observed time horizon is higher, especially for t→∞."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input quadruples (s,p,o,t) -> Neighborhood Aggregation -> RNN Encoder -> Pseudo-point Generator -> Weighted Gaussian Process -> Prediction
- Critical path: Neighborhood Aggregation → RNN → Pseudo-point Generation → Weighted GP → Prediction
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Continuous vs. discretized time: Continuous allows finer predictions but requires GP fitting
  - Number of pseudo-points N: More points increase expressiveness but raise computational cost
  - RNN vs. other sequence models: RNN is simple but may not capture long-range dependencies as well as transformers
- Failure signatures:
  - High variance predictions for all query times may indicate poor pseudo-point distribution
  - Degraded performance on datasets with weak temporal dynamics suggests over-engineering
  - Training instability could arise from improper GP hyperparameter initialization
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Remove uncertainty modeling (fix variance) and compare MRR/H@3
  2. Ablation study: Replace mean aggregation with sum or max aggregation to test concurrent event modeling
  3. Ablation study: Use discretized time slices instead of continuous-time GP and compare performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model's performance degrade when predicting events beyond the observed time horizon, particularly in scenarios with sparse historical data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that WGP-NN "satisfies the locality property, namely that the uncertainty outside the observed time horizon is higher, especially for t→∞."
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions this property theoretically, it doesn't provide quantitative analysis of how uncertainty grows with prediction distance from the last observed timestamp, or how this varies across different dataset densities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing uncertainty (variance) growth curves for predictions at increasing time distances from the last observation, stratified by data density in different regions of the temporal graph.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the computational complexity of WGP-NN during inference, and how does it scale with the number of entities and predicates in large temporal knowledge graphs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that pseudo-points are used "to reduce computational complexity" and discusses hyperparameter tuning, but doesn't provide detailed complexity analysis or scaling experiments.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating state-of-the-art performance but doesn't analyze how the model's computational requirements grow with graph size or how this compares to baseline methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Runtime analysis showing inference time versus graph size, comparison with baseline models' inference speeds, and discussion of memory requirements for large-scale deployments.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is WGP-NN to noise in the temporal knowledge graph data, and how does it handle conflicting information about the same event occurring at different times?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "events occurring at the same timestamp are considered independent from each other given the past observed history" but doesn't address how the model handles contradictory information or data noise.
- Why unresolved: While the model captures uncertainty through Gaussian processes, there's no discussion of how it handles conflicting observations or what happens when the same event is reported at different timestamps.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments introducing controlled noise into datasets, analysis of model performance degradation with noise levels, and case studies showing how the model resolves conflicting temporal information.

## Limitations
- The 2nd order approximation for the uncertainty-adapted loss function lacks detailed specification
- YAGO preprocessing from period-based to event-based format is underspecified
- Limited discussion of how the model handles conflicting temporal observations
- No complexity analysis or scaling experiments for large graphs

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core WGP-NN architecture design and its three-module structure
- Medium confidence: The superiority claims over baseline models given strong empirical results
- Low confidence: The theoretical justification for using Gaussian processes in this specific context and the exact implementation of the uncertainty loss function
