# Two Steps Forward and One Behind: Rethinking Time Series Forecasting with Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04553
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04553
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: Simple models (SLP, MLP) consistently outperform complex Transformer architectures for time series forecasting

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the prevailing trend of using Transformer-based models for time series forecasting by demonstrating their limitations and proposing simpler, more effective alternatives. The authors show that a basic Persistence model often outperforms state-of-the-art Transformer architectures like Informer and Autoformer, especially for longer forecasting windows. They introduce simplified Transformer models (Sinformer, Sencoder) that achieve state-of-the-art performance among Transformer-based approaches, and further propose extremely shallow models (SLP, MLP) that consistently outperform all Transformer-based models across various datasets and forecasting horizons.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate multiple forecasting approaches including Persistence baseline, Transformer-based models (Sinformer, Sencoder), and shallow models (SLP, MLP) on six time series datasets. All models use Additive Time To Vector (AddT2V) embedding for periodic pattern extraction. Models are trained for 50 epochs using Adam optimizer with exponentially decaying learning rate (10^-3 to 10^-6), with performance measured by Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across four forecasting window sizes (96, 192, 336, 720 points) and extended to 23,040 points for one dataset.

## Key Results
- SLP model achieves state-of-the-art performance among all models, including Transformers
- Persistence baseline outperforms Informer and Autoformer in nearly half of tested cases
- Sencoder (Transformer without decoder) slightly outperforms Sinformer on average
- Shallow models handle extremely long forecasting windows (up to 23,040 points) better than Transformer-based approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Simpler models can outperform complex Transformers in time series forecasting when periodicity is present
- Mechanism: The AddT2V embedding captures high-level periodic patterns and maps them directly into the prediction space, making the forecasting task more tractable for simple models
- Core assumption: Time series with sufficient periodic patterns and data can be effectively modeled without attention mechanisms
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we empirically show how simplifying this forecasting model almost always leads to an improvement, reaching the state of the art among Transformer-based architectures"
  - [section] "we used a revised version of the architecture called Additive Time To Vector (AddT2V) in our experiments"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism
- Break condition: When time series lack clear periodic patterns or have insufficient data for pattern extraction

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Removing the decoder from Transformer architecture improves forecasting performance
- Mechanism: The decoder adds complexity without proportional benefit, and removing it while maintaining sinusoidal activation in the encoder output simplifies the model while preserving essential functionality
- Core assumption: The decoder's role in time series forecasting is less critical than in language tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The second Transformer-based model is a simplification of Sinformer where the decoder is removed and a sinusoidal activation is added to the encoder output"
  - [section] "On average, the Sencoder performs slightly better than the Sinformer"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for decoder removal benefits
- Break condition: When sequential dependencies require complex decoding operations

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Persistence baseline is a necessary benchmark for evaluating forecasting models
- Mechanism: A simple parametric model provides a reality check against overfitting and helps identify when complex models fail to capture simple patterns
- Core assumption: If a complex model cannot outperform a naive baseline, it indicates fundamental issues with the approach
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we propose the use of an aparametric model, known as the Persistence model, as a constant of comparison"
  - [section] "the Informer... performs worse than the Persistence model in almost half of the cases"
  - [section] "the shallow models consistently produce the best overall results"
- Break condition: When the persistence baseline becomes too simplistic for the complexity of the data

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input layer -> AddT2V embedding -> Optional encoder (for Sinformer/Sencoder) -> Dense layers -> Output layer (sinusoidal or linear) -> Loss calculation (MAE)

- Critical path:
  1. Data preprocessing (standardization only)
  2. AddT2V embedding computation
  3. Model forward pass
  4. Loss calculation
  5. Backpropagation
  6. Parameter update (Adam optimizer with decaying learning rate)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Complexity vs. performance: Simpler models (SLP, MLP) often outperform complex Transformers
  - Memory vs. window length: Shallow models handle extremely long windows better
  - Interpretability vs. accuracy: Simple models are easier to analyze but may miss complex patterns

- Failure signatures:
  - Model consistently underperforming persistence baseline
  - Memory overflow errors during training (for Transformer models)
  - High variance in predictions across different runs

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare Persistence model vs. SLP on a univariate dataset with clear periodicity
  2. Test Sencoder vs. Sinformer on a multivariate dataset to evaluate decoder necessity
  3. Run MLP vs. Transformer-based models on a dataset with long forecasting windows to demonstrate memory advantages

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what specific conditions do shallow models consistently outperform Transformer-based models in time series forecasting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that SLP and MLP models consistently outperform Transformer-based models across various datasets and forecasting horizons, with the authors noting that "any shallow model is preferable to any Transformer-based model" on average.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates superior performance of shallow models in tested scenarios, it doesn't provide a comprehensive theoretical framework explaining when and why this superiority occurs across different time series characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying time series characteristics (periodicity, noise levels, stationarity, length) to identify specific conditions where shallow models excel, combined with theoretical analysis of why these conditions favor simpler architectures.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the fundamental limitation of attention mechanisms that prevents them from effectively capturing temporal relationships in time series data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors cite Zeng et al.'s argument that "permutation-invariant attention mechanisms... may result in a loss of temporal information" and that Transformers may be "less effective at extracting temporal relations in an ordered set of continuous points."
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates this limitation empirically but doesn't provide a deep theoretical explanation for why attention mechanisms struggle with temporal ordering in continuous numerical sequences.
- What evidence would resolve it: Rigorous mathematical analysis of attention mechanisms' ability to preserve temporal ordering, combined with experiments isolating temporal versus semantic dependencies in time series.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the AddT2V embedding mechanism enable extremely long-range forecasting (up to 23,040 points) while maintaining stability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that the SLP model "demonstrates remarkable ability to forecast extremely long time series (up to 23040 points) with stable performance" and suggest that AddT2V's design helps "exploit periodicities during the training phase."
- Why unresolved: While the empirical results are impressive, the paper doesn't provide a detailed explanation of the mathematical properties of AddT2V that enable such long-range forecasting stability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis of AddT2V's frequency domain properties and controlled experiments varying its parameters to understand the relationship between its design and long-range forecasting capability.

## Limitations

- Results based on specific datasets and may not generalize to all time series types
- No extensive ablation studies to isolate impact of individual architectural choices
