# MultiAdam: Parameter-wise Scale-invariant Optimizer for Multiscale Training of Physics-informed Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02816
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02816
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MultiAdam achieves 1-2 orders of magnitude improvement in PINN predictive accuracy over strong baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental training instability in physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) caused by domain scaling, which creates severe imbalance between partial differential equation (PDE) loss and boundary loss terms. Through theoretical analysis of second-order PDEs, the authors demonstrate that scaling the domain can multiply PDE loss by orders of magnitude while boundary loss remains unchanged. They prove error bounds linking PINN loss terms to actual solution error, showing that proper loss reweighting can guarantee convergence.

The proposed MultiAdam optimizer maintains separate second-momentum estimates for PDE and boundary losses, using these to re-scale gradients individually. Experiments on Poisson, Helmholtz, and Burgers equations show MultiAdam achieves significant improvements in accuracy and stability compared to Adam, NTK reweighting, and multitask learning methods, while being robust to domain scaling variations.

## Method Summary
MultiAdam addresses PINN training instability by maintaining separate second-moment estimates for PDE and boundary loss gradients, effectively creating two independent Adam optimizers that share parameters but update them with different momentum statistics. The optimizer tracks second-moment statistics $v_t$ separately for PDE and boundary gradients, then applies these to re-scale gradients before combining them for parameter updates. This scale-invariant approach automatically estimates and corrects loss imbalance through gradient momentum statistics without requiring manual hyperparameter tuning. The method's effectiveness stems from its ability to handle the fundamental imbalance that occurs when domain scaling multiplies PDE loss by orders of magnitude while leaving boundary loss unchanged.

## Key Results
- MultiAdam achieves 1-2 orders of magnitude improvement in predictive accuracy over strong baselines
- The method demonstrates robustness to domain scaling, maintaining stable convergence across different problem scales
- MultiAdam outperforms Adam, NTK reweighting, and multitask learning approaches on Poisson, Helmholtz, and Burgers equations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MultiAdam works by maintaining separate second-moment momentum estimates for PDE and boundary loss gradients, which allows it to automatically correct for the scale imbalance that occurs when domain scaling affects PDE loss differently than boundary loss. The optimizer computes separate $v_t$ statistics for each loss type, then uses these to re-scale gradients before combining them for parameter updates. This creates a scale-invariant optimization process where the relative importance of PDE and boundary terms is automatically balanced based on their gradient statistics rather than their absolute magnitudes. The theoretical foundation shows that proper loss reweighting, guided by these momentum statistics, can guarantee convergence to the true solution by maintaining appropriate error bounds between the PINN loss terms and actual solution error.

## Foundational Learning

Why needed: PINNs combine data-driven learning with physical law enforcement. Quick check: Can the network predict both known boundary conditions and satisfy the governing PDE?

- **Domain Scaling Effects**: How changing the spatial domain size affects loss term magnitudes. Why needed: Scaling can multiply PDE loss by orders of magnitude while leaving boundary loss unchanged. Quick check: Compare loss magnitudes before and after domain scaling.

Quick check: Confirm that bounded constants in error bounds are well-estimated for specific problems.

- **Scale-Invariant Optimization**: Optimization methods that remain effective regardless of input/output scaling. Why needed: PINNs require optimization that works across different domain scales. Quick check: Test optimizer performance across multiple orders of magnitude in domain size.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Domain scaling -> Loss imbalance -> Separate momentum tracking -> Gradient re-scaling -> Parameter update

**Critical Path**: The optimization process flows from computing PDE and boundary losses, through separate second-moment tracking, to gradient re-scaling and parameter updates. The key innovation is the separation of momentum statistics for different loss types.

**Design Tradeoffs**: MultiAdam trades increased memory usage (tracking two sets of momentum statistics) for improved stability and accuracy. The method sacrifices some simplicity compared to standard Adam but gains automatic scale-invariance without manual hyperparameter tuning.

**Failure Signatures**: Training instability manifesting as divergence or poor convergence, particularly when domain scaling creates large imbalances between PDE and boundary losses. Standard Adam may fail to converge or converge to poor solutions when loss terms are severely imbalanced.

**First Experiments**: 1) Compare MultiAdam vs Adam on a simple Poisson equation with varying domain scales; 2) Test MultiAdam on a Helmholtz equation with complex boundary conditions; 3) Evaluate convergence speed and final accuracy on a Burgers equation with shock formation.

## Limitations
- Analysis focuses on second-order PDEs with specific boundary conditions, leaving uncertainty about generalization to higher-order or coupled PDE systems
- Performance gains may depend on specific problem characteristics and initialization, with 1-2 orders of magnitude improvement claims requiring context
- Primary validation demonstrates stability across domain scaling rather than other potential sources of instability

## Confidence

- 1-2 orders of magnitude improvement: Medium confidence, as gains depend on problem characteristics
- Robustness claims: Medium confidence, primarily validated for domain scaling scenarios
- Generalization to higher-order PDEs: Low confidence, as analysis is limited to second-order equations
