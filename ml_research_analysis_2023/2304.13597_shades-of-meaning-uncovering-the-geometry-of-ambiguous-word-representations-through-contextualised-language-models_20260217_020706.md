# Shades of meaning: Uncovering the geometry of ambiguous word representations through contextualised language models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13597
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13597
- Reference count: 18
- Key outcome: BERT embeddings of ambiguous words show greater semantic spread than unambiguous words, with homonyms forming distinct clusters and polysemes showing overlapping clusters

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether contextualised language models can capture the ambiguity structure of words by analysing BERT representations of homonymous, polysemous, and unambiguous words across over 2.5 million contexts. The research finds that contextual embeddings of ambiguous words exhibit greater semantic spread than those of unambiguous words, with homonyms producing non-overlapping clusters while polysemes show overlapping clusters consistent with psychological theories. A Bayesian classifier achieved high disambiguation accuracy (98% for "bark", 84% for "shade") using relatively superficial text statistics to recover fine-grained semantic distinctions.

## Method Summary
The researchers extracted contextual embeddings for 8 ambiguous and 4 unambiguous target words from BERT, using over 2.5 million contexts. They calculated average cosine distances between embeddings to measure semantic spread, comparing homonymous, polysemous, and unambiguous words. Deep case studies of "bark" and "shade" examined clustering patterns using PCA dimensionality reduction. A Bayesian classifier was trained on human-judged ground truth to disambiguate meanings based on the contextual embeddings, testing whether shallow text statistics could recover semantic distinctions.

## Key Results
- Contextual embeddings of homonymous and polysemous words show significantly greater semantic spread than unambiguous words
- "Bark" (homonym) exhibits distinct, non-overlapping clusters for animal sound versus tree bark
- "Shade" (polyseme) shows overlapping clusters for meanings like shadow versus color
- Bayesian classifier achieves 98% accuracy for "bark" and 84% accuracy for "shade" disambiguation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study leverages BERT's contextual embedding capabilities to capture nuanced semantic relationships by examining how word representations shift across different contexts. The approach works because BERT's transformer architecture encodes rich contextual information that reflects how humans process ambiguous words differently depending on usage. The geometric analysis of embedding spaces reveals that ambiguous words naturally separate into distinct semantic regions, while the Bayesian classifier exploits these patterns to recover intended meanings from relatively simple text statistics.

## Foundational Learning
- **Semantic Spread Analysis**: Measuring average cosine distances between word embeddings across contexts to quantify ambiguity
  - Why needed: Provides a geometric measure of how much a word's meaning varies across contexts
  - Quick check: Calculate spread for unambiguous words like "run" vs truly ambiguous words

- **PCA Dimensionality Reduction**: Reducing high-dimensional BERT embeddings to 2D/3D for visual clustering analysis
  - Why needed: Makes complex embedding relationships interpretable for human analysis
  - Quick check: Verify clusters remain distinct after PCA projection

- **Bayesian Classification for Disambiguation**: Using probability distributions over embeddings to classify word senses
  - Why needed: Provides interpretable, probabilistic framework for recovering intended meanings
  - Quick check: Test classification accuracy on held-out contexts

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
BERT Encoder -> Embedding Extraction -> Semantic Spread Calculation -> PCA Visualization -> Bayesian Classifier

**Critical Path:**
BERT context encoding → embedding extraction → semantic spread computation → cluster analysis → disambiguation classification

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Context window size (3 words) balances computational efficiency against contextual information
- PCA for visualization vs. full-dimensional analysis for accuracy
- Human judgements as ground truth vs. automated sense inventories

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor disambiguation accuracy indicates insufficient contextual information or ambiguous ground truth
- Overlapping clusters in PCA may suggest truly graded meanings rather than discrete senses
- Inconsistent semantic spread patterns across words may indicate model-specific biases

**First Experiments:**
1. Test different context window sizes (1, 3, 5 words) on classification accuracy
2. Compare semantic spread across multiple BERT variants (base, large, multilingual)
3. Apply same methodology to truly unambiguous control words to establish baseline spread

## Limitations
- Analysis limited to English language and BERT model architecture only
- Small sample of 8 ambiguous and 4 unambiguous target words may not represent full complexity
- Human judgements for ground truth introduce potential subjectivity in sense boundaries
- 3-word context window may miss important disambiguating information from wider context

## Confidence
- Contextual embeddings capture semantic spread differences between ambiguous and unambiguous words: High
- Homonyms produce non-overlapping clusters while polysemes produce overlapping clusters: Medium (based on case studies of only two words)
- Bayesian classifier can accurately disambiguate meanings using text statistics: Medium (high accuracy for one word, lower for another)

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the analysis across multiple language models (RoBERTa, GPT, etc.) and languages to test generalisability of the semantic spread findings
2. Expand the target word set to include a broader range of semantic categories and ambiguity types, particularly testing words with more than two meanings
3. Compare classification performance when using varying context window sizes (1, 3, 5, 7 words) to determine optimal context for disambiguation accuracy
