# Learning Emotional Representations from Imbalanced Speech Data for Speech Emotion Recognition and Emotional Text-to-Speech

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05709
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05709
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Proposes Emotion Extractor combining raw- and latent-level Mixup augmentations with contrastive loss for SER and TTS on imbalanced datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning robust emotional representations from imbalanced speech datasets for Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) and Emotional Text-to-Speech (TTS). The authors propose an Emotion Extractor that combines raw-level and latent-level Mixup augmentations with a contrastive loss to align representations from both augmentation types. The model is evaluated on three imbalanced SER datasets (EmoV-DB, IEMOCAP, and CREMA-D) and used to improve emotional expressiveness in TTS synthesis. Experimental results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on SER tasks and improved expressiveness in TTS compared to strong baselines.

## Method Summary
The Emotion Extractor is designed to learn generalizable emotional representations from imbalanced speech data by combining raw-level and latent-level Mixup augmentations with a contrastive loss. Raw-level Mixup involves linear interpolation of raw spectrograms, while latent-level Mixup performs interpolation in the latent space. A similarity loss encourages consistency between the representations from both augmentation types. For SER, the model is trained with weighted cross-entropy loss plus the similarity loss, and evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation with Unweighted Average Recall (UAR) as the metric. For TTS, the pre-trained Emotion Extractor provides emotional representations to the RFTacotron model, and the synthesized speech is evaluated through subjective preference tests and Mean Opinion Score (MOS) for naturalness.

## Key Results
- The Emotion Extractor surpasses state-of-the-art baselines on three imbalanced SER datasets (EmoV-DB, IEMOCAP, and CREMA-D)
- The model enables the TTS system to synthesize more expressive speech compared to a strong baseline
- The combination of raw- and latent-level Mixup augmentations with contrastive alignment shows strong generalization across different domains and tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Emotion Extractor works by leveraging both raw-level and latent-level Mixup augmentations to create diverse training samples that help the model learn more robust and generalizable emotional representations. The contrastive loss aligns the representations from both augmentation types, encouraging the model to capture consistent emotional information regardless of the augmentation applied. This approach helps the model overcome the challenges posed by imbalanced datasets, where the majority class (typically Neutral) can dominate the learning process. By forcing the model to learn from both augmented representations, it becomes more resilient to class imbalance and can better capture the nuances of different emotional expressions.

## Foundational Learning
- **Mixup augmentation**: Interpolates between samples to create virtual training examples. Why needed: Helps model generalize better by exposing it to interpolated emotional states. Quick check: Verify that interpolated samples produce smooth transitions between emotions.
- **Contrastive learning**: Aligns representations from different views of the same sample. Why needed: Ensures consistency in emotional representation across augmentation types. Quick check: Monitor similarity loss convergence during training.
- **Imbalanced learning strategies**: Weighted loss functions to handle class imbalance. Why needed: Prevents model from being biased towards majority class. Quick check: Compare class-wise UAR to ensure minority classes are well-represented.
- **Cross-modal representation learning**: Applying learned representations to different tasks (SER and TTS). Why needed: Demonstrates generalizability of emotional representations. Quick check: Validate performance drop when transferring between domains.
- **Mel-spectrogram preprocessing**: Standard audio feature extraction. Why needed: Provides consistent input representation for neural networks. Quick check: Ensure spectrogram quality and consistency across samples.
- **5-fold cross-validation**: Robust evaluation methodology for limited data. Why needed: Provides reliable performance estimates with small datasets. Quick check: Verify that folds are balanced and representative.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Raw Spectrogram -> Raw-Level Mixup -> Encoder -> Latent Space -> Latent-Level Mixup -> Contrastive Loss -> Emotion Representation

**Critical Path**: The most critical components are the Mixup augmentations and the contrastive loss that aligns their representations. The raw-level Mixup provides diversity in the input space, while latent-level Mixup ensures diversity in the learned representation space. The contrastive loss is essential for maintaining consistency between these two views.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The paper trades off computational complexity (from applying two types of Mixup) for improved robustness and generalization. The choice of VGG19 for SER versus a Reference Encoder-like architecture for TTS reflects different architectural needs for different tasks, balancing task-specific optimization with shared representation learning.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Overfitting to the majority Neutral class (poor minority class performance)
- Unstable training due to improper implementation of similarity loss
- Degraded performance when transferring representations to new domains
- Sensitivity to Mixup interpolation weights

**3 First Experiments**:
1. Train with only raw-level Mixup and compare performance to full model
2. Train with only latent-level Mixup and compare performance to full model
3. Remove the similarity loss and observe impact on alignment between augmentation types

## Limitations
- Architecture details of the Emotion Extractor, particularly for TTS applications, are not fully specified
- Effectiveness of the similarity loss implementation between raw- and latent-level representations needs empirical validation
- Performance on extremely imbalanced datasets with more severe class distribution skew remains untested
