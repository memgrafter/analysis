# Revisiting Non-Autoregressive Translation at Scale

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16155
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16155
- Reference count: 9

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that scaling model size and training data significantly improves non-autoregressive machine translation (NAT) performance, particularly on large-scale datasets. The research addresses common NAT weaknesses such as repetition, fluency, and adequacy through systematic scaling. The authors show that scaling is complementary to knowledge distillation, with both approaches yielding better results when combined. However, scaling also increases model size and decoding latency, prompting the proposal of a cone architecture that achieves comparable performance while maintaining decoding speed.

## Method Summary
The authors systematically scaled model size and training data for non-autoregressive translation systems, demonstrating significant improvements in translation quality on large-scale datasets. They combined this scaling approach with knowledge distillation techniques, showing that the two methods are complementary. To address the increased latency and model size resulting from scaling, they proposed a cone architecture featuring a deeper and wider encoder paired with a shallower and narrower decoder. This architectural modification aims to balance translation quality with decoding efficiency, making NAT more practical for real-world deployment.

## Key Results
- Scaling model size and training data significantly improves NAT performance on large-scale datasets
- Scaling alleviates common NAT weaknesses including repetition, fluency, and adequacy issues
- The cone architecture achieves comparable performance to scaled models while maintaining decoding speed

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Scaling improves NAT performance by providing the model with more parameters to capture complex translation patterns and more training data to learn diverse linguistic phenomena. This addresses the inherent challenges of NAT, which must generate translations in parallel rather than sequentially, by giving the model more capacity and exposure to learn the necessary alignments and transformations. The cone architecture works by leveraging a powerful encoder to capture rich source representations while using a more efficient decoder that can generate translations quickly without sacrificing quality, effectively balancing the trade-off between computational efficiency and translation accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Source text -> Wide/Deeper Encoder -> Latent representation -> Narrow/Shallow Decoder -> Target translation

**Critical path**: The bottleneck in NAT performance lies in the decoder's ability to generate accurate translations without the benefit of sequential conditioning. The cone architecture addresses this by ensuring the encoder captures sufficient source information while the decoder focuses on efficient generation.

**Design tradeoffs**: The cone architecture trades decoder capacity for speed while maintaining quality through a more powerful encoder. This represents a shift from the traditional symmetric encoder-decoder design, prioritizing decoding efficiency while preserving translation quality through enhanced source representation.

**Failure signatures**: NAT models without sufficient scaling often exhibit repetition, poor fluency, and inadequate coverage of source content. The cone architecture may fail when the encoder cannot capture sufficient source information or when the decoder lacks enough capacity to generate complex target structures despite its efficiency.

## Limitations
- The cone architecture's effectiveness on smaller, low-resource language pairs remains unclear
- The trade-off between model size and decoding latency may still limit deployment in resource-constrained environments
- The study does not explore the impact of scaling on domain-specific translation tasks

## Confidence
- **High**: Scaling model size and training data significantly improves NAT performance on large-scale datasets
- **Medium**: The cone architecture balances translation quality and efficiency, though generalizability to smaller datasets is uncertain
- **Low**: The claim that scaling is universally complementary to knowledge distillation lacks sufficient exploration across diverse scenarios
