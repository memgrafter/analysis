# PET-NeuS: Positional Encoding Tri-Planes for Neural Surfaces

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05594
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05594
- Reference count: 40
- Achieves 57% reduction in Chamfer distance on Nerf-synthetic dataset compared to state-of-the-art methods

## Executive Summary
PET-NeuS introduces a novel approach for surface reconstruction from multi-view images by extending the NeuS framework with tri-plane representations, positional encoding, and self-attention convolution. The method addresses the challenge of high-frequency noise in tri-plane features while preserving local geometric details. By modulating tri-plane features with positional encoding and using multi-scale self-attention convolution to generate frequency-matched features, PET-NeuS achieves significant improvements in surface reconstruction quality with a 57% reduction in Chamfer distance on Nerf-synthetic and 15.5% on DTU datasets.

## Method Summary
PET-NeuS combines tri-plane representations with positional encoding and self-attention convolution to improve neural surface reconstruction. The method uses three orthogonal feature planes (Txy, Tyz, Txz) with 512×512 resolution and 24 feature channels. Positional encoding with 8 frequency scales modulates these tri-plane features to suppress high-frequency noise. Multi-scale self-attention convolution with window sizes of 4, 8, and 16 generates features across different frequency bands that match the positional encoding frequencies. Two 3-layer MLPs map the processed features to SDF and color predictions using volumetric rendering. The framework is trained with color reconstruction loss, Eikonal regularization (λ=0.1), and Adam optimizer (lr=5e-4) for approximately 9 hours on DTU dataset.

## Key Results
- Achieves 57% reduction in Chamfer distance on Nerf-synthetic dataset
- Improves surface reconstruction by 15.5% on DTU dataset
- Effectively suppresses high-frequency noise while preserving local geometric details
- Provides more accurate and visually pleasing surface reconstructions compared to state-of-the-art baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Positional encoding modulates tri-plane features to suppress high-frequency noise while preserving local details
- Mechanism: Multiplying tri-plane features with sin/cos functions of different frequencies introduces frequency bounds that constrain the grid-based representation and reduce discretization discontinuities
- Core assumption: Tri-plane features without positional encoding introduce high-frequency noise due to their interpolation-based nature and lack of inductive biases
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "positional encoding with learnable weights to combat noise in the reconstruction process"; section states "Using tri-planes leads to a more expressive data structure but will also introduce noise in the reconstructed surface"
- Break condition: If positional encoding introduces too much smoothing, fine local details may be lost, or if frequency scaling is inappropriate, noise suppression may be ineffective

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Self-attention convolution with different window sizes generates multi-frequency tri-plane features that match positional encoding frequencies
- Mechanism: Using window sizes of 4, 8, and 16, single-layer self-attention convolution produces features in different frequency bands, which are concatenated with original features
- Core assumption: Fixed Gaussian kernels smooth features across depth discontinuities, causing wrong surface structure, while dynamic self-attention convolution can preserve these boundaries
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "learnable convolution operations on the tri-plane features using self-attention convolution to produce features with different frequency bands"; section notes that fixed Gaussian kernels "smooth features across depth discontinuities"
- Break condition: If window sizes are not properly chosen, frequency matching may be poor, leading to either insufficient noise suppression or loss of detail

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Geometric initialization adapted for tri-planes improves convergence and reconstruction quality
- Mechanism: Converting a properly initialized MLP into a tri-plane representation by looking up features at R×R coordinates of the form (x,y,0), (0,y,z), and (x,0,z)
- Core assumption: Standard geometric initialization for MLPs cannot be directly applied to grid-based representations like tri-planes
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "novel positional encoding strategy to be used in conjunction with tri-plane features"; section states that geometric initialization "is designed exclusively for MLPs" and cannot be directly used with tri-planes
- Break condition: If the MLP used for initialization is not properly configured or the coordinate mapping is incorrect, initialization may fail to provide desired geometric properties

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the trade-off between reconstructing fine details and adding high-frequency noise in flat surface areas manifest in different types of scenes or datasets?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention that they observed a trade-off between reconstructing fine details and adding high-frequency noise to otherwise flat surface areas, especially in areas of high-frequency changes in light transport
- Why unresolved: This trade-off was observed experimentally, but not analyzed theoretically or across a wide range of scenes and datasets
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing surface reconstruction quality across diverse datasets with varying levels of detail and noise, combined with theoretical analysis of the underlying causes of this trade-off

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the computation time of PET-NeuS be improved by one or two orders of magnitude without drastically sacrificing quality?
- Basis in paper: The authors state that one limitation of their method is the long computation time and suggest this as an exciting avenue for future work
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore optimization techniques or architectural changes that could significantly reduce computation time
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementation and validation of methods that achieve substantial speed improvements while maintaining comparable reconstruction quality, such as optimized implementations, model compression, or architectural modifications

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would PET-NeuS perform on dynamic scenes or videos where the geometry and appearance change over time?
- Basis in paper: The current implementation of PET-NeuS is designed for static scenes, as it does not incorporate temporal information or dynamic modeling capabilities
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on static multi-view reconstruction and does not address the challenges of dynamic scenes
- What evidence would resolve it: Extension of PET-NeuS to handle dynamic scenes, potentially by incorporating temporal information, and evaluation on datasets containing dynamic content

## Limitations

- The method has long computation time, with the authors suggesting this as an area for future improvement
- The trade-off between fine detail reconstruction and high-frequency noise in flat surface areas needs further investigation across diverse datasets
- The framework is currently designed for static scenes and does not handle dynamic content or temporal information

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The core architectural improvements (tri-plane representation, positional encoding, self-attention convolution) are technically sound and well-grounded in the literature. The reported performance improvements are plausible given the methodology.
- Medium Confidence: The noise suppression mechanism through positional encoding is theoretically justified but lacks quantitative ablation studies showing the specific contribution of this component. The claim that tri-plane features inherently introduce noise is asserted but not empirically validated.
- Low Confidence: The exact implementation details of the self-attention convolution windows and the geometric initialization procedure for tri-planes are not fully specified, making direct reproduction challenging.
