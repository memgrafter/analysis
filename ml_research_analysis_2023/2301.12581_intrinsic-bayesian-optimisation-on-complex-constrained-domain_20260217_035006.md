# Intrinsic Bayesian Optimisation on Complex Constrained Domain
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12581
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12581
- Reference count: 17
- Introduces Intrinsic Bayesian Optimization for complex constrained domains and manifolds

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Intrinsic Bayesian Optimization (In-BO), a novel method for optimizing objective functions on complex constrained domains and manifolds where traditional Euclidean-based approaches fail. In-BO uses a Sparse Intrinsic Gaussian Process (SIn-GP) surrogate model based on heat kernels estimated from Brownian Motion paths on the manifold, allowing it to respect the intrinsic geometry and boundaries of the domain. The method is demonstrated to consistently find global optima on challenging domains like U-shaped regions, Bitten Tori, and real-world Aral Sea chlorophyll data, where standard Bayesian Optimization often gets stuck in local optima.

## Method Summary
In-BO addresses the fundamental limitation of standard Bayesian Optimization on constrained domains by replacing Euclidean distance with intrinsic manifold distances. The core innovation is the Sparse Intrinsic Gaussian Process (SIn-GP) that uses heat kernels to capture the manifold's geometric structure. Heat kernels are estimated through Brownian Motion paths simulated on the manifold, providing a probabilistic measure of geodesic distance. This intrinsic distance metric ensures that the surrogate model and acquisition function respect the manifold's topology, preventing proposals in infeasible regions. The method combines this intrinsic GP with standard BO components (acquisition functions, optimization loops) but operates entirely in the intrinsic geometry of the domain.

## Key Results
- In-BO consistently finds global optima on U-shaped domains and Bitten Tori regardless of initialization, while standard BO frequently converges to local optima
- On the Aral Sea chlorophyll dataset, In-BO successfully identifies the true global optimum while standard BO fails due to its Euclidean distance bias
- The method demonstrates robustness to initialization, a critical advantage over standard BO on complex constrained domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
In-BO works by fundamentally changing how distances are measured on the optimization domain. Standard BO uses Euclidean distances, which can be meaningless or misleading on constrained manifolds where the shortest path between points must follow the manifold's surface. By estimating heat kernels through Brownian Motion, In-BO captures the true geodesic distances that respect the manifold's geometry and boundaries. This intrinsic distance metric ensures that the Gaussian Process surrogate model accurately represents correlations between points as they exist on the manifold, not as they would be in Euclidean space. Consequently, the acquisition function optimizes over the true manifold geometry rather than a distorted Euclidean approximation.

- **Heat Kernels**: Fundamental solutions to the heat equation on manifolds; needed to capture intrinsic distances between points on the manifold. Quick check: verify heat kernel satisfies the heat equation and appropriate boundary conditions.
- **Brownian Motion on Manifolds**: Stochastic process used to approximate geodesic distances through random walks on the manifold surface. Quick check: confirm convergence of Brownian paths to true geodesic distances as path length increases.
- **Intrinsic Gaussian Processes**: GP models that use manifold distances rather than Euclidean distances for covariance computation. Quick check: validate that covariance matrix respects manifold topology.
- **Sparse Variational Inference**: Approximation technique to make GP inference computationally tractable by using inducing points. Quick check: ensure inducing point selection captures manifold structure.
- **Manifold Learning**: Field focused on discovering low-dimensional structures in high-dimensional data. Quick check: verify manifold dimension estimation accuracy.
- **Constrained Bayesian Optimization**: BO variants that handle constraints through various techniques like projection or augmented acquisition functions. Quick check: compare constraint satisfaction rates across methods.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Brownian Motion Simulation -> Heat Kernel Estimation -> Intrinsic Distance Matrix -> Sparse Intrinsic GP -> Acquisition Function -> Optimization Loop

**Critical Path**: The heat kernel estimation via Brownian Motion is the computational bottleneck and accuracy bottleneck. The quality of intrinsic distances directly determines GP performance, which in turn determines optimization success.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Computational cost of Brownian Motion simulation vs. accuracy of heat kernel estimation; number of inducing points vs. approximation quality in SIn-GP; exploration-exploitation balance in acquisition function on complex manifolds.

**Failure Signatures**: Convergence to local optima (indicates poor heat kernel estimation or insufficient Brownian paths); excessive computational time (suggests need for better Brownian sampling strategy); constraint violations (indicates errors in manifold structure estimation).

**First 3 Experiments**:
1. Verify heat kernel estimation accuracy by comparing against known analytical solutions on simple manifolds (sphere, torus)
2. Test SIn-GP performance on synthetic constrained domains with known optima before real applications
3. Conduct sensitivity analysis on Brownian Motion parameters (step size, number of paths) to establish robust defaults

## Limitations
- Computational complexity of heat kernel estimation via Brownian Motion may become prohibitive for high-dimensional manifolds
- Assumes known or accurately estimable manifold structure, but real-world structure estimation could introduce significant errors
- Limited evaluation scope (simple manifolds and single real-world dataset) needs broader validation across diverse applications

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Theoretical foundation connecting heat kernels to manifold geometry is well-established
- **Medium Confidence**: Empirical results showing superior performance over standard BO are convincing but limited in scope
- **Medium Confidence**: Real-world application demonstrates practical relevance but needs more thorough statistical analysis

## Next Checks
1. Test In-BO on higher-dimensional manifolds (d > 3) and evaluate computational scalability, particularly heat kernel estimation time complexity as dimensionality increases

2. Conduct ablation studies to quantify impact of key hyperparameters (number of inducing points, Brownian Motion steps, kernel parameters) on optimization performance and robustness

3. Compare In-BO against alternative constrained BO methods using projection techniques or constraint-aware acquisition functions, not just standard BO with Euclidean distances
