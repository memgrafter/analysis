# How to Train Your DRAGON: Diverse Augmentation Towards Generalizable Dense Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07452
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07452
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: First BERT-base-sized dense retriever achieving state-of-the-art effectiveness in both supervised (MS MARCO) and zero-shot (BEIR, LoTTE) evaluations

## Executive Summary
This paper presents DRAGON, a framework for training generalizable dense retrievers that excel at both supervised and zero-shot retrieval tasks without increasing model size. The authors identify that diverse relevance labels from multiple teacher retrievers, combined with large-scale query augmentation using cropped sentences and synthetic queries, are key to achieving strong zero-shot performance. Their progressive label augmentation strategy guides the student model through increasingly complex supervision, resulting in the first BERT-base-sized dense retriever to achieve state-of-the-art effectiveness across both supervised and zero-shot evaluations.

## Method Summary
DRAGON employs a unified framework of data augmentation for contrastive learning, using multiple teacher retrievers (uniCOIL, Contriever, GTR-XXL, ColBERTv2, SPLADE++) to provide diverse relevance labels. The framework combines cropped sentences (information-rich but less human-like) with synthetic queries (more human-like but expensive) for query augmentation. Training follows a progressive supervision strategy where simpler teachers (uniCOIL) guide early learning, with progressively more complex teachers introduced later. This approach is applied to MS MARCO training data, and the resulting model achieves state-of-the-art performance on both MS MARCO and zero-shot BEIR/LoTTE evaluations.

## Key Results
- DRAGON achieves state-of-the-art effectiveness on MS MARCO in supervised setting
- DRAGON is the first BERT-base-sized dense retriever to achieve state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on BEIR and LoTTE
- Outperforms existing dense retrievers and even competes with larger models using complex late interaction
- Combines MAE pre-training with DRAGON further improves zero-shot evaluations without sacrificing in-domain performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Diverse Teacher Labels
Diverse relevance labels from multiple retrievers improve zero-shot generalization more than strong supervision from a single cross-encoder. Training with uniform supervision from diverse teachers (sparse, dense, multi-vector retrievers) exposes the model to multiple perspectives on relevance, preventing overfitting to a single notion of relevance. Progressive supervision further structures learning by introducing harder teachers later in training.

### Mechanism 2: Large-Scale Query Augmentation
Large-scale query augmentation with diverse query types (cropped sentences + synthetic queries) is more effective than using only human-like queries. Cropped sentences provide more information-rich, keyword-heavy queries that challenge teachers to disagree, leading to more diverse labels. Synthetic queries add human-like variety. The combination balances coverage and difficulty.

### Mechanism 3: Progressive Supervision
Progressive supervision with carefully ordered teachers (easy-to-hard) improves zero-shot generalization over uniform supervision. Early training with simpler teachers (uniCOIL) builds foundational skills, while later exposure to complex teachers (ColBERTv2) refines nuanced matching signals. This curriculum structure prevents early confusion from complex signals.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Contrastive learning and InfoNCE loss
  - **Why needed here**: DRAGON trains dense retrievers using contrastive learning to learn similarity between queries and relevant documents while distinguishing from negatives.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the difference between the InfoNCE loss and standard cross-entropy loss in the context of dense retrieval?

- **Concept**: Bi-encoder architecture and dot product similarity
  - **Why needed here**: The model encodes queries and documents independently into low-dimensional vectors and uses dot product for similarity scoring, enabling efficient ANN search.
  - **Quick check question**: Why does the bi-encoder architecture enable faster retrieval compared to cross-encoders?

- **Concept**: Data augmentation for contrastive learning
  - **Why needed here**: The framework treats query augmentation and label augmentation as key components to scale up training data and improve supervision quality.
  - **Quick check question**: How does query augmentation differ from relevance label augmentation in this framework?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Query → BERT-base encoder → CLS vector → Dot product with document vectors → InfoNCE loss → Update
- **Critical path**: Query → Encode → Compare → Loss → Update
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Uniform vs progressive supervision: Uniform is simpler but progressive yields better zero-shot performance at cost of training complexity.
  - Cropped vs synthetic queries: Cropped are cheaper and more diverse but less human-like; synthetic queries are more natural but expensive to generate.
  - Symmetric vs asymmetric encoders: Asymmetric encoders show better generalization in initial experiments.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor zero-shot performance: Likely due to insufficient teacher diversity or poor supervision ordering.
  - Overfitting to MS MARCO: May indicate lack of diverse queries or too much focus on supervised signals.
  - Training instability: Could result from mismatched teacher difficulty or aggressive learning rates.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train with single teacher (uniCOIL) on cropped sentences to establish baseline.
  2. Add second teacher (Contriever) with uniform supervision to test diversity benefit.
  3. Implement progressive supervision with uniCOIL→Contriever trajectory to test curriculum effect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Does DRAGON benefit from generative pre-training in addition to masked auto-encoding (MAE) pre-training?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper shows that DRAGON + (combining MAE pre-training and their approach) sees further improvement on zero-shot evaluations without sacrificing in-domain ones, indicating that MAE pre-training may be orthogonal to their approach based on contrastive learning. However, it is unclear whether generative pre-training would provide additional benefits.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not explore the combination of generative pre-training and their approach based on contrastive learning.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments comparing DRAGON trained with and without generative pre-training, as well as comparing it to other models that use generative pre-training.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can DRAGON serve as a strong foundation model for domain adaptation retrieval tasks?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper mentions that DRAGON can serve as a strong foundation retrieval model for domain adaptation retrieval tasks (Wang et al., 2022; Dai et al., 2022) or the existing retrieval augmented language models (Izacard et al., 2022; Mallen et al., 2022; Shi et al., 2023).
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not provide evidence of DRAGON's effectiveness in domain adaptation retrieval tasks or its potential to improve existing retrieval augmented language models.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments evaluating DRAGON's performance in domain adaptation retrieval tasks and its potential to improve existing retrieval augmented language models.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Is there a more efficient way to perform relevance label augmentation using a cross encoder?
- **Basis in paper**: The paper discusses the latency cost per query for relevance label augmentation with different neural rankers and demonstrates that leveraging all the retrievers to augment relevance labels are still more efficient than a cross encoder. However, it does not explore alternative methods to improve the efficiency of cross encoder-based relevance label augmentation.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper does not investigate alternative methods to improve the efficiency of cross encoder-based relevance label augmentation.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments comparing the efficiency of different methods for cross encoder-based relevance label augmentation, including potential improvements in latency and effectiveness.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of diverse teacher labels assumes teachers capture truly complementary relevance signals, but quantitative analysis of teacher label overlap is not provided.
- The progressive supervision strategy's effectiveness depends critically on the ordering of teachers, but limited empirical validation of whether the proposed sequence is optimal.
- The reliance on cropped sentences assumes information-richness correlates with better generalization, which isn't directly validated across domains.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Core empirical findings showing clear improvements in both supervised and zero-shot evaluations compared to strong baselines.

**Medium confidence**: Mechanism claims identifying diversity and progressive supervision as key factors, though causal mechanisms could benefit from additional ablation studies.

**Low confidence**: Optimal configuration claims, as the paper presents a specific configuration but doesn't provide exhaustive ablation studies to establish true optimality.

## Next Checks

1. **Teacher diversity analysis**: Quantitatively measure label agreement/disagreement across teacher pairs to empirically validate the diversity assumption. Compute metrics like label overlap, pairwise agreement rates, and information-theoretic diversity scores.

2. **Teacher ordering ablation**: Systematically test alternative teacher ordering strategies (random, difficulty-based, diversity-based) to determine whether the proposed progressive schedule is truly optimal or if simpler approaches work equally well.

3. **Query type contribution isolation**: Conduct controlled experiments that isolate the contribution of each query type (cropped sentences, synthetic queries, original queries) by training models with only one query type at a time, then in combinations, to quantify their individual and synergistic effects.
