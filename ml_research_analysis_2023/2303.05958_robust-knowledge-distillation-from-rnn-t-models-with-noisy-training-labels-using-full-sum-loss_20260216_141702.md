# Robust Knowledge Distillation from RNN-T Models With Noisy Training Labels Using Full-Sum Loss

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05958
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05958
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Full-sum distillation outperforms soft distillation for RNN-T models, especially with noisy teacher models, by distilling sequence-level posteriors. It is robust to alignment mismatches and scales well to large in-house datasets, achieving 11-28% relative WER improvements.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces full-sum distillation (FS-Distill) as a robust knowledge distillation method for RNN-T models when training labels are noisy. Unlike soft distillation which requires exact alignment between teacher and student, FS-Distill operates at the sequence-level posterior probability, making it resilient to alignment mismatches and teacher noise. The method demonstrates significant WER improvements, particularly when the teacher model quality is poor.

## Method Summary
The method uses full-sum distillation to train student RNN-T models by minimizing the L1 or MSE loss between teacher and student sequence-level posteriors. Unlike traditional soft distillation that requires matching token-level alignments, FS-Distill distills the entire sequence probability distribution. The approach includes an optional normalization step using N-best hypotheses to further improve robustness. Training uses a mix of supervised ground truth and unsupervised data, with the teacher model frozen during student training.

## Key Results
- FS-Distill outperforms soft distillation for RNN-T models, especially with bad teachers
- L1 loss performs better than MSE loss in FS distillation due to outlier robustness
- FS-Norm-Distill further improves performance by normalizing with N-best hypotheses
- Method scales to large in-house datasets with 11-28% relative WER improvements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FS-Distill is robust to noisy teacher labels and alignment mismatches.
- Mechanism: FS-Distill operates at the sequence-level posterior probability, not at the token/frame level. This means it does not depend on exact alignment between teacher and student, unlike soft distillation which requires matching per-token logits. When the teacher model is noisy (high WER), FS-Distill still learns the overall structure of the output distribution rather than overfitting to misaligned or incorrect individual tokens.
- Core assumption: Sequence-level posteriors contain sufficient information to train a student model even when individual alignments differ or teacher outputs are noisy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Full-sum distillation outperforms other distillation methods for RNN-T models, especially for bad teachers."
  - [section 4]: "FS distillation method does not depend on time dimension as compared to soft distillation which means that the teacher and student models can have different time subsampling rates."
  - [corpus]: Weak correlation (FMR 0.511) with similar KD robustness studies; no direct citation overlap.
- Break condition: If the sequence posterior distribution is too distorted by noise, the student model may still learn incorrect patterns despite robustness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: L1 loss outperforms MSE loss in FS distillation due to robustness to outliers.
- Mechanism: MSE squares the error, so large discrepancies (outliers) dominate the gradient and destabilize training. L1 loss is linear in error magnitude, so it is less sensitive to occasional large errors, making it more stable when teacher posteriors contain noise or misalignment.
- Core assumption: Training stability and outlier robustness are more important than minimizing average squared error in noisy distillation scenarios.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1.2]: "Using L1 loss gives much better performance in terms of WER compared to MSE loss... training convergence is affected by outliers when using MSE loss."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; inferred from standard ML literature on loss functions.
- Break condition: If teacher posteriors are very clean and aligned, MSE might converge faster; the robustness advantage of L1 would be less critical.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Normalizing teacher posteriors with N-best hypotheses (FS-Norm-Distill) reduces outlier effects and improves distillation quality.
- Mechanism: Instead of distilling the raw sequence posterior, FS-Norm-Distill distills the normalized probability relative to the top-N hypotheses. This constrains the student to learn the relative ranking and distribution among likely sequences, not just the absolute posterior, which reduces the impact of spurious high-probability sequences from a noisy teacher.
- Core assumption: The relative distribution among top-N hypotheses is more reliable than the absolute posterior when the teacher is noisy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "We can formulate the FS-Distill loss... to distill the approximated normalized sequence posterior probabilities using a N-best hypotheses list generated by the teacher model."
  - [section 5.1.3]: "Applying normalization further improves the WER of the student model especially when using MSE loss since it reduces outliers."
  - [corpus]: No direct citations; similar ideas appear in ensemble distillation literature.
- Break condition: If N is too small, important hypotheses may be excluded; if N is too large, noise may be reintroduced.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Teacher model -> Sequence posteriors -> N-best hypotheses list -> Normalization -> FS-Distill loss
  - Student model -> RNN-T loss + FS-Distill loss -> Backpropagation -> Parameter updates
  - Data pipeline -> Supervised + unsupervised data -> Beam search -> Pseudo labels

- Critical path:
  1. Teacher generates pseudo labels / sequence posteriors on unsupervised data.
  2. Student and teacher compute RNN-T losses on same input.
  3. FS-Distill loss (L1 or MSE, optional normalization) computed and backpropagated to student.
  4. Student updated; teacher frozen.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Causal vs non-causal teacher: Non-causal teachers can have better alignments but may not be deployable; causal teachers are more realistic but potentially noisier.
  - N-best size: Larger N captures more hypothesis diversity but increases computation and may reintroduce noise.
  - Loss function: L1 is more stable with noisy teachers; MSE may converge faster if data is clean.

- Failure signatures:
  - Student WER plateaus or degrades: Possible noisy teacher, misaligned alignments, or inappropriate loss.
  - Training instability or exploding gradients: Likely MSE loss with outliers, or numerical issues in posterior computation.
  - No improvement over baseline: Teacher quality too low, or distillation loss not properly scaled/weighted.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare student WER using L1 vs MSE loss with a clean teacher on a small public dataset.
  2. Apply FS-Distill with a noisy teacher (high WER) and measure robustness vs soft distillation.
  3. Test FS-Norm-Distill with varying N-best sizes to find the optimal balance between diversity and noise.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does full-sum distillation perform when the student model has a significantly different architecture (e.g., Transformer vs RNN-T) compared to the teacher?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on RNN-T models but mentions potential architecture mismatches.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only compare different RNN-T architectures (ConformerL, ConformerM, ConformerS) and don't explore cross-architecture distillation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing full-sum distillation performance when transferring knowledge from RNN-T to Transformer models or vice versa.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using more than N-best hypotheses in the FS-Norm-Distill variant on final WER performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using N-best hypotheses for sequence discriminative knowledge distillation but only reports results for a fixed N.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore the sensitivity of results to the number of hypotheses used in the normalization term.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study showing WER performance across different N values (e.g., 5, 10, 50, 100) for various datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does full-sum distillation scale when using extremely large teacher models (e.g., 10B+ parameters) compared to moderate-sized ones?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using a 1B parameter teacher on LibriSpeech but doesn't explore much larger models.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focus on models in the 120M-1B parameter range without exploring the extreme end of the scale.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing full-sum distillation performance when using teacher models of increasing size (1B, 10B, 100B parameters) on the same tasks.

## Limitations
- Teacher quality definition unclear: The paper defines "bad teachers" as those with 22-31% WER but doesn't report the absolute WER of teacher models or pseudo-label quality.
- Alignment mismatch quantification missing: No quantitative measures of alignment drift between teacher and student models.
- Hyperparameter sensitivity not thoroughly explored: Limited ablation studies on N-best size, loss weighting, and beam search parameters.

## Confidence
- FS-Distill outperforms soft distillation for noisy teachers: High
- L1 loss is more robust than MSE for noisy teachers: Medium
- FS-Norm-Distill improves robustness via N-best normalization: Medium
- Method scales to large in-house datasets: Low (limited ablation)
