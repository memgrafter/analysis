# Do We Really Need Complicated Model Architectures For Temporal Networks?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11636
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11636
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Simple MLP-based architecture outperforms complex RNN and self-attention models on temporal link prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the conventional wisdom that complex architectures like RNNs and self-attention are necessary for temporal graph learning. The authors propose GraphMixer, a remarkably simple architecture using only MLPs and neighbor mean-pooling, achieving state-of-the-art performance on temporal link prediction benchmarks. GraphMixer demonstrates faster convergence and better generalization than more complex baselines, suggesting that simpler methods can be more effective for temporal graph learning tasks.

## Method Summary
GraphMixer processes temporal graphs using a three-component architecture: a link-encoder (MLP-Mixer with time encoding) that processes temporal link information, a node-encoder (neighbor mean-pooling) that captures node features and neighborhood context, and a link classifier (2-layer MLP) that makes final predictions. The model uses fixed cosine-based time encoding instead of trainable alternatives, processes only 1-hop most recent neighbors from undirected graphs, and employs zero-padding in the MLP-Mixer to preserve sequence length information.

## Key Results
- GraphMixer achieves state-of-the-art performance on temporal link prediction benchmarks
- Simpler architecture demonstrates faster convergence and better generalization than complex baselines
- Fixed time-encoding function with cosine projection is more stable and effective than trainable alternatives
- Zero-padding in MLP-Mixer enables sequence length discrimination that self-attention with mean-pooling cannot achieve

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
MLP-Mixer with zero-padding can distinguish temporal sequences that self-attention with mean-pooling cannot. Zero-padding preserves sequence length information while mean-pooling erases it. MLP-Mixer processes padded sequences as fixed-length tokens where padding tokens are treated differently than actual data tokens, allowing the model to learn patterns that depend on sequence length. Self-attention with mean-pooling averages all tokens together, making it impossible to distinguish [a1, a1] from [a1] or determine if [a1, a2] is longer than [a3].

### Mechanism 2
Fixed time-encoding function with cosine projection is more stable and effective than trainable time-encoding. The fixed time-encoding uses cos(tω) where ω contains exponentially decreasing frequencies, creating a smooth, continuous representation where similar timestamps have similar encodings. The trainable version z(t) = cos(tw+b) suffers from exploding gradients because ∂cos(tw+b)/∂w = t·sin(tw+b) scales with timestamp magnitude, causing training instability.

### Mechanism 3
Simpler input data structure and neural architecture lead to better generalization. Using undirected graphs with 1-hop most recent neighbors provides cleaner signal-to-noise ratio than multi-hop or uniform sampling. The simpler architecture (MLP + mean-pooling) has fewer parameters and less complex optimization landscape, making it easier to train and less prone to overfitting.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input → Time encoding → Link-encoder (MLP-Mixer) → Node-encoder (mean-pooling) → Classifier (MLP) → Output
- **Critical path:** The time encoding happens first, then link information is processed, node information is aggregated, and finally the two representations are combined for prediction
- **Design tradeoffs:** Simplicity vs expressiveness (simpler architecture generalizes better but may miss complex patterns), fixed vs trainable time encoding (fixed is more stable but less flexible), 1-hop vs multi-hop neighbors (1-hop is cleaner but may miss important long-range information)
- **Failure signatures:** Poor performance on tasks requiring long-range temporal dependencies, failure to capture complex temporal patterns, sensitivity to timestamp scale, poor generalization when training data distribution differs significantly from test data
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test link prediction accuracy on a simple temporal graph dataset
  2. Compare performance with and without time encoding
  3. Compare performance with 1-hop vs 2-hop neighbors

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what specific conditions does GraphMixer's performance advantage over complex architectures diminish?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows GraphMixer excels on temporal link prediction but doesn't systematically test failure cases or edge conditions where complex architectures might be necessary
- Why unresolved: The experiments focus on benchmark datasets where GraphMixer performs well, but don't explore scenarios with more complex temporal patterns, longer sequences, or higher-order interactions
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments on synthetic datasets with varying complexity levels, or analysis of GraphMixer's performance degradation on real-world datasets with more complex temporal dynamics

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does GraphMixer's performance scale with graph size and density compared to RNN/SAM-based methods?
- Basis in paper: While the paper compares wall-clock times and parameters, it doesn't provide systematic analysis of how performance and computational requirements scale with different graph sizes and densities
- Why unresolved: The experiments use fixed-size datasets without varying graph characteristics to understand scaling behavior and computational trade-offs
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on graphs of varying sizes and densities showing performance trends and computational requirements as a function of graph characteristics

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between GraphMixer's architecture simplicity and its generalization ability?
- Basis in paper: The paper identifies simplicity as a key factor but doesn't provide theoretical analysis of why simpler architectures generalize better in this context
- Why unresolved: The paper empirically demonstrates better generalization but doesn't explain the underlying mechanisms or provide theoretical bounds
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal analysis connecting architectural complexity to generalization bounds, or ablation studies isolating the specific aspects of simplicity that contribute to generalization

## Limitations
- Findings primarily validated on undirected graphs with 1-hop neighborhoods
- Effectiveness of zero-padding may be task-specific and not generalize to all scenarios
- Fixed time-encoding approach may be too restrictive for datasets with highly variable temporal scales

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core empirical finding that GraphMixer outperforms baselines on tested datasets
- Medium confidence: The generalizability of simplicity principles to other temporal graph learning tasks
- Medium confidence: The superiority of fixed time-encoding over trainable alternatives across all temporal graph scenarios
- Low confidence: The claim that zero-padding enables MLP-Mixer to distinguish temporal sequences better than self-attention in all contexts
