# Adversarial Rewards in Universal Learning for Contextual Bandits

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07186
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07186
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Shows optimistic universal learning for contextual bandits with adversarial rewards is impossible in general

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the fundamental limits of universal learning in contextual bandits, where rewards can be adversarial and depend on actions and contexts. The authors establish necessary and sufficient conditions for universal learning under various adversarial reward models, showing that the set of learnable processes is strictly larger than i.i.d., stationary, or ergodic processes but smaller than what's learnable in supervised learning. A key finding is that optimistic universal learning with adversarial rewards is impossible in general, contrasting with all previously studied online learning settings. The paper introduces novel stochastic process conditions (C4, C5, C6, C7) and provides algorithms that achieve universal consistency for processes satisfying these conditions.

## Method Summary
The paper characterizes learnable processes through measure-theoretic conditions on stochastic processes and develops algorithms achieving universal consistency under different adversarial reward models. The main algorithmic approach combines personalization (using EXP3. IX learners for each context instance) and generalization (using Hedge over a dense countable set of policies), with period-based probability updates. For online and prescient reward models, conditions C4 and C5 are shown to be necessary and sufficient respectively. The algorithms are designed to handle non-stationarity by switching between strategies based on estimated performance, using exponentially weighted averages to update strategy probabilities at exponentially increasing time intervals.

## Key Results
- Optimistic universal learning for contextual bandits with adversarial rewards is impossible in general
- Condition C4 is necessary for universal learning under online rewards
- Condition C5 is sufficient for universal learning under adversarial rewards
- The set of learnable processes is strictly larger than stationary/ergodic processes but smaller than supervised learning
- No single algorithm can be optimistically universal across all process classes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Personalization and generalization strategies are incompatible under non-stationary adversarial rewards
- Mechanism: When rewards can change adversarially based on past contexts and actions, algorithms trying to both personalize (learn best action for each context instance) and generalize (learn from population-level patterns) will fail at one or both objectives
- Core assumption: Reward mechanism can be adversarial, adapting based on past contexts and selected actions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that optimistic universal learning for contextual bandits with adversarial rewards is impossible in general"
  - [section] "the learning rule provided by [1] for stationary rewards under C2 processes heavily relies on the assumption that the rewards are stationary"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Condition C4 is necessary for universal learning under online rewards
- Mechanism: For online rewards where the reward distribution can depend on the entire past context sequence, a process must visit measurable partitions of the context space sublinearly and uniformly across all exponential time scales
- Core assumption: Reward mechanism can depend on the complete history of contexts (online model)
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we give necessary and sufficient conditions for universal learning under various non-stationarity models"
  - [section] "Condition C4 is defined as follows... X ∈ C4 and for any sequence of disjoint measurable sets (Ai)i≥ 1 of X"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Condition C5 is sufficient for universal learning under adversarial rewards
- Mechanism: By constructing an algorithm that uses a combination of personalization (EXP3. IX learners for each distinct instance) and generalization (Hedge algorithm over a dense countable set of policies), universal consistency can be achieved when there exists a rate at which we can add duplicates while preserving the C1 property
- Core assumption: There exists an increasing sequence of times (Ti) such that the extended process (Xt)t∈T with T = ∪i≥0 Ti ∩ {t ≥ Ti} belongs to C1
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also give necessary and sufficient conditions for universal learning under various non-stationarity models"
  - [section] "Theorem 26... C5 ⊂ Cadv"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Strategy 0 (personalization via EXP3. IX per instance) -> Strategy 1 (generalization via Hedge over dense policy set) -> Period-based probability updates -> Learning rule

- Critical path: For a new process and reward model, first check which class (C1, C2, C4, C5, C6, C7) the process belongs to, then select the appropriate algorithm (EXPINF for C1, modified Hedge+EXP3. IX for C5, etc.) and implement the period-based switching mechanism

- Design tradeoffs: No single algorithm can be optimistically universal - must adapt to specific process class. Tradeoff between generality of process class and complexity of learning algorithm

- Failure signatures: If algorithm fails to achieve universal consistency on a process X, indicates X likely falls outside the class for which algorithm was designed (e.g., C2 process with non-stationary rewards for EXPINF)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the EXPINF algorithm and verify universal consistency on C1 processes with adversarial rewards
  2. Construct a process that satisfies C4 but not C2, and test the necessity of C4 condition
  3. Implement the modified Hedge+EXP3. IX algorithm for C5 processes and verify universal consistency under adversarial rewards

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is an exact characterization of learnable processes Cadv, Cprescient, Conline or Coblivious for finite action spaces A and context spaces X admitting a non-atomic probability measure?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper explicitly states this as an open question in the main results section
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that C5 ⊊ C4 for these spaces, but the exact characterization of the intermediate classes (Cadv, Cprescient, Conline, Coblivious) remains unknown
- What evidence would resolve it: A complete characterization of these classes, possibly showing C5 = C7 or providing a different exact characterization

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is C5 = C7 for context spaces X admitting a non-atomic probability measure?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper explicitly asks this question in the main results section
- Why unresolved: The paper shows C5 ⊂ C7 ⊂ C6 ⊊ C4 but cannot prove whether C5 = C7
- What evidence would resolve it: Either a proof that C5 = C7 or a counterexample showing C5 ⊊ C7

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can we show C7 ⊊ C6 for context spaces X admitting a non-atomic probability measure?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper states they believe C7 ⊊ C6 but cannot prove it
- Why unresolved: The paper provides arguments for why C7 ⊊ C6 might be true but lacks a formal proof
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof that C7 ⊊ C6 or a counterexample showing C7 = C6

## Limitations
- Results depend on idealized assumptions about stochastic process structure that may not hold in practice
- Algorithms for sufficient conditions require maintaining dense countable policy sets, which may be computationally infeasible
- Adversarial reward models assume worst-case behavior that may be overly pessimistic

## Confidence

- Impossibility of optimistic universal learning: High
- Necessity of C4 condition: Medium-High  
- Sufficiency of C5 condition: Medium
- Algorithm constructions: Medium
