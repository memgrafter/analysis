# Estimating the Causal Effects of Natural Logic Features in Neural NLI Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.08572
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08572
- **Reference count**: 10
- **One-line primary result**: Models fine-tuned on the HELP dataset show improved sensitivity to context monotonicity and robustness to irrelevant word-pair changes compared to models trained only on large benchmark datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a causal framework for quantifying the robustness and sensitivity of neural NLI models with respect to natural logic features like monotonicity and word-pair relations. The authors construct intervention sets from the NLI-XY dataset to estimate total and direct causal effects of context and word-pair changes on model predictions. They find that high-performing models like roberta-large-mnli exhibit poor sensitivity to context changes affecting gold labels, suggesting over-reliance on word-pair relations. In contrast, models fine-tuned on the HELP dataset show improved sensitivity to context changes and robustness to irrelevant word-pair changes. The results highlight that benchmark accuracy does not necessarily correlate with strong reliance on important causal features, and that strategies to improve context monotonicity handling also increase robustness to irrelevant word-pair changes.

## Method Summary
The authors adapt causal inference methodology to the NLI task by constructing intervention sets from the NLI-XY dataset. They generate intervention pairs where either context or word-pair changes while other variables remain constant, then estimate total causal effects (TCE) and direct causal effects (DCE) using equations from Stolfo et al. (2022). The intervention schema involves conditioning on specific combinations of context monotonicity, word-pair relations, and gold labels to create valid intervention pairs. Models are evaluated on both benchmark accuracy and their causal effect profiles to assess robustness to irrelevant changes and sensitivity to impactful changes.

## Key Results
- High-performing models like roberta-large-mnli exhibit poor sensitivity to context changes affecting gold labels, indicating over-reliance on word-pair relations
- Models fine-tuned on the HELP dataset show improved sensitivity to context changes and robustness to irrelevant word-pair changes
- The HELP dataset explicitly targets natural logic reasoning, including context monotonicity
- Strategies to improve context monotonicity handling also increase robustness to irrelevant word-pair changes

- Core assumption: The NLI-XY dataset contains sufficient variation in context monotonicity and word-pair relations to generate meaningful intervention sets.

## Limitations

- The causal effect estimation methodology relies heavily on the quality and representativeness of the NLI-XY dataset, which has limited supporting evidence in the corpus
- The claim that HELP fine-tuning specifically improves robustness to irrelevant word-pair changes lacks direct supporting evidence and could be influenced by other factors
- The paper doesn't address potential confounding factors in the intervention pairs or how the size of intervention sets affects the reliability of TCE/DCE estimates

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The theoretical framework of using causal diagrams and interventions to evaluate NLI models is sound and well-established in the causal inference literature
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical findings showing that high-performing models exhibit poor sensitivity to context changes are plausible given known issues with spurious correlations in NLI benchmarks, but would benefit from replication on additional datasets
- **Low confidence**: The claim that HELP fine-tuning specifically improves robustness to irrelevant word-pair changes lacks direct supporting evidence and could be influenced by other factors in the fine-tuning process
