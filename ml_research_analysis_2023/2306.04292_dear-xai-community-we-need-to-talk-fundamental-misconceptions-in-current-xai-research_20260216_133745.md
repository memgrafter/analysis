# Dear XAI Community, We Need to Talk! Fundamental Misconceptions in Current XAI Research

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04292
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04292
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Paper identifies fundamental misconceptions in XAI research including purpose-free techniques, problematic benchmarking, and incorrect assumptions about learned concepts, proposing purpose-driven approaches and interdisciplinary collaboration as solutions.

## Executive Summary
This paper delivers a critical analysis of current eXplainable AI (XAI) research, identifying several fundamental misconceptions that undermine the field's progress. The authors argue that many XAI techniques are developed without clear purposes, evaluated using inappropriate benchmarks, and based on flawed assumptions about what deep neural networks learn. They emphasize the need for purpose-driven XAI that clearly defines what needs to be explained (explanandum) and what provides the explanation (explanans), along with proper interpretation guidelines and interdisciplinary collaboration.

## Method Summary
The paper employs conceptual analysis and critical review of current XAI practices rather than empirical experimentation. It examines the theoretical foundations of explanation in AI, identifying problematic assumptions and practices in the field. The authors analyze the distinction between explanations and justifications, critique the reliance on benchmarks without ground truth, and propose a framework for more rigorous XAI research. The method involves identifying misconceptions, explaining their implications, and suggesting constructive steps forward for the field.

## Key Results
- Many XAI techniques are developed without clarifying their purpose or explaining what needs to be explained
- The field incorrectly applies supervised learning benchmarking paradigms to XAI, where ground truth explanations are fundamentally unavailable
- There is a persistent belief that one explanation technique can serve all purposes, despite the diversity of explananda and conflicting nature of explanation purposes
- Current XAI research often assumes deep neural networks accidentally learn human concepts, which the authors argue is a flawed assumption
- The paper calls for purpose-driven XAI, clear explanation targets, proper interpretation guidelines, and interdisciplinary collaboration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Purpose-free XAI techniques proliferate because researchers conflate explanation with justification, leading to methods that produce intuitive-looking outputs without establishing clear explanatory relationships.
- **Mechanism:** When researchers present explanation techniques without clarifying their explanandum (what needs to be explained) or explanans (what explains it), they inadvertently create methods that serve as justifications rather than explanations. The community's acceptance of visually compelling heatmaps as sufficient evidence reinforces this pattern.
- **Core assumption:** The presence of interpretable visualizations automatically constitutes a valid explanation, regardless of whether an explanatory relationship has been formally established.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Many explanation techniques are still proposed without clarifying their purpose... advertised with ever more fancy-looking heatmaps"
  - [section] "Many papers in our field use standards to motivate explanations that we find particularly questionable... explainees are given images or annotations that should convince them that the explanation technique actually highlights the right things"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors don't directly address the purpose-justification conflation
- **Break condition:** When researchers explicitly define both explanandum and explanans before proposing any technique, and evaluation criteria shift from visual appeal to explanatory adequacy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The belief that one explanation technique can serve all purposes persists because researchers underestimate the diversity of explananda and the conflicting nature of explanation purposes.
- **Mechanism:** Researchers develop and promote single techniques (like SHAP or LIME) as universal solutions, failing to recognize that different explananda (model prediction vs. real-world phenomenon) require fundamentally different explanatory approaches. This leads to inappropriate technique selection and misleading results.
- **Core assumption:** All explanation purposes can be served by optimizing for the same formal properties, regardless of the specific explanandum.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "There is a persistent belief in our community that we only need to find and research the single best explanation technique... Explanation Purposes are generally in conflict"
  - [abstract] "Many explanation techniques are still proposed without clarifying their purpose"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on XAI applications rather than technique diversity
- **Break condition:** When research explicitly maps explanation techniques to specific explananda and demonstrates that techniques optimized for one purpose fail for others.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Benchmarks without ground truth proliferate because researchers incorrectly apply supervised learning benchmarking paradigms to XAI, where ground truth explanations are fundamentally unavailable.
- **Mechanism:** Researchers create benchmarks based on formal properties or intuitive appeal rather than actual explanatory adequacy, because they assume that if supervised learning can use benchmarks, XAI should too. This creates a false sense of progress and comparability.
- **Core assumption:** The success of benchmarks in supervised learning implies that similar approaches should work for explanation quality assessment.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "Benchmarks are meant to be objective comparisons between competitors... Without a ground-truth, it is hard to come up with metrics that quantify desirable properties"
  - [abstract] "explanation techniques are motivated with questionable goals, such as building trust, or rely on strong assumptions about the 'concepts' that deep learning algorithms learn"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus neighbors about benchmarking approaches
- **Break condition:** When the community shifts from benchmark-based evaluation to purpose-specific evaluation criteria that don't require ground truth.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Explanatory relationship (explanandum and explanans)**
  - Why needed here: Understanding that explanations require both a phenomenon to be explained and a factor that explains it is fundamental to avoiding purpose-free techniques
  - Quick check question: Can you identify the explanandum and explanans in a counterfactual explanation for loan approval?

- **Concept: Causal vs. associative explanation**
  - Why needed here: Distinguishing between explanations that establish causal relationships versus mere associations is crucial for developing meaningful XAI techniques
  - Quick check question: Does permutation feature importance establish causal or associative explanations, and why does this matter?

- **Concept: Explanandum diversity in XAI**
  - Why needed here: Recognizing that different explananda (model behavior vs. real-world phenomenon) require different explanatory approaches prevents the "one technique to rule them all" misconception
  - Quick check question: Would the same explanation technique be appropriate for debugging a model versus explaining its real-world implications?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - Purpose definition module → Explanandum identification → Expanans selection → Technique construction → Interpretation guidelines → Evaluation framework
  - Cross-cutting concerns: Interdisciplinary expertise integration, ethical considerations, human factors

- **Critical path:**
  1. Define explanation purpose and explanandum
  2. Select appropriate explanans type
  3. Construct technique ensuring explanatory relationship
  4. Develop clear interpretation instructions
  5. Evaluate against purpose-specific criteria
  - Critical failure point: Skipping purpose definition leads to all downstream components being misaligned

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - General vs. specific techniques: More general techniques are harder to interpret but cover more use cases
  - Causal vs. associative explanations: Causal explanations are more meaningful but computationally expensive to establish
  - Local vs. global explanations: Local explanations are more faithful but less comprehensive

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Vague purpose statements → Techniques that work "sometimes" but fail systematically
  - Missing explanandum → Methods that produce outputs but don't explain anything specific
  - No interpretation guidelines → Users misinterpret outputs, leading to distrust or misuse
  - Benchmark-only evaluation → Techniques optimized for wrong criteria

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Take an existing XAI technique and explicitly identify its explanandum and explanans. Test whether the technique actually establishes the claimed explanatory relationship.
  2. Implement two explanation techniques designed for different explananda (e.g., model debugging vs. real-world understanding) and demonstrate their failure when applied to the wrong explanandum.
  3. Create a purpose-driven evaluation framework for a specific XAI technique and compare it against benchmark-based evaluation to show the difference in results and insights gained.

## Limitations
- The paper presents conceptual arguments rather than empirical validation, limiting quantitative support for its claims
- Claims about widespread misconceptions across the entire XAI community lack systematic survey data
- Proposed solutions require significant cultural shifts in XAI research methodology that are difficult to implement and measure

## Confidence
- High confidence: The necessity of defining explanandum and explanans for meaningful explanations
- Medium confidence: The assertion that explanation purposes are fundamentally in conflict and require different techniques
- Low confidence: Claims about widespread misconceptions across the entire XAI community without systematic survey data
