# Safety-Aware Task Composition for Discrete and Continuous Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17033
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17033
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Extends Boolean task algebra for RL with safety constraints, introducing minimum-violation and prioritized safety semantics that provably enforce safe composition of policies in both discrete and continuous action spaces.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for safety-aware task composition in reinforcement learning that extends Boolean task algebra to incorporate safety constraints. The authors propose two safety semantics - minimum-violation (minimizing passage through undesired states) and prioritized safety (strictly avoiding certain states) - and show how to enforce either through a penalty-based reward structure. The approach is proven correct for deterministic MDPs and extended to continuous action spaces using policy/value function composition. Experiments demonstrate the method's effectiveness in grid worlds (both discrete and image observations) and a 3D physics simulation with continuous actions.

## Method Summary
The framework builds on MDPs with labeled states and propositions, introducing a penalty multiplier Cp that creates a reward hierarchy ensuring safety constraints are respected during task composition. For each task, optimal policies are trained using value iteration (discrete) or Q-learning/TD3 (continuous) with the safety-aware reward structure. Boolean composition operations (conjunction, disjunction, negation) are then applied to these policies using defined rules that preserve the safety semantics. The minimum-violation approach minimizes passage through bad states while allowing necessary violations, whereas prioritized safety strictly avoids certain states but may lead to chattering in some compositions.

## Key Results
- Proved that penalty-based reward structure enforces both minimum-violation and prioritized safety semantics in deterministic MDPs
- Extended Boolean composition from discrete to continuous action spaces using policy/value function composition
- Demonstrated zero-shot composition capability in 3D physics simulation with continuous actions
- Showed theoretical trade-offs between safety notions, with prioritized safety potentially leading to chattering in negated task compositions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The penalty multiplier Cp ensures that minimum-violation paths are chosen over paths that terminate early at undesired goals.
- Mechanism: The reward hierarchy uses a multiplicative penalty structure (Cp) that makes the total reward of any path passing through bad states worse than the worst-case minimum-violation path to the desired goal.
- Core assumption: Cp ≥ AvoidPathLen(g1, g2, q) for all relevant goals g1, g2 and propositions q, ensuring the penalty for bad steps dominates any reward from early termination.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "minimum-violation (minimizing passage through undesired states)"
  - [section]: "We call Cp ∈ N a penalty multiplier when defined by: Cp := argminN[∀g1, g2 ∈ G , q ∈ P . N ≥ AvoidPathLen(g1, g2, q)∧N ≥ AvoidPathLen(g1, g2, ¬q)]"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on action space structures, not safety-aware composition penalties.
- Break condition: If Cp is set too low relative to path lengths, early termination at undesired goals becomes preferable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Boolean composition operations preserve safety semantics when applied to policies trained with the penalty-based reward structure.
- Mechanism: The extended Q-value functions maintain knowledge of optimal values for each goal-task pair, and the composition rules (min for conjunction, max for disjunction) respect the safety hierarchy encoded in the rewards.
- Core assumption: Optimal policies encode minimum-violation paths for each goal-task pair, and the composition preserves this optimality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "show how to enforce either safety semantics, prove correctness (under some assumptions)"
  - [section]: "For a given policy ¯π trained under the reward structure given by (7) (resp. (8)), the policy from any given state encodes a path that corresponds to the shortest minimum-violation (resp. prioritized safety) path from that state."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence - corpus focuses on action space structures, not Boolean composition correctness.
- Break condition: If the optimal policy for a negated task encodes chattering paths that violate the safety constraint, composition may fail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The continuous action space extension preserves minimum-violation semantics through policy/value function composition.
- Mechanism: For continuous action spaces, the disjunction and conjunction of two tasks is computed by comparing the optimal actions from each task's policy at each state, selecting the action that maximizes the minimum-violation reward.
- Core assumption: The optimal policy for each task's value function encodes the minimum-violation path to each goal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "extend Boolean composition from discrete action spaces to continuous action spaces"
  - [section]: "From a policy standpoint, let's consider ¯Q∗1 ∨ ¯Q∗2. The optimal action will be π∗¯Q∗1∨¯Q∗2 : S → A"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on action space structures, not continuous action space composition.
- Break condition: If the optimal policy for a negated task encodes chattering paths that violate the safety constraint, composition may fail.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  MDP environment with labeled states and propositions -> Penalty multiplier Cp for safety hierarchy -> Extended Q-value functions for each task -> Composition rules for Boolean operations -> Training algorithms (value iteration/Q-learning/TD3) for each task -> Composition module for deploying composed policies

- Critical path:
  1. Define MDP environment and propositions
  2. Set penalty multiplier Cp
  3. Train extended Q-value functions for each task
  4. Compose desired policies using Boolean operations
  5. Deploy composed policy in environment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Discrete vs. continuous action spaces (requires different training algorithms)
  - Minimum-violation vs. prioritized safety semantics (different reward structures and assumptions)
  - Number of tasks vs. composition complexity (exponential growth in possible compositions)

- Failure signatures:
  - Chattering in composed policy (indicates violation of Assumption 3)
  - Early termination at undesired goals (indicates Cp set too low)
  - Convergence issues during training (indicates reward structure too sparse or penalties too harsh)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train individual tasks on a simple grid world and verify minimum-violation paths
  2. Compose two tasks using conjunction and verify the composed policy satisfies both tasks
  3. Extend to a continuous action space environment and verify the composition rules work as expected

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the theoretical lower bound on the penalty multiplier Cp that guarantees minimum-violation safety semantics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives Cp based on AvoidPathLen(g1, g2, q) and AvoidPathLen(g1, g2, ¬q) in Definition 7.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a sufficient condition for Cp but does not prove it is also necessary or determine the minimal value.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof showing that Cp cannot be reduced below the derived value without compromising the safety semantics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of prioritized safety semantics compare to minimum-violation semantics in environments with dense obstacle layouts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses trade-offs between the two safety notions and mentions that prioritized safety can lead to chattering in certain compositions, but does not provide empirical comparisons in challenging environments.
- Why unresolved: The experimental evaluation focuses on simpler grid world and physics environments without densely packed obstacles.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing both approaches across environments with varying obstacle densities and measuring metrics like path length, safety violations, and convergence speed.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the Boolean task algebra be extended to support temporal logic specifications beyond reachability and safety?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions temporal logic planning as motivation but focuses only on Boolean composition for reachability and safety.
- Why unresolved: The current framework is limited to simple Boolean operators and does not handle temporal operators like "eventually" or "always" that appear in temporal logic.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal extension of the reward structure and composition rules to incorporate temporal operators, along with proofs of correctness and experimental validation.

## Limitations

- Theoretical guarantees rely heavily on deterministic MDP assumptions, particularly the finite non-satisfying regions for negated tasks
- Practical tuning of penalty multiplier Cp across diverse environments remains an open challenge
- Prioritized safety composition between multiple negated tasks lacks complete theoretical guarantees and may lead to chattering

## Confidence

- High confidence: The minimum-violation mechanism (Cp penalty structure ensuring safe paths dominate early termination rewards)
- Medium confidence: Boolean composition correctness preserving safety semantics (proof relies on deterministic assumptions)
- Low confidence: Prioritized safety composition between multiple negated tasks (limited theoretical guarantees)
