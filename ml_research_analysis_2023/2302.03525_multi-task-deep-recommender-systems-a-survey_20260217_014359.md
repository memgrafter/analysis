# Multi-Task Deep Recommender Systems: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03525
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03525
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comprehensive survey of Multi-Task Deep Recommender Systems (MTDRS) with taxonomy, applications, datasets, and future research directions

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive survey of Multi-Task Deep Recommender Systems (MTDRS), examining how these systems leverage shared knowledge across multiple recommendation tasks to achieve mutual improvement. The survey systematically categorizes MTDRS methods based on task relationships (parallel, cascaded, auxiliary with main) and methodological approaches (parameter sharing, optimization, training mechanisms). The paper identifies key challenges including negative transfer, multi-scenario modeling, integration of large pre-trained models, AutoML, explainability, and task-specific biases, while highlighting promising research directions.

## Method Summary
MTDRS leverages multi-task learning to simultaneously predict multiple recommendation tasks by exploiting shared knowledge across tasks. The methodology involves parameter sharing patterns (hard, sparse, soft, expert sharing), optimization strategies for multi-objective trade-off, and various training mechanisms including joint and alternating training. The core approach uses shared bottom layers to extract common features while task-specific layers capture individual task patterns. Training employs weighted sum of task-specific losses, with applications spanning CTR prediction, CVR estimation, ratings, and watch predictions across datasets like MovieLens, Yelp, Amazon, and Ali-CCP.

## Key Results
- MTDRS taxonomy organized by task relation (parallel, cascaded, auxiliary) and methodology (parameter sharing, optimization, training)
- Four parameter sharing patterns identified: hard sharing, sparse sharing, soft sharing, and expert sharing
- Applications span CTR/CVR prediction, rating prediction, and watch prediction tasks
- Public datasets identified include MovieLens, Yelp, Amazon, Ali-CCP, and Criteo
- Future directions include addressing negative transfer, multi-scenario modeling, pre-trained models, AutoML, and explainability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-task deep recommender systems improve recommendation performance by leveraging shared knowledge across related tasks.
- Mechanism: MTDRS employs parameter sharing, optimization, and training mechanisms to capture complex task relevance and extract useful information among tasks.
- Core assumption: Related tasks in recommendation share common patterns and knowledge that can be exploited to improve overall performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Multi-task learning (MTL) aims at learning related tasks in a unified model to achieve mutual improvement among tasks considering their shared knowledge."
  - [section] "On the one hand, by exploiting data and knowledge across multiple tasks, MTDRS can achieve mutual enhancement among the tasks."
- Break condition: If tasks are not related or share minimal common patterns, parameter sharing may lead to negative transfer and performance degradation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: MTDRS addresses data sparsity issues by using information from auxiliary tasks to improve main task performance.
- Mechanism: Auxiliary tasks are designed to provide additional information and regularization for the main task, improving its performance through shared representation learning.
- Core assumption: Auxiliary tasks contain useful information that can help improve the performance of the main task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Auxiliary with main task refers to the circumstance that a task is specified as the main task while others, i.e., associated auxiliary tasks help to improve its performance."
  - [section] "Adding auxiliary tasks aim at helping to enhance the performance of primary tasks."
- Break condition: If auxiliary tasks are not well-designed or do not provide relevant information, they may not contribute to the main task's performance and could even introduce noise.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: MTDRS models can be applied to multi-scenario recommendation by treating different scenarios as different tasks.
- Mechanism: The shared bottom layers in MTDRS extract common features across scenarios, while task-specific layers capture scenario-specific patterns.
- Core assumption: Different scenarios in recommendation share some common patterns and can benefit from shared representation learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Both MSR and MTR require generating adaptive representations among multiple scenarios or tasks in a fine-grained manner and modeling complex inter-dependency."
  - [section] "MTDRS models can be applied for MSR simply regarding different scenarios as different tasks like HMoE [32]."
- Break condition: If scenarios are too dissimilar or have conflicting objectives, treating them as tasks in MTDRS may lead to negative transfer and suboptimal performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Shared bottom layers -> Task-specific towers (one per task) -> Loss computation for each task
- Critical path: Data preprocessing -> Feature extraction through shared layers -> Task-specific processing -> Loss computation -> Backpropagation with multi-task gradients
- Design tradeoffs: Choosing parameter sharing pattern involves balancing model flexibility, computational efficiency, and negative transfer risk
- Failure signatures: Negative transfer (some tasks degrade), overfitting on sparse tasks, underfitting due to excessive sharing, poor generalization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we design effective self-auxiliary tasks in multi-task deep recommender systems without relying on domain-specific knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that Wang et al. [63] propose under-parameterized self-auxiliaries to achieve better generalization, but notes that the frameworks are manually-auxiliary and require domain knowledge.
- Why unresolved: Designing auxiliary tasks that are both general enough to be applicable across domains and specific enough to provide meaningful learning signals remains challenging.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating the effectiveness of self-auxiliary tasks across multiple domains and recommendation tasks, showing improved performance compared to manually designed auxiliary tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective strategies for handling task-specific biases in multi-task deep recommender systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies task-specific biases as a challenge, noting that most existing models only focus on one specific bias, such as sample selection bias, implicit selection bias, or inherent estimation bias.
- Why unresolved: Different tasks in multi-task learning can have different behavior patterns, leading to various biases that are not well understood or addressed in current literature.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of comprehensive bias mitigation techniques that can be applied across multiple tasks and domains, with quantitative measures of bias reduction and performance improvement.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can large pre-trained models be effectively integrated into multi-task deep recommender systems without relying on prompt design or tuning for each specific task?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that large pre-trained models like P5 [19], M6-Rec [10], and UniMIND [12] rely on prompt design or tuning for each specific task, which is inefficient and different from common MTDRS models.
- Why unresolved: Integrating pre-trained models efficiently into MTDRS frameworks without task-specific prompt engineering remains an open challenge, especially given the diversity of recommendation tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of novel architectures or training strategies that enable seamless integration of pre-trained models into MTDRS frameworks, demonstrating improved performance and efficiency compared to task-specific prompt approaches.

## Limitations

- Rapidly evolving field may have missed recent developments in large pre-trained models and AutoML applications
- Taxonomy may not capture all emerging architectural patterns as the field progresses
- Limited empirical validation across diverse datasets to support theoretical claims

## Confidence

- High confidence: The fundamental mechanism of parameter sharing for knowledge transfer across related tasks is well-established in MTL literature and consistently supported by empirical evidence in recommendation systems.
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of different parameter sharing patterns (hard, sparse, soft, expert) depends heavily on task relatedness and dataset characteristics, with limited comparative studies providing definitive guidance on optimal choices.
- Medium confidence: The claim that MTDRS addresses data sparsity is theoretically sound but requires careful auxiliary task design; empirical evidence shows mixed results depending on task selection and implementation details.
