# Cross Modal Distillation for Flood Extent Mapping

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08180
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08180
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Cross-modal distillation achieves 72.74% IoU on Sen1Floods11, outperforming weak label baseline (66.21%)

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a cross-modal distillation framework to improve flood extent mapping from synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imagery. The approach transfers supervision from a teacher model trained on paired SAR and multi-spectral images to a student model trained only on SAR imagery. By using the teacher to generate soft labels for a large unlabeled dataset, the student learns to map flood extent without requiring hand-labeled SAR data. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Sen1Floods11 dataset, demonstrating the effectiveness of leveraging multi-spectral information to improve SAR-only flood detection.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a cross-modal distillation approach that leverages a teacher model trained on paired Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 multi-spectral imagery to supervise a student model trained only on Sentinel-1 data. The teacher generates soft probabilistic labels for unlabeled Sentinel-1 images, which are then used to train the student via knowledge distillation. The framework incorporates edge-weighted loss to emphasize boundary pixels and improves weak labels by incorporating permanent water occurrence maps. The student model uses an identical architecture to the teacher (Deeplabv3+ with Xception65 backbone) but learns from the teacher's soft outputs rather than hand-labeled data.

## Key Results
- Cross-modal distillation achieves 72.74% IoU on Sen1Floods11 test set
- Outperforms weak label supervised baseline (66.21% IoU) by 6.53 percentage points
- Demonstrates effectiveness of soft label supervision compared to binary thresholding
- Shows generalization capability by training on Sen1Floods11 and testing on Floods-DA dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cross-modal distillation leverages a richer Sentinel-2 input to provide higher quality soft labels for Sentinel-1-only training.
- Mechanism: The teacher model trained on paired Sentinel-1+Sentinel-2 images produces probabilistic outputs (soft labels) that better capture label uncertainty and geographic variability compared to binary weak labels from thresholding.
- Core assumption: The richer Sentinel-2 modality contains discriminative features that transfer effectively to the target Sentinel-1 task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "cross modal distillation framework that transfers supervision from a teacher trained on richer modality (multi-spectral images) to a student model trained on SAR imagery"
  - [section]: "soft labels predicted by the teacher capture uncertainty better compared to the binary labels"
- Break condition: If Sentinel-2 features are not aligned in time or spatially degraded, teacher predictions will not be reliable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using historical water occurrence maps improves weak labels by adding permanent water features missed by thresholding.
- Mechanism: Permanent water pixels are identified by long-term averaging of water presence, and these are overlaid onto the weak labels to correct large-scale river omissions.
- Core assumption: The water occurrence map reliably represents permanent water bodies across the studied regions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The label initially misses the river because they are formed by thresholding Sentinel-2 image and the label is sensitive to the value of the threshold."
  - [section]: "The weak label is improved by additionally marking the pixels having their water occurrence probability above a certain threshold, as wet in the label."
- Break condition: If the historical map is outdated or the threshold is too low, spurious permanent water pixels will be added.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining a large unlabelled Sentinel-1 dataset with cross-modal distillation yields better generalization than supervised baselines.
- Mechanism: The teacher model trained on small hand-labelled data generalizes to diverse geographies; its soft outputs supervise a larger Sentinel-1-only student, providing consistent training signals across many unlabeled examples.
- Core assumption: The unlabelled dataset is geographically diverse enough for the teacher to provide useful guidance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The data in each batch is sampled equally from both the data sources to ensure equal weighting for the datasets."
  - [section]: "This shows that there were still more gains to be had by increasing the weak label dataset size."
- Break condition: If the unlabelled dataset is too geographically narrow, the teacher will overfit to local patterns.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Knowledge Distillation (teacher-student setup)
  - Why needed here: Allows transfer of learned features from the richer Sentinel-2 modality to the target Sentinel-1 task without requiring labeled Sentinel-1 data.
  - Quick check question: In cross-modal distillation, what is the role of the teacher versus the student model?

- Concept: Cross Entropy Loss with Edge Weighting
  - Why needed here: Edge-weighted loss emphasizes harder boundary pixels, which are more informative for flood extent segmentation.
  - Quick check question: How does edge weighting affect the learning of segmentation boundaries compared to uniform weighting?

- Concept: KL Divergence for Soft Label Supervision
  - Why needed here: KL divergence aligns the student's predicted distribution with the teacher's soft labels, capturing uncertainty and avoiding overconfidence.
  - Quick check question: Why might KL divergence be preferred over mean squared error when supervising with soft labels?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Sentinel-1+Sentinel-2 images -> Teacher model (Deeplabv3+) -> Soft labels -> Student model (Deeplabv3+) -> Flood extent prediction
- Critical path:
  1. Train teacher on Dl (hand-labelled).
  2. Freeze teacher, generate soft labels on unlabelled data.
  3. Train student on unlabelled data using teacher's soft labels.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sentinel-2 input improves teacher quality but requires paired data.
  - Edge weighting improves boundary learning but increases parameter count.
  - Cross-modal setup avoids manual annotation but depends on modality alignment.
- Failure signatures:
  - Student converges to teacher's errors if teacher overfits.
  - Low IoU if teacher soft labels are too noisy.
  - Model fails if Sentinel-2 images are heavily clouded.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: Train student on Sen1Floods11 weak labels only, measure IoU.
  2. Teacher-only: Train teacher on Dl, evaluate on Sen1Floods11 test set.
  3. Full pipeline: Run teacher→student distillation, compare IoU to baseline.

## Limitations
- Dependence on Sentinel-2 data availability and quality, problematic in cloudy regions or when temporal alignment is imperfect
- Effectiveness of water occurrence maps assumes stable permanent water features, which may not hold in dynamic environments
- Limited to specific flood-prone regions covered by the Sen1Floods11 dataset, potentially limiting generalizability

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The improvement over weak label baselines (72.74% vs 66.21% IoU) is well-supported by the experimental results presented.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that cross-modal distillation outperforms supervised baselines is supported but could benefit from additional ablation studies.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalization claims to new geographies are not extensively validated beyond the tested datasets.

## Next Checks

1. **Temporal Stability Test**: Evaluate the model's performance across different flood events separated by significant time periods to assess temporal generalization and identify potential degradation in performance.

2. **Cross-Regional Generalization**: Test the trained model on flood data from completely different geographic regions not represented in the training data to validate robustness to different terrains and flood characteristics.

3. **Teacher Model Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically vary the quality and temporal alignment of Sentinel-2 inputs to the teacher model to quantify how sensitive the student's performance is to teacher quality.
