# Fairness Index Measures to Evaluate Bias in Biometric Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10919
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10919
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Three new fairness measures (SFI, CFI, DFI) evaluate demographic bias in biometric systems through pre-decision score distribution analysis with exponential weighting for demographic imbalance

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces three novel fairness measures for evaluating demographic bias in biometric verification systems by analyzing the statistical characteristics of genuine and imposter score distributions rather than relying on post-decision accuracy metrics. The measures—Separation Fairness Index (SFI), Compactness Fairness Index (CFI), and Distribution Fairness Index (DFI)—provide complementary perspectives on fairness by examining separation of expected values, compactness of distributions, and overall distribution similarity across demographic groups. A key innovation is the introduction of an exponential weighting strategy that accounts for demographic imbalance in test datasets, ensuring that under-represented groups receive appropriate emphasis in the fairness evaluation.

## Method Summary
The method analyzes genuine and imposter score distributions from biometric verification systems across multiple demographic groups to compute fairness indices. Three primary components are calculated: separation of expected values between genuine and imposter scores (for SFI), standard deviation of score distributions (for CFI), and Kullback-Leibler divergence between distributions (for DFI). These components are combined using three fusion strategies: normal (mean across groups), extremal (maximum across groups), and weighted (exponential weighting based on sample sizes). The exponential weighting function assigns higher importance to under-represented demographic groups while avoiding excessive weight for groups with very few samples. The approach is designed to be modality-independent, applicable to any biometric system that generates matching scores.

## Key Results
- The proposed measures analyze pre-decision score distributions rather than post-decision accuracy metrics, providing a more comprehensive fairness evaluation
- Exponential weighting strategy effectively handles demographic imbalance by emphasizing under-represented groups through a function of sample sizes
- The measures are demonstrated to be modality-independent and applicable across face, fingerprint, and other biometric systems
- Three variants of each measure (normal, extremal, weighted) provide different perspectives on fairness evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-decision score distribution analysis provides more comprehensive fairness evaluation than post-decision accuracy metrics.
- Mechanism: By analyzing genuine and imposter score distributions across demographic groups, the proposed measures capture disparities in how well the system matches pairs of samples, rather than just whether they were correctly matched.
- Core assumption: The underlying score distributions contain sufficient information to characterize fairness beyond simple accuracy metrics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we introduce multiple measures, based on the statistical characteristics of score distributions, for the evaluation of demographic fairness"
  - [section] "The use of distributions of genuine and imposter scores of a biometric recognition system towards evaluation of its demographic fairness has several advantages"
- Break condition: If the score distributions are too similar across demographic groups, the measures may fail to detect subtle fairness issues that manifest in decision boundaries.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Exponential weighting strategy effectively handles demographic imbalance in test datasets.
- Mechanism: The proposed exponential function assigns higher weights to under-represented demographic groups while avoiding excessive weight assignment to groups with very few samples.
- Core assumption: The exponential function with parameters derived from test dataset properties provides appropriate balance between under-represented and over-represented groups.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We propose the following mechanism to compute fusion weights... The parameters of the exponential are determined from the properties of the test dataset"
  - [section] "This weighing approach emphasizes the contributions of under-represented demographic groups"
- Break condition: If the exponential parameters are not properly tuned to the specific dataset characteristics, the weighting may either over-emphasize small groups or fail to adequately address imbalance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The proposed measures are modality-independent and applicable across various biometric systems.
- Mechanism: By focusing on statistical characteristics of score distributions rather than modality-specific features, the measures can be applied universally to any biometric verification system.
- Core assumption: Score distributions capture sufficient information about system behavior regardless of the underlying biometric modality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proposed measures are independent of the biometric modality, and thus, applicable across commonly used biometric modalities"
  - [section] "Being agnostic to the modality, the proposed measures are applicable across various biometric modalities"
- Break condition: If certain biometric modalities produce score distributions with fundamentally different characteristics that are not captured by the proposed statistical measures.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input score distributions -> Statistical analysis (SFI, CFI, DFI) -> Exponential weighting -> Fusion (normal/extremal/weighted) -> Fairness measures

- Critical path:
  1. Collect score distributions for genuine and imposter pairs across demographic groups
  2. Compute primary fairness components for each demographic group
  3. Apply exponential weighting strategy based on sample sizes
  4. Combine components using appropriate fusion rule (normal, extremal, or weighted)
  5. Generate final fairness measure values

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. fairness: The measures focus on fairness rather than overall system accuracy
  - Complexity vs. interpretability: Multiple variants provide different perspectives but increase complexity
  - Modality independence vs. modality-specific optimization: General approach may miss modality-specific nuances

- Failure signatures:
  - If score distributions are nearly identical across demographic groups, all measures may indicate high fairness even when subtle issues exist
  - If demographic imbalance is extreme, even the weighted variants may produce unreliable results due to insufficient data from small groups
  - If the score function does not adequately capture the quality of matches, the measures may not reflect true fairness

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test on synthetic data with known demographic bias patterns to verify the measures correctly identify fairness issues
  2. Apply to a balanced face recognition dataset to establish baseline behavior of all three measures
  3. Evaluate on an imbalanced dataset to test the effectiveness of the exponential weighting strategy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does demographic imbalance in training data affect fairness measures differently than imbalance in test data?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that demographic imbalance in the training set is beyond the scope of the current work, while focusing on test data imbalance.
  - Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the potential impact but does not investigate or provide evidence on how training data imbalance affects fairness measures.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimental comparison of fairness measures when training data is imbalanced versus when test data is imbalanced, showing how each affects the proposed fairness indices.

- **Open Question 2**: Can the proposed fairness measures effectively detect bias in biometric systems when demographic groups have different distributions in feature space but similar verification accuracy?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper emphasizes that measures based on differential outcome (like accuracy) may miss important disparities in score distributions between demographic groups, suggesting the need for pre-decision analysis.
  - Why unresolved: While the paper proposes measures based on score distributions, it does not provide extensive empirical validation showing their effectiveness in detecting bias when accuracy metrics fail.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Case studies or experiments demonstrating cases where the proposed measures detect bias that accuracy-based measures miss, particularly when different demographic groups have similar overall accuracy but different score distributions.

- **Open Question 3**: How do the proposed fairness measures perform when biometric systems use different score functions or distance metrics?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the proposed measures are dependent on the chosen score function and that the output is normalized in the range [0, 1], but does not explore the impact of different score functions on the measures.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not provide analysis or experiments showing how different score functions or distance metrics affect the fairness measures' ability to detect bias.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of the proposed fairness measures across multiple biometric systems using different score functions or distance metrics, showing consistency or variation in detected fairness.

## Limitations
- The measures rely on quality and representativeness of score distributions, which may be affected by dataset limitations or system biases
- Exponential weighting may not fully compensate for extremely skewed datasets with very few samples from some groups
- Modality-independent approach may miss system-specific nuances in different biometric modalities

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The theoretical framework for analyzing score distributions to evaluate fairness is well-established and the mathematical foundations are sound.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the exponential weighting strategy depends on proper parameter selection and may vary across different dataset characteristics.
- **Medium Confidence**: The modality-independence claim is supported by the abstract methodology but requires empirical validation across diverse biometric systems.
