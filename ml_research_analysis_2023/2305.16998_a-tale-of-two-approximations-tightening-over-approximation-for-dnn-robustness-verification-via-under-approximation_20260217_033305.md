# A Tale of Two Approximations: Tightening Over-Approximation for DNN Robustness Verification via Under-Approximation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16998
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16998
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: 100%-1000% improvement in verified robustness ratio and 10.64% (up to 66.53%) improvement in certified lower bound compared to state-of-the-art approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of DNN robustness verification under input perturbations by tightening over-approximation bounds for nonlinear activation functions. The authors identify that existing over-approximation methods overlook the importance of the activation function's actual domain, leading to overestimation. They propose a dual-approximation approach that combines underestimated domains (computed via Monte Carlo or gradient-based methods) with overestimated domains to define tighter linear bounds. Experiments show their method achieves significant improvements in verified robustness ratio and certified lower bound compared to state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed dual-approximation approach combines underestimated and overestimated domains for activation functions to define tighter linear bounds. The method uses either Monte Carlo sampling or gradient-based techniques to compute under-approximations of the activation function's domain. These under-approximations are then combined with traditional over-approximations to create more precise linear bounds. The approach can be integrated with existing verification frameworks like CROWN and DeepPoly, improving their precision without requiring fundamental changes to their core algorithms.

## Key Results
- 100%-1000% improvement in verified robustness ratio compared to state-of-the-art methods
- 10.64% (up to 66.53%) improvement in certified lower bound for robustness verification
- Significant reduction in overestimation error for ReLU activation functions
- Effective integration with existing verification frameworks like CROWN and DeepPoly

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's mechanism works by addressing a fundamental limitation in current over-approximation methods: they ignore the actual domain constraints of activation functions. By incorporating underestimated domains computed through sampling or gradient methods, the linear bounds become tighter and more accurate. This dual-approximation approach captures the true behavior of activation functions more precisely, reducing overestimation and enabling more robust verification results.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Input perturbation set -> Under-approximation computation (Monte Carlo/Gradient) -> Over-approximation bounds -> Dual-approximation bounds -> Verification framework (CROWN/DeepPoly) -> Verified robustness results

**Critical path**: The most critical path is from input perturbation set through under-approximation computation to the final verification results, as this determines the tightness of the bounds.

**Design tradeoffs**: The method trades computational overhead from under-approximation computation for improved verification precision. Monte Carlo sampling offers simplicity but may require many samples, while gradient-based methods are more efficient but require differentiable approximations.

**Failure signatures**: The method may fail when the under-approximation is too loose (sampled points miss critical regions) or when the gradient-based method converges to poor local minima.

## Limitations
- Monte Carlo method may become computationally prohibitive for high-dimensional inputs or complex perturbation sets
- Performance on non-image datasets or regression tasks is not demonstrated
- Practical overhead of computing underestimated domains alongside verification is not thoroughly analyzed
