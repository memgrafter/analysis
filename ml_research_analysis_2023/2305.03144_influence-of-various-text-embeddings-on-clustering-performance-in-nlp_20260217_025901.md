# Influence of various text embeddings on clustering performance in NLP

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03144
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03144
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: DBSCAN and HDBSCAN outperform other clustering algorithms on text embeddings, but identify many outliers, with embedding choice significantly affecting performance

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how different text embedding techniques influence clustering performance on product review data. The author compares Word2Vec averages, BERT CLS tokens, and BERT averages across four clustering algorithms (KMeans, single linkage hierarchical, DBSCAN, HDBSCAN) using three evaluation metrics. Results demonstrate that embedding choice strongly affects clustering outcomes, with density-based methods generally performing best but labeling many points as outliers. The optimal number of clusters often differs from the number of star ratings, highlighting the importance of embedding selection and careful hyperparameter tuning.

## Method Summary
The study uses Amazon product reviews dataset (1,177 samples after filtering) with star ratings 1-5. Three embedding types are generated: Word2Vec averages, BERT CLS tokens, and BERT averages, each scaled with StandardScaler. These embeddings are clustered using four algorithms: KMeans, single linkage hierarchical clustering, DBSCAN, and HDBSCAN with tuned hyperparameters. Performance is evaluated using silhouette scores (internal validation), adjusted Rand index (external validation), and cluster purity.

## Key Results
- DBSCAN and HDBSCAN outperform KMeans and single linkage hierarchical clustering on text embeddings
- Density-based algorithms identify significantly more outliers, complicating interpretation
- No single embedding type is universally superior across all clustering algorithms
- The optimal number of clusters often differs from the number of star ratings
- Embedding choice drastically affects clustering algorithm performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The choice of text embedding drastically affects clustering algorithm performance.
- Mechanism: Different embeddings encode semantic relationships in distinct vector spaces. Word2Vec uses context-free averages, while BERT embeddings (CLS or average) capture contextual meaning. These differences cause variations in cluster cohesion and separation.
- Core assumption: The embedding space structure is preserved by the clustering algorithm.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our results indicate that the type of embedding chosen drastically affects the performance of the algorithm"
  - [section] "Each embedding type encodes the text differently. Moreover, as embeddings vary in the number of dimensions, their distribution in space is also different."
- Break condition: If the clustering algorithm is invariant to input feature scaling or distance metrics, embedding differences become negligible.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Density-based algorithms (DBSCAN, HDBSCAN) outperform partitioning and hierarchical methods on high-dimensional text embeddings.
- Mechanism: Density-based methods detect clusters based on local density variations, which align better with the manifold structure of text embeddings. They handle noise and outliers inherent in text data.
- Core assumption: Text embeddings form dense regions corresponding to semantically coherent clusters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "DBSCAN outperforms KMeans and single linkage agglomerative clustering but also labels more data points as outliers."
  - [section] "density based algorithms may perform better than other types of clustering algorithms"
- Break condition: If the embedding space is uniformly dense or sparse, density-based methods lose their advantage.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The optimal number of clusters may differ from the number of star ratings.
- Mechanism: Star ratings are discrete user labels that don't necessarily reflect the natural semantic groupings in the text. Clustering reveals latent structure that may have more or fewer groups.
- Core assumption: User ratings and semantic similarity are not perfectly aligned.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the number of clusters may differ from the number of star ratings"
  - [section] "the number of underlying clusters in the dataset may be more than the number of labels in the dataset"
- Break condition: If ratings perfectly align with semantic clusters, the optimal number of clusters would match the rating categories.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the number of outliers identified by DBSCAN and HDBSCAN affect the validity of clustering performance comparisons?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that DBSCAN and HDBSCAN identify many outliers, which complicates interpretation and may inflate clustering quality metrics.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not quantify how outlier proportion impacts relative algorithm performance or whether outlier handling changes the ranking of best-performing embeddings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis with varying outlier thresholds or metrics adjusted for outlier proportion to assess impact on clustering validity.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would fine-tuning language models on the review dataset yield better clustering performance than using pretrained embeddings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that fine-tuning BERT and Word2Vec on the review dataset might result in embeddings better suited for clustering.
- Why unresolved: The study only uses pretrained embeddings without exploring fine-tuned alternatives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing clustering performance using fine-tuned vs. pretrained embeddings.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Is the number of underlying clusters in review data greater than the number of star ratings, and how does this affect clustering quality?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that density-based algorithms identify more clusters than star ratings, suggesting possible additional underlying groupings.
- Why unresolved: The study does not explore whether additional clusters correspond to meaningful review subcategories or are artifacts of clustering.
- What evidence would resolve it: Qualitative analysis of cluster content or additional metadata to validate whether extra clusters represent distinct review types.

## Limitations
- Single dataset used (1,177 Amazon product reviews) limits generalizability to other text domains
- Hyperparameter tuning may not have exhaustively explored the full parameter space
- Focus on three embedding types and four algorithms excludes other potentially relevant combinations
- No exploration of fine-tuned embeddings on the review dataset

## Confidence
- High confidence: The fundamental finding that embedding choice affects clustering performance is well-supported with multiple evaluation metrics
- Medium confidence: The superiority of DBSCAN/HDBSCAN over other algorithms, given the sensitivity to hyperparameter settings and outlier labeling
- Medium confidence: The claim about cluster numbers differing from star ratings, as this requires external validation that wasn't extensively detailed

## Next Checks
