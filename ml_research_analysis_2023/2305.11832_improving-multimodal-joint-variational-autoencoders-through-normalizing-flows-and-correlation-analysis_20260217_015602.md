# Improving Multimodal Joint Variational Autoencoders through Normalizing Flows and Correlation Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11832
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11832
- Reference count: 30
- Key outcome: JNF-DCCA achieves coherence precision > 0.79 vs < 0.6 for baselines, FID < 11 vs > 42, with scalable inference using Product of Experts

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning joint distributions across multiple modalities by proposing a multimodal variational autoencoder that uses Normalizing Flows to enrich unimodal posteriors and Deep Canonical Correlation Analysis (DCCA) to extract shared information. The method conditions unimodal posteriors on DCCA embeddings to improve cross-modal generation coherence while using Product of Experts for scalable inference from subsets of modalities. Experiments on MNIST-SVHN, CelebA-64, and a trimodal dataset demonstrate superior performance in coherence (precision), diversity (FID), and likelihood estimates compared to existing multimodal VAE approaches.

## Method Summary
The method consists of a joint encoder that processes all modalities together, separate decoders for each modality, and enriched unimodal posteriors using Masked Autoregressive Flows (MAF). During training, the model uses a two-step approach: first training the joint encoder and decoders, then training unimodal encoders conditioned on DCCA embeddings. At inference, Product of Experts (PoE) combines unimodal posteriors to enable sampling from any subset of modalities without requiring additional encoders. The DCCA embeddings extract shared information between modalities, which is used to condition the unimodal posteriors and restrict the latent space to relevant information for cross-modal generation.

## Key Results
- Coherence precision > 0.79 for JNF-DCCA vs < 0.6 for baseline models on MNIST-SVHN
- Diversity improvements with FID < 11 vs > 42 for some baselines
- Scalable inference demonstrated on trimodal dataset (MNIST, SVHN, FashionMNIST)
- Strong performance across multiple metrics: coherence, diversity, and likelihood estimates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Conditioning unimodal posteriors on DCCA embeddings improves coherence by restricting latent space to shared information.
- Mechanism: DCCA embeddings extract modality-shared information while filtering out modality-specific details. Conditioning posteriors on these embeddings ensures latent variables capture only the information needed for cross-modal generation, avoiding irrelevant background or style variations.
- Core assumption: The DCCA embeddings effectively separate shared from modality-specific information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The unimodal posteriors are conditioned on the Deep Canonical Correlation Analysis embeddings which preserve the shared information across modalities leading to more coherent cross-modal generations."
  - [section] "A second observation is that to generate a modality from another one we only need the information shared by both and not the entire data... Building on that intuition, in this paper we choose to use DCCA representations as such functions as it is a versatile method that can be applied to various datasets."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations to DCCA performance in this paper's context.
- Break condition: If DCCA fails to extract meaningful shared information (e.g., modalities are truly independent or DCCA capacity is insufficient), conditioning on embeddings provides no benefit and may degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Normalizing Flows enrich unimodal posteriors, improving diversity by modeling complex posterior distributions.
- Mechanism: Replacing restrictive Gaussian unimodal posteriors with Normalizing Flows (specifically Masked Autoregressive Flows) allows modeling of complex, multi-modal posterior distributions. This enables capturing true data structure rather than forcing Gaussian assumptions.
- Core assumption: The true posterior distributions are non-Gaussian and can be well-approximated by Normalizing Flows.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Furthermore, we use Normalizing Flows to enrich the unimodal posteriors and achieve more diverse data generation."
  - [section] "In order to properly estimate the true unimodal posterior distributions... the unimodal posteriors need a lot of flexibility... In our model, we propose to address this limitation by enriching these distributions using Normalizing Flows."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations to MAF performance in this paper's context.
- Break condition: If Normalizing Flows cannot adequately approximate the true posterior (e.g., insufficient flow depth, poor training), diversity improvements may not materialize.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Product of Experts (PoE) enables scalable inference for subset posteriors without additional encoders.
- Mechanism: Instead of introducing new encoders for each subset of modalities, PoE combines existing unimodal posteriors through multiplicative combination. This provides a principled approximation for p(z|(xi)i∈S) that scales to any number of modalities.
- Core assumption: Unimodal posteriors are good approximations of true unimodal posteriors, making their PoE a reasonable approximation of the joint posterior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "It has been argued... that the JMV AE is not a scalable model when more than two modalities are considered... In this paper, we argue that we can actually use a Product of Experts of well fitted unimodal posteriors to approximate it."
  - [section] "Assuming that the unimodal posteriors are such that qφi(z|xi) ≈ pθ(z|xi) we can use an approximation of pθ(z|(xi)i∈S) as follows: q(z|(xi)i∈S) ∝ ∏i∈Sqφi(z|xi)p(z)|S|−1."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations to PoE performance in this paper's context.
- Break condition: If unimodal posteriors are poorly fitted or the true joint posterior has complex structure not captured by PoE, subset posterior approximations will be inaccurate.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Variational Inference and Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)
  - Why needed here: The entire model training relies on maximizing ELBO for joint distribution learning and unimodal posterior fitting.
  - Quick check question: Can you derive the ELBO for a standard VAE and explain why it's a lower bound on log-likelihood?

- Concept: Normalizing Flows and density estimation
  - Why needed here: Understanding how Normalizing Flows transform simple distributions into complex ones and how to compute their densities is crucial for implementing the MAF-based unimodal posteriors.
  - Quick check question: How do you compute the density of z' = f(z) where f is an invertible transformation, and why is this efficient for certain flow architectures?

- Concept: Canonical Correlation Analysis and Deep CCA
  - Why needed here: DCCA is used to extract shared information between modalities, so understanding CCA's objective and how DCCA extends it to neural representations is essential.
  - Quick check question: What is the objective of CCA and how does DCCA modify this for neural network representations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Joint encoder qφ(z|X) -> Decoders pθ(xi|z) -> Unimodal encoders qφi(z|xi) or qφi(z|gi(xi)) -> DCCA encoders gi(xi) -> Product of Experts

- Critical path:
  1. Train joint encoder and decoders using ELBO
  2. Train DCCA encoders (if using DCCA variant)
  3. Train unimodal encoders using LJM objective
  4. For inference, use PoE sampling for subset posteriors

- Design tradeoffs:
  - DCCA vs no DCCA: DCCA improves coherence but adds training complexity and may not help if modalities are already well-aligned
  - MAF flow depth: More flows increase expressiveness but also training complexity and potential overfitting
  - PoE vs other aggregation: PoE is scalable but may not capture all joint posterior structure

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor coherence: Likely DCCA not extracting meaningful shared information or unimodal posteriors poorly fitted
  - Low diversity: MAF flows insufficient, or LJM objective too restrictive
  - Training instability: Learning rate too high, batch size too small, or modality collapse in PoE aggregation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on MNIST-SVHN with and without DCCA to verify coherence improvement on simple dataset
  2. Vary number of MAF flows to find sweet spot between expressiveness and training stability
  3. Test PoE sampling on trimodal dataset to verify scalability claim

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of JNF-DCCA change when using different methods for extracting shared information between modalities instead of DCCA?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "The general DCCA embedding used in this paper might be replaced by another method more specific to the type of data that is used to improve the results."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests DCCA for extracting shared information. It does not explore alternative methods or compare performance with other correlation analysis techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing JNF-DCCA performance using different methods for extracting shared information (e.g., Deep CCA variants, contrastive learning approaches) on the same datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of dimensions to retain in DCCA embeddings for different datasets and how does this choice impact model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions choosing 9 dimensions for MNIST-SVHN based on singular value analysis, but states "we also investigate how this choice impacts the performance of the JNF-DCCA model" in Appendix B.
- Why unresolved: While the paper investigates the impact of DCCA dimensions on MNIST-SVHN, it doesn't provide a systematic approach for determining optimal dimensions across different datasets or modalities.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study showing how to select optimal DCCA dimensions based on dataset characteristics, along with performance comparisons across multiple datasets using different dimensionalities.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the two-step training approach compare to other training strategies for multimodal VAEs in terms of convergence speed and final performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a two-step training approach and states "we decided to rely on a two-step training since it allows to mitigate the trade-off induced by the parameter α" but only compares it to one-step training with warmup.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares the two-step approach to one specific alternative (one-step with warmup). It doesn't explore other training strategies or provide comprehensive analysis of training dynamics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments evaluating multiple training strategies (e.g., joint training, progressive training, curriculum learning) on the same datasets, measuring both convergence speed and final performance metrics.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the JNF-DCCA model perform on multimodal datasets with more than three modalities or with very high-dimensional data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates scalability to three modalities (MNIST-SVHN-FashionMNIST) and mentions using DCCA to reduce dimensionality, but doesn't test with more than three modalities or extremely high-dimensional data.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to datasets with at most three modalities and moderate dimensionality. The paper doesn't address challenges that might arise with larger numbers of modalities or very high-dimensional inputs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing JNF-DCCA on datasets with 4+ modalities or very high-dimensional data (e.g., medical imaging with multiple scan types, video with audio), along with analysis of computational complexity and performance degradation patterns.

## Limitations

- Performance gains vary significantly across datasets, with stronger results on MNIST-SVHN than CelebA-64
- Critical architectural details (MADE block configurations, HMC sampling parameters) are not specified
- DCCA conditioning assumes clean separation of shared vs modality-specific information, which may not hold for all dataset pairs
- Limited analysis of when DCCA provides diminishing returns on already-aligned modalities

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core architectural contributions (MAF-enriched posteriors, PoE inference) are technically sound and the general training methodology is well-specified.
- **Medium confidence**: The reported performance improvements are substantial but depend heavily on unreported hyperparameters and architectural details that significantly impact results.
- **Low confidence**: The DCCA conditioning mechanism's effectiveness across diverse modality pairs is demonstrated but the paper lacks analysis of when this approach succeeds vs fails.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on DCCA necessity**: Train the model with and without DCCA conditioning on datasets where modalities are already highly aligned to determine when DCCA provides diminishing returns.

2. **Flow depth sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the number of MAF flows to identify the point of diminishing returns and potential overfitting, particularly on smaller datasets like MNIST-SVHN.

3. **PoE approximation quality**: Compare PoE-based subset posteriors against true subset posteriors (when computationally feasible) on the trimodal dataset to quantify approximation error and identify failure modes.
