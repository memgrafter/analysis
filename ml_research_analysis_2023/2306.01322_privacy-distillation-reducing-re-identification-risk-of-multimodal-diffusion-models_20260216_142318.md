# Privacy Distillation: Reducing Re-identification Risk of Multimodal Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01322
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01322
- Authors: [Not specified in source]
- Reference count: 13
- Key outcome: Privacy Distillation reduces re-identification risk by more than 3-fold while maintaining AUC 0.810 on 5-class pathology classification

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the risk of patient re-identification when sharing medical imaging data via generative models. The authors propose Privacy Distillation, a framework that trains a text-to-image diffusion model on synthetic data filtered to remove re-identifiable images, thereby reducing the risk of exposing identifiable patient information. The method involves training an initial model on real data, generating synthetic data, filtering it using a re-identification network to exclude images with high re-identification scores, and then training a second model on the filtered synthetic data. Experiments on chest X-ray data show that Privacy Distillation can effectively reduce re-identification risk while maintaining downstream classification performance.

## Method Summary
The Privacy Distillation framework trains a first diffusion model on real chest X-ray images with associated text reports, generates a synthetic dataset from this model, filters the synthetic data using a re-identification network to remove images with high re-identification scores, and trains a second diffusion model on the filtered synthetic data only. The re-identification network classifies whether two images belong to the same patient, capturing identity-relevant features beyond pixel similarity. Text-image alignment using CLIP-like similarity ensures synthetic images align with their conditioning text before training the final distilled model.

## Key Results
- Re-identification ratio reduced from 22.3% to 7.2% after filtering (more than 3-fold reduction)
- Downstream classification performance maintained with AUC of 0.810 on 5-class pathology classification
- FID scores remained comparable between models trained on real data vs. filtered synthetic data
- Privacy Distillation more effective than pixel-level similarity filtering approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training a second diffusion model on filtered synthetic data reduces re-identification risk while maintaining classification utility.
- Mechanism: The first model memorizes patient-specific features from real data. The filtering step removes images with high re-identification scores before training the second model, preventing memorization of identifiable features.
- Core assumption: Re-identification risk is primarily due to model memorization of specific patient features during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our solution consists of (1) training a first diffusion model on real data (2) generating a synthetic dataset using this model and filtering it to exclude images with a re-identifiability risk (3) training a second diffusion model on the filtered synthetic data only."
  - [section]: "We hypothesise that synthetic images can enable re-identification due to model memorisation."
- Break condition: If the filtering threshold δ is set too high, useful synthetic data may be removed, reducing downstream performance. If too low, identifiable data may remain.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using a re-identification network (f_re-id) rather than pixel-level similarity better captures identity-based memorisation.
- Mechanism: The re-identification network learns to classify whether two images belong to the same patient, capturing identity-relevant features beyond pixel similarity.
- Core assumption: Identity memorisation involves high-level patient-specific features that pixel-based metrics cannot capture.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Instead of pixel-based (Carlini et al., 2023) or structural-based (Kumar et al., 2017) similarities, we measure identity with a deep model ℓ = f_re-id"
  - [section]: "The model is trained to classify images as belonging to the same patient or not."
- Break condition: If the re-identification network is poorly trained or not representative of actual re-identification risks, the filtering may be ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Contrastive reranking using CLIP-like similarity ensures synthetic images align with their conditioning text.
- Mechanism: After filtering for privacy, synthetic images are reranked based on text-image alignment to maintain dataset quality for downstream tasks.
- Core assumption: Text-image alignment is crucial for maintaining downstream task performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We leverage CXR-BERT... for the text encoder... An alignment score salign = f_im2tex_θ (ˆxi, ci) is computed between an image and a text prompt"
  - [section]: "We also need to ensure that the images in D_filtered used for training ϵθ_distill correspond to their conditioning."
- Break condition: If the text-image alignment metric is poorly correlated with downstream task performance, this step may not improve utility.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Real dataset (D_real) -> First diffusion model (θ_real) -> Synthetic dataset (D_synth) -> Re-identification network (f_re-id) -> Filtering step -> Text-image alignment model (f_im2tex) -> Second diffusion model (θ_distill) -> Downstream classifier

- Critical path:
  1. Train first diffusion model on real data
  2. Generate synthetic dataset
  3. Filter using re-identification network
  4. Rerank using text-image alignment
  5. Train second diffusion model on filtered data
  6. Evaluate downstream performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Privacy vs. utility: Stricter filtering (higher δ) increases privacy but may reduce downstream performance
  - Re-identification network accuracy vs. computational cost
  - Text-image alignment metric choice affects downstream task relevance

- Failure signatures:
  - High re-identification ratio in distilled model indicates filtering threshold too low
  - Poor downstream performance suggests over-filtering or text-image alignment issues
  - High FID score but low re-identification ratio may indicate loss of dataset diversity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train first model on subset of data and measure re-identification ratio vs. dataset size
  2. Apply filtering with different δ thresholds and measure impact on re-identification ratio and downstream performance
  3. Compare re-identification network-based filtering vs. pixel-level similarity filtering

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. What is the optimal trade-off between privacy and downstream model utility when adjusting the filtering threshold (δ) in Privacy Distillation?
2. How does the Privacy Distillation method perform when applied to other imaging modalities beyond chest X-rays?
3. What are the long-term effects of iteratively applying Privacy Distillation on the quality and privacy of synthetic data?

## Limitations
- Limited to chest X-ray data from MIMIC-CXR, limiting generalizability to other medical imaging modalities
- Evaluation focuses primarily on a single downstream classification task, leaving questions about broader applicability
- Dependence on the quality of the re-identification network, which is trained on the same dataset used for downstream tasks

## Confidence
- High confidence in core methodology and experimental results, though some implementation details remain underspecified
