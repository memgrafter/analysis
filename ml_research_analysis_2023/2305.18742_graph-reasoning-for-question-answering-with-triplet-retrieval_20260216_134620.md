# Graph Reasoning for Question Answering with Triplet Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18742
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18742
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: Achieves up to 4.6% absolute accuracy improvement over state-of-the-art methods on CommonsenseQA and OpenbookQA

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a simple yet effective method for knowledge graph (KG)-enhanced multi-choice question answering (MCQA) that converts KG triplets into natural language passages and uses standard text retrieval methods. The approach first linearizes KG triplets from ConceptNet into passages, then retrieves relevant passages using a hybrid of BM25 and dense passage retrievers (DPR). Retrieved passages are reranked using a pre-trained cross-encoder before being concatenated with questions and fed into a language model for final answer selection. Experiments show this method outperforms state-of-the-art KG-based QA systems by up to 4.6% accuracy without requiring specialized KG encoders trained from scratch.

## Method Summary
The method converts KG triplets into natural language passages using template-based linearization, then applies hybrid retrieval combining BM25 and DPR to find relevant passages for each question-choice pair. Retrieved passages are reranked using a pre-trained cross-encoder, and the top K passages are concatenated with the question and choices before being processed by a fine-tuned RoBERTa-large language model to select the correct answer. The approach is designed to be simple and effective, leveraging standard text retrieval and pre-trained models rather than requiring specialized KG encoders or complex graph neural networks.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on CommonsenseQA and OpenbookQA benchmarks
- Outperforms existing methods by up to 4.6% absolute accuracy
- Ablation studies show hybrid retrieval contributes up to 3.52% improvement, reranking adds 1.10-4.00% depending on dataset
- Method is simple, effective, and does not require extra KG encoders trained from scratch

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Converting KG triplets into natural language passages enables better retrieval performance than raw graph encoding
- Mechanism: The linearization process transforms structured triplet data into continuous text that can be indexed and retrieved using standard text retrieval methods (BM25, DPR) without requiring specialized graph encoders
- Core assumption: Natural language passages retain sufficient semantic information from the original triplets for effective retrieval and reasoning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose a simple yet effective method to first retrieve the most relevant triplets from KGs and then rerank them, which are then concatenated with questions to be fed into language models"
  - [section 2.2]: "For each triplet p ∈ P , it has a head entity h, a relation r and a tail entity t. We map r into rp and d is formed by linearly concatenating <h, rp, t>"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct comparison between passage-based and graph-encoding approaches found in neighbor papers

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Hybrid retrieval combining sparse (BM25) and dense (DPR) methods captures complementary information for better performance
- Mechanism: BM25 provides keyword matching for exact term overlap while DPR captures semantic similarity through learned embeddings, together covering both lexical and semantic matching needs
- Core assumption: The two retrieval methods capture orthogonal aspects of relevance, making them complementary rather than redundant
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3]: "We retrieve passages from corpus D for an MCQA example (q, C) with hybrid (i.e., both sparse and dense) retrievers since they are complementary"
  - [section 3.3]: "When removing BM25, results on CommonsenseQA and OpenbookQA drop significantly up to 3.52%. When removing DPR, the result on CommonsenseQA drops slightly while the result on OpenbookQA drops 3.20%"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct analysis of hybrid retrieval effectiveness found in neighbor papers

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Reranking retrieved passages with cross-encoders improves final QA performance by selecting more relevant knowledge
- Mechanism: Cross-encoders evaluate query-passage pairs holistically, capturing interactions between question and passage that the dual-encoder DPR cannot, leading to better passage selection
- Core assumption: The reranking step can effectively distinguish between similarly scored passages from the initial retrieval
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.4]: "We further rerank 2N passages retrieved by hybrid retriever with pre-trained cross-encoder"
  - [section 3.3]: "when further removing reranking module, model performance on CommonsenseQA drops 1.10% and on OpenbookQA drops 4.00% accuracy"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct analysis of reranking effectiveness found in neighbor papers

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Knowledge Graph → Triplet Linearization → Passage Corpus
  - Question + Choice → Hybrid Retriever (BM25 + DPR) → Top Passages
  - Top Passages → Reranker (Cross-Encoder) → Top K Passages
  - Question + Choice + Top K Passages → Language Model → Answer Score

- Critical path: The most critical path is: Question → Hybrid Retrieval → Reranking → Language Model Reasoning, as errors compound through each stage

- Design tradeoffs: Using natural language passages simplifies retrieval but loses explicit graph structure; hybrid retrieval adds complexity but improves coverage; reranking adds computational cost but improves quality

- Failure signatures: 
  - Poor retrieval performance suggests issues with linearization templates or retrieval parameters
  - Reranking not improving results suggests mismatch between cross-encoder and retrieval task
  - Language model not improving with passages suggests poor passage selection or insufficient context

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test retrieval quality: Run a sample of questions through retrieval only and manually check if relevant passages are found
  2. Test reranking effectiveness: Compare passage rankings before and after reranking on a validation set
  3. Test end-to-end performance: Run full pipeline on a small subset to verify all components work together correctly

## Limitations

- The method is constrained to multi-choice question answering systems and limited to commonsense reasoning tasks
- Performance may degrade when knowledge graphs lack relevant information for certain questions
- The approach loses explicit graph structure through linearization, potentially missing important relational patterns

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that hybrid retrieval improves performance is well-supported by the ablation results showing consistent gains across both datasets. The mechanism is straightforward and the evidence is direct.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the reranking step has reasonable support from the ablation study, though the magnitude of improvement varies significantly between datasets (1.10% on CSQA vs 4.00% on OBQA). This suggests the reranking may be more critical for certain question types.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that this approach "outperforms state-of-the-art methods by up to 4.6% absolute accuracy" lacks context about whether competing methods used similar KG encoders or retrieval strategies. Without a controlled comparison using identical knowledge sources and evaluation protocols, these absolute numbers are difficult to validate.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation of retrieval complementarity**: Conduct a controlled experiment where BM25 and DPR retrieve from the same set of passages (rather than their respective top-100), then analyze whether hybrid retrieval still provides benefits when operating on identical retrieval pools.

2. **Direct comparison with graph-based methods**: Implement a baseline that performs retrieval using graph embeddings or graph neural networks on the original triplet structure, comparing performance directly against the passage-based approach on identical knowledge graph subsets.

3. **Template sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the linearization templates (different relation phrase mappings, different ordering of elements) and measure the impact on retrieval performance to determine whether the specific template choices significantly affect results.
