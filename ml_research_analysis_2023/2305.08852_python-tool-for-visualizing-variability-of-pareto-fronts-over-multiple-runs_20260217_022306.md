# Python Tool for Visualizing Variability of Pareto Fronts over Multiple Runs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.08852
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08852
- **Reference count:** 4

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Python package designed to address the challenge of evaluating the stability of multi-objective optimization (MOO) methods. The key innovation is the visualization of Pareto front variability across multiple runs, accounting for the randomness introduced by different random seeds. By leveraging the concept of empirical attainment surface (EAS), the tool provides a means to visualize uncertainty and stability in MOO results, which is often overlooked in traditional evaluations.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a Python package that utilizes the empirical attainment surface (EAS) to visualize the variability of Pareto fronts over multiple independent runs of multi-objective optimization algorithms. Users provide an array of results from these runs, and the package generates visualizations that capture the uncertainty and stability of the Pareto front solutions. Additionally, the package offers functionality to plot hypervolume (HV) over time, providing insights into the optimization process's progression. The tool is implemented in Python and is available on GitHub.

## Key Results
- Introduces a Python package for visualizing Pareto front variability using EAS
- Enables visualization of uncertainty in MOO results over multiple runs
- Provides optional plotting of hypervolume over time for additional insights

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The tool works by employing the empirical attainment surface (EAS) concept, which is a statistical method for analyzing the distribution of Pareto front solutions across multiple runs. EAS captures the variability and uncertainty inherent in stochastic optimization processes by considering the frequency of attainment of different regions of the objective space. This approach allows for a more comprehensive evaluation of MOO methods by accounting for the randomness introduced by different random seeds.
