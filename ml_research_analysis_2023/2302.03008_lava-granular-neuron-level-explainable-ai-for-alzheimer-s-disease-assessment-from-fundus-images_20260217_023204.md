# LAVA: Granular Neuron-Level Explainable AI for Alzheimer's Disease Assessment from Fundus Images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03008
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03008
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: 75% accuracy in binary AD/NC classification from retinal fundus images

## Executive Summary
This paper presents LAVA, a novel model-agnostic XAI framework that assesses the Alzheimer's Disease (AD) continuum directly from retinal fundus images without clinical evaluation. The framework uses neuron-level explanations to identify critical neurons in CNN models and employs hierarchical clustering to discover sub-classes within the AD spectrum. By leveraging the strong correlation between retinal vascular changes and AD pathology, LAVA provides interpretable assessments of disease progression while maintaining competitive classification accuracy.

## Method Summary
LAVA operates through a multi-stage pipeline: first, a VGG-16 binary classifier distinguishes AD from normal control subjects using pre-processed retinal fundus images with vessel segmentation via AutoMorph. The framework then employs neuron probing with ε-SVR+RFE feature selection to identify critical neurons that contribute most to classification decisions. Finally, hierarchical agglomerative clustering with connectivity constraints is applied to the activation patterns of these critical neurons to discover sub-classes within the AD continuum, which are validated against cognitive test scores and vascular features.

## Key Results
- Achieved 75% accuracy in binary AD/NC classification on UK Biobank dataset
- Identified seven sub-groups across the AD continuum through hierarchical clustering
- Demonstrated strong correlations between cognitive test scores and vascular features
- Showed that neuron-level explanations provide richer interpretability than input-level saliency maps

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Neuron-level explanations provide richer interpretability than input-level saliency maps because they capture abstract feature interactions learned by CNNs. By probing activation patterns of critical neurons rather than raw pixels, the framework reveals how learned features interact and correlate, which is essential for understanding complex medical patterns.

### Mechanism 2
Hierarchical clustering on neuron activation patterns reveals latent disease sub-classes that correlate with cognitive test scores and vascular features. Critical neuron activations form a new feature space where samples with similar activation patterns are clustered, revealing disease continuum stages that align with clinical measures.

### Mechanism 3
The ε-SVR+RFE feature selection method effectively identifies critical neurons by maximizing likelihood estimation of mutual information with respect to output labels. Support vector regression assigns contribution coefficients to neurons, and recursive feature elimination iteratively prunes least important neurons until desired criticality is achieved.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Mutual Information (MI) for feature selection
  - Why needed here: MI quantifies the dependency between neuron activations and disease labels, enabling identification of neurons most informative about AD progression
  - Quick check question: What does high mutual information between a neuron and the output label indicate about that neuron's relevance?

- Concept: Hierarchical Agglomerative Clustering (HAC) with connectivity constraints
  - Why needed here: HAC discovers natural groupings in the neuron activation space that correspond to disease continuum stages, while connectivity constraints prevent merging of dissimilar clusters
  - Quick check question: How does the k-nearest neighbor graph constrain the clustering process in semi-supervised learning?

- Concept: Support Vector Regression (SVR) for coefficient-based feature importance
  - Why needed here: SVR provides a principled way to estimate how much each neuron contributes to the model's predictions through its coefficient values
  - Quick check question: What role does the regularization parameter C play in balancing model complexity and training accuracy in SVR?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data Acquisition → Classification (VGG-16) → Neuron Probing (ε-SVR+RFE) → Granularity Explanation (HAC) → Continuum Assessment
- Critical path: The neuron probing and granularity explanation phases are critical - errors here propagate to incorrect continuum assessment
- Design tradeoffs: Using intermediate layers vs. early layers for critical neuron extraction; balancing number of clusters vs. granularity level
- Failure signatures: Low Jaccard similarity between cross-validation models suggests unstable critical neuron selection; poor correlation between clusters and cognitive measures indicates model misalignment
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test neuron probing on a simple CNN with synthetic data to verify critical neuron identification
  2. Apply HAC on the activation space and visualize dendrogram to confirm appropriate number of clusters
  3. Validate that identified clusters correlate with external clinical measures using statistical tests

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the LAVA framework effectively differentiate between Alzheimer's Disease and other neurodegenerative diseases using retinal fundus images?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that subjects with mixed dementia and other neurodegenerative diseases were excluded from the study, but it does not explore the framework's ability to differentiate between AD and other conditions.
- Why unresolved: The study focused solely on AD classification and did not investigate the framework's performance on other neurodegenerative diseases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a study that includes subjects with various neurodegenerative diseases and evaluating the LAVA framework's ability to accurately classify AD and differentiate it from other conditions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the LAVA framework perform when applied to retinal fundus images from diverse populations with different demographic and genetic backgrounds?
- Basis in paper: The study used the UK Biobank dataset, which may not be representative of diverse populations. The paper does not discuss the framework's performance on data from different demographic and genetic backgrounds.
- Why unresolved: The study's dataset limitations and lack of discussion on the framework's generalizability to diverse populations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the LAVA framework on retinal fundus images from diverse populations and comparing its performance across different demographic and genetic backgrounds.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the LAVA framework be used to predict the progression of Alzheimer's Disease in individuals over time?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the framework's ability to identify sub-classes within the AD spectrum and its potential to assign an AD-score to subjects. However, it does not explore the framework's ability to predict disease progression over time.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on identifying sub-classes and assigning AD-scores but did not investigate the framework's ability to predict disease progression.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a longitudinal study that tracks individuals with AD over time and evaluates the LAVA framework's ability to predict disease progression based on retinal fundus images.

## Limitations

- Dataset size is relatively small (200 samples) for deep learning applications, limiting generalizability
- The framework relies on binary AD/NC classification, which oversimplifies the complex AD continuum
- Vascular feature extraction depends on AutoMorph segmentation accuracy, which may introduce errors
- The clustering methodology assumes that neuron activation patterns consistently reflect disease progression across subjects

## Confidence

- High confidence in the binary classification accuracy (75%) due to cross-validation validation
- Medium confidence in neuron-level explanations as superior to pixel-level methods, requiring further empirical comparison
- Medium confidence in the seven-cluster AD continuum identification, pending external validation
- Low confidence in the clinical significance of identified sub-groups without larger independent validation

## Next Checks

1. Cross-dataset validation: Test the framework on independent retinal fundus datasets (e.g., from different institutions or countries) to verify robustness and generalizability
2. Longitudinal analysis: Apply the framework to longitudinal data to confirm that identified clusters track disease progression over time
3. Clinical correlation validation: Conduct statistical analysis with additional clinical biomarkers (CSF, PET scans) to strengthen the relationship between vascular features and AD pathology
