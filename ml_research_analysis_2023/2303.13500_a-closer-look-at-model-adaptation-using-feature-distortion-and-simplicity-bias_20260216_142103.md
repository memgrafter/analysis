# A Closer Look at Model Adaptation using Feature Distortion and Simplicity Bias

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13500
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: Mitigating feature distortion alone is insufficient for safe model adaptation; complementary simplicity bias mitigation improves both safety and generalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates model adaptation protocols for pretrained models, focusing on their ability to generalize well and safely. The authors identify that while feature distortion mitigation is a common goal in adaptation protocols, it is not sufficient for safe adaptation. They introduce the concept of simplicity bias—the tendency of models to favor simpler decision boundaries—as a complementary perspective that significantly impacts safety and generalization. Through experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets, they demonstrate that different adaptation protocols exhibit varying susceptibility to simplicity bias. To address this, they propose three modified linear probing strategies that effectively mitigate both feature distortion and simplicity bias, leading to improved performance across multiple safety and generalization metrics.

## Method Summary
The paper studies adaptation protocols for pretrained models, focusing on their ability to generalize well and safely while mitigating feature distortion and simplicity bias. The authors use synthetic dominoes dataset, CIFAR-10, DomainNet, and Living17 datasets with pretrained models (MoCo-V2 ResNet-50, CLIP ResNet-50). They evaluate ID and OOD accuracy, calibration error, robustness to corruptions and adversarial attacks, and anomaly detection performance. The method involves implementing LP, FT, and LP+FT protocols, along with modified LP+FT variants using uncertainty-driven perturbations, virtual adversarial training, and model-soups. The proposed strategies aim to address both feature distortion and simplicity bias during the adaptation process.

## Key Results
- Mitigating feature distortion alone is insufficient for safe model adaptation
- Simplicity bias significantly impacts both safety and generalization performance
- Modified LP+FT strategies effectively mitigate both feature distortion and simplicity bias
- Applying mitigation strategies during the LP step is more effective than during FT

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that feature distortion and simplicity bias are distinct phenomena affecting model adaptation. While feature distortion concerns the preservation of useful representations during fine-tuning, simplicity bias relates to the model's preference for simpler decision boundaries that may not generalize well. By addressing both issues simultaneously through modified adaptation protocols, models achieve better balance between fitting the training data and maintaining generalization capability. The uncertainty-driven perturbations and model-soups approaches help create more robust representations that are less susceptible to both forms of bias.

## Foundational Learning
- **Feature Distortion**: The degradation of pretrained features during adaptation, which can harm generalization performance. Understanding this concept is crucial because it's one of the primary challenges in model adaptation.
- **Simplicity Bias**: The tendency of models to favor simpler decision boundaries, which can lead to poor generalization on complex or out-of-distribution data. This concept is needed to understand why feature distortion mitigation alone is insufficient.
- **Linear Probing (LP)**: A protocol where only the final classification layer is trained while keeping pretrained features frozen. This serves as a baseline for understanding feature preservation.
- **Fine-Tuning (FT)**: The process of training the entire model (or parts of it) on new data, which can introduce feature distortion if not properly controlled.
- **Model Soups**: Combining multiple adapted models to create more robust predictions, which helps mitigate individual model biases.

**Design Tradeoffs**
- LP preserves features but may underfit complex tasks
- FT can adapt better to new tasks but risks feature distortion
- LP+FT balances both but introduces complexity in hyperparameter tuning
- Simplicity bias mitigation adds computational overhead but improves safety

**Failure Signatures**
- High ID accuracy but poor OOD performance indicates simplicity bias
- Calibration errors suggest feature distortion or overconfidence
- Poor robustness to corruptions/adversarial attacks indicates insufficient safety measures
