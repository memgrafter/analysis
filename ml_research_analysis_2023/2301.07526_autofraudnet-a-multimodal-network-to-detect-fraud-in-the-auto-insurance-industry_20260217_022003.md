# AutoFraudNet: A Multimodal Network to Detect Fraud in the Auto Insurance Industry

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07526
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07526
- Reference count: 5
- We present a multimodal learning framework, AutoFraudNet, to detect fraudulent auto-insurance claims, achieving over 3% improvement in PR AUC through slow fusion of visual, textual, and tabular modalities.

## Executive Summary
AutoFraudNet is a multimodal learning framework designed to detect fraudulent auto-insurance claims by leveraging visual, textual, and tabular data. The framework employs a cascaded slow fusion paradigm combined with BLOCK Tucker fusion blocks to effectively integrate heterogeneous data modalities. Through extensive experiments on a real-world dataset, AutoFraudNet demonstrates superior performance compared to unimodal and bimodal approaches, with particular emphasis on addressing the severe class imbalance inherent in fraud detection tasks.

## Method Summary
AutoFraudNet processes multimodal insurance claims through specialized pipelines: visual damage images through in-house pre-trained CNNs (CDS, UD), Japanese claim text through fine-tuned BERT, and tabular metadata with visibility scores. The framework employs a cascaded slow fusion architecture where features are progressively merged in two fusion layers, using BLOCK Tucker blocks for complex cross-modal interactions. Training incorporates class-balanced sampling and multi-head auxiliary losses to provide intermediate supervision, with optimization using cross-entropy loss and Adam optimizer with early stopping.

## Key Results
- AutoFraudNet achieves over 3% improvement in PR AUC compared to unimodal and bimodal baselines
- Visual and tabular modalities show complementary strengths, with bimodal fusion outperforming individual modalities
- BLOCK Tucker fusion strategy outperforms simpler concatenation methods in bimodal experiments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Slow fusion cascades improve cross-modal reasoning by allowing early modality-specific processing before interaction
- Mechanism: Features from different modalities are progressively merged in a staged fashion, reducing the complexity of direct joint training
- Core assumption: Modalities benefit from individual encoding before fusion
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our framework utilizes a cascaded slow fusion framework and state-of-the-art fusion block, BLOCK Tucker, to alleviate the challenges of joint-training."
  - [section 4.3] "To combat these problems, we use slow-fusion (SF), a common paradigm in which features from various modalities are gradually fused together in a cascaded manner"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors focus on medical claims or fraud detection, not slow fusion architecture
- Break condition: If early-stage concatenation leads to gradient vanishing or modality dominance issues

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: BLOCK Tucker fusion outperforms simpler concatenation by learning higher-order interactions between modalities
- Mechanism: Uses a bilinear tensor decomposition to capture complex cross-modal relationships
- Core assumption: Second-order interactions are more informative than linear combinations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "To effectively fuse the different modalities, we leverage a cascaded slow fusion paradigm along with a state-of-the-art fusion block, BLOCK Tucker."
  - [section 4.2] "Following the success of popular fusion strategies, we conduct extensive bimodal experiments using the canonical versions of (i) Concat MLP, (ii) Linear Sum, (iii) BLOCK, (iv) BLOCK Tucker..."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus contains no direct references to BLOCK Tucker or tensor-based fusion methods
- Break condition: If data dimensionality is too low to justify tensor decomposition overhead

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-head auxiliary losses improve convergence by providing intermediate supervision signals
- Mechanism: Intermediate fusion outputs are used to train separate classification heads alongside the final output
- Core assumption: Earlier intermediate representations contain useful discriminative information
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "Inspired by the idea behind the inception network... we introduce a further improvement: each of AF1,AF2 is additionally fed into a separate classification head to provide more granular supervision during training."
  - [section 4.3] "Combining all the losses computed from the three classification layers, the overall optimization objective becomes: L = arg minθ{LF1 + LF2 + LC}"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus references to multi-head auxiliary losses
- Break condition: If auxiliary heads introduce conflicting gradients that destabilize training

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Visual pipeline: Multiple images → CDS/UD CNNs → claim-level embeddings (ACDS, A_UD)
  - Tabular pipeline: Structured metadata → one-hot encoding (A_Struct); Visibility scores → statistical aggregation (A_SPUD)
  - Fusion layer 1: Bimodal fusion of (CDS, SPUD) and (UD, Struct) using BLOCK Tucker
  - Fusion layer 2: Concatenation of intermediate features + single FC layer
  - Optional auxiliary heads: Separate classification heads on intermediate fusion outputs

- Critical path: Image → CDS/UD → bimodal fusion → intermediate features → multimodal fusion → softmax prediction

- Design tradeoffs: Multi-head auxiliary losses improve performance but increase parameter count slightly; slow fusion reduces overfitting but may limit early cross-modal interaction

- Failure signatures: Poor fraud detection recall despite high overall accuracy; overfitting indicated by validation PR AUC plateauing while training PR AUC continues to improve

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train unimodal baselines for each feature (CDS, UD, SPUD, Struct, Text) to establish individual performance
  2. Conduct bimodal experiments pairing visual with tabular features using different fusion strategies
  3. Implement slow fusion framework with BLOCK Tucker in first layer and test various strategies for second layer

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would AutoFraudNet perform on claims data from other countries or insurance types (e.g., health, property)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors use a Japanese auto insurance dataset and note that their framework "asserts the availability of all modalities" but does not address how it would perform with different data characteristics.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates performance on one dataset type and does not conduct cross-domain or cross-type experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing AutoFraudNet on multi-national auto insurance datasets and non-auto insurance fraud datasets while reporting PR AUC and other metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would integrating attention mechanisms improve interpretability and potentially performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention in the limitations that "our framework lacks explainability in its current form" and suggest "adoption of attention maps to highlight which parts of an image, or which words/phrases of textual comments, triggered an alarm" as future work.
- Why unresolved: The current model architecture does not include any attention or explainability components.
- What evidence would resolve it: Adding attention mechanisms to AutoFraudNet and comparing both interpretability metrics and fraud detection performance against the current version.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does AutoFraudNet handle claims with missing modalities, and what is the performance impact?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that "our framework asserts the availability of all modalities by dropping claims with missing features" and note that handling missing modalities is a limitation they plan to address.
- Why unresolved: The current implementation cannot process incomplete claims, which are common in real-world scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing a gated or imputation-based approach to handle missing modalities and comparing performance on incomplete datasets versus the current drop-based approach.

## Limitations

- The proprietary dataset prevents independent validation of the reported performance gains and claimed 3% PR AUC improvement
- Reliance on in-house pre-trained CNNs (CDS, UD) and specific Japanese fine-tuning of BERT creates reproducibility barriers
- The framework requires all modalities to be present, dropping incomplete claims and limiting real-world applicability

## Confidence

- High confidence: The unimodal and bimodal experimental comparisons, demonstrating complementary nature of modalities
- Medium confidence: The architectural claims around slow fusion and BLOCK Tucker effectiveness, limited by lack of ablation studies
- Low confidence: The generalizability of results to other fraud detection domains due to dataset specificity
