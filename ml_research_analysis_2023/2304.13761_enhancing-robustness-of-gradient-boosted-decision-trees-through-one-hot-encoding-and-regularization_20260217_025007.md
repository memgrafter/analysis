# Enhancing Robustness of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees through One-Hot Encoding and Regularization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.13761
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13761
- **Reference count**: 5
- **Primary result**: GBDT OHE with regularization improves robustness against covariate perturbations while maintaining or improving performance compared to traditional GBDT models.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the vulnerability of Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT) to small covariate perturbations in unseen data. The authors propose GBDT OHE (One-Hot-Encoded GBDT), a novel approach that transforms GBDT models into a linear framework using one-hot encoding. This transformation enables the application of linear regression regularization techniques to enhance model robustness. The method involves refitting GBDT models with L1 or L2 regularization, which has been shown to improve robustness while maintaining or improving predictive performance. Numerical experiments on real datasets demonstrate significant improvements in robustness against covariate perturbations compared to traditional GBDT models.

## Method Summary
The GBDT OHE method converts GBDT models into a linear regression form by encoding each leaf in the trees as a dummy variable through one-hot encoding. This transformation allows the application of linear regression techniques and regularization methods (L1 or L2) to the GBDT model. The refitted model with regularization aims to reduce sensitivity to small perturbations in input data while controlling model complexity. The approach leverages a risk decomposition method to analyze and improve robustness by balancing bias, variance, and perturbation terms. The method was evaluated on real-world datasets including Airfoil, California Housing, and Bike Sharing data.

## Key Results
- GBDT OHE models with regularization significantly improve robustness against covariate perturbations compared to traditional GBDT models
- Models with appropriate regularization sizes can maintain or improve the performance of tuned XGBoost models
- Larger regularization sizes provide more robust predictions while smaller sizes preserve original model performance
- The method effectively balances model bias, variance, and perturbation terms to enhance overall robustness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: One-hot encoding converts GBDT models into a linear regression form, enabling the application of linear regression regularization techniques.
- Mechanism: By encoding each leaf in a GBDT as a dummy variable, the prediction of the GBDT can be represented as a linear combination of these leaf indicators. This transformation allows the use of L1 or L2 regularization on the leaf coefficients.
- Core assumption: Each leaf in the GBDT can be uniquely identified and encoded as a separate feature without loss of model expressiveness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Equation (7) indicates that the final model FM(xi) can be viewed as a linear model, with each 1Rjm(xi) as a feature (dummy variable)."
  - [section] "Each φk,k > 0 is an indicator function of one unique leaf in the GBDT model and φ0 is a vector of all ones representing the constant term."
- Break condition: If the number of leaves becomes extremely large relative to the number of samples, the model may become computationally infeasible or overfit despite regularization.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Regularization improves GBDT robustness by reducing the sensitivity of the model to small perturbations in the input data.
- Mechanism: The risk decomposition shows that the model variance and perturbation terms are both affected by regularization. L1 regularization can control perturbation by adding a penalty proportional to the absolute values of the weights, while L2 regularization can control perturbation by adding a penalty proportional to the sum of the squares of the weights.
- Core assumption: The perturbation term can be effectively controlled through regularization without significantly increasing the model bias.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We further introduce GBDT OHE with regularization to refit and regularize a GBDT model, and provide both theoretical and numerical results of its impact on model robustness and performance."
  - [section] "Figure 6 shows the results. In the Figure, curve labelled 'Sum up' shows the sum of the bias squared term, variance term, and perturbation term."
- Break condition: If the regularization strength is too high, the model bias may increase significantly, offsetting the gains in robustness.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The proposed method can maintain or improve the performance of the original GBDT model while enhancing its robustness.
- Mechanism: By refitting the GBDT model with regularization, the method can decrease the in-class bias and variance terms of the original GBDT model. This is achieved by finding a trade-off between model complexity and robustness, which can lead to better generalization on unseen data.
- Core assumption: The refitting process with regularization can effectively reduce the in-class bias and variance without significantly increasing the model misspecification bias.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "GBDT OHE models with relatively small regularization sizes can keep or improve the performance of the tuned XGBoost models XGB or XGB reg."
  - [section] "GBDT one-hot encoding models with relatively larger regularization sizes can provide more robust predictions."
- Break condition: If the refitting process is not properly tuned, it may lead to overfitting or underfitting, which can degrade the model's performance and robustness.

  - High model bias: Indicates that the regularization strength is too high

  - Poor generalization: Indicates that the model is not robust enough to handle unseen data
