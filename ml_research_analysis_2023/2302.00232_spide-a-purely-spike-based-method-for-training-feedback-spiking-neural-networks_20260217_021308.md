# SPIDE: A Purely Spike-based Method for Training Feedback Spiking Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00232
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00232
- Reference count: 12
- Key outcome: Introduces SPIDE, achieving competitive accuracy on MNIST (99.37%), CIFAR-10 (89.61%), CIFAR-100 (64.07%), and CIFAR10-DVS (60.7%) with claimed 100× energy reduction potential

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SPIDE, a purely spike-based training method for spiking neural networks (SNNs) that extends implicit differentiation on equilibrium states (IDE) by solving implicit differentiation through spike-based computation. The method uses coupled neurons to represent negative spikes and adjusts reset potentials to reduce approximation errors. SPIDE achieves competitive classification performance across standard benchmarks while demonstrating high energy efficiency through sparse firing and low-latency training.

## Method Summary
SPIDE extends implicit differentiation on equilibrium states (IDE) by solving implicit differentiation through spike-based computation. The key innovation involves using coupled neurons to represent negative spikes, enabling ternary spiking neuron dynamics. The method also adjusts reset potential to reduce approximation errors and enable training with fewer time steps. By maintaining purely spike-driven computation throughout training, SPIDE eliminates the need for conventional backpropagation and its associated computational overhead.

## Key Results
- Achieves 99.37% accuracy on MNIST, 89.61% on CIFAR-10, 64.07% on CIFAR-100, and 60.7% on CIFAR10-DVS
- Demonstrates high energy efficiency through sparse firing patterns
- Enables low-latency training by achieving equilibrium states with fewer time steps
- Shows theoretical potential for 100× energy reduction compared to conventional training methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SPIDE works by leveraging equilibrium states in spiking neural networks and solving implicit differentiation through spike-based computation. The coupled neuron approach allows representation of negative values through spike interactions, while reset potential adjustment minimizes approximation errors during training. This creates a self-consistent training loop where all computations, including gradient calculation, occur through spike dynamics rather than conventional numerical methods.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Coupling neurons increases parameter count but enables negative spike representation
- Reset potential adjustment improves accuracy but requires careful tuning
- Pure spike-based computation reduces energy but may limit precision

### Failure Signatures
- Inability to reach equilibrium states within time steps
- Unstable coupling dynamics leading to divergence
- Approximation errors accumulating during training

## Limitations
- Theoretical energy reduction claims are based on computational complexity rather than measured energy consumption
- Scalability to larger, more complex networks beyond standard benchmarks remains unclear
- Coupling mechanism complexity may affect training stability in practical implementations
- Low-latency training assumptions may not generalize across all network configurations
