# Enhancing Contrastive Learning with Noise-Guided Attack: Towards Continual Relation Extraction in the Wild

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.07085
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07085
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Primary result**: NaCL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on FewRel and TACRED with varying noise rates (10%, 30%, 50%)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework called NaCL (Noise-guided Attack for Continual Learning) to address the challenge of continual relation extraction in the presence of noisy labels. The framework combines contrastive learning with a noise-guided attack mechanism to generate hard negative samples from noisy data. By rebooting an auxiliary model for clean sample selection and performing targeted attacks on noisy samples, NaCL achieves significant improvements in accuracy and forgetting metrics compared to existing methods. The approach demonstrates robustness against both closed-set and open-set label noise, making it suitable for real-world applications where label corruption is inevitable.

## Method Summary
NaCL addresses noisy continual relation extraction through a two-pronged approach. First, it employs an auxiliary model that is re-initialized (rebooted) at each new task to select clean samples based on confidence scores, avoiding interference from prior knowledge. Second, it performs noise-guided attack on selected noisy samples to generate hard negatives that align with their noisy labels. The main model is then trained using contrastive learning objectives with clean positives and hard negatives, while a memory buffer stores representative clean samples for replay to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This framework effectively handles both the noise problem and the continual learning challenge simultaneously.

## Key Results
- NaCL achieves consistent performance improvements over state-of-the-art baselines on FewRel and TACRED datasets with noise rates of 10%, 30%, and 50%
- The framework demonstrates robustness against both closed-set and open-set label noise scenarios
- NaCL significantly reduces forgetting compared to baseline methods while maintaining high accuracy on current tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Rebooted selection process helps identify clean samples by avoiding prior knowledge interference
- Mechanism: The auxiliary model is re-initialized at each new task, allowing it to learn from scratch without being influenced by previously learned relations. The confidence score from this rebooted model serves as a reliable indicator for distinguishing clean from noisy samples.
- Core assumption: A model trained from scratch on a new task will have higher confidence on clean samples compared to noisy ones, regardless of prior knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "First, at each new task, the auxiliary model will be re-initialized to train for new relations learning. Intriguingly, we term it as reboot, which can make the model escape the interference of prior knowledge so that its logit outputs can be a measure of clean sample selection for current task."
  - [section 3.2]: "For the sake of overcoming the problem originating from knowledge intervention, we propose to introduce an auxiliary model fA(·,θ∗) and reboot it to help select clean samples at each incremental task."
- Break condition: If the new task has significant overlap with previous tasks, the rebooted model may still carry some bias from prior knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Noise-guided attack generates hard negatives that improve contrastive learning
- Mechanism: Noisy samples are perturbed in feature space to match their noisy labels, creating adversarial examples that are difficult to distinguish from positives. These hard negatives enrich the contrastive learning process by providing more challenging negative samples.
- Core assumption: Perturbing noisy samples towards their noisy labels creates examples that are both informative (hard to distinguish) and correctly labeled in the context of contrastive learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Second, this model will translate a novel sight into feature space for correction by performing noise-guided attack. This attack can actively drive the feature distribution of noisy negatives more aligned with their given labels."
  - [section 3.3]: "Noise-guided attack intends to modify the feature to let them match the noisy labels, compared with relabeling that modifies labels to match the given sample features."
- Break condition: If the perturbation is too large, the adversarial examples may become unrealistic and harm the learning process.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Contrastive learning with clean positives and hard negatives prevents catastrophic forgetting
- Mechanism: By maintaining a clean set of positives and using hard negatives generated through noise-guided attack, the model learns robust representations that generalize well to new tasks while preserving knowledge of old tasks through memory replay.
- Core assumption: Contrastive learning with high-quality positive and negative samples is effective for learning representations that are both discriminative and generalizable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Compared to direct noise discarding or inaccessible noise relabeling, we present modifying the feature space to match the given noisy labels via attacking can better enrich contrastive representations."
  - [section 3.3]: "From the perspective of contrastive representation learning, under the noise-guided attack, noisy samples serving as the negatives all move towards the same direction of the feature space where their noisy label lies."
- Break condition: If the memory buffer becomes too contaminated with noisy samples, the old knowledge preservation may fail.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Auxiliary model (fA) -> Clean sample selection -> Noise-guided attack -> Main model (fM) -> Contrastive learning -> Memory buffer -> Old knowledge preservation

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize auxiliary model for new task
  2. Train auxiliary model to select clean samples
  3. Perform noise-guided attack on noisy samples
  4. Train main model with contrastive loss using clean positives and hard negatives
  5. Update memory buffer with clean samples from current task
  6. Replay memory buffer for old knowledge preservation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a separate auxiliary model adds computational overhead but enables effective noise handling
  - Perturbation size in noise-guided attack must balance between generating hard negatives and maintaining realistic samples
  - Memory buffer size affects both performance and computational requirements

- Failure signatures:
  - High forgetting rate indicates ineffective old knowledge preservation
  - Poor performance on noisy data suggests ineffective clean sample selection or noise-guided attack
  - Performance plateau or degradation may indicate insufficient model capacity or suboptimal hyperparameters

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline evaluation: Compare NaCL with finetuning and joint training on clean data to establish performance bounds
  2. Noise sensitivity analysis: Evaluate NaCL with varying noise ratios (10%, 30%, 50%) to understand robustness
  3. Ablation study: Remove noise-guided attack to quantify its contribution to performance improvements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the noise-guided attack performance scale with different types of label noise distributions beyond the uniform random noise used in the experiments?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions "open-set" and "closed-set" noise but only evaluates uniform random noise across tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one type of synthetic noise generation method, limiting understanding of the method's robustness to real-world noise patterns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with structured noise patterns (class-dependent, asymmetric noise), comparisons with real-world noisy datasets, and ablation studies varying noise correlation structures.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical upper bound for NaCL's performance when memory buffer size approaches the full dataset size?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a fixed memory size of 20 per relation but doesn't analyze the memory-limited scenario.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on limited memory scenarios but doesn't explore how performance scales with memory capacity or when it approaches the upper bound.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying memory buffer sizes from very small to very large, analysis of the trade-off between memory capacity and forgetting, comparison with upper bounds from full rehearsal.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the reboot mechanism affect catastrophic forgetting in scenarios where tasks have overlapping relation spaces?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces the reboot mechanism to avoid knowledge intervention but only evaluates it on disjoint task partitions.
- Why unresolved: The experiments use mutually exclusive relation spaces between tasks, so the impact of reboot on overlapping concepts isn't tested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with overlapping relations between tasks, analysis of the trade-off between forgetting prevention and interference in overlapping scenarios.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the computational overhead of the noise-guided attack compared to other noisy label handling methods in continual learning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper describes the attack mechanism but doesn't provide computational complexity analysis or runtime comparisons.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates effectiveness, it doesn't quantify the computational cost relative to simpler methods like sample discarding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Runtime measurements comparing NaCL with baseline methods, analysis of how attack steps and perturbation size affect training time, GPU/CPU utilization comparisons.

## Limitations
- The framework's reliance on an auxiliary model introduces additional computational overhead that may limit scalability
- Performance is primarily evaluated on relation extraction tasks with synthetic noise injection, which may not fully capture real-world complexity
- The reboot mechanism assumes that prior knowledge interference is the primary source of clean sample selection errors, which may not hold for all task distributions

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using contrastive learning with clean positives and hard negatives for continual relation extraction is well-established and effectively demonstrated.
- **Medium Confidence**: The noise-guided attack approach for generating hard negatives is innovative but requires careful hyperparameter tuning for optimal performance.
- **Medium Confidence**: The reboot mechanism for clean sample selection shows promise but may have limitations when tasks have significant overlap with previous ones.
