# Modality-Aware Negative Sampling for Multi-modal Knowledge Graph Embedding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11618
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11618
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: MANS outperforms existing methods on link prediction and triple classification tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of modality alignment in multi-modal knowledge graph embedding by proposing MANS, a novel negative sampling strategy. The method focuses on sampling negative visual embeddings to better align structural and visual information for entities. Through experiments on two benchmark datasets, MANS demonstrates significant improvements over existing methods, achieving up to 9.9% relative improvement in Hit@1 on FB15K. The approach is designed to be lightweight and efficient while producing more semantically rich embeddings with clearer boundaries between different modalities.

## Method Summary
MANS introduces a visual negative sampling strategy (MANS-V) that specifically targets the alignment between structural and visual embeddings in multi-modal knowledge graphs. The approach samples negative visual embeddings to address the modality alignment problem, which is further combined with normal negative sampling to create three more comprehensive strategies: MANS-T, MANS-H, and MANS-A. These variants are designed to leverage different aspects of the multi-modal information while maintaining computational efficiency. The method is evaluated on standard link prediction and triple classification tasks using established benchmark datasets.

## Key Results
- MANS achieves up to 9.9% relative improvement in Hit@1 on FB15K benchmark
- The approach outperforms existing methods on both link prediction and triple classification tasks
- MANS-A variant shows the best overall performance across evaluation metrics
- The method is lightweight and efficient compared to other negative sampling approaches
- Visualization results indicate clearer boundaries between structural and visual embeddings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of MANS stems from its targeted approach to addressing modality misalignment in multi-modal knowledge graphs. By specifically sampling negative visual embeddings, the method forces the model to better distinguish between relevant and irrelevant visual information during training. This targeted negative sampling creates stronger gradients for the visual modality, helping to align it more closely with the structural information. The combination with traditional negative sampling ensures that both modalities are properly learned while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to only two benchmark datasets (FB15K and WN18)
- Visual embeddings rely on pre-trained features without exploring different extraction methods
- Computational overhead scaling to larger knowledge graphs not thoroughly addressed
- Visualization results are qualitative without quantitative metrics for comparison
