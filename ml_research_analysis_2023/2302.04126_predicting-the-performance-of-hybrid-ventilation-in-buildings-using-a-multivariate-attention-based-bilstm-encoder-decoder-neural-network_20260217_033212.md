# Predicting the performance of hybrid ventilation in buildings using a multivariate attention-based biLSTM Encoder-Decoder neural network

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04126
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04126
- Reference count: 40
- The model predicts 24 hours ahead using past seven days of data, achieving 1.2% average CVRMSE across all zones and prediction steps.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a deep neural network that accurately forecasts indoor air temperature in buildings with hybrid ventilation systems. The model combines bi-directional LSTM and transformer components with multi-head attention to predict temperature 24 hours ahead using past seven days of weather, operational, and zone-specific data. Trained on EnergyPlus simulation data from a 5-zone office building, the model successfully predicts indoor air temperature across all zones when windows open or close, with prediction error stabilizing after 24 steps ahead (6 hours). The study demonstrates the potential of using such models for control-oriented applications in buildings with hybrid ventilation systems.

## Method Summary
The method uses a multivariate multi-head attention-based biLSTM encoder-decoder neural network to predict 24-hour ahead indoor air temperature. The model takes past seven days of weather data, occupancy patterns, equipment loads, temperature setpoints, window opening signals, and temporal features as inputs. EnergyPlus simulation data from a 5-zone office building generates the training data, which is split into 60% training, 20% validation, and 20% testing sets. The model simultaneously predicts multiple percentiles (50th, 90th, 95th, and 99th) using quantile regression to provide probabilistic forecasts. The network architecture includes biLSTM encoders and decoders with attention mechanisms to capture both short-term and long-term temporal dependencies in building thermal dynamics.

## Key Results
- Model predicts 24 hours ahead using past seven days of weather, operational, and zone-specific data
- Average CVRMSE across all zones and prediction steps was 1.2%
- Prediction error stabilized after 24 steps ahead (6 hours)
- Model successfully predicted indoor air temperature across all zones when windows were opened or closed

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The biLSTM encoder-decoder architecture with multi-head attention captures both short-term and long-term dependencies in building thermal dynamics.
- Mechanism: The biLSTM processes input sequences in both directions, learning temporal patterns from the past, while multi-head attention attends to different parts of the sequence in parallel, enabling the model to weigh relevant information more heavily and retain direct connections to all previous timestamps.
- Core assumption: Building thermal dynamics exhibit both local (short-term) and global (long-term) temporal patterns that can be effectively modeled using recurrent networks combined with attention mechanisms.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states the model predicts 24 hours ahead using past seven days of data; section notes recurrent networks excel at capturing local temporal characteristics while transformer models learn long-term dynamics.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The quantile regression approach provides probabilistic forecasts (90%, 95%, 99% confidence intervals) that capture uncertainty in temperature predictions.
- Mechanism: By simultaneously predicting multiple percentiles at each time step, the model learns to estimate not just point forecasts but also the distribution of possible outcomes, allowing decision-makers to understand the range of likely temperatures.
- Core assumption: Building temperature prediction uncertainty follows a distribution that can be reasonably approximated by predicting specific quantiles.
- Evidence anchors: Section describes simultaneous prediction of various percentiles (50th, 90th, 95th, and 99th) at each time step using quantile regression.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using EnergyPlus-generated synthetic data enables the model to learn building dynamics across a wide range of operating conditions without requiring extensive real-world data collection.
- Mechanism: EnergyPlus simulations can generate diverse scenarios (different weather patterns, occupancy schedules, window opening patterns) that might be impractical to collect in real buildings, allowing the trained model to be fine-tuned with small amounts of real data for specific building applications.
- Core assumption: EnergyPlus simulations accurately represent real building thermal dynamics and can generate sufficiently diverse training scenarios.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states training and test data were generated from an EnergyPlus model of a 5-zone office building.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks
  - Why needed here: Building temperature dynamics are inherently sequential - current temperature depends on past temperatures and control actions. LSTMs are specifically designed to handle long-term dependencies in sequential data.
  - Quick check question: What problem do LSTMs solve that regular RNNs cannot handle well in building temperature prediction?

- Concept: Attention mechanisms and transformer architecture
  - Why needed here: Standard LSTMs can suffer from "short-term memory" over longer sequences. Attention mechanisms allow the model to focus on relevant parts of the input sequence regardless of their position, which is crucial for capturing building dynamics over the 7-day input window.
  - Quick check question: How does multi-head attention differ from single attention in terms of what it can learn about building thermal dynamics?

- Concept: Transfer learning and synthetic data generation
  - Why needed here: Collecting sufficient real-world building data for training is expensive and time-consuming. Using EnergyPlus to generate synthetic training data allows the model to learn general building dynamics that can be adapted to specific buildings with minimal real data.
  - Quick check question: Why is transfer learning particularly useful for building energy modeling applications?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input encoder (biLSTM) → Multi-head attention layers → Cross-attention layers → Decoder (biLSTM) → Output layer → Quantile predictions
- Critical path: Past 7 days of data → Input encoder → Attention mechanisms → Decoder → 24-hour predictions
- Design tradeoffs: The complex architecture with multiple attention layers provides better accuracy but increases computational cost and training time compared to simpler RNN-only approaches. The quantile regression approach adds prediction intervals but requires predicting multiple outputs per time step.
- Failure signatures: Poor predictions during rapid temperature changes, degraded performance when window opening signals are frequent or irregular, model overfitting to EnergyPlus simulation characteristics rather than real building physics.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test biLSTM-only encoder-decoder without attention to establish baseline performance and quantify attention mechanism contribution.
  2. Vary the number of past days used as input (e.g., 3 days vs 7 days) to determine optimal input sequence length for this building type.
  3. Compare quantile regression approach with simple point prediction to assess value of uncertainty quantification for control applications.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model's performance change when trained on real building data versus EnergyPlus simulated data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that the model was trained on data generated from an EnergyPlus model and suggest that the approach could be extended to real buildings after tuning with a small dataset.
- Why unresolved: The study only used simulated data, so the model's performance on real-world data remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the model on actual building data and comparing its performance metrics (e.g., CVRMSE) to those obtained with simulated data.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of including wind direction as an input feature on the model's prediction accuracy, particularly for zones affected by wind changes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that SPACE2-1 had the largest error, potentially due to wind direction affecting its temperature dynamics, suggesting that including wind direction could improve predictions.
- Why unresolved: Wind direction was not included as an input feature in the study, leaving its potential impact on model accuracy unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Incorporating wind direction into the model's input features and evaluating any changes in prediction accuracy for affected zones.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the model's accuracy vary with different types of buildings, such as residential or industrial, compared to the office building used in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study focused on a specific 5-zone office building, implying that the model's applicability to other building types is yet to be explored.
- Why unresolved: The model was only tested on one type of building, so its generalizability to other building types is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and testing the model on different types of buildings and comparing the prediction accuracy across these building types.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the effect of varying the temporal granularity of the input data on the model's prediction performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study used a temporal granularity of 15 minutes, but did not explore how changes in this granularity might affect the model's performance.
- Why unresolved: The impact of different temporal granularities on prediction accuracy was not investigated in the study.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the model with different temporal granularities (e.g., 5 minutes, 30 minutes) and comparing the resulting prediction accuracies.

## Limitations
- The study relies entirely on EnergyPlus simulation data, which may not fully capture real-world building behavior, particularly for hybrid ventilation systems with complex occupant interactions.
- The model's performance on actual buildings with different configurations, sizes, or climate conditions remains unverified.
- The computational complexity of the attention-based architecture may limit its deployment in resource-constrained control systems.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The model architecture and training methodology are well-specified and technically sound. The reported CVRMSE of 1.2% demonstrates strong predictive accuracy within the simulation environment.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that this approach enables energy-efficient control through predictive strategies is plausible but not directly validated through control experiments. The uncertainty quantification via quantile regression is methodologically correct but its practical value for control decisions is not demonstrated.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about the model's generalizability to buildings with different thermal characteristics, occupancy patterns, or climate zones are not supported by experimental evidence.
