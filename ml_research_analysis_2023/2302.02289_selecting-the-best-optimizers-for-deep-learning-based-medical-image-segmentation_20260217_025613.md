# Selecting the Best Optimizers for Deep Learning based Medical Image Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02289
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02289
- Reference count: 39
- Proposed CLMR optimizer achieved over 2% improvement in Dice metric for cardiac MRI/CT segmentation compared to ADAM

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of selecting optimal optimizers for medical image segmentation by proposing a novel Cyclic Learning/Momentum Rate (CLMR) optimizer. The CLMR approach combines adaptive and accelerated optimization schemes by alternating learning rate and momentum rate during training. Evaluated on the ACDC 2017 dataset using four different architectures (Encoder-Decoder, U-Net, DenseNet1, DenseNet2), CLMR demonstrated superior performance over traditional adaptive optimizers like ADAM. The method achieved over 2% improvement in Dice metric for both single and multi-object segmentation tasks while maintaining lower computational costs.

## Method Summary
The CLMR optimizer introduces a cyclic scheduling mechanism that alternates both learning rate and momentum rate during training, contrasting with traditional optimizers that typically vary only one parameter. The method combines benefits from adaptive optimizers (like ADAM's parameter-wise adaptation) with momentum-based acceleration schemes. Training involves scheduling these parameters in cyclic patterns to balance exploration and exploitation during optimization. The approach was evaluated across four neural network architectures on cardiac MRI/CT segmentation tasks from the ACDC 2017 dataset, with performance measured using Dice similarity coefficient.

## Key Results
- CLMR achieved over 2% improvement in Dice metric compared to ADAM optimizer
- Performance gains observed across both single and multi-object cardiac segmentation tasks
- Superior results maintained across four different architectures: Encoder-Decoder, U-Net, DenseNet1, and DenseNet2
- Demonstrated lower computational cost than traditional adaptive optimizers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The CLMR optimizer works by cyclically varying both learning rate and momentum parameters during training, which helps escape local minima and improves generalization. The alternating schedule allows the optimizer to combine the rapid convergence of high learning rates with the stability of momentum-based updates, while also preventing overfitting that can occur with static adaptive optimizers. This dynamic adjustment enables better exploration of the loss landscape compared to traditional methods that use fixed or monotonically decreasing schedules.

## Foundational Learning

**Cyclic Learning Rate Scheduling**: Periodic variation of learning rates during training to improve convergence and generalization
- *Why needed*: Static learning rates can cause premature convergence or slow training
- *Quick check*: Verify the optimizer implements a cyclical pattern rather than monotonic decay

**Momentum-based Optimization**: Uses velocity terms to accelerate convergence in relevant directions
- *Why needed*: Helps overcome local minima and speeds up training along consistent gradients
- *Quick check*: Confirm momentum parameters are being cyclically varied alongside learning rates

**Adaptive Optimization**: Parameter-specific learning rates based on historical gradient information
- *Why needed*: Different parameters may require different learning rates for optimal convergence
- *Quick check*: Ensure the optimizer maintains per-parameter adaptation capabilities

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input images → Backbone architecture (Encoder-Decoder/U-Net/DenseNet) → CLMR optimizer → Loss computation → Parameter updates → Output segmentation masks

**Critical Path**: Forward pass through backbone → Loss calculation → CLMR parameter update (learning rate + momentum cycling) → Backward pass → Parameter synchronization

**Design Tradeoffs**: CLMR trades increased parameter schedule complexity for improved convergence and generalization, requiring careful tuning of cycle frequencies and amplitude parameters versus the simplicity of traditional optimizers.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Poor performance if cycle frequencies are too high (oscillations) or too low (insufficient exploration)
- Convergence issues when learning rate and momentum cycles are not properly synchronized
- Degraded performance if the architecture cannot benefit from dynamic parameter scheduling

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation to a single dataset (ACDC 2017), restricting generalizability to other imaging modalities
- Absence of ablation studies to isolate the individual contributions of learning rate versus momentum rate cycling
- Computational cost claims lack detailed comparative analysis with baseline methods
- No statistical significance testing reported for performance improvements

## Confidence

- Low confidence: Computational efficiency and generalization claims are not fully substantiated
