# Improving Uncertainty Quantification of Deep Classifiers via Neighborhood Conformal Prediction: Novel Algorithm and Theoretical Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10694
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10694
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Novel algorithm that reduces prediction set size while maintaining coverage guarantees through neighborhood-based conformal prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Neighborhood Conformal Prediction (NCP), a novel algorithm that improves uncertainty quantification for deep classifiers by leveraging learned representations to create adaptive prediction sets. The method identifies k nearest neighbors among calibration examples using learned representations and assigns importance weights proportional to distance, producing smaller prediction sets than traditional conformal prediction methods while maintaining statistical coverage guarantees. Theoretical analysis proves that under mild conditions of separation and concentration in learned representations, NCP outperforms standard CP approaches. Experiments across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet demonstrate significant reductions in prediction set size while preserving coverage.

## Method Summary
NCP extends traditional conformal prediction by incorporating neighborhood structure in learned feature space. The algorithm first extracts representations from a deep network, then identifies k nearest neighbors from calibration examples for each test instance. These neighbors are assigned weights inversely proportional to their distance, creating an adaptive scoring function that reflects local density and class distribution. During prediction, the method generates prediction sets by thresholding class scores based on weighted conformal scores from the neighborhood, rather than treating all calibration examples equally as in standard CP. This neighborhood-aware approach allows the method to adapt to local data geometry, producing tighter prediction sets in regions of high confidence while maintaining coverage guarantees.

## Key Results
- NCP achieves significantly smaller prediction set sizes compared to state-of-the-art CP methods while maintaining target coverage
- Theoretical guarantees show NCP produces smaller prediction sets than traditional CP when learned representations satisfy separation and concentration conditions
- Experiments validate effectiveness across diverse architectures and datasets including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet
- The method demonstrates consistent improvements in efficiency without sacrificing the fundamental coverage guarantees of conformal prediction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
NCP leverages the inductive biases of deep networks to capture meaningful structure in the data representation space. By weighting calibration examples based on their proximity in this learned space, the method creates prediction sets that adapt to local data geometry rather than applying uniform thresholds across the entire input space. The neighborhood weighting allows the algorithm to recognize regions where the classifier is more confident (dense neighborhoods of similar examples) versus uncertain (sparse or ambiguous regions), leading to more efficient uncertainty quantification.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Input -> Deep Network -> Learned Representation -> k-NN Search -> Weighted Conformal Scores -> Prediction Set

Critical Path: The algorithm's core computation flows through representation extraction, neighbor identification, and weighted score aggregation. The learned representation quality directly impacts neighbor selection accuracy, which determines the effectiveness of the adaptive weighting scheme.

Design Tradeoffs: The method balances computational efficiency against prediction quality through the choice of k neighbors. Larger k values provide more stable estimates but increase computation and may dilute local specificity. The distance weighting scheme trades off between global uniformity (standard CP) and local adaptation.

Failure Signatures: Poor performance occurs when learned representations fail to capture meaningful similarity structure, when class boundaries are inherently ambiguous, or when high intra-class variation prevents effective neighbor identification. The method may also struggle in extremely high-dimensional spaces where distance metrics become less meaningful.

First Experiments: 1) Verify that nearest neighbors in learned space correspond to semantically similar examples, 2) Test coverage guarantees on synthetic data with known structure, 3) Benchmark prediction set sizes against baseline CP across varying k values.

## Limitations
- Theoretical assumptions about separation and concentration in learned representations may not hold for datasets with high intra-class variation or ambiguous boundaries
- Distance-based neighbor weighting may become less effective in extremely high-dimensional representation spaces
- Computational overhead scales with calibration set size, potentially limiting scalability to very large datasets
- Performance depends on the quality of learned representations, which may vary across different network architectures and training regimes
