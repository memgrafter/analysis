# Why Capsule Neural Networks Do Not Scale: Challenging the Dynamic Parse-Tree Assumption

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01583
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01583
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Capsule neural networks (CapsNets) fail to learn viewpoint-invariant parse-trees due to static routing, vanishing gradients, and lack of affine invariance

## Executive Summary
Capsule neural networks were designed to learn hierarchical parse-trees representing part-whole relationships in images through dynamic routing-by-agreement. However, this paper demonstrates that CapsNets, particularly the implementation by Sabour et al. (2017), do not actually achieve this goal. Through theoretical analysis and experiments on AffNIST and CIFAR10 datasets, the authors show that routing becomes nearly static after initial activation, capsule activations are not invariant to affine transformations, and vanishing gradients cause many capsules to become permanently inactive during training. These limitations prevent CapsNets from scaling beyond small datasets and challenge the fundamental assumption that they learn viewpoint-invariant hierarchical representations.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates CapsNets using a modified architecture with the Mazzia backbone (four convolutional layers) and multiple routing layers. The routing-by-agreement algorithm is implemented with 10 routing iterations and a modified squashing function. Models are trained on MNIST until reaching 99.2% validation accuracy, then evaluated on AffNIST to measure affine transformation robustness. Additional experiments are conducted on CIFAR10. The authors analyze routing diversity using the dys metric, measure capsule activation correlation under affine transformations, track dead capsule rates across training, and examine gradient flow through the network.

## Key Results
- Routing-by-agreement produces near-static routing with dys values below 2 for deeper layers, indicating low input-specific routing diversity
- Capsule activations show decreasing correlation under affine transformations, demonstrating lack of viewpoint invariance
- Vanishing gradients cause a significant portion of capsules to become permanently inactive, with dead capsule rates increasing with network depth
- CapsNets achieve lower accuracy on AffNIST compared to standard MNIST, confirming poor robustness to affine transformations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CapsNets do not learn viewpoint-invariant parse-trees
- Mechanism: Capsule activation magnitudes change with viewpoint changes, breaking the invariance required for parse-trees
- Core assumption: Parse-trees should encode part-whole relationships that remain stable under viewpoint changes
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CapsNets suffer from a vanishing gradient problem that results in the starvation of many capsules during training."
  - [section 4.2]: "We observe that the capsule activation correlation decreases for increasingly stronger affine transformations"
  - [corpus]: Weak. No direct citation found in neighbors.
- Break condition: If capsule activation correlation remains high across affine transformations, this mechanism would not hold

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: CapsNets suffer from vanishing gradients that lead to capsule starvation
- Mechanism: Gradients scale with capsule activation magnitude, so inactive capsules receive no gradient updates and remain dead
- Core assumption: Gradients should propagate effectively through all layers to maintain capsule activity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "CapsNets suffer from a vanishing gradient problem that results in the starvation of many capsules during training."
  - [section 4.3]: "We observe that the number of dead capsules cdr increases with the depth of the model."
  - [section 4.3]: "Theorem 2: Once a capsule becomes dead, it never becomes active again."
- Break condition: If dead capsules can be reactivated during training, this mechanism would not hold

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CapsNets do not exhibit dynamic routing behavior necessary for parse-tree formation
- Mechanism: Routing-by-agreement results in near-static routing where capsules mostly route to the same targets regardless of input
- Core assumption: Dynamic routing should create input-specific parse-trees that encode part-whole relationships
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Routing-by-agreement (RBA) in CapsNets results in near-static routing with low diversity"
  - [section 4.1]: "dys values below two for Layers 2 and 3 indicate low routing dynamics"
  - [section 4.1]: "A route is mostly predetermined once a capsule is activated; hence, the routing is almost static"
- Break condition: If routing diversity increases significantly across inputs, this mechanism would not hold

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Backbone network (CNN) → PrimeCaps layer → Routing layers → Output layer → Decoder network
- Critical path: Input image → Backbone → PrimeCaps → Routing layers → Output capsules → Classification/Decoder
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Depth vs. capsule starvation: Deeper networks have more dead capsules
  - Number of capsules vs. computational cost: More capsules increase parameters exponentially
  - Capsule dimension vs. representational capacity: Higher dimensions allow more complex features but increase computational cost
- Failure signatures:
  - Low routing diversity (dys values close to 1)
  - Decreasing capsule activation correlation under affine transformations
  - High percentage of dead capsules in deeper layers
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a CapsNet on MNIST/AffNIST and measure routing diversity (dys) across layers
  2. Apply affine transformations to test images and measure capsule activation correlation
  3. Track capsule activation norms during training to identify when capsules become dead

## Limitations
