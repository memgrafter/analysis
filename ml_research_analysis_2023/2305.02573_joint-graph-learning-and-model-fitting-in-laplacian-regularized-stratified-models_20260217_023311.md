# Joint Graph Learning and Model Fitting in Laplacian Regularized Stratified Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02573
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02573
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Joint optimization framework for graph weights and model parameters in LRSM outperforms fixed-weight approaches on imbalanced strata

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical sensitivity issue in Laplacian regularized stratified models where fixed graph weights cause large estimation errors when parameter scales and sample sizes are imbalanced across strata. The authors propose a joint optimization framework that simultaneously learns edge weights and fits model parameters, incorporating a log-determinant regularization term to encourage graph connectivity and robustness. The method employs an accelerated proximal gradient algorithm with convergence guarantees despite the non-convexity and lack of global smoothness.

## Method Summary
The method jointly optimizes model parameters Θ and graph weights W by minimizing a composite objective function containing local loss terms, Laplacian regularization, log-determinant connectivity regularization, and elastic net regularization on W. The optimization uses a monotone accelerated proximal gradient algorithm with adaptive stepsizes, initialized from either prior knowledge or a fully connected graph. The log-determinant term encourages meaningful graph connectivity while preventing trivial solutions, and the method includes early stopping based on validation performance to prevent overfitting.

## Key Results
- Joint learning reduces sensitivity to parameter scale imbalance by up to 80% compared to fixed weights on synthetic data
- Real-world experiments show 5-15% improvement in accuracy, AUC, and F1 scores over standard LRSM
- The method learns meaningful graph structures that transfer information effectively between imbalanced strata
- Convergence is guaranteed to critical points despite non-convexity, with linear or sublinear rates depending on KL property satisfaction

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing -> Graph learning module -> Model fitting module -> Evaluation module -> Optimization engine

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize graph weights (either from prior or as zero matrix)
  2. Compute gradient of smooth loss term
  3. Update graph weights via proximal operator
  4. Check monotonicity condition for accelerated scheme
  5. Repeat until convergence or early stopping criterion

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Log-determinant vs. other connectivity regularizers: Log-determinant provides better theoretical guarantees for graph connectivity but is computationally more expensive
  - Joint vs. alternating optimization: Joint optimization handles sensitivity issues but requires more complex algorithms
  - Elastic net vs. pure ℓ1 regularization: Elastic net provides better initialization guidance but adds complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - Graph weights collapse to zero despite log-determinant regularization: Likely due to insufficient μ parameter or poor initialization
  - Slow convergence or oscillation: May indicate inappropriate stepsize selection or violation of local smoothness assumptions
  - Overfitting on training data: Could result from insufficient regularization on graph weights or model parameters

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run on synthetic data with known imbalanced strata to verify sensitivity reduction compared to fixed graph weights
  2. Compare joint learning vs. grid search for graph weights on real data with moderate imbalance
  3. Test early stopping performance on data with limited prior knowledge about graph structure

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the theoretical guarantees for the proposed method when the local loss functions lk are non-convex or non-smooth?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper assumes that lk is differentiable and r has a closed-form proximal operator, but does not address non-convex or non-smooth cases
- Why unresolved: The current analysis relies on the strong smoothness of lk and the convexity of r, which may not hold in practice
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a theoretical framework for non-convex and non-smooth local loss functions and regularization terms

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed method perform when the number of strata K is very large (e.g., thousands or more)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that K is generally not too large and hundreds is preferred, but does not provide extensive experiments with large K
- Why unresolved: The computational complexity of the method, particularly the log-determinant term, may become prohibitive for large K
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with datasets containing a large number of strata and analyzing the computational performance and scalability of the proposed method

- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically evaluating the performance of the proposed method with different choices of W0, including random initialization and learned priors, on various datasets and problem settings

- Performance depends heavily on appropriate initialization and hyperparameter selection
- Theoretical guarantees require specific assumptions about local loss functions that may not hold in practice
