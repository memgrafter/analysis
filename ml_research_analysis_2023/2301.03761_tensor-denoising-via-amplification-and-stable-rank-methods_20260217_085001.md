# Tensor Denoising via Amplification and Stable Rank Methods

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03761
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03761

- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel tensor denoising framework using amplification and stable rank methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a comprehensive framework for tensor denoising that leverages tensor amplification techniques and new notions of tensor rank. The authors develop methods to decompose noisy tensors into clean low-rank components and noise, using amplification maps to approximate tensor spectral and nuclear norms. The framework introduces two new rank concepts - stable slice rank and stable X-rank - and demonstrates improved denoising performance across synthetic and real-world datasets, particularly in high-noise scenarios.

## Method Summary
The core methodology involves decomposing a noisy tensor X into a clean tensor S and noise tensor E, then using tensor amplification to approximate tensor norms for denoising. The approach introduces two new rank notions: stable slice rank and stable X-rank. Stable slice rank measures the minimum number of rank-1 tensors needed to express a tensor as a sum of Kronecker products, while stable X-rank measures the maximum rank across all slices of a tensor along any mode. The amplification-based denoising uses order-specific amplification maps to approximate tensor norms, while the stable X-rank method employs an alternating least squares optimization to minimize the stable X-rank.

## Key Results
- Amplification-based denoising achieves comparable performance to existing methods in high SNR settings and superior performance in noisy settings
- Stable X-rank method shows superior denoising performance on real-world ECG waveform tensors
- Theoretical bounds established for the approximation error in tensor norm calculations using amplification maps
- New rank notions (stable slice rank and stable X-rank) successfully applied to practical denoising problems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework exploits the low-rank structure inherent in many real-world tensors. By using tensor amplification, the methods can approximate tensor norms that are otherwise computationally intractable. The amplification maps act as approximations to the tensor spectral and nuclear norms, enabling effective separation of signal from noise. The new rank notions capture structural properties of tensors that traditional rank measures miss, allowing for more accurate decomposition of noisy tensors.

## Foundational Learning

**Tensor Rank**: A fundamental concept measuring the minimum number of rank-1 tensors needed to express a given tensor. Essential for understanding low-rank approximations in denoising. *Quick check*: Verify understanding by expressing simple 3D tensors as sums of rank-1 components.

**Kronecker Product**: A mathematical operation combining two matrices/vectors into a larger matrix/vector. Used in defining stable slice rank. *Quick check*: Practice computing Kronecker products for small matrices.

**Nuclear Norm**: The sum of singular values of a tensor, serving as a convex relaxation of rank. Key for regularization in denoising. *Quick check*: Calculate nuclear norms for simple matrices and verify against sum of singular values.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**: How can amplification-based denoising be improved for higher rank tensors?
- The authors note that amplification maps correspond to approximations of the spectral norm, which only measures the highest singular value for a given tensor, and suggest that a decomposition method that can find successively smaller singular values and their corresponding rank 1 components could improve performance.
- The paper does not provide a concrete method for achieving this, only suggesting a potential direction.
- A successful implementation and evaluation of a gradient-based descent optimization method that can find lower singular values for higher rank tensors, showing improved denoising performance compared to current methods.

**Open Question 2**: How can amplification maps be computed for tensor orders beyond 3 and 4?
- The authors state that amplification-based denoising is limited by the need to determine order-specific amplification maps, and that currently only those for orders three and four have been computed.
- The paper does not provide a method for computing amplification maps for higher order tensors.
- A successful computation and validation of amplification maps for tensor orders greater than 4, along with improved denoising performance on real-world data of those orders.

**Open Question 3**: Why did XRank achieve superior denoising performance on ECG waveform tensors compared to other methods?
- The authors observe that only XRank had appreciable denoising performance on tensors derived from ECG signals, but do not provide an explanation for this superior performance.
- The paper does not investigate the underlying reasons for XRank's success on this specific type of data.
- A detailed analysis comparing the characteristics of ECG tensor data to synthetic tensor data, explaining why XRank's approach is particularly suited to ECG denoising.

- Theoretical analysis focused on specific noise models, may not generalize to all tensor corruption types
- Limited real-world data experiments with small sample sizes
- Comparison to existing methods not exhaustive
