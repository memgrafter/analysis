# Rolling Lookahead Learning for Optimal Classification Trees

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10830
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10830
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: A rolling subtree lookahead algorithm that outperforms both optimal and myopic benchmarks in 808 out of 1330 instances

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a rolling subtree lookahead algorithm for constructing optimal classification trees that balances the scalability of myopic approaches with the accuracy of optimal methods. The core idea is to construct trees by iteratively solving 2-depth optimal subtrees, starting from the root and rolling down to deeper nodes. A novel mixed-integer optimization formulation is proposed for the 2-depth tree problem, which leverages pre-computation of loss function components and has an integral feasible region, yielding LP relaxation solutions that are always optimal. The approach mitigates the learning pathology observed in deep lookahead methods.

## Method Summary
The method involves iteratively constructing 2-depth optimal subtrees using a mixed-integer optimization formulation. First, all possible leaf decision rules are enumerated and their loss function components are pre-computed. Then, the optimization model selects the best combination of these rules that forms a valid tree structure. This process is repeated, rolling down the tree, until no misclassifications remain or the maximum depth is reached. The approach uses one-hot encoding for categorical features and discretization for numerical features.

## Key Results
- Outperforms optimal and myopic benchmarks in 808 out of 1330 instances
- Improves out-of-sample accuracy by up to 23.6% and 14.4% respectively
- Maintains reasonable computation times while achieving superior accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-computing loss function components for each possible leaf decision rule reduces the complexity of the optimization problem from O(p^3) to O(p^2).
- Mechanism: By enumerating all possible pairs of split conditions for each leaf node and pre-computing the loss function component for each pair, the optimization problem becomes a selection problem instead of an enumeration problem.
- Core assumption: The loss function is decomposable.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section]
- Break condition: The loss function is not decomposable, or the number of features is very small.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The rolling subtree approach mitigates the learning pathology observed in optimal approaches by limiting the lookahead to two steps.
- Mechanism: Instead of constructing the entire tree at once, the rolling subtree approach constructs the tree in smaller, manageable chunks of 2-depth subtrees.
- Core assumption: Looking two steps ahead is sufficient to avoid learning pathology while still providing better accuracy than myopic approaches.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section]
- Break condition: The dataset is very small, or the tree depth is very shallow.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The integral polyhedron of the [OCT-2] formulation ensures that the LP relaxation always provides an optimal integer solution.
- Mechanism: The [OCT-2] formulation has a totally unimodular constraint matrix, which means that the LP relaxation of the problem always provides an integer solution.
- Core assumption: The constraint matrix of the [OCT-2] formulation is totally unimodular.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section]
- Break condition: The constraint matrix is not totally unimodular, or the LP relaxation does not provide an integer solution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the rolling subtree lookahead algorithm scale with increasing lookahead depth beyond 2?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only tests a 2-step lookahead and mentions that extending to 3-depth lookahead would require substantial pre-computation time.
- Why unresolved: The computational complexity of enumerating leaf decision rules grows exponentially with lookahead depth, making deeper lookaheads potentially intractable.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing 2-step, 3-step, and 4-step lookahead variants on datasets of varying sizes, showing accuracy gains versus computation time trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between lookahead depth and tree depth for maximizing out-of-sample accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes a "switching behavior" around depth 5 where optimizing for Gini impurity becomes better than optimizing for misclassification error, and introduces a hybrid approach.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests fixed lookahead depth of 2, and the switching point may vary across datasets or depend on other factors like class imbalance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying both lookahead depth and tree depth across multiple datasets, identifying patterns in when deeper lookaheads become beneficial.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would parallelization strategies affect the practical scalability of the rolling subtree algorithm?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "tree growing across different branches could be run independently in parallel to further decrease computational time" but does not implement this.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore or quantify the potential speedups from parallel execution of subtree optimizations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and benchmarking of parallel versus sequential versions of the algorithm, showing computation time reductions and any trade-offs in solution quality.

## Limitations

- The total unimodularity claim for the [OCT-2] formulation requires careful verification, as the constraint structure appears complex.
- The computational advantage of pre-computing loss components depends heavily on the dataset size and feature dimensionality.
- The learning pathology mitigation claim is based on limited theoretical justification in the paper.

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Pre-computation): Medium - The computational savings are well-reasoned but depend on specific dataset characteristics
- Mechanism 2 (Rolling subtree): High - The iterative approach is clearly defined and directly addresses the pathology issue
- Mechanism 3 (Integral polyhedron): Medium - While TU claims are stated, the constraint structure needs verification
