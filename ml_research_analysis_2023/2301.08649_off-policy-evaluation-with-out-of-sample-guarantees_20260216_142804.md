# Off-Policy Evaluation with Out-of-Sample Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08649
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08649
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: Sample-splitting method provides finite-sample coverage guarantees for entire loss distribution under target policy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of off-policy evaluation when observational data is collected under a different decision process than the target policy. The key innovation is a sample-splitting method that provides finite-sample coverage guarantees for the entire loss distribution, not just the mean. By leveraging the marginal sensitivity model to bound the divergence between true and nominal past policies via odds ratios, the method can handle unmeasured confounding while providing informative limits that trade off credibility and informativeness.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses a sample-splitting approach where data is divided into two sets: D0 for computing weight bounds and D1 for constructing a weighted empirical CDF. The method characterizes model divergence using odds ratios bounded by Γ, and for each confidence level α, finds the smallest loss threshold ℓ_α that covers the out-of-sample loss with probability at least 1-α. This provides valid limit curves that quantify the trade-off between the credibility of model assumptions and the informativeness of the inference.

## Key Results
- Provides finite-sample coverage guarantees for the entire loss distribution under target policy
- Valid limits remain informative even under unmeasured confounding
- Informativeness of limits trades off with credibility of model assumptions (quantified by odds bound Γ)
- Experiments on synthetic data demonstrate valid coverage and the trade-off between informativeness and credibility

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Sample-splitting ensures finite-sample coverage guarantees for the entire loss distribution
- Mechanism: Data split into D0 (weight bounds) and D1 (weighted CDF) avoids bias from reusing data
- Core assumption: Causal factorization holds and marginal sensitivity model bounds divergence via odds ratios
- Evidence anchors: Abstract claims finite-sample coverage; Theorem 4.1 shows ℓ_α limits loss with probability ≥ 1-α
- Break condition: Coverage fails if sample split is not independent or Γ is misspecified

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Marginal sensitivity model enables robust evaluation under unmeasured confounding
- Mechanism: Bounds ratio of odds between true past policy and nominal model (Γ) without requiring exact U knowledge
- Core assumption: Unknown odds ratio bounded by Γ (nominal model at most Γ times more/less likely than true policy)
- Evidence anchors: Section characterizes model divergence via odds of taking action A; follows marginal sensitivity methodology
- Break condition: Invalid bounds if Γ underestimated; overly conservative if overestimated

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Method provides informative limits trading credibility and informativeness via Γ
- Mechanism: As Γ increases, method becomes more credible but less informative (wider limits)
- Core assumption: Monotonic relationship between Γ and informativeness is measurable
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions trade-off quantified by odds bound; section discusses increasing Γ increasing credibility but decreasing inference strength
- Break condition: Poor calibration of informativeness metric prevents proper trade-off assessment

- Critical path: Sample split → Compute W bounds → Build weighted CDF → Find smallest β for each α → Output (α, ℓ_α) pairs

## Limitations
- Reliance on correct specification of odds bound Γ, which may be difficult to estimate in practice
- Potential computational challenges when evaluating optimization for each α
- Limited validation beyond synthetic data with known data-generating processes

## Confidence

- Finite-sample distributional coverage: Medium confidence - sound theoretical framework but limited empirical validation across diverse real-world scenarios
- Sample splitting mechanism: High confidence - well-established technique in causal inference and conformal prediction
- Credibility-informativeness trade-off: Medium confidence - theoretical framework provided but lacks extensive empirical characterization
