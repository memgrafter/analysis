# Predicting the Quality of Revisions in Argumentative Writing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00667
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00667
- Reference count: 20
- This paper introduces a novel approach for predicting the quality of revisions in argumentative writing by leveraging ChatGPT-generated argumentative contexts (ACs).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for predicting the quality of revisions in argumentative writing by leveraging ChatGPT-generated argumentative contexts (ACs). The method uses Chain-of-Thought prompts to extract relevant claims, evidence, and reasoning from essays, then summarizes them into concise contexts paired with revisions for quality prediction. Experiments on elementary and college essay corpora demonstrate that these ACs significantly outperform location-based baselines, with CoT prompts yielding better results than single-pass prompts. The approach highlights the importance of contextual understanding in assessing revision quality and offers a promising direction for automated writing evaluation systems.

## Method Summary
The approach uses ChatGPT to generate argumentative contexts for argumentative revision (AR) pairs through zero-shot Chain-of-Thought prompts. These contexts capture claims, evidence, and reasoning from essays and are paired with revisions for quality prediction. A DistilRoBERTa-based classifier encodes both ARs and ACs, with an MLP layer predicting whether revisions are successful or unsuccessful. The method is evaluated on elementary and college essay corpora using 10-fold cross-validation, comparing performance against location-based baselines.

## Key Results
- Argumentative contexts (ACs) significantly outperform location-based baselines for AR quality predictions
- Chain-of-Thought prompts yield better results than single-pass prompts for AC generation
- AC-Reasoning performs better than AC-Evidence, suggesting reasoning revisions are more self-justifiable

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Chain-of-Thought prompts improve ChatGPT's ability to extract and summarize argumentative contexts compared to single-pass prompts.
- Mechanism: CoT prompts first extract relevant sentences (claims, evidence, reasoning) from essays, then summarize them into concise contexts, allowing ChatGPT to focus on argumentative relationships rather than just surface-level text.
- Core assumption: Argumentative context extraction benefits from step-by-step reasoning rather than direct summarization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Experiments using both elementary and college essay corpora show the superiority of the proposed ACs over existing location-based contexts for AR quality predictions."
  - [section]: "Furthermore, the observation that reasoning ARs results are generally better than evidence ARs indicates that reasoning ARs might be self-justifiable which means it might require fewer contexts than the evidence to identify AR quality."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus provides annotated data but doesn't directly measure CoT vs single-pass effectiveness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Argumentative contexts are more effective than location-based contexts for predicting revision quality.
- Mechanism: ACs capture argumentative relationships (claims, evidence, reasoning) rather than just adjacency, providing semantically relevant context for assessing whether a revision strengthens or weakens an argument.
- Core assumption: The quality of a revision depends on its relationship to the essay's argumentative structure, not its position.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The ability to revise in response to feedback is critical to students' writing success. In the case of argument writing in specific, identifying whether an argument revision (AR) is successful or not is a complex problem because AR quality is dependent on the overall content of an argument."
  - [section]: "Another interesting finding is that the Base-Long performed better than the Base-Short in evidence ARs but worse in reasoning ARs. This suggests that the longer context is not always helpful in the case that evidence contexts are usually sparsely distributed in the essay so the longer context will introduce more noise."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains annotated revisions but doesn't directly compare ACs vs location-based contexts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DistilRoBERTa-based classifiers can effectively predict revision quality using extracted argumentative contexts.
- Mechanism: The model learns to attend to argumentative relationships in contexts rather than just revision text, enabling quality assessment based on whether revisions align with essay claims and evidence.
- Core assumption: Argumentative context features are sufficient for quality prediction when paired with revision text.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Experiments using both elementary and college essay corpora demonstrate the superiority of the proposed ACs over baselines."
  - [section]: "Results in Table 5 show that the DistilRoBERTa model is able to learn from even small-size data without data augmentation."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus provides training data but doesn't directly validate DistilRoBERTa's effectiveness.

  - Why needed here: The prediction task requires understanding what makes a revision improve or weaken an argument
  - Quick check question: Given two revisions, can you determine which one successfully improves the argument and why?

- Concept: Chain-of-Thought prompting methodology
  - Why needed here: The core innovation uses CoT prompts to extract and summarize argumentative contexts
  - Quick check question: Can you design a CoT prompt that first extracts specific elements from text, then summarizes them?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Essay → Sentence alignment → Revision annotation → ChatGPT AC generation → DistilRoBERTa encoding → MLP prediction

- Critical path: Essay → Sentence alignment → Revision annotation → ChatGPT AC generation → DistilRoBERTa encoding → MLP prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Two-sentence limit on ACs balances context informativeness with avoiding overshadowing revisions
  - Zero-shot prompts avoid fine-tuning costs but may limit prompt effectiveness
  - Sentence-level revision focus enables granular analysis but misses document-level patterns

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor AC generation: revisions lack argumentative context, leading to random predictions
  - Overfitting: model performs well on training data but poorly on test data
  - Context overshadowing: model attends to ACs more than revisions, losing revision-specific signals

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare AC-Reasoning vs Base-Long baseline on elementary essay corpus
  2. Test CoT vs Single prompts for AC generation on reasoning revisions
  3. Evaluate model performance on college essays without data augmentation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do CoT prompts perform across different essay genres beyond argumentative writing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows CoT prompts outperform single prompts for argumentative writing but doesn't explore other genres.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses exclusively on argumentative writing and doesn't test generalizability to other essay types like narrative or expository writing.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing CoT and single prompts across multiple essay genres with the same evaluation metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of essay length on the effectiveness of CoT-generated contexts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper limits generated contexts to two sentences but doesn't systematically explore optimal context length across essays of varying lengths.
- Why unresolved: The fixed two-sentence constraint was chosen for technical reasons but optimal context length may vary with essay length and complexity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying context length across essays of different lengths while measuring prediction accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do CoT-generated contexts compare to human-generated contexts in terms of prediction accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper validates machine-generated contexts against baselines but doesn't include human-generated contexts as a comparison point.
- Why unresolved: While CoT contexts outperform location-based baselines, the absolute quality relative to human judgment remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison of CoT-generated contexts with expert-annotated contexts using the same AR quality prediction task.

## Limitations

- The study relies entirely on ChatGPT's zero-shot AC generation without validation against human judgments
- Small corpus sizes (596 elementary essays, 60 college essays) may limit generalizability
- Sentence-level focus on revisions may miss document-level argumentative patterns
- Fixed two-sentence constraint on contexts may not be optimal across all essay types

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The finding that argumentative contexts outperform location-based baselines is well-supported by the experimental results across both corpora.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that Chain-of-Thought prompts outperform single-pass prompts is supported but could benefit from more rigorous ablation studies.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that DistilRoBERTa can effectively learn from small datasets without data augmentation needs more validation.

## Next Checks

1. **Human Validation of AC Quality**: Conduct a study where human annotators evaluate whether ChatGPT-generated argumentative contexts accurately capture the relevant claims, evidence, and reasoning for each revision.

2. **Cross-Corpus Generalization Test**: Train models on one corpus (e.g., elementary essays) and evaluate on the other (college essays) to assess whether the approach generalizes across educational levels and argumentative complexity.

3. **Alternative Context Generation Methods**: Compare ChatGPT-generated ACs against contexts extracted using rule-based methods or fine-tuned models to determine whether the performance gains stem from ChatGPT's capabilities or from the contextual approach itself.
