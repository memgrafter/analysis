# AdapterSoup: Weight Averaging to Improve Generalization of Pretrained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07027
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07027
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AdapterSoup improves perplexity on novel domains by averaging adapter weights trained on different domains

## Executive Summary
AdapterSoup introduces a method for improving the generalization of pretrained language models to new domains through weight-space averaging of adapters trained on different domains. The approach involves training domain-specific adapters and then determining which adapters to average at test time using text clustering and semantic similarity methods. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements on novel domains without requiring additional training, with the best performance achieved using text clustering for adapter selection.

## Method Summary
The method trains individual adapters on 21 different training domains using GPT-2 small with a bottleneck size of 64. For each evaluation domain, the approach computes domain similarity using either text clustering (Gaussian Mixture Models) or sentence similarity (sentence-BERT embeddings). The top-k most similar adapters are then averaged in weight space and used for inference. The paper also explores averaging adapters trained with different learning rates on the same domain, finding that larger learning rates lead to better generalization to novel domains.

## Key Results
- AdapterSoup improves perplexity on 8/10 novel domains compared to single adapters when using clustering-based selection
- Text clustering outperforms sentence similarity for adapter selection across most domains
- Adapters trained with larger learning rates (7e-3, 4e-3, 7e-4) generalize better to novel domains than those trained with smaller rates (5e-4)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Weight-space averaging of adapters trained on different domains improves generalization by combining complementary domain knowledge
- Mechanism: Averaging weights of adapters from diverse but related domains creates a model that inherits features from multiple domains, enabling better handling of inputs with characteristics from more than one training domain
- Core assumption: Domains are sufficiently related that averaging their adapter weights creates a beneficial combination rather than harmful interference
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We introduce AdapterSoup, an approach that performs weight-space averaging of adapters trained on different domains"; [section 4.1] "Using Clustering as described in§2.3 leads to perplexity improvements in 8/10 novel domains compared to sentence similarity"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Selecting adapters for averaging based on domain similarity metrics improves effectiveness
- Mechanism: Identifying which training domains are most similar to the novel domain allows selective combination of adapters likely to contribute useful knowledge while avoiding noise
- Core assumption: Domain similarity metrics like clustering or sentence similarity can effectively identify beneficial adapters to combine
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.1] "Using Clustering as described in§2.3 leads to perplexity improvements in 8/10 novel domains compared to sentence similarity"; [section 2.3] "We explore various approaches for choosing which adapters to combine, such as text clustering and semantic similarity"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Averaging adapters trained with different learning rates preserves in-domain performance while maintaining generalization
- Mechanism: Adapters trained with lower learning rates converge closer to local optima, performing well on training domains, while those with higher learning rates explore more diverse solutions that generalize better to novel domains
- Core assumption: The relationship between learning rate and the balance between specialization and generalization is consistent across domains
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.2] "AdapterSoups made from adapters trained with small learning rates (5e-4) performed best in-domain... but AdapterSoups made from adapters trained with larger learning rates (7e-3, 4e-3, and 7e-4) generalize better to novel domains"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base PLM (GPT-2 small) -> Domain-specific adapters -> Selection mechanism (clustering/similarity) -> Weight averaging -> Averaged adapter for inference

- Critical path:
  1. Train individual adapters on each training domain
  2. For a novel domain, compute similarity metrics to all training domains
  3. Select top-k most similar domains based on similarity scores
  4. Load selected adapters and average their weights
  5. Use averaged adapter for inference on the novel domain

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single adapter vs. AdapterSoup: Single adapter has lower storage cost but may miss cross-domain patterns; AdapterSoup has higher storage but better generalization
  - Number of adapters to combine: More adapters may capture more patterns but risk interference; fewer adapters are safer but may miss important information
  - Selection method: Clustering may be more accurate but computationally expensive; semantic similarity is faster but potentially less precise

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on novel domains: May indicate that training domains are too dissimilar from novel domains or that selection method is ineffective
  - Worse performance than single adapter: Could suggest that averaging is introducing harmful interference between adapters
  - High variance in results: Might indicate that the selection method is inconsistent or that some domains are particularly challenging

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train adapters on two very similar domains (e.g., two news websites) and test AdapterSoup on a third similar domain to verify the basic averaging mechanism works
  2. Train adapters on two dissimilar domains (e.g., news and medical text) and test AdapterSoup to observe interference effects
  3. Test different selection methods (clustering vs. semantic similarity) on the same set of domains to compare their effectiveness in choosing adapters to combine

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the discussion and results, several questions emerge regarding the broader applicability and limitations of the approach.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to perplexity on language modeling tasks without testing on downstream tasks like classification or question answering
- Computational overhead of maintaining multiple adapters and selection process at inference time is not thoroughly discussed
- Assumes domains in the C4 dataset are well-separated, which may not reflect real-world scenarios with fuzzy domain boundaries

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- AdapterSoup improves perplexity on novel domains compared to single adapters - directly supported by experimental results showing 8/10 novel domains improving with clustering-based selection
- Weight averaging of adapters is computationally efficient compared to full model ensembling - clearly stated and the architectural design supports this claim

**Medium Confidence:**
- Text clustering outperforms sentence similarity for adapter selection - supported by experimental results but only tested on a limited set of domains
- Adapters trained with larger learning rates generalize better to novel domains - supported by experiments but the underlying mechanism is speculative

**Low Confidence:**
- The approach will generalize to other PLM architectures beyond GPT-2 small - not tested in the paper
- The benefits extend to non-language modeling tasks - not evaluated in the current work

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-task validation**: Evaluate AdapterSoup on a classification or question-answering task using the same adapter averaging approach to verify generalization beyond language modeling.

2. **Domain boundary sensitivity**: Test the selection mechanism on domains with fuzzy boundaries or gradual transitions (e.g., news articles that blend politics and sports) to assess robustness when domains aren't clearly separated.

3. **Scaling analysis**: Measure inference latency and memory usage when using AdapterSoup versus single adapters across different numbers of combined adapters to quantify the practical deployment costs.
