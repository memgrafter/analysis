# Xplainer: From X-Ray Observations to Explainable Zero-Shot Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13391
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13391
- Reference count: 23
- Authors: (Not specified in source)
- Key outcome: Zero-shot diagnosis prediction method that significantly outperforms previous approaches on chest X-ray datasets

## Executive Summary
Xplainer introduces a zero-shot diagnosis prediction method that is explainable by design, leveraging contrastive vision-language models for medical image classification. The approach uses BioVil, a CLIP model pretrained on radiology report-image pairs, combined with observation-based prompting to make predictions without label supervision. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on two chest X-ray datasets (CheXpert and ChestX-ray14) by designing informative prompts and using contrastive observation-based prompting. Xplainer's predictions are directly traceable to underlying clinical observations, making the diagnosis explainable and transparent.

## Method Summary
Xplainer adapts contrastive vision-language models to multi-label medical diagnosis by using observation-based prompting. The method employs BioVil, a CLIP model pretrained on radiology reports and images, to encode both images and text prompts into a shared embedding space. For each pathology, the model computes probabilities using both positive and negative observation prompts ("There is/are <observation> indicating <pathology>" vs "There is/are no <observation> indicating <pathology>"). These observation probabilities are then combined using a log-sum-normalized formula to produce final pathology probabilities. The approach requires no fine-tuning on target datasets, making it a true zero-shot method.

## Key Results
- Significantly outperforms previous zero-shot methods on CheXpert and ChestX-ray14 datasets
- Contrastive observation-based prompting (using both positive and negative prompts) is most effective approach
- Model is explainable by design, with diagnosis predictions directly based on underlying descriptor predictions
- Demonstrates that designing informative prompts is crucial for improving model performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The model leverages contrastive vision-language pretraining to align medical image and text embeddings, enabling zero-shot classification by matching images to descriptive clinical observations
- Mechanism: BioVil, a CLIP model pretrained on radiology report-image pairs, encodes both images and text prompts into a shared embedding space. The cosine similarity between an image embedding and text embeddings of clinical observations directly provides probability estimates for those observations
- Core assumption: The pretraining data distribution (radiology reports paired with images) is sufficiently similar to the target task so that the learned embedding alignment transfers effectively to new datasets without fine-tuning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Xplainer adapts the classification-by-description approach of contrastive vision-language models to the multi-label medical diagnosis task"
  - [section 2.1] "We built upon BioVil, a CLIP model pretrained on pairs of radiology reports and images"
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct corpus support for the specific claim about contrastive pretraining transfer; evidence comes from cited papers like [13] (Radford et al., 2021) and [1] (Boecking et al., 2022)
- Break condition: If the clinical observations used at inference differ significantly in style or terminology from the pretraining data, the embedding alignment degrades and similarity scores become unreliable

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using contrastive observation-based prompting (positive and negative prompts per observation) improves zero-shot performance by explicitly modeling the presence and absence of clinical signs
- Mechanism: For each observation, the model computes two similarity scores: one with a positive prompt ("There is/are <observation> indicating <pathology>") and one with a negative prompt ("There is/are no <observation> indicating <pathology>"). A softmax over these scores yields the final probability of the observation's presence, helping the model distinguish between similar but opposite clinical descriptions
- Core assumption: The CLIP model's text encoder can effectively differentiate between semantically similar positive and negative prompts when both are provided, and the contrastive formulation improves calibration
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that designing informative prompts is crucial to improve model performance and that contrastive observation-based prompting is the most effective approach"
  - [section 2.2] "Instead of thresholding the similarity between a positive prompt and an image, we prompt the model with both a positive and a negated version of each observation prompt and compare their probabilities"
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct corpus support; the mechanism is inferred from ablation results comparing "Basic Prompt" (positive only) vs "Contrastive Prompt" (positive and negative)
- Break condition: If the model's text encoder cannot reliably encode negation, the negative prompts may not effectively suppress false positives, reducing the benefit of contrastive prompting

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Modeling the final pathology probability as a joint product over log-transformed observation probabilities provides an explainable diagnosis by design, where the prediction is directly traceable to detected observations
- Mechanism: After obtaining observation probabilities P(op_i) for all descriptors, the pathology probability is computed as P(p) = (Σ log(P(op_i))) / N. This formulation ensures that the diagnosis emerges from the aggregation of underlying observation predictions, making the decision path transparent
- Core assumption: Observations are conditionally independent given the pathology, and all observations contribute equally to the final diagnosis probability
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our model is explainable by design, as the final diagnosis prediction is directly based on the prediction of the underlying descriptors"
  - [section 2.1] "Given these observation probabilities P(op_i), i∈ 1, ..., N, we estimate a joined probability to determine the likelihood of the presence of a pathology P(p): P(p) = (Σ log(P(op_i))) / N"
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct corpus support; the assumption of equal weighting is not validated in the paper
- Break condition: If observations are highly interdependent (e.g., certain pairs always co-occur), the independence assumption breaks down, and the joint probability may misrepresent the true pathology likelihood

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Contrastive learning and embedding alignment in vision-language models
  - Why needed here: Xplainer depends on the ability of BioVil to map images and text into a shared space where similarity reflects semantic relatedness; understanding this mechanism is essential to grasp how zero-shot inference works
  - Quick check question: What is the role of cosine similarity in a contrastive vision-language model like CLIP, and why is it used instead of dot product?

- Concept: Prompt engineering and zero-shot classification
  - Why needed here: The performance of Xplainer hinges on crafting prompts that closely match the style of radiology reports; knowing how prompt structure affects model output is critical for adapting the method
  - Quick check question: How does adding a pathology indication and report-style phrasing to prompts improve the model's ability to distinguish between similar observations?

- Concept: Multi-label classification and probability calibration
  - Why needed here: The model must output probabilities for multiple pathologies simultaneously, and proper calibration ensures that the joint probability over observations translates into meaningful diagnosis scores
  - Quick check question: Why does Xplainer use a log-sum-normalized formula for combining observation probabilities, and what assumption does this make about the observations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input images -> BioVil image encoder -> Image embeddings -> Cosine similarity with text embeddings -> Observation probabilities -> Log-sum-normalized aggregation -> Pathology probabilities -> Output diagnosis

- Critical path:
  1. Load image(s) and associated prompts for each pathology
  2. Encode image and all prompts using BioVil encoders
  3. Compute cosine similarities for positive and negative prompts
  4. Apply softmax to obtain observation probabilities
  5. Aggregate across images (if multi-view)
  6. Combine observation probabilities into pathology probabilities
  7. Output diagnosis and explanations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using BioVil's pretrained embeddings avoids fine-tuning but limits adaptability to datasets with very different report styles
  - Contrastive prompting improves discrimination but doubles the number of prompts and inference time
  - Equal weighting of observations simplifies the model but may ignore clinically important dependencies between signs

- Failure signatures:
  - Low AUC across pathologies suggests misalignment between prompt style and pretraining data
  - High false positives for pathologies with similar observations indicate poor differentiation in the embedding space
  - Inconsistent results across multiple images may point to suboptimal aggregation strategy

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate that cosine similarity between image and text embeddings correlates with clinical similarity by manually checking top-k matches for a few test images
  2. Compare performance of basic observation prompting (positive only) vs contrastive prompting (positive and negative) on a held-out validation set
  3. Test the effect of prompt refinement (ChatGPT vs radiologist-verified) by running inference with both prompt sets and measuring AUC change

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we model varying importance and complex relationships between descriptors in zero-shot diagnosis?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "As a first try to model the importance of descriptors, we look into a supervised, out-of-domain approach to model these inter-dependencies... We observe a slight performance increase... This shows that the descriptor importance learned on MIMIC can partially be transferred to an out-of-domain dataset. We believe investigating methods to consider varying importance and complex relations between the descriptors is an essential and exciting direction to investigate in future work"
- Why unresolved: The authors only tested a simple Naive Bayes approach and acknowledge that more sophisticated methods are needed to capture varying descriptor importance and complex relationships
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance comparison of different modeling approaches (e.g., attention mechanisms, graph neural networks, or other probabilistic models) that explicitly capture descriptor relationships and importance in zero-shot diagnosis

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the framework be extended to other medical imaging domains beyond chest X-rays?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "We envision that our approach can be extended to other medical imaging domains and have practical applications in real-world scenarios"
- Why unresolved: The authors only evaluated their approach on chest X-ray datasets and did not explore its applicability to other imaging modalities
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing the effectiveness of the Xplainer framework on different medical imaging domains (e.g., CT, MRI, ultrasound) and comparing performance to domain-specific approaches

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the model handle rare or previously unseen pathologies in zero-shot diagnosis?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors demonstrate that their observation-based approach can adapt to new clinical findings without requiring labeled data, but they do not explicitly test the model's performance on rare or unseen pathologies
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide evidence on how well the model generalizes to rare or previously unseen pathologies that were not part of the training data
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the model's performance on rare vs. common pathologies, and its ability to generalize to completely new pathologies not seen during training

## Limitations
- Performance highly dependent on similarity between pretraining data (radiology reports) and target dataset styles
- Equal weighting assumption in joint probability calculation may not reflect clinical reality where some observations are more diagnostic than others
- Requires careful prompt engineering which can be resource-intensive and may need domain expert involvement

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Contrastive pretraining alignment): **High** - Strong theoretical foundation and empirical results with direct citations to established work
- Mechanism 2 (Contrastive observation prompting): **Medium** - Empirical improvement shown but lacks detailed theoretical analysis
- Mechanism 3 (Joint probability calculation): **Low** - Equal weighting assumption not validated and independence assumption likely violated in practice
