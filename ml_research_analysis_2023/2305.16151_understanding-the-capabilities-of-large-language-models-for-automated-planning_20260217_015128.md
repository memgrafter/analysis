# Understanding the Capabilities of Large Language Models for Automated Planning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.16151
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16151
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Primary result**: LLMs pre-trained on code outperform those trained on natural language for plan generation, and fine-tuning significantly improves performance, but generalization to unseen domains remains limited.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how large language models (LLMs) can be applied to automated planning by generating sequences of actions to achieve specific goals. The research addresses four key questions: whether LLMs can effectively solve planning problems, how different pre-training data affect performance, whether fine-tuning or prompting approaches work better, and whether LLMs can generalize plans to new situations. Using six classical planning domains and evaluating multiple LLMs through both fine-tuning and prompting techniques, the study introduces a new metric to measure plan generalization. Results show that code-pretrained models significantly outperform natural language models, fine-tuning produces better results than prompting, but LLMs struggle with generalizing plans to unseen domains.

## Method Summary
The study uses six classical planning domains (Ferry, Blocksworld, Miconic, Tower of Hanoi, Grippers, Driverlog) and generates 18,000 problems per domain using IPC problem generators. Optimal plans are generated using the FastDownward planner. The research compares two approaches: fine-tuning pre-trained LLMs (T5, CodeT5, Codegen) on a compact PDDL representation of planning problems, and prompting OpenAI models (text-davinci-03, code-davinci-02) using zero-shot and few-shot strategies. A new plan generalization error metric (Epg) is introduced to measure how well generated plans match optimal plans for unseen problems. The study evaluates plan generation capabilities through multiple metrics including satisficing, optimal, invalid plans, and degree of correctness.

## Key Results
- Code-pretrained LLMs significantly outperform natural language models in generating valid and optimal plans
- Fine-tuning pre-trained models produces substantially better plan generation than zero-shot prompting
- LLMs demonstrate limited ability to generalize plans to unseen domains, object name variations, and different plan lengths
- The proposed plan generalization error metric effectively quantifies differences between generated and optimal plans

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Fine-tuning LLMs pre-trained on code significantly improves their ability to generate near-optimal plans compared to zero-shot prompting. This works because fine-tuning updates model weights using labeled planning problems, allowing the model to adapt its internal representations to PDDL syntax and semantics, while prompting relies on pre-existing knowledge that may not align with planning domain specifications.

### Mechanism 2
LLMs pre-trained on both natural language and programming code are more effective at plan generation than those trained solely on natural language. This works because code-pretrained models have learned representations that align more closely with the formal syntax and structure of PDDL, enabling them to better parse and generate plans that adhere to domain constraints.

### Mechanism 3
The plan generalization error metric (Epg) effectively quantifies how well LLMs generate plans that match optimal plans for unseen problems. This works by using Hamming distance between the sequence of actions in generated and optimal plans, providing a normalized measure of deviation that accounts for the total number of actions in the domain.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data preparation: PDDL domain and problem files → compact representation → training/testing datasets → Models: Pre-trained LLMs (T5, CodeT5, Codegen, GPT variants) → fine-tuning or prompting → Evaluation: Plan verification tool (VAL) → metrics (satisficing, optimal, degree of correctness, Epg) → Generalization tasks: plan length, object name randomization, unseen domains
- **Critical path**: Data preparation → Model training/fine-tuning → Plan generation → Plan verification → Evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Compact representation reduces token length but may lose information; fine-tuning requires labeled data but yields better performance; Hamming distance is simple but may not capture all plan similarities
- **Failure signatures**: Low satisficing or optimal plan percentages indicate model struggles; high Epg values suggest poor generalization; long inference times indicate generation difficulties
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Fine-tune CodeT5 on compact PDDL representation and evaluate on test set; 2) Compare zero-shot and few-shot prompting of code-davinci on same test set; 3) Test best fine-tuned model on three plan generalization tasks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What specific architectural modifications to LLMs would enhance their ability to generalize across unseen planning domains? The paper concludes fine-tuned models struggle with unseen domains and recommends further research into improving generalization capabilities.
- How does the performance of fine-tuned LLMs compare to traditional planners on complex, real-world planning problems with uncertain or dynamic environments? The study focuses on deterministic problems that don't reflect real-world complexity.
- What is the relationship between the diversity of pre-training data and the generalization capabilities of LLMs in planning domains? The study only compares code vs. text pre-training without exploring how different distributions of planning data affect performance.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to six classical planning domains that may not represent real-world complexity
- Plan generalization error metric relies on Hamming distance which may not capture all aspects of plan similarity
- Compact PDDL representation used for fine-tuning is not fully specified, affecting reproducibility

## Confidence
- **High**: Code-pretrained LLMs outperform natural language models for plan generation tasks
- **Medium**: Fine-tuning significantly improves plan generation compared to zero-shot prompting
- **Medium**: LLMs exhibit limited plan generalization capabilities, particularly for unseen domains
