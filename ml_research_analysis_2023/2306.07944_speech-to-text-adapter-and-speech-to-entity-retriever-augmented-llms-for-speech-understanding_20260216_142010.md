# Speech-to-Text Adapter and Speech-to-Entity Retriever Augmented LLMs for Speech Understanding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07944
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07944
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Achieves 34.6% dialog state tracking accuracy on DSTC11 challenge, outperforming cascaded systems

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for improving speech understanding tasks by leveraging large language models (LLMs). The proposed method, called retrieval-augmented speech language model (ReSLM), consists of a joint speech and language model (SLM) and a Speech2Entity retriever. The SLM uses a Speech2Text adapter to map speech encodings into text token embeddings, allowing the model to utilize pre-trained LLMs directly for speech tasks. The Speech2Entity retriever uses speech to retrieve relevant entities, which are then added to the original SLM input as a prefix. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach on the DSTC11 dialog state tracking task, achieving a significant improvement in performance compared to cascaded systems. Specifically, ReSLM achieves a 34.6% accuracy in dialog state tracking, compared to 24.7% for SLM and 31.8% for a cascaded system. The authors also show that the ASR performance improves from 9.4% to 8.5% WER when augmenting the ASR task with the dialog understanding task.

## Method Summary
The method involves a joint speech and language model (SLM) with a Speech2Text adapter and a Speech2Entity retriever. The Speech2Text adapter maps speech encodings into text token embeddings, allowing the model to utilize pre-trained LLMs directly for speech tasks. The Speech2Entity retriever uses speech to retrieve relevant entities, which are then added to the original SLM input as a prefix. The model is trained using multi-task objective including ASR and DST tasks.

## Key Results
- ReSLM achieves 34.6% dialog state tracking accuracy on DSTC11 challenge
- Significant improvement over cascaded system (31.8%) and SLM (24.7%)
- ASR performance improves from 9.4% to 8.5% WER when augmenting with dialog understanding

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CTC-based blank filtering reduces speech sequence length to match text token rate, enabling effective joint modeling
- Mechanism: The CTC model outputs blank symbols that can be filtered out, compressing speech from 75ms frames to semantic tokens every ~305ms
- Core assumption: Blank-filtered speech retains sufficient semantic information for downstream NLU tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "using a CTC-based blank-filtering, we can reduce the speech sequence length to that of text"
  - [section] "Filtering CTC blank frames results in a very strong compression of the speech signal and makes down-stream NLU tasks substantially easier while preserving the semantic information"
- Break condition: If blank-filtered speech loses critical phonetic or prosodic information needed for accurate entity recognition

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Speech2Text adapter successfully maps speech encodings to text embedding space without training the large language model
- Mechanism: Self-attention layers transform CTC-filtered speech embeddings to match text token embedding space, allowing frozen LLM use
- Core assumption: Speech and text embeddings share sufficient geometric structure for adapter-based mapping
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "which maps speech into text token embedding space without speech information loss"
  - [section] "The Speech2Text adapter consists of a few self-attention layers to map the CTC-filtered speech encodings to the text token embeddings"
- Break condition: If speech and text embedding spaces have fundamentally different structures that cannot be bridged by simple adapter layers

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Retrieval-augmented approach addresses rare entity recognition by retrieving entities from speech input
- Mechanism: Speech2Entity retriever uses acoustic encodings to find relevant entities from a pool, which are prepended to text input
- Core assumption: Acoustic representations contain sufficient information to identify relevant entities even when text recognition fails
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Speech2Entity retriever, which uses speech to retrieve relevant entities, and then adds them to the original SLM input as a prefix"
  - [section] "The main task of the retriever is to extract a subset of entities from a list of given task-specific entities that are relevant for the current speech input"
- Break condition: If acoustic-only entity retrieval cannot distinguish between similar-sounding entities or lacks context

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CTC encoder → blank filtering → Speech2Text adapter → LLM encoder → retriever → prepended entities → LLM decoder
- Critical path: Speech → CTC encoder → blank filtering → adapter → LLM → output
- Design tradeoffs: Frozen LLM vs. trainable LLM (performance vs. parameter efficiency), entity retrieval recall vs. precision
- Failure signatures: Poor adapter mapping (speech embeddings don't align with text space), retriever noise overwhelming input, blank filtering losing critical information
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test CTC blank filtering: Compare WER and semantic content retention with and without blank filtering on a validation set
  2. Test Speech2Text adapter: Measure cosine similarity between speech-transformed and text embeddings across diverse speech inputs
  3. Test retriever quality: Evaluate precision@k and recall@k for entity retrieval on held-out speech with known entities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of ReSLM scale with increasing size of the entity pool?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention using a pool of 2.5k entities for training and a separate pool of 14k entities for evaluation.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the performance impact of using entity pools of different sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing ReSLM performance with varying entity pool sizes.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the Speech2Entity retriever be improved to achieve better precision without sacrificing recall?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that the retriever has poor precision (3.6% at R@10) but this does not significantly impact ReSLM performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore methods to improve the retriever's precision.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing ReSLM performance with improved Speech2Entity retriever models.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does ReSLM perform on other speech understanding tasks beyond dialog state tracking?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that "while this work reports results on a dialog tracking task, the model is applicable more widely for speech understanding tasks."
- Why unresolved: The paper only reports results on the DSTC11 dialog state tracking task.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating ReSLM on other speech understanding tasks such as intent classification or slot filling.

## Limitations
- Architectural complexity makes it difficult to isolate component contributions to performance
- Limited quantitative analysis of Speech2Entity retriever quality and precision-recall tradeoffs
- Evaluation focuses only on DSTC11 dialog state tracking task, limiting generalization claims

## Confidence
**High Confidence Claims**:
- Baseline SLM (24.7% JGA) and cascaded system (31.8% JGA) performance accurately reported
- 9.4% to 8.5% WER improvement demonstrates joint modeling benefits
- Overall architecture (CTC encoder → blank filtering → adapter → LLM) is technically sound

**Medium Confidence Claims**:
- 34.6% JGA improvement over baselines, though exact component contributions unclear
- Blank filtering preserves semantic information, though robustness across acoustic conditions uncertain
- Speech2Text adapter bridges embedding spaces, though performance may vary with speech domain

**Low Confidence Claims**:
- Specific contribution of Speech2Entity retriever to performance gains
- General applicability beyond dialog state tracking and MultiWOZ domain
- Robustness to out-of-vocabulary entities and rare names
