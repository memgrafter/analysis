# Controlled Text Generation with Natural Language Instructions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces INSTRUCT CTG, a novel approach to controlled text generation that leverages instruction-tuning and in-context learning. The method extracts constraints from natural texts using NLP tools, verbalizes them into natural language instructions, and fine-tunes a pre-trained language model. Compared to existing methods, INSTRUCT CTG is more flexible, has a smaller impact on generation quality and speed, and can adapt to new constraints through few-shot learning. Experiments show that INSTRUCT CTG achieves high constraint satisfaction rates and generates fluent text, outperforming state-of-the-art methods while being significantly faster.

## Method Summary
INSTRUCT CTG is a controlled text generation framework that incorporates various constraints by conditioning on natural language descriptions and demonstrations of the constraints. The method extracts constraints from natural texts using NLP tools and heuristics, verbalizes them into natural language instructions, and fine-tunes a pre-trained language model. During inference, these instructions are prepended to the input, allowing the model to understand and incorporate constraints without modifying the decoding procedure. The approach leverages the instruction-following and few-shot learning abilities of large language models to achieve high constraint satisfaction rates while maintaining generation fluency and speed.

## Key Results
- INSTRUCT CTG achieves high constraint satisfaction rates and generates fluent text, outperforming state-of-the-art methods.
- The method is more flexible to different constraint types and has a much smaller impact on generation quality and speed compared to existing search-based or score-based methods.
- INSTRUCT CTG can adapt to new constraints without re-training through the use of few-shot task generalization and in-context learning abilities of instruction-tuned language models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: InstructCTG achieves high constraint satisfaction rates by leveraging instruction-tuning and in-context learning abilities of large language models.
- Mechanism: The model is fine-tuned on synthesized data where constraints are verbalized into natural language instructions. During inference, these instructions are prepended to the input, allowing the model to understand and incorporate constraints without modifying the decoding procedure.
- Core assumption: Large language models have emergent abilities to follow natural language instructions and learn from demonstrations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By prepending natural language descriptions of the constraints and a few demonstrations, we fine-tune a pre-trained language model to incorporate various types of constraints."
  - [section 3.5] "After data synthesis, we use the combination of instruction-tuning... by connecting the natural language instruction for the constraints and a few demonstrations... as the input text."
- Break condition: If the language model does not have sufficient instruction-following ability or if the constraints cannot be effectively verbalized into natural language instructions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: InstructCTG generates more fluent text compared to decoding-time methods because it does not modify the output distribution space.
- Mechanism: Unlike search-based or score-based methods that alter the decoding procedure, InstructCTG generates text using conventional decoding algorithms (e.g., beam search or top-k sampling) after conditioning on the instruction prompt. This preserves the original output distribution and generation quality.
- Core assumption: Modifying the decoding procedure negatively impacts generation quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Compared to existing search-based or score-based methods, InstructCTG is more flexible to different constraint types and has a much smaller impact on the generation quality and speed because it does not modify the decoding procedure."
  - [section 2] "While effective in guiding the model to incorporate the constraints, existing decoding-time methods for controlled text generation suffer from a number of limitations. First, both search-based and score-based methods modify the output sequence distribution and thus generally result in worse generation quality."
- Break condition: If the instruction prompt significantly shifts the output distribution despite not modifying decoding.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: InstructCTG can adapt to new constraints through few-shot learning without re-training by providing demonstrations in the prompt.
- Mechanism: The model is trained to understand examples of constraints provided in the prompt. When encountering a new constraint, a user can write a natural language description and include a few demonstrations, enabling the model to generate text satisfying the new constraint.
- Core assumption: The model learns a meta-learning capability during training that allows it to generalize from few examples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Additionally, InstructCTG allows the model to adapt to new constraints without re-training through the use of few-shot task generalization and in-context learning abilities of instruction-tuned language models."
  - [section 3.5] "Moreover, to improve the ability of InstructCTG to few-shot generalize to unseen constraints without fine-tuning the model, we incorporate meta in-context learning... by including a few demonstrations of the constraints in the prefix."
- Break condition: If the model cannot effectively learn from demonstrations or if the new constraints are too dissimilar from those seen during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data Synthesis Module -> Instruction-Tuned Model -> Inference Module

- Critical path:
  1. Input text is processed to extract constraints using NLP tools.
  2. Constraints are verbalized into natural language instructions.
  3. Instructions and demonstrations are combined into a prompt.
  4. Prompt is fed into the instruction-tuned model.
  5. Model generates text using standard decoding algorithms.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Flexibility vs. Performance: InstructCTG is more flexible to different constraint types but may not achieve the same constraint satisfaction rates as specialized decoding-time methods.
  - Speed vs. Quality: By not modifying decoding, InstructCTG is faster but relies on the model's ability to understand instructions for constraint satisfaction.
  - Training Complexity: Requires synthesizing a large dataset with various constraints, which involves using NLP tools and designing verbalization templates.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low constraint satisfaction rates despite high fluency: The model may not be effectively understanding the instructions.
  - Poor performance on new constraints: The model may not have learned to generalize from demonstrations.
  - Slow inference times: Inefficient prompt construction or model architecture issues.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test constraint satisfaction and fluency on seen constraints using the provided test sets.
  2. Evaluate few-shot learning ability on unseen constraints by providing demonstrations in the prompt.
  3. Measure inference speed compared to baseline methods like NeuroLogic decoding and Diffusion-LM.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of the method depends on the quality of NLP tools and heuristics used for constraint extraction, which may not generalize well to diverse domains and text types.
- The success of InstructCTG relies heavily on the instruction-following and few-shot learning abilities of large language models, which may not be robust or transfer well to the task of controlled text generation.
- The evaluation of the method is limited to specific constraint types and datasets, and its performance on more complex, multi-constraint scenarios and in real-world applications remains to be seen.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The claim that InstructCTG can adapt to new constraints through few-shot learning is well-supported by the experimental results.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that InstructCTG generates more fluent text compared to decoding-time methods is supported by the lower perplexity scores, but could be strengthened by incorporating human evaluations or additional metrics.
- Low Confidence: The claim that InstructCTG has a much smaller impact on generation speed is based on a single comparison and would benefit from more comprehensive speed comparisons with a variety of baseline methods.
