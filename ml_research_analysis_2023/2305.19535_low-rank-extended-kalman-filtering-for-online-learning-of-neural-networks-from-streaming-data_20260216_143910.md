# Low-rank extended Kalman filtering for online learning of neural networks from streaming data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19535
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19535
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel low-rank EKF method achieves linear complexity and outperforms baselines in online neural network learning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a low-rank extended Kalman filter (EKF) approach for online learning of neural network parameters from streaming data. The key innovation is approximating the posterior precision matrix as diagonal plus low-rank, enabling linear time complexity in the number of model parameters. The method demonstrates superior sample efficiency and adaptation to changing data distributions compared to online gradient descent and stochastic variational inference baselines across classification and regression tasks.

## Method Summary
The method recursively estimates the posterior over neural network parameters using an extended Kalman filter framework. It represents the posterior precision matrix as a diagonal matrix plus a low-rank matrix, enabling efficient updates through rank-1 updates and low-rank approximations. The algorithm alternates between predict and update steps, using singular value decomposition to maintain the low-rank structure. Hyperparameters like system noise and dynamics scaling are tuned to optimize performance. The approach provides uncertainty estimates by propagating the posterior through the model, which is particularly useful for online decision-making tasks like contextual bandits.

## Key Results
- LO-FI outperforms online gradient descent and stochastic variational inference in terms of sample efficiency and adaptation to changing data distributions
- Achieves lower test misclassification rates and root mean squared errors compared to baselines on Fashion-MNIST and UCI regression datasets
- Demonstrates improved cumulative reward in contextual bandit settings compared to other online learning methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The diagonal plus low-rank precision matrix approximation enables linear time complexity in the number of parameters.
- Mechanism: By decomposing the posterior precision matrix into diagonal plus low-rank components, the algorithm can efficiently update the covariance using rank-1 updates and low-rank approximations, avoiding the cubic complexity of full matrix inversion.
- Core assumption: The posterior precision matrix can be well-approximated by a diagonal matrix plus a low-rank matrix without significant loss of accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "uses a novel low-rank plus diagonal decomposition of the posterior precision matrix, which gives a cost per step which is linear in the number of model parameters"
  - [section] "We therefore call our method LO-FI, which is short for low-rank extended Kalman filter"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on low-rank Kalman filtering but not specifically on the diagonal-plus-low-rank decomposition for neural networks
- Break condition: When the posterior precision matrix has significant off-diagonal structure that cannot be captured by a low-rank approximation, leading to poor performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The method achieves faster learning and adaptation to changing distributions compared to diagonal approximations.
- Mechanism: By maintaining a low-rank approximation of the posterior precision matrix, the method captures parameter correlations that diagonal approximations ignore, leading to more efficient learning and faster adaptation to distribution shifts.
- Core assumption: Parameter correlations in the posterior are important for efficient learning and adaptation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "experimental results... show that this approach outperforms various baseline algorithms, including online gradient descent and stochastic variational inference methods, in terms of sample efficiency and adaptation to changing data distributions"
  - [section] "In contrast to methods based on stochastic variational inference, our method is fully deterministic, and does not require step-size tuning"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss low-rank approximations but don't specifically compare to diagonal approximations for neural networks
- Break condition: When the data distribution is static and parameter correlations are not crucial for learning, diagonal approximations may perform comparably.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The method provides useful uncertainty estimates for online decision-making tasks.
- Mechanism: The posterior distribution over parameters, represented as a Gaussian with diagonal plus low-rank precision, can be propagated through the model to obtain uncertainty estimates for predictions, which are crucial for tasks like contextual bandits.
- Core assumption: The Gaussian approximation to the posterior is sufficient for capturing uncertainty relevant to decision-making.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the posterior uncertainty in the parameters can be propagated into the uncertainty of the predictive distribution for observations, which is crucial for online decision-making tasks"
  - [section] "We show experimentally that we can combine the LOFI posterior with Thompson sampling to get improved results over other methods"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - corpus papers discuss Kalman filtering for uncertainty estimation, but not specifically for neural networks in decision-making contexts
- Break condition: When the posterior uncertainty is poorly approximated by a Gaussian, leading to unreliable uncertainty estimates for decision-making.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Predict step -> Update step -> SVD projection -> Prediction for observations
- Critical path: Predict → Update → SVD projection → Prediction for observations
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Rank L vs. computational cost: Higher rank provides better approximation but increases computation
  - Diagonal vs. spherical approximation: Spherical approximation is faster but may be less accurate
  - Plugin approximation vs. Monte Carlo: Plugin is faster but may underestimate uncertainty
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance when posterior precision has significant off-diagonal structure
  - Slow adaptation when rank is too low to capture important parameter correlations
  - Numerical instability when observation noise is very low

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of LO-FI compare to particle filtering methods in terms of sample efficiency and adaptation to changing data distributions?
- Basis in paper: The paper states "There are many papers on continual learning, which is related to online learning. However the CL literature usually assumes the task boundaries... are given to the learner" and "It is possible to go beyond Gaussian approximations by using particle filtering (see e.g., (Yang et al., 2023)). However, we focus on faster deterministic inference methods, since speed is important for many real time online decision making tasks (Ghunaim et al., 2023)."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any experimental comparison between LO-FI and particle filtering methods, despite acknowledging their existence and potential relevance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing LO-FI to particle filtering methods on the same datasets and tasks, measuring sample efficiency, adaptation speed, and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the rank L of the low-rank approximation on the performance of LO-FI for different types of data distributions (stationary vs non-stationary)?
- Basis in paper: The paper states "In fig. 12 we show how changing the rank L of LO-FI affects performance within the range 1 to 50. For NLL it has little impact; for misclassification we see that larger L is better, with gains plateauing at L = 10." and "In fig. 16a, we show misclassification for the current task as a function of LO-FI rank; as before, performance increases with rank, and plateaus at L = 10."
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows the effect of rank on performance for specific datasets, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how the optimal rank varies with the type of data distribution (stationary vs non-stationary) or other factors like model complexity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the rank L for different types of data distributions (stationary, piecewise stationary, slowly changing) and model architectures, analyzing the trade-off between performance and computational cost.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of LO-FI compare to other online Bayesian inference methods, such as stochastic variational inference (SVI) and online Laplace approximation, in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency?
- Basis in paper: The paper states "We show experimentally that this results in much faster (more sample efficient) learning, which results in more rapid adaptation to changing distributions, and faster accumulation of reward when used as part of a contextual bandit algorithm" and "we show experimentally that LOFI outperforms online Laplace in terms of NLL on various regression tasks." It also mentions LRVGA (low-rank recursive variational Gaussian approximation) as a baseline method.
- Why unresolved: While the paper compares LO-FI to some online Bayesian inference methods, it does not provide a comprehensive comparison with all relevant methods, such as SVI, or a detailed analysis of the trade-offs between accuracy and computational efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing LO-FI to a wide range of online Bayesian inference methods, including SVI and online Laplace approximation, on various tasks and datasets, measuring accuracy, computational efficiency, and sample efficiency.

## Limitations
- The diagonal-plus-low-rank approximation may not capture complex posterior correlations in high-dimensional parameter spaces
- Performance heavily depends on appropriate rank selection, which requires careful hyperparameter tuning
- Limited theoretical analysis of the approximation error and its impact on convergence guarantees

## Confidence

- High confidence in the computational complexity claims: The linear time complexity through low-rank approximation is well-established in the matrix computation literature
- Medium confidence in empirical performance claims: While experimental results are promising, the comparison is limited to specific datasets and may not generalize to all problem domains
- Medium confidence in the adaptation claims: The method shows good adaptation to changing distributions in experiments, but the mechanism for why this outperforms other methods could be better explained
