# GCI: A (G)raph (C)oncept (I)nterpretation Framework

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04899
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04899
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: GCI achieves 0.76 AUCROC completeness score on BBBP molecular property prediction task

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GCI, a framework for quantitatively analyzing concepts extracted from Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The framework encodes human-provided interpretations as binary functions and measures their alignment with extracted concepts, enabling evaluation of concept extractors, measurement of concept-interpretation alignment, and assessment of interpretation completeness. Experiments on synthetic datasets demonstrate GCI's ability to differentiate between concept extractors of varying quality, while a molecular property prediction task shows practical utility with a 0.76 AUCROC completeness score.

## Method Summary
GCI provides a quantitative framework for analyzing concepts extracted from GNNs by encoding human interpretations as binary functions and measuring their alignment with extracted concepts. The framework computes Interpretation-Alignment (IA) matrices that show how well extracted concepts match human-provided interpretations, and measures completeness by treating interpretations as features for classification tasks. GCI was validated on synthetic Barabási-Albert graphs and applied to Blood-Brain Barrier Penetration (BBBP) molecular property prediction using chemical functional groups as interpretations.

## Key Results
- GCI achieves 0.76 AUCROC completeness score on BBBP molecular property prediction task
- GCExplainer demonstrates near-perfect alignment with interpretations in synthetic experiments, while other extractors show significantly lower alignment
- IA matrices quantitatively differentiate concept extractor quality, enabling objective comparison of interpretation alignment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GCI quantitatively measures concept-interpretation alignment by encoding interpretations as binary functions and computing precision.
- Mechanism: The framework applies each interpretation function to all graphs in a concept and computes the fraction that match, producing an Interpretation-Alignment (IA) matrix.
- Core assumption: Interpretation functions accurately capture the intended semantic meaning of human-provided interpretations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "GCI encodes concept interpretations as functions, which can be used to quantitatively measure the alignment between a given interpretation and concept definition."
  - [section 3.3] "Using the above definitions, we can measure the alignment between a given concept and an interpretation as q : G×H→ R, where q(G,h ) is defined as: q(G,h ) =|{h(g) is true, g∈G}|/|G|"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on molecular property prediction but don't validate the alignment computation method
- Break condition: If interpretation functions are too coarse or miss edge cases, alignment scores become misleading and fail to capture true concept-interpretation relationships.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GCI enables evaluation of concept extractor quality by comparing extracted concepts against ground truth interpretations.
- Mechanism: By computing IA matrices for different concept extractors (GCExplainer, NoisyGCExplainer, RandomExplainer), GCI reveals which extractors best align with human interpretations.
- Core assumption: Ground truth interpretations are available and correctly capture the underlying concepts in the data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "Crucially, the IA matrix quantitatively shows the vast difference between explainers (with GCExplainer aligning the best). Such a conclusion would have been much more difficult to draw from simple visual inspection."
  - [section 4.1] "GCExplainer has near-perfect alignment with the interpretations, whilst the other two explainers demonstrate a significantly lower alignment."
  - [corpus] Moderate - related work validates interpretability but doesn't benchmark concept extractor comparison methods
- Break condition: If ground truth interpretations are incomplete or incorrect, evaluation becomes circular and fails to identify the best concept extractor.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GCI measures interpretation completeness and predictability by treating interpretations as features for classification tasks.
- Mechanism: GCI applies interpretation functions to the entire dataset to create an interpretation representation, then measures classification accuracy (completeness) and model predictability scores.
- Core assumption: Interpretation completeness correlates with practical utility for the end task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4] "we can apply our interpretation functions directly to a model's training graph dataset, and thus obtain an interpretation representation of this dataset. We can then measure the completeness of that representation, in the same way as the completeness of a concept representation."
  - [section 4.3] "we also measured the completeness of the implemented interpretations, achieving a 0.76 AUCROC score, only 0.12 points below the original model score of 0.88."
  - [corpus] Weak - related work focuses on interpretability metrics but doesn't validate completeness as a proxy for task relevance
- Break condition: If interpretations capture redundant information or miss task-relevant features, completeness scores become inflated without practical benefit.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Concept Extractor -> Interpretation function registry -> IA matrix computation engine -> Completeness/Predictability measurement module -> Visualization components
- Critical path: Concept extraction → Interpretation encoding → IA matrix computation → Completeness evaluation → Visualization
- Design tradeoffs: Tradeoff between interpretation granularity (more detailed vs. more general functions) and computational cost; tradeoff between precision of alignment measurement vs. interpretability of results
- Failure signatures: Low IA scores across all interpretations indicate poor concept extraction; high completeness but low predictability suggests interpretations don't match model learned features; inconsistent IA matrices across different extractors suggest interpretation functions are ambiguous

## Limitations

- Framework relies on human-provided interpretation functions whose quality and completeness are not independently validated
- Synthetic experiments may not generalize to real-world complexity and diverse graph domains
- Limited comparison against established interpretability methods like LIME or SHAP for benchmarking

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The framework's core mathematical formulation and synthetic dataset experiments demonstrating concept extractor differentiation
- **Medium Confidence**: The BBBP molecular property prediction results and interpretation completeness scores
- **Low Confidence**: Generalization claims to other graph domains and scalability assertions without empirical validation
