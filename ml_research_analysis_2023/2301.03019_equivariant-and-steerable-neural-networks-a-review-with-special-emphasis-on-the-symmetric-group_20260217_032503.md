# Equivariant and Steerable Neural Networks: A review with special emphasis on the symmetric group

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
This paper offers a comprehensive review of group equivariant and steerable neural networks, with particular emphasis on their application to the symmetric group. The authors consolidate existing knowledge on achieving invariance to various transformations beyond translations, generalizing from regular convolutional neural networks to group equivariant networks and further to steerable networks. While primarily a review, the paper makes a notable contribution by working out specific details for the symmetric group, which are not commonly found in the literature. The authors explain how these networks achieve efficiency gains through parameter sharing and the use of group representations, providing a deeper understanding of constructing and applying these networks to problems involving permutations and coordinate transformations.

## Method Summary
The paper synthesizes mathematical foundations of group equivariant and steerable neural networks, including group theory and representation theory. It explains the generalization from regular CNNs to group equivariant networks and further to steerable networks, highlighting efficiency gains through parameter sharing and group representations. The authors apply this theory to the symmetric group, providing explicit constructions of basis filters and discussing implications for capsule design and nonlinearities. The review also touches on the equivalence between group equivariant networks and regular steerable networks, though this equivalence is presented more as a theoretical observation than a practical implementation guide.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
These networks achieve equivariance through the mathematical framework of group theory and representation theory. By designing network architectures that respect the symmetries of a particular transformation group (like the symmetric group), the networks can learn features that are invariant to these transformations. The key mechanism is parameter sharing across different group elements, which allows the network to generalize across transformations while maintaining efficiency. Steerable networks extend this by allowing features to transform in specific ways under group actions, providing more flexibility while maintaining equivariance.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input -> Feature Maps -> Group Equivariant Convolution -> Nonlinearity -> Group Pooling -> Output

**Critical Path**: The critical path involves the group equivariant convolution operation, which ensures that the network respects the symmetries of the chosen transformation group. This is followed by appropriate nonlinearities and pooling operations that maintain equivariance.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The main tradeoff is between expressiveness and computational efficiency. While group equivariant networks can capture more symmetries, they require more parameters and computational resources. Steerable networks offer a middle ground by allowing controlled transformation of features. The choice of group (like the symmetric group) determines which symmetries the network can exploit.

**Failure Signatures**: Networks may fail when the chosen group does not match the symmetries present in the data, leading to either over- or under-fitting. Computational complexity can become prohibitive for large groups. The theoretical equivalence between network types may not hold in practice due to implementation details or numerical precision issues.
