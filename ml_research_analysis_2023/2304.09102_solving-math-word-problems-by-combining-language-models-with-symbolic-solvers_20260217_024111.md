# Solving Math Word Problems by Combining Language Models With Symbolic Solvers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.09102
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09102
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Key outcome:** Declarative incremental formalization with symbolic solvers outperforms procedural reasoning on challenging algebra problems

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for solving math word problems by combining large language models (LLMs) with external symbolic solvers. The method incrementally formalizes problems as variables and equations, then offloads the actual solving to SymPy. The approach achieves comparable accuracy to Program-Aided Language models on grade-school problems while outperforming them by 20% on more challenging algebra textbook problems.

## Method Summary
The method uses few-shot prompting with LLMs to generate step-by-step solutions that incrementally introduce variables and equations. Each sentence in the solution either introduces a new variable or states a new equation, with each equation only using previously introduced variables. The LLM generates these formalizations but does not attempt to solve them. Instead, the resulting system of equations is passed to SymPy for solving. The approach is evaluated using Codex (code-davinci-002) with 3-shot examples and principles for the DECLARATIVE prompt strategy.

## Key Results
- Achieves comparable accuracy to PAL on GSM8K benchmark of grade-school math problems
- Outperforms PAL by 20% absolute on ALGEBRA dataset of challenging algebra textbook problems
- Dramatically improves accuracy (69.4% to 22.4%) by offloading equation solving to SymPy versus relying on LLM arithmetic

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Declarative incremental formalization improves equation generation quality by decomposing problems into variable definitions and constraints.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of generating procedural steps (like PAL), the LLM generates interleaved natural language and formal equations incrementally. Each sentence introduces a new variable or equation, and each equation only uses previously introduced variables. This mirrors human problem-solving strategies in algebra.
- **Core assumption:** Incremental variable and equation declaration is more robust than procedural reasoning for algebraic word problems.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our approach achieves comparable accuracy to the original PAL on the GSM8 K benchmark of math word problems and outperforms PAL by an absolute 20% on ALGEBRA, a new dataset of more challenging word problems extracted from Algebra textbooks."
  - [section] "Each sentence in the solution either introduces a new variable or states a new equation... Each equation only uses previously introduced variables."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. No directly comparable mechanism cited in neighbors.
- **Break condition:** If the problem requires procedural reasoning (e.g., step-by-step calculation) or contains complex dependencies that require backtracking, the incremental declarative approach may struggle.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Offloading equation solving to SymPy avoids arithmetic errors and improves final accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** The LLM generates symbolic equations but does not attempt to solve them. Instead, SymPy is used to solve the system of equations algebraically. This removes the arithmetic reasoning burden from the LLM.
- **Core assumption:** Symbolic solvers are more accurate than LLMs for arithmetic and algebraic computation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our approach achieves comparable accuracy to the original PAL on the GSM8 K benchmark... and outperforms PAL by an absolute 20% on ALGEBRA"
  - [section] "Instead of relying on the LLM to solve the equations directly, we pass the equations to an external symbolic solver to do the calculation."
  - [section] "Asking the LLM to solve the equations directly leads to a dramatic drop in accuracy (from 69.4% to 22.4%), which highlights the benefit of using an external solver."
- **Break condition:** If the LLM generates incorrect or inconsistent equations, the symbolic solver will fail to find a solution.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Few-shot prompting with carefully crafted examples improves LLM performance on formalization.
- **Mechanism:** The DECLARATIVE prompt uses 3-shot examples that illustrate step-by-step and declarative thinking. The examples include interleaved natural language and formal equations, and the prompt includes a list of principles for the solution.
- **Core assumption:** Few-shot prompting with relevant examples can effectively guide the LLM to follow the desired solution structure.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "To solve a new word problem, xtest, we append it to p and pass p||xtest to an LLM, which generates ytest as the solution for xtest."
  - [section] "Few-shot prompting is a technique that uses LLMs to solve a task by providing the LLMs with a few demonstrations of the task as part of the input at inference time"
  - [section] "The second prompt (3-shot) uses three examples that we designed to help illustrate step-by-step and declarative thinking and the formalization format we expect."
- **Break condition:** If the examples are not representative or the principles are not clear, the LLM may not follow the desired format.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** LLM (Codex) -> Few-shot prompt (DECLARATIVE) -> Symbolic solver (SymPy) -> Output solution
- **Critical path:**
  1. Input math word problem
  2. Append to DECLARATIVE prompt
  3. Generate solution using LLM
  4. Extract variables and equations
  5. Solve equations using SymPy
  6. Output final answer
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Declarative vs. procedural: Declarative is better for algebraic problems, procedural is better for calculation-heavy problems.
  - LLM vs. symbolic solver: LLM is good for understanding the problem, symbolic solver is good for arithmetic.
  - Few-shot examples: More examples can improve performance but increase prompt length.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - LLM generates incorrect equations: Check the generated solution and the examples in the prompt.
  - SymPy fails to solve equations: Check the consistency of the generated equations.
  - Performance is low: Check the quality of the few-shot examples and the principles.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test the DECLARATIVE prompt with a simple math word problem and check if the LLM generates the correct format.
  2. Test the integration with SymPy by solving a system of equations generated by the LLM.
  3. Compare the performance of the DECLARATIVE approach with PAL on a small dataset.

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on proprietary LLMs (Codex) and carefully curated few-shot examples
- Declarative approach's robustness to more complex, multi-step reasoning problems is not fully characterized
- Does not explore sensitivity of results to prompt design or quality of few-shot examples in depth

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The effectiveness of offloading equation solving to SymPy is well-supported by the dramatic accuracy drop (69.4% to 22.4%) when the LLM attempts direct solving.
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that incremental declarative formalization is more robust than procedural reasoning is supported by results but not rigorously proven. The paper does not conduct ablation studies isolating the impact of incrementalization versus symbolic offloading.
- **Medium Confidence:** The 20% absolute improvement on ALGEBRA over PAL is compelling, but ALGEBRA is a new, smaller dataset. Generalization to broader or more complex algebraic domains remains untested.

## Next Checks
1. **Prompt Robustness Test:** Systematically vary the few-shot examples (e.g., number, diversity, complexity) and measure impact on accuracy to determine how sensitive the DECLARATIVE approach is to prompt design.
2. **Incremental vs. Non-Incremental Ablation:** Modify the DECLARATIVE prompt to allow non-incremental equation generation (e.g., permit referencing future variables) and measure the drop in accuracy to isolate the benefit of incrementalization.
3. **Solver Independence Test:** Replace SymPy with a different symbolic solver (e.g., Mathematica or Z3) and verify that the accuracy remains stable, confirming that gains are not solver-specific.
