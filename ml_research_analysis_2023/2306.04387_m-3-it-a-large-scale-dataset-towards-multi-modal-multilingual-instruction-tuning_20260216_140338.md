# M$^3$IT: A Large-Scale Dataset towards Multi-Modal Multilingual Instruction Tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04387
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04387
- Reference count: 40
- Introduces M3IT dataset and Ying-VLM model demonstrating strong performance on knowledgeable VQA and cross-lingual tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces M3IT, a large-scale multi-modal, multilingual instruction tuning dataset designed to enhance the alignment of vision-language models (VLMs) with human instructions. M3IT comprises 40 diverse tasks, 400 manually written instructions, and 2.4 million instances, including image captioning, visual question answering, reasoning, classification, and video-language tasks. The dataset covers 80 languages and features tasks such as knowledgeable VQA, Chinese vision-language tasks, and video-language tasks. A VLM model, Ying-VLM, was developed and trained on M3IT, demonstrating strong performance on knowledgeable VQA tasks, generalization to unseen video tasks, and comprehension of Chinese instructions. The dataset and model are open-sourced to encourage further research in the vision-language field.

## Method Summary
The M3IT dataset was created by compiling and reformating 40 vision-language tasks into a unified vision-to-text schema with 400 manually written instructions. The Ying-VLM model was trained using a two-stage process: first aligning visual and textual features using LAION 400M, then performing multi-modal instruction tuning on M3IT. The model uses BLIP-2 as the vision encoder and Ziya-13B (derived from LLaMA) as the language model. The dataset includes 80 languages through translation of key tasks, and features automatic evaluation using ROUGE-L and GPT-4 evaluation as a proxy for human judgment.

## Key Results
- Ying-VLM demonstrates superior performance on knowledgeable VQA tasks compared to baseline models
- The model shows improved generalization to unseen video-language tasks
- M3IT-trained model effectively comprehends Chinese instructions, validating cross-lingual capabilities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-modal instruction tuning improves cross-task generalization by exposing the model to diverse task formats and instructions
- Mechanism: The dataset reformats 40 vision-language tasks into a unified vision-to-text schema with 400 manually written instructions, enabling the model to learn generalizable patterns across tasks
- Core assumption: A large and diverse instruction set enables the model to generalize to unseen tasks and languages
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our M3IT dataset comprises 40 carefully curated datasets, including 2.4 million instances and 400 manually written task instructions, reformatted into a vision-to-text structure."
  - [section] "Our dataset compiles diverse tasks of classical vision-language tasks, including captioning, visual question answering (VQA), visual conditioned generation, reasoning and classification."
- Break condition: If the model fails to generalize to unseen tasks despite the diverse training data, indicating the instruction diversity is insufficient

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Instruction tuning enables knowledge transfer from large language models to vision-language tasks
- Mechanism: The model is first aligned with visual features using image captioning on LAION400M, then fine-tuned on the M3IT dataset to leverage the knowledge embedded in the LLM
- Core assumption: The LLM contains world knowledge that can be transferred to vision-language tasks through instruction tuning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experimental results reveal that Ying-VLM surpasses strong baseline models in knowledgeable VQA tasks and exhibits improved generalization performance to unseen video and cross-lingual tasks."
  - [section] "Stage I Visual-Text Alignment: To align the visual and textual feature space, we utilize the instructions in the coco captioning and perform an initial alignment training on LAION 400M [41]."
- Break condition: If the model fails to answer knowledgeable VQA questions despite being fine-tuned on M3IT, indicating the knowledge transfer is ineffective

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Translating key tasks into multiple languages enables cross-lingual generalization
- Mechanism: A subset of datasets is translated into 80 languages using an advanced translation system, allowing the model to learn multilingual representations
- Core assumption: Translating tasks into multiple languages provides sufficient multilingual training data for the model to generalize
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Key tasks are translated into 80 languages with an advanced translation system, ensuring broader accessibility."
  - [section] "Stage IV: Key Datasets Translation To boost the language diversity and support the evaluation across different languages, we select a subset of datasets... and translate their evaluation data into 100 languages following FLORES-101 [13]."
- Break condition: If the model fails to comprehend instructions in Chinese despite being fine-tuned on translated tasks, indicating the multilingual training data is insufficient

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: BLIP-2 (vision encoder) -> Ziya-13B (language model) -> Instruction tuning on M3IT
- Critical path: Two-stage training process: Stage I - Visual-Text Alignment using LAION 400M, Stage II - Multi-modal Instruction Tuning
- Design tradeoffs: Using a frozen vision encoder (BLIP-2) reduces training time but may limit the model's ability to learn visual representations specific to the tasks in M3IT
- Failure signatures: If the model fails to generalize to unseen tasks, it may indicate that the instruction diversity is insufficient. If the model fails to answer knowledgeable VQA questions, it may indicate that the knowledge transfer from the LLM is ineffective. If the model fails to comprehend instructions in Chinese, it may indicate that the multilingual training data is insufficient
- First 3 experiments:

  2. Evaluate the model's performance on a knowledgeable VQA task to test knowledge transfer
  3. Evaluate the model's performance on a Chinese vision-language task to test cross-lingual generalization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the diversity of instructions affect the performance of vision-language models (VLMs) trained on the M3IT dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the influence of instruction diversity by limiting the number of instructions used in each dataset to 1, 2, 4, and 8, and reports varying levels of performance
- Why unresolved: The paper provides preliminary results suggesting that using four instructions per task is sufficient for achieving decent performance, but does not conduct a more in-depth analysis of the impact of instruction diversity on model performance
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying the number of instructions per task and analyzing the corresponding performance of VLMs trained on the M3IT dataset would provide insights into the optimal level of instruction diversity for effective training

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of VLMs trained on the M3IT dataset change with the inclusion of different types of tasks, such as reasoning or generation tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the effect of task number by randomly shuffling tasks and selecting subsets to train the model, reporting performance improvements with increased task numbers
- Why unresolved: The paper does not analyze the specific impact of different task types on model performance, leaving open the question of which types of tasks contribute most to improved generalization and performance
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation study training VLMs on subsets of the M3IT dataset that include or exclude specific task types, and comparing the resulting performance, would provide insights into the contribution of different task types to model effectiveness

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can instruction tuning on the M3IT dataset effectively improve the performance of VLMs on tasks that require fine-grained semantics reasoning over text descriptions and image contents?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper includes tasks such as Winoground in the M3IT dataset, which requires fine-grained semantics reasoning, but does not explicitly evaluate the performance of VLMs on this task
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide results or analysis on the effectiveness of instruction tuning on tasks that require fine-grained semantics reasoning, leaving open the question of whether the M3IT dataset can improve VLM performance on such tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating VLMs trained on the M3IT dataset on tasks that require fine-grained semantics reasoning, such as Winoground, and comparing their performance to baseline models, would provide insights into the effectiveness of instruction tuning on these challenging tasks

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies heavily on GPT-4 as a proxy for human judgment, which may not capture all aspects of instruction following quality
- The manual instruction writing process and translation pipeline introduce potential biases that aren't fully characterized
- Claims about knowledge transfer from LLMs and cross-lingual generalization have lower confidence due to limited out-of-domain testing

## Confidence

- Dataset quality and diversity: Medium
- Model performance on knowledgeable VQA: Medium
- Cross-lingual generalization capability: Low
- Generalization to unseen video tasks: Low
