# Machine learning in and out of equilibrium

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03521
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03521
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SGD with minibatch sampling leads to nonequilibrium stationary states, but without-replacement sampling enables equilibrium sampling for Bayesian learning

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes deep connections between stochastic gradient descent (SGD) training of neural networks and nonequilibrium statistical mechanics. Using a Fokker-Planck equation approach, the authors show that conventional SGD with with-replacement minibatching violates the Einstein relation, creating persistent probability currents in parameter space and entropy production. They demonstrate that the choice between with-replacement and without-replacement minibatching fundamentally changes the stationary distribution. The authors then leverage this insight to propose SGWORLD, a novel algorithm that achieves equilibrium sampling from posterior distributions orders of magnitude faster than standard methods in Bayesian learning contexts.

## Method Summary
The authors model SGD dynamics using a Fokker-Planck equation where the probability distribution of network parameters evolves under the influence of minibatch noise. They derive expressions for the effective loss landscape and diffusion matrix, showing these depend critically on whether minibatches are sampled with or without replacement. For Bayesian inference, they introduce SGWORLD, which combines without-replacement minibatching with Langevin dynamics and a correction term for the effective landscape shift. The method is validated on a simple regression problem where the posterior distribution is analytically known, demonstrating superior convergence compared to standard stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics.

## Key Results
- Conventional SGD violates the Einstein relation between mobility and diffusion matrices, leading to nonequilibrium stationary states
- The effective loss landscape and diffusion matrix are highly sensitive to minibatch sampling method (with vs without replacement)
- SGWORLD algorithm converges to posterior distributions orders of magnitude faster than SGLD in test systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SGD with with-replacement minibatching leads to nonequilibrium stationary states due to violation of the Einstein relation
- Mechanism: Minibatch noise creates a diffusion matrix not proportional to the mobility matrix, generating persistent probability currents
- Core assumption: Minibatch noise can be modeled as Gaussian with zero mean
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states "Conventional SGD leads to a nonequilibrium stationary state with persistent currents in the parameter space"
- Break condition: Non-Gaussian minibatch noise or large learning rates invalidating small-noise approximation

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Without-replacement minibatching induces different noise distribution and effective loss landscape shift
- Mechanism: Epoch-level Markovianity creates distinct diffusion matrix and perturbed effective loss landscape
- Core assumption: Noise at epoch level can be approximated as Gaussian
- Evidence anchors: Abstract notes "effective loss landscape and diffusion matrix that determine the shape of the stationary distribution vary depending on the simple choice of minibatching"
- Break condition: Minibatch size too large relative to dataset, invalidating independent epoch assumption

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SGWORLD exploits smaller noise fluctuations in WOR to converge faster to posterior
- Mechanism: Reduced diffusion matrix magnitude makes Einstein relation more closely satisfied, enabling better posterior approximation
- Core assumption: Posterior distribution is approximately Gaussian near MAP estimate
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states SGWORLD "outperforms SGLD, converging to the posterior orders of magnitude faster"
- Break condition: Highly non-Gaussian posterior or learning rate too large

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data -> Model (NN parameters θ) -> Loss function L(θ) -> SGD optimizer (WR/WOR) -> Analysis (FP equation, fluctuation theorems)
- Critical path: 1) Initialize parameters 2) For each epoch: a) Sample minibatches (WR/WOR) b) Update parameters via SGD c) Analyze stationary state 3) For SGWORLD: add Langevin noise and landscape correction after each epoch
- Design tradeoffs: WR vs WOR (simplicity vs noise control), learning rate (convergence speed vs stationary state quality), minibatch size (noise reduction vs computational cost)
- Failure signatures: Stationary distribution non-convergence (check small-noise approximation), SGWORLD convergence issues (verify Gaussian posterior assumption and correction term implementation)
- First 3 experiments: 1) Compare WR/WOR minibatching on regression network for stationary distribution validation 2) Implement SGWORLD on Bayesian linear regression for posterior convergence 3) Vary learning rate and minibatch size to study stationary state property changes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Impact of SGD with momentum on stationary distribution and entropy production compared to conventional SGD
- Basis in paper: Authors mention FP formalism can generalize to momentum-based algorithms but don't explore this
- Why unresolved: Focus on conventional SGD, leaving momentum-based extensions for future work
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments comparing stationary distributions and entropy production rates with and without momentum

### Open Question 2
- Question: How activation function choice affects stationary distribution and entropy production in SGD
- Basis in paper: Uses tanh activation but doesn't explore other functions' impact
- Why unresolved: Authors focus on specific activation for illustration, leaving exploration of others for future work
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments comparing stationary distributions and entropy production rates with different activation functions

### Open Question 3
- Question: Extension of SGWORLD to other Bayesian ML tasks beyond linearized regression
- Basis in paper: Authors propose SGWORLD and demonstrate on simple example but don't explore complex tasks
- Why unresolved: Focus on simple example to illustrate concept, leaving performance on real-world tasks for future work
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments applying SGWORLD to image classification, NLP, or reinforcement learning tasks

## Limitations
- Small-noise approximation requires small learning rates, potentially limiting practical applicability
- Fokker-Planck approach assumes local linearity, which may break down in highly non-convex deep network landscapes
- Limited empirical validation across diverse network architectures and real-world datasets

## Confidence

**Major Claim Confidence:**
- SGD leads to nonequilibrium stationary states: Medium (strong theoretical derivation but limited empirical validation)
- WR vs WOR creates fundamentally different stationary distributions: Low (novel theoretical prediction with minimal experimental verification)
- SGWORLD converges orders of magnitude faster: Low (only tested on simplified linear regression)
