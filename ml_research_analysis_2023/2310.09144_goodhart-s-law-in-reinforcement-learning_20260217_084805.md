# Goodhart's Law in Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2310.09144
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09144
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Optimizing a misspecified proxy reward in RL can lead to decreased performance on the true reward, with a geometric explanation based on angle thresholds during steepest ascent optimization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates Goodhart's Law in the context of reinforcement learning, where optimizing a proxy reward that imperfectly correlates with the true reward can lead to catastrophic performance degradation. The authors provide a geometric explanation for why this occurs in Markov Decision Processes, showing that Goodharting happens when the angle between proxy and true reward vectors exceeds a critical threshold during optimization. They propose both an optimal early stopping method and a regularized training objective that provably avoid Goodharting while maintaining performance on the proxy reward.

## Method Summary
The paper uses value iteration to train policies on proxy rewards under different optimization pressures, then measures performance on true rewards to quantify Goodharting. The core method involves computing occupancy measures of policies and analyzing the geometric relationship between proxy and true reward vectors. For early stopping, they derive a criterion based on the angle θ between rewards, stopping when improvement rates drop below sin(θ) times the maximum possible reward change. The regularized objective maximizes proxy reward minus a penalty proportional to the perpendicular component of the policy occupancy measure relative to the proxy reward direction.

## Key Results
- Goodharting occurs across diverse environments (Gridworld, Cliff, RandomMDP, TreeMDP) with varying reward correlations
- The normalized drop height (NDH) metric quantifies the magnitude of Goodharting, with values > 0 indicating performance degradation
- Early stopping at the derived threshold provably avoids Goodharting with theoretical guarantees on worst-case reward performance
- The regularized training objective maximizes worst-case true reward when there is uncertainty about the true reward function

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Goodharting occurs when the angle between the proxy and true reward vectors exceeds a critical threshold during steepest ascent optimization.
- Mechanism: As optimization pressure increases, the policy's occupancy measure follows a path of steepest ascent in the policy polytope. When this path hits the boundary, it deflects in a direction determined by projecting the proxy reward onto the boundary. If this projection is far from the true reward direction, the true reward decreases despite continued proxy optimization.
- Core assumption: The policy polytope is convex and the optimization path follows steepest ascent.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Goodharting happens when the angle between the proxy and true reward vectors passes a critical threshold during steepest ascent optimization"
  - [section] "Steepest ascent is the process that changes ⃗η in the direction that most rapidly increases ⃗η · R... The path of steepest ascent forms a piecewise linear curve whose linear segments lie on the boundary of Ω"
  - [corpus] Weak - neighboring papers discuss proxy rewards and Goodhart's law but don't specifically analyze the geometric angle threshold mechanism
- Break condition: If the proxy reward is perfectly aligned with the true reward (angle = 0) or if the policy polytope has no boundaries that cause deflection away from the true reward direction.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Early stopping at a specific point in the optimization curve provably avoids Goodharting.
- Mechanism: The paper derives a stopping criterion based on the angle θ between proxy and true rewards. When the optimization algorithm is concave (which most are approximately), stopping when the improvement rate drops below sin(θ) ||Mτ R1|| ensures no policy in the resulting set can Goodhart for any reward within angle θ of the proxy.
- Core assumption: The optimization algorithm is concave with respect to policy changes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We use these theoretical insights to propose an optimal early stopping method that provably avoids the aforementioned pitfall"
  - [section] "Corollary 1 tells us that this stopping point, in a certain sense, is worst-case optimal"
  - [corpus] Weak - neighboring papers discuss proxy rewards but don't provide the specific early stopping mechanism with theoretical guarantees
- Break condition: If the provided θ does not actually bound the true angle between proxy and true rewards, or if the optimization algorithm is not concave.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Optimizing a regularized objective that penalizes deviation from the proxy reward direction maximizes worst-case true reward.
- Mechanism: The paper proposes an objective that maximizes proxy reward minus a penalty term proportional to the perpendicular component of the policy occupancy measure relative to the proxy reward direction. This encourages policies that stay close to the proxy reward direction while maximizing its value.
- Core assumption: The regularization strength κ = tan(θ)||Mτ R1|| appropriately balances proxy reward maximization with alignment preservation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Moreover, we derive a training method that maximises worst-case reward, for the setting where there is uncertainty about the true reward function"
  - [section] "a policy π maximises minR∈Fθ JR1(π) if and only if it maximises JR1(π) − κ||ηπ||sin(arg(ηπ, R1))"
  - [corpus] Weak - neighboring papers discuss proxy rewards but don't present this specific regularized optimization framework
- Break condition: If the uncertainty about the true reward is not well-characterized by the angle θ, or if the penalty term κ is mis-specified.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Environment generator (Gridworld, Cliff, RandomMDP, TreeMDP variants) -> Reward function sampler (Terminal, Path, Cliff reward types) -> Optimization engine (Maximal Causal Entropy and Boltzmann Rationality implementations) -> Early stopping controller (algorithm that monitors angle thresholds) -> Evaluation framework (measures NDH and other Goodharting metrics) -> Visualization tools (policy embeddings in occupancy measure space)

- Critical path:
  1. Sample environment and reward functions
  2. Initialize policy and begin optimization
  3. At each iteration, compute occupancy measures and check stopping criterion
  4. If stopping criterion met, output policy; otherwise continue optimization
  5. Evaluate final policy performance on true reward

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost of exact occupancy measure calculation vs. approximation via rollouts
  - Tightness of angle bound θ vs. potential reward loss from conservative stopping
  - Choice between MCE and BR optimization methods (different approximation qualities)
  - Dimensionality of policy space vs. ability to visualize and debug optimization trajectories

- Failure signatures:
  - NDH > 0 indicates Goodharting occurred (proxy optimization decreased true reward)
  - Early stopping stopping too early (reward loss much higher than expected)
  - Early stopping not stopping (optimization continues past critical threshold)
  - Numerical instability in occupancy measure calculations (NaN values)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify Goodharting occurs in simple Gridworld environment with known proxy/true reward pairs
  2. Test early stopping algorithm on same environment, confirming NDH = 0 after stopping
  3. Vary angle bound θ and measure relationship between θ and reward loss from conservative stopping

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what precise conditions does the angle between the proxy and true reward vectors cause Goodharting to occur?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explains that Goodharting occurs when the angle between the proxy and true reward vectors passes a critical threshold during steepest ascent optimization, but does not provide exact conditions for when this happens.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides a geometric explanation and shows empirically that Goodharting occurs, but does not derive precise mathematical conditions for when the angle causes Goodharting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Deriving exact mathematical conditions (e.g. specific angle thresholds or relationships between reward vectors) that predict when Goodharting will occur based on the geometry of the policy polytope.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the early stopping method be made more practical for large state-action spaces?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that computing the occupancy measure η exactly is infeasible for large environments due to the exponential number of deterministic policies, and suggests approximations like rollouts which may be expensive and noisy.
- Why unresolved: The paper proposes early stopping as a solution but does not address how to make it scalable to large MDPs where computing η exactly is intractable.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating approximation methods for computing η that are scalable to large MDPs, and showing these approximations maintain the theoretical guarantees of the early stopping method.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can reward functions be iteratively improved over time to reduce the risk of Goodharting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper briefly mentions the idea of using methods to estimate the angle between the proxy and true reward over time, and using theorems to determine when more information about the true reward is needed, but does not develop this further.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides a theoretical framework for early stopping but does not explore how to actively refine the proxy reward over time as more data becomes available.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing and evaluating algorithms that actively query the true reward or collect new data to refine the proxy reward during optimization, and showing these methods reduce Goodharting compared to using a fixed proxy reward.

## Limitations
- The geometric analysis assumes policy optimization follows steepest ascent paths in a convex policy polytope, which may not hold for all RL algorithms
- The early stopping criterion requires precise knowledge of the angle θ between proxy and true rewards, which may be difficult to estimate in real-world applications
- Exact occupancy measure calculations have exponential complexity, limiting scalability to larger environments

## Confidence
- High confidence: The empirical observation that Goodharting occurs across diverse environments and reward functions
- Medium confidence: The geometric explanation of Goodharting through angle thresholds and boundary deflections
- Medium confidence: The theoretical guarantees for the early stopping method, though practical implementation details may affect performance
