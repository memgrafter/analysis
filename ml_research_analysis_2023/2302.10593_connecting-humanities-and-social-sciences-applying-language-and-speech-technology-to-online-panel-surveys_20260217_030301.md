# Connecting Humanities and Social Sciences: Applying Language and Speech Technology to Online Panel Surveys

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10593
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10593
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: ASR system achieved 24.7% WER with deletions as main error type; fine-tuned BERTje showed high agreement on non-neutral sentiment but negative bias; topic modeling diverged for some domains (e.g., democracy) but converged for others (e.g., trust)

## Executive Summary
This study integrates automatic speech recognition (ASR) into online survey methodology to compare spoken and typed responses. Dutch panel respondents provided open-ended answers via voice or keyboard, allowing researchers to evaluate how ASR errors propagate through downstream analyses. The research demonstrates that while transformer-based models enable useful analysis without extensive domain-specific training, ASR errors significantly impact topic modeling results and sentiment analysis performance requires careful calibration to avoid systematic biases.

## Method Summary
The study collected 1796 audio recordings from LISS panel respondents answering open-ended survey questions, with matching keyboard responses for comparison. Audio was processed through Questfox ASR system, then manually corrected to create reference transcripts. Sentiment analysis used a fine-tuned BERTje model trained on Dutch Book Reviews Dataset. Topic modeling combined sentence-transformer embeddings with HDBSCAN clustering and TF-IDF weighting. Performance was evaluated using WER for ASR quality, Fleiss' Kappa for inter-rater sentiment agreement, and Spearman correlation for topic similarity between manual and automatic transcriptions.

## Key Results
- ASR system achieved 24.7% word error rate with deletions being the dominant error type
- Fine-tuned BERTje model showed high agreement with human raters on non-neutral sentiment responses but exhibited systematic negative label bias
- Topic modeling using HDBSCAN and TF-IDF produced similar topics for manual and automatic transcriptions in some domains (trust, marriage) but diverged significantly in others (democracy)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuned transformer models can perform sentiment analysis on survey data without extensive domain-specific training
- Mechanism: Pretrained models like BERTje capture general linguistic patterns and can generalize to new domains when fine-tuned on a smaller task-specific dataset
- Core assumption: The linguistic patterns in survey responses are similar enough to the patterns in the training data (Dutch book reviews) for the model to transfer effectively
- Evidence anchors: "A big advantage of transformer-based models is that they are trained on a large amount of language materials and do not necessarily need training on the target materials" and "With the development of transformer-based pre-trained language models such as BERT it is possible to achieve good performance without training a dataset specific model"
- Break condition: If the survey language differs significantly in style or vocabulary from the training data, performance will degrade

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ASR errors primarily affect downstream topic modeling by introducing noise that can cause clustering algorithms to split related content into different topics
- Mechanism: Errors like deletions and substitutions alter word frequencies and semantic relationships, leading to incorrect vector representations in the sentence-transformer model
- Core assumption: The topic modeling pipeline is sensitive to word-level accuracy because it relies on TF-IDF scores and word embeddings
- Evidence anchors: "The difference between automatic and manual transcriptions influences the generated topics... For example, Tables 6 and 7 give examples of similar and dissimilar topics found for the manual and automatic transcriptions" and "ASR errors... lead to incorrect vector representations in the sentence-transformer model"
- Break condition: If ASR accuracy improves significantly (e.g., WER below 10%), the impact on topic modeling may become negligible

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Manual transcription bias exists when correcting ASR output because the human transcriber is influenced by the incorrect transcript
- Mechanism: Starting from an ASR transcript, the transcriber may overlook errors or make different corrections than if transcribing from scratch, leading to a "golden standard" that is closer to the ASR output than the true spoken words
- Core assumption: Human perception is influenced by prior exposure to the erroneous text, affecting judgment of what constitutes the correct transcription
- Evidence anchors: "The reference transcripts were made by manually correcting the ASR output of the Questfox recognizer. This is a significantly faster and less labour intensive approach compared to transcribing audio from scratch. Although, it is important to note that this approach does introduce an element of bias towards the recognition output being used as the starting point"
- Break condition: If transcribers are unaware of the ASR output or work independently, the bias may be reduced

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Word Error Rate (WER)
  - Why needed here: WER quantifies the accuracy of ASR systems by measuring the minimum number of edits needed to transform the ASR output into the reference transcription
  - Quick check question: If an ASR transcript has 3 insertions, 2 deletions, and 1 substitution compared to the reference, and the reference has 100 words, what is the WER?

- Concept: Transformer-based models (e.g., BERT)
  - Why needed here: These models provide contextualized word embeddings that capture semantic meaning, enabling effective fine-tuning for tasks like sentiment analysis without requiring large task-specific datasets
  - Quick check question: Why are transformer models particularly suited for transfer learning compared to older architectures like RNNs?

- Concept: Hierarchical Density-Based Spatial Clustering (HDBSCAN)
  - Why needed here: HDBSCAN groups similar text responses into topics without requiring the number of clusters to be specified in advance, which is useful when the optimal number of topics is unknown
  - Quick check question: How does HDBSCAN differ from traditional k-means clustering in handling outliers and determining the number of clusters?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection -> ASR processing -> Manual correction -> Sentiment analysis -> Topic modeling -> Evaluation
- Critical path: 1. Collect audio responses from survey participants 2. Process audio through ASR system 3. Manually correct ASR output to create reference transcripts 4. Apply sentiment analysis model to both ASR and reference texts 5. Apply topic modeling pipeline to both ASR and reference texts 6. Compare results between ASR and reference to evaluate impact of errors
- Design tradeoffs: Using manual correction of ASR output saves time but introduces bias; Fine-tuning a pre-trained model requires less data but may not capture domain-specific nuances; HDBSCAN handles varying cluster densities but may be sensitive to noise from ASR errors
- Failure signatures: High WER indicates poor ASR quality affecting downstream analyses; Low Fleiss' Kappa between human raters suggests unclear sentiment in responses; Low Spearman correlation between manual and automatic topics indicates significant impact of ASR errors
- First 3 experiments: 1. Compare sentiment analysis performance on a small subset of responses using both fine-tuned BERTje and human ratings to establish baseline accuracy 2. Run topic modeling on a controlled dataset (e.g., responses to a single question) with both manual and ASR transcriptions to observe the impact of specific error types 3. Test different minimum cluster sizes in HDBSCAN on a sample dataset to find the optimal setting before scaling up

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of ASR systems vary across different demographic groups in the survey population, and what specific factors contribute to these variations?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that "the development and implementation of these tools is skewed, and they tend to work less optimally for the majority of languages" and suggests that "the rich socioeconomic and demographic background data in such surveys would allow for a full study of which groups of speakers the ASR technologies operate best for and which groups need to be improved"
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the need for such analysis but does not provide the actual comparative performance data across demographic groups
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance metrics (WER) broken down by demographic categories such as age, education level, socioeconomic status, and regional background, along with correlation analysis showing which factors most strongly influence ASR accuracy

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal minimum cluster size for HDBSCAN topic modeling when processing ASR-transcribed survey responses, and how does this threshold vary across different content domains?
- Basis in paper: The paper states "To aid with the selection of the minimum cluster size, we utilized the topic coherence metric" and notes that "The number of clusters found based on the word embeddings differed quite extensively for the democracy set"
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions using topic coherence to select cluster size, it doesn't report the optimal values found or analyze whether these vary by domain
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of different cluster sizes with their corresponding coherence scores for each content domain, identification of the optimal threshold for each, and analysis of how these thresholds relate to ASR error rates

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would fine-tuning the BERTje sentiment analysis model on survey-specific data affect its performance and reduce the observed negative label bias?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that "The automatic sentiment analysis shows a bias towards negative labels compared to the human raters. This could be due to a difference between the materials the model was fine-tuned and applied on"
- Why unresolved: The paper applies a pre-fine-tuned model without testing whether domain-specific training would improve performance or correct the bias
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative performance metrics (precision, recall, F1-score) between the current pre-fine-tuned model and a version fine-tuned on a subset of the survey data, showing changes in bias and overall accuracy

## Limitations
- Manual correction approach may introduce systematic bias favoring ASR output
- Limited evaluation of neutral-response filtering impact on sentiment analysis
- Single-domain validation restricts generalizability of findings

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: ASR error quantification (WER 24.7%) and its characterization as primarily deletion errors
- **Medium Confidence**: BERTje sentiment analysis performance metrics and observed negative bias
- **Low Confidence**: Generalizability of topic modeling results to other survey domains or languages
