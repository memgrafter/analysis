# Joint Path planning and Power Allocation of a Cellular-Connected UAV using Apprenticeship Learning via Deep Inverse Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.10071
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10071
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposed IRL-based approach achieves expert-level performance in joint path planning and power allocation for cellular-connected UAVs, outperforming behavioral cloning in unseen scenarios

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an interference-aware joint path planning and power allocation mechanism for cellular-connected unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) operating in sparse suburban environments. The system aims to maximize uplink throughput while minimizing interference to ground user equipment, considering path constraints and flight resource limitations. The authors employ apprenticeship learning via inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) using both Q-learning and deep reinforcement learning frameworks to learn optimal UAV behavior from expert demonstrations. The proposed approach is compared against behavioral cloning, demonstrating superior performance in handling unseen situations.

## Method Summary
The paper investigates a joint optimization problem where a cellular-connected UAV must navigate from an initial point to a destination while simultaneously managing its transmit power to maximize uplink throughput and minimize interference to ground users. The approach uses apprenticeship learning via inverse reinforcement learning, where expert demonstrations define the desired UAV behavior. Both Q-learning and deep reinforcement learning variants of IRL are implemented and compared against a behavioral cloning baseline that uses supervised learning. The framework learns the reward function from expert trajectories and then uses this learned reward to guide the UAV's decision-making process.

## Key Results
- IRL-based approach achieves expert-level performance in joint path planning and power allocation
- Proposed method outperforms behavioral cloning in handling unseen situations and generalizing to new scenarios
- The framework successfully balances multiple objectives including throughput maximization, interference minimization, and resource constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging expert demonstrations to learn the underlying reward structure of the optimization problem through inverse reinforcement learning. Instead of manually designing a reward function, the IRL approach infers the reward function that explains the expert's behavior, capturing complex trade-offs between multiple objectives. This learned reward function then guides the UAV's policy through reinforcement learning, enabling it to replicate expert-level performance while maintaining flexibility to adapt to new situations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)**: A framework for learning reward functions from expert demonstrations; needed to capture complex multi-objective trade-offs without manual reward engineering; quick check: verify expert demonstrations cover diverse scenarios
- **Apprenticeship Learning**: Learning by observing and imitating expert behavior; needed to bootstrap the learning process with high-quality demonstrations; quick check: ensure expert demonstrations are optimal or near-optimal
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)**: Neural network-based reinforcement learning for handling high-dimensional state spaces; needed for scalability and generalization; quick check: verify network architecture matches problem complexity
- **Q-learning**: Model-free reinforcement learning algorithm for learning action-value functions; needed for discrete action spaces and tabular representations; quick check: ensure proper exploration-exploitation balance
- **Behavioral Cloning**: Supervised learning approach that directly mimics expert demonstrations; needed as a baseline for comparison; quick check: verify sufficient training data coverage

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Expert Demonstrations -> IRL Module (Q-learning/DRL) -> Reward Function -> RL Policy -> UAV Actions -> Environment Feedback
**Critical Path**: Expert demonstrations → IRL reward learning → policy optimization → UAV execution → performance evaluation
**Design Tradeoffs**: Expert knowledge requirement vs. generalizability, computational complexity of DRL vs. sample efficiency, multi-objective balancing vs. optimization simplicity
**Failure Signatures**: Poor expert demonstrations leading to suboptimal reward learning, insufficient exploration causing policy convergence to local optima, mismatch between training and deployment environments
**First Experiments**: 1) Validate IRL reward learning with synthetic expert demonstrations, 2) Test policy performance in controlled single-objective scenarios, 3) Evaluate generalization to slightly modified environmental conditions

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on expert knowledge for defining desired UAV behavior may introduce bias and limit generalizability
- Simulation environment limited to sparse suburban settings, restricting applicability to diverse real-world scenarios
- Comparison limited to behavioral cloning baseline without evaluation against other advanced RL approaches

## Confidence
- **Methodology**: High confidence in IRL theoretical foundation and implementation
- **Simulation Results**: Medium confidence due to limited environmental diversity and assumption of perfect expert demonstrations
- **Real-world Transferability**: Low confidence without extensive field validation
