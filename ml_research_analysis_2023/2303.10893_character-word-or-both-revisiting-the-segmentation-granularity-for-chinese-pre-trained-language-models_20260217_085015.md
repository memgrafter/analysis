# Character, Word, or Both? Revisiting the Segmentation Granularity for Chinese Pre-trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10893
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10893
- Authors: Multiple
- Reference count: 22
- Key outcome: MigBERT achieves state-of-the-art performance on Chinese NLP tasks by integrating character and word information.

## Executive Summary
This paper revisits the segmentation granularity for Chinese pre-trained language models (PLMs). Most Chinese PLMs treat input as character sequences, ignoring rich word-level semantics. The authors propose MigBERT, a mixed-granularity Chinese BERT that jointly considers characters and words. To achieve this, they design a new pre-training task (MMLM) that masks both words and characters. They evaluate MigBERT on various Chinese NLP tasks and achieve new state-of-the-art performance. Ablation studies show that words are semantically richer than characters. They also apply MigBERT to Japanese and obtain the same conclusion, proving its effectiveness and robustness.

## Method Summary
The authors propose MigBERT, a mixed-granularity Chinese BERT that jointly considers characters and words. To achieve this, they design a new pre-training task called MMLM (Mixed Masked Language Modeling) that masks both words and characters. The model is trained on Chinese text corpora with word boundaries annotated, allowing it to learn representations that capture both character-level and word-level information. The pre-training process involves standard BERT objectives along with the new MMLM task, which encourages the model to predict masked words and characters simultaneously.

## Key Results
- MigBERT achieves state-of-the-art performance on various Chinese NLP tasks.
- Words are semantically richer than characters, as shown by ablation studies.
- MigBERT's effectiveness and robustness are demonstrated by applying it to Japanese tasks with similar conclusions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method works by integrating character and word information into a single model architecture. By jointly considering characters and words, MigBERT can capture both fine-grained character-level information and higher-level word-level semantics. The MMLM pre-training task encourages the model to learn robust representations that are useful for both character-level and word-level predictions. This mixed-granularity approach allows the model to better handle the complexities of Chinese language, where words are not explicitly separated by spaces.
