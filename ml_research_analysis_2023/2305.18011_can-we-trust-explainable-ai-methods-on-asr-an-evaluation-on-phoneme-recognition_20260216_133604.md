# Can We Trust Explainable AI Methods on ASR? An Evaluation on Phoneme Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18011
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18011
- Reference count: 0
- This paper evaluates the trustworthiness of explainable AI (XAI) techniques applied to automatic speech recognition (ASR) by adapting LIME to a phoneme recognition task.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the trustworthiness of explainable AI techniques applied to automatic speech recognition by adapting LIME to identify which audio segments most influence phoneme predictions. The authors propose three variants of LIME - LIME, LIME-WS (window-based segments), and LIME-TS (fixed-time segments) - and evaluate them using validity metrics that check if ground truth phoneme segments appear in top-ranked explanation segments. LIME-TS, which uses fixed-duration time segments, achieves the best performance with ground truth segments appearing in the top 3 segments 96% of the time, outperforming other variants by up to 44.8%.

## Method Summary
The study adapts LIME for phoneme recognition by generating explanations that identify which audio segments influence specific phoneme predictions. Three variants are evaluated: standard LIME with no constraints, LIME-WS using sliding windows on TIMIT-defined segments, and LIME-TS using fixed 70ms time segments. The methods perturb audio inputs by masking segments and measuring impact on phoneme predictions. Explanation quality is assessed using validity metrics that check if ground truth phoneme segments from TIMIT annotations appear in the top-ranked segments of the explanation.

## Key Results
- LIME-TS outperforms LIME and LIME-WS by up to 44.8% and 17%, respectively
- LIME-TS achieves 96% validity3 score (ground truth segment in top 3 ranked segments)
- LIME-WS shows improved performance over standard LIME by using sliding window constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LIME-TS outperforms LIME and LIME-WS by restricting perturbations to time-based segments that better match the local nature of phoneme recognition.
- Mechanism: By dividing audio into fixed-duration, non-overlapping time segments (70ms) that approximate the average duration of TIMIT segments (78ms), LIME-TS ensures perturbations occur within the temporal window most relevant to the phoneme being explained. This reduces noise from distant segments that don't influence the current phoneme.
- Core assumption: Phoneme recognition outputs are primarily influenced by local audio context rather than distant segments.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "LIME-TS outperforms LIME and LIME-WS by up to 44.8% and 17%, respectively."
  - [section] "We believe this is because when we use fixed-length time segments in LIME-TS rather than TIMIT defined segments in the LIME-WS, nearly 40% of ground truth audio segments (that are TIMIT based) overlap with multiple time segments (usually 2). In such cases, multiple time segments may be ranked with high importance."
- Break condition: If phoneme recognition outputs depend heavily on long-range dependencies (beyond ~70ms), LIME-TS's local perturbation approach would miss important contextual information.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The sliding window approach in LIME-WS and LIME-TS reduces computational waste by eliminating perturbations that don't affect the target phoneme.
- Mechanism: By constraining perturbations to a fixed window around the phoneme of interest, the method eliminates "worthless mutants" where masked segments have no effect on the output phoneme, making the computation more efficient and focused.
- Core assumption: The influence of a phoneme is confined to a small number of adjacent phonemes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "owing to the local nature of the PR task, the output phoneme being explained is not likely to be influenced by distant audio segments. Taking this into account, will eliminate worthless mutants where the masked out audio segments have no effect on the phoneme being examined, making the computation more effective."
  - [abstract] "LIME-TS outperforms LIME and LIME-WS by up to 44.8% and 17%, respectively."
- Break condition: If phoneme recognition involves complex interactions across distant segments, the sliding window would artificially limit the explanation's scope.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The validity metric using ground truth phoneme segments provides an objective measure of explanation quality.
- Mechanism: By comparing the top-ranked segments from the explanation against manually annotated ground truth segments from TIMIT, the validity metric (validity1, validity3, validity5) quantifies how often the explanation correctly identifies the audio segment responsible for the phoneme prediction.
- Core assumption: Ground truth phoneme segment annotations accurately reflect which audio segments influence phoneme predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We assess the explanation by checking if the ground truth segment that it is associated with is in the top 1, top 5 or top 10 segments in the explanation's importance ranking. We have a validity metric for each of these."
  - [abstract] "We find a variant of LIME based on time partitioned audio segments, that we propose in this paper, produces the most reliable explanations, containing the ground truth 96% of the time in its top three audio segments."
- Break condition: If ground truth annotations are noisy or don't capture the true influence patterns, the validity metric would give misleading quality assessments.

- Critical path: Input audio → perturbation generation (with window constraints) → Kaldi model prediction → alignment with ground truth → importance scoring → validity evaluation

- Failure signatures: Low validity scores across all metrics indicate explanations aren't capturing ground truth segments; gender-based validity differences suggest model bias; high random ranking scores suggest the explanation method isn't adding value

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the proposed XAI techniques perform when evaluated on more complex speech recognition tasks beyond phoneme recognition, such as word-level or continuous speech recognition?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that evaluating explanations on more complex speech tasks is a significant challenge due to the involvement of language models and long-span dependencies.
- Why unresolved: The TIMIT phoneme recognition task provides a controlled setting with ground truth mappings, but more complex tasks lack this clear ground truth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating the same XAI techniques (LIME, LIME-WS, LIME-TS) on continuous speech recognition tasks with word-level outputs, using alternative evaluation metrics or establishing proxy ground truths.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive are the LIME-WS and LIME-TS techniques to the choice of sliding window size or time segment duration?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses specific window sizes (LIME-WS) and segment durations (LIME-TS) without exploring how performance changes with different parameters.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't investigate parameter sensitivity or provide guidance on optimal parameter selection for different types of speech data.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying window sizes and segment durations across different speakers, accents, and speech content to determine optimal parameter ranges.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the proposed XAI techniques generalize to other ASR datasets beyond TIMIT, such as Librispeech or Common Voice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that LIME-TS uses time-based segmentation which is more generalizable to datasets without expert-annotated segments.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to the TIMIT dataset, and the authors don't demonstrate performance on other datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the same techniques to Librispeech or Common Voice and comparing performance metrics across datasets with different characteristics (noisy environments, multiple speakers, etc.).

## Limitations
- The evaluation relies on TIMIT ground truth phoneme segment annotations, which may not perfectly capture the true influence patterns of audio segments on phoneme predictions.
- The study focuses on a single common sentence ('SA1') across speakers, limiting generalizability to diverse linguistic content.
- The 70ms time segment duration in LIME-TS was chosen based on average TIMIT segment length but may not be optimal for all phonemes or acoustic conditions.

## Confidence
- High confidence: LIME-TS achieves superior performance over LIME and LIME-WS with 96% validity3 score
- Medium confidence: The mechanism by which time segmentation improves explanation quality is well-supported by empirical results
- Medium confidence: The sliding window approach effectively reduces computational waste for local phoneme recognition
- Low confidence: The validity metric's ability to fully capture explanation quality given potential noise in ground truth annotations
