# ChiroDiff: Modelling chirographic data with Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03785

## Executive Summary
ChiroDiff introduces a diffusion model framework for chirographic data that overcomes limitations of autoregressive approaches by capturing holistic temporal concepts. Unlike traditional methods that rely on one-way visibility and fixed sampling rates, ChiroDiff learns non-autoregressive density while preserving sequential order, making it robust to varying temporal resolutions. The model uses a denoising diffusion probabilistic framework to transform Brownian motion into structured motion, enabling effective generation and reconstruction. Quantitative evaluations on VMNIST, KanjiVG, and QuickDraw datasets show superior performance in terms of Chamfer Distance, FID scores, and computational efficiency compared to competing methods like SketchRNN, SketchODE, and CoSE.

## Method Summary
ChiroDiff implements a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) that operates on velocity-encoded chirographic sequences rather than absolute positions. The model uses a bidirectional GRU encoder with sinusoidal positional embeddings to predict noise at each diffusion step, with training objective based on noise prediction. Velocity representation (v(j) = x(j+1) - x(j)) captures motion dynamics rather than static positions, while an analog bits approach handles binary pen-up states as continuous variables. The parametric noise estimator is not hard-constrained by sequence length, enabling length-conditioned resampling and generation at variable temporal resolutions. Training uses AdamW optimizer with cosine learning rate scheduling, and sampling employs the DDIM algorithm for deterministic generation.

## Key Results
- Superior reconstruction quality on VMNIST with Chamfer Distance improving by 28.5% compared to SketchRNN at 2× sampling rate
- Better generation quality on QuickDraw with FID scores of 3.48 compared to 4.96 for SketchODE
- 40% faster convergence and 60% reduced sampling time compared to autoregressive baselines
- Robust performance across varying temporal sampling rates without degradation in quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Non-autoregressive diffusion modeling allows holistic temporal concept learning in chirographic data.
- Mechanism: By diffusing the entire sequence at once rather than generating sequentially, the model can capture global dependencies and spatial relationships that autoregressive models miss due to one-way visibility constraints.
- Core assumption: The diffusion process can effectively transform random noise into structured chirographic patterns when given access to the full sequence context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our model named 'ChiroDiff', being non-autoregressive, learns to capture holistic concepts and therefore remains resilient to higher temporal sampling rate up to a good extent."
  - [section 4]: "Unlike autoregressive models, CHIRO DIFF offers a way to draw conditional samples from the model without an explicit encoder when conditioned on homogeneous data"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Velocity-based representation enables better modeling of continuous-time dynamics.
- Mechanism: Modeling velocities (v(j) = x(j+1) - x(j)) instead of absolute positions allows the model to focus on motion patterns and derivatives, capturing the underlying creative process rather than just static shapes.
- Core assumption: Higher-order derivatives (velocity) contain more semantically meaningful information about the drawing process than absolute positions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "we found it beneficial not to directly use absolute points sequence X but instead use velocities V = [··· , (v(j),p (j)),··· ]"
  - [section 4]: "By modelling higher-order derivatives (velocity instead of position), the model focuses on high-level concepts rather than local temporal details"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Length-conditioned resampling enables generation at variable temporal resolutions.
- Mechanism: The model's parametric noise estimator ϵθ(·,t) is not hard-constrained by sequence length, allowing generation to start from any length prior, making it robust to different sampling rates.
- Core assumption: A well-trained diffusion model captures high-level geometric concepts that generalize across different temporal resolutions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "there is no hard conditioning on the cardinality of the sequence|X| or|V| due to our choice of the parametric model ϵθ(·,t)"

- Component map: Noisy data → Bi-GRU encoding → Noise estimation → Reverse diffusion → Generated data
- Critical path: Noise → Bi-GRU encoding → Noise estimation → Reverse diffusion → Generated data
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Velocity vs position encoding: Velocity focuses on motion but requires position reconstruction

  - Poor reconstruction quality indicates insufficient model capacity or training
  - Mode collapse suggests loss landscape issues or improper regularization

## Limitations
- The velocity encoding approach may struggle with complex stroke patterns requiring precise positional information
- Model cannot perform true online generation due to bi-directional context dependency
- Computational efficiency gains may not hold for extremely long sequences or high temporal resolutions
- Analog bits approximation introduces errors in handling discrete pen-up states

## Confidence
- High confidence: Non-autoregressive diffusion modeling outperforming autoregressive approaches in computational efficiency and temporal robustness
- Medium confidence: Velocity encoding benefits generalizing across different chirographic domains
- Low confidence: Model maintaining quality when extrapolating to temporal resolutions far beyond training distribution
