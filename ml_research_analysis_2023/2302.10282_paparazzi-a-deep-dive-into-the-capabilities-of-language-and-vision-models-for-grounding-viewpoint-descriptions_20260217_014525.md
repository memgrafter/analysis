# Paparazzi: A Deep Dive into the Capabilities of Language and Vision Models for Grounding Viewpoint Descriptions

## Quick Facts

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10282

- Reference count: 19

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether pre-trained language and vision models like CLIP can effectively ground perspective descriptions of 3D objects and identify canonical views based on text queries. The authors propose a new task called text-viewpoint retrieval and develop a framework for analyzing and scaling image-text models with 3D data. Through extensive experiments, they demonstrate that while pre-trained CLIP performs poorly on most canonical views, fine-tuning with hard negative sampling and random contrasting yields significant improvements, even with limited training data.

## Method Summary
The authors implement a Paparazzi agent that orbits a spherical camera around 3D objects to sample images from various viewpoints. They use a pre-trained CLIP model to score pairs of image-viewpoint descriptions and propose fine-tuning the model using an extended loss function that includes contrastive loss on negative examples, random examples, and hard negative examples. The framework is evaluated on both synthetic data generated from ShapeNet models and real images from LAION-5B, using metrics such as KL-divergence, precision@k, and recall@k.

## Key Results
- Pre-trained CLIP performs poorly on most canonical views, struggling to ground text descriptions like "car from the bottom" to their corresponding views
- Fine-tuning CLIP with hard negative sampling and random contrasting yields significant improvements in viewpoint retrieval performance on both synthetic and real data
- Bayesian search outperforms greedy search in viewpoint retrieval, finding optimal viewpoints with fewer scoring function calls when using the fine-tuned model

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CLIP's pre-trained zero-shot capabilities do not generalize to 3D viewpoint understanding because the model lacks structured training on viewpoint-text pairings.
- Mechanism: The contrastive loss in CLIP is optimized for 2D human-centric images paired with captions. Without explicit 3D viewpoint embeddings or 3D object geometry, CLIP cannot reliably ground text descriptions like "car from the bottom" to their corresponding views.
- Core assumption: Viewpoint descriptions are not represented in CLIP's learned embedding space in a way that aligns with 3D object geometry.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning CLIP with hard negative sampling and random contrasting improves viewpoint retrieval because it explicitly optimizes for viewpoint-text alignment in the 3D context.
- Mechanism: The extended loss function (Equation 4) includes terms for negative examples, random examples, and hard negatives. This encourages the model to distinguish between similar viewpoints (e.g., left vs. right) and generalize across object categories.
- Core assumption: Hard negatives and random contrasting provide the necessary signal for the model to learn viewpoint distinctions.
- Evidence anchors:

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Bayesian search outperforms greedy search in viewpoint retrieval because it strategically samples from regions likely to contain optima, leveraging the smoothness of the fine-tuned scoring function.
- Mechanism: Bayesian optimization updates its prior based on stepwise function values, focusing sampling on promising regions. This is more efficient than greedy search's local optimization approach.
- Core assumption: The scoring function is smooth enough for Bayesian optimization to effectively identify optimal viewpoints.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: 3D scene with object -> Orbiting camera with spherical coordinates -> Image-text matching model (CLIP) -> Scoring function (cosine similarity of embeddings) -> Search algorithm (greedy or Bayesian) -> Loss functions (contrastive, random contrasting, hard negative sampling)
- Critical path:
  1. Generate synthetic images of 3D objects from various viewpoints
  2. Fine-tune CLIP on viewpoint-text pairs using extended loss objectives
  3. Use the fine-tuned model to score image-text pairs
  4. Apply search algorithm to find optimal viewpoints
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Synthetic vs. real data: Synthetic data is controllable but may not fully represent real-world scenarios. Real data is more realistic but harder to generate for arbitrary viewpoints.
  - Greedy vs. Bayesian search: Greedy search is simpler but may get stuck in local optima. Bayesian search is more sophisticated but computationally expensive.
  - Loss function complexity: Adding hard negative sampling and random contrasting improves performance but increases training complexity.
- Failure signatures:
  - Pre-trained CLIP: Poor retrieval performance, nonsensical viewpoints
  - Fine-tuning without hard negatives: Improved performance but still struggles with viewpoint distinctions
  - Non-smooth scoring function: Search algorithms perform poorly, especially Bayesian search
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate pre-trained CLIP on synthetic viewpoint retrieval task
  2. Fine-tune CLIP with contrastive loss and evaluate performance
  3. Fine-tune CLIP with extended loss objectives (random contrasting + hard negative sampling) and compare to previous experiments

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the proposed framework be extended to handle more complex 3D scenes with multiple objects and occlusion?
- Can the proposed fine-tuning approach be adapted to improve viewpoint understanding in other pre-trained language and vision models beyond CLIP?
- How does the performance of the proposed framework change when using different types of 3D data, such as 3D models with varying levels of detail or different representations like point clouds or meshes?

## Limitations
- The approach relies on synthetic data for training, which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world scenarios with varying lighting conditions and occlusions.
- The effectiveness of hard negative sampling depends on careful tuning of the weighting parameter G and the sampling distribution, which could affect reproducibility.
- The framework is currently designed for single-object scenes and does not address the challenges of multiple objects and occlusion in complex 3D environments.

## Confidence
- **High**: The effectiveness of fine-tuning CLIP with hard negative sampling and random contrasting for viewpoint retrieval is well-supported by the experimental results, showing consistent improvements across multiple metrics and datasets.
- **Medium**: The superiority of Bayesian search over greedy search is demonstrated, but this confidence is conditional on the scoring function being smooth, which may not hold for all fine-tuning configurations or in the presence of noisy data.
- **Low**: The claim that CLIP's pre-trained zero-shot capabilities do not generalize to 3D viewpoint understanding is based on the specific experimental setup and may not hold for other types of 3D tasks or different object categories.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-category generalization**: Evaluate the fine-tuned models on object categories not included in the original training set (e.g., furniture, animals) to assess whether the viewpoint understanding generalizes beyond the five tested categories.
2. **Real-world robustness**: Test the approach on real-world images with varying lighting conditions, occlusions, and partial views to determine if the improvements from fine-tuning hold in less controlled environments.
3. **Alternative search algorithms**: Compare the performance of Bayesian search against other optimization algorithms (e.g., evolutionary strategies, simulated annealing) to verify if the advantage over greedy search is specific to Bayesian optimization or if other methods could achieve similar or better results.
