# Stepsize Learning for Policy Gradient Methods in Contextual Markov Decision Processes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07741
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07741
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Meta-learning approach for adaptive step size selection in policy gradient methods outperforms fixed step sizes and other benchmarks in simulated environments

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a meta-learning framework called meta-MDP to address the problem of step size selection in policy gradient methods for contextual Markov Decision Processes. The authors formalize the problem as learning a dynamic step size within a meta-MDP that generalizes contextual MDPs to include the learning model and policy parameterization. By using Fitted Q-Iteration with ExtraTrees regression, the method learns to recommend adaptive step sizes for different policies and tasks. Experimental results on various simulated environments demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves faster convergence and better performance compared to fixed step sizes and other learning rate adaptation methods. The paper also provides theoretical guarantees on the Lipschitz continuity of the expected return and its gradient in meta-MDPs.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a meta-learning approach called meta-MDP to tackle the problem of hyperparameter selection, specifically step size, in policy gradient methods. They generalize contextual MDPs to include the learning model and policy parameterization, formalizing the problem as learning a dynamic step size within a meta-MDP. The method uses Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI) with ExtraTrees regression to learn a policy that recommends adaptive step sizes based on meta-features such as policy parameters, natural gradient, and context. The meta-MDP framework allows the learning of step sizes that are adaptive to different policies and tasks, addressing the limitations of fixed step sizes. Theoretical guarantees on the Lipschitz continuity of the expected return and its gradient in meta-MDPs are provided. Experiments on various simulated environments show that the proposed approach outperforms fixed step sizes and other benchmarks, achieving faster convergence and better performance.

## Key Results
- Meta-MDP approach learns adaptive step sizes that outperform fixed step sizes in simulated environments
- Faster convergence and better performance compared to other learning rate adaptation methods (e.g., HOOF, metagrad)
- Theoretical guarantees on Lipschitz continuity of expected return and its gradient in meta-MDPs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The meta-MDP approach works by learning a policy that recommends adaptive step sizes based on meta-features such as policy parameters, natural gradient, and context. This allows the method to tailor the step size to the specific policy and task at hand, rather than using a fixed step size that may not be optimal for all situations. The use of Fitted Q-Iteration with ExtraTrees regression enables efficient learning of the meta-policy in a data-driven manner. By generalizing contextual MDPs to include the learning model and policy parameterization, the meta-MDP framework provides a principled way to learn step sizes that are adaptive to different policies and tasks.

## Foundational Learning
- **Contextual MDPs**: MDPs where the transition dynamics and reward function depend on a context variable. Needed to model the variability in tasks and policies.
- **Policy Gradient Methods**: Reinforcement learning algorithms that directly optimize the policy parameters using gradient ascent on the expected return. Needed as the base algorithm for which step size learning is applied.
- **Fitted Q-Iteration (FQI)**: A batch reinforcement learning algorithm that approximates the optimal action-value function using regression. Needed to learn the meta-policy that recommends adaptive step sizes.
- **ExtraTrees Regression**: An ensemble learning method that constructs multiple decision trees on random subsets of the data. Used as the regression method in FQI to learn the meta-policy.
- **Lipschitz Continuity**: A property of functions that bounds the rate of change. The theoretical guarantees rely on the Lipschitz continuity of the expected return and its gradient in meta-MDPs.
- **Meta-features**: A set of informative features that capture the characteristics of the policy and task. Needed to represent the meta-state in the meta-MDP and enable adaptive step size recommendations.
