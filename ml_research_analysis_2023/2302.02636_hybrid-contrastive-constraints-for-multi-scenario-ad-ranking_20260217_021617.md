# Hybrid Contrastive Constraints for Multi-Scenario Ad Ranking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02636
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02636
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Hybrid contrastive constrained approach improves multi-scenario ad ranking with up to 5.37% CTR gains and minimal computational overhead

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a hybrid contrastive constrained approach for multi-scenario ad ranking that leverages data from multiple scenarios to improve performance in individual scenarios. The method introduces two specialized contrastive losses—generalized for capturing cross-scenario commonalities and individual for modeling scenario-specific characteristics—along with innovations like label-aware contrastive samples, diffusion noise enhancement, and reciprocal similarity weighting. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements over strong baselines on both public and production datasets, with particular effectiveness in addressing data sparsity challenges.

## Method Summary
The approach uses a shared-specific architecture where a scenario-shared component learns common knowledge across scenarios through generalized contrastive loss, while scenario-specific components capture unique characteristics via individual contrastive loss. The generalized contrastive loss contrasts representations across scenarios using label-aware positive samples and diffusion noise-enhanced negatives, while the individual contrastive loss employs dropout-based augmentation and cross-scenario encoding for hard negatives. Reciprocal similarity weighting reduces false positive/negative samples by assigning lower weights to dissimilar pairs. The model is trained end-to-end with a combined loss function and shows effectiveness in both CTR and CTCVR prediction tasks.

## Key Results
- Outperforms strong baselines (BaseDNN, MultiDNN, SharedBottom, MMoE, HMoE, STAR, M2M, FOREC) on public AliExpress dataset with consistent AUC improvements
- Achieves 5.37% relative CTR increase and 4.21% GMV improvement in production Ali-ads dataset online A/B test
- Particularly effective in sparse scenarios, demonstrating the value of cross-scenario knowledge transfer
- Adds minimal computational overhead compared to baseline models while maintaining similar response times

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Generalized contrastive loss helps the shared component learn common knowledge across scenarios by pulling samples with the same label and pushing those with different labels.
- Mechanism: The generalized contrastive loss contrasts representations of samples across different scenarios, using label-aware and diffusion noise-enhanced samples to guide the shared component in learning commonalities.
- Core assumption: Samples with the same label across different scenarios share meaningful common knowledge that can be captured by the shared component.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The core of our approach consists of two elaborated contrastive losses, namely generalized and individual contrastive loss, which aim at capturing common knowledge and scenario-specific knowledge, respectively."
  - [section 3.2] "The generalized contrastive loss is applied to the scenario-shared component for capturing the common knowledge from multiple scenarios."
- Break condition: If the assumption that samples with the same label share meaningful common knowledge across scenarios is false, the generalized contrastive loss would not be effective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Individual contrastive loss enhances the scenario-specific component by contrasting representations from different scenarios to capture scenario-specific knowledge.
- Mechanism: The individual contrastive loss uses dropout-based augmentation for positive samples and cross-scenario encoding for negative samples to help the scenario-specific component focus on unique characteristics of each scenario.
- Core assumption: The scenario-specific component can effectively learn unique characteristics of each scenario by contrasting with other scenarios.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The core of our approach consists of two elaborated contrastive losses, namely generalized and individual contrastive loss, which aim at capturing common knowledge and scenario-specific knowledge, respectively."
  - [section 3.3] "The individual contrastive loss aims to enhance the capacity of scenario-specific component to model the characteristics of the corresponding scenario data, so as to better capture the scenario-specific knowledge."
- Break condition: If the scenario-specific component cannot effectively learn unique characteristics by contrasting with other scenarios, the individual contrastive loss would not be effective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Reciprocal similarity weighting reduces the influence of false positive/negative samples in contrastive learning by assigning lower weights to dissimilar pairs.
- Mechanism: The reciprocal similarity weighting mechanism calculates a similarity score between feature embeddings and uses its inverse as the weight for contrastive pairs, reducing the impact of false contrastive samples.
- Core assumption: The reciprocal similarity score between feature embeddings can effectively identify false positive/negative samples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2.3] "The basic idea is that a positive contrastive pair should have a large similarity, while a negative contrastive pair should have a small similarity. If the cases become opposite, the corresponding pairs are likely to be false positive/negative pairs."
- Break condition: If the reciprocal similarity score cannot effectively identify false positive/negative samples, the weighting mechanism would not be effective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the diffusion noise enhanced negative samples perform compared to other methods of generating hard negatives in contrastive learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces diffusion noise enhanced negative samples and states they are more diverse and can span a broader space than standard contrastive samples.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not compare diffusion noise enhanced negatives to other hard negative sampling techniques like adversarial samples or momentum-based sampling.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing diffusion noise negatives against other hard negative generation methods in terms of downstream ranking performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between generalized and individual contrastive losses across different types of scenarios (e.g., sparse vs dense, similar vs dissimilar)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses two contrastive losses but does not analyze how their relative importance varies across scenario types.
- Why unresolved: The experiments use fixed coefficients for the contrastive losses without exploring scenario-specific optimal configurations.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis showing how performance varies with different λ1 and λ2 settings across scenarios with different characteristics (sparsity, similarity, etc.).

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed reciprocal similarity weighting mechanism perform in scenarios with highly imbalanced label distributions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper introduces reciprocal similarity weighting to handle false positives/negatives, but doesn't address scenarios where one label dominates.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't discuss performance in highly imbalanced scenarios where similarity-based weighting might behave differently.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the reciprocal similarity weighting against other weighting schemes in scenarios with varying class imbalance ratios.
