# Quantifying the Intrinsic Usefulness of Attributional Explanations for Graph Neural Networks with Artificial Simulatability Studies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15961
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15961
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes artificial simulatability studies using explanation-supervisable GNNs as "students" to quantify explanation usefulness via Student-Teacher Simulatability (STS) metric, validated on synthetic and molecular datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces artificial simulatability studies as a method to quantify the intrinsic usefulness of attributional explanations for graph neural networks. Instead of using human subjects, the authors propose using explanation-supervisable graph neural networks as "student" models to simulate human learning from explanations. The Student-Teacher Simulatability (STS) metric measures the performance difference between students trained with and without explanations, providing a scalable alternative to human-based evaluation. Experiments demonstrate that relevant explanations can significantly boost sample efficiency while adversarial explanations cause performance degradation.

## Method Summary
The method trains two identical student models - a reference student trained on targets only and an explanation student trained on both targets and explanations. Both models are evaluated on the same test set, and the STS metric captures the median difference in performance across multiple independent repetitions. The approach is validated using synthetic graph datasets with ground truth explanations and real-world molecular datasets, comparing different explanation methods and student architectures.

## Key Results
- STS metric effectively captures the benefit of explanations, showing significant performance differences between relevant and adversarial explanations
- Maximum explanation benefit occurs at intermediate training dataset sizes, with diminishing returns for larger datasets
- Both reference and explanation students benefit from larger datasets, but the relative advantage of explanations is strongest at smaller dataset sizes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Artificial simulatability studies can effectively replace human trials by using explanation-supervisable neural networks as "student" models
- Mechanism: Student models are trained on input data and explanations, then evaluated on unseen test data. Performance differences indicate explanation usefulness
- Core assumption: Explanation-supervisable neural networks can effectively learn from explanations in a way that mimics human learning
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "use explanation-supervisable graph neural networks to perform simulatability studies"
- Break condition: Student models cannot effectively learn from explanations or performance differences are not statistically significant

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: STS metric captures explanation benefit by measuring median performance difference between explanation-trained and reference students
- Mechanism: Multiple independent repetitions provide robust measurement accounting for stochastic training
- Core assumption: Multiple repetitions needed for statistical significance due to neural network training stochasticity
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "STS metric captures this benefit, calculated as the median difference in prediction performance between the two student models over multiple runs"
- Break condition: Median difference is not robust to outliers or doesn't account for training stochasticity

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Relevant explanations boost sample efficiency while adversarial explanations cause performance degradation
- Mechanism: Helpful explanations provide additional information for efficient learning; misleading explanations harm learning
- Core assumption: Explanations contain information that is either helpful or harmful to learning
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Results show that relevant explanations can significantly boost sample efficiency... while adversarial explanations cause performance degradation"
- Break condition: Explanations do not contain helpful or harmful information, or performance differences are not significant

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input graphs with node/edge features and targets -> Explanation data (node/edge importance tensors) -> Student models (prediction + explanation output) -> STS metric calculation -> Statistical significance testing

- Critical path:
  1. Prepare input data and explanations
  2. Initialize reference and explanation students
  3. Train reference student on targets only
  4. Train explanation student on targets and explanations
  5. Evaluate both students on test data
  6. Calculate STS metric and perform statistical tests
  7. Repeat for multiple runs and analyze results

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simple student architectures show more pronounced effects but have higher variance
  - Complex student architectures achieve better absolute performance but diminish relative explanation benefit
  - More training data improves absolute performance but reduces potential explanation benefit

- Failure signatures:
  - STS values close to zero or negative for relevant explanations
  - High variance in STS values across repetitions
  - Explanation students not outperforming reference students even with ground truth explanations
  - Statistical tests failing to reject the null hypothesis

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run student-teacher analysis with R=5 repetitions on simple synthetic dataset to verify basic methodology
  2. Vary training dataset size to identify where explanations provide maximum benefit
  3. Test with random/noisy explanations as baseline to ensure they don't show significant positive STS values

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can artificial simulatability studies be extended beyond attributional explanations to other modalities like counterfactuals or prototypes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Mentioned as future work, noting extension to concept-based and prototype-based explanations is promising
- Why unresolved: Current framework relies on supervised training of explanations, demonstrated only for attributional explanations
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstration of student-teacher analysis working with counterfactual explanations or prototype-based explanations

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between student network complexity and explanation usefulness?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Experiments show both overly simple and complex architectures have disadvantages
- Why unresolved: No principled way to determine sweet spot without extensive parameter search
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic study identifying architectural characteristics that consistently yield meaningful STS values

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does student-teacher simulatability compare to human-based studies in ecological validity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Positions artificial simulatability as cost-effective alternative but doesn't compare outcomes
- Why unresolved: Demonstrates artificial approach captures useful information but doesn't validate alignment with human understanding
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison where same explanations are evaluated using both artificial and human simulatability studies

## Limitations

- Core methodology assumes neural networks can effectively mimic human learning from explanations, which remains unproven
- Results may not generalize across different explanation methods and student architectures without further validation
- No direct comparison performed between artificial and human-based simulatability studies

## Confidence

- High confidence in mathematical formulation of STS metric and experimental methodology
- Medium confidence in synthetic dataset results showing clear effects of relevant vs. adversarial explanations
- Low confidence in direct comparison to human simulatability studies (no such comparison performed)
