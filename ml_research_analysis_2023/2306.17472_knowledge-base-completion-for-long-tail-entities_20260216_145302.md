# Knowledge Base Completion for Long-Tail Entities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17472
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17472
- Reference count: 17
- Outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on long-tail KBC with 43% recall vs 7% for best baseline

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses knowledge base completion (KBC) for long-tail entities in Wikidata, which have few triples and pose challenges for existing methods due to ambiguity and lack of context. The authors propose a two-stage method using different language models: a question-answering model (SpanBERT) for candidate retrieval from Wikipedia context, and an entity disambiguation model (GENRE) for validation and canonicalization. Experiments on the new MALT dataset show significant improvements over baselines, achieving 43% recall compared to 7% for the best baseline while maintaining competitive precision.

## Method Summary
The approach uses a two-stage pipeline: first, SpanBERT-large fine-tuned on SQuAD2.0 generates candidate answers by retrieving relevant Wikipedia sentences for subject-predicate pairs using carefully crafted prompts. Second, GENRE validates and disambiguates these candidates by mapping surface forms to knowledge graph entities. The method leverages Wikipedia context to improve performance and addresses challenges of multi-token facts and entity ambiguity in long-tail KBC. Candidate corroboration with threshold α filters low-confidence predictions.

## Key Results
- Achieves 43% recall compared to 7% for best baseline on MALT dataset
- Maintains competitive precision (44%) while significantly improving recall
- Outperforms baselines on all metrics (precision, recall, F1) for long-tail KBC

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using two different LMs in a two-stage pipeline improves KBC performance on long-tail entities compared to single-stage methods.
- Mechanism: SpanBERT QA generates high-recall candidates by retrieving Wikipedia sentences, while GENRE disambiguates candidates to KG entities. Their combination leverages complementary strengths.
- Core assumption: SpanBERT QA excels at recall while GENRE excels at precision.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our method leverages two different LMs in two stages: for candidate retrieval and for candidate verification and disambiguation."
- Break condition: If entity disambiguation model lacks coverage of target KG entities, pipeline fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Contextualizing prompts with relevant Wikipedia sentences significantly improves fact retrieval from LMs.
- Mechanism: Feeding Wikipedia sentences about subject entities into LM with cloze-style prompts provides disambiguation context.
- Core assumption: Wikipedia articles contain sufficient context about long-tail entities.
- Evidence anchors: [appendix A.1.2] "adding context can remarkably improve the performances... BERT-large (4.7→ 12.3) and GENRE (7.4 → 18.9)."
- Break condition: If Wikipedia article lacks information or contains conflicting information.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Two-stage design with different LM types specifically addresses multi-token facts and entity ambiguity.
- Mechanism: SpanBERT QA handles multi-token phrases via span extraction, while GENRE handles ambiguity via entity disambiguation training.
- Core assumption: Combination of span extraction and entity disambiguation addresses dual challenges of long-tail KBC.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4] "65.3% triple facts where O entity is multi-word phrase, and 58.6% ambiguous facts."
- Break condition: If KG schema changes significantly or entities have different naming conventions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input layer: Subject-predicate pair
  - Stage 1: SpanBERT QA model with Wikipedia context retrieval
  - Stage 2: GENRE entity disambiguation model
  - Output layer: Ranked list of KBC facts

- Critical path:
  1. Retrieve Wikipedia article for subject entity
  2. Extract relevant sentences for each predicate
  3. Run SpanBERT QA on each sentence-prompt pair
  4. Aggregate candidates and scores
  5. Run GENRE on each candidate with context
  6. Filter and rank final predictions

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Two different LMs increase complexity but leverage complementary strengths
  - Wikipedia context retrieval adds latency but significantly improves performance
  - Two-stage design allows candidate pruning but may miss facts if early candidates are incorrect

- Failure signatures:
  - Low recall: Wikipedia articles lack sufficient context, or SpanBERT QA fails to extract correct spans
  - Low precision: GENRE entity disambiguation incorrectly maps candidates, or Wikipedia context is misleading
  - Slow performance: Inefficient Wikipedia retrieval or excessive number of context sentences

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test two-stage pipeline on small MALT subset with ground truth to verify full pipeline works
  2. Compare performance of different context selection strategies (all sentences vs. top-k relevant sentences)
  3. Evaluate impact of candidate pruning thresholds on precision-recall tradeoff

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the two-stage KBC method be further improved by incorporating additional context or prompt engineering techniques?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that "proper prompts are needed for a better recall, e.g., by additional information or prompt engineering" and that their method still leaves "substantial room for further improvement".
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores a limited set of prompt templates and does not investigate impact of more sophisticated prompt engineering or additional context sources.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance with different prompt templates, context sources, or prompt engineering techniques on MALT dataset.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does performance of two-stage KBC method compare to human-curated knowledge base completion?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that their method is designed to "aid a human curator by judicious suggestions for facts that would augment the KG" and reports human evaluation results.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide direct comparison between two-stage method and human-curated KBC in terms of precision, recall, or F1 score.
- What evidence would resolve it: Study comparing performance of two-stage method and human curators on held-out test set of long-tail facts from Wikidata.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the two-stage KBC method be adapted to handle entities not present in underlying knowledge graph?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that their methodology "cannot predict objects that are not already known to that LM and its underlying KB".
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore potential extensions or modifications to enable handling entities outside scope of underlying knowledge graph.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating performance of adapted version on dataset containing entities not present in underlying knowledge graph.

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on Wikipedia articles creates potential vulnerability if coverage is sparse or outdated
- Performance metrics based on relatively small dataset (MALT) with only 8 relations
- GENRE entity disambiguation requires significant training data and may struggle with out-of-distribution entities
- Method's complexity (two LMs + Wikipedia retrieval) introduces latency limiting real-time applications

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Two-stage pipeline design using complementary LMs is effective for long-tail KBC
- Wikipedia context significantly improves LM performance on this task
- Method outperforms existing baselines on MALT dataset metrics

**Medium Confidence:**
- Approach will generalize to other knowledge graphs beyond Wikidata
- Performance gains will scale to larger, more diverse KBC datasets
- Wikipedia dependency is manageable across different domains

**Low Confidence:**
- Method's performance on real-world KBC tasks without ground truth validation
- Robustness to evolving knowledge graph schemas
- Computational efficiency for production deployment
