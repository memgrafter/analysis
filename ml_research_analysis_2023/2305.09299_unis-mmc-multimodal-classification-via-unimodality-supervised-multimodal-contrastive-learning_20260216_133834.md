# UniS-MMC: Multimodal Classification via Unimodality-supervised Multimodal Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09299
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: Achieves 94.7% accuracy on UPMC-Food-101 and 80.2% accuracy on N24News headline classification

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UniS-MMC, a novel multimodal classification method that leverages unimodal predictions as weak supervision to align unimodal representations toward the more effective modality. The method captures task-related unimodal representations and unimodal predictions, then aligns unimodal representations with the more effective modality using multimodal contrastive learning under supervision of unimodal predictions. Experiments on UPMC-Food-101 and N24News datasets demonstrate that UniS-MMC outperforms current state-of-the-art multimodal methods.

## Method Summary
UniS-MMC is a multimodal classification method that uses unimodality-supervised contrastive learning to align unimodal representations. The approach employs a multi-task framework where shared encoders produce unimodal representations for both unimodal prediction loss and multimodal prediction loss. The method introduces a unimodality check step that captures task-related unimodal representations and provides weak supervision for modality effectiveness. Contrastive learning then pulls effective representations together, pushes both ineffective representations apart, and aligns ineffective representations toward the effective ones based on unimodal prediction correctness.

## Key Results
- Achieves 94.7% accuracy on UPMC-Food-101 dataset
- Achieves 80.2% accuracy on N24News headline classification
- Outperforms current state-of-the-art multimodal methods on both datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
UniS-MMC improves multimodal classification by aligning unimodal representations toward the more effective modality under weak supervision from unimodal predictions. For each sample, unimodal representations are classified as "effective" (correct prediction) or "ineffective" (incorrect prediction). Contrastive learning then pulls effective representations together (positive pairs), pushes both ineffective representations apart (negative pairs), and aligns ineffective representations toward the effective ones (semi-positive pairs). The modality with a correct unimodal prediction contains more reliable information for the task than the incorrect one.

### Mechanism 2
Introducing a unimodality check step captures task-related unimodal representations and provides weak supervision for modality effectiveness. Each modality's hidden representation is passed through a unimodal classifier; prediction correctness is recorded. This check acts as a gating mechanism for contrastive pair formation. The unimodal classifier's prediction is a reliable proxy for the modality's informativeness for the downstream task.

### Mechanism 3
Multi-task-based framework enables simultaneous unimodal and multimodal learning, with unimodal predictions feeding contrastive alignment. Shared encoders produce unimodal representations for both unimodal prediction loss (Luni) and multimodal prediction loss (Lmulti). Contrastive loss (Lmmc) aligns representations based on unimodal prediction correctness. Sharing representations between unimodal and multimodal tasks preserves complementary information while enabling alignment supervision.

  - Why needed here: To reuse unimodal representations for both unimodal and multimodal predictions, reducing redundancy and enabling supervision transfer

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Modality encoders (ViT for image, BERT/RoBERTa for text) → shared unimodal representations → Unimodal classifiers → unimodal predictions → unimodality check → Contrastive loss module → Multimodal classifier → final classification from concatenated unimodal representations

- **Critical path:**
  1. Encode raw inputs → unimodal representations
  2. Classify unimodally → determine pair types
  3. Apply multimodal contrastive loss
  4. Concatenate representations → multimodal classification

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Parameter sharing speeds training but may conflate unimodal and multimodal objectives
  - Semi-positive pair alignment adds complexity but improves robustness to noisy modalities
  - Weak supervision avoids extra labeling but depends on unimodal classifier quality

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Degraded unimodal accuracy indicates shared encoder is not preserving modality-specific signals
  - Over-alignment of representations may cause loss of complementary information
  - High variance in contrastive loss may indicate unstable weak supervision signal

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Compare unimodal-only, unimodal + multimodal (AggMM), and full UniS-MMC to isolate effect of contrastive alignment
  2. Ablate the semi-positive pair setting to measure its contribution to robustness
  3. Swap unimodal backbones (BERT ↔ RoBERTa) to test sensitivity to unimodal prediction quality

## Limitations
- Method's reliance on unimodal prediction correctness as weak supervision creates potential fragility if either modality has poor unimodal accuracy
- Semi-positive pair mechanism adds complexity without clear theoretical grounding for why partial alignment should help rather than harm
- Experimental validation is limited to two relatively small datasets, leaving generalization to larger or noisier multimodal domains unclear

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (weak supervision through unimodal correctness): Medium - conceptually sound but sensitive to unimodal classifier quality
- Mechanism 2 (semi-positive pair alignment): Low - novel approach with limited theoretical justification
- Mechanism 3 (multi-task parameter sharing): Medium - standard practice but potential objective conflicts not thoroughly explored
