## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Penalty Gradient Normalization (PGN), a novel normalization technique that addresses the training instability of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) caused by sharp gradient spaces. PGN imposes a penalty gradient norm constraint on the discriminator function, which increases its capacity while improving stability. The method is theoretically proven to be gradient norm bounded and can be applied to different GAN architectures with minimal modification. Experimental results demonstrate that GANs trained with PGN outperform existing methods in terms of Inception Score and Frechet Inception Distance across three standard datasets.

## Method Summary
PGN normalizes the discriminator output by dividing it by the gradient norm, ensuring the modified function has a bounded gradient. This simple yet effective technique can be integrated into existing GAN architectures without requiring major changes to the model structure or training process. The method is compatible with both CNN and ResNet architectures and works by constraining the discriminator's gradients to prevent the generator from receiving extremely large or small gradients that can cause instability.

## Key Results
- PGN achieves state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10, STL-10, and CelebA-HQ datasets
- Improves Inception Score and Frechet Inception Distance compared to existing methods
- Demonstrates training stability across different GAN architectures with minimal modification required
- Outperforms baseline methods including SN-GAN, GN-GAN, and CR variants
