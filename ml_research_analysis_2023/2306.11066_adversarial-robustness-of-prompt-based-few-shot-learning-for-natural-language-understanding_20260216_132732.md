# Adversarial Robustness of Prompt-based Few-Shot Learning for Natural Language Understanding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11066
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11066
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: Vanilla prompt-based few-shot methods show lower adversarial robustness than fully fine-tuned models

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an extensive empirical evaluation of adversarial robustness in prompt-based few-shot learning methods for natural language understanding tasks. The study compares state-of-the-art prompt-based approaches including LM-BFF, PET, and iPET against fully fine-tuned models across 6 GLUE tasks using the AdvGLUE benchmark. Results demonstrate that while vanilla prompt-based few-shot methods exhibit notable performance degradation under adversarial perturbations compared to fully fine-tuned models, using unlabeled data (iPET) and multiple prompts (PET) can significantly improve robustness. The study also reveals that increasing few-shot examples and model size enhances adversarial robustness for vanilla few-shot learning methods.

## Method Summary
The study conducts systematic comparisons of prompt-based few-shot learning methods against fully fine-tuned models across multiple GLUE benchmark tasks. The evaluation employs the AdvGLUE adversarial benchmark to assess model robustness under adversarial perturbations. Key methods evaluated include LM-BFF (vanilla prompt-based few-shot learning), PET (multiple prompts), and iPET (using unlabeled data). The experiments systematically vary the number of few-shot examples and model sizes to analyze their impact on adversarial robustness. Performance is measured as relative task performance drops when facing adversarial attacks.

## Key Results
- Vanilla prompt-based few-shot methods (LM-BFF) show significantly lower adversarial robustness than fully fine-tuned models
- iPET and PET methods can improve adversarial robustness compared to vanilla approaches
- Increasing few-shot examples and model size leads to improved adversarial robustness for vanilla few-shot methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The improved adversarial robustness in iPET and PET methods stems from their ability to leverage additional information beyond the limited few-shot examples. iPET's use of unlabeled data allows the model to learn more robust representations by exposing it to a broader distribution of examples, while PET's multiple prompts provide diverse perspectives on the task, making the model less sensitive to adversarial perturbations that might exploit specific prompt formulations. The increased model size and additional few-shot examples provide more parameters and training signals, respectively, enabling better learning of robust decision boundaries that are less vulnerable to adversarial attacks.

## Foundational Learning

**Natural Language Understanding (NLU)**: Understanding and processing human language for tasks like classification, sentiment analysis, and question answering. Why needed: Core domain being evaluated for robustness. Quick check: Can models correctly classify sentiment in clean examples.

**Prompt-based Learning**: Using natural language prompts to guide model behavior instead of explicit fine-tuning. Why needed: The primary learning paradigm being evaluated. Quick check: Can models follow instructions in zero-shot settings.

**Few-Shot Learning**: Learning from a very limited number of labeled examples (typically 8-32 per class). Why needed: The constrained training scenario being studied. Quick check: Can models learn new tasks from 16 examples per class.

**Adversarial Robustness**: A model's ability to maintain performance when faced with adversarially perturbed inputs. Why needed: The core evaluation metric of the study. Quick check: Performance drop when adding small perturbations to inputs.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data (few-shot examples + unlabeled data) -> Prompt Engineering (prompt design) -> Model (GPT-style architecture) -> Training (LM-BFF/PET/iPET) -> Evaluation (GLUE + AdvGLUE)

**Critical Path**: Prompt design → Few-shot example selection → Model training → Adversarial evaluation. The quality of prompt design and few-shot selection directly impacts both baseline performance and robustness.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Few-shot methods prioritize parameter efficiency and in-domain performance over robustness. The tradeoff is between using limited data efficiently versus building more robust representations through additional data or model capacity.

**Failure Signatures**: Large performance drops on adversarial examples while maintaining reasonable performance on clean data. Models become overly sensitive to prompt phrasing and specific input patterns.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to GLUE tasks and their corresponding AdvGLUE adversarial sets
- Focus on GPT-style models may not generalize to BERT or T5 architectures
- Adversarial evaluation depends on specific attack methods in AdvGLUE, not representing all realistic scenarios

## Confidence
- High confidence: Vanilla prompt-based FSL methods show lower adversarial robustness than fully fine-tuned models
- Medium confidence: iPET and PET methods improve adversarial robustness compared to vanilla approaches
- Medium confidence: Increasing few-shot examples and model size improves robustness

## Next Checks
