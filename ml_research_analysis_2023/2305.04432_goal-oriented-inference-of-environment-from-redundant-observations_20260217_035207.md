# Goal-oriented inference of environment from redundant observations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04432
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04432
- Reference count: 40
- GOEI outperforms CEI in nonstationary environments with 2.7 bits information reduction

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning optimal behavior in environments where observations contain redundant information not directly relevant to reward prediction. The authors formulate this as a Redundantly Observable Markov Decision Process (ROMDP) and propose Goal-Oriented Environment Inference (GOEI) as a solution. GOEI identifies "core states" relevant to reward prediction from redundant observations using a generative model structure that clusters observations based on their relationships to action-reward contingencies. The method dynamically expands state space using Dirichlet processes and incorporates online learning with forgetting to handle nonstationary environments. When combined with Action-value Thompson Sampling, GOEI achieves faster learning speeds and better adaptability to environmental changes compared to conventional methods.

## Method Summary
GOEI takes a fundamentally different approach from conventional Complete Environment Inference (CEI) methods by focusing on identifying only the core states that matter for reward prediction rather than attempting to infer transition rules among all observations. The method uses a generative model structure that clusters observations based on their relationships to action-reward contingencies, enabling efficient identification of core states. State space expansion is handled dynamically through Dirichlet processes, allowing the model to adapt to environmental complexity. The approach incorporates online learning with forgetting mechanisms to handle nonstationary environments where reward rules and transition dynamics change over time. This goal-oriented inference strategy enables GOEI to achieve approximately 2.7 bits of information reduction while maintaining zero information loss about core states, resulting in faster learning and better adaptation compared to CEI.

## Key Results
- GOEI achieves approximately 2.7 bits of information reduction compared to CEI
- Maintains zero information loss about core states while reducing redundancy
- Outperforms CEI in both nonstationary reward-rule and transition-rule environments
- Demonstrates faster learning speeds and better adaptability to environmental changes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
GOEI works by fundamentally reframing the environment inference problem from complete state space reconstruction to goal-oriented core state identification. Instead of attempting to learn transition dynamics among all observations, GOEI uses a generative model that clusters observations based on their relationships to action-reward contingencies. This selective approach allows the method to focus computational resources on the aspects of the environment that directly impact decision-making. The dynamic state space expansion through Dirichlet processes enables the model to adaptively incorporate new information while the online learning with forgetting mechanisms allow it to efficiently track changes in nonstationary environments. By maintaining focus on reward-relevant information, GOEI avoids the computational burden and potential overfitting associated with learning irrelevant transition dynamics.

## Foundational Learning

**Redundantly Observable Markov Decision Process (ROMDP)**: An extension of MDP where observations contain redundant information not directly relevant to reward prediction. Why needed: Provides the theoretical framework for understanding environments with irrelevant observations. Quick check: Can the environment be modeled where multiple observations map to the same underlying state?

**Core State Identification**: The process of identifying states that are directly relevant to reward prediction from redundant observations. Why needed: Enables focus on reward-relevant information while ignoring computational noise. Quick check: Does the method correctly identify which observations correspond to reward-relevant states?

**Generative Model Clustering**: Using generative models to cluster observations based on their relationships to action-reward contingencies. Why needed: Provides the mathematical foundation for grouping redundant observations. Quick check: Do clustered observations show similar reward patterns under the same actions?

**Dirichlet Process for State Expansion**: A Bayesian nonparametric approach for dynamically expanding state space. Why needed: Allows adaptive handling of environmental complexity without predefining state space size. Quick check: Does the state space grow appropriately as new observation patterns emerge?

**Online Learning with Forgetting**: Learning mechanisms that can track changes in nonstationary environments by forgetting outdated information. Why needed: Essential for handling environments where reward rules and transition dynamics change over time. Quick check: Can the model adapt when reward contingencies suddenly change?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Observations -> Generative Model -> Core State Clustering -> Action-value Thompson Sampling -> Policy Update -> Environment Interaction

**Critical Path**: The generative model processes observations to identify core states, which are then used by the action-value Thompson sampling component to make decisions. The policy update mechanism incorporates new information while the forgetting mechanism ensures adaptation to nonstationary changes.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades completeness of environment modeling for computational efficiency and faster learning by focusing only on reward-relevant information. This creates a bias toward core states but enables better performance in nonstationary settings.

**Failure Signatures**: The method may fail when core state identification is ambiguous due to high observation redundancy, or when the generative model incorrectly clusters observations from different reward regimes. Nonstationary environments with rapid changes may also challenge the forgetting mechanism.

## Limitations
- Performance in environments with continuous rather than discrete observations requires further validation
- Claims of "zero information loss about core states" assume perfect core state identification, which lacks empirical validation under varying redundancy levels
- Scalability to environments with highly complex observation spaces and non-Markovian dynamics remains uncertain
