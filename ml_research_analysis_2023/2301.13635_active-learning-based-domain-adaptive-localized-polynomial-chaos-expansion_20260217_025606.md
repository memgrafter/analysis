# Active Learning-based Domain Adaptive Localized Polynomial Chaos Expansion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.13635
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13635
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces DAL-PCE, an active learning method for building surrogate models that outperforms global PCE and SSE on problems with strong nonlinearities and discontinuities

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for constructing surrogate models of complex functions with nonlinearities, discontinuities, and singularities. The method, called Domain Adaptive Localized Polynomial Chaos Expansion (DAL-PCE), addresses limitations of traditional global Polynomial Chaos Expansion (PCE) by decomposing the input random space into sub-domains, each approximated by low-order polynomial expansions. The decomposition strategy is guided by an active learning approach that uses a novel Θ criterion to balance exploration and exploitation, allowing the method to adapt to the function's structure while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
DAL-PCE builds upon the foundation of Polynomial Chaos Expansion (PCE) but introduces a localized approach to address the Gibbs phenomenon that occurs when using global polynomial approximations for functions with discontinuities. The method partitions the input space into sub-domains where low-order polynomial expansions can effectively capture the local behavior. The partitioning is guided by an active learning strategy that uses a novel Θ criterion, which balances exploitation of the current surrogate model with exploration of regions where uncertainty is high. This adaptive approach allows DAL-PCE to focus computational resources on regions that contribute most to the overall uncertainty, making it particularly effective for high-dimensional problems with low-dimensional discontinuities.

## Key Results
- DAL-PCE outperforms both global PCE and Stochastic Spectral Embedding (SSE) in accuracy for problems with strong nonlinearities and discontinuities
- The method demonstrates superior robustness, particularly for high-dimensional problems with low-dimensional discontinuities
- Active learning approach with the Θ criterion effectively balances exploration and exploitation during the adaptive decomposition process

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DAL-PCE works by addressing the fundamental limitation of global polynomial approximations when dealing with discontinuities and strong nonlinearities. By decomposing the input space into smaller sub-domains, each region can be approximated by low-order polynomials that are well-suited to local behavior. The active learning approach with the Θ criterion ensures that the decomposition focuses on regions where the surrogate model has high uncertainty or where discontinuities are likely to occur, preventing unnecessary refinement in smooth regions while maintaining accuracy where it matters most.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Input Space -> Domain Decomposition Engine -> Local PCE Approximators -> Active Learning Controller -> Uncertainty Quantification

**Critical Path**
1. Initial global PCE approximation
2. Error estimation and identification of regions needing refinement
3. Domain decomposition guided by Θ criterion
4. Local PCE construction in sub-domains
5. Uncertainty quantification and refinement loop

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Local vs. global approximations: DAL-PCE sacrifices global smoothness for local accuracy
- Exploration vs. exploitation: The Θ criterion must balance finding new discontinuities against refining known ones
- Computational cost vs. accuracy: More sub-domains increase accuracy but also computational complexity

**Failure Signatures**
- Over-refinement in smooth regions leading to unnecessary computational cost
- Under-refinement near discontinuities causing significant approximation errors
- Poor convergence if the Θ criterion is not properly tuned

## Limitations

- Limited validation on problems with more than 10 input dimensions, leaving scalability to extremely high-dimensional problems uncertain
- Computational cost of the adaptive decomposition process may become prohibitive for large-scale applications with expensive function evaluations
- Performance sensitivity to hyperparameter choices (maximum polynomial order, exploration-exploitation balance) is not thoroughly analyzed
