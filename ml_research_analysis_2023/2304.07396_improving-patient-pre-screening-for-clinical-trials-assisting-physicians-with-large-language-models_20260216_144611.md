# Improving Patient Pre-screening for Clinical Trials: Assisting Physicians with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.07396
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07396
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Key result:** LLM-assisted screening with physician oversight achieved 100% recall and 71% precision while reducing manual screening workload by 90%

## Executive Summary
This study presents a proof-of-concept system using large language models to assist physicians in screening patients for clinical trial eligibility. The approach combines one-shot, selection-inference, and chain-of-thought prompting strategies with InstructGPT to evaluate patient medical profiles against trial eligibility criteria. The system demonstrated that physician-in-the-loop approaches can achieve perfect recall (1.0) while reducing the number of criteria physicians need to manually check by an estimated 90%, compared to physician-only screening that achieved 0.5 recall.

## Method Summary
The study employed InstructGPT with a multi-technique prompting strategy combining one-shot examples, selection-inference reasoning, and chain-of-thought explanations. The system was evaluated on 10 synthetic patient profiles and 146 clinical trials, with the LLM identifying screenable criteria and providing transparent reasoning chains for physician review. The evaluation compared LLM-only screening against physician-in-the-loop approaches, measuring recall, precision, and workload reduction metrics.

## Key Results
- LLM correctly identified screenable criteria in 72% of cases
- LLM achieved 72% accuracy in evaluating screenable criteria
- Physician-in-the-loop approach achieved 100% recall versus 50% for LLM alone
- Physician-in-the-loop approach reduced criteria to check by ~90% while maintaining 71% precision

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system works by leveraging the LLM's natural language understanding capabilities to parse complex eligibility criteria and match them against patient medical histories. The chain-of-thought reasoning provides transparent decision pathways that physicians can verify, combining automated screening efficiency with human clinical judgment for critical decisions.

## Foundational Learning
**Clinical trial eligibility criteria:** Structured requirements specifying patient characteristics for trial inclusion/exclusion. Needed to understand what the system must evaluate. Quick check: Can identify inclusion/exclusion criteria types in sample trials.
**Synthetic patient profiles:** Artificially generated medical records with controlled variables. Needed for consistent evaluation baseline. Quick check: Profiles contain all required medical history elements.
**Chain-of-thought prompting:** Technique where models show step-by-step reasoning. Needed for transparent decision-making. Quick check: Can trace each eligibility decision back through reasoning steps.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Patient profile -> LLM with prompting strategy -> Screenable criteria identification -> Chain-of-thought reasoning -> Physician review interface
**Critical path:** Patient data input → LLM evaluation → Criteria screening → Reasoning generation → Physician verification
**Design tradeoffs:** Synthetic vs real patient data (consistency vs realism), automated vs manual screening (speed vs accuracy), transparency vs complexity in reasoning chains
**Failure signatures:** LLM misses criteria requiring temporal reasoning, struggles with ambiguous medical terminology, fails on edge cases not covered in training examples
**3 first experiments:** 1) Test on real patient records from EHR systems, 2) Compare different LLM models with same prompting strategy, 3) Measure actual time savings in clinical workflow

None

## Limitations
- Synthetic patient profiles may not capture real clinical data complexity and variability
- Small sample of 146 clinical trials may not represent full heterogeneity of eligibility requirements
- Manual annotation process for ground truth introduces potential subjectivity without quantification

## Confidence
**High confidence:** Physician-in-the-loop approach significantly improves recall (1.0 vs 0.5) while reducing workload by ~90% is well-supported by experimental results
**Medium confidence:** 72% accuracy rate for screenable criteria evaluation is reliable within synthetic dataset context, but generalizability to real clinical data remains uncertain
**Low confidence:** Claimed 90% reduction in physician workload requires validation in actual clinical workflows, as it was estimated rather than directly measured

## Next Checks
1. Conduct prospective validation using real patient medical records from electronic health systems to assess performance on authentic clinical data with natural language variability and incomplete documentation
2. Implement A/B testing comparing physician-only screening versus LLM-assisted screening in a clinical trial center, measuring both accuracy metrics and actual time savings across multiple trial types and medical specialties
3. Perform error analysis on LLM failures to identify systematic patterns (e.g., specific criterion types, medical conditions, or reasoning chains) that could inform model improvements or trigger physician review protocols
