# Contrastive Learning for Low-light Raw Denoising

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03352
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03352
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Denoising contrastive regularization (DCR) improves low-light raw denoising with PSNR of 31.97, SSIM of 0.870, and LPIPS of 0.065 on still images

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel denoising contrastive regularization (DCR) method for low-light raw image denoising. The approach combines a FastDVDnet denoising backbone with a new Wnet feature embedding network that extracts high-frequency information via wavelet transforms. By pulling denoised features toward clean features and pushing them away from noisy features in feature space, the method achieves state-of-the-art results on challenging low-light datasets including images at 0.6 millilux and videos under starlight (<0.001 lux).

## Method Summary
The method employs a denoising contrastive regularization framework that uses a FastDVDnet network for initial denoising, paired with a novel Wnet feature embedding network. Wnet uses Haar wavelet transforms to isolate high-frequency components (HL, LH, HH subbands) and learns noise-specific features through binary classification. The DCR loss computes cosine similarities between denoised, clean, and noisy feature embeddings across multiple intermediate layers, weighted from 1/32 to 1, and combines this with L1 and LPIPS losses for training.

## Key Results
- Achieves PSNR of 31.97, SSIM of 0.870, and LPIPS of 0.065 on still low-light images
- Outperforms FastDVDnet baseline on real low-light datasets
- Demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on starlight video dataset
- Shows improved visual quality compared to existing methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The DCR loss pulls denoised features toward clean features and pushes them away from noisy features in feature space.
- Mechanism: For each denoised image anchor, the loss computes cosine similarity to positive (clean) and negative (noisy) pairs across multiple intermediate layers of Wnet, then applies an L1 loss on these similarities.
- Core assumption: Cosine similarity in the Wnet feature space is a meaningful proxy for perceptual closeness between clean and denoised images.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "DCR makes the denoised image closer to the clean image and far away from the noisy image in feature space"
  - [section] "we adopt the mean value of pixel-wise cosine similarity with L1 loss as the similarity between feature maps"
- Break condition: If the Wnet feature space does not correlate with human perception, the DCR will not improve visual quality.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Wnet is more effective at extracting high-frequency texture information relevant to denoising than generic backbones like VGG.
- Mechanism: Wnet uses Haar wavelet transforms to isolate HL, LH, HH subbands and learns noise-specific features via binary classification on synthetic noisy-clean pairs.
- Core assumption: High-frequency subbands are the primary carriers of noise information, and a shallow CNN can capture this better than deep semantic networks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we build a new feature embedding network called Wnet, which is more effective to extract high-frequency information"
  - [section] "we use the Haar wavelet transform to extract the informative high-frequency components (HL, LH, and HH) and then use CNN to learn these features"
- Break condition: If the wavelet decomposition fails to align with sensor noise patterns, Wnet's features will be suboptimal.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-scale feature extraction from different convolution layers improves contrastive learning's effectiveness.
- Mechanism: DCR aggregates cosine similarity losses from the 1st through 5th convolution layers of Wnet, weighted increasingly from 1/32 to 1.
- Core assumption: Combining coarse and fine features yields a richer similarity signal than using a single layer.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we use a combination of a perceptual loss (LPIPS... and proposed Closs for our training objective"
  - [section] "We set the Closs in Eq. (1) after the latent features of the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th and 5th convolution layers from the pre-trained Wnet"
- Break condition: If deeper layers dominate too heavily, the loss may ignore fine-grained noise differences.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Raw sensor noise modeling (Poisson-Gaussian)
  - Why needed here: The method assumes realistic noise synthesis for training Wnet and DCR; without this, positive/negative pairs are not meaningful.
  - Quick check question: What are the two main components of real CMOS sensor noise and how do they scale with signal intensity?

- Concept: Wavelet decomposition (Haar transform)
  - Why needed here: Wnet relies on decomposing images into frequency subbands to isolate noise; understanding this is essential to grasp why Wnet is designed this way.
  - Quick check question: Which Haar subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH) contain the highest frequency information relevant for denoising?

- Concept: Contrastive learning loss formulations
  - Why needed here: DCR's novelty lies in applying contrastive learning to low-level vision; one must know how anchors, positives, and negatives are defined and compared.
  - Quick check question: In contrastive learning, what role does the temperature parameter play in the softmax over similarity scores?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Noisy input -> FastDVDnet -> denoised output; simultaneously -> Wnet -> feature embeddings -> Closs; final loss -> gradient update
- Critical path: Noisy input → FastDVDnet → denoised output; simultaneously → Wnet → feature embeddings → Closs; final loss → gradient update
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Shallow vs deep Wnet: shallow reduces semantic drift but may underfit complex noise; deep may overfit to low-frequency structure
  - Haar vs learned front-end: Haar is fixed and interpretable but may not capture sensor-specific noise patterns; learned may adapt better but needs more data
  - Layer weighting: aggressive weighting of deep layers may suppress fine noise; uniform weighting may underutilize rich features
- Failure signatures:
  - Visual artifacts in smooth regions: Wnet may be over-emphasizing high-frequency similarity
  - Poor generalization to new sensors: synthetic noise model mismatch or Wnet overfits to training noise
  - Training instability: Closs dominates L1/LPIPS if α is too high
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation: Replace Wnet with frozen VGG, retrain DCR, compare PSNR/LPIPS on validation set
  2. Layer sensitivity: Train with only 1st, 3rd, and 5th layers active in Closs, analyze impact on texture preservation
  3. Noise model swap: Swap physics-based noise model for learned noise2noise model, measure robustness to synthetic-real gap

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed Wnet architecture compare to other potential feature embedding networks for low-light denoising tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces Wnet as a task-related feature embedding network and claims it is more effective at extracting high-frequency information compared to pre-trained VGG models. However, it does not compare Wnet to other potential architectures specifically designed for low-level vision tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares Wnet to pre-trained VGG models and does not explore other potential architectures or ablate the components of Wnet to determine their individual contributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive comparison of Wnet against other feature embedding architectures (e.g., ResNet, DenseNet) on the same task, along with an ablation study of Wnet's components, would provide clarity on its effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different weight coefficients (ωi) in the contrastive loss function on the final denoising performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the weight coefficients for the contrastive loss are set to specific values (1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, and 1 for the 1st to 5th convolution layers respectively) but does not provide an analysis of how these values were chosen or how they affect performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the sensitivity of the denoising performance to these weight coefficients or provide a justification for the chosen values.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis showing the performance impact of varying these weight coefficients, potentially through a grid search or sensitivity analysis, would clarify their importance and optimal values.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed method perform on real-world low-light images with complex noise patterns not captured in the training dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates the method on real low-light datasets, including still images taken in 0.6 millilux and videos under starlight (<0.001 lux). However, it does not discuss the method's performance on images with noise patterns or artifacts not present in the training data.
- Why unresolved: Real-world low-light images may contain noise patterns or artifacts not captured in the training dataset, and the paper does not address how the model generalizes to such cases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on a diverse set of real-world low-light images with various noise patterns and artifacts, and comparing its performance to other methods, would provide insight into its robustness and generalization capabilities.

## Limitations

- The specific network architecture details for FastDVDnet and Wnet are not provided, requiring reconstruction from the original FastDVDnet paper
- The synthetic noise model parameters used for training are referenced but not explicitly detailed
- The effectiveness of the DCR method may be limited by the quality of the feature embedding space and the assumption that cosine similarity in this space correlates with perceptual quality

## Confidence

- High confidence: The overall methodology and experimental results showing PSNR of 31.97, SSIM of 0.870, and LPIPS of 0.065 on still images
- Medium confidence: The mechanism of Wnet for extracting high-frequency information and the effectiveness of multi-scale feature extraction
- Low confidence: The specific implementation details of the contrastive loss and the optimal weighting of loss components
