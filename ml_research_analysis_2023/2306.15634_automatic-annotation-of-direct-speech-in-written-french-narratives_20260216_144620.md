# Automatic Annotation of Direct Speech in Written French Narratives

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.15634
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15634
- **Reference count:** 25
- **Key outcome:** Unified framework for AADS in French narratives with token-level F1 up to 95% using CamemBERT

## Executive Summary
This work establishes a unified framework for designing and evaluating models for automatic annotation of direct speech in French narratives. The authors consolidate the largest-to-date French narrative dataset annotated with direct speech at the word level and adapt baselines from other languages. They conduct extensive evaluations focused on generalization, comparing rule-based methods with deep learning approaches. The study reveals that while rule-based methods work well on well-formatted texts, they struggle with incises and poor formatting, whereas deep learning models achieve better overall performance and are more robust to formatting variations.

## Method Summary
The authors created a comprehensive evaluation framework by consolidating a large French narrative dataset annotated at the word level for direct speech. They adapted existing baselines from other languages and conducted systematic comparisons between rule-based and deep learning approaches. The rule-based methods relied on formatting cues and punctuation patterns, while deep learning models were fine-tuned on CamemBERT architecture. Evaluation focused on both token-level accuracy and full span recognition across different chapters of the source text, revealing significant performance variation.

## Key Results
- Rule-based methods perform well on well-formatted texts but struggle with incises and poor formatting
- Deep learning models achieve token-level F1-scores up to 95% and are more robust to formatting variations
- Best-performing model is fine-tuned CamemBERT, though performance varies widely across chapters (73% to 98% F1)
- All models struggle with recognizing full direct speech spans despite strong token-level performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by leveraging the structural patterns of direct speech in French narratives while addressing the unique challenges of French punctuation and formatting conventions. Deep learning models capture contextual dependencies that rule-based systems miss, particularly in handling complex incises and formatting variations. The token-level annotation approach enables precise evaluation of model performance across different linguistic contexts within the narrative.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Direct Speech Annotation**: Marking words belonging to spoken dialogue within narrative text - needed for dialogue extraction and narrative analysis; quick check: verify token-level annotations align with human judgments

2. **French Narrative Formatting**: Understanding French conventions for dialogue presentation including guillemets, incises, and typographic variations - needed because French formatting differs significantly from English; quick check: validate rule-based patterns against multiple French text sources

3. **Token vs Span Evaluation**: Distinguishing between token-level accuracy and full span recognition - needed because high token accuracy doesn't guarantee correct dialogue extraction; quick check: compare F1 scores with exact match rates for dialogue spans

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Text preprocessing -> Token classification -> Span reconstruction -> Evaluation metrics

**Critical Path:** Raw text → Feature extraction → Model prediction → Post-processing → Evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs:** Rule-based methods offer interpretability and speed but lack robustness; deep learning models provide better generalization but require more data and computational resources

**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance on incises indicates formatting dependency; chapter-wise variation suggests domain specificity; high token accuracy with low span accuracy reveals contextual misunderstanding

**3 First Experiments:**
1. Evaluate rule-based methods on texts with varying formatting quality
2. Compare token-level vs span-level metrics across different model architectures
3. Analyze performance variation across chapters to identify domain-specific features

None

## Limitations

- Performance varies significantly across chapters (73% to 98% F1), indicating context-dependent robustness
- Models struggle with full span recognition despite strong token-level performance, limiting practical utility
- Dataset derived from single novel raises concerns about generalization to other narrative styles and genres

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Rule-based methods' limitations with incises and formatting variations; overall superiority of deep learning models over rule-based approaches
- **Medium confidence**: Token-level performance metrics; CamemBERT as best-performing model; chapter-wise performance variation
- **Low confidence**: Generalization claims to other narrative types; practical utility of current models for complete dialogue extraction; effectiveness of proposed domain knowledge integration

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-genre validation**: Evaluate models on French narratives from different genres (romance, detective, historical) and time periods to assess true generalization beyond 19th-century literary style

2. **Span recognition benchmarking**: Implement and evaluate models using span-level metrics (exact match, partial match) rather than token-level F1 to better assess practical utility for dialogue extraction tasks

3. **Ablation analysis on chapter variation**: Conduct systematic analysis of chapter-specific features (vocabulary, sentence structure, dialogue frequency) that correlate with performance variation to identify model weaknesses and improvement opportunities
