# Less is More: Mitigate Spurious Correlations for Open-Domain Dialogue Response Generation Models by Causal Discovery
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01962
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01962
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: First study on spurious correlations in open-domain dialogue using causal discovery; shows irrelevant/generic responses due to spurious correlations in conversation histories

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the issue of spurious correlations in open-domain dialogue response generation models, which lead to irrelevant or generic responses. The authors propose a causal discovery approach to identify and mitigate these spurious correlations. By using a conditional independence classifier and a constrained self-training method, the model identifies key utterances that directly cause the response. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in relevance, informativeness, and fluency over competitive baselines.

## Method Summary
The proposed method identifies spurious correlations in dialogue data by treating conversation histories as causal graphs. A conditional independence classifier is trained to detect key utterances that directly cause the response, distinguishing them from spurious context. Due to data scarcity, a constrained self-training approach is employed, where the model iteratively refines its predictions using rule-based extraction of positive and negative examples. This model-agnostic method can be integrated with existing dialogue generation models to improve response quality by focusing on causally relevant information.

## Key Results
- The method significantly outperforms competitive baselines in relevance, informativeness, and fluency.
- Human and automatic evaluations confirm the effectiveness of the approach.
- The constrained self-training method effectively mitigates data scarcity issues.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by modeling dialogue histories as causal graphs and identifying spurious correlations that lead to irrelevant or generic responses. By using a conditional independence classifier, the model can distinguish between causally relevant and spurious context, ensuring that responses are generated based on meaningful conversation history. The constrained self-training method allows the model to improve its predictions even with limited data, enhancing its ability to generalize to real-world dialogue scenarios.

## Foundational Learning
- **Causal Graphs**: Represent the relationships between variables in a dialogue; needed to identify spurious correlations.

- **Conditional Independence**: Used to determine if an utterance is a direct cause of the response; needed to filter out irrelevant context.
  - Quick check: Does the classifier accurately identify key utterances?
- **Self-Training**: Iteratively improves model predictions with limited data; needed to address data scarcity.
  - Quick check: Does the model converge and improve with each iteration?

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Conversation History -> Causal Graph -> Conditional Independence Classifier -> Key Utterances -> Response Generator
- **Critical Path**: The model first constructs a causal graph from the conversation history, then uses the conditional independence classifier to identify key utterances, which are fed into the response generator to produce relevant responses.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: The model-agnostic design allows integration with existing models but may not leverage end-to-end causal discovery methods. The reliance on simulated spurious correlations may not capture all real-world patterns.
- **Failure Signatures**: Overfitting to simulated spurious patterns, bias from rule-based extraction, and poor generalization to other languages or domains.

## Limitations
- Reliance on simulated spurious correlations may not capture all real-world patterns.
- Performance relative to end-to-end causal discovery methods or larger pre-trained models is not compared.
- Evaluation focuses on English open-domain dialogues; generalization to other languages or domains remains unclear.

## Confidence
- High: Empirical findings on the prevalence of spurious correlations and the effectiveness of the conditional independence classifier.
- Medium: Scalability of the constrained self-training method and robustness of causal assumptions.
- Low: Potential for overfitting to simulated patterns and performance in noisy, real-world settings.
