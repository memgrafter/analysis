# When to Read Documents or QA History: On Unified and Selective Open-domain QA

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04176
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04176
- Reference count: 8
- This paper proposes a selective QA model, UR-QA, that combines document corpus and QA-pair corpus using calibration to estimate answer confidence and select between sources.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of selective QA in open-domain settings by combining document corpora and QA-pair corpora through a unified reader architecture. The key innovation is using calibration to estimate answer confidence via two new measures: answerability (whether context can answer the question) and consistency (sampling uncertainty of the decoder). These confidence estimates guide selection between document and QA-pair sources, outperforming existing models on NQ and TQA datasets.

## Method Summary
The method involves retrieving top passages from both document and QA-pair corpora, processing them with a unified generative reader (T5-based), and estimating confidence through answerability and consistency scores. The model treats QA-history as hypothetical documents by concatenating retrieved QA pairs, then applies verbal prompting to convert confidence scores into discrete tokens for unified calibration. The final answer is selected from the source with highest confidence, combining document and QA-pair knowledge while avoiding calibration overfitting common in generative models.

## Key Results
- UR-QA outperforms existing models on NQ and TQA datasets
- Improved calibration helps select better answers by leveraging complementarity of document and QA-pair sources
- The proposed confidence measures (answerability and consistency) provide more reliable indicators than raw LM likelihood
- Selective use of knowledge sources based on calibrated confidence improves overall QA performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Improved calibration via answerability and consistency scores helps the model "know when it doesn't know" and selectively choose between document and QA-pair sources.
- Mechanism: The model learns two new confidence measures beyond LM likelihood: (1) Answerability predicts whether the context can answer the question, (2) Consistency measures the sampling uncertainty of the decoder. These are aggregated into a final confidence score used to select the final answer from either source.
- Core assumption: The proposed confidence measures are more reliable indicators of answer quality than raw LM likelihood, which suffers from calibration overfitting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our distinction is interpreting the problem as calibration, which estimates the confidence of predicted answers as an indicator to decide when to use a document or QA-pair corpus."
  - [section 3.3.1] "MLE loss in Eq. (2) enforces to train LM solely towards maximizing the likelihoods of observed sequences. Because the observed sequences (or labeled answers) can have diverse surface forms, MLE training inevitably leads to miscalibration."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on different aspects of QA but do not directly address the specific calibration approach proposed here.
- Break condition: If the learned answerability and consistency scores do not outperform raw LM likelihood in practice, the selective QA mechanism breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The unified reader architecture allows leveraging complementary strengths of documents and QA-pairs by treating QA-history as a hypothetical document with in-context examples.
- Mechanism: Top-k QA pairs are retrieved and concatenated into a single passage, which is then processed by the same generative reader used for documents. This allows the model to directly compare the quality of answers from both sources.
- Core assumption: Treating QA-history as a hypothetical document preserves the relevant information and allows the generative reader to effectively reason over both sources.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our distinction is interpreting the problem as calibration, which estimates the confidence of predicted answers as an indicator to decide when to use a document or QA-pair corpus."
  - [section 3.2] "As shown in Figure 1, we retrieve top-m QA pairs from QA corpus as in-context examples, and finetune a QA model with the in-context examples."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers explore different ways to combine knowledge sources but do not directly address the specific unified reader approach proposed here.
- Break condition: If the generative reader cannot effectively process the concatenated QA-history as a single document, the unified reader architecture breaks down.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Prompting the language model to verbally express the confidence scores allows for a unified calibration and answer selection process.
- Mechanism: The answerability and consistency scores are converted into discrete words (e.g., "True"/"False", "High"/"Medium"/"Low") and included in the output template. The language model is trained to generate this template, allowing the confidence scores to be estimated from the likelihoods of the relevant tokens.
- Core assumption: The language model can effectively learn to generate the verbalized confidence scores as part of the answer generation process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our distinction is exploring the use of calibration for selective QA, and overcoming the calibration overfitting we observed from existing methods, by proposing new likelihoods based on answerability and consistency."
  - [section 3.3.4] "To learn Sans and Scon via verbal estimator, we convert the scores into discrete words. Specifically, Sans is expressed as either True or False."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers explore different ways to prompt language models but do not directly address the specific calibration prompting approach proposed here.
- Break condition: If the language model cannot effectively learn to generate the verbalized confidence scores, the prompting-based calibration breaks down.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Retriever for documents (FiD-KD) -> Retriever for QA-pairs (RePAQ) -> Generative reader (T5-based) -> Answerability and consistency estimators -> Prompting-based calibration -> Answer selection logic

- Critical path: 1. Retrieve top-k documents and QA-pairs 2. Process both sources with the generative reader 3. Estimate answerability and consistency scores 4. Prompt the language model to generate verbalized confidence scores 5. Select the final answer based on the aggregated confidence scores

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a generative reader allows for more flexible answer generation but may be less precise than extractive methods
  - Treating QA-history as a hypothetical document simplifies the architecture but may lose some information compared to more complex fusion methods
  - Prompting the language model for calibration adds complexity but allows for a unified process

- Failure signatures:
  - Low answerability or consistency scores across all examples may indicate issues with the estimators
  - Large discrepancies between the confidence scores from different sources may indicate issues with the prompting or selection logic
  - Poor performance on out-of-domain examples may indicate issues with the generalization of the unified reader

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the performance of the individual answerability and consistency estimators on a held-out validation set
  2. Ablation study comparing the performance of the full model with and without the prompting-based calibration
  3. Cross-domain evaluation to assess the generalization of the unified reader to new question types or knowledge sources

## Limitations

- The ablation studies do not fully isolate the impact of the proposed calibration measures from other architectural choices, making it difficult to attribute performance gains to specific mechanisms.
- The analysis does not explore potential information loss from treating QA-history as a hypothetical document, which may oversimplify the fusion of knowledge sources.
- The paper does not investigate the model's performance with knowledge sources beyond documents and QA-pairs, limiting understanding of its generalizability.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The experimental results demonstrating improved performance on NQ and TQA datasets are well-supported by the presented metrics (EM, ECE, AUC).
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism by which answerability and consistency scores improve calibration is theoretically sound but relies on assumptions about the effectiveness of verbal prompting that could benefit from further empirical validation.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that QA-history can be effectively treated as a hypothetical document is supported by the results but the analysis does not explore potential information loss from this simplification.
