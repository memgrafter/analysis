# Fundamental Tradeoffs in Learning with Prior Information

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13479
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13479
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: The paper introduces the prioritized risk as a way to analyze fundamental tradeoffs between prior information and learning performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the prioritized risk as a novel framework for analyzing fundamental tradeoffs between prior information and learning performance. Unlike traditional approaches like minimax and Bayes risk, the prioritized risk captures settings where reality doesn't conform to the learner's prior. The authors develop reduction-based techniques to extend classical minimax lower bound methods to obtain lower bounds on prioritized risk for estimation problems, and introduce a generalized Fano inequality for handling unbounded losses in more general settings.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a reduction-based approach that extends classical minimax lower-bound techniques to obtain lower bounds on prioritized risk for estimation problems. This involves constructing (δ, π)-packings and using prior-weighted test functions to translate lower bounds on testing error to lower bounds on prioritized risk. For more general settings with unbounded losses, they introduce a novel generalized Fano inequality that uses the Donsker-Varadhan change of measure inequality to handle unbounded losses while obtaining bounds involving mutual information and best achievable average loss.

## Key Results
- The prioritized risk framework captures fundamental tradeoffs between prior accuracy and learning performance where reality doesn't conform to the learner's prior
- Reduction-based techniques successfully extend classical minimax methods to obtain lower bounds on prioritized risk for estimation problems
- The generalized Fano inequality handles unbounded losses and provides lower bounds for general learning problems beyond estimation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The prioritized risk captures fundamental tradeoffs between prior accuracy and learning performance in settings where reality doesn't conform to the learner's prior.
- Mechanism: By weighting the risk by the prior π(θ), the prioritized risk creates a fundamental limit - for any learner, there exists a θ where either the risk is high or the prior is low.
- Core assumption: The supremum in the prioritized risk definition can be achieved (sup=max, inf=min).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The paper introduces the prioritized risk as a way to analyze fundamental tradeoffs between prior information and learning performance."
  - [section] "Then, from (10), we see that there must exist a θ with low prior where the learner performs poorly"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of prioritized risk or fundamental tradeoffs in related papers
- Break condition: If the learner can achieve zero risk for all θ with π(θ) above some threshold, the fundamental tradeoff no longer holds.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The reduction from estimation to testing allows extending classical minimax lower bound techniques to obtain lower bounds on prioritized risk.
- Mechanism: By constructing a (δ, π)-packing and using a prior-weighted test function, we can translate lower bounds on testing error to lower bounds on prioritized risk.
- Core assumption: The (δ, π)-packing exists and the balls of radius δ/π(θv) are non-overlapping.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We present a general reduction-based approach for extending classical minimax lower-bound techniques"
  - [section] "Proposition 4.1 (Reduction from estimation to testing)"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of reduction techniques or (δ, π)-packings in related papers
- Break condition: If the KL divergence between distributions grows too slowly with n, the total variation distance bound becomes too weak.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The generalized Fano inequality handles unbounded losses and allows obtaining lower bounds on prioritized risk for general learning problems beyond estimation.
- Mechanism: By using the Donsker-Varadhan change of measure inequality, we can handle unbounded losses and obtain a bound involving mutual information and the best achievable (exponentiated) average loss.
- Core assumption: The loss function L can be unbounded but the mutual information term remains finite.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also introduce a novel generalization of Fano's inequality... for lower bounding the prioritized risk in more general settings involving unbounded losses."
  - [section] "This inequality handles problems with unbounded losses (in contrast to prior generalized Fano inequalities)"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of generalized Fano inequalities or unbounded losses in related papers
- Break condition: If the mutual information becomes too large relative to the best achievable average loss, the lower bound becomes trivial.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prior information π(θ) -> Learning algorithm σ -> Dataset -> Action/Hypothesis -> Loss -> Prioritized risk
- Critical path: Prior information → Learning algorithm → Dataset → Action/Hypothesis → Loss → Prioritized risk
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Choice of prior π(θ): More concentrated priors lead to stricter tradeoffs but may be more informative
  - Loss function L(θ, a): Different losses lead to different prioritized risk bounds and tradeoffs
  - Reduction-based vs. generalized Fano approaches: Reduction-based for estimation problems, generalized Fano for general problems
- Failure signatures:
  - Prioritized risk bounds that are too loose to be meaningful
  - Inability to construct appropriate (δ, π)-packings for reduction-based approach
  - Mutual information terms that dominate the generalized Fano bound

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can we derive a general framework to determine the optimal learning algorithm for a given prioritized risk?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that Bayesian inference with prior π is not necessarily optimal from the perspective of the prioritized risk, leaving open the direction of identifying algorithms that achieve optimal prioritized risk.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides lower bounds on prioritized risk and compares different learners, it does not provide a systematic method to find the optimal learner for a given problem.
- What evidence would resolve it: A general framework that takes a learning problem and a prior as input and outputs the optimal learning algorithm that minimizes the prioritized risk.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the prioritized risk framework apply to more complex learning problems beyond estimation, regression, and reinforcement learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper illustrates the framework on problems in estimation, regression, and reinforcement learning, but does not explore other domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on specific problem types, leaving open the question of how the framework generalizes to other learning problems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the prioritized risk framework to a variety of complex learning problems (e.g., natural language processing, computer vision, multi-agent systems) and demonstrating its effectiveness in capturing the tradeoff between prior information and learning performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between the prioritized risk and other generalization bounds, such as PAC-Bayes bounds, and can they be unified into a single framework?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that PAC-Bayes provides upper bounds on risk, while prioritized risk provides lower bounds, and raises the question of whether PAC-Bayes provides the optimal tradeoff for any classes of learning problems.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed comparison or unification of the prioritized risk with other generalization bounds.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis that shows the relationship between prioritized risk and other generalization bounds, and a unified framework that incorporates both upper and lower bounds on risk.

## Limitations
- The assumption that the supremum in the prioritized risk definition can be achieved may not hold in all settings, potentially weakening the claimed fundamental tradeoffs
- The reduction-based approach relies heavily on constructing appropriate (δ, π)-packings, but the paper provides limited guidance on how to construct these packings for arbitrary problems
- The generalized Fano inequality requires careful computation of mutual information, which can be challenging in practice
