# An Empirical Evaluation of the Rashomon Effect in Explainable Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15786
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15786
- Reference count: 40
- Authors examine the Rashomon Effect in explainable ML, finding that model and dataset dependencies significantly impact explanation stability and method selection.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides the first empirical evaluation of the Rashomon Effect in explainable machine learning, where multiple models achieving similar performance employ different solution strategies. The authors develop a unified framework to examine three comparison scenarios: numerical stability of explanations, solution diversity across models, and disagreement between attribution methods. Through extensive quantitative experiments across datasets, models, and attribution methods, they demonstrate that explanation variability is substantial and context-dependent. Their findings reveal that hyperparameter choices significantly impact explanation quality in a model-dataset specific manner, solution diversity limits the use of ML as an epistemic tool, and no attribution method consistently outperforms others across different scenarios.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct comprehensive experiments using random forests and neural networks trained on multiple datasets. They evaluate three attribution methods (LIME, SHAP, DeepLIFT) across various hyperparameter configurations. For solution diversity analysis, they train 50 models per dataset achieving similar performance levels and compare their explanations. The study employs quantitative metrics including average KL divergence, stability scores, and disagreement measures to assess explanation variability. Experiments systematically vary model architectures, hyperparameter settings, and attribution method parameters to understand how these factors influence explanation consistency and diversity.

## Key Results
- Hyperparameter choice for explanations shows strong dependence on both model type and dataset characteristics, with no universal optimal settings
- High solution diversity across models achieving similar performance severely limits ML's utility as an epistemic tool for understanding decision-making
- No consistent ranking of attribution methods across datasets exists, though certain method pairs (LIME-SHAP, DeepLIFT-SHAP) consistently occupy mid-range disagreement positions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Rashomon Effect manifests in explainable ML through three interconnected mechanisms. First, numerical instability occurs when small changes in hyperparameters or training data produce significantly different explanations, revealing sensitivity in the attribution process itself. Second, solution diversity emerges because multiple distinct model architectures can achieve comparable predictive performance while employing fundamentally different decision strategies. Third, attribution method disagreement arises from the different theoretical foundations and computational approaches underlying each explanation technique, leading to divergent interpretations of the same model behavior.

## Foundational Learning

**Rashomon Effect in ML**: Multiple models can achieve similar performance while using different decision strategies - essential for understanding explanation variability and model uncertainty.
*Why needed*: Provides theoretical framework for why explanations differ across models with similar accuracy.
*Quick check*: Compare explanations from two models with similar accuracy but different architectures on the same input.

**Attribution Method Stability**: The sensitivity of explanation methods to hyperparameter changes and numerical precision.
*Why needed*: Quantifies reliability of explanations under realistic implementation conditions.
*Quick check*: Measure explanation variance when varying LIME kernel width or SHAP background samples.

**Solution Space Analysis**: Examining the diversity of decision boundaries that achieve similar performance metrics.
*Why needed*: Reveals the extent of explanation variability inherent in the model class.
*Quick check*: Train multiple models with similar accuracy and compute pairwise explanation dissimilarity.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Preprocessing -> Model Training -> Attribution Method Application -> Explanation Comparison -> Quantitative Analysis
**Critical Path**: Model training with consistent performance targets -> Attribution method execution -> Explanation stability measurement -> Diversity assessment across models
**Design Tradeoffs**: Model diversity vs. performance similarity (more diverse models show greater explanation differences but may have performance variance) and attribution method choice vs. computational cost (some methods are more expensive but potentially more stable)
**Failure Signatures**: High explanation variance with stable performance indicates Rashomon Effect presence; consistent explanation ranking across datasets suggests method robustness; low diversity scores indicate limited exploration of solution space
**First Experiments**: 1) Compare LIME explanations across 10 random forest models with similar accuracy on a binary classification task, 2) Measure SHAP stability when varying background sample size from 10 to 1000, 3) Calculate pairwise KL divergence between DeepLIFT explanations from neural networks with identical architectures but different random seeds

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop effective search strategies to explore the Rashomon Set in complex or resource-constrained scenarios where training a large number of models is infeasible?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the variance in results illustrates that the number of viable solution strategies is extensively large, making it highly improbable to discover all possibilities when training a large number of models is infeasible.
- Why unresolved: While the paper acknowledges the challenge of exploring the Rashomon Set in resource-constrained scenarios, it does not provide specific solutions or strategies for effective exploration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating successful search strategies for exploring the Rashomon Set in complex scenarios with limited resources, such as techniques that leverage prior knowledge or incorporate efficient sampling methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the implications of the Rashomon Effect on the use of explanations in user-centered scenarios where users interact with model explanations and expect consistent behavior over time?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the deployment of a new model, even if performance is similar, would pose a risk to user trust due to the likely change in explanations.
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights the potential risk to user trust but does not provide specific recommendations or solutions for maintaining consistent explanations when deploying new models.
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies or experiments that assess the impact of explanation changes on user trust and provide insights into effective strategies for maintaining consistency in user-centered scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we determine the most suitable explanation method or combination of methods for a specific task or dataset, considering the variability in explanations across different methods?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that there is no consistent ranking of attribution methods across datasets and that practitioners seeking diverse explanations would benefit from comparing specific method pairs.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides insights into the variability of explanations across methods, it does not offer a systematic approach for selecting the most suitable explanation method or combination of methods for a given task or dataset.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies that evaluate the performance and suitability of different explanation methods across various tasks and datasets, along with guidelines or frameworks for selecting the most appropriate method based on specific requirements or characteristics of the task.

## Limitations

- Study focuses on specific model architectures (random forests and neural networks) which may limit generalizability to other model types
- Limited dataset scope may not capture the full diversity of real-world applications and data distributions
- Attribution methods examined (LIME, SHAP, DeepLIFT) represent only a subset of available explanation techniques

## Confidence

High: Claims about numerical stability issues and their dependence on hyperparameter choices
Medium: Observations regarding solution diversity across models and its impact on epistemic utility
Low: Assertions about consistent patterns in attribution method disagreement due to complex interactions between methods and model architectures
