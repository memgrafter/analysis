# Text-Conditional Contextualized Avatars For Zero-Shot Personalization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07410
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07410
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents a pipeline for zero-shot personalization of text-to-image generation using avatars

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a pipeline for generating images of user avatars in text-described scenes without requiring model training on the specific avatar. The approach separates pose generation from avatar rendering, using a text-to-3D pose diffusion model trained on a large-scale dataset of pseudo-poses extracted from in-the-wild images. The generated poses are retargeted to any user's avatar and rendered, then used to condition a latent diffusion model that generates contextualized scenes. Human evaluations demonstrate the method produces more faithful avatar images compared to baselines while being scalable and style-agnostic.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of a three-step pipeline: (1) text-to-3D pose generation using a Transformer-based diffusion model trained on a large-scale ITPP dataset, (2) avatar rendering by re-targeting generated SMPL poses to user avatars and rendering RGBA images, and (3) personalized image generation via fine-tuning a latent diffusion model conditioned on the rendered avatar and text prompt. The pose generation model uses classifier-free guidance and operates on text encodings with diffusion timestep embeddings. The image generation model is fine-tuned with additional conditioning on gray body renders and downsample factors to improve body orientation understanding while maintaining avatar texture invariance.

## Key Results
- The proposed method generates more faithful avatar images compared to baseline approaches in human evaluations
- The text-to-3D pose diffusion model trained on the ITPP dataset shows significant improvement over existing text-to-motion models
- The pipeline is scalable to millions of users and avatar style agnostic without requiring training on individual avatars

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The method achieves zero-shot personalization by separating pose generation from avatar rendering.
- Mechanism: A text-to-3D pose diffusion model generates SMPL parameters from text, which are then retargeted to any user's avatar and rendered independently. This decoupling allows the same pose generator to work with any avatar style without retraining.
- Core assumption: SMPL parameters are sufficiently expressive to capture the intended human pose from text while being agnostic to avatar appearance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our pipeline is zero-shot, avatar texture and style agnostic, and does not require training on the avatar at all"
  - [section 3.1] "we use a pre-trained VPoser [...] to efficiently model the distribution of pose priors"
- Break condition: If SMPL cannot represent complex poses (e.g., hand gestures) or if retargeting introduces significant pose distortions, the avatar will not match the intended text description.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Large-scale image-text pseudo-pose dataset enables better generalization than motion capture datasets.
- Mechanism: By extracting pseudo-poses from in-the-wild images using a pose detector and body regressor, the model sees diverse poses and contexts not present in controlled mocap datasets.
- Core assumption: Pseudo-poses extracted from images are sufficiently accurate to train a pose generation model that generalizes to unseen poses.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "We processed all images by running Detectron2 keypoint detector [...] extracted their 3D pseudo-pose SMPL annotations"
- Break condition: If pseudo-pose extraction introduces significant errors, the model will learn incorrect pose distributions leading to unrealistic or incorrect poses.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Conditioning the image generation model on both the rendered avatar and a gray body render improves avatar faithfulness.
- Mechanism: The UNet in the latent diffusion model receives multiple conditioning signals: the RGBA avatar image, a downsampled version for texture invariance, and a gray body render for structural guidance. This multi-signal conditioning helps the model understand avatar structure while being less sensitive to appearance details.
- Core assumption: Gray body renders provide sufficient structural information to guide the image generation without being overly specific to any particular avatar style.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.5.4] "additionally condition the UNet on the gray body render of the 3D pose" and "augment the personalization conditioning by downsampling the spatial dimensions"
- Break condition: If the gray body render does not capture sufficient pose detail or if the downsampling removes too much information, the model may hallucinate incorrect body parts or fail to preserve the avatar's identity.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: 3D human pose representation using SMPL
  - Why needed here: SMPL provides a differentiable, parametric representation of human body shape and pose that can be generated from text and rendered with any avatar
  - Quick check question: What are the 3D joint rotations in SMPL parameterized by, and how many parameters define the full body pose?

- Concept: Diffusion models for conditional generation
  - Why needed here: Diffusion models can generate diverse, high-quality samples conditioned on text and other signals, which is essential for generating varied poses and contextualized avatar images
  - Quick check question: How does classifier-free guidance improve sample quality during inference in diffusion models?

- Concept: Cross-attention in transformers for text conditioning
  - Why needed here: The text-to-3D pose model uses a transformer decoder with cross-attention to text embeddings, allowing it to generate poses that match the semantic content of the text description
  - Quick check question: What is the role of positional embeddings in the transformer architecture for sequence modeling?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text → Text-to-3D pose model → Retargeting → Avatar rendering → Image generation → Final output
- Critical path: Text prompt flows through pose generation, then avatar rendering, and finally image generation with conditioning signals
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using SMPL vs. other pose representations: SMPL is standard but may not capture all nuances; alternatives like 6D rotations were tested but required regularization
  - Large-scale pseudo-pose dataset vs. motion capture: Pseudo-pose dataset provides diversity but may have noise; motion capture provides accuracy but limited variety
  - Multi-signal conditioning vs. single conditioning: Multiple signals improve faithfulness but increase complexity and potential for misalignment
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor pose generation: Avatar appears in unrealistic or incorrect poses relative to text
  - Identity loss: Generated images do not preserve the user's avatar appearance
  - Scene misalignment: Avatar is not properly integrated into the generated scene
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate poses from a diverse set of text prompts and visualize them as stick figures to check if the poses match the text descriptions
  2. Render a fixed avatar with generated poses and perform a human evaluation comparing against baseline pose generation methods
  3. Generate images with a simple outpainting baseline and with the full system, then perform a human evaluation on avatar faithfulness and scene matching

## Limitations

- The pipeline relies on an internal avatar rendering engine that is not open-sourced, creating a significant dependency that may limit reproducibility
- Pseudo-pose accuracy from the ITPP dataset is not quantitatively validated, and errors in pseudo-pose extraction could propagate through the entire pipeline
- Human evaluation methodology lacks transparency regarding evaluator demographics, number of evaluators, and specific evaluation interface

## Confidence

- High confidence: The zero-shot personalization mechanism and overall pipeline architecture are well-established concepts with clear implementation details
- Medium confidence: Improvements from using the ITPP dataset over motion capture data are supported by results, but pseudo-pose extraction accuracy remains uncertain
- Low confidence: Specific improvements from multi-signal conditioning (gray body render and downsampling) are not fully explained with ablation studies
