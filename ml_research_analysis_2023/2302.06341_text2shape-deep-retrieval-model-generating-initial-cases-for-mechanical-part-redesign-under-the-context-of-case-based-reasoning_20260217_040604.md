# Text2shape Deep Retrieval Model: Generating Initial Cases for Mechanical Part Redesign under the Context of Case-Based Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06341
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06341
- Reference count: 40
- Achieves 0.98 accuracy in 3D shape retrieval from text descriptions

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of manually retrieving design cases from large databases in mechanical part redesign by developing an automated text2shape deep retrieval model. The approach combines recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to retrieve 3D shapes from text descriptions of structural features. The model is trained on a 1000-sample dataset linking text descriptions to 3D voxel models of linking rods, using triplet loss for similarity calculation and achieving high accuracy in initial case retrieval for mechanical part redesign.

## Method Summary
The methodology involves feature engineering to identify key structural features of linking rods, construction of a 1000-sample training dataset linking text descriptions to 3D voxel models, and training an integrated RNN-CNN model using triplet loss for similarity calculation. Orthogonal experiments are employed for hyperparameter tuning. The approach aims to overcome the inefficiency of manual retrieval methods by automating the process of finding relevant 3D shapes from text descriptions, thereby facilitating mechanical part redesign within a case-based reasoning framework.

## Key Results
- Achieves highest accuracy of 0.98 in retrieving 3D shapes from text descriptions
- Successfully demonstrates the effectiveness of the RNN-CNN architecture for text-to-shape retrieval
- Shows promising results for initial case retrieval in mechanical part redesign

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The integration of RNN and CNN architectures allows for effective processing of both sequential text data and spatial 3D shape information. The triplet loss function enables the model to learn meaningful similarity metrics between text descriptions and 3D shapes by comparing anchor, positive, and negative samples during training. This dual-modality approach captures both semantic relationships in text and geometric relationships in 3D shapes, enabling accurate retrieval of relevant design cases.
