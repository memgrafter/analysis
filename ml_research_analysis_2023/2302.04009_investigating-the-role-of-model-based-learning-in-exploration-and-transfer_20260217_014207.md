# Investigating the role of model-based learning in exploration and transfer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04009
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04009
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Model-based agents with intrinsic exploration and fine-tuning outperform model-free baselines in transfer learning across multiple domains

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates when and why model-based learning helps with transfer in reinforcement learning. The authors propose a framework of unsupervised pre-training followed by fine-tuning on downstream tasks, using both model-based (MuZero) and model-free agents trained with intrinsic exploration (RND). They evaluate transfer across three domains: in-distribution procedural (Crafter), in-distribution identical (RoboDesk), and out-of-distribution (Meta-World). The primary findings are: (1) model-based exploration and fine-tuning outperform model-free baselines, (2) transferring the dynamics model and prior policy contribute most to successful transfer, and (3) transfer performance degrades when environment dynamics change between pre-training and fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The authors propose unsupervised pre-training with intrinsic exploration (RND) followed by fine-tuning on downstream tasks. They use a model-based agent (MuZero + SPR) and model-free baseline (Q-learning) with identical network architectures. The framework tests four variations: MB→MB, MB→MF, MF→MB, and MF→MF, plus a Scratch baseline. Ablations analyze contributions of observation encoder, prior heads, dynamics model, and dynamics heads. Evaluation occurs across three domains (Crafter, RoboDesk, Meta-World) using success rates and scores.

## Key Results
- Model-based pre-training and fine-tuning outperforms all model-free baselines
- Dynamics model and prior policy are the most important components for successful transfer
- Transfer performance degrades significantly when pre-training and fine-tuning environments have different dynamics
- On Crafter, MB→MB achieves 16.4±1.5 score and 12.7±0.4 reward after 1M steps

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Model-based agents learn better representations of the environment dynamics during unsupervised exploration than model-free agents.
- **Mechanism:** The MuZero-style dynamics model, trained via self-predictive representation (SPR) loss, captures structured information about environment transitions. This allows agents to anticipate consequences of actions without reward signals, leading to more efficient exploration via RND intrinsic rewards.
- **Core assumption:** Environments have learnable, structured dynamics that can be modeled with neural networks.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "model-based methods perform better exploration than their model-free counterparts in reward-free environments"; [section] "model-based optimization leads to significantly more effective exploration"
- **Break condition:** If environment dynamics are chaotic or non-stationary, the dynamics model may fail to learn useful representations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dynamics model and policy learned during pre-training encode task-agnostic knowledge that transfers well to new reward functions.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-training without task rewards allows agents to learn general skills and world knowledge. The dynamics model captures "how" the environment works, while the policy captures useful behavioral priors. This knowledge provides a better starting point than random initialization when fine-tuned on new tasks.
- **Core assumption:** Pre-training and fine-tuning environments share similar underlying dynamics, even if reward functions differ.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "knowledge is transferred most effectively when performing model-based pre-training and fine-tuning"; [section] "Both the dynamics components and the prior heads play an important role in transfer performance"
- **Break condition:** If pre-training and fine-tuning environments have significantly different dynamics, transferred knowledge may be less useful or harmful.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Model-based fine-tuning is more effective than model-free fine-tuning because it can leverage the transferred dynamics model for planning.
- **Mechanism:** During fine-tuning, the model-based agent uses its dynamics model for Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) planning. This allows planning over future states even with limited task-specific data, whereas model-free agents rely solely on learned value estimates which may be less sample-efficient initially.
- **Core assumption:** The dynamics model learned during pre-training is sufficiently accurate for planning in the fine-tuning environment.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "model-based fine-tuning also outperforms model-free fine-tuning"; [section] "The performance of the MB→MF agent suggests that the fine-tuning algorithm plays an important role"
- **Break condition:** If the dynamics model is inaccurate or planning horizon is too short, model-based fine-tuning may not provide advantages.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Reinforcement Learning with intrinsic motivation**
  - Why needed here: The paper relies on intrinsic exploration (RND) to pre-train agents without task rewards, requiring understanding of how agents can learn from curiosity-driven signals.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between intrinsic rewards and task rewards, and why is this distinction important for transfer learning?

- **Concept: Model-based vs Model-free RL**
  - Why needed here: The paper directly compares these two paradigms in terms of exploration and transfer performance, requiring understanding of their fundamental differences.
  - Quick check question: How does a model-based agent use its learned dynamics model differently from how a model-free agent uses its value function during action selection?

- **Concept: Transfer learning in RL**
  - Why needed here: The entire paper is framed around transferring knowledge from pre-training to fine-tuning, requiring understanding of what components can be transferred and why.
  - Quick check question: What are the potential benefits and risks of transferring learned policies versus learned dynamics models between tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Observation Encoder -> Prior Heads (Policy, Value, Reward) -> Dynamics Model (State, Reward) -> Dynamics Heads (Policy, Value, Reward) -> SPR Loss -> RND Intrinsic Reward
- **Critical path:** Pre-training → RND-driven exploration → Dynamics model learning → Policy learning → Fine-tuning on task rewards → Planning with transferred components
- **Design tradeoffs:** Model-based offers better sample efficiency and planning capabilities but at higher computational cost; dynamics model complexity vs transfer effectiveness; intrinsic reward design simplicity vs optimality
- **Failure signatures:** Poor exploration during pre-training indicates ineffective intrinsic reward signal; no improvement from transfer suggests pre-training/fine-tuning environment mismatch; model-based fine-tuning performing worse indicates inaccurate dynamics model
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run pre-training on Crafter with both MB and MF agents, compare exploration efficiency via RND reward accumulation
  2. Perform ablation study on MB→MB agent: transfer only observation encoder, then add prior heads, then dynamics model, measuring transfer performance
  3. Test transfer to out-of-distribution environment (Meta-World) to identify when model-based transfer breaks down

## Limitations
- Weak evidence for specific mechanism claims despite strong overall findings
- Limited comparison to other transfer learning methods beyond model-free baselines
- Does not investigate how amount of unsupervised pre-training data affects transfer effectiveness
- Does not explore specific factors limiting out-of-distribution transfer or potential solutions

## Confidence

**High:** Model-based pre-training and fine-tuning outperforms model-free baselines; dynamics model and prior policy contribute most to transfer; transfer degrades with out-of-distribution dynamics

**Medium:** Model-based exploration is more efficient than model-free; planning advantages explain fine-tuning benefits

**Low:** Specific claims about how dynamics model representations capture "task-agnostic knowledge" or why model-free exploration is less efficient

## Next Checks

1. **Exploration efficiency validation:** Design controlled experiments comparing RND reward accumulation rates between model-based and model-free agents during pre-training to directly measure exploration efficiency differences

2. **Planning advantage test:** Create experiments where the dynamics model is frozen but planning is disabled during fine-tuning, comparing against full model-based fine-tuning to isolate planning benefits

3. **Dynamics similarity analysis:** Quantitatively measure environmental similarity between pre-training and fine-tuning domains using dynamics model prediction accuracy to better understand when transfer degrades
