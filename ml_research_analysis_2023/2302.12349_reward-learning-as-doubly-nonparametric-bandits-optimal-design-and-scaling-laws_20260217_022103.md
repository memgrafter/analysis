# Reward Learning as Doubly Nonparametric Bandits: Optimal Design and Scaling Laws

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12349
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12349
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Develops framework for reward learning via noisy oracle queries, achieving improved regret bounds over GP-UCB/GP-TS for kernel MABs

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a general theoretical framework for reward learning as a doubly nonparametric bandit problem, where both policies and rewards are modeled as functions in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHSs). The paper derives non-asymptotic excess risk bounds for a ridge regression-based plug-in estimator and uses these bounds to design optimal query strategies. The main theoretical contribution shows that learning is possible when the policy class grows slower than the reward class, with the excess risk primarily controlled by the eigenvalue spectrum of a certain linear operator. The framework recovers and improves upon existing results for kernel multi-armed bandits, achieving tighter regret bounds than standard algorithms like GP-UCB and GP-TS for various kernel classes.

## Method Summary
The method models policies and rewards as nonparametric functions in RKHSs, with learning achieved through queries to a noisy oracle. A ridge regression estimator is used to learn the reward function from queried policies, and excess risk bounds are derived based on the covariance matrix of queried policies. The optimal query selection strategy maximizes spectral alignment between the policy space and the map M connecting policies to rewards. The approach combines theoretical analysis of the RKHS spectral properties with practical query design algorithms to achieve improved learning rates compared to existing methods.

## Key Results
- Excess risk depends primarily on eigenvalue spectrum of operator S^(1/2)_π M^T S^(1/2)_r
- Learning possible when policy class grows slower than reward class (steeper eigenvalue decay)
- Proposed algorithm achieves better regret bounds than GP-UCB/GP-TS for Matérn and RBF kernels
- Dimension-free learning demonstrated with theoretical predictions matching experimental results

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The excess risk of the plug-in estimator depends primarily on the spectral properties of the operator S^(1/2)_π M^T S^(1/2)_r.
- Mechanism: The ridge regression estimator minimizes the squared distance between the estimated reward and the true reward in the RKHS. The excess risk bound shows that this distance is controlled by the eigenvalues of the operator, which capture how well the policy and reward spaces align.
- Core assumption: The map M is a bounded linear operator between the policy and reward spaces.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The main result shows that the excess risk depends primarily on the eigenvalue spectrum of a certain linear operator on the RKHSs"
  - [section] "The excess risk will ultimately depend on the the eigen-spectrum of the operator S^(-1/2)_π M^T SrM S^(-1/2)_π"
- Break condition: If the map M is not bounded or the spaces are not well-aligned, the spectral properties may not control the excess risk.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Learning is possible as long as the policy class grows slower than the reward class.
- Mechanism: The power law decay assumption on the eigenvalues implies that the effective dimensionality of the spaces is finite. If the policy class grows slower (steeper decay), then the operator S^(-1/2)_π M^T SrM S^(-1/2)_π has a faster decaying spectrum, making it easier to learn a good policy.
- Core assumption: The eigenvalues of the operator satisfy a power law decay.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "learning possible as long as the policy class grows slower than the reward class"
  - [section] "Qualitatively, our main results show that the excess risk asymptotically vanishes as long as the policy class grows at a slower rate relative to the reward class"
- Break condition: If the policy class grows as fast or faster than the reward class, the excess risk may not vanish.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The proposed algorithm outperforms existing adaptive sampling algorithms like GP-UCB for kernel multi-armed bandits.
- Mechanism: The passive sampling algorithm queries the top eigenvectors of the operator S^(1/2)_π M^T S^(1/2)_r, which are the most informative directions for learning. This is more efficient than the adaptive UCB approach which requires confidence intervals.
- Core assumption: The policy set contains policies that align well with the top eigenvectors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our bounds either improve or are competitive with known regret guarantees for the Matérn kernel"
  - [section] "Even though our proposed method is passive, it achieves better rates than well-known adaptive sampling algorithms"
- Break condition: If the policy set does not contain aligned policies, the performance may degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: RKHS spaces for policies and rewards -> Linear map M between spaces -> Oracle for noisy reward queries -> Ridge regression estimator -> Query selection algorithm -> Excess risk bound analysis

- Critical path:
  1. Select query policies based on the spectral properties of M
  2. Obtain noisy oracle responses
  3. Estimate reward using ridge regression
  4. Output policy that maximizes the estimated reward

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Passive vs. adaptive query selection
  - Dimensionality of policy and reward spaces
  - Choice of kernel functions
  - Regularization parameter in ridge regression

- Failure signatures:
  - Excess risk not decreasing with more queries
  - Poor alignment between policy and reward spaces
  - Overfitting due to high dimensionality or low regularization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary the number of queries and observe the excess risk decay
  2. Change the dimensionality of the policy and reward spaces and measure the impact on learning rates
  3. Compare the performance of the proposed algorithm with GP-UCB on a kernel MAB problem

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed framework perform when the regularity assumption on the policy space Cπ is relaxed or removed?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Section 7 states "Our current analysis relies on a regularity assumption on the policy space Cπ; can we obtain bounds on the excess risk in the absence of this assumption?"
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides analysis under the regularity assumption and does not explore what happens when this assumption is violated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing performance with various relaxed versions of the assumption, or theoretical analysis deriving bounds without the assumption.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the closed-loop dynamics between reward and policy learning when the learner actively queries for feedback instead of using passive queries?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Section 7 mentions "Going forward, it would be interesting to study the closed loop dynamics between the reward and the policy learning algorithm when the learner actively queries for feedback."
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on passive query models and does not analyze the active learning setting where queries are selected sequentially based on previous responses.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of regret bounds or excess risk bounds for active learning algorithms in the doubly nonparametric setting, or empirical comparison of passive vs active query strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are the theoretical upper bounds on excess risk tight in a minimax sense across different decay parameters β?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Section 6 states "This plot is suggestive of the fact that our theoretical upper bounds might be tight in a minimax way over choices of decay parameter β."
- Why unresolved: While experiments show the observed risk rates match the predicted rates, there is no formal lower bound analysis proving minimax optimality.
- What evidence would resolve it: A matching lower bound proof showing that no algorithm can achieve better rates than the proposed algorithm for any β, or experimental evidence showing other algorithms consistently performing worse across a range of β values.

## Limitations
- The theoretical framework relies heavily on RKHS assumptions that may not hold in practice
- Experiments are limited to synthetic settings with specific kernel choices (RBF, Matérn)
- The passive query selection strategy may underperform adaptive methods in practice

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The connection between excess risk and the spectral properties of the operator S^(1/2)_π M^T S^(1/2)_r is well-established through the ridge regression analysis. The power law decay assumptions and their implications for learning rates are mathematically rigorous.

**Medium Confidence**: The superiority of the proposed algorithm over GP-UCB and GP-TS is demonstrated only for specific kernel classes (RBF, Matérn). The conjectured minimax optimality of the bounds requires further validation across diverse problem instances.

**Low Confidence**: The practical performance of the passive query selection strategy in non-stationary or high-noise environments is uncertain. The framework's applicability to complex reward structures beyond the assumed RKHS framework is questionable.
