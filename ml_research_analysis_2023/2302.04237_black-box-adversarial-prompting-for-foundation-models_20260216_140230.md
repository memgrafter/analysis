# Black Box Adversarial Prompting for Foundation Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.04237
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04237
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Key outcome**: Black-box framework for generating adversarial prompts for unstructured image and text generation using Token Space Projection (TSP) to bridge continuous embeddings and discrete tokens

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a black-box framework for generating adversarial prompts for unstructured image and text generation models. The core innovation is the Token Space Projection (TSP) operator, which bridges the continuous word embedding space with the discrete token space, enabling the use of black-box attacks to find adversarial prompts. The framework successfully finds standalone or prepended prompts that cause text-to-image models to output specific image classes and text generation models to alter unstructured text generation, such as encouraging positive sentiments or increasing the frequency of specific letters.

## Method Summary
The method introduces Token Space Projection (TSP) to enable black-box optimization over discrete token spaces by mapping continuous embeddings to the nearest token in the restricted token set. The framework treats prompt generation as a black-box optimization problem, using Square Attack and TuRBO algorithms to iteratively sample and update embeddings in continuous space. Prepended adversarial tokens can modify model behavior by creating compositions where the adversarial prompt dominates the model's interpretation of benign prompts. The approach is evaluated on Stable Diffusion for image generation and OPT-125M for text generation, measuring success through Most Probable Class Success (MPC) and Out-Performing Simple Baseline Prompts (OPB) metrics.

## Key Results
- Successfully generates adversarial prompts that change text-to-image model outputs from mountains to dogs using prepended tokens
- Achieves high MPC success rates on unrestricted image generation tasks with 5,000 function evaluations
- Demonstrates text generation manipulation including sentiment reversal and character frequency maximization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: TSP enables black-box optimization by mapping continuous embeddings to discrete tokens
- **Mechanism**: Projects continuous embeddings to nearest token, allowing gradient-free optimization to navigate embedding space while producing valid tokens
- **Core assumption**: Embedding space is smooth enough for black-box optimization
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions bridging continuous and discrete spaces; section 2.1 details TSP implementation; corpus papers discuss prompt attacks without validating TSP specifically
- **Break condition**: If embedding space is too discontinuous or projection introduces prohibitive discontinuities

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Black-box optimization effectively finds adversarial prompts without gradient information
- **Mechanism**: Square Attack and TuRBO iteratively sample and update embeddings, with TSP ensuring valid tokens
- **Core assumption**: Black-box optimization can navigate high-dimensional embedding space despite projection step
- **Evidence anchors**: Section 2.2 treats problem as black-box optimization; section 4 shows quantitative success rates; corpus papers lack direct evidence on black-box optimization effectiveness
- **Break condition**: If optimization landscape is too noisy or projection causes getting stuck in poor local minima

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Prepending adversarial prompts can overcome existing benign prompts
- **Mechanism**: Prepended tokens modify model's interpretation of benign prompt, overriding original intent
- **Core assumption**: Model processes prepended tokens first and their influence dominates
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions standalone or prepended prompts; section 4.1.4 demonstrates success with examples; corpus papers mention prompt injection without validating prepending specifically
- **Break condition**: If model has strong prompt ordering invariance or ignores prepended tokens

  - Why needed here: Paper extends adversarial attacks from classification to unstructured generation

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Black-box foundation model (Stable Diffusion, OPT-125M) -> Token Space Projection (TSP) operator -> Black-box optimizer (TuRBO or Square Attack) -> Loss functions (classifier loss, feature loss) -> Evaluation metrics (MPC success, OPB success)

- **Critical path**: 1) Initialize embeddings in continuous space 2) Project to tokens via TSP 3) Query black-box model with projected tokens 4) Compute loss from generated output 5) Update embeddings via optimizer 6) Repeat until convergence or max queries

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - TSP vs. direct token optimization: TSP enables continuous optimization but adds projection noise
  - Query budget vs. optimization quality: More queries improve results but increase computational cost
  - Restricted vs. unrestricted token sets: Restrictions increase security but may reduce attack effectiveness

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Optimization plateaus early (suggests poor landscape or too restrictive token set)
  - Generated tokens don't form coherent prompts (TSP projection issues)
  - Loss doesn't decrease despite many queries (optimizer convergence problems)

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run TuRBO with TSP on simple text-to-image class target (e.g., "dog") with unrestricted tokens, measure success rate
  2. Test TSP projection quality by checking if projected tokens are semantically similar to original embeddings
  3. Compare Square Attack vs. TuRBO on same task to validate optimization method choice

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does performance scale with token embedding space size (different tokenization schemes or vocabulary sizes)?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention of using m=768 dimension and optimizing over d=4 tokens, noting this dimensionality is "well out of reach for traditional Bayesian optimization"
- Why unresolved: Paper only tests with one specific tokenizer (CLIP ViT-L/14 with 49,408 tokens for image, GPT-2 byte-level BPE with 50,265 tokens for text)
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying tokenizer type, vocabulary size, and embedding dimension with quantitative success rate comparisons

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between function evaluations and success rate across different model architectures and tasks?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention that "sample efficiency is critical" and provides results for specific evaluation budgets (5,000 unrestricted image, 10,000 restricted prepending, 3,000 text generation)
- Why unresolved: Paper sets arbitrary limits based on computational constraints without systematically exploring success rate scaling with evaluation budget
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies showing success rate curves as function of evaluations for various model architectures and task types

### Open Question 3
- Question: How transferable are adversarial prompts across different model architectures and training checkpoints?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from paper demonstrating successful prompts for specific models without testing cross-model effectiveness
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on single-model effectiveness without exploring broader applicability of discovered adversarial prompts
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of adversarial prompts optimized for one model checkpoint on different versions, architectures, and providers

## Limitations

- TSP projection quality and semantic coherence preservation is not empirically validated
- Limited exploration of restricted vs unrestricted token sets and their impact on attack success
- No ablation studies comparing TSP to alternative bridging mechanisms like Gumbel-softmax relaxation
- Reliance on synthetic evaluation metrics without validating real-world adversarial impact correlation
- Limited cross-model validation and analysis of transferability between different foundation models

## Confidence

**High Confidence (90%+):**
- Core architecture (TSP + black-box optimization) is technically sound and implementable
- Basic quantitative results showing attack success rates across different models and tasks
- Problem formulation (black-box adversarial prompting) is well-defined and novel

**Medium Confidence (60-90%):**
- Effectiveness of TSP as bridging mechanism - theoretically plausible but depends on embedding space characteristics
- Generalization of results across different foundation models and tasks - limited cross-model validation
- Security implications of prepended prompts - demonstrated but not thoroughly explored for defense implications

**Low Confidence (below 60%):**
- Long-term effectiveness against models with adversarial training or input sanitization
- Performance with larger prompt lengths beyond tested 4 tokens
- Robustness to model updates or architectural changes in foundation models
