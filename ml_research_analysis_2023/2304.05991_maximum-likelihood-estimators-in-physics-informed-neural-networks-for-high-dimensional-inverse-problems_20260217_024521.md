# Maximum-likelihood Estimators in Physics-Informed Neural Networks for High-dimensional Inverse Problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05991
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05991
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PINNs formulated as MLE with SVD preconditioning provide a hyperparameter-free framework for inverse problems in complex reaction systems.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to inverse problems in high-dimensional ODE systems, particularly for chemical kinetics, by formulating physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) as maximum-likelihood estimators (MLE). The key innovation is the use of singular-value decomposition (SVD) of the ODE coupling matrix to generate reduced, uncorrelated subspaces for representing PINN solutions and projecting residuals. This approach eliminates the need for hyperparameter tuning, enables rigorous error propagation from data interpolation to physics model residuals via Taylor expansion, and mitigates ill-conditioning in covariance matrix inversion. The resulting "kinetics-informed neural networks" (KINNs) with MLE and SVD-based preconditioning provide a robust framework for inverse problems in complex reaction systems.

## Method Summary
The method reformulates inverse PINNs as maximum-likelihood estimators, assuming normally distributed residuals for both data interpolation and physics model outputs. By expressing the covariance of model residuals as a projection of interpolation covariance through model gradients, the approach eliminates the need for a regularization hyperparameter. SVD of the ODE coupling matrix (stoichiometry matrix) provides orthogonal bases for range and nullspace, enabling dimensionality reduction and preconditioning for stable covariance matrix inversion. The surrogate approximator (SA) is constructed with a range-nullspace decomposition structure to separate time-invariant and time-dependent components. The optimization uses Adam with JIT-compiled gradients via JAX, and covariance matrices are periodically updated to estimate precision matrices.

## Key Results
- MLE formulation eliminates hyperparameter tuning by propagating error from interpolation to physics model residuals via Taylor expansion
- SVD of stoichiometry matrix provides reduced, uncorrelated subspaces for PINN solutions and residual projections
- Range-nullspace decomposition enables dimensionality reduction by separating time-invariant nullspace components from time-dependent range components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Maximum-likelihood estimation reformulates inverse PINNs to avoid hyperparameter tuning by propagating error from interpolation to physics model residuals via Taylor expansion.
- Mechanism: The MLE approach assumes normally distributed residuals for both data interpolation and physics model outputs. By expressing the covariance of model residuals as a projection of interpolation covariance through the model gradients (Eq. 4.1.2), the method eliminates the need for a regularization hyperparameter like α in the original KINNs formulation.
- Core assumption: The SA states and derivatives are independent (or can be approximated as such for sufficiently complex NNs), and residuals are normally distributed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) can be formulated as maximum-likelihood estimators (MLE) to avoid hyperparameter tuning and enable rigorous error propagation from data interpolation to physics model residuals via Taylor expansion."
  - [section]: "Maximizing likelihood estimators are equivalent to minimizing the negative log-likelihood of error PDFs [ 50, 51]."
- Break condition: If the SA states and derivatives are not sufficiently independent, the Taylor expansion approximation fails and the MLE formulation loses its rigor.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SVD of the ODE coupling matrix provides reduced, uncorrelated subspaces that mitigate ill-conditioning in covariance matrix inversion.
- Mechanism: The SVD decomposes the stoichiometry matrix into orthogonal bases for the range and nullspace. Projecting residuals and covariance matrices onto the range basis removes correlated terms, enabling stable inversion of the projected covariance matrices. This preconditioning allows robust estimation of precision matrices.
- Core assumption: The coupling matrix M has a structure that can be decomposed into orthogonal subspaces via SVD, and the residuals are primarily confined to the range space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Furthermore, we show that singular-value decomposition (SVD) of the ODE coupling matrices (reaction stoichiometry matrix) provides reduced uncorrelated subspaces in which PINNs solutions can be represented and over which residuals can be projected."
  - [section]: "The range basis serves as preconditioner since the projection of KINNs residuals on it removes correlated terms from the error covariance matrix, which mitigate the issue of ill-conditioning with respect to inversion."
- Break condition: If the stoichiometry matrix is nearly full rank or the system dynamics are not well-represented by the range-nullspace decomposition, the SVD preconditioning may not provide sufficient conditioning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Range-nullspace decomposition enables dimensionality reduction by separating time-invariant nullspace components from time-dependent range components.
- Mechanism: The SVD-based decomposition allows the SA to be constructed such that the time dependence is only contained in the span of UR (range basis), while the nullspace components are time-invariant. This intrinsic dimensionality reduction means only the range components need to be mapped by the NN, reducing the complexity of the optimization problem.
- Core assumption: The system dynamics can be separated into time-invariant and time-dependent components based on the structure of the stoichiometry matrix.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Furthermore, we show that singular-value decomposition (SVD) of the ODE coupling matrices (reaction stoichiometry matrix) provides reduced uncorrelated subspaces in which PINNs solutions can be represented and over which residuals can be projected."
  - [section]: "The SA representation is constructed to structurally separate the orthogonal components of the time-independent (nullspace) and -dependent (range) solutions."
- Break condition: If the system does not have a clear separation between time-invariant and time-dependent dynamics, or if the nullspace is not sufficiently informative, the dimensionality reduction may not be effective.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Maximum-likelihood estimation (MLE)
  - Why needed here: MLE provides a principled way to formulate the inverse PINN problem without requiring manual tuning of hyperparameters. It allows rigorous propagation of errors between data interpolation and physics model residuals.
  - Quick check question: How does MLE differ from least-squares estimation in the context of inverse problems with noisy data?

- Concept: Singular-value decomposition (SVD)
  - Why needed here: SVD decomposes the ODE coupling matrix into orthogonal bases, enabling dimensionality reduction and preconditioning for stable covariance matrix inversion. It separates the system dynamics into range and nullspace components.
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between the rank of the SVD and the dimensionality of the reduced subspace in the context of chemical kinetics?

- Concept: Taylor expansion for error propagation
  - Why needed here: The Taylor expansion connects variations in the model residuals to variations in both the states and model parameters. This allows the covariance of model residuals to be expressed as a function of the interpolation covariance and model gradients.
  - Quick check question: How does the Taylor expansion approximation affect the accuracy of error propagation when the model is highly nonlinear?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Surrogate Approximator (SA) with normalization operator CN -> MLE Formulation -> SVD Preconditioning -> Optimization (Adam with JAX)

- Critical path:
  1. Build SA with range-nullspace decomposition structure.
  2. Initialize SA and kinetic model parameters.
  3. Compute projected errors and negative log-likelihood.
  4. Update SA and model parameters using Adam.
  5. Periodically update covariance matrices and precision matrices.
  6. Output kinetic model parameters.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using SVD for dimensionality reduction vs. other methods (e.g., PCA): SVD is more natural for ODE systems due to its connection to the stoichiometry matrix.
  - Assuming independence of SA states and derivatives: May not hold for small NNs, but simplifies the MLE formulation.
  - Using a fixed number of epochs for covariance updates: Balances computational cost with the need for stable estimates.

- Failure signatures:
  - Ill-conditioned covariance matrices despite SVD preconditioning: Indicates the system dynamics are not well-separated by the range-nullspace decomposition.
  - Slow or unstable convergence: May indicate the SA is not sufficiently complex to represent the system dynamics independently of the derivatives.
  - Large discrepancies between numerical integration and SA estimates: Suggests the SA is not accurately capturing the system behavior.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the MLE formulation on a simple ODE system (e.g., linear decay) with known parameters and synthetic data. Compare the estimated parameters to the true values and assess the convergence of the negative log-likelihood.
  2. Apply the SVD preconditioning to a small chemical kinetics system with a rank-deficient stoichiometry matrix. Verify that the projected covariance matrices are well-conditioned and that the estimated parameters are stable.
  3. Construct an SA with the range-nullspace decomposition structure for a medium-sized ODE system. Train the SA and compare the accuracy of the estimates to a standard PINN approach without the decomposition.

## Limitations
- The assumption of normally distributed residuals may not hold for all systems, particularly those with heavy-tailed noise or non-Gaussian errors.
- The SVD preconditioning relies on the structure of the stoichiometry matrix, which may not be rank-deficient or well-conditioned for all ODE systems.
- The paper does not provide extensive empirical results comparing the rKINNs approach to other state-of-the-art methods for inverse problems in chemical kinetics.
