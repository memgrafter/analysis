# On the Global Convergence of Natural Actor-Critic with Two-layer Neural Network Parametrization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10486
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10486
- Reference count: 40
- First finite-time sample complexity result for Natural Actor-Critic without linear or low-rank MDP structures

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the first global convergence and sample complexity guarantees for Natural Actor-Critic (NAC) algorithms with neural network parametrization. The authors propose NAC2L, which uses a two-layer ReLU neural network for the critic and solves a convex optimization problem to estimate the Q-function at each iteration. By leveraging recent results on convex reformulations of two-layer ReLU networks, they prove a sample complexity of O(1/ε⁴(1-γ)⁴), removing the need for linear or low-rank MDP assumptions that previous works required.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes NAC2L, which parametrizes the critic using a two-layer neural network and estimates the Q-function through a convex optimization problem at each iteration. The key innovation is using the convex reformulation framework of two-layer ReLU networks, where the optimization problem over network weights can be reformulated as a convex problem over the coefficients of the activation patterns. This allows the authors to leverage tools from convex optimization to establish global convergence. The algorithm iteratively updates the actor policy using natural gradient descent based on the estimated advantage function, while the critic is updated by solving the convex reformulation of the two-layer network fitting problem.

## Key Results
- Establishes O(1/ε⁴(1-γ)⁴) sample complexity for Natural Actor-Critic with neural network parametrization
- First convergence result for NAC without requiring linear or low-rank MDP structures
- Provides explicit bounds for error components arising from actor and critic updates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The convergence relies on the convex reformulation of two-layer ReLU neural networks, which transforms the non-convex parameter estimation problem into a convex optimization over coefficients. This allows standard convergence analysis tools to be applied. The natural gradient update for the actor is shown to converge to a globally optimal policy under the assumption that the convex reformulation oracle provides accurate estimates. The sample complexity bound is derived by carefully analyzing the error propagation from both actor and critic updates, showing that each component can be bounded independently.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: State-Action Pairs -> Two-layer ReLU Network -> Convex Reformulation -> Critic Estimate -> Natural Gradient Update -> Actor Policy

Critical Path: The algorithm's performance depends critically on the accuracy of the convex reformulation oracle. Errors in solving the convex problem directly propagate to the critic estimate, which then affects the natural gradient direction for the actor update. The bottleneck is the computational complexity of solving the convex reformulation at each iteration.

Design Tradeoffs: The use of two-layer ReLU networks provides a balance between expressiveness and tractability through convex reformulation. Deeper networks would increase representational power but lose the convex reformulation property. The diagonal matrix sampling strategy (Assumption 2) trades off computational efficiency against approximation accuracy.

Failure Signatures: If the convex reformulation becomes ill-conditioned (rare activation patterns), the critic estimates may become inaccurate, leading to poor policy updates. If the diagonal matrix set D̃ is too small, the critic approximation error grows. If the natural gradient step size is too large, policy updates may become unstable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the sample complexity bounds be extended to multi-layer neural networks beyond the two-layer case studied in this paper?
- Basis in paper: The paper explicitly states that "one can likely use the framework described in (Belilovsky et al., 2019) to extend the results to a multi-layer setup" as a limitation in the conclusions section.
- Why unresolved: The current analysis relies on the specific convex reformulation of two-layer ReLU networks, which does not directly generalize to deeper architectures. The mathematical framework for extending this to multi-layer networks is not developed.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal extension of the convex reformulation technique to multi-layer networks with proven equivalence to the original non-convex problem, along with corresponding sample complexity bounds.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the exact relationships between the error terms ǫ|˜D| (due to using a subset of diagonal matrices) and the size |˜D| of the sampled diagonal matrix set?
- Basis in paper: Assumption 2 states that E[|Qθ∗ − Qθ∗|˜D||] ≤ ǫ|˜D| for any ν ∈ P(S×A), but does not specify how ǫ|˜D| depends on |˜D|.
- Why unresolved: The paper treats ǫ|˜D| as a constant for theoretical analysis, but in practice its value depends on the relationship between the sampled set ˜D and the full set DX. The paper only mentions that "setting |˜D| to be the same order of magnitude as d gives us a sufficient number of diagonal matrices" without providing quantitative bounds.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies or theoretical bounds showing how ǫ|˜D| scales with |˜D| and the dimensionality d, providing explicit relationships between sample size and approximation error.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the sample complexity results be improved by incorporating the additional information from the Monte Carlo estimates of Aπλk?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes in Appendix A that the analysis of (Agarwal et al., 2020b) and (Liu et al., 2020) do not consider (i) the extra 1/(1-γ) state action samples required to obtain Monte Carlo estimate Aπλk and (ii) the error incurred due to the gap between the Monte Carlo estimate Aπλk and the actual Q-function.
- Why unresolved: The current analysis does not incorporate these additional error terms into the sample complexity bound, potentially leading to suboptimal bounds. The exact impact of these error terms on the overall sample complexity is not quantified.
- What evidence would resolve it: A refined analysis that explicitly incorporates the additional sample complexity and error terms from Monte Carlo estimation, showing how they affect the overall sample complexity bound and whether they can be bounded more tightly.

## Limitations
- Assumes access to an ideal convex reformulation oracle which may not be efficiently implementable
- Theoretical analysis relies on strong assumptions about the critic network properties
- No experimental validation provided to verify practical performance
- Sample complexity bound may be conservative due to not incorporating Monte Carlo estimation errors

## Confidence

**Medium Confidence** in the theoretical claims due to:
- Dependence on convex reformulation assumptions that may face computational challenges
- Strong assumptions about critic network properties that may not hold uniformly
- Lack of empirical validation of the proposed algorithm
- Sample complexity analysis that doesn't fully account for practical implementation considerations
