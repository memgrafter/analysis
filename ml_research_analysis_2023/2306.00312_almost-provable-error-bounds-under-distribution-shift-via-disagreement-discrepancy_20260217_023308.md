# (Almost) Provable Error Bounds Under Distribution Shift via Disagreement Discrepancy

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00312
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00312
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: A new method, DIS2, provides provable error bounds under distribution shift by maximizing disagreement between a classifier of interest and a critic linear classifier.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method for deriving error bounds for deep neural networks under distribution shift using unlabeled test data. The key idea is to use a disagreement discrepancy, which measures the disagreement between the classifier of interest and a "critic" classifier on the source and target distributions. The authors derive a bound that is inspired by H∆H-divergence but is easier to evaluate and tighter in practice. They also propose a new "disagreement loss" for maximizing multiclass disagreement, which they show performs better than prior methods. Experiments on a wide range of benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed method gives valid error bounds while achieving average accuracy comparable to competitive estimation baselines.

## Method Summary
The method trains a critic linear classifier to maximize disagreement discrepancy using a novel disagreement logistic loss. The critic is trained on deep features (or logits) extracted from a classifier of interest, with the goal of maximizing disagreement between the classifier of interest and the critic on both source and target data. The disagreement discrepancy is then used to bound the target error, with the option to drop a concentration term for improved prediction accuracy. The method is evaluated on a range of datasets and compared to baselines like WILDs, ODD, and URS.

## Key Results
- The DIS2 method achieves a mean absolute error of 0.0935 and a coverage of 0.7500 on test data.
- The disagreement logistic loss outperforms two other losses in maximizing disagreement discrepancy.
- Dropping the concentration term improves prediction accuracy while maintaining valid error bounds.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by training a critic classifier to maximize disagreement with the classifier of interest on both source and target data. The disagreement discrepancy is then used to bound the target error, providing a provable guarantee on the classifier's performance under distribution shift.

## Foundational Learning
- **Disagreement Discrepancy**: A measure of disagreement between two classifiers on source and target distributions. Needed to bound the target error under distribution shift.
- **H∆H-Divergence**: A measure of the discrepancy between two distributions based on the disagreement between classifiers. Provides the theoretical foundation for the disagreement discrepancy bound.
- **Concentration Term**: A term that accounts for the finite sample size and ensures the validity of the error bound. Can be dropped for improved prediction accuracy.
- **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**: A dimensionality reduction technique used to select the most informative features for the critic classifier. Helps improve the tightness of the error bound.
- **Logistic Loss**: A loss function used to train the critic classifier. The disagreement logistic loss is specifically designed to maximize disagreement between classifiers.
- **Distribution Shift**: A change in the data distribution between training and test time. The method aims to provide error bounds under distribution shift.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Classifier of Interest -> Deep Features/Logits -> Critic Classifier -> Disagreement Discrepancy -> Error Bound
- **Critical Path**: The critical path is the training of the critic classifier to maximize disagreement discrepancy, followed by the computation of the error bound using the disagreement discrepancy and concentration term.
- **Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades off between the tightness of the error bound and the prediction accuracy by optionally dropping the concentration term. Using PCA to select features also trades off between the tightness of the bound and the computational cost.
- **Failure Signatures**: Bound violations due to overly restrictive feature dimensionality or domain-adversarial training. Poor disagreement maximization if using unsuitable loss functions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the cumulative ℓ1 ratio truly linearly correlate with the tightness and validity of the error bound for all types of distribution shifts, or are there specific shifts where this correlation breaks down?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows a scatter plot of the cumulative ℓ1 ratio versus the error prediction gap, suggesting a linear correlation. However, this is only shown for a subset of shifts.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides evidence for a subset of shifts, and it's unclear if the correlation holds for all types of distribution shifts, especially those not included in the experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on a wider variety of distribution shifts and analyzing the correlation between the cumulative ℓ1 ratio and the tightness of the error bound for each type of shift.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the disagreement loss (ℓdis) be further improved to achieve even higher disagreement discrepancy, especially for distribution shifts where it currently underperforms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares ℓdis to two other losses and shows that it achieves the highest average disagreement discrepancy. However, it also mentions that ℓdis could be a poor proxy for maximizing disagreement in certain cases.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a comparison between ℓdis and two other losses, and it's unclear if there are other potential loss functions that could achieve even higher disagreement discrepancy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing new loss functions specifically designed to maximize disagreement discrepancy, and comparing their performance to ℓdis across a wide range of distribution shifts.

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the cumulative ℓ1 ratio is a simple metric that roughly linearly correlates with the tightness of the bound, but it could be greatly improved.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide an alternative method for identifying the ideal number of principal components, and it's unclear if there are other metrics or approaches that could be more effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing alternative methods for identifying the ideal number of principal components, such as using information criteria or cross-validation techniques, and comparing their performance to the cumulative ℓ1 ratio.

## Limitations
- The method relies on the ability of the critic classifier to maximize disagreement discrepancy, which is not fully characterized.
- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of hyperparameter tuning on the performance of the method.
- The method requires access to deep features or logits from the classifier of interest, which may not always be available.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The theoretical foundation of the disagreement discrepancy bound and its relationship to H∆H-divergence.
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical performance of DIS2 relative to baselines like WILDs, ODD, and URS on benchmark datasets.
- **Low confidence**: The exact procedure for selecting principal components and the impact of the concentration term on bound tightness.
