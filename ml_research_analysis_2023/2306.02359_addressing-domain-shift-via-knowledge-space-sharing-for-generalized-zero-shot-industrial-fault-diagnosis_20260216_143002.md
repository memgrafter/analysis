# Addressing Domain Shift via Knowledge Space Sharing for Generalized Zero-Shot Industrial Fault Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.02359
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02359
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: KSS model achieves harmonic mean accuracy of 49.44-59.01% on TEP benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art methods by 6-17%

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the domain shift problem in generalized zero-shot industrial fault diagnosis where unseen faults are misclassified as seen faults due to lack of training samples. The proposed Knowledge Space Sharing (KSS) model includes a generation mechanism (KSS-G) that creates realistic unseen fault samples by recombining transferable attribute features under auxiliary knowledge guidance, and a discrimination mechanism (KSS-D) that models seen categories in knowledge space to distinguish unseen faults. The KSS model demonstrates superior performance on the Tennessee-Eastman process benchmark compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
The KSS model consists of two main components: KSS-G and KSS-D. KSS-G generates realistic samples for unseen fault categories by extracting attribute-specific features from seen samples, reorganizing them under expert knowledge guidance, and reconstructing them into fake samples. KSS-D trains in a supervised manner using both real and generated samples, modeling each seen category's distribution in the knowledge space using Gaussian Mixture Models and employing control limits to distinguish seen from unseen faults. The model uses a two-stage classification approach where AP1 performs coarse classification and AP2 handles fine-grained classification for samples identified as unseen.

## Key Results
- Harmonic mean accuracy of 49.44%, 51.99%, 49.29%, 59.01%, and 40.63% across five different train/test splits on TEP benchmark
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods by 6-17% absolute improvement in harmonic mean accuracy
- Successfully addresses domain shift problem by generating realistic unseen fault samples and modeling seen categories in knowledge space
- Achieves significant improvements over existing ZSL and GZSL approaches for industrial fault diagnosis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Knowledge-guided Feature Recombination
KSS-G addresses domain shift by generating realistic unseen fault samples through knowledge-guided attribute feature recombination. The generator extracts attribute-specific features from seen samples, reorganizes them under expert knowledge guidance, and reconstructs them into realistic fake samples for both seen and unseen categories. This mechanism bridges the semantic gap between seen and unseen faults by leveraging auxiliary knowledge about fault attributes.

### Mechanism 2: Knowledge Space Modeling with GMMs
KSS-D mitigates domain shift by modeling seen fault distributions in the knowledge space and using control limits to distinguish unseen faults from seen faults. After projecting samples into the knowledge space via attribute learners, Gaussian Mixture Models model each seen category's distribution. Control limits based on negative log-likelihood distinguish test samples as seen or unseen, providing a principled way to handle the domain shift problem.

### Mechanism 3: Two-Stage Classification
The two-stage classification approach in KSS-D improves accuracy by first filtering seen categories then refining within the knowledge space. AP1 performs coarse classification by matching test samples to seen category attributes. If no match is found, AP2 retrains with generated samples and uses GMM-based fine-grained classification. This hierarchical approach reduces computational load and error propagation while maintaining high discrimination accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: KSS-G (generator) → KSS-D (discriminator) → TZSD model
  - KSS-G: Attribute feature extractors → Attribute recognizers → Feature group reorganization → Reconstruction module → Aid-discriminator
  - KSS-D: AP1 (coarse classification) → AP2 (retrained with generated samples) → GMM modeling → Control limit-based classification
  - TZSD model: Fine-grained classification of unseen samples identified by KSS-D

- **Critical path**: KSS-G generates fake samples → KSS-D trains on real + fake samples → KSS-D classifies test samples → TZSD handles remaining unseen samples

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using knowledge space vs. feature space for discrimination: Knowledge space provides interpretability but may lose some discriminative information
  - GAN-based generation vs. VAE: GAN provides better sample quality but is harder to train stably
  - Two-stage classification vs. single-stage: Two-stage reduces computational load but adds complexity

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor generation quality: KSS-D and TZSD models will perform poorly due to unrealistic samples
  - Inaccurate knowledge space modeling: KSS-D will misclassify many samples as seen or unseen
  - Overfitting to seen categories: Harmonic mean accuracy will be low despite high seen accuracy

  2. Train KSS-D with generated samples and test on held-out seen samples only
  3. Full end-to-end test with both seen and unseen samples and calculate harmonic mean accuracy
