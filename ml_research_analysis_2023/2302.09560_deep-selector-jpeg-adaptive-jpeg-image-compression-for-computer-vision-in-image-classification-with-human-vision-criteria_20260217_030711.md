# Deep Selector-JPEG: Adaptive JPEG Image Compression for Computer Vision in Image classification with Human Vision Criteria

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09560
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09560
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Adaptive JPEG compression method that achieves 0.2-1% accuracy gains at same compression ratios while satisfying human vision constraints

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes Deep Selector-JPEG, an adaptive JPEG compression method that targets image classification while satisfying human vision criteria. The method adaptively selects a Quality Factor (QF) for each input image to achieve a good trade-off between Compression Ratio (CR) and DNN classifier Accuracy (Rate-Accuracy performance) while ensuring the MS-SSIM of the compressed image is greater than a threshold value predetermined by human vision with a high probability. Deep Selector-JPEG is designed via light-weighted or heavy-weighted selector architectures, and experimental results show that it achieves better Rate-Accuracy performance over the ImageNet validation set for all tested DNN classifiers with gains in classification accuracy between 0.2% and 1% at the same CRs while satisfying human vision constraints. Deep Selector-JPEG can also roughly provide the original classification accuracy at higher CRs.

## Method Summary
Deep Selector-JPEG trains independent binary classifiers for each Quality Factor (QF) value in the set L={10,20,30,40,50,60,70,80,90}. Each classifier predicts whether that QF is "feasible" for a given image based on two criteria: (1) MS-SSIM ≥ threshold with high probability, and (2) ground truth label rank maintained or improved versus original. At inference, the lowest feasible QF is selected. The method uses either light-weighted architecture (freezing pre-trained DNN except last two blocks) or heavy-weighted architecture (training entire DNN per binary classifier) to balance computational complexity and accuracy. The approach is evaluated on ImageNet validation set across multiple DNN classifiers including Inception V3 and MobileNetV2.

## Key Results
- Achieves 0.2-1% accuracy gains at same compression ratios compared to standard JPEG compression
- Satisfies MS-SSIM thresholds predetermined by human vision with high probability
- Works across multiple DNN architectures including Inception V3, MobileNetV2, and others
- Can maintain original classification accuracy at higher compression ratios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deep Selector-JPEG adaptively selects Quality Factor (QF) per image to optimize Rate-Accuracy performance while satisfying MS-SSIM-based human vision constraints.
- Mechanism: The method trains independent binary classifiers for each QF value in L={10,20,30,40,50,60,70,80,90}. Each classifier predicts whether that QF is "feasible" for a given image based on two criteria: (1) MS-SSIM ≥ threshold with high probability, and (2) GT label rank maintained or improved versus original. At inference, the lowest feasible QF is selected.
- Core assumption: A pre-trained DNN classifier (e.g., Inception V3 or MobileNetV2) can reliably label feasibility by checking rank preservation and MS-SSIM constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Deep Selector-JPEG selects adaptively a Quality Factor (QF) to compress the image so that a good trade-off between the Compression Ratio (CR) and DNN classifier Accuracy can be achieved... while the MS-SSIM of such compressed image is greater than a threshold value predetermined by HV with a high probability."
  - [section 2.1] "The set of feasible QFs is determined based on two criteria: (1) MS-SSIM value of the JPEG compressed image with any feasible QF is greater than or equal to the MS-SSIM threshold... (2) Rank for the GT label of the JPEG compressed image with the given QF is either the same or better than the rank of the GT label of the original image."
  - [corpus] No direct mention of adaptive QF selection or MS-SSIM ranking constraints. This mechanism is novel to this paper.
- Break condition: If the pre-trained DNN mislabels feasibility (e.g., fails to detect rank preservation), selected QFs may not satisfy vision constraints, degrading RA performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using either light or heavy DNN architectures for the selector enables trade-offs between accuracy gains and computational complexity.
- Mechanism: Two forms of selector architecture: (1) Form One freezes a pre-trained DNN except last two blocks as shared feature extractor for all binary classifiers; (2) Form Two trains entire DNN per binary classifier. MobileNetV2 provides lightweight option, Inception V3 provides heavyweight option.
- Core assumption: The feature representations from pre-trained DNNs are sufficiently discriminative for predicting QF feasibility across diverse images.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Deep Selector-JPEG is designed via light-weighted or heavy-weighted selector architectures."
  - [section 2.1] "In both forms, we decompose the given problem with n QFs into n independent binary classification problems. Thus, each hypothesis yj is induced to predict the feasibility of its corresponding QFj... In terms of architecture, Form One freezes a pre-trained image classification DNN S′ except for its last two blocks... Based on the common features, each hj that consists of the last two blocks of S′ is induced during training to make its own independent binary classification decision... To further improve RA performance, the entire S′ is learned for each yj independently in Form Two."
  - [corpus] No direct mention of this two-form architecture design or its trade-offs. This is specific to this paper.
- Break condition: If feature representations are not discriminative enough, binary classifiers will produce poor feasibility predictions, leading to suboptimal QF selection.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Selecting the lowest feasible QF per image achieves better Rate-Accuracy performance than using fixed QF across all images.
- Mechanism: By allowing different images to be compressed at different QFs based on their individual feasibility, the method adapts compression aggressiveness to image content. Images that tolerate lower QFs (higher compression) without losing classification accuracy are compressed more aggressively, improving overall CR without sacrificing accuracy.
- Core assumption: Different images have different tolerances for compression before classification accuracy degrades, and these tolerances correlate with image content features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For each image, Deep Selector-JPEG selects adaptively a Quality Factor (QF) to compress the image so that a good trade-off between the Compression Ratio (CR) and DNN classifier Accuracy can be achieved... while the MS-SSIM of such compressed image is greater than a threshold value predetermined by HV with a high probability."
  - [section 1] "Table 1 shows an image instance from the ImageNet validation set fed to the Inception V3 (IV3) model at different quality factors. This table indicates that a JPEG compressed version with a lower QF could yield a higher rank of the ground-truth (GT) label in comparison with the original image with a reasonable HV quality."
  - [corpus] No direct mention of adaptive per-image QF selection or its impact on RA performance. This is the core novelty of this paper.
- Break condition: If feasibility criteria are too restrictive or too permissive, the adaptive selection may not find optimal QFs, reducing RA gains.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Original ImageNet image -> JPEG compression at multiple QFs -> Feature extraction (frozen or trainable) -> Binary classifiers for each QF -> Feasibility predictions -> Lowest feasible QF selection -> Compressed image output

- Critical path: 1. Input image → frozen feature extractor → binary classifiers → feasibility predictions 2. Feasibility predictions → lowest feasible QF selection 3. Original image → JPEG encoding at selected QF → compressed output

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Form One vs Form Two: Form One is computationally cheaper but may sacrifice accuracy; Form Two provides better accuracy at higher computational cost
  - Number of QFs in L: More QFs provide finer control but increase training complexity
  - MS-SSIM threshold: Higher thresholds ensure better perceptual quality but may limit compression options

- Failure signatures:
  - Selector selects high QFs consistently: Feasibility criteria too restrictive or feature extractor insufficiently discriminative
  - Accuracy drops significantly: Pre-trained DNN mislabels feasibility or rank preservation criterion too strict
  - Inconsistent results across test DNNs: Selector overfits to specific DNN architecture used for labeling

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify feasibility labeling: Take 100 ImageNet images, compress at all QFs in L, check MS-SSIM and rank preservation manually to validate automated labeling
  2. Train single binary classifier: Pick one QF (e.g., QF=30) and train a single binary classifier to verify the basic feasibility prediction mechanism works
  3. Compare Form One vs Form Two: Train both architectures on the same dataset and measure RA performance difference to quantify trade-off between complexity and accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of Deep Selector-JPEG scale when applied to more diverse computer vision tasks beyond image classification, such as object detection or semantic segmentation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses specifically on image classification tasks with DNN classifiers, but mentions that the selection idea could be transferred to other computer vision tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results or analysis for tasks other than image classification, leaving the effectiveness of the method for other CV applications unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the Rate-Accuracy performance of Deep Selector-JPEG on object detection, semantic segmentation, or other computer vision tasks, compared to standard JPEG compression at equivalent compression ratios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using a larger range of Quality Factors (QFs) on the Rate-Accuracy performance of Deep Selector-JPEG, particularly at higher MS-SSIM thresholds?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that when MS-SSIM ≥ 0.9 or 0.95, there are limited options for QFs, which impacts accuracy gains, and suggests that using a bigger range of QFs might improve performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a fixed set of QFs {10, 20, ..., 90} and does not explore the effects of expanding this range, particularly for higher MS-SSIM thresholds.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the Rate-Accuracy performance of Deep Selector-JPEG using an expanded range of QFs versus the current fixed range, across different MS-SSIM thresholds.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of pre-trained DNN classifier used for labeling (S) affect the performance of Deep Selector-JPEG across different test DNN classifiers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses IV3 or MobileNetV2 as the pre-trained DNN classifier for labeling, and notes that performance gains are slightly higher when the selector trained with labels from a DNN is applied to the same DNN classifier.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not systematically explore the impact of using different pre-trained DNNs for labeling on the overall performance of Deep Selector-JPEG across various test DNN classifiers.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study comparing the performance of Deep Selector-JPEG when using different pre-trained DNNs for labeling, and analyzing the correlation between the labeling DNN and the test DNNs' performance.

## Limitations

- The exact implementation details of the feature extraction and binary classification architectures are not fully specified, making reproduction challenging
- The specific precision constants and decision thresholds for each QF classifier are not provided, though ranges are mentioned
- The paper doesn't explore the impact of using different pre-trained DNNs for labeling on cross-DNN generalization performance

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The core mechanism of adaptive QF selection based on feasibility criteria (MS-SSIM and rank preservation) is well-supported by the paper's methodology and experimental results. The improvement in Rate-Accuracy performance over standard JPEG compression is demonstrated across multiple DNN architectures.

**Medium confidence**: The two-form architecture design (light vs heavy) and its trade-offs are conceptually sound but lack sufficient implementation details for complete verification. The generalization across different DNN classifiers is promising but could benefit from testing on additional architectures.

**Low confidence**: The exact impact of different MS-SSIM thresholds and precision constants on RA performance is difficult to assess without knowing the specific values used or the sensitivity analysis performed.
