# Large language models and (non-)linguistic recursion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07195
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07195
- Reference count: 0
- GPT-4 successfully identifies, produces, and analyzes recursive structures in both linguistic and visual domains

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can exhibit recursive behavior, a cognitive property unique to human language. Through a series of carefully designed prompts, the authors test GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 on tasks involving linguistic recursion (nested clauses, adjectival modification, possessive recursion) and visual recursion (ASCII art, Mermaid syntax, TikZ diagrams). The results show that GPT-4 can successfully perform these tasks while GPT-3.5 struggles significantly. The authors conclude that GPT-4 may be the first language model with general recursive reasoning ability.

## Method Summary
The study employs a prompt-based methodology where models are explicitly instructed to identify, label, and generate recursive structures. For linguistic recursion, models analyze sentences with center-embedding and various modification types. For visual recursion, models generate recursive images using ASCII art, Mermaid syntax, and TikZ in LaTeX. The authors also test iterative prompting by asking models to evaluate and correct their own outputs. Performance is compared between GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 across multiple task types and complexity levels.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 successfully identifies different types of recursion (adjectival, possessive, clausal) in sentences
- GPT-4 generates correct recursive structures in both linguistic and visual domains
- GPT-4 improves performance through self-evaluation and iterative correction of errors
- GPT-3.5 struggles with identifying recursion types and generating further layers of recursive structure

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-4's larger parameter count enables general recursive reasoning across linguistic and visual domains.
- Mechanism: The model learns to map abstract recursive patterns onto concrete representations through distributed representations that capture hierarchical structure.
- Core assumption: Recursive behavior is emergent from sufficient scale and diversity in training data, not from explicit symbolic rules.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we demonstrate that when explicitly prompted, GPT-4 can both produce and analyze recursive structures"
  - [section] "GPT-4’s ability to construct center-embedded sentences without being explicitly prompted to do so thus suggests that the model acquired grammatical structure beyond simple distributional tendencies"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct neighbor papers address emergent recursive reasoning in large models
- Break condition: Performance degrades when recursive depth exceeds the model's learned representation capacity, or when task requires explicit symbolic manipulation not captured in training data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Explicit prompting enables meta-linguistic awareness of recursion in GPT-4.
- Mechanism: The model uses in-context learning to apply linguistic theory when given structured prompts, treating recursion as a pattern to be recognized and reproduced.
- Core assumption: Meta-linguistic tasks require the model to step outside pure pattern completion and engage in self-reflective analysis of structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose several prompt designs aimed at eliciting and analyzing recursive behavior in LLMs"
  - [section] "we find that GPT-4 largely succeeds on the tasks in both domains... The model correctly adds layers of causal complementation, adjectival modification, adverbial modification, possession, and clausal modification"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct neighbor papers discuss meta-linguistic awareness in LLMs
- Break condition: Model fails when prompts are ambiguous or when recursive structures are too complex for in-context reasoning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Iterative prompting enables GPT-4 to correct its own recursive structure errors.
- Mechanism: The model uses self-evaluation feedback loops to refine representations, treating each evaluation as additional context for improvement.
- Core assumption: Recursive reasoning improves through iterative refinement rather than single-pass generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find that GPT-4 largely succeeds on the tasks in both domains"
  - [section] "When prompted to evaluate its own reply, the model often identifies the problems and fixes them... simply asking it to evaluate its previous answer results in a dramatic improvement"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct neighbor papers address iterative reasoning in recursive tasks
- Break condition: Model becomes stuck in local optima or fails to recognize errors even after multiple evaluation prompts.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prompt → Model processing → Recursive structure generation → Self-evaluation (if iterative) → Output refinement
- Critical path: The model receives a structured prompt, processes it through transformer layers with attention mechanisms, generates recursive output, optionally evaluates and refines through iterative prompting
- Design tradeoffs: Larger models enable recursive reasoning but increase computational cost; explicit prompting enables meta-linguistic awareness but requires careful prompt design; iterative refinement improves accuracy but adds latency
- Failure signatures: Incorrect recursive structure identification, non-recursive output when recursion requested, syntax errors in TikZ/ASCII output, failure to recognize own errors

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does GPT-4's ability to generate and analyze recursive structures represent a fundamental shift in language model capabilities, or is it merely an extension of existing pattern-matching abilities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that GPT-4 "may be the first language model with emergent and general (i.e. non-domain-specific) recursive ability" and that its success "suggests that it may be the first LLM capable of general recursive reasoning."
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates GPT-4's impressive performance on recursive tasks, it does not definitively prove that this ability is fundamentally different from previous models' pattern-matching capabilities. The authors acknowledge that GPT-4's training data likely contains linguistics textbooks discussing recursion, raising the possibility of memorization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of GPT-4's performance on novel recursive structures not present in its training data, alongside investigations into the model's internal representations of recursive concepts, could help determine whether its ability represents a fundamental shift or an advanced form of pattern-matching.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the limitations of GPT-4's recursive abilities, and how do these limitations compare to human recursive processing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "GPT-4’s performance on the tasks is very good, although the model makes mistakes in syntactic analyses of recursive structures" and that "GPT-4’s initial replies to a prompt are not always correct."
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates GPT-4's capabilities, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of its limitations or compare these limitations to human recursive processing. The authors mention that humans also require explicit instruction to understand recursion, but do not explore the similarities and differences between human and GPT-4 recursive processing in depth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed studies comparing GPT-4's performance on recursive tasks with human performance, particularly focusing on error patterns and processing difficulties, could help elucidate the similarities and differences between artificial and human recursive processing.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the temperature setting affect GPT-4's ability to generate recursive structures, and what does this reveal about the model's internal representation of recursion?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "lowering the temperature values below 1.0 results in more conservative output (and more Sierpiński triangles)" and that "raising the temperature above 1.0 increases the frequency with which elaborate original structures are generated."
- Why unresolved: While the paper briefly mentions the effect of temperature on GPT-4's output, it does not explore this relationship in depth or discuss what it might reveal about the model's internal representation of recursion. The authors note that changing the temperature does not "considerably improve either model's performance," but do not elaborate on the implications of this finding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic studies varying temperature settings and analyzing the resulting recursive structures could provide insights into how GPT-4 represents and generates recursive concepts. Additionally, investigations into the model's internal activations during recursive task performance at different temperature settings could reveal more about its internal representation of recursion.

## Limitations

- The study relies entirely on prompted behavior rather than probing the model's intrinsic recursive representations
- GPT-3.5's failures may reflect prompt engineering limitations rather than fundamental architectural constraints
- Visual recursion tasks use domain-specific formalisms (Mermaid, TikZ) that may benefit from memorization rather than genuine recursive understanding

## Confidence

- GPT-4's recursive identification and generation: High
- GPT-4's self-correction capability: Medium
- GPT-4 as "first LLM with general recursive reasoning": Low
