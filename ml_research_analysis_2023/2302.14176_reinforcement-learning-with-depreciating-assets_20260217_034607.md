# Reinforcement Learning with Depreciating Assets

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14176
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14176
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: Introduces depreciating assets into infinite-horizon RL optimization with Bellman-style characterization and Q-learning algorithm

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the concept of depreciating assets into the framework of infinite-horizon quantitative optimization in reinforcement learning. The key contribution is a Bellman-style equational characterization of optimality under asset depreciation, inspired by classical exponential discounting, where the value of an asset is scaled by a fixed discount factor at each time step after it is obtained. The authors propose a notion of asset depreciation and formulate a model-free reinforcement learning approach to obtain optimal policies. The primary result is a Tauberian theorem that establishes the relationship between discounted and average depreciating payoffs, allowing for the computation of optimal values and policies in polynomial time.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces depreciating assets into RL by defining a discounted depreciating payoff where asset values decay geometrically over time. The authors establish a Bellman-style optimality equation for this setting and show that the discounted depreciating payoff equals the standard discounted payoff scaled by 1/(1-λγ). This scaling relationship enables leveraging existing MDP theory. They prove that optimal policies are stationary and deterministic, and formulate a Q-learning variant that converges to the optimal Q-value under Robbins-Monroe conditions. The Tauberian theorem connects discounted and average depreciating payoffs, enabling polynomial-time computation of optimal values.

## Key Results
- Proved that discounted depreciating payoff equals standard discounted payoff scaled by 1/(1-λγ)
- Established Bellman optimality equations for depreciating assets with stationary deterministic optimal policies
- Developed a Q-learning variant for depreciated values that converges under Robbins-Monroe conditions
- Proved a Tauberian theorem relating discounted and average depreciating payoffs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The depreciated value can be expressed as a scaled version of the standard discounted value.
- Mechanism: By rewriting the depreciating sum, the authors show that the discounted depreciating payoff equals the standard discounted payoff scaled by 1/(1-λγ). This allows leveraging existing Bellman optimality equations and contraction mapping results.
- Core assumption: The series involved converge absolutely, enabling application of Mertens' theorem to factor the Cauchy product.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that this is not a mere coincidence, and prove that this equality holds also for general MDPs."
  - [section] "By splitting the term λn−1 occurring in the definition of the discounted depreciating payoff into the product λn−k λk−1 and distributing these factors into the inner summation, we obtain the expression... The series (3) may be factored into the Cauchy product... Since both terms in this Cauchy product converge absolutely, Mertens' theorem applies."
- Break condition: If either series fails to converge absolutely, Mertens' theorem cannot be applied and the scaling relationship breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Optimal policies for discounted depreciating payoffs are stationary and deterministic.
- Mechanism: Since the discounted depreciating value equals the scaled standard discounted value, the same Bellman optimality equations apply. The contraction mapping property ensures that stationary deterministic policies suffice for optimality.
- Core assumption: The contraction mapping framework applies to the scaled value function just as it does to the standard discounted value.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We characterize the optimal value of the discounted depreciating payoff via Bellman-style optimality equations and use them to show that stationary deterministic policies are sufficient for achieving optimality."
  - [section] "Corollary 1 (Strategic Complexity). For any discounted depreciating payoff over any finite MDP, there exists an optimal policy that is stationary and deterministic."
- Break condition: If the MDP is infinite or the state space is uncountable, the finiteness assumption breaks and stationarity may not be sufficient.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The discounted depreciating Q-learning algorithm converges to the optimal Q-value under Robbins-Monroe conditions.
- Mechanism: The Q-learning update for depreciated values is a scaled version of standard Q-learning. Since the underlying value functions are related by a constant factor, convergence conditions transfer directly.
- Core assumption: The Robbins-Monroe convergence conditions for learning rates ensure convergence of the Q-learning process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The optimality equation also facilitates a formulation of a variant of Q-learning that is compatible with asset depreciation, thereby providing a model-free reinforcement learning approach to obtain optimal policies in the learning setting."
  - [section] "Theorem 5. If each state-action pair of the environment is encountered infinitely often and the learning rates satisfy the Robbins-Monroe convergence criteria... then iterating (9) converges almost surely to the discounted depreciating Q-value as n→∞"
- Break condition: If state-action pairs are not encountered infinitely often or learning rates don't satisfy Robbins-Monroe conditions, convergence is not guaranteed.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the results of this paper be extended to state-dependent depreciation factors?
- Basis in paper: The authors explicitly state that the notion of state-based discount factors has been studied in the context of classic optimization and learning, and they pose the question of extending their results to the setting with state-dependent depreciation factors.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any results or analysis for state-dependent depreciation factors, leaving this as an open question for future research.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof or counterexample demonstrating whether the main results of the paper hold when depreciation factors are allowed to vary across states.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can optimization and learning be performed for continuous-time MDPs with depreciating assets?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention that continuous-time MDPs provide a dense-time analog of discrete-time MDPs and that optimization and RL algorithms for such systems are well understood. They then pose the question of whether it is possible to solve optimization and learning for CTMDPs with depreciating assets.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any results or analysis for continuous-time MDPs with depreciating assets, leaving this as an open question for future research.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof or counterexample demonstrating whether the main results of the paper can be extended to continuous-time MDPs with depreciating assets, along with any necessary modifications to the algorithms or analysis.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between depreciating assets and past-discounted characterizations of regret in online learning and optimization?
- Basis in paper: The authors posit that imperfect decision makers may view their regret in a depreciated sense, since a suboptimal action in the recent past tends to cause more regret than an equally suboptimal action in the distant past. They hope that their results will spur further interest in developing foundations of past-discounted characterizations of regret in online learning and optimization.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any results or analysis on the relationship between depreciating assets and regret in online learning and optimization, leaving this as an open question for future research.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof or counterexample demonstrating whether depreciating assets can be used to model past-discounted regret in online learning and optimization, along with any necessary modifications to the algorithms or analysis.

## Limitations
- Analysis limited to finite MDPs, not capturing all real-world scenarios with depreciating assets
- Assumes fixed discount factors without exploring state-dependent or time-varying depreciation rates
- Limited empirical validation with most results being theoretical rather than experimental
