# AMR Parsing with Instruction Fine-tuned Pre-trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.12272
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12272
- **Reference count**: 7
- **Key outcome**: FLAN-T5 instruction fine-tuning significantly improves AMR parsing performance, achieving state-of-the-art Smatch scores of 86.4 on AMR2.0, 84.9 on AMR3.0, and 82.3 on BioAMR.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the application of instruction fine-tuned language models, specifically FLAN-T5, to the task of AMR parsing. By fine-tuning FLAN-T5 models on AMR datasets, the authors demonstrate substantial improvements over previous state-of-the-art models across multiple AMR benchmarks. The paper also explores parameter-efficient fine-tuning with LoRA, showing that applying LoRA after full fine-tuning further enhances performance. These findings suggest that instruction fine-tuned models can effectively generalize to structured output tasks like AMR parsing.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune FLAN-T5 models (both large and XL variants) on AMR parsing tasks by treating AMR graph generation as a text-to-text problem. They serialize AMR graphs and add task prefixes like "amr generation" to the input text. The models are first fully fine-tuned on AMR datasets, then optionally fine-tuned with LoRA to improve performance. The approach leverages the instruction-following capabilities of FLAN-T5 models, which were pre-trained on 1.8K diverse tasks, to handle the structured output requirements of AMR parsing.

## Key Results
- FLAN-T5-large achieves Smatch scores of 86.4 on AMR2.0, 84.9 on AMR3.0, and 82.3 on BioAMR
- FLAN-T5-XL sets new state-of-the-art scores on all three AMR benchmarks
- LoRA fine-tuning after full fine-tuning further improves performance
- Instruction fine-tuning significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art models on all tested AMR tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Instruction fine-tuning on diverse tasks improves model generalization to unseen parsing tasks.
- **Mechanism**: FLAN-T5 models are pre-trained on 1.8K instruction-annotated tasks, which teaches the model to map natural language instructions to appropriate task outputs. This general mapping capability transfers to AMR parsing when fine-tuned on AMR data.
- **Core assumption**: The instruction-following capability learned from diverse tasks is not task-specific and can generalize to structured output tasks like AMR parsing.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "Instruction fine-tuned language models on a collection of instruction annotated datasets (FLAN) have shown highly effective to improve model performance and generalization to unseen tasks."
- **Break condition**: If the model cannot map instructions to structured outputs or if AMR parsing requires task-specific knowledge not present in instruction training.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: LoRA fine-tuning after full fine-tuning improves model performance by preventing overfitting.
- **Mechanism**: Full fine-tuning adapts all model parameters to the training data, which can lead to overfitting. LoRA then adds low-rank adaptations that provide additional capacity without overfitting, acting as a regularizer.
- **Core assumption**: The full fine-tuned model has learned general patterns but needs fine-tuning to adapt to specific task nuances without losing generalization.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "full fine-tuning followed by the parameter efficient fine-tuning, LoRA, further improves the model performances, setting new state-of-the-arts"
- **Break condition**: If LoRA's rank and alpha parameters are not optimal, or if the full fine-tuned model is already well-generalized and LoRA adds unnecessary complexity.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: FLAN-T5 models achieve strong performance due to their prefix language model design and instruction fine-tuning.
- **Mechanism**: As prefix language models, FLAN-T5 takes natural text prefix as input and produces continuation as output. This design, combined with instruction fine-tuning, enables the model to understand task specifications and generate appropriate outputs.
- **Core assumption**: The prefix language model architecture is well-suited for instruction-following tasks and can effectively handle the AMR parsing task when fine-tuned.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section] "They are prefix language models and achieve strong few-shot performance even compared to much larger models, such as PaLM 62B."
- **Break condition**: If the prefix language model architecture is not suitable for structured output tasks or if instruction fine-tuning does not generalize well to AMR parsing.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: FLAN-T5 model (T5-based encoder-decoder architecture) → Instruction fine-tuning on 1.8K tasks → AMR parsing fine-tuning → Optional LoRA fine-tuning
- **Critical path**: Input text with task prefix → FLAN-T5 encoder → AMR graph generation → Post-processing (de-serialization and wiki restoration)
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using FLAN-T5 vs. other models (BART), full fine-tuning vs. LoRA, different LoRA configurations (rank and alpha)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor Smatch scores, overfitting on training data, inability to generalize to test data
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Fine-tune FLAN-T5-large on AMR2.0 training data and evaluate on AMR2.0 test set
  2. Apply LoRA fine-tuning to the full fine-tuned model and compare performance
  3. Fine-tune FLAN-T5-XL on AMR3.0 training data and evaluate on AMR3.0 test set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Why does full fine-tuning followed by LoRA fine-tuning improve performance over full fine-tuning alone?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper observes this phenomenon but states "we do not yet understand exactly why this should be the case."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper conjectures that LoRA prevents overfitting but does not provide empirical evidence or detailed analysis to support this claim.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Experiments comparing model performance with different amounts of training data, ablation studies removing specific components of the LoRA fine-tuning process, or analysis of model behavior during training would help explain this improvement.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: Can instruction fine-tuning be effectively applied to other standard parsing tasks beyond AMR, such as Universal Dependencies or Semantic Role Labeling?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper notes that "a majority of standard parsing tasks including abstract meaning representation (AMR), universal dependency (UD), semantic role labeling (SRL) has been excluded from the FLAN collections for both model training and evaluations."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only tests FLAN-T5 on AMR parsing tasks, leaving the effectiveness of instruction fine-tuning for other parsing tasks unexplored.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Applying the same fine-tuning approach to UD and SRL tasks and comparing performance against state-of-the-art models would determine if instruction fine-tuning generalizes to other parsing tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the optimal configuration for LoRA fine-tuning in sequence-to-sequence tasks?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states "We have not explored the full range of adjustable parameters of LoRA, e.g. k and o projections in addition to q and v projections, or values other than 8 and 16 for LoRA rank and 32 and 64 for LoRA alpha."
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only tests limited LoRA configurations and does not perform comprehensive hyperparameter optimization.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic experiments varying all LoRA hyperparameters (rank, alpha, projection types) and comparing performance would identify optimal configurations for sequence-to-sequence tasks.

## Limitations
- The evaluation relies heavily on automatic Smatch metrics, which may not fully capture semantic equivalence in complex AMR structures
- The paper's core findings are based on a limited set of tasks (AMR2.0, AMR3.0, BioAMR) and may not generalize to other structured prediction tasks
- The optimal LoRA configurations (rank and alpha values) appear to be task-specific, suggesting the need for hyperparameter tuning on each new dataset

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core finding that FLAN-T5 models significantly outperform previous state-of-the-art models on AMR parsing tasks. This is well-supported by empirical results across multiple datasets and consistent improvements over baseline models.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that LoRA fine-tuning on full fine-tuned models provides additional performance gains. While results support this, the mechanism is primarily conjecture-based, and the optimal LoRA configurations vary across tasks.
- **Medium Confidence**: The assertion that instruction fine-tuning on diverse tasks generalizes to structured output tasks like AMR parsing. This is supported by empirical results but lacks ablation studies on the necessity of instruction fine-tuning versus standard fine-tuning.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study**: Conduct controlled experiments comparing FLAN-T5 models fine-tuned with standard fine-tuning versus instruction fine-tuning on AMR parsing tasks to isolate the contribution of instruction fine-tuning.

2. **Cross-Dataset Generalization**: Evaluate the models on additional structured prediction tasks (e.g., dependency parsing, semantic role labeling) to test the generalizability of the instruction fine-tuning approach beyond AMR parsing.

3. **Error Analysis**: Perform detailed error analysis on challenging AMR parsing cases where models fail, particularly focusing on differences between FLAN-T5 models and previous state-of-the-art models to understand the nature of improvements.
