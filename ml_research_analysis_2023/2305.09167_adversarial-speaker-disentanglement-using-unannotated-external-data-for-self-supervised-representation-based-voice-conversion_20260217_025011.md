# Adversarial Speaker Disentanglement Using Unannotated External Data for Self-supervised Representation Based Voice Conversion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09167
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09167
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Any-to-one SSL-based VC with adversarial training using external unannotated corpora achieves MOS scores of 4.183 (±0.193) for naturalness and 3.985 (±0.212) for similarity using Hubert Soft tokens, outperforming baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of residual speaker information in self-supervised learning (SSL) representations degrading timbre similarity and quality in voice conversion (VC). The authors propose an any-to-one SSL-based VC method with adversarial training using external unannotated corpora. Two auxiliary discriminators are introduced: a conversion discriminator to distinguish converted Mel-spectrograms and an embedding discriminator to detect speaker information in content embeddings. Experimental results show the proposed method achieves comparable similarity and higher naturalness than supervised CTC-ASR-based methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of adversarial training for speaker disentanglement.

## Method Summary
The method employs adversarial training with external unannotated data to improve SSL-based VC by removing residual speaker information. It introduces a conversion discriminator that distinguishes whether Mel-spectrograms have been converted using external corpora, and an embedding discriminator that identifies speaker information in content embeddings from external sources. The approach uses SSL models (HuBERT, WavLM, Hubert Soft) as feature extractors without requiring annotated data, making it a data-efficient alternative to supervised methods.

## Key Results
- Proposed method achieves MOS scores of 4.183 (±0.193) for naturalness and 3.985 (±0.212) for similarity using Hubert Soft tokens
- Outperforms baseline without adversarial training in both naturalness and similarity metrics
- Achieves comparable similarity and higher naturalness than supervised CTC-ASR-based methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The adversarial training framework effectively removes residual speaker information from SSL representations by introducing two discriminators that work against the preservation of speaker characteristics. The conversion discriminator ensures the acoustic model produces converted speech that is indistinguishable from target speaker data, while the embedding discriminator forces the content encoder to produce speaker-independent representations by trying to fool the discriminator into classifying external speaker embeddings as non-speaker information.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)**: Learning representations without explicit labels - needed for data-efficient feature extraction; quick check: model can generate meaningful embeddings from raw audio
2. **Voice Conversion (VC)**: Transforming speech to match a target speaker's voice while preserving content - needed for speaker identity transfer; quick check: converted speech maintains linguistic content but changes timbre
3. **Adversarial Training**: Training models to compete against discriminators - needed to remove unwanted information; quick check: discriminator accuracy drops below random chance
4. **Speaker Disentanglement**: Separating speaker identity from linguistic content - needed for clean feature extraction; quick check: representations are invariant to speaker changes
5. **Mel-spectrograms**: Time-frequency representations of audio - needed as intermediate representation; quick check: visual inspection shows clear speech patterns
6. **Embedding Discrimination**: Classifying whether embeddings contain speaker information - needed for speaker information detection; quick check: discriminator achieves high accuracy on speaker vs non-speaker embeddings

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Audio -> SSL Feature Extractor -> Content Encoder -> Acoustic Model -> Conversion Discriminator & Embedding Discriminator -> Output Audio

Critical path: The feature extraction and content encoding stages are critical, as they must produce speaker-independent representations for successful conversion. The adversarial discriminators must be carefully balanced to avoid destabilizing training.

Design tradeoffs: Using external unannotated data provides flexibility but introduces domain mismatch risks. The any-to-one conversion simplifies the problem but limits practical applicability compared to many-to-many scenarios.

Failure signatures: Poor naturalness or similarity scores indicate either insufficient adversarial training or domain mismatch between external data and target domain. Discriminator collapse or mode collapse can occur if training is unstable.

First experiments: (1) Train with only the conversion discriminator, (2) Train with only the embedding discriminator, (3) Test with different SSL models (HuBERT vs WavLM vs Hubert Soft) to identify optimal feature extractor.

## Limitations
- The method focuses on any-to-one conversion rather than many-to-many scenarios, limiting real-world applicability
- Performance gains depend on the quality and diversity of external corpora, raising questions about scalability
- Evaluation relies on subjective MOS scores which can be influenced by listener bias and sample size

## Confidence
- Adversarial training effectively removes residual speaker information: Medium - results are promising but not conclusively proven across diverse datasets
- Higher naturalness than supervised methods: High - significant MOS score improvements demonstrate clear advantage
- Comparable similarity to supervised methods: Medium - similarity scores are close to baseline but not definitively superior

## Next Checks
1. Test the method on diverse external corpora to assess robustness and generalizability across different domains and recording conditions
2. Evaluate the method in many-to-many VC scenarios to broaden practical applicability beyond any-to-one conversion
3. Conduct ablation studies to isolate the impact of each adversarial component on performance and identify which discriminator contributes most to improvements
