# Continual Pre-training of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03241
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03241
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 15
- One-line primary result: Proposed DAS method achieves negative forgetting rate (-1.09) on six domain corpora while outperforming existing continual learning approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses continual domain-adaptive pre-training (DAP-training) of language models, where the goal is to incrementally adapt a pre-trained model to new domains while avoiding catastrophic forgetting and encouraging knowledge transfer. The proposed method, DAS, introduces a soft-masking mechanism that controls updates to the model based on the importance of units (attention heads or neurons) for preserving general and previously learned domain knowledge. DAS also employs a contrastive loss to integrate new domain knowledge with existing knowledge and uses a proxy KL-divergence loss to compute the importance of units for general knowledge when pre-training data is unavailable.

## Method Summary
DAS uses a soft-masking mechanism based on unit importance scores to control parameter updates during continual DAP-training. It computes importance for general knowledge using a proxy KL-divergence loss based on model robustness, and importance for domain knowledge using gradient magnitude. The method applies soft-masking during training (scaling gradients by 1 - I_{≤t-1}) and uses a contrastive loss to encourage complementary representations between previously learned and current domain knowledge. The final loss combines MLM loss with contrastive loss: L_{DAP-train} = L_{MLM} + λL_{contrast}.

## Key Results
- DAS achieves negative forgetting rate of -1.09, indicating successful knowledge transfer across domains
- Outperforms existing methods (RoBERTa, Post, NCL, DEMIX, BCL, CLASSIC, KD, EWC, DER++, HAT) on six domain corpora and corresponding end-task datasets
- Effective on both general-domain data (Wiki) and domain-specific data for computing importance scores

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Soft-masking based on unit importance preserves learned knowledge during continual DAP-training.
- Mechanism: The system computes importance scores for units (attention heads/neurons) based on their contribution to general knowledge (via proxy KL-divergence) or domain knowledge (via gradient magnitude). These scores act as soft masks (values in [0,1]) that scale gradients during backpropagation, reducing updates to important units.
- Core assumption: Units with higher importance scores are more critical for preserving specific knowledge, and soft-masking these units will prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The key novelty of our method is a soft-masking mechanism that directly controls the update to the LM."
  - [section]: "This is achieved by soft-masking the learning based on accumulated importance as follows... ˆ∇l = (1− I (≤t−1) l )⊗∇l"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence provided for soft-masking effectiveness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Contrastive learning between previously learned and full knowledge encourages knowledge integration.
- Mechanism: The system contrasts representations from the full network (including current domain) with representations from the masked network (preserving previous knowledge). This pushes the model to learn complementary representations that integrate new domain knowledge with existing knowledge.
- Core assumption: Contrasting these two representations will encourage the model to learn knowledge not already present in previous domains, leading to better integration.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Additionally, it contrasts the representations of the previously learned domain knowledge (including the general knowledge in the pre-trained LM) and the knowledge from the current full network to achieve knowledge integration."
  - [section]: "We contrast the previously learned knowledge and the full knowledge... to encourage complementary representation."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence provided for contrastive learning effectiveness in this specific context.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The proxy KL-divergence loss effectively computes unit importance for general knowledge without pre-training data.
- Mechanism: Since pre-training data is unavailable, the system uses a proxy based on model robustness - it measures the KL-divergence between model outputs with different dropout masks on a subset of current domain data to estimate which units are important for general knowledge.
- Core assumption: Units that cause larger changes in model output when perturbed (measured by dropout) are more important for preserving general knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "A novel proxy is also proposed to preserve the general knowledge in the original LM."
  - [section]: "To compute the robustness of the LM, we take a subset of the current domain data... and input xsub n twice to the LM to obtain two representations... and then compute the KL-divergence between them."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence provided for proxy KL-divergence effectiveness.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Transformer-based language model (RoBERTa) -> Virtual parameters for computing unit importance -> Soft-masking layer that scales gradients based on importance scores -> Contrastive learning module for knowledge integration -> MLM head for domain-adaptive pre-training

- Critical path:
  1. Initialization: Compute importance of units (attention heads/neurons) to general knowledge in LM using proxy KL-divergence
  2. For each new domain: 
     - Apply soft-masking using accumulated importance scores
     - Train with MLM + contrastive loss
     - Update importance scores for current domain
  3. End-task fine-tuning: Evaluate on downstream tasks without domain-ID

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Soft-masking vs. hard masking: Soft-masking allows some flexibility in updates while preserving important knowledge
  - Contrastive learning vs. direct knowledge preservation: Contrastive learning encourages integration rather than just preservation
  - Proxy method vs. direct importance computation: The proxy method enables computation without pre-training data

- Failure signatures:
  - High forgetting rate indicates soft-masking is not effectively preserving knowledge
  - Poor end-task performance suggests contrastive learning is not achieving proper knowledge integration
  - Unstable training may indicate importance scores are not being computed correctly

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test soft-masking effectiveness: Train with random importance scores vs. computed importance scores
  2. Validate proxy method: Compare results using proxy KL-divergence vs. MLM loss on a sample of pre-training data (if available)
  3. Assess contrastive learning: Train with and without contrastive loss to measure impact on knowledge integration

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of proxy dataset (e.g., Wiki vs. actual pre-training data) impact the effectiveness of the initialization function in identifying important units for general knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compare DAS using Wiki data as a proxy dataset with MLM and KL-divergence losses, showing DAS (Wiki + KL) outperforms DAS (Wiki + MLM).
- Why unresolved: The study only uses Wiki data as a proxy for the actual pre-training data, and the authors acknowledge that using the actual pre-training data would provide a more direct comparison.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing DAS initialized with importance scores computed from the actual pre-training data (if available) versus various proxy datasets (e.g., Wiki, other large general corpora) would reveal the impact of proxy dataset choice on identifying important units for general knowledge.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the size of the subset of data used for computing the importance of units to the general knowledge in the initialization function affect the performance of DAS?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention using a subset of the current domain data for computing the importance of units to the general knowledge, but do not explore the impact of subset size on performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide information on how the size of the subset affects the accuracy of importance scores and, consequently, the performance of DAS.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the size of the subset used for computing importance scores in the initialization function and evaluating the resulting DAS performance would reveal the optimal subset size for accurate importance estimation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed contrastive learning mechanism in DAS compare to other knowledge integration techniques, such as knowledge distillation or replay-based methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors claim that DAS's contrastive learning mechanism encourages complementary representations of the current domain and previously learned knowledge, leading to effective knowledge integration.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not directly compare DAS's contrastive learning approach to other knowledge integration techniques commonly used in continual learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing DAS with variants that use knowledge distillation or replay-based methods for knowledge integration would reveal the relative effectiveness of the contrastive learning approach.

## Limitations
- The proxy KL-divergence method for computing general knowledge importance relies on assumptions about dropout robustness that aren't directly tested against unavailable pre-training data
- Contrastive learning effectiveness is demonstrated only through end-task performance rather than direct measurement of complementary representation learning
- Soft-masking mechanism's theoretical justification could benefit from more rigorous ablation studies showing individual component contributions

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Overall experimental methodology is sound with proper evaluation on multiple domains and end-tasks
- Medium Confidence: Claims about knowledge transfer are supported by experimental results but underlying mechanisms need more direct validation
- Low Confidence: Effectiveness of proxy KL-divergence method for computing general knowledge importance without pre-training data remains uncertain

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study of soft-masking effectiveness**: Compare DAS performance against versions using random importance scores, uniform masking, and hard masking to isolate the contribution of the soft-masking mechanism to preventing catastrophic forgetting.

2. **Direct validation of proxy KL-divergence**: If any pre-training data samples are available (even a small subset), compare the proxy method's importance scores against those computed using actual MLM loss to assess the accuracy of the robustness-based proxy.

3. **Analysis of contrastive learning contribution**: Implement and evaluate a variant of DAS without the contrastive loss to quantify its specific impact on knowledge integration, and examine the learned representations to verify that they are indeed complementary rather than redundant.
