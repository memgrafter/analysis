# Coin Sampling: Gradient-Based Bayesian Inference without Learning Rates

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11294
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11294
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Coin Sampling eliminates learning rates in ParVI methods while maintaining performance through coin-betting strategies

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Coin Sampling, a suite of parameter-free particle-based algorithms for scalable Bayesian inference. The key innovation replaces the learning rate in existing ParVI methods like SVGD with a coin-betting strategy, eliminating the need for manual tuning while maintaining theoretical convergence guarantees under log-concavity assumptions. Empirical evaluation demonstrates that Coin Sampling achieves performance comparable to or better than tuned ParVI methods across synthetic and real-world tasks including ICA, logistic regression, Bayesian neural networks, and probabilistic matrix factorization.

## Method Summary
Coin Sampling replaces the learning rate parameter in ParVI methods with coin-betting strategies, where each particle acts as a gambler betting on subgradients of the functional being minimized. The algorithm uses Krichevsky-Trofimov estimators to guarantee wealth accumulation that bounds regret, which translates to convergence without requiring step size parameters. The framework maintains convergence under assumptions of bounded subgradients and geodesically convex functionals, with theoretical analysis establishing non-asymptotic convergence rates. The approach is applied to several variants including Coin SVGD, Coin LAWGD, and Coin KSDD, each adapting the coin-betting mechanism to different variational inference settings.

## Key Results
- Coin Sampling achieves performance comparable to or better than tuned ParVI methods without requiring learning rate specification
- In high-dimensional ICA experiments, Coin SVGD increasingly outperforms SVGD with the original optimal learning rate as dimensionality increases
- Across UCI datasets and Covertype classification, Coin Sampling matches or exceeds baseline performance in test accuracy and negative log-likelihood
- The parameter-free nature of Coin Sampling is particularly advantageous in high-dimensional settings where optimal learning rates are difficult to identify
