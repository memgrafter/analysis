# Check-COVID: Fact-Checking COVID-19 News Claims with Scientific Evidence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18265
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18265
- Reference count: 18
- Key outcome: Check-COVID dataset achieves F1 scores of 76.99 (fact-checking system) and 69.90 (GPT-3.5) on COVID-19 claim verification task

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Check-COVID, a new benchmark for fact-checking COVID-19 news claims using scientific evidence. The dataset contains 1,504 claims from news articles paired with sentence-level evidence from scientific abstracts, along with veracity labels (support, refute, not enough info). The task requires bridging the gap between everyday language and formal academic terminology while leveraging commonsense knowledge. Experiments demonstrate the challenge of the task and highlight the importance of in-domain data for COVID-19 fact-checking.

## Method Summary
The Check-COVID dataset was created by collecting COVID-19 claims from news articles and pairing them with relevant evidence sentences from scientific abstracts. Claims were labeled as support, refute, or not enough info based on whether the evidence confirmed, contradicted, or was insufficient to verify the claim. The dataset was used to train a fact-checking system using RoBERTa models fine-tuned sequentially on FEVER, SciFact, and Check-COVID, as well as to evaluate GPT-3.5's performance on the task.

## Key Results
- Fact-checking system achieves F1 score of 76.99 on Check-COVID dataset
- GPT-3.5 achieves F1 score of 69.90 on the same task
- NOTENOUGHINFO label accounts for real-world scenarios where evidence is insufficient
- Dataset and models publicly available at https://github.com/posuer/Check-COVID

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Check-COVID aligns everyday language with formal academic terminology
- Mechanism: The dataset pairs claims written in colloquial news language with evidence sentences from scientific abstracts, forcing models to learn semantic mappings between domains
- Core assumption: Models can learn cross-domain semantic mappings through exposure to aligned claim-evidence pairs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our data and models are released publicly at https://github.com/posuer/Check-COVID"
  - [section] "it requires grounding everyday vernacular in formal academic language"
- Break condition: If the vocabulary gap between news claims and scientific evidence becomes too large, models fail to establish meaningful alignments

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training on multiple datasets improves transfer learning for COVID-19 fact-checking
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning RoBERTa-Large models sequentially on FEVER, SciFact, and Check-COVID allows models to leverage general fact-checking patterns before specializing to COVID-19 domain
- Core assumption: General fact-checking patterns transfer across domains with minimal catastrophic forgetting
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments using both a fact-checking specific system and GPT-3.5, which respectively achieve F1 scores of 76.99 and 69.90 on this task"
  - [section] "we fine-tune RoBERTa-Large models sequentially on FEVER and/or SciFact and then on Check-COVID"
- Break condition: If domain shift between source datasets and COVID-19 claims is too large, transfer learning degrades performance

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Including NOTENOUGHINFO label improves real-world performance
- Mechanism: By explicitly modeling cases where evidence is insufficient, the system avoids overconfident predictions on unverifiable claims
- Core assumption: Real-world fact-checking scenarios include many cases where evidence is unavailable or insufficient
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also introduce a third label, NOTENOUGHINFO"
  - [section] "In fact-checking, often there is not adequate evidence to confidently certify or debunk a claim"
- Break condition: If the system predicts NOTENOUGHINFO too frequently, it fails to provide useful judgments even when evidence exists

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Semantic similarity between claims and evidence
  - Why needed here: The task requires matching claims written in everyday language to evidence written in scientific jargon
  - Quick check question: How would you measure similarity between "cloth masks are less effective" and "respirable size fraction of aerosol particles"?

- Concept: Temporal and numeric reasoning
  - Why needed here: Many claims involve comparing time periods or numerical values that require domain knowledge to interpret
  - Quick check question: Given evidence about testing frequency, how would you determine if "twice a week" is more frequent than "every two weeks"?

- Concept: Evidence selection from multiple sentences
  - Why needed here: Claims often require multiple evidence sentences to be fact-checked accurately
  - Quick check question: When would you need more than one sentence from an abstract to verify a claim?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Vespa (abstract retrieval) → RoBERTa rationale selector → RoBERTa label predictor
- Critical path: Abstract retrieval must succeed for subsequent components to work
- Design tradeoffs: Vespa provides up-to-date retrieval but introduces variability vs. static corpus approach
- Failure signatures: Low recall in rationale selection suggests Vespa retrieval issues; low precision suggests model overfitting
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test Vespa retrieval quality by checking if gold evidence abstracts appear in top-3 results
  2. Evaluate rationale selection on oracle abstracts to isolate retrieval vs. selection issues
  3. Test label prediction on gold rationales to verify final classification quality

## Limitations
- Small dataset size (1,504 claims) may not represent broader COVID-19 misinformation landscape
- Manual annotation process for NOTENOUGHINFO label introduces potential subjectivity
- Claims about difficulty requiring commonsense knowledge lack empirical validation

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The dataset creation methodology and the inclusion of the NOTENOUGHINFO label are well-supported by the paper's methodology section and align with established fact-checking practices.

**Medium Confidence**: The reported F1 scores and comparative performance between the fact-checking system and GPT-3.5 are based on the described experimental setup, but the small dataset size and lack of external validation limit generalizability.

**Low Confidence**: The paper's claims about the difficulty of the task requiring "commonsense knowledge and temporal/numeric reasoning" are asserted but not empirically validated through targeted experiments or ablation studies.

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset Representativeness Analysis**: Conduct statistical analysis comparing the 1,504 claims to broader COVID-19 misinformation datasets to assess coverage and potential sampling bias.

2. **Annotation Agreement Study**: Measure inter-annotator agreement rates for the NOTENOUGHINFO label across a subset of claims to quantify subjectivity in the labeling process.

3. **Cross-Domain Transfer Evaluation**: Test the fact-checking system on claims from different domains (e.g., climate change, politics) to evaluate whether the claimed semantic alignment capability generalizes beyond COVID-19.
