# BioREx: Improving Biomedical Relation Extraction by Leveraging Heterogeneous Datasets

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11189
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11189
- Reference count: 40
- BioREx achieves 79.6% F1 score on BioRED corpus, outperforming baseline of 74.4%

## Executive Summary
This work presents BioREx, a data-centric approach to improve biomedical relation extraction (RE) by leveraging heterogeneous datasets. The key challenge is that existing RE datasets are small and domain-specific, limiting the development of generalized and high-performing RE models. To address this, BioREx proposes a harmonization framework that systematically integrates heterogeneous annotations from different datasets into one large dataset. BioREx then trains a deep learning classifier using a BERT-based pre-trained language model. Evaluation on the BioRED corpus shows that BioREx significantly outperforms the baseline system, achieving a new SOTA F1 score of 79.6% compared to 74.4%. BioREx also improves performance on five other RE tasks and demonstrates robustness and generalizability on two independent tasks not seen during training.

## Method Summary
BioREx employs a five-step framework: determining the target task, collecting heterogeneous datasets, adjusting for consistency through harmonization, training a deep learning classifier using PubMedBERT with prompt-based input format, and applying the model to new articles. The harmonization framework addresses five categories of annotation differences including entity spans, document/sentence level distinctions, negative vs. unannotated instances, granular relation types, and entity definitions. The model incorporates entity boundary tags and prompt questions to provide contextual guidance for relation extraction.

## Key Results
- BioREx achieves 79.6% F1 score on BioRED test set, surpassing baseline of 74.4%
- Improves performance on five other RE tasks when using BioRED as external support data
- Demonstrates robustness and generalizability on two independent tasks (N-ary and RENET) not seen during training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Harmonizing heterogeneous annotations increases model performance by reducing conflicting signals
- Mechanism: The framework identifies five categories of annotation differences and applies specific adjustments to each, creating a consistent dataset for training
- Core assumption: Dataset heterogeneity directly causes performance degradation in multi-task models
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "systematic addressing the data heterogeneity of individual datasets and combining them into a large dataset"
  - [section 3.3] "Five solutions are provided to adjust the different datasets as shown in Table 1"
- Break condition: If harmonization introduces annotation noise or incorrect mappings, performance may degrade

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pre-training on a large heterogeneous dataset provides better general representations than fine-tuning individual task-specific models
- Mechanism: The model is first trained on the combined BioREx dataset, then fine-tuned for specific tasks, leveraging knowledge from multiple relation types and contexts
- Core assumption: Diverse training data leads to better generalization than task-specific training
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "BioREx significantly outperforms the baseline system... BioREx also improves performance on five other RE tasks"
- Break condition: If the combined dataset contains too much noise or conflicting information, pre-training may hurt performance

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using prompt-based input with entity boundary tags improves relation extraction by providing clear context
- Mechanism: The model adds entity tags (<D>, </D>, etc.) and a prompt question to emphasize the target entities and task, helping the PLM focus on relevant information
- Core assumption: Explicit entity boundaries and task prompts improve model attention and classification accuracy
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4] "For each instance, two boundary tags are inserted at the beginning and the end of the entities... We also added those tags to the vocabulary"
- Break condition: If prompts introduce additional noise or if the PLM already effectively uses entity context without explicit tags

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data harmonization module -> BERT-based PLM -> Classification head -> Fine-tuning module
- Critical path: Data harmonization → PLM training on combined dataset → Fine-tuning for target task → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Dataset size vs. annotation quality: Larger combined dataset may introduce noise from less reliable sources
  - Prompt complexity vs. model performance: More detailed prompts may improve accuracy but increase input length
  - Pre-training vs. fine-tuning balance: Too much pre-training may reduce task-specific adaptation capability
- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation when adding certain datasets suggests annotation incompatibility
  - High false negative rates for low-frequency entities indicates insufficient context capture
  - Inconsistent predictions across similar entity pairs suggests prompt or tag issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train BioREx on BioRED only, then on BioRED + one additional dataset, compare performance
  2. Train with and without entity boundary tags and prompts, compare performance
  3. Train on subsets of BioRED of different sizes with and without external datasets, evaluate performance vs. annotation effort

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well does the BioREx approach perform on biomedical relation extraction tasks beyond the gene, disease, and chemical entities used in the BioRED dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of BioREx on two independent tasks not previously seen in training data: drug-drug N-ary combination and document-level gene-disease RE
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows promising results on these two tasks, it does not evaluate the generalizability of BioREx to other types of biomedical entities or relation extraction tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Additional experiments applying BioREx to other biomedical relation extraction tasks with different entity types, such as protein-protein interactions or gene-drug interactions, would provide evidence for the generalizability of the approach

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the harmonization framework on the performance of BioREx?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes a harmonization framework to address the heterogeneity of individual datasets and combine them into a large dataset
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the specific impact of the harmonization framework on the performance of BioREx
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies that evaluate the performance of BioREx with and without specific components of the harmonization framework would provide insights into the impact of each component

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of BioREx compare to other state-of-the-art methods on biomedical relation extraction tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares the performance of BioREx to transfer learning and multi-task learning approaches, as well as the original BioRED system
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides comparisons to some existing methods, it does not evaluate BioREx against other state-of-the-art approaches specifically designed for biomedical relation extraction
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that compare the performance of BioREx to other state-of-the-art methods on benchmark biomedical relation extraction datasets would provide a comprehensive assessment of its effectiveness

## Limitations
- Lack of ablation studies to quantify individual contributions of harmonization steps to performance gains
- No direct comparison to non-prompted versions to validate the specific benefits of prompt-based input
- Unspecified training hyperparameters and proprietary nature of BioRED corpus limit reproducibility
