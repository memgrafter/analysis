# Exploration in Model-based Reinforcement Learning with Randomized Reward

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03142
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03142
- Reference count: 40
- This paper establishes the first worst-case regret analysis on randomized MBRL with function approximation

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates exploration in model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) using randomized reward functions. The authors propose PlanEx, an algorithm that performs exploration by iteratively planning with a fitted transition model and a randomized reward function. Under the kernelized linear regulator (KNR) model, reward randomization guarantees partial optimism, leading to near-optimal worst-case regret in terms of the number of interactions. The authors extend their theory to general function approximation and identify conditions for reward randomization to achieve provably efficient exploration.

## Method Summary
PlanEx consists of three steps in each iteration: (i) Model Fitting - fitting the transition parameter Wk by minimizing the prediction error, (ii) Planning - deriving a policy πk that balances exploitation and exploration using a perturbed reward function, and (iii) Interaction - executing the policy πk and collecting a trajectory to update the replay buffer. The algorithm uses reward randomization with noise scaled by model uncertainty (Λ⁻¹) to achieve partial optimism, ensuring that at least 16% of value function estimates exceed the true optimal value.

## Key Results
- Reward randomization guarantees partial optimism, yielding near-optimal worst-case regret
- Achieves O(√K) regret under KNR model with proper noise scaling
- Extends theory to general function approximation with identified conditions for efficient reward randomization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Reward randomization with Gaussian noise introduces partial optimism, ensuring that at least a constant proportion of value function estimates exceed the true optimal value.
- Mechanism: The algorithm perturbs rewards with noise whose variance scales with model uncertainty (Λ⁻¹). This makes the perturbed reward larger than the true reward with probability proportional to Φ(-1) ≈ 0.16, guaranteeing optimism in at least 16% of iterations.
- Core assumption: The transition model estimation error is bounded (good event G_w holds) and the noise variance scales appropriately with Λ⁻¹.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "reward randomization guarantees a partial optimism, which further yields a near-optimal worst-case regret", [section] Lemma 4 and its proof showing Vπ*(s1) - Vπk(s1) ≤ 0 with probability at least Φ(-1).

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The algorithm achieves near-optimal O(√K) regret by combining partial optimism with bounded estimation error.
- Mechanism: While only a fraction of iterations are optimistic, the algorithm bounds the cumulative regret by separating it into three components: optimism loss, estimation error, and maximum possible reward. The partial optimism ensures sufficient exploration, while the bounded estimation error keeps the regret accumulation under control.
- Core assumption: The transition model estimation error concentrates properly (as shown in Lemma 19) and the regret can be decomposed as shown in Theorem 5.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "near-optimal worst-case regret in terms of the number of interactions", [section] Theorem 5 showing E[R(K)] = O((dS + dφ)^(3/2)·H^(7/2)·log²(K)·√K).

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The reward randomization approach generalizes to general function approximation through calibrated model assumptions and specific reward perturbation designs.
- Mechanism: Under the calibrated model assumption (P(·|s,a) - Pk(·|s,a) ≤ β(δ)·ιk(s,a)), the algorithm uses reward randomization that satisfies optimism and concentration conditions. Examples include Gaussian perturbation and Bernoulli perturbation, both designed to ensure exploration while maintaining bounded regret.
- Core assumption: The transition dynamics can be estimated with bounded error relative to some complexity measure ιk, and the reward randomization can be designed to satisfy the two key conditions.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We further extend our theory to generalized function approximation and identified conditions for reward randomization to attain provably efficient exploration", [section] Theorem 9 and the two examples showing how different randomization schemes satisfy the conditions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Model Fitting -> Planning -> Interaction -> (back to Model Fitting)
- Critical path: The critical path is the iterative loop: collect data → fit model → randomize rewards → plan policy → execute and collect more data. Each iteration depends on the previous model fitting and data collection.
- Design tradeoffs: The algorithm trades off between exploration (through reward randomization) and exploitation (through model-based planning). The noise scaling parameter σk controls this tradeoff - too little noise leads to insufficient exploration, while too much noise makes the rewards uninformative.
- Failure signatures: If the algorithm fails to explore properly, you'll see the regret growing faster than √K. If the model fitting fails to converge, the noise scaling will become unstable. If the planning oracle is poor, even good models and reward randomization won't help.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the basic KNR model fitting and verify that the confidence bounds βk behave as expected on synthetic data
  2. Test the reward randomization with known transition dynamics to verify that partial optimism occurs with the expected probability Φ(-1)
  3. Run the full algorithm on a simple control task (e.g., linear quadratic regulator) to verify the √K regret scaling before moving to more complex environments

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can reward randomization be theoretically proven to achieve optimal worst-case regret in model-based reinforcement learning with function approximation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that reward randomization guarantees partial optimism, which yields near-optimal worst-case regret, but questions remain about its optimality in general function approximation.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides a near-optimal worst-case regret analysis under specific conditions (e.g., KNR model), but the extension to general function approximation is not fully explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive theoretical analysis demonstrating that reward randomization achieves optimal worst-case regret across various function approximation settings, including non-linear and high-dimensional cases.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the specific conditions under which reward randomization outperforms traditional bonus-based exploration methods in model-based reinforcement learning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests that reward randomization can be more modular and easier to implement compared to bonus-based approaches, but does not provide a direct comparison of their performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical evidence or theoretical guarantees that reward randomization consistently outperforms bonus-based methods across different environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the performance of reward randomization and bonus-based exploration methods in various model-based RL tasks, along with theoretical bounds on their regret.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of reward perturbation distribution (e.g., Gaussian vs. Bernoulli) affect the efficiency of exploration in model-based reinforcement learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes both Gaussian and Bernoulli perturbations as examples of valid reward randomization, but does not analyze their relative effectiveness.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comparative analysis of different perturbation distributions or their impact on exploration efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the exploration efficiency of different reward perturbation distributions, including their impact on regret bounds and empirical performance.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies heavily on the calibrated model assumption, which requires bounded estimation error relative to a complexity measure ιk
- Only a constant fraction (Φ(-1) ≈ 16%) of iterations are guaranteed to be optimistic, which could lead to slow convergence in some environments
- Practical performance relative to state-of-the-art MBRL methods is not evaluated empirically

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The core mechanism of reward randomization with Gaussian noise (Mechanism 1) is well-established mathematically, with clear proofs showing the Φ(-1) optimism probability. The regret decomposition in Theorem 5 follows standard techniques in bandit literature.
- **Medium Confidence:** The extension to general function approximation (Mechanism 3) depends on the calibrated model assumption holding in practice. While the theoretical conditions are satisfied in the examples provided, real-world applicability may vary.
- **Low Confidence:** The practical performance of the algorithm relative to state-of-the-art MBRL methods is not evaluated empirically in the paper, making it difficult to assess real-world utility.
