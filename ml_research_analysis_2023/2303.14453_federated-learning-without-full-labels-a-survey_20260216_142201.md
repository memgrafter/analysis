# Federated Learning without Full Labels: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14453
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14453
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comprehensive survey of federated learning methods handling scenarios without full labels, categorizing approaches into federated semi-supervised learning, federated self-supervised learning, and federated transfer learning.

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of federated learning (FL) methods that can handle scenarios without full labels. It categorizes approaches into three main paradigms: federated semi-supervised learning, federated self-supervised learning, and federated transfer learning. The survey highlights key challenges including data heterogeneity, privacy protection, and the tradeoff between efficiency and performance. It also summarizes commonly used datasets for evaluation and discusses future research directions such as trustworthiness, generalization to unseen domains, and automatic FL methods.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically categorizes federated learning methods without full labels into three paradigms. Federated semi-supervised learning addresses scenarios where some clients have labeled data while others have only unlabeled data, using techniques like pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization. Federated self-supervised learning leverages unlabeled data through contrastive learning and other self-supervised techniques. Federated transfer learning focuses on scenarios where labeled data from source domains can be leveraged to improve learning on target domains with limited labels. The survey analyzes the key challenges and mechanisms in each paradigm, including data heterogeneity, privacy protection, and efficiency-performance tradeoffs.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Label isolation can be mitigated by having clients train with unlabeled data while servers aggregate labeled data.
- Mechanism: In the label-at-client setting, clients with labeled data train locally, and the server only has access to unlabeled data. The server generates pseudo-labels for the unlabeled data using its labeled data, and clients are trained on both their labeled data and the pseudo-labeled unlabeled data.
- Core assumption: The server has sufficient labeled data to generate accurate pseudo-labels for the unlabeled data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In existing efforts of FL [12, 13, 14], it is assumed that all data held by all participants are fully labeled, and that a supervised learning problem is to be solved. However, the assumption may not hold in practice for two reasons."
  - [section] "In the label-at-client setting, the labeled data are located at the clients, while the server only has access to unlabeled data."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors focus on general FL surveys and blockchain-based FL, not specifically on label isolation.
- Break condition: If the server does not have sufficient labeled data to generate accurate pseudo-labels, the model performance will degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data heterogeneity can be addressed by using personalized models or domain adaptation techniques.
- Mechanism: In federated self-supervised learning, each participant can train a personalized model on their local data, which is then aggregated with other participants' models. Alternatively, domain adaptation techniques can be used to align the feature representations across different participants.
- Core assumption: The participants' data distributions are not too dissimilar, and there is some overlap in the feature space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Data heterogeneity, i.e. the local data held by different participants have different data distributions, is an important property in FL that causes accuracy degradation [13, 14]."
  - [section] "Data heterogeneity also poses challenges in the problem of FL without full labels. For example, as the number of labeled data is limited, local models tend to overfit the local data more easily, which causes a greater amount of weight divergence [37] and performance degradation."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors do not discuss data heterogeneity in the context of FL without full labels.
- Break condition: If the participants' data distributions are too dissimilar, personalized models or domain adaptation techniques may not be effective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Privacy can be protected by using homomorphic encryption or secret sharing techniques.
- Mechanism: In federated transfer learning, homomorphic encryption can be used to encrypt the feature representations of the source and target domains, allowing for secure aggregation and model training. Alternatively, secret sharing techniques can be used to split the data into multiple shares, which are then distributed among the participants.
- Core assumption: The participants have sufficient computational resources to perform homomorphic encryption or secret sharing operations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In the problem of FL without full labels, the number of labeled data is often limited. Therefore, participants have to repetitively access and exchange information about them to exploit the knowledge in the labels. This leads to risks of privacy leakage of the labeled data."
  - [section] "To protect data privacy, the whole training process is protected with homomorphic encryption."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus neighbors do not discuss privacy protection techniques in the context of FL without full labels.
- Break condition: If the participants do not have sufficient computational resources, homomorphic encryption or secret sharing techniques may not be feasible.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Clients -> Server (communication channel) -> Clients

- Critical path:
  1. Clients perform local model training on their data.
  2. Clients send the updated model parameters to the server.
  3. Server aggregates the received model parameters to obtain the global model.
  4. Server sends the global model back to the clients.
  5. Repeat steps 1-4 for multiple communication rounds.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Privacy vs. performance: Using techniques like homomorphic encryption or secret sharing can protect privacy but may impact model performance.
  - Communication cost vs. model accuracy: Reducing the frequency of communication rounds can save communication costs but may lead to lower model accuracy.

- Failure signatures:
  - If the model performance is poor, it could be due to label isolation, data heterogeneity, or privacy leakage.
  - If the communication costs are high, it could be due to frequent communication rounds or large model sizes.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a simple federated learning algorithm with fully labeled data to establish a baseline.
  2. Extend the baseline algorithm to handle unlabeled data using semi-supervised learning techniques.
  3. Evaluate the impact of data heterogeneity on model performance and experiment with techniques to mitigate it.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can federated semi-supervised learning methods effectively handle the label isolation problem when clients have only unlabeled data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies label isolation as a key challenge, stating "participants may not have sufficient domain expertise, or they lack the motivation and tools to label data" leading to "unlabeled data only" scenarios.
- Why unresolved: Existing methods like FedMatch and SemiFL propose disjoint learning and fine-tuning approaches, but the paper notes these can lead to catastrophic forgetting and performance degradation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different techniques for bridging knowledge between labeled and unlabeled data without data exchange, measuring both accuracy and privacy leakage.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective ways to balance performance and efficiency in federated learning with large-scale unlabeled data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights "the large-scale unlabeled data in the problem creates a tradeoff between performance and efficiency."
- Why unresolved: While AdaFedSemi proposes tuning participation rate and confidence threshold, the paper notes this requires careful parameter tuning and may not generalize across different scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different sampling strategies, communication reduction techniques, and adaptive parameter tuning methods across diverse datasets and FL settings.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can federated transfer learning methods effectively select the most appropriate source domain(s) for knowledge transfer without direct observation of source data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "selecting the most appropriate source domain poses an additional challenge compared to the single-source setting."
- Why unresolved: While methods like FADA propose gap statistics weighting, the paper notes these require intricate design or manual effort and may not generalize well.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical evaluations of different source domain selection criteria across diverse multi-source transfer learning scenarios, measuring both transfer effectiveness and computational overhead.
