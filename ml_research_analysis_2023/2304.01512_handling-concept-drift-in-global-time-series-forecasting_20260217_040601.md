# Handling Concept Drift in Global Time Series Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01512
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01512
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: GDW method significantly outperformed statistical benchmarks and LightGBM baselines across all concept drift types and evaluation metrics

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses concept drift in global time series forecasting by proposing two novel methods: Error Contribution Weighting (ECW) and Gradient Descent Weighting (GDW). These methods use two forecasting models trained separately on recent data and all data, respectively, and dynamically combine their weighted forecasts. The GDW method achieved significantly higher accuracy than statistical benchmarks and LightGBM baselines across sudden, incremental, and gradual concept drift scenarios.

## Method Summary
The proposed methods use two LightGBM models trained on different data subsets: one on recent data (partial model) and one on all available data (full model). These models are dynamically weighted based on prediction error (ECW) or gradient descent optimization (GDW). The final forecast is the weighted average of both models' predictions. The methods were evaluated on three simulated datasets representing different concept drift types using prequential evaluation with increasing block sizes.

## Key Results
- GDW method outperformed all baselines on mean RMSE, median RMSE, mean MAE, and median MAE across all concept drift types
- ECW and GDW methods achieved better accuracy than statistical benchmarks (AR3, AR5, ETS) and LightGBM baselines
- The methods successfully handled sudden, incremental, and gradual concept drift scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Two models trained on different time windows (recent vs. full history) can jointly adapt to concept drift while preserving general accuracy
- Mechanism: The partial model (recent data) quickly adapts to new concepts due to recency bias, while the full model (all data) maintains high accuracy on stable parts of the series. Their weighted average balances adaptation speed and general accuracy
- Core assumption: Recent observations are more relevant when concept drift occurs, and the two models' errors are complementary
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "These methods use two forecasting models which are separately trained with the most recent series and all series, and finally, the weighted average of the forecasts provided by the two models are considered as the final forecasts."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Error-based weighting (ECW) allows dynamic adjustment of model contributions based on recent prediction performance
- Mechanism: The method calculates prediction errors for both sub-models and assigns higher weights to the model with lower error. This creates a feedback loop where the better-performing model gains influence over time
- Core assumption: Prediction error is a reliable indicator of which model is currently more appropriate for the data distribution
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "These methods use two forecasting models which are separately trained with the most recent series and all series, and finally, the weighted average of the forecasts provided by the two models are considered as the final forecasts."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Gradient descent weighting (GDW) provides smoother, more stable weight updates compared to direct error contribution
- Mechanism: Instead of directly using error ratios, GDW uses gradient descent to update weights based on the gradient of the prediction error with respect to the weights, providing more stable convergence
- Core assumption: Gradient-based updates can prevent large weight swings and provide smoother adaptation to concept drift
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our proposed ECW and GDW methods are different based on the method of calculation of the sub-model weights."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Two LightGBM models (partial and full) -> Weight calculation modules (ECW or GDW) -> Error tracking system -> Forecast aggregation layer
- Critical path: 1. Train partial model on recent data 2. Train full model on all data 3. Generate predictions from both models 4. Calculate prediction errors 5. Compute model weights (ECW or GDW) 6. Combine forecasts using calculated weights
- Design tradeoffs: Model complexity vs. adaptation speed, Memory usage (storing two models) vs. accuracy, Weight calculation method (ECW vs. GDW) affects stability
- Failure signatures: Oscillating weights between models, One model consistently dominating weights, Poor performance on gradual concept drift
- First 3 experiments: 1. Implement ECW with exponential weighting on simulated sudden drift data 2. Compare ECW vs. GDW performance on incremental drift 3. Test weight stability with different learning rates in GDW

## Limitations
- Experiments limited to simulated datasets, no validation on real-world data
- Only tested with LightGBM as base learner, though methods claim applicability to other GFMs
- No exploration of using more than two sub-models for potentially improved performance
