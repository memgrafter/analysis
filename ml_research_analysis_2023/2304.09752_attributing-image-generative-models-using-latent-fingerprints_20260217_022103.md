# Attributing Image Generative Models using Latent Fingerprints

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09752
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09752
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: Latent fingerprints improve model attribution accuracy while preserving image quality by embedding binary-coded user keys as semantic perturbations in latent space.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces latent fingerprints as a method for attributing image generative models to their users. The approach embeds binary-coded user keys as subtle semantic perturbations along principal components of the latent space of pre-trained generators like StyleGAN2 and latent diffusion models. Unlike prior methods requiring additional training or architecture modifications, latent fingerprints need minimal computation and no training overhead, making them scalable to large models. Experiments demonstrate that latent fingerprints achieve high attribution accuracy (99% on StyleGAN2) while maintaining reasonable image quality (FID of 12.35 and PSNR of 21.11 dB), outperforming baseline methods that sacrifice quality for accuracy.

## Method Summary
The method embeds binary-coded user keys as semantic perturbations in the latent space of pre-trained generators. It extracts latent distribution samples from the generator, computes the covariance matrix, and performs PCA to identify semantic dimensions. User keys are embedded by perturbing latent codes along selected principal components with a strength parameter σ. For attribution, the method optimizes latent codes to match given images using LPIPS or postprocess-specific LPIPS metrics, with parallel search initialization to avoid local minima. The approach requires no additional training and minimal computation, making it scalable to large models.

## Key Results
- Achieves 99% attribution accuracy on StyleGAN2 with FID of 12.35 and PSNR of 21.11 dB
- Outperforms baseline shallow fingerprinting methods in accuracy-quality tradeoff
- Demonstrates effectiveness of latent semantic dimensions as fingerprints for robust model attribution

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Latent semantic dimensions serve as effective fingerprints because they enable semantic perturbations that preserve generation quality while maintaining attribution accuracy. The method embeds binary-coded user keys as subtle semantic perturbations along principal components of the latent space. By using directions that correspond to meaningful semantic changes in the image space, the perturbations can be made small enough to preserve visual quality while still being detectable by the attribution function. This works under the assumption that principal components of the latent distribution reveal disentangled semantic dimensions that enable smooth mapping to the content space.

### Mechanism 2
The accuracy-quality tradeoff is governed by the choice of fingerprinting dimensions, strength, and capacity. Using eigenvectors associated with larger eigenvalues of the latent space covariance matrix improves attribution accuracy but degrades generation quality. Conversely, using smaller eigenvalues preserves quality but reduces accuracy. This tradeoff is intrinsic and cannot be eliminated, based on the assumption that the Gram matrix of the generator's Jacobian and the covariance of the latent space share similar eigenvector structures revealing semantic dimensions.

### Mechanism 3
Postprocess-specific LPIPS metrics improve attribution accuracy against image postprocessing attacks. By training LPIPS on a modified dataset where the second image is postprocessed rather than a different distortion, the metric becomes more sensitive to the fingerprint than to mild postprocesses. This allows the attribution function to remain effective even when images are degraded, under the assumption that the modified training data distribution makes LPIPS more discriminative for fingerprint detection under postprocessing.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-trained generator (StyleGAN2/LDM) -> Latent space w -> Latent fingerprint embedding (U, V, σ) -> Attribution function (LPIPS optimization) -> Parallel search initialization

- Critical path:
  1. Extract latent distribution samples from pre-trained generator
  2. Compute covariance matrix and perform PCA
  3. Select fingerprint dimensions V and orthogonal complement U
  4. Embed fingerprints by perturbing latent codes
  5. Generate fingerprinted images
  6. For attribution, optimize latent code to match given image and extract φ

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs quality: Larger σ and major principal components improve accuracy but degrade quality
  - Capacity vs accuracy: Higher key capacity (larger dφ) reduces attribution accuracy
  - Computation vs attribution quality: Parallel search improves convergence but increases computation time

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor attribution accuracy: Likely due to inappropriate choice of V (too small eigenvalues) or insufficient σ
  - Quality degradation: Fingerprint strength σ too large or V includes major principal components
  - Slow attribution: Parallel search not converging, may need more initial guesses or tighter bounds

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test attribution accuracy on clean images with varying σ and V choices to establish baseline tradeoff
  2. Apply postprocessing attacks and measure accuracy degradation with standard vs postprocess-specific LPIPS
  3. Vary key capacity dφ and measure impact on both accuracy and quality metrics

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can attribution accuracy be formally certified for latent fingerprinting methods given the non-linear nature of both the fingerprinting and fingerprint estimation processes? The paper acknowledges the lack of certification on attribution accuracy and suggests investigating whether existing neural net certification methods can be applied through forward reachability analysis of finger printed contents and backward reachability analysis of the fingerprint. This remains unresolved due to the challenging non-linear nature of the processes.

### Open Question 2
Can auxiliary datasets be used to learn novel and perceptually insignificant fingerprints that are robust against post-processes, such as background patterns? The paper mentions that the accuracy-quality tradeoff is governed by the data and suggests that it would be interesting to investigate if auxiliary datasets can help learn novel and perceptually insignificant fingerprints that are robust against post-processes. This approach is not explored in the paper.

### Open Question 3
What is the optimal trade-off between computational costs during generation and attribution in the context of model attribution? The paper acknowledges the trade-off between computational costs during generation and attribution and suggests that the choice of which aspect is more critical may depend on the specific application requirements. The paper does not provide a definitive answer or methodology for determining the optimal trade-off.

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation across diverse latent spaces beyond StyleGAN2 and LDM
- Novel postprocess-specific LPIPS approach lacks comparative analysis with other defense mechanisms
- Does not explore the use of auxiliary datasets for learning more robust fingerprints

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (semantic dimensions as fingerprints): Medium - Strong theoretical basis but limited empirical validation across diverse latent spaces
- Mechanism 2 (accuracy-quality tradeoff analysis): High - Rigorous mathematical derivation with clear propositions and proofs
- Mechanism 3 (postprocess-specific LPIPS): Low - Novel approach without comparative analysis or ablation studies
