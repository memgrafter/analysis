# Discovering COVID-19 Coughing and Breathing Patterns from Unlabeled Data Using Contrastive Learning with Varying Pre-Training Domains

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01864
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01864
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Achieves up to 0.81 accuracy for COVID-19 detection and 0.86 for unknown pattern discovery from unlabeled coughing/breathing data

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a contrastive learning approach for discovering COVID-19 coughing and breathing patterns from unlabeled data. The method uses pre-trained models developed from audio datasets similar to the target domain to extract representation vectors, which are then used for known and unknown pattern detection. Extensive experiments with four large audio datasets and one image dataset demonstrate that the proposed model can effectively distinguish COVID-19 patterns from unlabeled data and labeled non-COVID coughs. The study also finds that audio-based pre-training outperforms image-based pre-training, and that domain similarity in pre-training data is crucial for good performance.

## Method Summary
The approach involves developing pre-trained models using contrastive learning on audio datasets, then using these models to extract feature representations from unlabeled data. The OpenCon approach is employed for known and unknown pattern discovery. Audio augmentations (pitch shift and room reverberation) are used during pre-training, and the method is tested across multiple domains including ESC-48, AudioSet-48, Coswara, and FluSense datasets. The models are evaluated on their ability to distinguish COVID-19 coughing and breathing patterns from other respiratory sounds.

## Key Results
- Achieved up to 0.81 accuracy for distinguishing COVID-19 coughing and breathing from unlabeled data
- Reached up to 0.86 accuracy for unknown pattern discovery from labeled non-COVID coughs
- Pre-trained models using audio domains outperformed those using image domains by 19.7% - 44.6%
- Audio augmentations provided 1.1% - 2.4% better accuracy than image augmentations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contrastive learning can learn domain-invariant representations that generalize across different audio sources.
- Mechanism: The model maximizes similarity between augmented views of the same instance while minimizing similarity across different instances. This pulls representations of similar sounds closer together in embedding space regardless of the domain.
- Core assumption: The acoustic features of coughs and breathing from different diseases share enough structural similarity that they can be meaningfully compared in the same embedding space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "extensive experiments with four large audio datasets and one image dataset show that the proposed model can effectively distinguish COVID-19 coughing and breathing from unlabeled data"
  - [section] "The pre-trained model is used to extract a general-purpose representation from unlabelled data with a contrastive loss function"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence found for contrastive learning on respiratory audio - weak anchor
- Break condition: If the acoustic patterns between known and unknown diseases are too dissimilar, the learned representations will fail to capture the distinguishing features of the new disease.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pre-training on a domain similar to the target domain yields better performance than pre-training on a dissimilar domain.
- Mechanism: Domain similarity in pre-training data allows the model to learn relevant acoustic features that transfer effectively to the target domain. Audio-based pre-training captures temporal and spectral characteristics more relevant than image-based pre-training.
- Core assumption: The acoustic characteristics of sounds in the pre-training domain are sufficiently similar to those in the target domain for meaningful feature transfer.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "developing pre-trained models with data from a domain similar to the test domain...are better than using image augmentations"
  - [section] "pre-trained models developed with audio domains, i.e., ESC or AudioSet, perform 19.7% – 44.6% better than the models developed from image domains"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence found for domain similarity in contrastive learning for audio - weak anchor
- Break condition: If the pre-training domain is too dissimilar from the target domain, the learned representations will not capture the relevant features for the target task.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Audio augmentations preserve temporal and sequential information better than image augmentations for contrastive learning.
- Mechanism: Audio augmentations like pitch shifting and room reverberation maintain the temporal structure of sound events, while image augmentations like random cropping and Gaussian blur may disrupt this temporal information.
- Core assumption: The temporal and sequential characteristics of audio events are critical for distinguishing between different types of coughs and breathing patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "using audio augmentations are better than image augmentations"
  - [section] "AA approach is 1.1% – 2.4% more accurate than the IA approach when comparing the average accuracy values"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence found for audio vs image augmentation effects in contrastive learning - weak anchor
- Break condition: If the temporal information is not critical for the classification task, the advantage of audio augmentations over image augmentations may disappear.

## Limitations

- Limited dataset size (only 5,655 samples for main test domain) may affect generalizability
- Reliance on a single COVID-19 dataset (Coswara) without external validation
- Comparison between audio and image augmentations uses unbalanced augmentation sets
- Absence of ablation studies to isolate the contribution of individual components

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The general framework of using pre-trained models with contrastive learning for feature extraction shows promise, as evidenced by positive results across multiple datasets and tasks. The finding that pre-training on audio domains outperforms image domains is reasonably well-supported by experimental results.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific accuracy claims (0.81 for COVID-19 detection and 0.86 for unknown pattern discovery) should be interpreted cautiously given the limited dataset size and absence of external validation. The comparison between audio and image augmentations, while showing differences, lacks statistical significance testing and may be influenced by specific augmentations chosen.

**Low Confidence**: The broader claims about early disease outbreak prediction and generalizability to other diseases extend beyond what current experimental evidence supports. The study focuses specifically on COVID-19 without testing the approach on other respiratory diseases.

## Next Checks

1. Test the pre-trained models on independent COVID-19 datasets not used in the original study to assess generalizability and robustness across different recording conditions and populations.

2. Conduct systematic ablation studies to isolate the contribution of each component (contrastive learning vs supervised learning, audio vs image augmentations, different pre-training domains) to understand which aspects are most critical for performance.

3. Perform statistical tests (e.g., paired t-tests) on performance differences between different pre-training approaches and augmentation strategies to determine whether observed differences are statistically significant rather than due to random variation.
