# Approximately Bayes-Optimal Pseudo Label Selection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08883
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08883
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 11
- Key outcome: Bayesian framework for pseudo-label selection that reduces overfitting and confirmation bias by averaging over parameter uncertainty

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Bayesian framework for pseudo-label selection (PLS) in semi-supervised learning that addresses overfitting and confirmation bias issues. The core contribution is proving Bayes optimality of selecting instances based on the posterior predictive distribution of pseudo-samples, which averages over all possible parameter values rather than relying on a single estimated parameter vector. The authors derive an analytical approximation of this selection criterion using Laplace's method and Gaussian integrals, resulting in a computationally efficient formula involving the likelihood and Fisher-information matrix.

## Method Summary
The authors formalize PLS as a decision problem where actions are selecting unlabeled instances and utilities are pseudo-label likelihoods. They prove that the Bayes-optimal selection criterion is the posterior predictive distribution (PPP) of pseudo-samples, which integrates over the posterior parameter distribution. To make this computationally tractable, they apply Laplace's method to approximate the PPP, resulting in a selection criterion that balances likelihood (measuring fit quality) with Fisher information (measuring parameter uncertainty). The method is demonstrated for generalized linear models and generalized additive models.

## Key Results
- BPLS reduces confirmation bias by averaging over all plausible parameter values rather than using point estimates
- The Laplace approximation provides a computationally efficient selection criterion: log(ℓ(θ̂)) - ½ log|I(θ̂)|
- Empirical evaluation shows BPLS outperforms traditional PLS methods when facing high-dimensional data prone to overfitting

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** BPLS reduces confirmation bias by averaging over all plausible parameter values rather than relying on a single estimated parameter vector.
- **Mechanism:** The posterior predictive distribution integrates over the posterior parameter distribution, capturing uncertainty in both data and model parameters.
- **Core assumption:** The posterior distribution meaningfully represents parameter uncertainty after observing labeled data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "By averaging over all parameter values, PPP is more robust towards the initial fit compared to the predictive distribution based on a single optimal parameter vector."
  - [section] "Both the data and the estimated parameters (as functions of the data) will change throughout the process of self-training."
- **Break condition:** If the posterior distribution is too concentrated (e.g., with large labeled datasets), the averaging effect becomes negligible.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The Laplace approximation of the PPP provides a computationally tractable selection criterion that balances likelihood and Fisher information.
- **Mechanism:** The approximation log(ℓ(θ̂)) - ½ log|I(θ̂)| combines the maximized likelihood with a penalty term based on the Fisher information matrix.
- **Core assumption:** The likelihood function is sufficiently smooth and well-behaved near its maximum for Laplace's method to provide accurate approximations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "Its relation to the marginal likelihood allows us to come up with an approximation based on Laplace's method and the Gaussian integral...ℓ(θ̂)− 1/2 log|I(θ̂)|."
  - [section] "The second term penalizes high curvature of the pseudo-label likelihood function...since the Fisher-information is its second derivative."
- **Break condition:** If the likelihood surface has multiple sharp peaks or is highly non-Gaussian, the Laplace approximation may be inaccurate.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** BPLS's Bayes optimality ensures that selecting pseudo-labels based on PPP maximizes expected utility under the posterior distribution.
- **Mechanism:** By formalizing PLS as a decision problem, the authors prove that maximizing PPP corresponds to the Bayes-optimal action.
- **Core assumption:** The decision-theoretic framework appropriately models the PLS problem.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "We derive this selection criterion by proving Bayes optimality of the posterior predictive of pseudo-samples."
  - [section] "Action a* = arg max_i p(D∪(xi,ŷi)|D) is Bayes optimal for any updated prior π(θ)=p(θ|D)."
- **Break condition:** If the utility function doesn't properly capture the true goal of PLS, Bayes optimality doesn't guarantee good practical performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data preprocessing -> Model fitting (GLM/GAM) -> Likelihood computation -> Fisher information computation -> BPLS scoring -> Pseudo-label selection -> Model update
- **Critical path:** 1) Fit initial model on labeled data D; 2) For each unlabeled instance xi: predict ŷi, re-fit model on D ∪ (xi, ŷi), compute BPLS score; 3) Select instance with highest score; 4) Add to training data and repeat
- **Design tradeoffs:** Computational cost vs. accuracy; model flexibility vs. complexity; prior informativeness vs. robustness
- **Failure signatures:** Poor performance when q/n ratio is low; numerical instability with near-singular Fisher information; degraded performance with misspecified priors
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Reproduce results on simulated binomial data with varying n and q; 2) Compare BPLS against probability score on breast cancer dataset; 3) Test uninformative vs. informative BPLS on dataset with domain knowledge

## Limitations
- Effectiveness critically depends on presence of overfitting in initial model fit
- Laplace approximation may be unreliable for models with highly non-Gaussian posteriors
- Computational costs scale with need to refit models for each candidate pseudo-label

## Confidence

- Bayes optimality proof and theoretical framework: **High**
- Laplace approximation accuracy: **Medium**
- Practical performance gains: **Medium**
