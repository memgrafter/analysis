# Deep Incomplete Multi-view Clustering with Cross-view Partial Sample and Prototype Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15689
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15689
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel CPSPAN framework outperforms existing DIMVC methods across five benchmark datasets with varying missing rates

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of Deep Incomplete Multi-view Clustering (DIMVC) by proposing the Cross-view Partial Sample and Prototype Alignment Network (CPSPAN). Unlike existing methods that rely on contrastive learning, CPSPAN introduces a novel approach using pair-observed data alignment as proxy supervised signals and a prototype alignment module based on optimal transport theory. The framework consists of three jointly learning modules: incomplete multi-view representation learning, cross-view partial sample alignment, and shifted prototype alignment. Experimental results demonstrate CPSPAN's effectiveness and stability across five benchmark datasets with varying missing rates, showing superior performance compared to existing DIMVC methods.

## Method Summary
CPSPAN addresses DIMVC by focusing on two key innovations: pair-observed data alignment and prototype alignment. The framework first learns representations from incomplete multi-view data, then aligns samples across views using observed correspondences as supervised signals. To handle the prototype-shifted problem caused by incomplete data, it employs optimal transport theory to align prototype sets between different views. The three modules work jointly: incomplete multi-view representation learning captures view-specific features, cross-view partial sample alignment establishes instance-to-instance correspondence, and shifted prototype alignment ensures consistent clustering across views. This approach avoids the limitations of contrastive learning methods while effectively handling data incompleteness.

## Key Results
- CPSPAN outperforms existing DIMVC methods across five benchmark datasets with varying missing rates
- The method achieves competitive performance even with high missing rates (up to 90%)
- Significant improvements in clustering accuracy, normalized mutual information, and F-measure metrics
- Demonstrated stability and effectiveness across diverse datasets including BBCSport, BBC, Reuters, Handwritten, and Caltech101

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CPSPAN works by addressing two fundamental challenges in DIMVC: sample alignment across views and prototype consistency. The pair-observed data alignment uses actual observed correspondences between views as supervised signals, creating more reliable instance-to-instance mappings than contrastive learning approaches. The prototype alignment module corrects for the shifted prototypes that naturally occur when working with incomplete data, using optimal transport to find the most efficient alignment between prototype distributions. This dual approach ensures both fine-grained sample correspondence and high-level cluster consistency, resulting in more accurate and stable clustering outcomes.

## Foundational Learning

**Optimal Transport Theory**: A mathematical framework for finding the most efficient way to move mass from one distribution to another. Why needed: To align prototype sets between views while accounting for their shifted distributions due to incomplete data. Quick check: Verify that the transport cost matrix is properly normalized and that the resulting alignment is stable across different missing rate scenarios.

**Cross-view Correspondence Learning**: Techniques for establishing relationships between samples across different views. Why needed: To create reliable instance-to-instance mappings that guide the clustering process. Quick check: Ensure that the observed correspondences are sufficiently dense to support reliable alignment, especially at high missing rates.

**Prototype-based Clustering**: Methods that represent clusters through their centroids or prototypes. Why needed: To provide stable cluster representations that can be aligned across views despite data incompleteness. Quick check: Monitor prototype drift during training to ensure stable cluster formation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Incomplete Multi-view Representation Learning -> Cross-view Partial Sample Alignment -> Shifted Prototype Alignment

**Critical Path**: The representation learning module provides the initial feature embeddings, which are then refined through cross-view alignment using observed correspondences. The aligned representations are used to compute prototypes, which are subsequently aligned across views using optimal transport. This aligned prototype set is then used for final clustering decisions.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades computational complexity (optimal transport is expensive) for better handling of incomplete data and more accurate alignments. The pair-observed approach requires sufficient overlapping samples but provides more reliable signals than contrastive methods.

**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation occurs when:
- Missing rates exceed 90% with sparse overlapping samples
- Optimal transport becomes computationally intractable for very large datasets
- View-specific features become too dissimilar, making cross-view alignment difficult

## Limitations

- Computational complexity of optimal transport may limit scalability to large datasets or high numbers of views
- Performance depends on sufficient overlapping samples across views, which may not exist in extreme data incompleteness scenarios
- Experimental validation focuses on standard benchmark datasets that may not capture real-world data complexity and distribution characteristics

## Confidence

- Computational scalability claims: Medium confidence
