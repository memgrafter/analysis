# Analyzing Leakage of Personally Identifiable Information in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.00539
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00539
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** Novel attacks extract up to 10× more PII than existing methods; DP reduces but doesn't eliminate leakage (~3% of PII sequences remain)

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a taxonomy of PII leakage in language models with rigorous game-based definitions for extraction, reconstruction, and inference attacks. The authors introduce novel attacks that are significantly more effective than existing methods, extracting up to 10× more PII. Empirically, they demonstrate that record-level differential privacy reduces but does not eliminate PII leakage, with models still leaking approximately 3% of PII sequences. A key insight reveals a positive correlation between membership inference success and PII reconstruction success. The work includes comprehensive experiments across three datasets and multiple model sizes, with code publicly available.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a framework for analyzing PII leakage through three attack types: extraction (sampling text from models and identifying PII), reconstruction (filling masked PII using suffix information), and inference (identifying PII from candidate sets). They propose a sample-efficient estimation method for PII extractability based on grammatical rules and token interchangeability assumptions. Privacy defenses include differential privacy (record-level, ε=8) and PII scrubbing using NER tools. The evaluation uses three datasets (ECHR, Enron, Yelp-Health) with GPT-2 models (small, medium, large, XL) fine-tuned with different privacy configurations.

## Key Results
- Novel reconstruction attacks achieve 10× higher accuracy than baseline TAB attack
- Differential privacy reduces PII leakage by approximately an order of magnitude but doesn't eliminate it
- Larger models leak more PII than smaller models
- Positive correlation exists between membership inference success and PII reconstruction success
- DP effectiveness diminishes when PII is duplicated across records

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PII extractability can be accurately estimated without exhaustive sampling by leveraging grammatical rules and token interchangeability within PII classes.
- **Mechanism:** The algorithm samples N sentences, tags PII of the same class as the target using NER, replaces each tagged PII with the target sequence, accumulates the model's conditional probabilities for these replacements, and averages them to estimate extractability.
- **Core assumption:** (1) PII follow grammatical rules such that Pr(C|S;θ) = 0 when grammar doesn't allow PII generation after S, and (2) PII of the same class are interchangeable.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We propose a sample-efficient estimation of PII extractability by making the following two assumptions: (1) PII follow grammatical rules and (2) PII of the same class are interchangeable." [section 3.1] "From these two assumptions, we derive Algorithm 4 for approximating the extractability of a piece of PII." [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence comparing estimated vs actual extractability across multiple models/datasets.
- **Break condition:** If PII classes are not truly interchangeable (e.g., "John Doe" vs "Jane Doe" in specific contexts) or if grammatical rules are violated, the estimation becomes inaccurate.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Differential privacy (DP) reduces PII leakage by approximately an order of magnitude compared to undefended models, but does not eliminate it completely.
- **Mechanism:** DP training adds calibrated noise to gradients during optimization, which limits the model's ability to memorize specific training examples, including PII sequences. The noise level is controlled by ε parameter.
- **Core assumption:** DP provides meaningful privacy protection when records are not heavily duplicated and PII is not shared across multiple users/groups.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We empirically demonstrate that record-level DP limits the threat of PII leakage to a large extent but does not eliminate it completely." [section 4.5] "In the DP model, we observe that the extractability of PII is consistently about an order of magnitude lower than in the undefended model." [corpus] Moderate - corpus shows DP effectiveness but also reveals limitations when PII is duplicated across records.
- **Break condition:** When PII is heavily duplicated across records/users, DP's record-level guarantees become insufficient, as the same sensitive information appears in multiple adjacent datasets.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** There is a positive correlation between sentence-level membership inference success and PII reconstruction success.
- **Mechanism:** Sentences that are vulnerable to membership inference attacks (i.e., the model can distinguish whether they were in the training set) tend to also be vulnerable to PII reconstruction attacks, because both rely on the model's memorization of specific training examples.
- **Core assumption:** The factors that make a sentence memorable for membership inference (e.g., low perplexity for training members) are similar to those that make PII reconstructable.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "A key insight links membership inference to PII reconstruction success." [section 4.8] "Fig. 7c shows the memorization score relative to our reconstruction attack. We observe that there is a positive correlation between a sentence's memorization score and the success rate of our PII reconstruction attack." [corpus] Moderate - corpus evidence shows correlation but doesn't establish causation or explore all edge cases.
- **Break condition:** If membership inference and PII reconstruction rely on different memorization mechanisms, the correlation may break down.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Differential Privacy and its variants (record-level, user-level, group-level)
  - Why needed here: Understanding how DP provides privacy guarantees and its limitations is crucial for interpreting why DP reduces but doesn't eliminate PII leakage
  - Quick check question: What is the fundamental difference between record-level and user-level DP, and why might record-level DP be insufficient for PII protection when the same PII appears across multiple user records?

- **Concept:** Language model architecture (autoregressive vs masked)
  - Why needed here: The paper compares GPT-2 (autoregressive) with masked language models, and understanding the architectural differences is key to grasping why certain attacks work better on one type
  - Quick check question: How does the ability of masked LMs to condition on both prefix and suffix differ from autoregressive LMs, and why does this matter for PII reconstruction attacks?

- **Concept:** Named Entity Recognition (NER) and its error characteristics
  - Why needed here: The paper relies heavily on NER for tagging PII in datasets and generated text, and understanding NER limitations is crucial for interpreting results
  - Quick check question: What are the typical precision and recall rates for NER systems on PII, and how might these errors propagate through the experiments and affect the measured leakage?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data preprocessing (NER-based PII tagging and scrubbing) -> Model training (Undefended, DP, scrubbed, DP+scrubbed variants) -> Attack modules (Extraction, Reconstruction, Inference) -> Evaluation (Perplexity measurement, membership inference attacks, leakage metrics)

- **Critical path:** 1. Preprocess datasets with NER to identify PII 2. Train models with different defense configurations 3. Execute extraction attack (sampling N sentences, tagging PII, computing frequencies) 4. Execute reconstruction attack (using suffix information, sampling candidates, ranking by perplexity) 5. Execute inference attack (given candidate set, rank by perplexity) 6. Measure baseline leakage from public models 7. Compute metrics (precision, recall, accuracy, AUC)

- **Design tradeoffs:** Sampling vs exhaustive search: The paper uses sampling for efficiency but acknowledges it may miss some PII / NER accuracy vs privacy: More aggressive NER may over-tag PII, while less aggressive NER may miss sensitive information / Model size vs privacy: Larger models have higher utility but also higher leakage risk / DP parameters (ε) vs utility: Lower ε provides better privacy but worse utility

- **Failure signatures:** High baseline leakage: Indicates public models already contain PII, making it hard to attribute leakage to private training / Low precision in extraction: Suggests many generated PII are not actually from training data / High variance across runs: Indicates sampling instability or model randomness affecting results / Contradiction between estimated and observed extractability: Suggests assumptions about interchangeability or grammar are violated

- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Reproduce extraction attack on ECHR dataset with GPT-2 small, medium, large models to verify precision/recall trends 2. Implement and test the estimated extractability algorithm against observed extractability to validate the approximation 3. Run reconstruction attack comparing TAB attack vs proposed suffix-aware attack on a small dataset to verify 10× improvement claim

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the interaction between differential privacy (DP) and data deduplication techniques affect the overall privacy guarantees and utility of language models?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicit - The paper mentions that DP does not account for the context in which sensitive information is shared, such as within groups of users. It also references Zhao et al.'s work on combining DP and deduplication techniques to mitigate PII leakage.
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper demonstrates that DP reduces but does not eliminate PII leakage, it does not explore the potential synergistic effects of combining DP with deduplication techniques. The impact on privacy guarantees and utility trade-offs remains unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical studies comparing the effectiveness of DP alone versus DP combined with deduplication techniques in terms of PII leakage reduction and utility preservation. This would involve evaluating models trained with various combinations of DP parameters and deduplication strategies.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Can the proposed PII extraction and reconstruction attacks be adapted to work on masked language models (MLMs) like BERT, and how would their effectiveness compare to attacks on autoregressive models like GPT-2?
- **Basis in paper:** Inferred - The paper discusses the differences between autoregressive models (GPT-2) and MLMs (BERT), noting that MLMs are trained to reconstruct masked words and can trivially fill in single masks. It also mentions that attacks on GPT-2 are potentially more threatening due to the exposed next-word prediction API.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper focuses on evaluating attacks on autoregressive models but does not explore their applicability or effectiveness against MLMs. The inherent differences in model architecture and API access could significantly impact the feasibility and success of the proposed attacks.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementing and evaluating the PII extraction and reconstruction attacks on MLMs like BERT, comparing their success rates and limitations against those observed on autoregressive models. This would involve adapting the attacks to leverage the MLM's ability to fill in masked tokens and assessing the impact of API differences.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does the quality of Named Entity Recognition (NER) tools impact the accuracy of PII leakage measurements, and what are the implications for real-world deployments of privacy-preserving language models?
- **Basis in paper:** Explicit - The paper acknowledges that it relies on off-the-shelf text anonymization tools powered by NER models to tag PII in generated sentences. It also notes that these tools are prone to error, which could lead to undercounting instances of sensitive information leakage.
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper uses NER tools to identify PII, it does not quantify the impact of NER accuracy on the measured PII leakage. The potential for both false positives and false negatives in PII tagging could significantly affect the reliability of the privacy metrics and the effectiveness of deployed models.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting experiments to measure the impact of NER accuracy on PII leakage metrics by systematically varying the precision and recall of the NER tools. This would involve evaluating models with different NER configurations and assessing how changes in PII tagging accuracy affect the reported leakage rates.

## Limitations
- NER dependency and error propagation: The entire methodology relies on NER tools for both defense and evaluation, with no detailed performance metrics provided
- Sampling-based attack limitations: Extraction attacks use sampling rather than exhaustive search, introducing uncertainty about the true magnitude of improvements
- DP training implementation details: Limited implementation details and convergence diagnostics for DP training with small datasets

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The theoretical framework for defining extraction, reconstruction, and inference attacks is well-grounded. The correlation between membership inference and PII reconstruction success is empirically demonstrated with moderate-to-strong evidence. The observation that DP reduces PII leakage by an order of magnitude is consistently observed across experiments.

- **Medium Confidence:** The estimated extractability algorithm based on grammatical rules and PII interchangeability shows promise but lacks direct validation against ground truth. The claim that larger models leak more PII is supported but doesn't account for potential scaling effects in DP training. The effectiveness of PII scrubbing is demonstrated but limited by NER accuracy.

- **Low Confidence:** The exact magnitude of PII leakage improvements (e.g., "10×" for reconstruction) is uncertain due to sampling limitations. The paper's conclusions about DP's insufficiency for PII protection in duplicated records are based on limited dataset sizes and may not generalize to larger, more diverse datasets.

## Next Checks
1. **NER Performance Validation:** Conduct a detailed error analysis of the NER tools (Flair and Presidio) on manually annotated samples from each dataset. Measure precision, recall, and F1 scores specifically for PII categories. Re-run the extraction and scrubbing experiments with these measured error rates to quantify how NER inaccuracies affect the leakage measurements.

2. **Ground Truth Extractability Comparison:** For a small subset of the data (e.g., 1000 sentences), perform exhaustive extraction by sampling a large number of completions and comparing the results with the estimated extractability from the proposed algorithm. This will validate whether the grammatical rules and interchangeability assumptions hold in practice and quantify the approximation error.

3. **DP Parameter Sensitivity Analysis:** Systematically vary the ε parameter (e.g., ε ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}) and measure the tradeoff between utility (perplexity) and privacy (PII leakage) across all datasets. Include convergence diagnostics for each setting, and analyze whether the relationship between ε and leakage is linear or exhibits threshold effects. This will provide more nuanced guidance on DP parameter selection for PII protection.
