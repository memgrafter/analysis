# On the Capacity Limits of Privileged ERM

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02658
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02658

- Reference count: 23

## Executive Summary
This work studies the Privileged ERM algorithm for privileged learning, which aims to improve classification accuracy using privileged information available only during training. The paper analyzes the VC dimension of the loss class induced by Privileged ERM and provides both lower and upper bounds. It shows that the previously claimed VC dimension formula is incorrect and provides a counter example. The analysis reveals that worst-case generalization guarantees for Privileged ERM cannot improve over standard ERM unless the capacity of the privileged information is similar or smaller to that of the non-privileged information. This points to an important limitation of the Privileged ERM approach, especially when the privileged information is high-dimensional. The paper suggests that Privileged ERM might still be helpful even when the capacity of the privileged information is large, but under different assumptions or frameworks.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes the Privileged ERM algorithm, which minimizes a joint loss incorporating both non-privileged features and privileged information available only during training. The key analysis involves computing the VC dimension of the loss class F(H,Φ) induced by this optimization, comparing it to the VC dimensions of the individual hypothesis classes H (non-privileged) and Φ (privileged). The paper provides upper and lower bounds on VC(F(H,Φ)) and constructs a counterexample showing that a previously claimed exact formula for VC dimension is incorrect. The main result is that unless the VC dimension of the privileged information class is similar or smaller to that of the non-privileged class, Privileged ERM cannot provide better worst-case generalization guarantees than standard ERM.

## Key Results
- The previously claimed VC dimension formula VC(L(H,Φ)) = VC(L(H)) + VC(L(Φ)) is incorrect
- Privileged ERM's worst-case generalization guarantees cannot improve over standard ERM when d* >> d
- The correct upper bound is VC(F(H,Φ)) ≤ 4log₂(4e)(d+d*+1)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Privileged ERM cannot guarantee generalization improvement over standard ERM unless the privileged information class has low VC dimension relative to the non-privileged class.
- Mechanism: The optimization problem in Privileged ERM (Eq. 3) uses a joint loss that incorporates privileged information. The VC dimension of the loss class F(H,Φ) governs generalization. If VC(Φ) is too large compared to VC(H), the capacity of F(H,Φ) grows, erasing any statistical benefit from privileged information.
- Core assumption: The zero-one loss is used and the capacity analysis via VC dimension is tight enough to characterize generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "worst-case guarantees for Privileged ERM cannot improve over standard non-privileged ERM, unless the capacity of the privileged information is similar or smaller to that of the non-privileged information"
  - [section] "VC(F(H,Φ)) ≤ 4 log₂(4e)(d+d*+1)" and the proof that this bound is insufficient when d* >> d
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus contains related VC dimension papers but not direct privileged ERM capacity results.
- Break condition: If the privileged information class has low VC dimension (d* ≤ d), or if surrogate losses that penalize high capacity differently are used.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The original claimed equality VC(L(H,Φ)) = VC(L(H)) + VC(L(Φ)) is false; the correct upper bound is sub-additive.
- Mechanism: The proof error was traced to incorrect quantifier ordering. The correct VC dimension bound is at most 4 log₂(4e)(d+d*+1), which is less than d+d*+1 when both d and d* are large.
- Core assumption: The VC dimension of the union of two classes is at most the sum plus one, and the k-fold union bound holds.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We provide a counter example to a claim made in that work regarding the VC dimension of the loss class induced by this problem; We conclude that the claim is incorrect."
  - [section] "Theorem 4.1. For any integer d > 0... VC(F(Hd,Φd)) = 3d" and the construction showing the original claim fails
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus has VC dimension papers but not the specific error in Pechyony and Vapnik's proof.
- Break condition: If the loss class has special structure not captured by VC dimension (e.g., real-valued losses).

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Even when the capacity of privileged information is large, Privileged ERM might still help under different assumptions or frameworks.
- Mechanism: The paper suggests that alternative frameworks (e.g., using surrogate losses, outlier detection via privileged information, or different optimization objectives) could allow privileged information to help even when its VC dimension is large.
- Core assumption: The framework can be modified to exploit privileged information in ways not captured by the zero-one loss analysis.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In our closing discussion, we suggest another way in which Privileged ERM might still be helpful, even when the capacity of the privileged information is large."
  - [section] "In these cases, privileged learning may have a computational advantage, as it may be possible to use privileged information to make the computational problem of minimizing the true loss easier."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for alternative frameworks.
- Break condition: If the modified framework does not address the fundamental capacity issue or introduces other limitations.

## Limitations
- The analysis assumes zero-one loss and VC dimension, which may not capture all useful forms of privileged information
- The framework assumes perfect access to privileged information during training, which may not reflect real-world scenarios with noisy or incomplete privileged features
- The results focus on worst-case generalization, potentially missing practical benefits in specific domains
