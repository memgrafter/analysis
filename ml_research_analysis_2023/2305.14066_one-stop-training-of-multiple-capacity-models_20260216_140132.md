# One-stop Training of Multiple Capacity Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.14066
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14066
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Primary result**: Proposes Two-Stage Joint-Training (TSJT) framework that jointly trains high and low-capacity models, achieving comparable or better performance than individual training with faster convergence

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training multiple models with varying capacities for deployment in different scenarios. The authors propose a one-stop training framework that jointly trains high-capacity and low-capacity models simultaneously using a novel Two-Stage Joint-Training (TSJT) algorithm. The approach integrates supervisions from different flexible-capacity models, leading to faster and more efficient convergence. Extensive experiments on the WMT10 multilingual machine translation benchmark demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms low-capacity baseline models and achieves comparable or better performance on high-capacity models.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a one-stop training framework to jointly train high-capacity and low-capacity models simultaneously, addressing the challenge of training models with varying capacities for deployment in different scenarios. The core method idea involves a novel joint training algorithm called Two-Stage Joint-Training (TSJT) and two composite model architectures (shared and independent). The TSJT algorithm divides the training process into two stages: in the first stage, submodels work collaboratively to integrate supervisions from each other, and in the second stage, submodels optimize independently. Extensive experiments on the WMT10 multilingual machine translation benchmark show that the proposed method outperforms low-capacity baseline models and achieves comparable or better performance on high-capacity models.

## Key Results
- The proposed method achieves comparable or better performance on high-capacity models compared to individually trained baselines
- Low-capacity models trained with the joint framework outperform their individually trained counterparts by 1-2 BLEU points on average
- The two-stage training approach leads to faster convergence and more efficient training compared to separate training of each model capacity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint training with KL divergence supervision accelerates convergence by guiding low-capacity models toward high-capacity model's representation space early in training.
- Mechanism: In Stage 1, the KL loss between MoE and dense/device outputs creates a "guiding force" that pulls the low-capacity models toward more generalizable solutions before they settle into suboptimal local minima.
- Core assumption: The KL divergence effectively captures the representational knowledge gap between models of different capacities, and minimizing it leads to better generalization for the low-capacity models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our approach integrates supervisions from different flexible-capacity models simultaneously, leading to faster and more efficient convergence"
  - [section] "The idea of the TSJT algorithm is similar to the pre-training and fine-tuning of the language model. The first stage emphasizes global optimization, enforcing consistency constraints between submodels of varying capacities to aid in their quicker and more efficient convergence."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Two-stage training allows initial collaborative learning followed by independent optimization to reach better local minima.
- Mechanism: Stage 1 uses KL constraint to bring models to a shared good region of parameter space, then Stage 2 removes constraint allowing independent fine-tuning to find optimal solutions without being restricted by the constraint.
- Core assumption: The initial collaborative learning creates a beneficial starting point that wouldn't be reached through independent training alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In the first stage, the submodels work collaboratively to integrate supervisions from each other, ultimately reaching their optimal checkpoint. In the second stage, TSJT empowers submodels to optimize independently and seek their individual optimal solutions."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Shared architecture allows knowledge transfer through shared parameters while independent architecture allows flexible capacity ratios.
- Mechanism: Shared architecture forces dense model to learn from MoE's shared layers during backward pass, while independent architecture allows completely separate learning paths but loses parameter sharing benefits.
- Core assumption: Shared parameters in shared architecture create an implicit knowledge transfer channel between models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The shared architecture provide two submodels with specific shared parameters. We take MoE and dense model as example in the shared architecture. The MoE model is consist of moe layer in even-numbered layers and standard transformer layer in odd-numbered layers."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: TSJT algorithm -> Two composite architectures (Shared, Independent) -> KL divergence constraint in Stage 1 -> Independent optimization in Stage 2

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize composite model architecture
  2. Stage 1 training: Joint optimization with KL constraint until LKL ≤ tsep
  3. Stage 2 training: Independent optimization without KL constraint
  4. Extract individual submodels for deployment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Shared vs Independent architecture: Shared allows parameter efficiency and implicit knowledge transfer but constrains capacity ratios; Independent allows flexible ratios but loses sharing benefits
  - Stage 1 constraint strength (α): Higher α accelerates convergence but may over-constrain; lower α may not provide enough guidance
  - Threshold tsep: Determines when to switch stages - affects both convergence speed and final performance

- Failure signatures:
  - KL loss plateaus early: May indicate α is too low or models are already consistent
  - Training diverges or becomes unstable: α may be too high, overwhelming the primary task loss
  - Stage 2 performance worse than Stage 1: tsep threshold may be too low, removing constraint too early

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify basic functionality: Train shared architecture on a single language pair with default hyperparameters
  2. Test stage transition: Monitor KL loss and verify it crosses tsep threshold as expected
  3. Compare architectures: Train both shared and independent versions on same data to observe performance differences

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

## Limitations
- Experimental validation limited to single multilingual machine translation benchmark (WMT10)
- Performance improvements are relatively modest (1-2 BLEU points on average)
- KL divergence mechanism lacks ablation studies to quantify its specific contribution

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The basic functionality of jointly training multiple capacity models is demonstrated and works as described. The two-stage training process is implemented correctly and produces consistent results across experiments.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that Stage 1's KL constraint accelerates convergence is supported by training curves but lacks rigorous ablation studies comparing against alternative regularization methods. The superiority of the proposed method over baseline training is demonstrated but the margin of improvement (1-2 BLEU) suggests the practical impact may be limited.
- **Low confidence**: The generalization of the approach to other task domains and the claim that the method leads to "superior solutions" compared to independent training of each model capacity are not sufficiently validated with diverse experiments or theoretical analysis.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on KL loss contribution**: Run experiments with Stage 1 but without KL constraint (only cross-entropy), and compare convergence speed and final performance to quantify the specific benefit of the KL regularization mechanism versus the two-stage structure alone.

2. **Cross-domain validation**: Apply the same framework to a computer vision task (e.g., image classification with ResNet variants) to test whether the joint training benefits observed in machine translation transfer to other modalities and architectures.

3. **Threshold sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the t_sep threshold value and α coefficient across multiple orders of magnitude to identify optimal ranges and determine whether the method is robust to hyperparameter choices or requires precise tuning for each application.
