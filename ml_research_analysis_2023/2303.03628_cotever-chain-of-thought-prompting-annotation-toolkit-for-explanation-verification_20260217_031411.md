# CoTEVer: Chain of Thought Prompting Annotation Toolkit for Explanation Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03628
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03628
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Toolkit enables efficient collection of high-quality explanation data through verification rather than manual writing, improving LLM explanation faithfulness

## Executive Summary
CoTEVer is an annotation toolkit designed to verify and revise explanations generated by large language models for complex reasoning tasks. The toolkit shifts the annotator burden from manually writing explanations to verifying machine-generated ones, supplemented with supporting evidence documents retrieved from the web. This approach addresses the challenge of collecting high-quality explanation data while reducing cognitive load on annotators. The collected data can be used for fine-tuning LLMs, fact verification, and information retrieval tasks, ultimately improving the faithfulness and reliability of generated explanations.

## Method Summary
CoTEVer works by taking user questions as input and generating explanations using GPT-3, then providing annotators with these explanations alongside evidence documents retrieved from the web. Annotators verify the factual correctness of the explanations and revise any incorrect parts, with the toolkit capturing both the revised explanations and the evidence documents used. The process leverages human-in-the-loop verification rather than manual generation, making data collection more efficient. The toolkit also includes error type classification to identify common issues like insufficient knowledge, outdated information, and wrong facts in LLM explanations.

## Key Results
- CoTEVer enables efficient collection of high-quality explanation data by shifting from manual writing to verification
- The toolkit successfully identifies and corrects common errors in LLM explanations including insufficient knowledge and wrong facts
- Collected data can be used for fine-tuning LLMs, fact verification, and information retrieval tasks to improve explanation faithfulness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CoTEVer improves explanation quality by shifting annotator burden from manual writing to verification.
- Mechanism: Annotators verify and correct machine-generated explanations rather than creating explanations from scratch, reducing cognitive load and improving accuracy.
- Core assumption: Verification requires less expertise than generation and is more efficient when supported by evidence documents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "CoTEVer, a tool-kit for annotating the factual correctness of generated explanations and collecting revision data of wrong explanations."
  - [section 1] "Inspired by human-in-the-loop methods, we ask annotators to verify a machine generated explanation instead of manually writing them"
  - [corpus] FMR score 0.5959 for related work on CoT verification, suggesting this approach is recognized in the field.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Providing evidence documents during verification grounds explanations in factual knowledge and reduces hallucination.
- Mechanism: The toolkit retrieves and ranks documents based on sub-questions from the explanation, providing annotators with supporting evidence to verify or correct claims.
- Core assumption: Access to relevant evidence documents enables annotators to identify and correct factual errors that would otherwise go unnoticed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "To help the annotators' judgement, we provide documents that can be used as evidence to the generated explanation."
  - [section 4] "The knowledge needed in such situation is included in the evidence documents"
  - [corpus] Related work on retrieval-augmented generation suggests this approach reduces hallucination (Shuster et al., 2021)

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CoTEVer enables collection of high-quality explanation data that can improve LLM reasoning through fine-tuning.
- Mechanism: The toolkit captures both revised explanations and the evidence documents used, creating datasets suitable for CoT fine-tuning and knowledge-intensive tasks.
- Core assumption: High-quality explanation data is critical for improving the faithfulness of LLM-generated explanations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

Open Question 1
- Question: How can the accuracy of explanation verification be improved using evidence documents?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests using supporting evidence documents retrieved from the web to help annotators verify the correctness of generated explanations.
- Why unresolved: While the paper presents a method for providing evidence documents, it does not explore the optimal way to present these documents to annotators or how to measure the impact of evidence documents on verification accuracy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to compare the accuracy of explanation verification with and without evidence documents, and testing different methods of presenting evidence documents to annotators.

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide details on how to implement these approaches or how to measure the impact of CoTEVer data on the faithfulness of LLM-generated explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to fine-tune LLMs using CoTEVer data and evaluating the improvement in explanation faithfulness, as well as exploring different methods of knowledge unlearning using CoTEVer data.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of CoTEVer heavily depends on the quality and relevance of retrieved evidence documents, which is not thoroughly evaluated in the paper.
- The tool's performance may vary significantly across different domains and types of questions.
- The paper doesn't provide comprehensive metrics on annotator efficiency or the learning curve associated with using the toolkit.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core mechanism of shifting annotation burden from generation to verification, supported by the paper's methodology and related work on human-in-the-loop approaches.
- Medium confidence in the effectiveness of evidence documents for grounding explanations, as the paper demonstrates the approach but doesn't provide extensive quantitative validation.
- Medium confidence in the generalizability of the collected data for improving LLM reasoning, as the paper claims this benefit but doesn't provide comprehensive empirical validation across multiple model fine-tuning scenarios.
