# Robust Multimodal Fusion for Human Activity Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04636
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04636
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Centaur achieves 11.59-17.52% higher accuracy in HAR compared to two related robust fusion architectures, especially in the presence of consecutive missing data in multiple sensor channels.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Centaur, a robust multimodal fusion model designed to handle noisy and incomplete sensor data for human activity recognition (HAR). Centaur addresses challenges such as time-varying noise and consecutive missing data points in multiple sensor channels, which are common in real-world settings. The model decouples data cleaning from activity recognition using a denoising autoencoder with convolutional layers for cleaning and a deep convolutional neural network with self-attention for activity recognition. Experiments on three multimodal HAR datasets (PAMAP2, OPPORTUNITY, and HHAR) demonstrate that Centaur's data cleaning module outperforms state-of-the-art autoencoder-based models and its multimodal fusion module outperforms four strong baselines.

## Method Summary
Centaur uses a two-module architecture: a convolutional denoising autoencoder for data cleaning and a CNN with self-attention for HAR. The model is trained using a stochastic data corruption scheme that simulates realistic sensor faults, including random noise, consecutive missing data in some channels, and missing data in all channels of some sensors. The data cleaning module learns compressed latent representations that are robust to various sensor faults, while the self-attention mechanism in the HAR module allows it to effectively capture cross-sensor correlations, even when some sensors are missing data. Centaur is evaluated on three multimodal HAR datasets and compared against strong baselines.

## Key Results
- Centaur achieves 11.59-17.52% higher accuracy in HAR compared to two related robust fusion architectures
- The data cleaning module outperforms state-of-the-art autoencoder-based models
- The multimodal fusion module outperforms four strong baselines (DeepCNN, DeepConvLSTM, SADeepSense, and UniTS)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Centaur's decoupled architecture enables effective handling of noise and missing data by separating data cleaning from activity recognition.
- **Mechanism**: The denoising autoencoder (DAE) with convolutional layers learns compressed latent representations that are robust to various sensor faults. These cleaned representations are then fed into a self-attention CNN that focuses on extracting cross-sensor correlations without being distracted by noise or missing data.
- **Core assumption**: The data cleaning and activity recognition tasks can be effectively separated and trained independently.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Centaur's stochastic corruption process during training simulates realistic sensor faults, enabling the model to learn robust representations.
- **Mechanism**: The corruption process introduces random noise and consecutive missing data across sensor channels, simulating real-world sensor issues. By training the DAE on corrupted data and optimizing for reconstruction of clean data, the model learns to handle these faults.
- **Core assumption**: The corruption process accurately simulates real-world sensor faults.
- **Evidence anchors**:

- **Break condition**: If the corruption process does not accurately represent real-world sensor faults, the model will not generalize well to actual sensor data.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Centaur's use of self-attention in the HAR module allows it to effectively capture cross-sensor correlations, even when some sensors are missing data.
- **Mechanism**: The self-attention mechanism learns to weigh the importance of different sensor channels based on their reliability and relevance to the activity being recognized. This allows the model to focus on the most informative channels and ignore those with missing or noisy data.
- **Core assumption**: Cross-sensor correlations are important for accurate activity recognition.
- **Evidence anchors**:

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Sensor data -> Data cleaning module (DAE with convolutional layers) -> HAR module (CNN with self-attention) -> Activity classification

- **Critical path**: Sensor data -> Data cleaning module -> HAR module -> Activity classification

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Decoupling data cleaning and HAR allows for independent optimization but may miss some cross-task synergies.
  - Using a DAE with convolutional layers is computationally efficient but may not capture all relevant patterns in the data.
  - The self-attention mechanism is powerful but adds computational complexity.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High RMSE in data cleaning indicates the DAE is not effectively denoising the data.
  - Low accuracy in HAR indicates the self-attention mechanism is not effectively capturing cross-sensor correlations.
  - Poor generalization to new datasets indicates the model is overfitting to the training data.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Evaluate the data cleaning module on a dataset with known noise and missing data patterns.
  2. Evaluate the HAR module on clean sensor data to establish a baseline performance.
  3. Evaluate the full Centaur model on a dataset with realistic sensor faults to assess its robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several areas for future research are implied by the limitations section and the discussion of Centaur's design.

## Limitations

- The stochastic corruption process may not fully capture all realistic sensor failure patterns beyond those explicitly simulated
- The assumption that decoupling data cleaning from activity recognition is optimal may not hold for all HAR scenarios
- The model's performance with dynamically changing corruption patterns during operation is not evaluated

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The decoupled architecture approach and its basic implementation
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of self-attention for cross-sensor correlation in the presence of missing data
- **Low confidence**: Generalization to sensor fault patterns beyond those explicitly simulated

## Next Checks

1. Test Centaur on datasets with sensor failures not included in the original corruption modes (e.g., burst errors, systematic bias drifts)
2. Evaluate the model's performance when trained end-to-end rather than with decoupled modules
3. Assess the sensitivity of results to corruption level parameters to determine if performance degrades gracefully or catastrophically
