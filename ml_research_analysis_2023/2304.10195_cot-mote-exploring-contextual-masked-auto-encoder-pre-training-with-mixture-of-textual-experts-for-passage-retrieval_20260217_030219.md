# CoT-MoTE: Exploring ConTextual Masked Auto-Encoder Pre-training with Mixture-of-Textual-Experts for Passage Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.10195
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10195
- **Reference count**: 7
- **Primary result**: CoT-MoTE achieves +0.6 MRR@10, +0.3 R@50 on MS-MARCO passage ranking and +1.0, +2.1 nDCG@10 on TREC-DL 2019 & 2020

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the imbalanced discrimination problem in dual-encoder architectures for passage retrieval, where Siamese and fully separated dual-encoders either have low discrimination or poor retrieval performance. The authors propose CoT-MoTE, a pre-training method that incorporates Mixture-of-Textual-Experts (MoTE) with Contextual Masked Auto-Encoder (CoT-MAE). MoTE uses individual FFN experts (Q-FFN and P-FFN) to encode queries and passages separately, while maintaining a shared self-attention layer for joint modeling. Experiments on large-scale passage retrieval benchmarks show steady improvement in retrieval performances, with CoT-MoTE achieving +0.6 MRR@10, +0.3 R@50 on MS-MARCO passage ranking and +1.0, +2.1 nDCG@10 on TREC-DL 2019 & 2020 compared to the previous state-of-the-art method, CoT-MAE v2. The quantitative analysis also demonstrates a more balanced discrimination of the latent embedding spaces, with lower query-passage KL and JS divergences.

## Method Summary
CoT-MoTE combines Contextual Masked Auto-Encoder pre-training with Mixture-of-Textual-Experts. The method uses separate FFN experts (Q-FFN for queries, P-FFN for passages) while maintaining a shared self-attention layer for joint modeling. The pre-training process involves masking tokens in the input and reconstructing them using the encoder-decoder architecture. During fine-tuning, the model uses contrastive learning loss with hard negatives mined from the retriever-s1. The approach aims to capture distinct linguistic properties of queries and passages while preserving their mutual information for effective retrieval.

- CoT-MoTE achieves +0.6 MRR@10, +0.3 R@50 on MS-MARCO passage ranking

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CoT-MoTE addresses the imbalanced discrimination problem by using separate FFN experts for queries and passages while maintaining a shared self-attention layer.
- Mechanism: The Q-FFN expert captures short question/phrase patterns from queries, while the P-FFN expert handles long declarative paragraph patterns from passages. The shared self-attention ensures joint modeling and preserves mutual information between query and passage embeddings.
- Core assumption: Queries and passages have sufficiently distinct linguistic properties (length, syntax, format) that warrant separate expert modeling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose to pre-train Contextual Masked Auto-Encoder with Mixture-of-Textual-Experts (CoT-MoTE). Specifically, we incorporate textual-specific experts for individually encoding the distinct properties of queries and passages."
  - [section] "Query FFN expert (Q-FFN) is designed to encode queries, which are typically short-length questions or phrases. while Passage FFN expert (P-FFN) is designed to encode passages, which are typically long textual paragraphs."
  - [corpus] The corpus analysis shows queries average 6 words in question/phrase format while passages average 56 words in declarative paragraph format, confirming the linguistic differences.
- Break condition: If query and passage linguistic properties become too similar (e.g., in specialized domains where queries are long sentences), the separate experts may provide diminishing returns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Mixture-of-Textual-Experts approach creates a more balanced discrimination in the latent embedding space compared to pure Siamese or fully separated dual-encoders.
- Mechanism: By using separate FFN experts, CoT-MoTE maintains distinct embedding distributions for queries and passages (like fully separated encoders) while the shared self-attention layer ensures sufficient overlap for query-passage interaction (like Siamese encoders). This creates a balanced distribution that optimizes both discrimination and interaction.
- Core assumption: The optimal embedding distribution for passage retrieval requires both discrimination of query/passage types and sufficient overlap for relevance scoring.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments on large-scale passage retrieval benchmarks show steady improvement in retrieval performances. The quantitive analysis also shows a more balanced discrimination of the latent embedding spaces."
  - [section] "We believe the individual modeling of queries and passages with individual FFN experts will be a simple yet effective solution to alleviate the problem within Siamese and fully separated dual-encoder."
  - [corpus] T-SNE visualization in Figure 4(A) shows MoTE exhibits local clustering while still partially covering each other, demonstrating balanced distribution.
- Break condition: If the shared self-attention layer becomes too dominant, it may erase the benefits of separate FFN experts, reverting to Siamese-like behavior.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pre-training with representation bottleneck using CoT-MoTE initializes the dual-encoder with better query-passage embeddings for downstream retrieval tasks.
- Mechanism: The CoT-MAE pre-training framework compresses contextual information into a single representation vector, which serves as initialization. CoT-MoTE enhances this by using textual-specific experts during pre-training, creating better-initialized FFN weights for the fine-tuning stage.
- Core assumption: Better pre-training initialization leads to improved fine-tuning performance for dense passage retrieval.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Contextual Masked Auto-Encoding has been proven effective in representation bottleneck pre-training of a monolithic dual-encoder for passage retrieval."
  - [section] "In our work, we propose to pre-train the queries and passages with specific textual experts. We adapt on Transformers Blocks for better modeling textual traits."
  - [corpus] Table 2 shows CoT-MoTE achieves +0.6 MRR@10 improvement over CoT-MAE v2, which uses the same pre-training framework but without MoTE.
- Break condition: If the pre-training corpus is too small or unrepresentative of the fine-tuning domain, the initialization benefits may not transfer effectively.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Shared self-attention layer (multi-head)
  - Query FFN expert (Q-FFN)
  - Passage FFN expert (P-FFN)
  - Masked input processing
  - Encoder-decoder structure for pre-training
  - Contrastive learning loss for fine-tuning

- Critical path:
  1. Input text → tokenization → [CLS, tokens, SEP]
  2. Masked tokens → encoder with appropriate FFN expert (Q-FFN for queries, P-FFN for passages)
  3. Shared self-attention processing
  4. For pre-training: decoder reconstructs masked tokens using pooled representation
  5. For fine-tuning: contrastive loss with hard negatives optimizes query-passage similarity

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Separate FFN experts increase parameter count but improve discrimination
  - Shared self-attention preserves interaction but may limit expert specialization
  - Pre-training with pseudo-queries adds complexity but improves initialization

- Failure signatures:
  - If Q-FFN and P-FFN weights become too similar during training, the model may degenerate to Siamese behavior
  - If shared self-attention dominates, the model may lose the benefits of separate experts
  - Poor pre-training corpus quality may lead to ineffective initialization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study comparing Siamese, fully separated, and CoT-MoTE architectures on MS-MARCO dev set to verify performance improvements
  2. Switch pre-training corpus experiment where Q-FFN and P-FFN are trained on swapped data to confirm experts learn query/passage-specific features
  3. KL/JS divergence comparison between architectures to quantify embedding distribution balance improvements

## Limitations

- The empirical validation of separate FFN experts' effectiveness is limited to domains where queries and passages have distinct linguistic properties, and may not generalize to all retrieval tasks.
- The architecture introduces additional complexity and computational overhead through separate FFN experts, without detailed analysis of the computational trade-offs.
- The paper does not provide controlled experiments to isolate the contribution of the shared self-attention layer versus the separate FFN experts.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The mechanism of using separate FFN experts for queries and passages is well-supported by the corpus analysis showing distinct linguistic properties (6-word queries vs 56-word passages). The quantitative improvements (+0.6 MRR@10, +1.0 nDCG@10) on standard benchmarks provide strong empirical support.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that MoTE creates a more balanced discrimination in the latent embedding space is supported by KL/JS divergence metrics and T-SNE visualizations, but the paper does not conduct ablation studies to isolate the contribution of the shared self-attention layer versus the separate FFN experts.

**Low Confidence**: The assumption that pre-training initialization is the primary driver of performance improvements is not thoroughly validated. The paper does not compare CoT-MoTE against architectures that use the same pre-training framework without MoTE, making it difficult to attribute improvements specifically to the MoTE component.

## Next Checks

1. **Domain Transferability Test**: Evaluate CoT-MoTE on retrieval tasks where queries and passages have similar linguistic properties (e.g., document retrieval with long queries) to verify whether the separate FFN experts provide benefits beyond the MS-MARCO domain.

2. **Ablation Study on Expert Contributions**: Conduct controlled experiments that systematically remove either the Q-FFN, P-FFN, or shared self-attention layer to quantify the individual contributions of each component to overall performance.

3. **Pre-training vs Fine-tuning Analysis**: Compare CoT-MoTE initialized with random weights against CoT-MoTE pre-trained with MoTE to determine whether the performance gains are primarily due to better pre-training or the MoTE architecture itself.
