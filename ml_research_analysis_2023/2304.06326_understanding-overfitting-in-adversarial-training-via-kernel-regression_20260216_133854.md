# Understanding Overfitting in Adversarial Training via Kernel Regression

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06326
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06326
- Reference count: 40
- This paper analyzes adversarial training and data augmentation in kernel regression to understand overfitting issues observed in deep learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a theoretical analysis of overfitting in adversarial training and data augmentation through the lens of kernel regression. The authors establish limiting formulas for these methods as noise size and regularization parameter approach zero, demonstrating that without proper regularization, they can have worse generalization error and Lipschitz constant than standard kernel regression. The work shows that appropriate regularization (in the order of epsilon squared) can make these methods outperform standard regression. These theoretical findings are verified through simulations on synthetic and MNIST datasets, providing mathematical explanations for why adversarial training tends to overfit and why regularization techniques like early stopping are effective.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes kernel ridge regression with adversarial training and data augmentation, studying limiting behavior as regularization parameter λ and perturbation size ε approach zero. The method involves implementing kernel regression with data augmentation where the augmentation set varies for each data point, and adversarial training using gradient-based perturbations. The analysis derives limiting formulas showing how these estimators behave asymptotically and establishes conditions under which they outperform or underperform standard kernel regression. The theoretical framework is validated through simulations comparing various estimators with different regularization parameters using MSE and Lipschitz constant metrics.

## Key Results
- Without appropriate regularization, augmented and adversarial estimators may have larger generalization error and Lipschitz constant than standard kernel regression
- With regularization parameter λ in the order of ε², these methods can outperform standard kernel regression
- Under-regularized versions lead to overfitting and functions vulnerable to adversarial attacks
- Properly regularized versions improve robustness and achieve better generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The limiting formula for augmented and adversarial estimators reveals why under-regularization leads to overfitting.
- **Mechanism:** When regularization parameter λ is much smaller than noise size squared (λ << ε²), the estimator approximates ridgeless regression and exhibits unstable behavior with high Lipschitz constant and generalization error.
- **Core assumption:** The perturbation set ∆ = ε∆₀ with ε → 0 allows Taylor expansion of kernel functions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "without appropriate regularization, these two methods may have larger generalization error and Lipschitz constant than standard kernel regression"
  - [section] Theorem 1 establishes that when λ = o(ε²), augmented estimator ≈ g₀ which has poor generalization properties
- **Break condition:** If the kernel lacks sufficient smoothness for Taylor expansion, or if perturbations are too large relative to data spacing.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Proper regularization in the order of ε² eliminates overfitting by controlling the functional norm in RKHS.
- **Mechanism:** When λ is in the order of ε², the augmented/adversarial estimator interpolates between standard regression and a projection-based estimator, achieving optimal generalization.
- **Core assumption:** The noise size ε and regularization λ approach zero simultaneously in a controlled manner.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "by selecting the appropriate regularization parameter, these two methods can outperform standard kernel regression"
  - [section] Theorem 3 shows that with λ = O(ε²), augmented estimator achieves better Lipschitz constant and MSE than ridgeless versions
- **Break condition:** If the relationship between ε and λ is not properly scaled, or if the kernel matrix becomes ill-conditioned.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The adversarial estimator's equivalence to augmented estimator depends on perturbation set geometry.
- **Mechanism:** When perturbation set is a unit ℓ² ball, adversarial and augmented estimators share the same limiting formula; for other perturbation sets, this equivalence breaks down.
- **Core assumption:** The perturbation set ∆₀ satisfies specific symmetry and boundary conditions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "The similarity of the limiting formula between augmented and adversarial estimator in Theorems 1 and 2 are partially due to the perturbation set ∆₀ being a unit ℓ² ball"
- **Break condition:** When using ℓᵖ perturbations with p ≠ 2, the limiting formulas diverge.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise relationship between the choice of regularization parameter λ and the Lipschitz constant of the resulting estimator in adversarial training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper establishes that with appropriate regularization (in the order of epsilon squared), adversarial training can outperform standard kernel regression, but with small regularization, the Lipschitz constant is larger than the standard estimator.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides theoretical bounds but does not give an exact functional relationship between λ and the Lipschitz constant.
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments varying λ and measuring the resulting Lipschitz constant for different kernel types and data distributions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of adversarial training compare to standard training when the perturbation size epsilon is large (not small as assumed in the theoretical analysis)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The theoretical analysis assumes small epsilon and derives limiting formulas, but real-world adversarial attacks often use larger perturbations.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the small-epsilon regime and does not explore the behavior when epsilon is large.
- What evidence would resolve it: Simulations with varying epsilon values and comparison of generalization error and Lipschitz constant between adversarial and standard training.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the overfitting behavior observed in adversarial training be mitigated by techniques other than regularization, such as data augmentation or early stopping?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that regularization techniques like early stopping can alleviate overfitting, but does not explore other potential mitigation strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the effect of regularization and does not investigate alternative methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using different mitigation strategies and measuring their impact on generalization error and Lipschitz constant.

## Limitations
- The theoretical analysis relies on the assumption that noise size ε and regularization parameter λ approach zero simultaneously, which may not reflect practical settings
- The analysis is conducted in the kernel regression framework, which may not fully capture the complexity of deep learning models
- The paper focuses on ℓ² perturbations, and extension to other perturbation sets requires separate analysis

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The mathematical derivations of limiting formulas for kernel regression estimators are rigorous and well-supported by the theoretical framework
- **Medium Confidence:** The connection between kernel regression results and deep learning practices is plausible but requires additional validation through experiments on actual neural networks
- **Low Confidence:** The practical applicability of the λ = O(ε²) regularization scaling in real-world adversarial training scenarios needs empirical verification beyond the kernel regression setting
