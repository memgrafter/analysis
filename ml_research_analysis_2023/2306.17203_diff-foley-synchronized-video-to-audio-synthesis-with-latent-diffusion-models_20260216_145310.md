# Diff-Foley: Synchronized Video-to-Audio Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17203
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17203
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art Inception Score of 62.37 on VGGSound dataset, outperforming SpecVQGAN (30.01)

## Executive Summary
Diff-Foley addresses the challenge of generating synchronized audio from silent videos through a novel two-stage approach. The method first employs contrastive audio-visual pretraining (CAVP) to align visual and audio features in a shared semantic-temporal space, then trains a latent diffusion model (LDM) on spectrogram latent space conditioned on these aligned features. By introducing double guidance that combines classifier-free and alignment classifier guidance, Diff-Foley achieves significant improvements in both sample quality and synchronization accuracy compared to existing Video-to-Audio models.

## Method Summary
Diff-Foley operates through two main stages: First, CAVP uses SlowOnly video encoder and PANNs audio encoder with semantic and temporal contrastive objectives to align audio-visual features. Second, an LDM conditioned on CAVP-aligned features generates audio in spectrogram latent space using a frozen SD-V1.4 encoder/decoder and cross-attention module. The system employs double guidance combining classifier-free and alignment classifier guidance during inference with DPM-Solver sampler for 25 steps. The method processes videos at 4 FPS with 224×224 frames and converts audio to 128-bin Mel-spectrograms at 16kHz.

## Key Results
- Achieves Inception Score of 62.37 on VGGSound dataset, significantly outperforming SpecVQGAN baseline (30.01)
- Demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on VGGSound and generalization capabilities on EPIC-Kitchens through downstream finetuning
- Improves temporal synchronization and audio-visual relevance through CAVP pretraining and double guidance techniques

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Contrastive Audio-Visual Pretraining (CAVP) aligns visual and audio features in a shared semantic-temporal space, improving synchronization in downstream V2A generation. CAVP uses semantic and temporal contrastive objectives to maximize similarity between aligned audio-visual pairs from the same video while minimizing similarity between mismatched pairs. The temporal objective samples different time segments within the same video to enforce fine-grained temporal alignment.

### Mechanism 2
Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) conditioned on CAVP-aligned features can generate synchronized audio because the aligned features provide rich audio-related information. The LDM learns to denoise spectrogram latents conditioned on visual features that already contain semantically and temporally aligned audio information from CAVP. The cross-attention module allows the model to attend to relevant visual features during denoising.

### Mechanism 3
Double guidance combining classifier-free and alignment classifier guidance improves both sample quality and synchronization by leveraging both unconditional and conditional score estimates with additional alignment constraints. The technique combines three terms: conditional score, unconditional score, and alignment classifier gradient. This provides both robustness and precise alignment control during the denoising process.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Contrastive learning**
  - Why needed here: CAVP relies on contrasting aligned vs. misaligned audio-visual pairs to learn meaningful representations
  - Quick check question: How does the temporal contrast objective differ from the semantic contrast objective in CAVP?

- **Concept: Diffusion probabilistic models**
  - Why needed here: The LDM component uses denoising diffusion to generate audio from noise, conditioned on visual features
  - Quick check question: What is the role of the cross-attention module in the LDM architecture?

- **Concept: Guidance techniques in diffusion models**
  - Why needed here: Double guidance combines classifier-free and classifier guidance to improve both quality and alignment
  - Quick check question: How does classifier-free guidance differ from classifier guidance in terms of implementation and requirements?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: CAVP model (audio encoder + video encoder + contrastive objectives) -> LDM model (frozen SD encoder + UNet backbone + cross-attention + double guidance) -> Alignment classifier -> Vocoder (Griffin-Lim)
- **Critical path**: CAVP training → LDM training → Double guidance implementation → Evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using frozen SD encoder/decoder saves training time but may limit reconstruction quality; 4 FPS sampling reduces computational cost but may miss fast temporal events; double guidance improves quality but adds training complexity
- **Failure signatures**: Poor synchronization indicates issues with CAVP feature alignment or LDM conditioning; low sample diversity suggests problems with CFG scale or unconditional term contribution; slow inference points to sampler choice or diffusion step count issues
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train CAVP with only semantic objective (no temporal) and compare alignment metrics
  2. Test LDM with different guidance scales (CFG only) to find optimal tradeoff
  3. Compare frozen vs. fine-tuned SD encoder/decoder on reconstruction quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does the performance of Diff-Foley scale with larger datasets, such as those with billions of data points?
  - Basis: The paper mentions that scalability on super large (billion-scale) datasets remains untested due to limited data computation resources
  - What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating Diff-Foley on larger datasets like LAION-5B and comparing performance to other state-of-the-art methods

- **Open Question 2**: What is the impact of using different diffusion samplers on the performance and inference speed of Diff-Foley?
  - Basis: The paper discusses different samplers (DDIM, PLMS, DPM-Solver) and their impact on inference speed but doesn't provide comprehensive performance evaluation
  - What evidence would resolve it: Thorough evaluation of Diff-Foley's performance using different samplers with metrics like Inception Score, Frechet Distance, and Alignment Accuracy

- **Open Question 3**: How does the double guidance technique affect the quality and diversity of generated audio samples?
  - Basis: The paper introduces double guidance and demonstrates its effectiveness but doesn't provide detailed analysis of its impact on sample diversity
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive analysis of diversity of generated samples using different guidance techniques and comparing results to baseline methods

## Limitations
- CAVP pretraining mechanism's effectiveness for temporal alignment remains weakly validated with limited quantitative comparison against CAVP ablations
- Double guidance technique shows strong empirical gains but lacks ablation studies isolating the contribution of the alignment classifier component
- Frozen SD-V1.4 encoder/decoder may constrain reconstruction quality, though this tradeoff isn't thoroughly explored

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: DIFF-FOLEY architecture is technically sound and follows established diffusion model principles; reported Inception Score improvement over SpecVQGAN is robust
- **Medium Confidence**: CAVP pretraining improves feature alignment, but specific contribution of temporal contrast objective needs more validation; double guidance improves quality, but individual components' contributions are unclear
- **Low Confidence**: Claim that CAVP enables "subtler audio-visual correlations" via cross-attention lacks direct experimental support comparing CAVP features to alternative visual features

## Next Checks
1. **CAVP Ablation Study**: Train DIFF-FOLEY with only semantic CAVP objectives (no temporal) and measure degradation in synchronization metrics to quantify temporal objective contribution
2. **Guidance Component Isolation**: Test LDM with only classifier-free guidance (no alignment classifier) and only alignment classifier guidance (no unconditional term) to determine each component's individual contribution to the 32-point IS improvement
3. **Encoder Backbone Comparison**: Compare frozen SD-V1.4 against fine-tuned or alternative encoders (e.g., ViT-based) for both CAVP and LDM components to assess impact on feature alignment and generation quality
