# MooseNet: A Trainable Metric for Synthesized Speech with a PLDA Module

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07087
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07087
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: MooseNet achieves top-3/4 VoiceMOS leaderboard rankings using PLDA backend with minimal training data

## Executive Summary
This paper presents MooseNet, a trainable speech metric for synthesized speech that combines self-supervised learning (SSL) embeddings with a Probabilistic Linear Discriminant Analysis (PLDA) backend. The authors demonstrate that PLDA can significantly improve MOS prediction MSE when applied to fixed SSL embeddings, even without fine-tuning the SSL model. They also show that fine-tuning SSL models with contrastive loss and multi-task training improves SRCC rankings, and that ensembles of MooseNet models consistently achieve top leaderboard performance.

## Method Summary
MooseNet uses pretrained SSL models (wav2vec, Hubert, XLSR) to extract utterance embeddings, which are then processed by a small feed-forward network and passed to a PLDA backend for MOS prediction. The PLDA module treats MOS prediction as a classification task into discrete bins, then computes a weighted sum of bin centers. Training involves fine-tuning the SSL model with LAMB optimizer and contrastive loss using noise-augmented utterances, along with multi-task objectives for STOI and SNR prediction.

## Key Results
- PLDA improves MOS prediction MSE when applied to fixed SSL embeddings, trained on only 136 utterances
- Contrastive loss and multi-task training with noise augmentation improve SRCC rankings
- Ensembles of MooseNet models (SSL baseline + PLDA + fine-tuned variants) achieve top 3-4 VoiceMOS leaderboard rankings
- Fine-tuned MooseNet with PLDA backend surpasses SSL baseline on VoiceMOS Challenge data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: PLDA Binning for Stable Regression
- Claim: PLDA improves MOS prediction MSE when applied to fixed SSL embeddings, even without fine-tuning the SSL model.
- Mechanism: PLDA treats MOS prediction as a classification into bins, then computes a weighted sum of bin centers. This converts a noisy regression problem into a more stable classification task, reducing overfitting.
- Core assumption: The MOS scores can be well approximated by discrete bins with equal sample distribution, and the SSL embeddings capture enough discriminative information for PLDA to separate these bins.

### Mechanism 2: Contrastive Learning for Quality-Relevant Embeddings
- Claim: Contrastive loss and multi-task training with noise-corrupted utterances improve ranking performance (SRCC).
- Mechanism: Data augmentation creates positive/negative pairs; contrastive loss pulls together similar MOS scores and pushes apart dissimilar ones. Multi-task objectives (e.g., STOI, SNR prediction) regularize the embedding space toward quality-relevant features.
- Core assumption: Quality-related transformations (noise addition) preserve or predictably alter the underlying MOS, so pairs are meaningful for contrastive learning.

### Mechanism 3: Ensemble Diversity for Robust Predictions
- Claim: Ensembles of MooseNet models (SSL baseline + PLDA backend + fine-tuned variants) improve both MSE and SRCC metrics.
- Mechanism: Combining diverse models (different random seeds, SSL sizes, and PLDA integration) reduces variance and captures complementary strengths; simple averaging or weighted averaging stabilizes predictions.
- Core assumption: Individual model errors are at least partially uncorrelated, so averaging reduces overall error.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Probabilistic Linear Discriminant Analysis (PLDA)
  - Why needed here: Provides a generative model that works well with fixed embeddings and handles the classification-to-regression conversion for MOS.
  - Quick check question: What is the main difference between PLDA and a standard linear discriminant analysis in terms of modeling assumptions?

- Concept: Self-supervised learning (SSL) speech representations
  - Why needed here: SSL models (e.g., HuBERT, XLSR) provide rich, general-purpose embeddings without requiring labeled MOS data for pretraining.
  - Quick check question: How does SSL pretraining on unlabeled speech data benefit downstream MOS prediction compared to training from scratch?

- Concept: Contrastive learning objectives
  - Why needed here: Encourages the model to learn embeddings where MOS-similar utterances are close, improving ranking performance (SRCC).
  - Quick check question: In contrastive loss, what defines a positive pair versus a negative pair when training for MOS prediction?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Wav2vec SSL encoder → frame-level features → global pooling → feed-forward network → PLDA backend → MOS score
- Critical path: SSL encoder → global pooling → FF layers → PLDA (if used) → MOS score
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Small FF size (e.g., 32 dims) → faster PLDA training but less expressive
  - Large SSL model (XLSR) → better embeddings but higher compute
  - PLDA vs direct regression → better MSE stability but no SRCC gain observed
- Failure signatures:
  - High variance in MSE across runs → check PLDA binning or SSL embedding quality
  - SRCC degrades with PLDA → PLDA may not help ranking; consider contrastive loss tuning
  - Training instability → verify half-precision training and batch size limits
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline SSL encoder with PLDA backend on 136 utterances; measure MSE improvement.
  2. Add contrastive loss with noise augmentation; measure SRCC change.
  3. Combine baseline + PLDA + contrastive; ensemble multiple seeds; compare to leaderboard metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does PLDA performance compare when trained on datasets with varying levels of label noise and inconsistency in MOS annotation procedures?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that the BVCC dataset has different MOS labeling procedures across domains, leading to inconsistent labels.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not conduct experiments to directly measure PLDA's robustness to label noise or inconsistency in MOS annotations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the amount and type of label noise in the training data, and measuring PLDA's performance on each.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal architecture and training procedure for the neural network component of MooseNet to maximize PLDA's performance improvement?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that PLDA consistently improves various neural MOS prediction models, even state-of-the-art models.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the full space of possible neural network architectures and training procedures to find the optimal configuration for PLDA.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the neural network architecture and training procedure, measuring PLDA's performance on each.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of MooseNet compare to human annotators in terms of ranking systems and predicting absolute MOS values, especially in low-resource scenarios?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows that the Improved Baseline model outperforms two human annotators for main track data when evaluated on the main test set with respect to system-level SRCC metric.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not conduct a direct comparison between MooseNet and human annotators in terms of ranking systems and predicting absolute MOS values, especially in low-resource scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing MooseNet's performance to human annotators in terms of ranking systems and predicting absolute MOS values.

## Limitations

- PLDA binning assumption validity: The paper assumes MOS scores can be discretized into 32 equally populated bins without empirical validation
- Generalizability concerns: PLDA improvements may be overfit to VoiceMOS Challenge data characteristics
- Limited SSL model comparison: Experiments focus on wav2vec 2.0 and XLSR models without exploring other SSL approaches

## Confidence

- PLDA with fixed SSL embeddings improves MSE: Medium confidence
- Fine-tuning SSL models with contrastive loss improves SRCC: Low confidence
- Ensembles consistently improve performance: Medium confidence

## Next Checks

1. **Binning Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically vary the number of bins (16, 32, 64, 128) and measure the impact on both training stability and final MOS prediction accuracy.

2. **Cross-Dataset Generalization Test**: Evaluate the MooseNet model trained on BVCC data on at least one other MOS dataset to assess whether PLDA improvements transfer beyond the training domain.

3. **Ablation of Contrastive Loss Components**: Implement controlled experiments isolating the contrastive loss contribution by training with and without augmentation, with different positive/negative pair definitions.
