# A Gromov--Wasserstein Geometric View of Spectrum-Preserving Graph Coarsening

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08854
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08854
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces a new perspective for graph coarsening by analyzing graph distances on the Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) space, showing that GW distance preservation depends primarily on spectral differences between original and coarsened graphs.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a theoretical foundation for spectrum-preserving graph coarsening by analyzing its relationship to Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distances. The authors prove that the difference between GW distances of original graphs and their coarsened versions is bounded by a term that depends only on the spectral difference between their similarity matrices. This result justifies spectrum-preserving coarsening for graph-level tasks. They then connect this bound to weighted kernel K-means clustering, leading to a new method called kernel graph coarsening (KGC). Experiments show KGC effectively preserves GW distances and improves accuracy in graph classification and regression tasks compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a graph coarsening method based on Gromov-Wasserstein distance preservation through spectrum preservation. The key insight is that minimizing the difference between GW distances of original and coarsened graphs can be achieved by minimizing the spectral difference between their similarity matrices. This minimization is shown to be equivalent to a weighted kernel K-means objective, enabling efficient optimization. The method uses signless Laplacians as similarity matrices to avoid negative entries while maintaining positive semi-definiteness. The KGC algorithm iteratively refines coarsened graphs through weighted kernel K-means clustering, initialized either randomly or using outputs from baseline methods.

## Key Results
- Theoretical bound showing GW distance preservation depends primarily on spectral differences
- KGC method effectively preserves GW distances with lower approximation errors than baseline methods
- Improved classification accuracy and regression MAE on benchmark graph datasets
- Better GW distance preservation compared to state-of-the-art graph coarsening methods

## Limitations
- The theoretical bounds assume similarity matrices are positive semi-definite, requiring the signless Laplacian construction that may capture different structural properties than traditional approaches.
- The GW distance approximation using Frobenius norm may not fully capture the geometric structure.
- The computational complexity of GW distance calculations remains quadratic in graph size, limiting scalability.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The mathematical derivation of GW distance bounds and their dependence on spectral differences is sound.
- **Medium confidence**: The equivalence between spectral difference minimization and weighted kernel K-means clustering is well-established, though practical convergence may vary.
- **Medium confidence**: The signless Laplacian as similarity matrix effectively resolves computational issues while preserving necessary structure for GW applications.
