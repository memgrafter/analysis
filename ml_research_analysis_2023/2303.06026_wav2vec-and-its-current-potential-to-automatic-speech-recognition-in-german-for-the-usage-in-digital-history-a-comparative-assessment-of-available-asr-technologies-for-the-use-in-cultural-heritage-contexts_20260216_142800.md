# wav2vec and its current potential to Automatic Speech Recognition in German for the usage in Digital History: A comparative assessment of available ASR-technologies for the use in cultural heritage contexts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06026
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06026
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: wav2vec2-based ASR achieves moderate accuracy for German cultural heritage recordings, outperforming Dragon but trailing Microsoft Azure in word error rates.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates wav2vec2-based automatic speech recognition (ASR) models for German in cultural heritage contexts, comparing open-source and commercial solutions. A wav2vec2 model was fine-tuned using CommonVoice German data and tested against 22 historical recordings spanning 1914–2018. Results show word error rates (WER) averaging 27.7% for the open-source model, outperforming Dragon (46.3% WER) but trailing Microsoft Azure (17.9% WER). Recognition rates exceed 90% for modern, high-quality recordings but drop sharply for older or dialect-heavy speech. The study highlights ASR's potential for DH applications, especially with standard German, while noting challenges like dialect diversity and legal barriers to cloud use.

## Method Summary
The study compared wav2vec2 and other ASR models using a test set of 22 historical German recordings from various archives, spanning 1914–2018. The wav2vec2 model was fine-tuned on CommonVoice German data and evaluated alongside commercial solutions like Dragon and Microsoft Azure. Word error rates (WER) and recognition rates were measured, with additional analysis of dialectal challenges and legal considerations for cloud-based systems.

## Key Results
- wav2vec2 achieves an average WER of 27.7%, outperforming Dragon (46.3% WER) but trailing Microsoft Azure (17.9% WER).
- Recognition rates exceed 90% for modern, high-quality recordings but drop significantly for older or dialect-heavy speech.
- ASR systems perform well with standard German but struggle with dialectical variations, achieving WERs of 40-60% for Austrian, Swiss, and Bavarian German.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The wav2vec2 model leverages self-supervised pretraining on large audio datasets, enabling it to learn robust speech representations without extensive labeled data. Fine-tuning on domain-specific data like CommonVoice German improves its adaptability to historical recordings, though challenges remain with dialect diversity and recording quality variations.

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study highlights that ASR systems struggle with dialectical German, achieving word error rates around 40-60% for recordings in Austrian, Swiss, and Bavarian German, while performing well with standard German.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a significant challenge but does not provide a clear solution for improving ASR performance on dialects without compromising accuracy for standard German.

## Limitations
- The evaluation dataset of 22 historical recordings is relatively small and may not be fully representative.
- Limited access to full technical specifications for proprietary models (Microsoft Azure, Dragon) constrains direct comparisons.
- The study does not fully quantify the impact of recording quality variations and dialect diversity on results.
