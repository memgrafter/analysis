# Text-to-Video: a Two-stage Framework for Zero-shot Identity-agnostic Talking-head Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2308.06457
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06457
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: YourTTS + SadTalker achieves best identity preservation and video quality for zero-shot talking head generation

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a two-stage framework for generating videos of specified persons saying arbitrary text without additional training. The method first uses zero-shot TTS models to convert text to speech while preserving target speaker identity, then applies audio-driven talking head generation to produce videos of the specified person speaking the text. The framework enables identity-agnostic talking head generation by separating audio synthesis from visual generation, allowing each stage to focus on its specific task while transferring identity information between stages.

## Method Summary
The framework operates in two stages: first, a zero-shot TTS model (YourTTS, Vits, Tacotron, or Tortoise) converts input text to speech while mimicking a target speaker's voice using reference audio. Second, an audio-driven talking head generation model (Audio2Head, StyleHEAT, or SadTalker) creates a video of the specified person speaking the synthesized audio. The approach leverages pretrained models for both stages, eliminating the need for identity-specific training data. YourTTS uses transfer learning with pretrained speaker encoders, while SadTalker employs 3DMM coefficients driven by audio features to generate synchronized facial animations.

## Key Results
- YourTTS best preserves speaker identity in synthesized audio compared to other TTS methods
- SadTalker produces highest quality talking head videos with good lip synchronization
- StyleHEAT shows severe quality issues with identity preservation and image generation
- The two-stage framework successfully generates identity-agnostic talking head videos without additional training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The two-stage framework achieves identity-agnostic talking head generation by separating audio synthesis from visual generation
- Mechanism: Stage 1 uses zero-shot TTS models to convert text to speech while preserving target speaker identity. Stage 2 uses audio-driven talking head generation to create videos of the specified person speaking the synthesized audio, without requiring identity-specific training data
- Core assumption: Identity information extracted in TTS stage can be effectively transferred to and utilized by talking head generation stage
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The method first uses zero-shot TTS models to convert text to speech while specifying the speaker identity, then applies audio-driven talking head generation to produce videos of the specified person speaking the text"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Zero-shot TTS models enable speaker identity preservation without identity-specific training
- Mechanism: YourTTS uses transfer learning with pretrained speaker encoders for speaker verification and vocoders, allowing it to generate speech that mimics a target speaker's voice characteristics from only a few seconds of reference audio
- Core assumption: Pretrained speaker verification models contain sufficient speaker identity information that can be transferred to TTS synthesis
- Evidence anchors: [section 2] "To overcome these limitations, Jia et al. conducted a benchmark study on TTS. They proposed a transfer learning approach that utilized pretrained speaker encoders for speaker verification and vocoders"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Audio-driven talking head generation can create synchronized videos from arbitrary audio without video-specific training
- Mechanism: Models like SadTalker extract 3DMM coefficients from reference images and generate sequential 3DMM coefficients based on audio input, allowing creation of talking head videos that synchronize with any provided audio
- Core assumption: 3DMM representations contain sufficient facial motion information that can be driven by audio features to create natural-looking talking head videos
- Evidence anchors: [section 2] "SadTalker represented head images using 3DMM coefficients and generated sequential 3DMM coefficients based on the audio input"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text + Reference Audio → TTS Models → Synthesized Speech → Talking Head Models → Video
- Critical path: Text → TTS (YourTTS) → Audio → Talking Head Generation (SadTalker) → Video
- Design tradeoffs:
  - YourTTS vs Vits vs Tacotron: YourTTS better preserves identity but may sacrifice some audio quality; Vits and Tacotron may produce higher quality audio but cannot specify speaker identity
  - SadTalker vs Audio2Head vs StyleHEAT: SadTalker produces higher quality images but less motion variation; Audio2Head has better lip synchronization but lower image quality; StyleHEAT has severe quality issues with identity preservation
  - Tradeoff between video quality and computational efficiency
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor speaker identity matching: Audio doesn't sound like target speaker
  - Lip synchronization issues: Mouth movements don't match audio
  - Identity preservation failure: Generated face doesn't resemble target identity
  - Motion artifacts: Discontinuous or unnatural head/shoulder movements
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline test: Use YourTTS with Steve Jobs reference audio to generate speech, then use SadTalker with Steve Jobs reference image to generate video - verify identity preservation across both stages
  2. Cross-identity test: Use YourTTS to generate speech in one identity's voice, then use SadTalker with a different identity's image - observe how identity information transfers (or fails to transfer)
  3. Quality comparison: Compare output quality when using different TTS methods (YourTTS vs Vits) with the same talking head generation model, and vice versa

## Limitations

- Evaluation focuses on qualitative assessments rather than comprehensive quantitative metrics
- Performance across diverse identities, speaking styles, and languages remains unverified
- Computational requirements and inference times for the two-stage pipeline are not discussed
- Limited testing on languages other than English and Chinese

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The basic two-stage framework architecture (TTS → audio-driven talking head generation) is technically sound and represents a valid approach for identity-agnostic talking head generation
- **Medium Confidence**: YourTTS performs better than other TTS methods for speaker identity preservation, and SadTalker produces higher quality talking head videos than the alternatives
- **Low Confidence**: The framework can reliably generalize to any specified person without additional training across diverse speaker types and visual appearances
