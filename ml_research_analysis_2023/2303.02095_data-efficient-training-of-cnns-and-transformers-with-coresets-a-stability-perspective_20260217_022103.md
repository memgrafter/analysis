# Data-Efficient Training of CNNs and Transformers with Coresets: A Stability Perspective

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02095
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02095
- Reference count: 7
- The paper benchmarks coreset selection methods for efficient training of CNNs and transformers

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the effectiveness of various coreset selection methods for efficient training of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers (ViTs) on image classification tasks. The authors compare sophisticated methods like CRAIG, GradMatch, and GLISTER against random selection across multiple datasets and architectures. The results reveal that while coreset selection can improve training efficiency, the choice of method and architecture should be tailored to specific dataset characteristics. Notably, random selection often performs comparably to more complex methods, especially for transformers pretrained on ImageNet21K.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted extensive experiments comparing four coreset selection methods (CRAIG, GradMatch, GLISTER, and random selection) across three datasets (ImageNet-1K, CheXpert, and Kvasir) and two architectures (ResNet50 and ViT-B/16). They evaluated performance at various coreset sizes (1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, and 50% of the full dataset) using top-1 accuracy as the primary metric. The experiments included both pretrained models and training from scratch, with additional analysis of data distribution complexity and class imbalance effects on coreset selection performance.

## Key Results
- Random selection often performs comparably to sophisticated coreset methods, especially for transformers pretrained on ImageNet21K
- For non-natural images or without pretraining, CNNs tend to outperform transformers at small coreset sizes
- The effectiveness of coreset selection methods is highly dependent on dataset characteristics and class imbalance
- Transformers pretrained on ImageNet21K show better performance than CNNs in data-limited scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper suggests that the effectiveness of coreset selection methods is influenced by the interplay between dataset characteristics and model architecture. For transformers pretrained on large datasets like ImageNet21K, the learned representations may already capture relevant features, making sophisticated coreset selection less critical. In contrast, for smaller datasets or non-natural images, the choice of coreset selection method becomes more important as the model needs to learn from a more limited and potentially less diverse set of examples. The relationship between data distribution complexity, class imbalance, and coreset selection performance indicates that these methods may be more effective when the dataset has specific characteristics that align with the selection criteria used by each method.

## Foundational Learning
The study builds on the concept of coreset selection in machine learning, which aims to select a representative subset of data for efficient model training. It extends previous work by systematically comparing multiple coreset selection methods across different architectures (CNNs and transformers) and dataset types. The research highlights the importance of considering both the model architecture and dataset characteristics when choosing a coreset selection method. It also demonstrates the potential of pretrained transformers to perform well in data-limited scenarios, which aligns with recent trends in transfer learning and few-shot learning.

## Architecture Onboarding
The paper focuses on two main architectures: ResNet50 (a CNN) and ViT-B/16 (a vision transformer). For ResNet50, the standard convolutional layers are used to extract features from images, followed by fully connected layers for classification. The ViT-B/16 architecture divides input images into 16x16 patches, linearly embeds them, adds positional embeddings, and processes them through multiple transformer encoder layers. Both architectures are evaluated with and without pretraining on ImageNet21K. The study suggests that the choice between these architectures in data-limited scenarios depends on factors such as dataset complexity and the presence of pretraining, with transformers often outperforming CNNs when pretrained on large datasets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do different coreset selection methods perform across a wider range of architectures beyond ResNet50 and ViT-B/16?
- What is the impact of varying batch sizes and learning rate schedules on the effectiveness of coreset selection methods?
- How do coreset selection methods behave with more diverse and challenging datasets not covered in this study?
- Can the insights from this study be extended to other domains beyond image classification, such as natural language processing or speech recognition?
- What are the long-term effects of using coreset selection methods on model generalization and robustness to distribution shifts?

## Limitations
- The study primarily focuses on ResNet50 and ViT-B/16 architectures, limiting generalizability to other model families
- Computational overhead of coreset selection methods is not thoroughly analyzed
- The impact of different batch sizes and learning rate schedules on coreset selection performance is not explored
- Experiments were conducted on a limited set of datasets, potentially missing important edge cases
- The study does not investigate the effects of coreset selection on model robustness or generalization to unseen data distributions

## Confidence
High confidence: Transformers pretrained on ImageNet21K often outperform CNNs in data-limited scenarios, and the comparative analysis of coreset selection methods is robust.

Medium confidence: The recommendation to tailor coreset selection methods to specific dataset characteristics is supported by the data, but exact criteria for such tailoring are not fully established.

Low confidence: The assertion that the choice between CNNs and transformers at small coreset sizes depends primarily on dataset complexity and pretraining oversimplifies the factors influencing model performance.

## Next Checks

2. Conduct a detailed analysis of the computational overhead of each coreset selection method, including time and memory requirements.

3. Investigate the impact of varying batch sizes and learning rate schedules on coreset selection performance, particularly for small coreset sizes and data-limited scenarios.

4. Extend the study to more diverse and challenging datasets, including those with complex data distributions and severe class imbalances.

5. Evaluate the long-term effects of coreset selection on model generalization and robustness to distribution shifts through extensive testing on out-of-distribution data.
