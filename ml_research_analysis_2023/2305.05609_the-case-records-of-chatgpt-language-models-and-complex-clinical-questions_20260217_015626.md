# The Case Records of ChatGPT: Language Models and Complex Clinical Questions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05609
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05609
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: GPT4 and GPT3.5 achieved 26% and 22% accuracy for correct diagnosis on first attempt, respectively

## Executive Summary
This study evaluated the ability of AI language models GPT4 and GPT3.5 to diagnose complex clinical cases from the Massachusetts General Hospital Case Records. The models were given 50 cases requiring diagnosis and diagnostic tests, with prompts requesting the top three specific diagnoses and associated tests. GPT4 correctly identified the diagnosis on the first attempt in 26% of cases and within three attempts in 46% of cases. GPT3.5 achieved 22% and 42% accuracy for the same metrics. For diagnostic tests, GPT4 achieved 28% accuracy on the first attempt and 44% within three attempts, while GPT3.5 achieved 24% and 50%, respectively. No significant differences were found between the models. These results demonstrate that while these AI models show potential for generating differential diagnoses, they remain limited in providing definitive diagnoses for complex, open-ended clinical cases.

## Method Summary
The study used 50 Case Records of the Massachusetts General Hospital published between January 1, 2022 and April 16, 2022. Each case required a diagnosis and associated diagnostic test. Models were given prompts requesting the top three specific diagnoses and associated diagnostic tests in bullet point format without reasoning. GPT4 and GPT3.5 were tested using the March 23, 2023 version of ChatGPT. Model outputs were compared against final clinical diagnoses and essential diagnostic tests to determine accuracy. Chi-squared testing was used to compare model performance, with significance set at p < 0.05.

## Key Results
- GPT4 achieved 26% accuracy for correct diagnosis on first attempt and 46% within three attempts
- GPT3.5 achieved 22% accuracy for correct diagnosis on first attempt and 42% within three attempts
- No significant differences were found between GPT4 and GPT3.5 performance (p > 0.05 for all comparisons)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT models perform better at generating differential diagnoses than providing definitive diagnoses for complex clinical cases
- Mechanism: The models' architecture excels at pattern matching and probabilistic reasoning across multiple possible explanations rather than committing to a single definitive answer
- Core assumption: Clinical reasoning in complex cases often involves considering multiple potential diagnoses simultaneously, which aligns with the probabilistic nature of LLMs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "these models demonstrate potential usefulness in generating differential diagnoses but remain limited in their ability to provide a single unifying diagnosis"
  - [section] "GPT4 and GPT3.5 accurately provided the correct diagnosis in 26% and 22% of cases in one attempt, and 46% and 42% within three attempts, respectively"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence for this specific mechanism
- Break condition: When cases require single definitive answers rather than considering multiple possibilities, or when the correct diagnosis is not among the top three most probable options

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The prompt structure significantly influences model performance
- Mechanism: The specific phrasing requesting "top three specific diagnoses" guides the model toward a constrained set of probable answers, improving accuracy within that bounded space
- Core assumption: LLMs respond strongly to explicit structural constraints in prompts, focusing their output generation within specified parameters
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Models were given a prompt requesting the top three specific diagnoses and associated diagnostic tests"
  - [section] "In the 39 cases where the first diagnosis was incorrect on Trial 1, the first diagnosis was correct in an additional 2 cases on one of the subsequent 4 trials"
  - [corpus] Moderate - related studies show prompt engineering significantly affects LLM medical task performance
- Break condition: When the prompt structure doesn't match the reasoning requirements of the case, or when cases require open-ended exploration without predefined constraints

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Model performance plateaus between GPT3.5 and GPT4 for this task type
- Mechanism: The specific task of complex clinical diagnosis may not sufficiently leverage the architectural improvements in GPT4, or both models face similar limitations when dealing with open-ended clinical reasoning
- Core assumption: Architectural differences between GPT3.5 and GPT4 provide diminishing returns for tasks requiring deep contextual reasoning across complex medical information
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "No significant differences were found between the two models"
  - [section] "Chi-squared testing in Stata 17.0 revealed no significant differences between GPT4 and GPT3.5 (p > 0.05 for all comparisons)"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence explaining why architectural improvements don't translate to better performance
- Break condition: When task complexity increases beyond the threshold where architectural improvements become meaningful, or when other task types better leverage GPT4's capabilities

  - Model selection: GPT4 vs GPT3.5 showed no significant difference, suggesting simpler models may suffice
  - Case complexity: Complex, open-ended cases may be beyond current LLM capabilities for definitive diagnosis

- Failure signatures:
  - Consistent failure to identify correct diagnosis across multiple trials
  - Output that is too general rather than specific (e.g., "anemia" instead of "iron deficiency anemia due to GI bleeding")
  - Failure to identify essential diagnostic tests even when diagnosis is correct

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test prompt variations (e.g., "most likely" vs "top three" vs "all possible" diagnoses) to measure impact on accuracy

## Limitations

- The study only tested one specific prompt structure, limiting understanding of how prompt engineering affects performance
- No comparison was made between AI model performance and human clinician performance on the same cases
- The sample size of 50 cases may be insufficient to draw definitive conclusions about model capabilities

## Confidence

- High confidence: The fundamental finding that current LLMs struggle with complex, open-ended clinical cases requiring definitive diagnosis
- Medium confidence: The claim that models perform better at differential diagnosis than definitive diagnosis, based on observed accuracy patterns
- Low confidence: The assertion that there are no significant differences between GPT4 and GPT3.5 performance, given the limited sample size and specific task type
