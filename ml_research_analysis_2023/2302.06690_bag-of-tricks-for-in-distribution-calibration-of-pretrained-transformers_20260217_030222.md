# Bag of Tricks for In-Distribution Calibration of Pretrained Transformers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.06690
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06690
- **Reference count**: 9
- **Primary result**: Proposes CALL framework combining BL+ERL, MixUp, and ensemble learning to improve PLM calibration without sacrificing accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of improving in-distribution calibration for pre-trained language models in text classification tasks. The authors conduct an extensive empirical analysis of three families of calibration methods: confidence penalty losses, data augmentations, and ensemble methods. Through systematic experiments on multiple datasets, they identify key limitations in existing approaches, including the trade-off between calibration and accuracy when using confidence penalty losses, and the loss of predictive diversity in ensemble methods due to overfitting. The proposed CALL framework combines BL+ERL, MixUp, and ensemble learning to overcome these limitations, achieving improved calibration performance without sacrificing accuracy.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes RoBERTa (roberta-base) on text classification datasets using various calibration methods including confidence penalty losses (entropy regularized loss, label smoothing), data augmentations (MixUp, EDA, SR), and ensemble methods (DE, MIMO, MCDrop). Models are trained for 10 epochs with Adam optimizer, weight decay, warmup, batch size, dropout rate, and learning rate as specified. Calibration performance is evaluated using accuracy, Expected Calibration Error (ECE) with 15 bins, and Negative Log Likelihood (NLL) multiplied by 10^2. The proposed CALL framework combines BL+ERL, MixUp, and ensemble learning to address the limitations of individual methods.

## Key Results
- Ensemble methods lose predictive diversity as training progresses, reducing their calibration effectiveness
- Confidence penalty losses exhibit a trade-off between calibration and accuracy
- MixUp data augmentation shows the closest distance between training and test data distributions, making it particularly effective for calibration
- CALL framework improves calibration without sacrificing accuracy, outperforming established baselines on multiple text classification datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Confidence penalty losses improve calibration by reducing overconfidence in model predictions
- Mechanism: Entropy regularized loss penalizes confident output distributions by adding negative entropy to the loss function, forcing the model to produce less peaked probability distributions
- Core assumption: PLMs are inherently overconfident due to their overparameterized nature, and this overconfidence can be reduced by penalizing high confidence in the loss function
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that the ensemble model overfitted to the training set shows sub-par calibration performance and also observe that PLMs trained with confidence penalty loss have a trade-off between calibration and accuracy."
  - [section 4.2] "Pereyra et al. (2017) propose the entropy regularized loss which penalizes confident output distributions in order to reduce overfitting."
- Break condition: The regularization strength is too high, causing accuracy to drop significantly while only providing marginal calibration improvements

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data augmentation methods improve calibration by exposing models to diverse patterns and reducing overfitting
- Mechanism: MixUp and EDA generate diverse augmented samples that pull the training data distribution closer to the unseen test data distribution, helping the model generalize better and produce more calibrated predictions
- Core assumption: PLMs overfit to the training data distribution, and exposure to diverse augmented samples helps match the training and test distributions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.5] "We analyze the distance between unseen and training data distribution, assuming that the augmentation scheme that pulls the distribution of training data towards the unseen data distribution will be effective for calibration."
  - [section 5.5] "RoBERTa trained with MixUp shows the closest distance between training data and test data, followed by EDA."
- Break condition: The augmentation method generates samples that are too far from the original data distribution, causing the model to learn incorrect patterns

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble methods improve calibration through predictive diversity, but this diversity can be lost due to overfitting
- Mechanism: Deep Ensemble and MIMO combine multiple models to produce averaged predictions, reducing calibration errors by leveraging the diversity of individual model predictions
- Core assumption: Diverse predictions from ensemble members lead to better-calibrated outputs, but this diversity can be reduced if individual members overfit to the same training data
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that the ensemble model overfitted to the training set shows sub-par calibration performance"
  - [section 5.5] "We investigate whether BL+ERL and MixUp methods can compensate for the aforementioned limitation of the ensemble method."
- Break condition: All ensemble members become too similar in their predictions due to overfitting, eliminating the diversity benefit

## Limitations

- The empirical analysis is primarily focused on RoBERTa as the base model, limiting generalizability to other transformer architectures
- The study only examines text classification tasks, leaving open questions about the effectiveness of CALL for other NLP tasks
- The evaluation metrics may not capture all aspects of model reliability, as ECE is sensitive to binning strategy

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The finding that ensemble methods lose predictive diversity during training due to overfitting is well-supported by the empirical evidence and multiple analyses presented in the paper. The observation that MixUp and BL+ERL can compensate for this limitation is also strongly supported.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that CALL consistently improves calibration across all datasets has moderate confidence due to the limited number of datasets tested. While the results are positive across SST2, 20NG, and TREC, additional datasets would strengthen this claim.

**Medium Confidence**: The assertion that there exists a trade-off between calibration and accuracy when using confidence penalty losses is supported by the experiments, but the specific nature and extent of this trade-off may vary depending on task and dataset characteristics.

## Next Checks

1. **Diversity Monitoring in Ensemble Training**: Implement systematic monitoring of ensemble member diversity throughout training by tracking the disagreement score between members. This would validate the observation that diversity decreases over time and help identify the exact training epochs where calibration performance degrades.

2. **Ablation Study on CALL Components**: Conduct a systematic ablation study that isolates the contribution of each component in CALL (BL+ERL, MixUp, ensemble) across different datasets. This would help quantify the marginal benefit of combining all three components versus using them individually.

3. **Cross-Architecture Validation**: Test the CALL framework on other transformer architectures beyond RoBERTa (such as BERT, DeBERTa, or DistilBERT) to verify whether the calibration improvements generalize across different PLM implementations. This would address a key limitation in the current study's scope.
