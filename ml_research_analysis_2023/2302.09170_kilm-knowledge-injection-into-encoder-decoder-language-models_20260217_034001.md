# KILM: Knowledge Injection into Encoder-Decoder Language Models
## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
KILM (Knowledge Injection into Encoder-Decoder Language Models) is a method that enhances pre-trained encoder-decoder models with entity knowledge through continued pre-training, without requiring architectural modifications or additional parameters. The approach uses a generative knowledge infilling objective with structured prompts that pair entities with their Wikipedia descriptions. KILM demonstrates significant improvements on knowledge-intensive tasks including entity disambiguation, appositive generation, question answering, and knowledge-grounded response generation. Notably, a 139M parameter KILM model outperforms state-of-the-art models with 30x more parameters on entity disambiguation tasks.

## Method Summary
KILM works by continued pre-training of existing encoder-decoder models like BART using a knowledge infilling objective. The method employs structured prompts that align entities with their short descriptions from Wikipedia, creating a generative task where the model must complete entity-knowledge pairs. This approach injects factual knowledge into the model's parameters without requiring architectural changes or additional parameters. The knowledge injection process involves training on entity-description pairs, enabling the model to better retain and utilize factual information during inference. The method preserves the base model's performance on general language tasks while specifically enhancing capabilities for knowledge-intensive applications.

## Key Results
- KILM improves knowledge retention and reduces hallucination in knowledge-intensive tasks
- 139M parameter KILM outperforms state-of-the-art models with 30x more parameters on entity disambiguation
- The method maintains BART's performance on general language tasks while enhancing knowledge handling capabilities
- Demonstrates better zero-shot performance on entity disambiguation tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
KILM works by leveraging the generative nature of encoder-decoder models to internalize factual knowledge through continued pre-training. The structured prompts create a constrained generation task where the model must learn to associate entities with their corresponding descriptions, effectively memorizing and organizing factual information in its parameters. This knowledge injection occurs at the parameter level rather than through architectural modifications, making it parameter-efficient. The generative knowledge infilling objective forces the model to understand entity-context relationships deeply, as it must generate coherent completions that accurately reflect factual knowledge.

## Limitations
- Evaluation primarily focused on English-language tasks with Wikipedia-based knowledge sources
- Knowledge infilling objective relies on structured prompts that may not capture all forms of entity knowledge
- Comparison with larger models requires careful interpretation due to potential differences in training data and optimization

## Confidence
**Knowledge Injection Effectiveness**: High confidence - well-supported by multiple benchmarks and ablation studies
**Zero-Shot Performance**: Medium confidence - requires closer scrutiny of evaluation protocol and comparison metrics
**Generalizability**: Low confidence - current implementation narrowly focused on English Wikipedia entities
