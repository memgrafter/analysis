# Bayesian Free Energy of Deep ReLU Neural Network in Overparametrized Cases

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15739
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15739
- Reference count: 32
- The paper proves that Bayesian free energy remains bounded even when deep ReLU networks are overparametrized with more layers than necessary to estimate the data-generating function.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies the Bayesian free energy of deep ReLU neural networks in overparametrized cases, proving that the free energy remains bounded even when the number of layers exceeds what is necessary to represent the data-generating function. The key insight is that ReLU activation functions allow redundant units to be "zeroed out" by optimal parameters, preventing additional layers from increasing model complexity. The paper establishes that the average free energy satisfies E[Fn] ≤ nS + λReLU log n + C, where λReLU > 0 remains bounded even as the network becomes deeper, showing that Bayesian generalization error does not increase with overparameterization.

## Method Summary
The paper applies Bayesian learning theory to deep ReLU neural networks, modeling them as statistical models where the true distribution is generated by a ReLU network with N* layers. The method constructs an "essential parameter set" WE that includes parameters where redundant layers output zero values, then proves the KL divergence for these parameters is O(1/n). By analyzing the volume of this parameter set and applying singular learning theory, the paper derives bounds on the Bayesian free energy that remain valid even when the model has N ≥ N* layers with N* < N.

## Key Results
- The Bayesian free energy E[Fn] ≤ nS + λReLU log n + C remains bounded even when the network is overparametrized with N > N* layers
- The generalization error E[Gn] = λReLU/n + o(1/n) remains controlled despite overparameterization
- Redundant parameters in overparametrized layers contribute only convergent matrix terms of order 1/√n to the KL divergence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Bayesian free energy of a deep ReLU network is bounded by O(nS + λReLU log n + C), where λReLU does not increase with the number of layers.
- Mechanism: The ReLU activation function's piecewise linearity and non-differentiability allow the model to have optimal parameters that "zero out" redundant units in overparametrized layers, effectively preventing an increase in model complexity from additional layers.
- Core assumption: The data-generating distribution is representable by a ReLU network with N* layers and the learning model uses N ≥ N* layers with widths satisfying the inclusion conditions (21-23).
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "the Bayesian free energy, which is equal to the minus log marginal likelihood or the Bayesian stochastic complexity, is bounded even if the number of layers are larger than necessary to estimate an unknown data-generating function." [section 3.2] "There exists a parameter ˆw, ˆb which satisfies q(y|x) = p(y| ˆw, ˆb,x ). Such parameters are called optimal parameter." Followed by construction showing redundant layers output zero.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The generalization error E[Gn] is bounded by λReLU/n + o(1/n) even when the network is overparametrized.
- Mechanism: Because the free energy increase from sample n to n+1 is bounded by λReLU log(n+1)/n, the expected generalization error, which equals this difference, remains controlled.
- Core assumption: The free energy expansion E[Fn] = nS + λReLU log n + C holds asymptotically, and the relationship E[Gn] = E[Fn+1] - E[Fn] - S applies.
- Evidence anchors: [section 2] "E[Gn] = E[Fn+1]− E[Fn]−S, where Gn is the Kullback-Leibler divergence from a data-generating distribution to the Bayesian predictive distribution and S is the entropy of the data-generating distribution." [section 3.3] "E[Fn]≤nS +λReLU logn +C ... If the generalization error has asymptotic expansion, then it should be E[Gn] = λReLU /n +o(1/n)."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Redundant parameters in overparametrized layers contribute only to a convergent matrix term of order 1/√n in the KL divergence.
- Mechanism: Parameters in redundant layers are structured such that their deviations from optimal values (captured in convergent matrices E(k)) contribute terms of O(1/√n) to the output difference, making their impact on the KL divergence vanish as n increases.
- Core assumption: The weight and bias parameters are in the "essential parameter set WE" defined in section 4, which constrains redundant parameters to have convergent or small-constant entries.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4] Definition of essential parameter set WE and Lemmas 4.5, 4.6 showing that f(N)(w,b,x) - f(N*)(w*,b*,x) = O(√n (||x|| + 1)), and thus K(w,b) = O(1/n). [section 5] Proof showing ∫WE exp(-nK(w,b))ϕ(w,b)dwdb ≥ C n^(-λ), with λ = 1/2 × (number of parameters in convergent matrices/vectors).

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Bayesian inference engine -> deep ReLU neural network model p(y|x,w,b) with N layers and Gaussian noise N(0,IHN)
- Critical path: 1) Define the essential parameter set WE based on the true parameter (w*,b*) and the model structure. 2) Prove that for parameters in WE, the KL divergence K(w,b) is O(1/n). 3) Show the volume of WE is Ω(n^(-λ)) where λ is half the number of convergent parameters. 4) Conclude that E[Fn] ≤ nS + λReLU log n + C.
- Design tradeoffs: Using ReLU allows the network to "zero out" redundant units, preventing overparametrization from increasing complexity. However, ReLU's non-differentiability means standard singular learning theory cannot be applied directly, requiring the convergent matrix construction. Overparametrization (N > N*) is beneficial for bias reduction without increasing variance.
- Failure signatures: If the data-generating function cannot be represented by any ReLU network of N* layers, the essential parameter set WE cannot be constructed and the bound fails. If the support of q(x) is unbounded and not nonnegative, the λReLU formula changes and the bound may be looser. If the prior ϕ(w,b) is too small on WE, the integral in the proof may not be bounded below by C n^(-λ).
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate synthetic data from a known ReLU network (N*, H*k) and fit a larger ReLU network (N ≥ N*, Hk ≥ H*k). Compute the empirical free energy and check if it scales as nS + λReLU log n + C.
  2. Vary the number of layers N while keeping other parameters fixed. Check if the fitted model's free energy remains bounded as N increases beyond N*.
  3. Change the support of the input distribution q(x) to be unbounded or include negative values. Verify if the λReLU formula and free energy bound still hold.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the presence of non-differentiable ReLU activation functions affect the Bayesian free energy bounds in overparameterized deep neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper studies a deep ReLU neural network in overparametrized cases and proves that the Bayesian free energy is bounded even if the number of layers are larger than necessary to estimate the data-generating function.
- Why unresolved: While the paper proves bounds on the Bayesian free energy, the specific impact of ReLU's non-differentiability on these bounds is not fully explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further theoretical analysis or empirical studies comparing the Bayesian free energy bounds with and without ReLU activation functions in overparameterized networks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the input distribution's support (bounded or nonnegative region) on the Bayesian free energy and generalization error in deep ReLU neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper provides different formulas for the Bayesian free energy bound depending on whether the support of the input distribution is bounded or contained in a nonnegative region.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the practical implications of these different formulas or provide empirical evidence for their validity in real-world scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental studies comparing the generalization error and Bayesian free energy bounds in deep ReLU networks with different input distribution supports.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the overparameterization of deep ReLU neural networks affect the generalization error in unrealizable cases where the data-generating distribution is not exactly representable by a ReLU function?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses unrealizable cases and suggests that the same inequality as the main theorem holds if the learning machine is sufficiently large to approximate the data-generating distribution.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a rigorous proof or empirical evidence for this claim in unrealizable cases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis or empirical studies demonstrating the validity of the Bayesian free energy bounds and their impact on generalization error in unrealizable cases with overparameterized deep ReLU networks.

## Limitations

- The theoretical construction showing redundant layers "zero out" depends on precise parameter relationships that may not hold in practice
- The paper provides no empirical validation of the free energy scaling claims or the essential parameter set construction
- The corpus analysis reveals no supporting citations for the key mechanisms, suggesting these results may be novel but unverified by independent work

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The relationship between free energy and generalization error (E[Gn] = E[Fn+1] - E[Fn] - S) is well-established in Bayesian learning theory
- **Medium confidence**: The asymptotic expansion E[Fn] ≤ nS + λReLU log n + C follows from singular learning theory but requires the specific convergent matrix construction
- **Low confidence**: The practical applicability of the essential parameter set construction and its ability to constrain redundant parameters as claimed
