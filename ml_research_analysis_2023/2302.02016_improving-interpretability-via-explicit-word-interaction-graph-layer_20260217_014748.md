# Improving Interpretability via Explicit Word Interaction Graph Layer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02016
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02016
- Reference count: 28
- Key outcome: WIGRAPH improves interpretability and prediction accuracy across multiple SOTA NLP models and datasets by learning global word interactions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces WIGRAPH, a trainable layer that learns global word interaction graphs to improve both interpretability and performance of neural NLP text classifiers. WIGRAPH can be easily plugged into any neural network-based text classifier after the word embedding layer. The approach uses a stochastic edge discovery module to learn interactions between words, a neural message passing module to update word representations, and a variational information bottleneck loss to suppress irrelevant interactions. Experiments demonstrate that WIGRAPH improves interpretability metrics while maintaining or enhancing prediction accuracy across multiple state-of-the-art NLP models and datasets.

## Method Summary
WIGRAPH is designed as a plug-and-play layer that learns a global interaction graph between words using a stochastic edge discovery module and updates word representations through graph convolution. The model is trained with a variational information bottleneck loss (VIB-WI) that balances maximizing mutual information between revised word representations and prediction labels while minimizing mutual information with the input. This forces the model to focus on important word interactions by restricting irrelevant information flow. The approach is tested on multiple text classification tasks including SST-1, SST-2, IMDB, AG News, TREC, and Subj datasets using various base models like LSTM, BERT, RoBERTa, and distilBERT.

## Key Results
- WIGRAPH improves interpretability metrics (AOPC from LIME and SampleShapley) across all tested datasets and models
- The approach maintains or enhances prediction accuracy compared to baseline models
- Qualitative visualizations show WIGRAPH effectively captures meaningful word interactions that contribute to model predictions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: WIGRAPH improves interpretability by learning global word interactions that guide the model's decision-making process
- Mechanism: The stochastic edge discovery module learns a global interaction graph A between words, which is then used in a graph convolution update to revise word representations based on their interacting neighbors
- Core assumption: Word interactions are critical for explaining how NLP models make decisions, and explicitly modeling these interactions will improve interpretability
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A few recent studies proposed to develop inherently interpretable models... Several other studies proposed to develop inherently interpretable models, but these require intensive engineering efforts."
  - [section 2.1] "Our primary strategy to describe how words relate is to treat words as nodes and their interaction as edges in an interaction graph."
- Break condition: If the learned interaction graph A does not capture meaningful relationships between words or if the graph convolution update does not effectively revise word representations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The variational information bottleneck loss (VIB-WI) forces the model to focus on important word interactions by restricting irrelevant information flow
- Mechanism: VIB-WI loss balances maximizing mutual information between Z (revised word representations) and Y (prediction label) while minimizing mutual information between Z and X (input), effectively suppressing irrelevant word interactions
- Core assumption: Restricting information flow from irrelevant word interactions will lead the model to focus on important interactions for predictions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.4] "VIB-WI loss aims to restrict the information of globally irrelevant word interactions flowing to subsequent network layers, hence forcing the model to focus on important interactions to make predictions."
  - [section 2.4] "Following the Information Bottleneck framework (Alemi et al. 2016), we aim to learn A and all subsequent layers' weights {W}, to make Z maximally informative of the prediction label Y, while being maximally compressive of X."
- Break condition: If the VIB-WI loss does not effectively balance the trade-off between maximizing relevant information and minimizing irrelevant information

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: WIGRAPH can be easily integrated into existing neural text classifiers without requiring significant architectural changes or external priors
- Mechanism: WIGRAPH is designed as a plug-and-play layer that can be inserted right after the word embedding layer in any neural text classifier, augmenting its interpretability without sacrificing prediction performance
- Core assumption: A simple model augmentation with a layer that learns word interactions can improve interpretability without requiring complex architectural changes or external knowledge
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our layer, we call WIGRAPH, can plug into any neural network-based NLP text classifiers right after its word embedding layer."
  - [section 2.3] "Our main goal is to improve the intrinsic interpretability of neural text classifiers with a simple model augmentation. Therefore, for a given neural text classifier, we propose to simply insert a WIGRAPH layer right after the word embedding input layer and before the subsequent network layers of that target model."
- Break condition: If inserting the WIGRAPH layer significantly degrades the performance of the base model or if it requires substantial architectural changes to integrate

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Word embedding layer -> WIGRAPH layer -> Subsequent network layers
- Critical path: Input words -> Word embeddings -> WIGRAPH layer (learn interactions -> update representations) -> Subsequent network layers -> Prediction
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational complexity: Learning the interaction graph and performing graph convolution may increase computational cost compared to the base model
  - Model interpretability vs. performance: While WIGRAPH aims to improve interpretability, it may slightly impact the prediction performance of the base model
  - Hyperparameter tuning: The performance of WIGRAPH depends on the careful tuning of hyperparameters such as βi, βg, and βsparse
- Failure signatures:
  - If the learned interaction graph A does not capture meaningful relationships between words, the model may not improve interpretability
  - If the VIB-WI loss is not properly balanced, the model may focus on irrelevant interactions or fail to consider important ones
  - If the WIGRAPH layer is not properly integrated into the base model, it may cause performance degradation or instability during training
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Integrate WIGRAPH into a simple LSTM-based text classifier and evaluate its performance on a binary sentiment classification task (e.g., SST-2)
  2. Compare the interpretability of the WIGRAPH-augmented model with the base model using post-hoc explanation methods like LIME or SampleShapley
  3. Analyze the learned interaction graph A and identify meaningful word interactions that contribute to the model's predictions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of WIGRAPH vary with different sparsity levels of the learned interaction graph?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions adding a sparsity regularization term to the loss function, but does not extensively explore the impact of different sparsity levels on model performance and interpretability
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic analysis of how varying the sparsity hyperparameter affects the quality of learned interactions, prediction accuracy, or interpretability metrics across different datasets and model architectures
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments varying the sparsity regularization strength and analyzing its impact on model performance, interaction quality, and interpretability measures across multiple datasets and model types

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can WIGRAPH effectively capture higher-order interactions beyond pairwise relationships between words or concepts?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on learning pairwise interactions but mentions that the message passing framework could potentially be extended to higher-order interactions in future work
- Why unresolved: The current implementation only considers pairwise interactions, and the paper does not explore whether capturing higher-order interactions (e.g., interactions among three or more words/concepts) would provide additional benefits in terms of interpretability or performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance and interpretability of WIGRAPH variants that capture higher-order interactions versus the current pairwise approach across multiple tasks and datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is WIGRAPH to noise in the interaction graph, such as incorrect or spurious learned interactions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] While the paper uses a variational information bottleneck to suppress irrelevant interactions, it does not explicitly evaluate the robustness of the model to noise in the learned interaction graph
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate how errors in the learned interaction graph (e.g., false positive or false negative interactions) affect the model's performance and interpretability, nor does it propose methods to improve robustness to such noise
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments systematically injecting noise into the learned interaction graph and measuring the impact on model performance and interpretability, as well as exploring techniques to improve robustness to graph noise

## Limitations
- Performance heavily depends on hyperparameter tuning (βi, βg, βsparse)
- Computational overhead of learning interaction graph and graph convolution may be prohibitive for very large models
- Assumes word interactions can be effectively captured in a global graph structure, which may not hold for all text types or languages

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of learning word interactions through graph convolution is well-established in the literature and the mathematical framework is sound
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of the VIB-WI loss in improving interpretability, as this depends heavily on proper hyperparameter tuning and may vary across datasets
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that WIGRAPH can be easily integrated into any neural text classifier without significant architectural changes, as practical implementation may reveal hidden complexities
