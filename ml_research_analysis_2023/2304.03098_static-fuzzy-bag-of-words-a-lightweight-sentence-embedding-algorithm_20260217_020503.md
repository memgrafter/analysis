# Static Fuzzy Bag-of-Words: a lightweight sentence embedding algorithm

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03098
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03098
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SFBoW achieves competitive STS benchmark performance with significantly lower computational resources than complex models

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SFBoW, a lightweight non-parametric sentence embedding model that improves upon the Fuzzy Bag-of-Words approach by providing fixed-dimensional sentence representations. The model uses fuzzy set theory and fuzzy Jaccard similarity to compute semantic similarity between sentences, avoiding the variable-sized embeddings of its predecessor DynaMax. Experiments on Semantic Textual Similarity benchmarks show that SFBoW achieves competitive results compared to more complex models like DynaMax and Sent2Vec, while requiring significantly less computational resources. The best performance was achieved using FastText word embeddings combined with either identity matrix or PCA-based universe matrices, with average Spearman correlation scores around 65-66 across five STS tasks.

## Method Summary
SFBoW generates sentence embeddings by treating sentences as fuzzy sets where word membership is determined by semantic similarity to a predefined universe matrix. The model constructs sentence embeddings through aggregation of pre-trained word embeddings using max operations, then computes similarity using fuzzy Jaccard index. Unlike DynaMax which creates variable-sized embeddings, SFBoW uses a fixed universe matrix (either identity, PCA-rotated, or clustering-based) to produce consistent dimensional embeddings. The approach is non-parametric, requiring only the embedding matrix parameters and minimal training, making it computationally efficient while maintaining competitive performance on STS benchmarks.

## Key Results
- SFBoW outperforms DynaMax and Sent2Vec on multiple STS tasks using FastText embeddings
- Identity matrix and PCA-based universe matrices perform better than clustering approaches
- Computational efficiency is significantly improved compared to parametric models
- Spearman correlation scores average around 65-66 across five STS tasks with FastText + PCA

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fuzzy Jaccard similarity better captures semantic similarity than cosine similarity for sentence embeddings
- Mechanism: By representing sentences as fuzzy sets where word membership is based on semantic similarity, the model captures partial matches between semantically related words that traditional set-based measures miss
- Core assumption: Semantic similarity between words can be effectively approximated using pre-trained word embedding similarity scores
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SFBoW provides competitive performances in Semantic Textual Similarity benchmarks, while requiring low computational resources"
  - [section]: "the fuzzy Jaccard index to compute semantic similarity from the sentence embeddings (differently from most of the other models, which rely on cosine similarity)"
  - [corpus]: "Average neighbor FMR=0.554, average citations=0.2" (weak corpus evidence for this specific mechanism)
- Break condition: When semantic similarity between words is not well-captured by the underlying word embedding model, leading to poor fuzzy membership calculations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fixed-dimensional embeddings enable efficient reuse across different sentence comparisons
- Mechanism: By using a predefined universe matrix (either identity or PCA-based), the model generates embeddings of consistent dimension regardless of sentence content, avoiding the variable-sized embeddings of DynaMax
- Core assumption: A single universe matrix can effectively represent the semantic space for diverse sentence pairs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "providing sentence embeddings with a predefined dimension"
  - [section]: "our follow up aimed at providing a unique matrix U and thus embeddings with a predefined dimension"
  - [corpus]: "weak corpus evidence for this specific mechanism" (no direct citations on this dimension fix approach)
- Break condition: When the fixed universe matrix cannot adequately capture the semantic variability across diverse sentence pairs, leading to information loss

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Lightweight models can achieve competitive performance without extensive training
- Mechanism: By aggregating pre-trained word embeddings through fuzzy set operations rather than training complex neural networks, the model achieves reasonable performance with minimal computational overhead
- Core assumption: Simple aggregation of word embeddings can capture sufficient sentence-level semantics for similarity tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SFBoW provides competitive performances in Semantic Textual Similarity (STS), without the necessity of high computational power"
  - [section]: "non-parametric models... require way less parameters – just those for the embedding matrix|V|× d – and little, if any, training"
  - [corpus]: "Average neighbor FMR=0.554" (moderate corpus evidence suggesting lightweight approaches are viable)
- Break condition: When sentence semantics require complex contextual understanding that simple aggregation cannot capture

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: The model represents sentences as fuzzy sets where words have varying degrees of membership based on semantic similarity
  - Quick check question: How does fuzzy membership differ from binary membership in traditional set theory, and why is this important for semantic similarity?

- Concept: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction
  - Why needed here: PCA is used to create the universe matrix by finding orthogonal directions of maximum variance in the word embedding space
  - Quick check question: What does each principal component represent in the context of word embeddings, and how does this relate to semantic features?

- Concept: Spearman's rank correlation coefficient
  - Why needed here: This is the evaluation metric used to compare model predictions against human similarity judgments
  - Quick check question: How does Spearman's correlation differ from Pearson's correlation, and why is it more appropriate for evaluating similarity rankings?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input: Pre-trained word embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText, or Sent2Vec)
  -> Universe matrix construction (identity, PCA, or clustering-based)
  -> Fuzzy embedding computation for each word using universe matrix
  -> Sentence embedding aggregation via max operation
  -> Similarity computation using fuzzy Jaccard index
  -> Output: Similarity score for sentence pairs

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained word embeddings
  2. Construct universe matrix (identity/PCA approach recommended)
  3. For each sentence pair:
     - Compute fuzzy embeddings for all words
     - Aggregate to sentence embeddings
     - Calculate fuzzy Jaccard similarity
  4. Evaluate against STS benchmarks using Spearman correlation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed vs. variable dimensionality: SFBoW trades some flexibility for reusability and computational efficiency
  - Universe matrix choice: Identity matrix is simplest but PCA may capture more semantic structure
  - Word embedding selection: FastText generally performs best but requires more storage than Word2Vec

- Failure signatures:
  - Low correlation scores on STS benchmarks indicate poor semantic capture
  - Performance degradation on sentences with rare words suggests vocabulary coverage issues
  - Inconsistent results across different STS tasks may indicate universe matrix limitations

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic SFBoW with identity matrix and FastText embeddings on STS 2015
  2. Compare performance with PCA-based universe matrix using same embeddings
  3. Test different word embedding sources (Word2Vec vs. GloVe vs. FastText) with fixed identity matrix

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SFBoW's performance compare to Sentence-BERT on datasets outside the STS benchmark?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "even if SFBoW doesn’t achieve state-of-the-art performances on the considered STS benchmark...it outperforms in the majority of tasks all the other compared models except Sentence-BERT"
- Why unresolved: The experiments only tested on STS tasks. The authors acknowledge that Sentence-BERT uses a much bigger model and corpus, but don't test cross-dataset generalization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing SFBoW and Sentence-BERT on other semantic similarity datasets (e.g., MRPC, QQP) to compare performance and resource efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of clusters k for the k-Means clustering approach in universe matrix construction?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper tested various k values (100, 1000, 10000, 25000) but states "there's still room for improvement" and clustering provided poor results compared to Identity/PCA approaches.
- Why unresolved: The experiments show inconsistent results across different k values and embedding types. The authors note that Spherical k-Means performs better than standard k-Means but don't determine the optimal k.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of additional k values and comparison of clustering quality metrics (WCSoS, BCSoS) across all embedding types to identify the optimal balance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of vocabulary subset affect SFBoW's performance and computational efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors tested three vocabulary subsets: entire vocabulary, top 100000 frequent words, and spell-checked dictionary subset. They note this was done "to reduce the computational effort needed for construction and usage of the universe matrix."
- Why unresolved: While computational efficiency was mentioned as a motivation, the paper doesn't provide quantitative analysis of the trade-off between performance and efficiency across the different vocabulary subsets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of runtime and memory usage for each vocabulary subset, correlated with performance metrics to determine the optimal subset size for different computational constraints.

## Limitations
- Clustering-based universe matrices show inconsistent performance across different configurations and embedding types
- The model's semantic capture may be limited when dealing with complex contextual understanding beyond simple word similarity
- No comprehensive ablation studies comparing different implementation choices or vocabulary subset sizes

## Confidence
- High confidence: The fixed-dimensional embedding approach and basic fuzzy Jaccard similarity mechanism
- Medium confidence: Performance claims relative to DynaMax and Sent2Vec, as these comparisons depend on specific experimental conditions
- Low confidence: The relative merits of different universe matrix construction methods, particularly clustering approaches
