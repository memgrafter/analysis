# Turning large language models into cognitive models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03917
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03917
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- One-line primary result: CENTaUR finetunes LLaMA on psychological datasets to predict human choices with significantly better accuracy than baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CENTaUR, a method for turning large language models into cognitive models by finetuning them on data from psychological experiments. The approach extracts embeddings from LLaMA and fits a linear layer to predict human choices in decision-making tasks. CENTaUR significantly outperforms both the raw LLaMA model and domain-specific baselines on two well-studied paradigms: choices from descriptions and decisions from experience. Most impressively, a model finetuned on both tasks successfully generalizes to predict behavior on a held-out experiential-symbolic task.

## Method Summary
CENTaUR finetunes LLaMA by extracting embeddings from task-specific prompts and fitting a regularized logistic regression model to predict human choices. The method uses 100-fold cross-validation with nested cross-validation for hyperparameter tuning. It evaluates on choices from descriptions (choices13k dataset) and decisions from experience (horizon task), comparing against random guessing, raw LLaMA, and domain-specific baselines like BEAST and hybrid exploration-exploitation models.

## Key Results
- For choices from descriptions, CENTaUR achieves 48,002.3 negative log-likelihood versus BEAST's 49,448.1
- For decisions from experience, CENTaUR achieves 25,968.6 versus hybrid model's 29,042.5
- A joint model finetuned on both tasks successfully generalizes to predict behavior on a held-out experiential-symbolic task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLaMA embeddings contain rich cognitive features that enable prediction of human decisions
- Mechanism: LLaMA is pre-trained on vast text data including language about decisions, risks, and probabilities. This training creates internal representations that capture human conceptualizations of decision contexts. When finetuned on specific behavioral datasets, these embeddings provide sufficient information for a simple linear layer to predict human choices accurately.
- Core assumption: Language models trained on large text corpora implicitly learn representations of decision-making concepts that correlate with human cognitive processes.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "large language models are powerful systems that excel at many tasks, ranging from translation to mathematical reasoning"; [section] "We obtained embeddings by passing prompts that included all the information that people had access to on a given trial through LLaMA and then extracting the hidden activations of the final layer"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Finetuning adapts LLaMA embeddings to specific human decision patterns
- Mechanism: The linear layer trained on top of LLaMA embeddings learns to map rich contextual embeddings to human choice probabilities. The finetuning process adjusts weights to minimize prediction error on behavioral data, effectively transforming generic language representations into task-specific cognitive models.
- Core assumption: A simple linear transformation can adequately map LLaMA embeddings to human choice probabilities when trained on sufficient behavioral data.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "With these two data sets at hand, we fitted a regularized logistic regression model from the extracted embeddings to human choices"; [section] "we fitted a joint model using both the data from the choices13k data set and the horizon task, and then evaluated how well the finetuned model captures human choices on a third task"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Shared embedding space enables generalization across decision-making tasks
- Mechanism: Because all task embeddings come from the same LLaMA model, they occupy a common representational space. This shared space allows a model finetuned on multiple tasks to leverage learned representations when predicting behavior on novel tasks, demonstrating transfer learning.
- Core assumption: Representations learned from different decision-making tasks share sufficient structure to enable generalization.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we demonstrate that finetuning on multiple tasks enables large language models to predict human behavior in a previously unseen task"; [section] "a model finetuned on two tasks also exhibited human-like behavior on a third, hold-out task"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  LLaMA model (65B parameters) -> Prompt generator -> Embedding extractor -> Linear layer (logistic regression) -> Evaluation pipeline -> Baseline comparison

- Critical path:
  1. Generate prompts for each trial in behavioral dataset
  2. Extract embeddings from LLaMA's final layer
  3. Split data into training/validation/test sets
  4. Fit regularized logistic regression on training set
  5. Tune regularization parameter on validation set
  6. Evaluate on test set
  7. Repeat cross-validation 100 times

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Embedding dimension vs. computational cost - using final layer provides rich features but requires significant memory
  - Linear vs. non-linear transformation - linear layer is simple but may miss complex relationships
  - Joint vs. separate finetuning - joint model enables transfer but may dilute task-specific learning

- Failure signatures:
  - NLL close to random guessing baseline indicates poor model fit
  - Large gap between training and test performance suggests overfitting
  - Individual-level NLL worse than aggregate suggests inability to capture individual differences

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run the complete pipeline on choices13k dataset only to verify basic functionality
  2. Compare LLaMA embeddings vs. raw text features as input to logistic regression
  3. Test different embedding layer depths (e.g., penultimate vs. final layer) for predictive performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can CENTaUR be scaled up to finetune on a larger number of tasks from the psychology literature to create a truly domain-general cognitive model?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the potential to scale up the approach to finetuning on more tasks from psychology literature to create a domain-general model of human cognition.
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents preliminary results finetuning on two tasks. It remains to be seen if scaling up to more tasks would allow the model to generalize to any hold-out task.
- What evidence would resolve it: Results from finetuning CENTaUR on a much larger number of diverse psychological tasks, and testing its ability to generalize to a wide range of new tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What can we learn about human cognition by applying explainability techniques to finetuned large language models like CENTaUR?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The discussion section mentions that having an accurate neural network model of human behavior provides the opportunity to apply a wide range of explainability techniques to learn more about human cognition.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not apply any explainability techniques to CENTaUR. It is unclear what insights such techniques could provide about the underlying cognitive processes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Results from applying techniques like layer-wise relevance propagation to analyze which input features excite particular neurons in CENTaUR, and how this relates to human cognitive processes.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How well can CENTaUR capture individual differences in decision-making beyond the horizon task where individual-level data was available?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that incorporating random effects in the finetuned layer allows CENTaUR to capture individual differences in the horizon task, improving fit significantly.
- Why unresolved: Individual-level data was not available for the choices13k dataset, so it is unclear if the random effects approach would generalize to other tasks beyond the horizon task.
- What evidence would resolve it: Results from applying the random effects approach to other psychological tasks where individual-level data is available, and comparing the fit to a fixed-effects only model.

## Limitations
- The approach relies heavily on LLaMA's ability to generate meaningful embeddings from textual descriptions of decision tasks
- The linear transformation assumes a relatively simple relationship between embeddings and choices, which may not hold for all decision tasks
- Results depend on the quality and diversity of the pre-training corpus containing sufficient decision-making contexts

## Confidence
- High confidence: The method's ability to predict human choices significantly better than random guessing and raw LLaMA
- Medium confidence: The claim that CENTaUR exhibits human-like behavior in exploration-exploitation patterns
- Medium confidence: The generalization to unseen tasks
