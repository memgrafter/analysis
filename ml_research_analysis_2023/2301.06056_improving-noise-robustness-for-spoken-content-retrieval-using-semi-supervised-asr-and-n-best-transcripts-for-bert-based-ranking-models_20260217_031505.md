# Improving Noise Robustness for Spoken Content Retrieval using Semi-supervised ASR and N-best Transcripts for BERT-based Ranking Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06056
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06056
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Combining semi-supervised transcripts with N-best early fusion for BERT DR reduced the MRR gap between manual and ASR transcripts by more than 50% from 14.32% to 6.58%

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of reduced search effectiveness in spoken content retrieval (SCR) systems when using automatic speech recognition (ASR) transcripts compared to manual transcripts. The authors investigate two approaches to mitigate ASR errors: semi-supervised ASR transcripts that leverage domain adaptation, and N-best ASR transcripts with early and late fusion methods for BERT-based ranking models. Their experiments demonstrate significant improvements in retrieval performance, particularly when combining both approaches, reducing the effectiveness gap between manual and ASR transcripts by more than half.

## Method Summary
The method involves training a semi-supervised ASR system on in-domain data from the How2 dataset to reduce word error rate (WER), then using both standard and semi-supervised ASR transcripts as input to BERT-based ranking models. For the N-best approach, multiple ASR hypotheses are generated and either early fused (by aligning and averaging word embeddings) or late fused (by combining similarity scores from separate BERT indexes). The study evaluates these approaches using Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) on a known-item search task with 500 random video titles from the How2 dataset.

## Key Results
- Semi-supervised ASR transcripts improved MRR by 2-5.5% over standard ASR transcripts
- N-best early fusion improved MRR by 3-4% for BERT DR systems
- Combined approach (semi-supervised + N-best early fusion) reduced MRR gap between manual and ASR transcripts by more than 50% (from 14.32% to 6.58%)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Semi-supervised ASR transcripts reduce WER by leveraging domain adaptation, which improves BERT-based ranking model effectiveness.
- Mechanism: By retraining the ASR system on in-domain data from the target dataset (How2), the acoustic and language models better match the spoken content characteristics, reducing transcription errors that degrade retrieval performance.
- Core assumption: The WER reduction from semi-supervised training directly correlates with improved MRR in retrieval, assuming the BERT model can leverage the cleaner input.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Semi-supervised ASR transcripts brought 2-5.5% MRR improvements over standard ASR transcripts"
  - [section 5.2] "Semi-supervised transcripts had a small impact on the improvement of MRR score by the BM25 model, the BERT re-ranking system improved the MRR score by 2%"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence linking WER reduction to MRR gains in this specific setting
- Break condition: If the domain mismatch is not significant, or if the semi-supervised adaptation does not generalize well to the test queries, WER reduction may not translate into MRR gains.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: N-best early fusion aggregates multiple ASR hypotheses to produce a more robust document representation for BERT DR.
- Mechanism: By aligning and averaging word embeddings across N-best hypotheses, the model creates a single document vector that captures the most likely content, reducing the impact of individual ASR errors.
- Core assumption: Averaging embeddings across aligned hypotheses smooths out individual recognition errors and preserves the most probable token sequence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "the tokens are transformed into word embeddings with the ith position of the aligned tokens combined by taking the average"
  - [section 5.3] "The 20-best early and late fusion over semi-supervised transcripts using ANCE BERT produced MRR of 60.15% and 59.20%"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence directly validating the alignment-averaging method's effectiveness in this task
- Break condition: If the N-best hypotheses are too dissimilar, alignment may introduce noise, or if the BERT model is sensitive to input token order, averaging may degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: N-best late fusion leverages multiple parallel BERT indexes to capture different ASR interpretations, improving recall without sacrificing precision.
- Mechanism: By creating separate dense vectors for each N-best transcript and combining their similarity scores, the system increases the chance that at least one hypothesis matches the query intent.
- Core assumption: Different ASR hypotheses may capture complementary aspects of the spoken content, and combining their retrieval scores improves overall ranking.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "The final result is calculated by summing the scores for each document from each index"
  - [section 5.3] "the late fusion approach consistently benefited from increase in N sizes"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence directly supporting the hypothesis that combining scores from multiple indexes improves MRR
- Break condition: If the hypotheses are highly redundant, late fusion may not add value; if the retrieval system cannot efficiently handle multiple indexes, latency may increase disproportionately.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: ASR system (standard vs. semi-supervised) -> transcript generation -> BERT encoder (dual encoder for DR) -> dense vector representations -> N-best fusion module (early or late) -> aggregated document representations or combined scores -> ANN search engine (Faiss) -> final ranked document list -> evaluation module -> MRR computation

- Critical path: ASR transcript generation -> BERT encoding -> (N-best fusion) -> similarity scoring -> ranking -> MRR evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Early fusion reduces the number of indexes but may lose some hypothesis diversity; late fusion preserves diversity but increases computational load.
  - Semi-supervised ASR improves transcript quality but requires additional training data and compute.
  - N-best fusion improves robustness but increases latency and resource usage.

- Failure signatures:
  - MRR does not improve after N-best fusion: hypotheses are too similar or alignment is introducing noise.
  - Latency increases without MRR gain: too many indexes or inefficient ANN search.
  - WER reduction does not translate to MRR improvement: domain mismatch persists or BERT model is not sensitive to transcript quality.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare MRR of standard ASR vs. semi-supervised ASR with BERT DR (no N-best) to confirm transcript quality impact.
  2. Implement and test 2-best early fusion on semi-supervised ASR transcripts to observe MRR changes.
  3. Compare early vs. late fusion using 5-best transcripts to determine which method yields better gains.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would BERT-based retrieval models perform on decode lattices directly compared to N-best transcripts or standard ASR output?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that applying BERT ranking models to lattice oracle transcripts further closed the MRR gap in 3-5% MRR to manual transcripts, motivating future work on BERT ranking which can effectively take as input decode lattices.
- Why unresolved: The study only examined lattice oracle transcripts as a theoretical upper bound but did not actually implement BERT models using lattice input.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing MRR scores of BERT re-ranking and DR models when using decode lattices as input versus using N-best transcripts or standard ASR output on the same evaluation set.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of negative examples for BERT DR training when memory constraints are relaxed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that 20 negative examples were used due to memory limits, and suggests that using more negative examples could potentially improve BERT models.
- Why unresolved: The study was limited by GPU memory constraints and did not explore the impact of using larger numbers of negative examples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of negative examples (e.g., 20, 50, 100, 200) while keeping other hyperparameters constant, measuring both convergence speed and final MRR scores.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the length limit of BERT tokens (512) impact retrieval effectiveness for longer spoken documents?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that only around 2.5% of the How2 collection exceeds the length limit of BERT and states this is unlikely to affect results, but suggests this could form future work.
- Why unresolved: The study did not examine the specific impact of token truncation on retrieval performance or explore strategies to handle longer documents.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing retrieval performance on documents of varying lengths (short, medium, long) with and without truncation, or with alternative strategies like hierarchical encoding or document segmentation.

## Limitations

- Limited corpus evidence directly linking WER reduction to retrieval effectiveness in this specific setting
- Mechanisms underlying N-best fusion effectiveness (early and late) are assumed rather than empirically validated
- Focus on specific task (known-item search on How2 dataset) without addressing potential limitations in open-domain retrieval or different query types

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The experimental setup is well-defined with clear baselines (BM25, BERT re-ranking, BERT DR) and specific evaluation metrics (MRR). The implementation details for BERT DR with ANCE training and the How2 dataset preprocessing are sufficiently specified for reproduction.

- Medium Confidence: The MRR improvements reported for semi-supervised ASR (2-5.5%) and N-best fusion (3-4%) are based on experiments with specific configurations (N=20 for fusion, 500 random queries). However, the study does not provide confidence intervals or statistical significance tests for these improvements.

- Low Confidence: The mechanisms underlying WER reduction → MRR improvement and N-best fusion effectiveness are assumed rather than empirically validated. There is no corpus evidence supporting the alignment-averaging method for early fusion or the hypothesis that combining scores from multiple indexes improves MRR.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct experiments to measure the correlation between WER reduction and MRR improvement across different query types and domains to validate whether WER is a reliable proxy for retrieval effectiveness.

2. Test the N-best fusion methods (early and late) with varying degrees of hypothesis similarity (e.g., N-best lists with high vs. low overlap) to identify conditions under which each method succeeds or fails.

3. Perform statistical significance tests on the MRR improvements and evaluate the methods on additional datasets or query types to assess generalization beyond the How2 known-item search task.
