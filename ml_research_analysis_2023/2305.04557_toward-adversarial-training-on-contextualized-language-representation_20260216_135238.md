# Toward Adversarial Training on Contextualized Language Representation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04557
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04557
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposed Contextualized representation-Adversarial Training (CreAT) improves adversarial robustness on PLMs by targeting encoder representations, achieving SOTA on AdvGLUE (59.1 → 61.1), HellaSWAG (93.0 → 94.9), and ANLI (68.1 → 69.3)

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the inconsistent performance gains from adversarial training (AT) on pre-trained language models (PLMs) across natural language understanding tasks. The authors discover that standard AT primarily fools the decoder part of models while having minimal impact on the encoder. They propose CreAT, which explicitly optimizes adversarial attacks to deviate the contextualized representation of the encoder, enabling global optimization that fools the entire model. CreAT produces consistent performance gains across a wider range of tasks compared to AT and demonstrates reduced sensitivity to hyperparameters.

## Method Summary
CreAT modifies standard adversarial training by introducing a dual optimization objective that simultaneously maximizes prediction divergence and representation divergence. During training, adversarial perturbations are optimized to both fool the model's predictions and maximally deviate the contextualized encoder representations measured by cosine similarity. The method uses projected gradient descent with a temperature coefficient τ to balance these objectives. CreAT is evaluated across multiple NLP tasks including MNLI, QQP, WNUT-17, DREAM, HellaSWAG, AlphaNLI, and RACE, demonstrating improved robustness and state-of-the-art performance on challenging adversarial benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Achieved new SOTA on AdvGLUE benchmark (59.1 → 61.1)
- Improved HellaSWAG performance from 93.0 to 94.9
- Enhanced ANLI results from 68.1 to 69.3
- Demonstrated less sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning compared to standard AT
- Showed consistent performance gains across diverse task types

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CreAT improves adversarial robustness by explicitly targeting the encoder's contextualized representations, not just the decoder.
- Mechanism: The adversarial perturbation is optimized to maximize the divergence between original and perturbed contextualized representations, forcing the encoder to produce more robust embeddings.
- Core assumption: The encoder's contextualized representations are the primary source of vulnerability in PLMs during adversarial attacks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The authors investigate AT from the perspective of the contextualized language representation outputted by PLM encoders."
  - [section 2.3] "we can derive that the performance gain from AT on PLMs is necessarily driven by the fact that the model needs to keep its contextualized representation robust"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in corpus neighbors

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: CreAT provides more consistent gains across diverse task types by globally optimizing perturbations.
- Mechanism: By simultaneously targeting both prediction divergence and representation divergence, CreAT creates adversarial examples that stress the entire model rather than just the classifier head.
- Core assumption: Task performance variability with standard AT stems from differential impact on encoder vs decoder.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3] "AT mainly fools the classifier on some of the tasks, but takes little impact on the encoder"
  - [section 3.2] "CreAT can be regarded as the 'global' form of AT, which solves the global worst case for the entire model"
  - [corpus] Moderate - related work on robust representation learning (e.g., "Enhancing Robust Representation in Adversarial Training")

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CreAT reduces hyperparameter sensitivity, making it easier to deploy across different tasks.
- Mechanism: The dual optimization objective (prediction + representation) creates a more stable optimization landscape, reducing dependence on fine-tuned ascent parameters.
- Core assumption: Standard AT's sensitivity to hyperparameters stems from its narrow focus on prediction divergence alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "We find that CreAT is less sensitive to the ascent step size α, and 1e-1 performs well on all datasets"
  - [section 2.3] "CreAT gives rise to a better direction to optimize the adversarial examples, to let them less sensitive to hyperparameters"

## Limitations

- The paper's core mechanism (encoder-focused perturbations are superior) lacks direct experimental validation through ablation studies
- Empirical validation is limited to only 7 tasks, which may not capture the full range of PLM applications
- State-of-the-art results are demonstrated using different attack methods than those used during training, making direct comparisons problematic

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The mathematical formulation of CreAT is rigorous and internally consistent. The dual optimization objective (prediction divergence + representation divergence) is well-defined, and the theoretical analysis connecting adversarial vulnerability to encoder representations is sound.

**Medium Confidence**: The empirical validation across 7 tasks shows consistent performance improvements over standard AT. The reduced hyperparameter sensitivity claim is supported by the experiments in Section 4.1, though the scope is limited.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that encoder-focused perturbations are fundamentally superior to decoder-focused ones lacks direct experimental validation. The generalizability to other PLM architectures beyond BERT and DeBERTa is assumed but not tested.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study on Optimization Objectives**: Run CreAT with only the representation divergence term (removing prediction divergence) and only the prediction divergence term (removing representation divergence) on the 7 tasks from Section 4.1. This would validate whether the dual optimization is truly necessary and which component drives performance improvements.

2. **Cross-Attack Consistency**: Evaluate CreAT-trained models against multiple adversarial attack methods beyond the ones used in AdvGLUE/ANLI/HellaSWAG. Test with PGD attacks of varying strengths, gradient-free attacks, and black-box attacks to verify that the robustness generalizes across attack types.

3. **Encoder vs Decoder Vulnerability Analysis**: Design an experiment that systematically measures the impact of perturbations on encoder representations versus decoder outputs. Use techniques like representation similarity analysis (e.g., centered kernel alignment) to quantify how much each component contributes to model vulnerability, directly testing the paper's core mechanism claim.
