# Overinformative Question Answering by Humans and Machines

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07151
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07151
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: Humans favor contextually relevant options when answering polar questions, while most neural models fail to match this pattern

## Executive Summary
Humans often provide overinformative answers to polar questions, going beyond the minimal information requested. This paper investigates whether such overinformativeness reflects reasoning about the questioner's practical goals rather than simply matching the requested item's similarity. Through two experiments, the authors demonstrate that humans exhibit context-sensitive relevance reasoning, favoring options that best help achieve the practical goal even when alternatives may be more similar to the requested item. When compared to state-of-the-art neural models, most fail to match human patterns, often listing all options or irrelevant ones. GPT-3 performs best but only with specific prompting, suggesting current models require explicit guidance to approximate human-like pragmatic reasoning.

## Method Summary
The study employed two experiments using Amazon Mechanical Turk participants. In Experiment 1, participants were presented with a practical goal scenario (e.g., helping someone find food when hungry) and asked to choose between a requested item, a competitor item, and an unrelated item. The competitor was designed to be more relevant to achieving the practical goal despite being less similar to the requested item. Experiment 2 replicated this design but varied the context across trials to test whether participants' choices would shift accordingly. The same scenarios were also tested with various neural models including extractive models, generative models, and GPT-3, both with and without specific prompting strategies.

## Key Results
- Humans consistently favored contextually relevant options (competitors) over similar options when answering polar questions
- In Experiment 2, participants switched their preferred answers when the same alternatives appeared in different contexts
- Most neural models failed to match human patterns, either listing all options or providing irrelevant information
- GPT-3 came closest to human performance but only with specific example answers or step-by-step reasoning prompts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism underlying human overinformativeness appears to be context-sensitive relevance reasoning rather than simple similarity matching. Humans appear to reason about what information would be most helpful for achieving the questioner's practical goal, considering the broader context rather than just the literal request. This suggests that humans engage in pragmatic reasoning about communicative intent, weighing the utility of different pieces of information for the specific situation at hand.

## Limitations
- Experimental scenarios use constrained, specific types of practical goals that may not generalize to all real-world contexts
- Binary choice paradigm simplifies the continuous nature of informativeness in natural conversation
- Comparison focuses on a narrow set of model architectures and prompting strategies

## Confidence
- Humans demonstrate context-sensitive relevance reasoning: High confidence
- Current neural models generally fail to match human patterns: High confidence
- GPT-3 success indicates fundamental gap in models' pragmatic reasoning: Medium confidence
