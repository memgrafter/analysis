# Automatic Detection and Rectification of Paper Receipts on Smartphones

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05763
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05763
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: 85.3% accuracy in detecting all four receipt corners vs 36.9% with edge detection

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for detecting and rectifying the perspective of paper receipts captured by smartphones. The method treats each of the four corners of a receipt as unique objects and trains a Single Shot Detection MobileNet model to identify them. To overcome the lack of large-scale real-world training data, the authors generate synthetic data by placing real receipt images on various background images and applying random projective transformations. The proposed method significantly outperforms traditional computer vision approaches, achieving 85.3% accuracy in detecting all four corners compared to only 36.9% using edge detection.

## Method Summary
The approach treats receipt corners as independent objects and trains a Single Shot Detection MobileNet object detection model to identify them. To address the lack of real-world training data, the authors generate synthetic data by compositing real receipt images onto various background images and applying random projective transformations. This synthetic dataset enables the model to learn corner detection across diverse perspectives and backgrounds. The detected corners are then used to compute a perspective transformation that rectifies the receipt image to a frontal view.

## Key Results
- 85.3% accuracy in detecting all four receipt corners on synthetic test data
- 36.9% accuracy achieved by traditional edge detection baseline
- Outperforms conventional computer vision approaches by a significant margin

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by converting the corner detection problem into an object detection task, allowing the model to learn corner-specific features rather than relying on geometric assumptions about receipt shapes. By treating each corner as a unique object, the model can better handle perspective distortion, varying lighting conditions, and different receipt designs. The synthetic data generation provides diverse training examples with known ground truth, enabling the model to learn robust corner detection across various backgrounds and perspectives.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Synthetic data generation -> MobileNet SSD training -> Corner detection -> Perspective transformation

Critical path: Image input → MobileNet feature extraction → SSD detection → Corner coordinate output → Perspective transformation matrix computation → Rectified image output

Design tradeoffs: The paper prioritizes detection accuracy over computational efficiency by using a more complex MobileNet architecture rather than simpler edge-based methods. The synthetic data approach trades real-world variability for controlled training conditions with perfect ground truth.

Failure signatures: The system may fail when receipts have damaged or occluded corners, when corners are very close together, or when the receipt contains graphics/artwork near corners that could be mistaken for actual corners by the detector.

## Limitations
- The 14.7% failure rate (100% - 85.3%) could impact practical applications requiring reliable receipt processing
- The method's dependence on synthetic training data raises questions about real-world generalization
- The object detection approach may struggle with cases where corners are occluded, damaged, or printed very close together
