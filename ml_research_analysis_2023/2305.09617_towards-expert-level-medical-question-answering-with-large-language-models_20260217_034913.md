# Towards Expert-Level Medical Question Answering with Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.09617
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09617
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Med-PaLM 2 achieves state-of-the-art results on medical question answering benchmarks, with over 19% improvement on MedQA compared to prior models

## Executive Summary
This work advances large language models in medicine by introducing Med-PaLM 2, which achieves expert-level performance on medical question answering tasks. Building on PaLM 2 with medical domain fine-tuning and novel ensemble refinement approaches, the model demonstrates significant improvements across multiple-choice benchmarks including MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, and MMLU clinical topics. In human evaluations, physicians preferred Med-PaLM 2's long-form answers to consumer medical questions over physician-generated answers on eight of nine clinically relevant axes. The model also shows robustness to adversarial testing designed to probe LLM limitations.

## Method Summary
Med-PaLM 2 builds upon PaLM 2 through medical domain fine-tuning on datasets including MedQA, MedMCQA, HealthSearchQA, LiveQA, and MedicationQA. The model employs chain-of-thought prompting to enable step-by-step reasoning, self-consistency to aggregate multiple reasoning paths, and a novel ensemble refinement approach that conditions on multiple generations to produce refined final answers. Evaluation includes multiple-choice benchmarks (MedQA, MedMCQA, PubMedQA, MMLU) and human assessment of long-form answers to consumer medical questions, comparing model performance against physician-generated responses.

## Key Results
- Achieved 86.5% accuracy on MedQA, improving upon prior models by over 19%
- Physicians preferred Med-PaLM 2 answers over physician answers on eight of nine clinically relevant axes in long-form evaluations
- Demonstrated robustness to adversarial testing across four categories of LLM failure modes
- Showed strong performance across diverse medical benchmarks including PubMedQA and MMLU clinical topics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Medical domain fine-tuning on PaLM 2 transfers domain-specific reasoning capabilities to the base model
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning adapts the model's parameters to specialized vocabulary, factual patterns, and reasoning styles present in medical texts
- Core assumption: Base PaLM 2 model has sufficient general reasoning ability that can be effectively specialized through domain fine-tuning
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "leveraging a combination of base LLM improvements (PaLM 2), medical domain fine-tuning"; [section] "instruction finetuning to the base LLM following the protocol used by Chung et al. [21]"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Ensemble refinement improves answer quality by aggregating multiple reasoning paths generated by the model
- Mechanism: Model generates multiple explanations and answers, then conditions on these to produce a refined final answer
- Core assumption: Model can generate multiple coherent reasoning paths, and some paths contain useful information
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "a novel ensemble refinement approach"; [section] "ER involves a two-stage process: first, given a (few-shot) chain-of-thought prompt and a question, the model produces multiple possible generations stochastically via temperature sampling"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Chain-of-thought prompting enables the model to perform step-by-step reasoning on complex medical questions
- Mechanism: Providing explicit demonstrations of reasoning steps in the prompt teaches the model to break down complex problems
- Core assumption: Medical questions often require multi-step reasoning that can be decomposed into explicit intermediate steps
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "prompting strategies including a novel ensemble refinement approach"; [section] "Chain-of-thought (CoT), introduced by Wei et al. [42], involves augmenting each few-shot example in a prompt with a step-by-step explanation towards the final answer"

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Base LLM (PaLM 2) → Medical fine-tuning → Prompting strategies (CoT, SC, ER) → Multiple-choice/long-form evaluation
- **Critical path**: Fine-tuning → Prompt engineering → Evaluation → Iteration
- **Design tradeoffs**: Larger models provide better performance but require more computational resources; ensemble refinement improves quality but increases inference cost
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on medical benchmarks indicates insufficient fine-tuning or inadequate prompting; safety issues indicate need for adversarial testing

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal length of LLM-generated medical answers for different use cases and contexts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper notes Med-PaLM 2 answers were longer than Med-PaLM and physician answers, discussing potential trade-offs between length and quality
- Why unresolved: Paper does not explore how answer length impacts different medical question-answering scenarios or user needs
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled studies comparing user comprehension, satisfaction, and actionability across different answer lengths for various types of medical questions and user contexts

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does inter-physician variation in answer generation impact the evaluation of LLM performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper notes physicians were asked to provide only one answer per question, limiting assessment of answer variability
- Why unresolved: Study does not explore how different physicians might approach the same question or how this variability affects comparisons with LLM outputs
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments where multiple physicians answer the same set of questions, followed by analysis of inter-physician agreement and comparison with LLM performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of medical expertise, lived experience, and specialization of raters on the evaluation of LLM answers?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper notes physicians evaluating answers had varying specialties but does not explore how this affected their ratings
- Why unresolved: Study does not examine how rater background influences evaluation outcomes or whether certain types of questions require specialized knowledge for accurate assessment
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing ratings from specialists versus generalists for questions in their respective domains, and analysis of how rater characteristics correlate with evaluation patterns

## Limitations
- Human evaluation component relies on physician raters who may have inherent biases toward novel AI systems
- Adversarial testing covers limited set of specific failure modes and may not capture all potential safety concerns
- Evaluation focuses primarily on English-language medical knowledge with limited assessment across diverse populations or medical systems
- Computational resources required for fine-tuning and inference may limit accessibility for many healthcare settings

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Med-PaLM 2 achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple-choice medical question answering benchmarks, with accuracy improvements of over 19% on MedQA
- **Medium confidence**: Physicians preferred Med-PaLM 2 answers to physician answers on most clinically relevant axes in long-form evaluations
- **Medium confidence**: Ensemble refinement significantly improves answer quality by aggregating multiple reasoning paths

## Next Checks
1. **Independent replication study**: Conduct independent evaluation using different physician raters and additional medical question datasets not used in original study to verify robustness across populations and question types
