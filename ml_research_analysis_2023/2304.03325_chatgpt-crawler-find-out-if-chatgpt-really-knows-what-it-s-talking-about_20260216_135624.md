# ChatGPT-Crawler: Find out if ChatGPT really knows what it's talking about

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03325
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03325
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GPT-4 significantly outperforms GPT-3 on conversational QA tasks across all evaluation metrics

## Executive Summary
This paper presents ChatGPT-Crawler, a pipeline for evaluating ChatGPT's performance on conversational question answering tasks. The study compares GPT-3 and GPT-4 responses on four datasets (CoQA, DialFact, FaVIQ, CoDAH) using BERT similarity scores and multiple evaluation metrics including BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, NIST, Jaccard, BART, and TER. Results demonstrate that GPT-4 substantially outperforms GPT-3 across all metrics, with higher scores for accuracy, relevance, and consistency. Human evaluation scores also favor GPT-4 (0.93-0.98 vs 0.84-0.93 for GPT-3), validating the automated metric results.

## Method Summary
The study employs a multi-stage evaluation pipeline that first generates questions using paraphrasing, augmentation, and sampling techniques, then uses ChatGPT-3 and GPT-4 to generate responses to these questions from four conversational QA datasets. The pipeline evaluates responses using a comprehensive set of automated metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, NIST, Jaccard, BART, TER, and BERT similarity) along with human evaluation for validation. The evaluation framework compares responses against reference answers to obtain NLI labels (entailment, contradiction, neutral) and assess response quality across multiple dimensions including semantic similarity, fluency, and factual accuracy.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3 across all evaluation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, NIST, Jaccard, BART, TER, BERT similarity, and human evaluation)
- Human evaluation scores favor GPT-4 (0.93-0.98) over GPT-3 (0.84-0.93) across all datasets
- GPT-3 responses show limitations including generic answers and inconsistency, while GPT-4 addresses these issues
- The pipeline demonstrates high scalability and flexibility for handling various conversational QA tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT-Crawler uses BERT similarity scores to evaluate ChatGPT's responses against correct answers.
- Mechanism: The pipeline computes BERT similarity between ChatGPT-generated responses and reference answers, producing NLI labels (entailment, contradiction, neutral) that indicate response quality.
- Core assumption: BERT embeddings capture semantic similarity between generated and reference answers well enough for QA evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The study employed BERT similarity scores to compare these responses with correct answers and obtain Natural Language Inference(NLI) labels."
  - [section] "To evaluate the quality of ChatGPT's responses, we employed various metrics such as BLEU, METEOR, BART, NIST, Jaccard, ROUGE-L, and TER scores."
- Break condition: If BERT embeddings fail to capture domain-specific nuances or if reference answers are ambiguous, the similarity scores may not accurately reflect response quality.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multiple evaluation metrics provide comprehensive assessment of ChatGPT's performance across different dimensions.
- Mechanism: The study uses BLEU (precision), ROUGE (recall), METEOR (harmonic mean), NIST (information weight), Jaccard (overlap), BART (semantic similarity), and TER (edit distance) to evaluate generated responses from multiple angles.
- Core assumption: Different metrics capture different aspects of text quality, and their combination provides a more complete evaluation than any single metric.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To assess the effectiveness of our pipeline, we evaluated it on four widely used datasets... Our pipeline also demonstrated a high level of scalability and flexibility, enabling it to handle a wide range of conversational QA tasks."
  - [appendix] Detailed equations for each metric showing how they capture different aspects of text quality.
- Break condition: If metrics are highly correlated or if some metrics are inappropriate for conversational QA tasks, the comprehensive assessment may be redundant or misleading.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Human evaluation provides ground truth for automated metric validation.
- Mechanism: Human evaluators score responses on a 1-0 scale (similar meaning vs dissimilar meaning), providing validation for automated metrics and identifying cases where metrics may disagree with human judgment.
- Core assumption: Human judgment represents the gold standard for response quality assessment in conversational QA tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Since these metrics are not perfect and do not always align with human judgments of text similarity or accuracy. Therefore, human evaluation scores(1- similar meaning, 0-dissimilar meaning) were calculated to obtain a more comprehensive assessment of the generated responses."
  - [table] Human evaluation scores show GPT-4 outperforming GPT-3 across all datasets (0.93-0.98 vs 0.84-0.93).
- Break condition: If human evaluators are inconsistent or biased, or if the binary scoring system oversimplifies response quality, human evaluation may not provide reliable ground truth.

## Limitations
- The evaluation framework relies heavily on automated metrics that may not fully capture conversational quality nuances
- Binary human scoring (1-0) oversimplifies the complex nature of response quality assessment
- The study focuses on GPT-3 vs GPT-4 comparison without exploring intermediate versions or alternative prompting strategies

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The finding that GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3 across all evaluation metrics is well-supported by the data and multiple validation approaches.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim about ChatGPT's promise for conversational QA tasks is reasonable but limited by the specific datasets and evaluation metrics used.
- **Medium Confidence**: The identification of GPT-3's limitations (generic answers, inconsistency) is supported by the data, though these may be partially attributable to prompt engineering rather than fundamental model capabilities.
