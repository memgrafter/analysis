# Scalable and Safe Remediation of Defective Actions in Self-Learning Conversational Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10528
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10528
- Reference count: 3
- One-line primary result: Achieves ~70% remediation of defective actions in RL-based conversational systems using historical regression/progression samples.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of maintaining policy stability in large-scale RL-based conversational systems, where frequent policy updates can regress performance on edge cases and user-reported defects. The authors propose a framework that leverages curated regression/progression (R/P) samples from historical incidents to both guard-rail against recurrence and guide policy improvements. The approach involves offline evaluation of new policies against R/P samples, pre-deployment guard-railing to prevent high-risk regressions, and augmentation of training with noise-injected R/P data to encourage desired behaviors. Experiments using real-world data show ~70% remediation of defective samples, with minimal impact on overall performance in both offline and online A/B settings. The method is deployed in production to enable long-term policy stability and reduction of hot-fix rules.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a framework for remediating defective actions in RL-based conversational systems using historical regression/progression (R/P) samples. The method involves offline evaluation of new policies against R/P samples, pre-deployment guard-railing to prevent high-risk regressions, and augmentation of training with noise-injected R/P data. R/P samples are curated from historical regression incidents and used to compute expected eligibility of defective actions under the new policy. Guard-railing blocks deployment if the intersection probability of experiment eligibility and sample status certainty exceeds a threshold. During training, R/P samples are injected with reshaped rewards and Gaussian noise augmentation to encourage desired behaviors while maintaining overall policy performance.

## Key Results
- Achieves ~70% remediation of defective actions on R/P samples in offline evaluation.
- Maintains overall policy performance with minimal impact on decision replication rate and expected reward.
- Successfully deployed in production to reduce hot-fix rules and enable long-term policy stability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Guard-railing prevents policy regressions by evaluating new policies against high-precision regression samples before deployment.
- Mechanism: Pre-deployment evaluation computes the expected eligibility of each R/P sample under the new policy. If the intersection probability of experiment eligibility and sample status certainty exceeds a threshold, deployment is blocked.
- Core assumption: The curated R/P samples accurately represent the defective behaviors that must be avoided.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The approach involves offline evaluation of new policies against R/P samples, pre-deployment guard-railing to prevent high-risk regressions"
  - [section 2.3] "we perform pre-deployment guard-railing right after every policy update using the evaluation parameters"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct citation of guard-railing in neighbor papers, suggesting this is a novel contribution.
- Break condition: If R/P samples are not representative or are outdated, the guard-railing may block benign updates or fail to catch actual regressions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Auxiliary loss from R/P samples enables long-term remediation by encouraging policy to replicate progression actions and avoid regression actions.
- Mechanism: R/P samples are injected into training batches with reshaped rewards (lowest for regression, highest for progression) and Gaussian noise augmentation. The auxiliary loss is combined with the main IPS objective to guide policy updates.
- Core assumption: The auxiliary loss signal is strong enough to influence the policy without destabilizing overall performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "augmentation of training with noise-injected R/P data to encourage desired behaviors"
  - [section 2.4] "We inject R/P samples in the training loop... The auxiliary loss (LRP) is computed from the regular loss objective"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighbor papers do not discuss noise-augmented R/P training, indicating this is likely a novel method.
- Break condition: If the weight of the auxiliary loss (η) is too high, it may overfit to R/P samples and degrade general performance; if too low, remediation effect is negligible.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Gaussian noise augmentation improves generalization of the policy on limited R/P data.
- Mechanism: During the forward pass, hidden-layer representations of R/P samples are perturbed with Gaussian noise, creating multiple augmented versions per sample to increase effective training set size.
- Core assumption: The noise scale (λ) is chosen appropriately to create useful perturbations without destroying meaningful signal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.4] "we perform representation space data augmentation... using Gaussian noise injection during the forward pass on each hypothesis at hidden-layer representations"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct mention of Gaussian noise augmentation in neighbor papers, suggesting this is a novel technique.
- Break condition: If λ is too large, noise may dominate the signal and prevent learning; if too small, augmentation provides little benefit.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Off-policy reinforcement learning and inverse propensity scoring (IPS) objective
  - Why needed here: The system updates its policy using logged interactions from a previous policy, requiring IPS to correct for the distribution shift between behavior and target policies.
  - Quick check question: In the IPS objective, what happens if the propensity score (Π0(a|X)) is very small for a logged action?

- Concept: Guard-railing and threshold-based blocking
  - Why needed here: Prevents deployment of policies that would repeat known defective actions on regression samples, ensuring business continuity.
  - Quick check question: How is the failure threshold (Tf) for guard-railing determined, and what trade-off does it balance?

- Concept: Data augmentation and noise injection for generalization
  - Why needed here: R/P datasets are small (e.g., ~50 samples), so augmentation is used to improve policy robustness and prevent overfitting.
  - Quick check question: Why might Gaussian noise be preferred over other augmentation methods in this context?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input -> Skill routing RL policy (Πθ) -> Action selection -> Reward feedback -> Logged data -> Offline training with R/P augmentation -> Pre-deployment evaluation -> Guard-railing -> Production deployment
- Critical path: Logged interaction data -> Training with R/P augmentation -> Policy update -> Pre-deployment evaluation -> Guard-railing check -> Production deployment
- Design tradeoffs: Using high-precision R/P samples improves defect remediation but relies on human curation; noise augmentation improves generalization but requires careful tuning of λ.
- Failure signatures: High remediation percentage but increased deviation in decision replication rate; guard-railing blocks too many updates; noise augmentation causes policy instability.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that guard-railing correctly blocks a policy that repeats a known regression action on a test R/P sample.
  2. Measure the effect of varying η (auxiliary loss weight) on remediation percentage and overall reward.
  3. Test the impact of different noise scales (λ) on the generalization of the policy to R/P samples.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions in the provided text. However, based on the limitations section, some open questions can be inferred:

- How to effectively manage the lifecycle of R/P samples, particularly the retirement of samples that become obsolete as the conversational system evolves?
- How to scale the effectiveness of the R/P remediation framework with the number and diversity of historical regression incidents in the dataset?
- What is the optimal balance between the number of R/P samples per batch (α) and the number of augmentations per sample (β) for maximizing remediation while minimizing impact on overall policy performance?

## Limitations
- Limited evidence from the corpus to validate the novelty and effectiveness of the proposed guard-railing, noise-augmented R/P training, and Gaussian noise augmentation techniques.
- Reliance on the quality and representativeness of curated R/P samples, which are not fully specified in terms of selection criteria or maintenance processes.
- Lack of detailed model architecture and reward function formulations, which could impact reproducibility.
