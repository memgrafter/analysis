# NLU on Data Diets: Dynamic Data Subset Selection for NLP Classification Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03208
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03208
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Dynamic pruning preserves full accuracy while training on 50% of data and reduces computational time by up to 41%

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces dynamic data pruning to improve efficiency of NLU finetuning on large language models. The method periodically scores training examples during training and removes low-importance samples, rather than pruning statically at the start. It extends the EL2N metric to multi-output classification for joint intent detection and slot filling. On GLUE and joint NLU datasets, the method preserves full accuracy while training on only 50% of data and reduces finetuning time by up to 41%. At 80% pruning, accuracy drops only 1% with a 66% time reduction. The dynamic approach outperforms static and single pruning, especially at high prune rates.

## Method Summary
The method uses dynamic data pruning with an EL2N metric extended to multi-output classification. It periodically re-scores training examples during training using an exponential moving average (EMA) of EL2N scores, allowing adaptive selection of important samples. The approach starts with full training for τ epochs, then enters pruning cycles where the top (1-ρ) proportion of examples are retained based on their scores. For multi-task joint intent detection and slot filling, the EL2N metric is mathematically extended to handle both sequence-level and token-level classification. The method is evaluated on GLUE benchmark datasets using RoBERTa-base and joint NLU datasets using JointBERT.

## Key Results
- Preserves full accuracy while training on 50% of data points, reducing computational times by up to 41%
- With 1% accuracy drop, prunes 80% of examples for 66% time reduction
- Dynamic pruning outperforms static and single pruning, especially at high prune rates where adaptive selection is most beneficial

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data pipeline -> Model -> Scoring module -> Pruning controller -> Training loop
- Critical path:
  1. Initial full training for τ epochs
  2. Periodic EL2N scoring and subset selection
  3. Training on retained subset for T epochs per cycle
  4. Repeat until E total epochs completed
- Design tradeoffs:
  - τ vs. T: Longer τ improves accuracy but reduces efficiency gains; shorter T increases pruning frequency but adds computational overhead
  - Prune rate vs. accuracy: Higher prune rates save more time but risk accuracy loss
  - EMA alpha: Balances responsiveness to new scores vs. stability from historical scores
- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy drops sharply when prune rate exceeds dataset's tolerance
  - Dynamic pruning underperforms static pruning on datasets with low ambiguity
  - Excessive pruning overhead when T is too small relative to scoring cost
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run full training on a GLUE dataset to establish baseline accuracy and timing
  2. Apply single pruning with τ=1 to compare against full training
  3. Test dynamic pruning with moderate prune rate (ρ=0.5) and default τ=4, T=4 to observe time-accuracy tradeoff

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness depends critically on proper parameter tuning (τ, T, ρ, EMA α), with limited guidance on optimal settings across different dataset characteristics
- The extension of EL2N to multi-output classification lacks corpus validation, and the assumption that gradient norms reliably indicate sample importance in multi-task settings remains theoretically grounded but empirically unproven
- The initial full-training phase is shown to improve stability but lacks systematic ablation studies to determine optimal duration

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The dynamic pruning approach outperforms static methods and single pruning, particularly at high prune rates where the adaptive selection proves beneficial
- **Medium confidence**: The time-accuracy trade-offs (50% data → full accuracy, 41% time reduction) are well-demonstrated on the tested datasets but may not generalize to all NLU tasks
- **Medium confidence**: The mathematical extension of EL2N to multi-output classification is sound but lacks external validation beyond the paper's experiments

## Next Checks

1. **Parameter sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary τ (1-8 epochs), T (2-8 epochs), and ρ (0.3-0.8) across multiple GLUE and joint NLU datasets to identify optimal parameter ranges and dataset-specific patterns.

2. **Corpus validation of EL2N extension**: Compare the extended EL2N scoring method against alternative importance metrics (e.g., influence functions, gradient-based sampling) on the same multi-task datasets to validate the gradient-norm proxy assumption.

3. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Apply the method to non-GLUE, non-joint NLU datasets (e.g., text classification on different domains, named entity recognition) to assess whether the observed time-accuracy trade-offs hold beyond the paper's experimental scope.
