# Query Performance Prediction: From Ad-hoc to Conversational Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10923
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10923
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Supervised QPP methods outperform unsupervised ones only with ample training data in conversational search settings.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates query performance prediction (QPP) in conversational search (CS), examining whether established QPP methods from ad-hoc search generalize to CS settings. The research focuses on three key areas: query rewriting-based retrieval, conversational dense retrieval (ConvDR), and performance prediction at different ranking depths. Experiments on three CS datasets reveal that supervised QPP methods distinctly outperform unsupervised ones only when ample training data is available. The findings highlight the unique challenges of QPP in CS and suggest directions for future research, particularly in improving query rewriting quality and developing few-shot learning techniques.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates QPP methods on three conversational search datasets (CAsT-19, CAsT-20, and OR-QuAC) using both supervised and unsupervised approaches. Supervised methods include NQA-QPP, BERT-QPP, and qppBERT-PL, while unsupervised methods encompass Clarity, WIG, NQC, σmax, n(σx%), and SMV. The experiments compare performance across query rewriting-based retrieval (BM25) and conversational dense retrieval (ConvDR) at different ranking depths (nDCG@3, nDCG@100, Recall@100). Training procedures involve 5 epochs for CAsT datasets with warm-up pre-training on OR-QuAC, and 1 epoch for OR-QuAC.

## Key Results
- Supervised QPP methods outperform unsupervised counterparts only when ample training data is available
- Point-wise supervised QPP methods generally outperform list-wise methods, with exceptions in few-shot settings
- Score-based unsupervised QPP methods show high effectiveness in assessing ConvDR due to its higher score variance
- Query rewriting quality significantly impacts QPP effectiveness in conversational search

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Supervised QPP methods outperform unsupervised ones only when training data is ample.
- Mechanism: Supervised methods learn query-document patterns from labeled data, capturing nuanced signals that unsupervised methods miss. With limited data, these patterns are unreliable and performance degrades.
- Core assumption: The availability of high-quality relevance labels for training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "supervised QPP methods distinctly outperform unsupervised counterparts only when a large-scale training set is available"
  - [section]: "On OR-QuAC, where training data is ample, all supervised QPP methods perform better than unsupervised methods"
  - [corpus]: Weak, no direct corpus studies cited for this specific claim.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Point-wise supervised QPP methods generally outperform list-wise ones.
- Mechanism: Point-wise methods process each query-document pair independently, which aligns well with the prediction of retrieval quality for a single query. List-wise methods capture inter-document relationships but require more data and may not add much value for CS.
- Core assumption: The retrieval quality for a query is primarily determined by the quality of individual query-document interactions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "point-wise supervised QPP methods outperform their list-wise counterparts in most cases"
  - [section]: "point-wise supervised QPP methods such as NQA-QPP and BERTQPP outperform the list-wise supervised method qppBERT-PL"
  - [corpus]: No specific corpus studies, but aligns with broader supervised learning trends.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Score-based unsupervised QPP methods perform well on ConvDR despite its neural nature.
- Mechanism: Score-based methods rely on the distribution of retrieval scores, not the specific retrieval algorithm. ConvDR's score distribution has higher variance, making top-ranked documents more distinguishable and easier to predict.
- Core assumption: The effectiveness of score-based methods depends on the retrieval score distribution, not the underlying algorithm.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "retrieval score-based unsupervised QPP methods show high effectiveness in assessing the conversational dense retrieval method, ConvDR"
  - [section]: "Compared to Table 3, the performance of NQC/WIG is even better than their effectiveness in assessing BM25" and "Figure 2 illustrates the retrieval score distributions of ConvDR and BM25... ConvDR displays a higher variance"
  - [corpus]: Limited, only internal experimental evidence.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Query/Conversation -> Query Rewriting (T5, QuReTeC, Human) -> Retrieval (BM25, ConvDR) -> QPP Methods (Supervised: NQA-QPP, BERT-QPP, qppBERT-PL; Unsupervised: Clarity, WIG, NQC, σmax, n(σx%), SMV) -> Performance Prediction
- Critical path: For each query, the CS method retrieves a ranked list, the QPP method estimates the retrieval quality, and the estimated quality is compared to the actual quality (nDCG@3, nDCG@100, Recall@100) for evaluation.
- Design tradeoffs: Supervised methods require labeled data but can capture complex patterns; unsupervised methods don't need labels but rely on simpler heuristics. Point-wise methods are data-efficient but may miss document relationships; list-wise methods capture relationships but need more data.
- Failure signatures: Supervised methods fail with insufficient data; unsupervised methods fail if score distributions are not discriminative; both may fail if query understanding is poor (e.g., with noisy query rewrites).
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare the performance of supervised and unsupervised QPP methods on a CS dataset with ample training data (e.g., OR-QuAC).
  2. Compare the performance of point-wise and list-wise supervised QPP methods on a CS dataset with limited training data (e.g., CAsT-19).
  3. Compare the performance of score-based unsupervised QPP methods on ConvDR and BM25 to understand the impact of score distribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of QPP methods vary across different conversational search datasets and domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates QPP methods on three conversational search datasets (CAsT-19, CAsT-20, and OR-QuAC) but does not extensively compare their performance across domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing QPP methods within each dataset but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how their effectiveness generalizes across different conversational search domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study that evaluates QPP methods on a diverse set of conversational search datasets representing different domains (e.g., customer service, healthcare, e-commerce) and compares their performance across these domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can QPP methods be improved by incorporating conversational context beyond the current query?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the challenges of QPP in conversational search due to the context-dependent nature of user queries, but does not explore the potential of incorporating broader conversational context.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on QPP methods that primarily rely on the current query and its rewritten version, without considering the broader conversational context that may influence query performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study that develops and evaluates QPP methods that incorporate conversational context beyond the current query, such as previous system responses, user feedback, or topic shifts, and compares their effectiveness to traditional QPP methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of QPP methods change when evaluating different types of conversational search systems?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates QPP methods on two types of conversational search systems (query rewriting-based retrieval and conversational dense retrieval) but does not extensively compare their performance across different system architectures.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing QPP methods within each system type but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how their effectiveness generalizes across different conversational search system architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study that evaluates QPP methods on a diverse set of conversational search systems with different architectures (e.g., hybrid systems, multi-modal systems) and compares their performance across these systems.

## Limitations

- Findings are primarily based on three conversational search datasets, limiting generalizability to other CS settings
- Performance heavily depends on the quality of query rewrites, which can vary significantly based on the rewriting approach used
- Evaluation focuses on short conversations (≤10 turns), leaving effectiveness in longer conversational contexts unexplored

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The superiority of supervised QPP methods over unsupervised ones when ample training data is available
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that point-wise supervised methods generally outperform list-wise ones
- **Medium Confidence:** The effectiveness of score-based unsupervised methods on ConvDR
