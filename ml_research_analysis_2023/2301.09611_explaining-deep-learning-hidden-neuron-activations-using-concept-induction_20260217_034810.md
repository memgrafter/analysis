# Explaining Deep Learning Hidden Neuron Activations using Concept Induction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09611
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09611
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Concept induction using description logics and Wikipedia knowledge can automatically attach meaningful labels to hidden neurons in CNN dense layers.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of interpreting hidden neuron activations in deep learning models by proposing a method that automatically attaches meaningful labels to neurons using concept induction over large-scale background knowledge. The approach combines a pre-trained CNN (ResNet50V2), a heuristic concept induction system (ECII), and Wikipedia's class hierarchy to generate human-understandable explanations for neuron activations. The method involves analyzing positive and negative sets of images, generating description logic class expressions, and verifying results through Google image searches.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune ResNet50V2 on the ADE20K semantic segmentation dataset for 20 epochs, achieving 92.47% training accuracy and 87.50% validation accuracy. They analyze activations from the dense layer (64 neurons) for 1,370 test images, identifying candidate neurons with >50% activation values >0. For each candidate neuron, they run ECII concept induction using three threshold strategies (CASE-I, II, III) with positive/negative image sets and Wikipedia's class hierarchy as background knowledge. Results are verified using Google Images to test whether identified concepts genuinely trigger the neuron's activation patterns.

## Key Results
- Activation percentages ranged from 0% to over 90% for different neurons across three threshold strategies
- CASE-II (positive >= 50% of max activation, negative = zero) showed the most stable and interpretable results
- Google image verification confirmed that identified concepts genuinely trigger corresponding neuron activations
- The approach successfully generated meaningful interpretations for hidden neuron activations in the CNN dense layer

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hidden neuron activations can be interpreted as meaningful concepts through symbolic reasoning over large-scale background knowledge.
- Mechanism: ECII generates description logic class expressions that characterize positive image sets while excluding negative sets, grounded in Wikipedia's class hierarchy.
- Core assumption: The distribution of concepts in activating vs. non-activating images is sufficiently distinct to allow meaningful concept induction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our results show that we can automatically attach meaningful labels from the background knowledge to individual neurons in the dense layer of a Convolutional Neural Network through a hypothesis and verification process."
  - [section] "In a nutshell – and more details are given below – a concept induction system accepts three inputs, a set of positive examples P, a set of negative examples N, and a knowledge base (or ontology) K... and where we have x occurring as instances (constants) in K for all x ∈ P ∪ N."
- Break condition: If the background knowledge doesn't contain relevant concepts or if the positive and negative sets have too much concept overlap, the concept induction will fail to produce meaningful labels.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The activation threshold criteria significantly impacts the quality of concept induction results.
- Mechanism: Different threshold strategies for defining positive and negative sets lead to different concept induction outcomes, with CASE-II showing the most stable results.
- Core assumption: The distribution of activations follows patterns where certain thresholds better separate semantically meaningful concepts from noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To decide on activation, we considered and analyzed – a threshold value around the activation values with the following three different criteria"
  - [section] "Table 3 shows the evaluation of 29 neurons for the remaining 20% of the Google Image Dataset. The activation percentage for each neuron is listed for all three cases."
- Break condition: If all threshold strategies produce similar low activation percentages, the approach may not be sensitive enough to capture meaningful neuron concepts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Verification through Google image search provides empirical validation of the concept interpretations.
- Mechanism: After concept induction identifies candidate concepts for a neuron, collecting and testing Google images for those concepts against the trained model verifies whether the concepts genuinely trigger the neuron's activation pattern.
- Core assumption: Google image search results for identified concepts will contain images that actually activate the corresponding neuron in the model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The next step would be to verify the activation of neurons by collecting some more data that is more generic and could serve as solid verification"
  - [section] "Table 3 lists the concepts we got from ECII corresponding to each case, representing the neuron's activation for neuron number 5."
- Break condition: If Google images for identified concepts consistently fail to activate the corresponding neuron, the concept induction interpretations are likely incorrect or the neuron is not concept-specific.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CNN feature extraction -> Dense layer activation analysis -> ECII concept induction -> Wikipedia knowledge base -> Google image verification
- Critical path: Image → CNN feature extraction → Dense layer activation analysis → Concept induction with ECII → Concept verification with Google images → Interpretation
- Design tradeoffs: Using a heuristic concept induction system (ECII) instead of exact methods trades computational efficiency for potentially less precise results; using only class hierarchy (not full ontology) limits expressivity but improves scalability
- Failure signatures: Low activation percentages across all threshold strategies indicate poor neuron-concept correspondence; inconsistent results between training set and Google image verification suggest overfitting or concept mismatch
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run ECII with CASE-II thresholds on a single neuron using the training set and verify the top 5 concepts using Google images

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of different threshold values for neuron activation on the interpretability and accuracy of the generated explanations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses using three different criteria for deciding activation (CASE-I, CASE-II, CASE-III) but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the impact of different threshold values on the results.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers three specific threshold-based criteria and does not explore the full range of possible threshold values or their effects on the interpretation quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying threshold values across a range and analyzing the resulting explanation quality and activation percentages for each threshold.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of background knowledge base (e.g., Wikipedia vs other ontologies) affect the quality and relevance of the generated explanations for neuron activations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses Wikipedia's class hierarchy as the knowledge base but acknowledges that other knowledge bases could be used.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses one knowledge base and does not compare the results with alternative knowledge bases to assess their impact on explanation quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using different knowledge bases (e.g., DBpedia, YAGO, domain-specific ontologies) and analyzing the differences in generated explanations and their relevance to the neuron activations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the proposed method be extended to explain activations in other layers of the neural network beyond the dense layer, and how would this affect the interpretability of the explanations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on explaining activations in the dense layer of a CNN but does not explore the possibility of extending the method to other layers.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate the applicability of the method to other layers of the neural network, such as convolutional or pooling layers, which may have different activation patterns and interpretability challenges.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the proposed method to explain activations in other layers of the neural network and analyzing the resulting explanations' quality, relevance, and interpretability compared to those from the dense layer.

## Limitations
- The approach's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality and coverage of the background knowledge (Wikipedia's class hierarchy)
- Evaluation is limited to a single CNN architecture (ResNet50V2) and dataset (ADE20K), raising generalizability concerns
- The verification method using Google Images introduces potential confounding factors from external data sources

## Confidence
**High confidence**: The concept induction mechanism (Mechanism 1) is well-supported by the paper's methodology and related work on description logics and symbolic reasoning.

**Medium confidence**: The threshold strategy comparison (Mechanism 2) shows promising results but lacks rigorous statistical analysis across multiple neurons and runs.

**Low confidence**: The Google Images verification method (Mechanism 3) provides empirical support but may be influenced by search algorithm biases and the quality of image annotations in Wikipedia.
