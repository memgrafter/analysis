# Explainable Deep Reinforcement Learning: State of the Art and Challenges

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09937
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09937
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Comprehensive survey categorizing XDRL methods by explanation paradigm, interpretable models, and explanation representation

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of explainable deep reinforcement learning (XDRL) methods, organizing them according to three key dimensions: the explanation paradigm they follow (global policy, local response, model inspection, or objective explanations), the interpretable models they employ, and the surface representation of explanations they produce. The review identifies fundamental challenges in XDRL, including the need for comprehensive and context-dependent explanations, transparency requirements, and the development of principled methodologies. The work highlights the diverse landscape of XDRL approaches and outlines critical open questions and challenges requiring further research.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically categorizes XDRL methods based on their explanation paradigms, distinguishing between global policy explanations that characterize overall behavior, local response explanations for specific decisions, model inspection techniques that reveal internal mechanisms, and objective explanations that clarify reward structures. The analysis examines the interpretable models used across these approaches and how explanations are presented to users. The review synthesizes findings from 40 references to establish a taxonomy of XDRL methods while identifying key challenges and open research directions in the field.

## Key Results
- Categorizes XDRL methods across four explanation paradigms: global policy, local response, model inspection, and objective explanations
- Identifies critical challenges including the need for context-dependent explanations and transparency requirements
- Highlights diversity of approaches ranging from policy-level explanations to response-specific interpretations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The survey's effectiveness stems from its systematic categorization framework that organizes XDRL methods along three orthogonal dimensions: explanation paradigm, interpretable model type, and surface representation. This structured approach enables clear comparison across diverse methods and reveals patterns in how different techniques address the fundamental challenge of making deep reinforcement learning decisions interpretable. The taxonomy provides a foundation for understanding the trade-offs between different explanation approaches and their suitability for various applications.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The survey identifies several critical open questions in XDRL research, including the development of principled methodologies for generating explanations, the need for context-dependent explanations that adapt to user expertise and application domains, and the challenge of balancing explanation fidelity with computational efficiency. The authors emphasize the importance of evaluating explanation quality through human studies and the need for standardized benchmarks to compare different XDRL approaches. Additionally, they highlight the open question of how to integrate explanations into the learning process itself rather than treating them as post-hoc analysis tools.

## Limitations

- Focuses on published methods without empirical comparison of their relative effectiveness
- Potential publication bias toward successful approaches may underrepresent failed methods
- Limited discussion of real-world deployment challenges and practical constraints
- May underrepresent work from non-English literature and informal approaches

## Confidence

High confidence in the methodological categorization framework based on explanation paradigm and interpretable model types, as this taxonomy is well-supported by the surveyed literature. Medium confidence in claims about the practical effectiveness of different explanation approaches, as the review focuses on methodological categorization rather than empirical validation. Low confidence in predictions about future research directions, as these are inherently speculative.
