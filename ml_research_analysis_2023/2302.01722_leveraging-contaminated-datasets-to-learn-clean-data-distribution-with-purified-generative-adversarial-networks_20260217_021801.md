# Leveraging Contaminated Datasets to Learn Clean-Data Distribution with Purified Generative Adversarial Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01722
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01722
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PuriGANs learn clean data distribution from contaminated datasets by augmenting discriminators to distinguish clean vs. contaminated samples, achieving 34.86 FID on F-MNIST with 40% contamination

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of training generative models on datasets contaminated with undesired samples. The proposed PuriGANs augment standard GAN discriminators with the ability to distinguish between clean and contaminated instances using an extra dataset of contamination samples. Theoretical analysis proves convergence to the target distribution under mild conditions. Experimental results show PuriGANs significantly outperform baselines on image generation, semi-supervised anomaly detection, and PU-learning tasks, with the three-level discriminator variant achieving the best performance.

## Method Summary
PuriGANs modify standard GAN training by augmenting the discriminator with additional outputs to classify clean, contaminated, and generated samples. Two variants are proposed: a two-level discriminator that uses a weighting parameter λ to control contamination discrimination, and a three-level discriminator that outputs distinct values for clean, contaminated, and generated samples. Both variants leverage an extra dataset containing only contamination instances to train the discriminator to recognize contamination. The generator learns to avoid producing contaminated outputs through the updated discriminator gradients, allowing end-to-end purification during training rather than as a pre-processing step.

## Key Results
- Three-level discriminator PuriGAN achieves 34.86 FID on F-MNIST under 40% contamination, outperforming LSGAN (62.44) and NDA (58.88)
- PuriGANs significantly outperform two-stage methods that separate contamination before GAN training
- Performance is robust across various contamination ratios (γp) and levels of available contamination data (γc)
- Theoretical guarantees show convergence to target distribution when target and contamination supports are disjoint

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
PuriGANs learn clean data distribution by augmenting discriminators with contamination detection using an extra contamination dataset. The discriminator receives an additional loss term that penalizes it for outputting non-zero values on contamination instances, forcing it to distinguish clean from contaminated samples. The generator then learns to avoid producing contaminated outputs through the updated discriminator gradients.

Core assumption: Target and contamination distributions have disjoint support (no overlap in feature space).

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract] "Theoretical analysis proves the proposed PuriGANs converge to the target distribution under mild conditions."
- [section] "By supposing the support of target and contamination distributions are disjoint, i.e., Supp(p+(x))∩Supp(p−(x)) = ∅ and λ→ +∞, the generator distribution pg(x) will converge to the target distribution p+(x)."

Break condition: If target and contamination distributions have overlapping support, the discriminator cannot effectively distinguish them, leading to poor purification.

### Mechanism 2
The three-level discriminator improves performance by providing finer-grained discrimination between clean, contaminated, and generated samples. The discriminator outputs three distinct values, allowing the generator to receive more informative gradients about how to avoid contamination while generating realistic samples.

Core assumption: The ratio of clean to contaminated instances (π) is known or can be estimated.

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract] "The three-level discriminator PuriGAN achieves the best performance with a 34.86 FID score on F-MNIST under 40% contamination."
- [section] "If d is set as d = 2π−1 / (π+1), the generator distribution pg(x) will converge to the target distribution p+(x)."

Break condition: Inaccurate estimation of π leads to suboptimal discrimination and reduced purification effectiveness.

### Mechanism 3
PuriGAN outperforms two-stage methods by jointly optimizing purification and generation in an end-to-end manner. By integrating purification into the GAN training loop, PuriGAN continuously leverages the extra contamination dataset to improve both the discriminator's robustness and the generator's output quality, rather than relying on a separate pre-processing step.

Core assumption: The extra contamination dataset is sufficiently representative of the contamination distribution in the main dataset.

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract] "Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed PuriGANs are able to generate much better desired images over competitive baselines under various kinds of conditions."
- [section] "The poor performance of the first stage will be passed down to the second stage, resulting in an even worse performance of the whole model."

Break condition: If the extra contamination dataset is too small or unrepresentative, the joint optimization cannot effectively counteract the contamination in the main dataset.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Generator (G) -> Discriminator (D) -> Loss computation -> Parameter update

- **Critical path**:
  1. Initialize G and D
  2. Sample from training dataset (X), extra contamination dataset (X-), and G's output
  3. Update D using augmented loss (including contamination classification)
  4. Update G using the updated D
  5. Repeat until convergence

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Two-level vs. three-level discriminator: Simpler vs. more expressive, but requires estimating π
  - Weighting parameter λ: Controls strength of contamination discrimination; too high can cause instability
  - Size of extra contamination dataset: Larger datasets improve purification but increase computational cost

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High FID scores on clean test data: Indicates model is still generating contaminated samples
  - Discriminator collapse (outputting all zeros or ones): Suggests λ is too high or contamination dataset is unrepresentative
  - Slow convergence: May indicate insufficient representation of contamination in extra dataset

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train PuriGAN on MNIST with small contamination and extra contamination dataset, verify it outperforms standard GAN
  2. Vary contamination ratio (γp) and ratio of collected contamination instances (γc) to observe performance impact
  3. Compare two-level and three-level discriminator variants to understand simplicity vs. performance tradeoff

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several unresolved issues emerge from the theoretical and experimental results.

## Limitations
- Theoretical convergence guarantees rely heavily on the disjoint support assumption between target and contamination distributions, which may not hold in practice
- Performance validation is limited to relatively simple image datasets (F-MNIST, CIFAR-10) without testing on more complex domains
- Three-level discriminator requires estimating the clean-to-contaminated ratio π, which may not be available in all practical scenarios

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of augmenting the discriminator with contamination detection works as described, supported by theoretical analysis and experimental results on controlled datasets
- **Medium confidence**: The superiority over two-stage methods is demonstrated, but comparison assumes ideal conditions for baseline methods
- **Medium confidence**: The three-level discriminator provides consistent improvement, but optimal setting of parameter d requires accurate estimation of π

## Next Checks
1. **Robustness test**: Evaluate PuriGAN on datasets where contamination distributions have significant overlap with target distributions to test the disjoint support assumption
2. **Scalability validation**: Apply PuriGAN to more complex datasets like ImageNet-100 or LSUN to verify performance on higher-dimensional data
3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Conduct ablation studies varying λ and d parameters across different contamination ratios to understand their impact on stability and purification effectiveness
