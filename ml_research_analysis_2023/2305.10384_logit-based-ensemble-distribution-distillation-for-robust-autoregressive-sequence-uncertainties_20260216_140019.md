# Logit-Based Ensemble Distribution Distillation for Robust Autoregressive Sequence Uncertainties

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10384
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10384
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 12
- Key outcome: L-EDD students match Deep Ensemble translation quality while outperforming them by up to ~10% AUROC in out-of-distribution detection.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses efficient uncertainty estimation for autoregressive sequence tasks by proposing Logit-based Ensemble Distribution Distillation (L-EDD). The method models ensemble logits using Laplace or Gaussian distributions rather than softmax probabilities, avoiding optimization challenges of prior probability-space approaches. Experiments on WMT'16 En-De and WMT'20 En-Ru translation tasks demonstrate that L-EDD students achieve comparable translation quality to Deep Ensembles while significantly improving out-of-distribution detection. Using Snapshot Ensembles as teachers further reduces training costs by 3× without sacrificing performance.

## Method Summary
L-EDD distills ensemble knowledge by modeling teacher ensemble logits with a Laplace or Gaussian distribution, then training a student to match this distribution. The approach normalizes logits per class, computes mean and scale parameters for the distribution, and uses negative log-likelihood as the distillation loss. Unlike prior EDD methods that operate in probability space, L-EDD works directly in the unbounded logit space, making it more robust to outliers and avoiding optimization issues with Dirichlet distributions. The method can be combined with traditional knowledge distillation through a weighted interpolation of losses.

## Key Results
- L-EDD students match Deep Ensemble translation quality on WMT'16 En-De and WMT'20 En-Ru
- L-EDD outperforms Deep Ensembles by up to ~10% AUROC on out-of-distribution detection
- Using Snapshot Ensembles reduces training costs by 3× without sacrificing performance
- L-EDD with Laplace distribution shows almost no correlation between uncertainty and sequence length

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Modeling ensemble logits with Laplace distribution enables better uncertainty estimation by avoiding Dirichlet EDD optimization challenges and being robust to outliers
- Core assumption: Ensemble logit distribution is well-approximated by diagonal Laplace distribution with independent per-class scales
- Break condition: Strong correlations across classes invalidate diagonal assumption

### Mechanism 2
- L-EDD outperforms Deep Ensembles on OOD detection by decoupling confidence from sequence length
- Core assumption: Length-dependent overconfidence of ensemble logits is not present in student's Laplace model
- Break condition: OOD datasets have similar sequence lengths to in-distribution data

### Mechanism 3
- Snapshot Ensembles reduce training cost by 3× without sacrificing performance
- Core assumption: Cyclic learning rate provides ensemble diversity comparable to independently trained Deep Ensembles
- Break condition: Cyclic learning rate fails to generate diverse enough models

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher ensemble -> Student model with Laplace/Gaussian head -> Logit normalization -> Distribution distillation loss -> Backpropagation

- Critical path:
  1. Generate teacher ensemble logits for training data
  2. Normalize logits per class
  3. Compute mean and scale parameters for Laplace
  4. Compute distillation loss against ensemble logits
  5. Backpropagate and update student parameters

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Laplace vs Gaussian: Laplace is more robust to outliers but may underestimate variance
  - Diagonal vs full covariance: Diagonal is computationally cheaper but ignores class correlations
  - Temperature in KD: Higher temperatures smooth teacher outputs but may reduce discriminative power

- Failure signatures:
  - Divergence during training: Likely from unnormalized logits or extreme gradients
  - Overconfident student: Check if mean logits are too peaked; adjust β interpolation
  - Poor OOD detection: Verify that length bias is not introduced in normalization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train single transformer on WMT'16 En-De with teacher-forcing, verify BLEU
  2. Generate Snapshot Ensemble and KD student, compare BLEU and uncertainty estimates
  3. Replace KD with L-EDD (Laplace), compare OOD detection AUROC and BLEU

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- What specific architectural or optimization modifications could enable EDD with Dirichlet distributions to scale effectively to large vocabulary tasks like machine translation?

- What is the fundamental reason why modeling ensemble logits with a Laplace distribution provides better out-of-distribution detection than using ensemble predictions directly?

- Why does the L-EDD approach show almost no correlation between total uncertainty and sequence length, while Deep Ensembles display a negative correlation?

## Limitations

- The diagonal Laplace assumption may not hold when ensemble logits have strong correlations across classes
- The mechanism behind length-bias elimination is not fully explained theoretically
- The 3× cost reduction assumes Snapshot Ensembles provide sufficient diversity, which requires empirical validation

## Confidence

- High: L-EDD Laplace achieves state-of-the-art OOD detection AUROC and matches Deep Ensemble BLEU
- Medium: Length bias elimination is a genuine property of L-EDD rather than a dataset-specific artifact
- Medium: Snapshot Ensembles provide ensemble diversity comparable to independently trained Deep Ensembles
