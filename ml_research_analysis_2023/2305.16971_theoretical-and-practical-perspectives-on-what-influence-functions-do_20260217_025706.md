# Theoretical and Practical Perspectives on what Influence Functions Do

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16971
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16971
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: Influence functions fail to predict leave-some-out retraining effects due to parameter divergence over time; they can only predict parameter changes for a limited number of fine-tuning steps

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes influence functions (IF) in deep learning, explaining why they fail to predict leave-some-out retraining effects despite theoretical promise. The authors identify five problematic assumptions and prove that parameter divergence over time is the key limitation - IF can only predict parameter changes for a limited number of fine-tuning steps. They validate this both theoretically (via a discrete Gronwall lemma) and empirically across BERT and ResNet models, showing that influence scores' predictive power degrades quickly. The paper concludes that IF should be used for error correction through limited fine-tuning on influential examples rather than retraining, and demonstrates this approach improves correction success rates by 2-15% compared to baselines while requiring fewer steps.

## Method Summary
The paper uses Arnoldi-based Influence Functions (ABIF) with 32 projectors and 64 Arnoldi iterations to compute influence scores on mispredicted examples from BERT (SST2) and ResNet (CIFAR10). Error correction is performed via Proponents-correction (fine-tuning on top-k influential examples with corrected labels) and Opponents-tuning (fine-tuning on top-k opponents). The experiments measure success rate (ratio of mispredictions corrected within 50 steps), prediction retention, and Pearson correlation R(t) between influence scores and loss shifts over time. Parameter divergence is tracked by comparing training on original vs perturbed losses with different ε values.

## Key Results
- Influence scores' predictive power degrades exponentially over training time due to parameter divergence
- Proponents-correction improves success rate by 2-15% over random baseline while requiring fewer fine-tuning steps
- Opponents-tuning performs poorly on CIFAR10 (54% of opponents have desired label)
- Parameter divergence bound shows exponential growth with integrated learning rate, invalidating first-order Taylor approximation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Influence functions (IF) can predict parameter changes when fine-tuning on a perturbed loss for a limited number of steps.
- **Mechanism:** The parameter divergence bound (Theorem 3) shows that the difference between training on the original loss L and perturbed loss L grows exponentially with the integrated learning rate. This exponential growth violates the first-order Taylor expansion assumption that IF rely on after a limited number of steps.
- **Core assumption:** Parameter divergence remains small enough that θε,T − θ0,T = O(ε) holds.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "influence fades over training time even with deterministic training"
  - [section] "the parameter divergence poses a clear limitation on the predictive power of IF: influence fades over training time"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly address parameter divergence
- **Break condition:** When integrated learning rate exceeds threshold where exponential divergence makes first-order approximation invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** IF can be used to correct mis-predictions by fine-tuning on influential examples for a limited number of steps.
- **Mechanism:** Proponents-correction and Opponents-tuning methods leverage the time-bound nature of IF by identifying influential examples and taking only a few fine-tuning steps on them, rather than retraining from scratch.
- **Core assumption:** Influential examples identified by IF remain relevant for a small number of fine-tuning steps.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "mis-predictions can be successfully corrected by taking only a few fine-tuning steps on influential examples"
  - [section] "we propose and verify a very simple method for correcting mis-predictions by taking only a few gradient steps on influential examples"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't address error correction methodology
- **Break condition:** When the number of fine-tuning steps exceeds the time-bound validity of IF predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Arnoldi-based Influence Functions (ABIF) can address Hessian degeneracy issues.
- **Mechanism:** When the model Hessian is singular, ABIF approximates the subspace spanned by top eigenvectors, allowing influence computation in the non-degenerate subspace while keeping degenerate parameters fixed.
- **Core assumption:** The non-degenerate subspace captures sufficient information for influence computation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "we diagonalize Hθ0; we let P1 be the subspace spanned by the eigenvectors corresponding to the non-zero eigenvalues"
  - [section] "we can approximate P1 using the Arnoldi iteration"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't discuss ABIF methodology
- **Break condition:** When the influence resides primarily in the degenerate subspace that ABIF cannot capture.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** ABIF computation -> Parameter divergence tracking -> Error correction engine -> Evaluation pipeline
- **Critical path:** Compute influence scores for test-prediction pairs → Measure parameter divergence to determine time-bound validity → Apply error correction using identified influential examples → Evaluate correction success and model retention
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - ABIF vs. Hessian-based IF: ABIF handles degenerate Hessians but may miss influence in degenerate subspaces
  - Single checkpoint vs. multi-checkpoint TracIn: Single checkpoint is faster but potentially less accurate
  - Number of fine-tuning steps: More steps may improve correction but exceed IF validity bounds
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Correlation R(t) approaching 0 over time indicates IF predictive power degradation
  - High parameter divergence ∥θε,T − θ0,T ∥ suggests first-order approximation breakdown
  - Low success rate in error correction indicates poor influence score quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal way to bound parameter divergence over time in neural networks beyond the pessimistic exponential bound provided by Theorem 3?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how parameter divergence grows exponentially with integrated learning rate and suggests the bound is "quite pessimistic"
- Why unresolved: The theoretical upper bound is too conservative to be practical, and the empirical evidence shows the actual divergence might follow a different pattern with two distinct phases
- What evidence would resolve it: A tighter analytical bound on parameter divergence that accounts for the two-phase behavior observed empirically, or a characterization of when the divergence transitions between phases

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can Influence Functions be effectively used for multi-class classification problems like CIFAR10, given their poor performance compared to binary classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows Opponents-tuning performs poorly on CIFAR10 (only 54% of opponents have desired label) and Proponents-correction shows only modest improvement over baseline
- Why unresolved: The paper conjectures this is related to worse predictive power of IF in ResNet but doesn't provide a definitive explanation or solution
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of IF-based correction methods specifically tailored for multi-class scenarios, or identification of fundamental differences in how influence operates across binary vs multi-class settings

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the key differences in how Influence Functions behave across different optimizer types (SGD vs Adam) and how can this be theoretically characterized?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows different fading behavior between BERT (Adam) and ResNet (SGD), and mentions sketching modifications for other optimizers but doesn't pursue it
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides preliminary observations about different optimizer behaviors without deeper theoretical analysis
- What evidence would resolve it: A unified theoretical framework explaining how different optimizers affect parameter divergence and influence score validity, validated across multiple optimizer types

## Limitations

- The theoretical analysis assumes deterministic training and specific learning rate schedules, which may not reflect real-world training with data shuffling and adaptive learning rates
- Error correction experiments use a relatively small sample (50 mispredicted examples) and limited dataset diversity, raising questions about generalizability to larger-scale problems
- The paper doesn't explore computational efficiency trade-offs between ABIF and alternative methods like the Conjugate Residual method

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core claim that parameter divergence over time limits IF's predictive power is well-supported by both theoretical proofs (Theorem 3) and extensive empirical validation across multiple models and datasets
- **Medium confidence**: The practical recommendation to use IF for limited fine-tuning steps is supported by error correction experiments, though the specific hyperparameters and their robustness require further validation
- **Low confidence**: The assertion that ABIF is the optimal solution for Hessian degeneracy, while theoretically sound, lacks comprehensive comparison with alternative approaches

## Next Checks

1. **Parameter divergence under non-deterministic training**: Replicate the parameter divergence experiments using SGD with momentum and data shuffling to validate whether the exponential growth bound holds under realistic training conditions
2. **Computational efficiency comparison**: Benchmark ABIF against the Conjugate Residual method in terms of runtime and memory usage while maintaining comparable accuracy in influence score computation
3. **Scale-up validation**: Test the error correction methods on larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and models (e.g., ViT) to verify whether the 2-15% improvement in success rates generalizes to more complex scenarios
