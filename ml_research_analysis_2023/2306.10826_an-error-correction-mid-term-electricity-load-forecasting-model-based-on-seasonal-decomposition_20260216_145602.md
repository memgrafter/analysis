# An Error Correction Mid-term Electricity Load Forecasting Model Based on Seasonal Decomposition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10826
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10826
- Reference count: 40
- An error correction based mid-term electricity load forecasting (LF) model that combines STL decomposition, LSTM, and XGBoost to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ECLF, an innovative mid-term electricity load forecasting model that addresses the challenge of accurately predicting electricity demand over medium-term horizons. The model leverages seasonal decomposition through STL to separate load data into seasonal, trend, and random components, then employs an error correction mechanism using LSTM and XGBoost to improve forecasting accuracy. By reconstructing the forecasted components, ECLF achieves significant improvements over traditional forecasting methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of combining decomposition techniques with advanced machine learning models.

## Method Summary
ECLF employs a three-stage approach to electricity load forecasting. First, it decomposes historical load data into seasonal, trend, and random components using STL (Seasonal and Trend decomposition using Loess). Second, LSTM networks are used to forecast the trend and random components separately, treating the LSTM outputs as new features. These features are then fed into XGBoost for a second-step forecasting refinement. Finally, the component forecasts are reconstructed to produce the final load forecast. This error correction mechanism allows ECLF to capture complex patterns in electricity consumption while maintaining interpretability through the decomposition process.

## Key Results
- ECLF achieves mean MAPE of 2.31% and 2.42% on two Chinese city datasets
- Significantly outperforms benchmark models in both accuracy and stability
- Ablation study confirms the effectiveness of the error correction mechanism

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of ECLF stems from its strategic decomposition of complex load patterns into more manageable components, allowing specialized models to focus on specific aspects of the data. By using LSTM to capture temporal dependencies in the trend and random components, and XGBoost to leverage both original and LSTM-derived features, the model can effectively correct errors at multiple stages. The seasonal decomposition through STL provides a stable foundation by isolating predictable seasonal patterns, while the error correction mechanism addresses the non-linear and complex relationships in the trend and random components that single models struggle to capture.

## Foundational Learning
- **STL Decomposition**: Separates time series into seasonal, trend, and residual components; needed to isolate predictable patterns and reduce complexity for subsequent models; quick check: verify stable seasonal patterns across multiple cycles
- **LSTM Networks**: Capture long-term temporal dependencies in sequential data; needed to model the complex patterns in trend and random components; quick check: monitor training loss convergence and gradient flow
- **XGBoost**: Gradient boosting framework for structured data; needed to leverage both original features and LSTM outputs for final refinement; quick check: evaluate feature importance to understand model behavior
- **MAPE Metric**: Mean Absolute Percentage Error; needed to measure forecasting accuracy in relative terms; quick check: ensure MAPE is calculated correctly, especially for near-zero values
- **Error Correction Mechanism**: Iterative refinement of predictions; needed to progressively reduce forecasting errors; quick check: compare performance with and without error correction stages

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: STL -> LSTM -> XGBoost -> Reconstruction

Critical Path: Data Input → STL Decomposition → LSTM Forecasting → XGBoost Refinement → Component Reconstruction → Final Forecast

Design Tradeoffs:
- Model Complexity vs. Accuracy: Combines three sophisticated models for improved accuracy but increases computational cost and reduces interpretability
- Decomposition Stability vs. Flexibility: STL assumes stable seasonal patterns, which may not hold during structural breaks or rapid changes
- LSTM Depth vs. Training Efficiency: Deeper LSTMs may capture more complex patterns but require more data and computational resources

Failure Signatures:
- High MAPE during extreme weather events or economic disruptions
- Degraded performance when seasonal patterns become unstable or shift
- Computational bottlenecks when processing large datasets or running on limited hardware

## Limitations
- Relies on high-quality load data without accounting for potential structural breaks or regime shifts in electricity consumption patterns
- Model complexity creates challenges for interpretability and computational efficiency
- Evaluation limited to only two Chinese cities, raising questions about generalizability across different geographical regions and economic contexts
