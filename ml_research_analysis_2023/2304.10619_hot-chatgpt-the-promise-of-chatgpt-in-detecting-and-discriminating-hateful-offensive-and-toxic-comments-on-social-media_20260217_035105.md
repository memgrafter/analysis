# "HOT" ChatGPT: The promise of ChatGPT in detecting and discriminating hateful, offensive, and toxic comments on social media

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.10619
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10619

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates ChatGPT's effectiveness in detecting and classifying hateful, offensive, and toxic (HOT) content on social media. The research compared ChatGPT's annotations against human annotations from MTurkers using five different prompts across four experiments. The results demonstrate that ChatGPT can serve as a promising tool for annotating large volumes of online content, achieving around 80% accuracy relative to human annotators. However, the study also highlights important considerations around prompt design and the classification of HOT content.

## Method Summary
The researchers conducted a comparative analysis between ChatGPT and human annotators (MTurkers) to evaluate HOT content detection. They used five different prompts to elicit responses from ChatGPT and ran four distinct experiments to assess performance. The study focused on measuring ChatGPT's accuracy, consistency, and tendency to classify content compared to human judgments. The methodology involved presenting both ChatGPT and human annotators with the same set of online comments to classify as hateful, offensive, or toxic.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieved approximately 80% accuracy compared to human annotations
- ChatGPT was more consistent in classifying non-HOT comments than HOT comments
- Binary prompts outperformed probability prompts for HOT classification tasks
- ChatGPT tended to classify content as toxic more frequently than human annotators

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ChatGPT's transformer-based architecture enables it to understand contextual relationships in language, which is crucial for detecting nuanced forms of harmful content. The model's pre-training on diverse internet text allows it to recognize patterns associated with hateful, offensive, and toxic language. The prompt engineering approach leverages ChatGPT's ability to follow instructions and apply consistent reasoning across multiple examples, potentially reducing individual bias that affects human annotators.
