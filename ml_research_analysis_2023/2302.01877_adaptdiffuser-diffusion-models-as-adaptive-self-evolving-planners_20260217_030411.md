# AdaptDiffuser: Diffusion Models as Adaptive Self-evolving Planners

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01877
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01877
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: AdaptDiffuser outperforms Diffuser by 20.8% on Maze2D, 7.5% on MuJoCo, and 27.9% on KUKA pick-and-place without requiring additional expert data for new tasks

## Executive Summary
AdaptDiffuser introduces a novel diffusion-based planner that leverages reinforcement learning reward gradients to generate synthetic demonstration data for goal-conditioned tasks. Unlike traditional approaches that depend heavily on expert data, AdaptDiffuser self-evolves by using RL rewards to guide the generation of diverse trajectories, which are then filtered through a discriminator to fine-tune the diffusion model. This self-bootstrapping mechanism enables the planner to improve on previously seen tasks and generalize to novel scenarios without additional expert demonstrations.

The approach demonstrates significant performance gains across multiple benchmarks, including MuJoCo locomotion tasks, Maze2D navigation, and a KUKA pick-and-place task. By eliminating the need for task-specific expert data while maintaining or improving performance, AdaptDiffuser addresses a key limitation of existing diffusion-based planners and presents a more scalable approach to goal-conditioned decision-making.

## Method Summary
AdaptDiffuser combines diffusion models with reinforcement learning to create a self-evolving planner for goal-conditioned tasks. The core innovation lies in using RL reward gradients as guidance signals to generate synthetic demonstration data, which is then filtered by a discriminator to identify high-quality trajectories. These filtered demonstrations are used to fine-tune the diffusion model, creating a feedback loop that improves the planner's performance over time. This approach eliminates the dependency on extensive expert demonstrations for both existing and novel tasks, enabling the system to adapt to new scenarios through self-generation of training data.

## Key Results
- Outperforms Diffuser by 20.8% on Maze2D benchmark tasks
- Improves MuJoCo locomotion performance by 7.5% over previous methods
- Achieves 27.9% better performance on KUKA pick-and-place task without additional expert data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AdaptDiffuser works by integrating reinforcement learning reward signals into the diffusion model training process. The RL guidance directs the generation of trajectories toward high-reward regions of the state-action space, while the discriminator filters out low-quality synthetic data. This creates a virtuous cycle where the diffusion model continuously improves its ability to generate useful trajectories for both seen and unseen tasks. The approach effectively combines the generative power of diffusion models with the optimization capabilities of reinforcement learning, enabling self-improvement without external expert supervision.

## Foundational Learning

**Diffusion Models**: Generative models that learn to reverse a noising process, gradually transforming random noise into realistic samples. Why needed: Provides the base architecture for generating trajectories. Quick check: Can generate realistic state-action sequences given random noise.

**Reinforcement Learning Reward Gradients**: Signals that indicate the desirability of actions in given states. Why needed: Guides the generation of useful synthetic trajectories. Quick check: Can direct synthetic data generation toward high-reward regions.

**Discriminator Networks**: Binary classifiers that distinguish between real and synthetic data. Why needed: Filters low-quality synthetic demonstrations to maintain training data quality. Quick check: Can effectively identify and reject poor-quality generated trajectories.

**Goal-Conditioned Planning**: Framework where policies must achieve specific target states. Why needed: The primary task setting for AdaptDiffuser. Quick check: Can condition trajectory generation on specific goal states.

**Self-bootstrapping**: The ability to improve performance through self-generated training data. Why needed: Enables adaptation to new tasks without external expert data. Quick check: Can improve on previously seen tasks and generalize to novel scenarios.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Environment -> RL Agent -> Reward Gradients -> Diffusion Model -> Discriminator -> Filtered Data -> Fine-tuned Diffusion Model -> Planner

**Critical Path**: The RL-guided diffusion model generates synthetic trajectories, which are filtered by the discriminator to produce high-quality demonstrations that fine-tune the model, creating a self-improving planning system.

**Design Tradeoffs**: AdaptDiffuser trades off the stability of expert-only training for the flexibility and scalability of self-generated data. This increases the risk of learning from suboptimal trajectories but enables adaptation to novel tasks without additional supervision.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor discriminator performance could allow low-quality synthetic data to corrupt the diffusion model. Inadequate RL guidance might generate trajectories that don't effectively explore the solution space. Overfitting to the discriminator could reduce the diversity of generated trajectories.

## Limitations

- Scalability to high-dimensional, complex tasks beyond MuJoCo and Maze2D environments remains unproven
- Performance on tasks with significantly different dynamics or reward structures than training data is uncertain
- The quality and diversity of self-generated synthetic data may limit performance in highly novel scenarios

## Confidence

- Performance improvements over Diffuser (20.8% Maze2D, 7.5% MuJoCo, 27.9% KUKA): Medium
- Self-bootstrapping capabilities without additional expert data: Medium
- Generalizability to arbitrary unseen tasks: Medium

## Next Checks

1. Test AdaptDiffuser on more complex manipulation tasks with higher-dimensional state and action spaces, such as multi-object rearrangement or tasks requiring tool use

3. Conduct an ablation study to quantify the impact of each component (diffusion model, discriminator, RL guidance) on the overall performance, and test the method's robustness to noise or perturbations in the synthetic data generation process
