# Transferring Multiple Policies to Hotstart Reinforcement Learning in an Air Compressor Management Problem

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12820
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12820
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 11
- One-line primary result: Policy Intersection significantly outperforms loading a single transferred controller, with performance improvements more pronounced when using multiple advisors

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of accelerating reinforcement learning in air compressor management by transferring multiple previously learned policies. The authors propose Policy Intersection, a Policy Shaping technique that leverages multiple controllers to guide the exploration of a new SAC agent. The method demonstrates significant performance improvements over single advisor transfer and no transfer learning baselines, particularly when recovering from initial learning dips. The approach effectively handles continuous action spaces and provides warm-start benefits without convergence issues.

## Method Summary
The method extends Policy Intersection to continuous action spaces for air compressor management. It samples actions from the learning policy, evaluates them with multiple advisor policies to get probability densities, creates a categorical distribution, and samples from this distribution. The approach combines multiple advisors by randomly selecting one at each time-step, allowing the agent to benefit from multiple perspectives rather than single advisor guidance. This is applied to a Soft Actor-Critic algorithm to control three piston compressors while maintaining tank pressure between 3-5 bars and minimizing energy consumption.

## Key Results
- Policy Intersection with multiple advisors significantly outperforms loading a single transferred controller
- Performance improvement is more pronounced when recovering from the initial learning dip
- The method achieves higher cumulative reward compared to both single advisor transfer and no transfer learning baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Policy Intersection outperforms loading a single advisor by combining multiple advisors' guidance
- Mechanism: At each time-step, a single advisor is randomly selected from the pool of available advisors. The agent samples many actions from its current policy, obtains probability densities from the selected advisor for these actions, computes a categorical distribution, and samples an action from this intersection. This allows the agent to benefit from multiple perspectives rather than just one advisor's guidance.
- Core assumption: The advisors' policies provide complementary and non-conflicting guidance that improves exploration efficiency
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method achieves a higher cumulative reward compared to loading a single advisor or using no transfer learning at all."
  - [section] "We observe in Figure 2 that exploiting multiple advisors instead of a single one significantly improve performance, especially when recovering from the dip in performance."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly address Policy Intersection for continuous actions
- Break condition: If advisors' policies conflict significantly or if one advisor dominates the others, the intersection may become too restrictive or biased.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The method effectively handles continuous action spaces through probability density intersection
- Mechanism: Instead of directly multiplying discrete probability distributions as in the original Policy Intersection method, this approach samples many actions from the actor, gets probability densities from the advisor for each sampled action, creates a categorical distribution over these actions, and samples from this distribution. This approximates the intersection of continuous probability distributions.
- Core assumption: The sampled actions adequately represent the continuous action space and the advisor can provide meaningful probability densities for them
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Assuming that the advisor can similarly be sampled, our approach to sampling the intersection between which actions are allowed by the actor and those allowed by the advisor is the following:"
  - [section] "This method implements an 'AND' between the actor and the advisor's policies."
  - [corpus] No corpus evidence directly supports this specific continuous action adaptation
- Break condition: If the action space is too high-dimensional or if the advisor cannot provide reliable probability densities, the method may fail to find good intersections.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transfer learning through Policy Intersection provides warm-start benefits without convergence issues
- Mechanism: The transferred policies guide the exploration of the new agent, helping it avoid poor initial actions while still allowing learning. The agent can learn from off-policy experiences generated under the advisor's guidance.
- Core assumption: The source tasks are similar enough that advisors provide relevant guidance but different enough that the agent still needs to learn
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Transfer Learning has the potential to make RL agents much faster at mastering new tasks, by allowing the reuse of knowledge acquired in previous tasks."
  - [section] "Policy Intersection allowsπL andπA to more cooperatively select actions, withπA able to increase or decrease the probability of actions, but without fully determining the action."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly address warm-start RL with Policy Intersection
- Break condition: If source and target tasks are too dissimilar, advisors may provide misleading guidance that hinders learning.
