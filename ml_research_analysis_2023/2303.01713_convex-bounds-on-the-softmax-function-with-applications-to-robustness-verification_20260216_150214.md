# Convex Bounds on the Softmax Function with Applications to Robustness Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01713
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01713
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Derives convex and concave bounds on the softmax function via exponential-reciprocal and log-sum-exp decompositions to enable scalable robustness verification of neural networks with softmax components.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to deriving convex and concave bounds on the softmax function, which is crucial for scalable robustness verification of neural networks with softmax components. The authors propose two decompositions of the softmax: an exponential-reciprocal decomposition and a log-sum-exp decomposition. They prove that the log-sum-exp bounds are tighter than the exponential-reciprocal bounds for two softmax inputs. The bounds are applied to verify the robustness of predictive uncertainty estimates of deep ensembles and transformers, showing improved precision compared to existing linear bounds.

## Method Summary
The authors derive bounds on the softmax function using two decompositions: exponential-reciprocal (ER) and log-sum-exp (LSE). For ER, they treat the sum of exponentials as an intermediate variable and bound it from below and above using convex relaxations. For LSE, they exploit the concavity of -LSE to obtain tighter bounds. The bounds are either used directly in convex optimization problems or linearized via tangent planes for integration into bound-propagation frameworks like CROWN/DeepPoly. The method is validated on synthetic data and real-world datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, SST-2) for predictive uncertainty estimation and self-attention mechanisms.

## Key Results
- The log-sum-exp upper bound is proven to be tighter than the exponential-reciprocal upper bound, and the log-sum-exp lower bound is tighter than the exponential-reciprocal lower bound for two softmax inputs.
- The bounds improve the precision of robustness verification for predictive uncertainty estimates of deep ensembles and transformers compared to existing linear bounds.
- Tangent-plane linearization of nonlinear bounds preserves soundness while improving scalability in bound-propagation frameworks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The exponential-reciprocal decomposition allows tighter convex and concave bounds on softmax by relaxing intermediate constraints.
- Mechanism: By treating the sum of exponentials (SE) as an intermediate variable q1 and bounding it both from below (using SE itself) and above (using a linear chord), then bounding the reciprocal 1/q1 similarly, the composed bounds on softmax become tighter than purely linear bounds.
- Core assumption: SE(·) is convex and 1/q1 is convex in q1, so their convex relaxations yield valid lower/upper bounds.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We derive bounds using both a natural exponential-reciprocal decomposition of the softmax as well as an alternative decomposition in terms of the log-sum-exp function."
  - [section 3.2] "Since SE(˜x) is convex, the above constraint is in the desired form as in (4): it specifies a convex set of (x,q1) and is compatible with convex optimization."
  - [corpus] No direct match, but related work on ReLU bounds (LFR, 2024) uses similar convex relaxation principles.
- Break condition: If SE(·) or 1/q1 are not strictly convex in the region of interest, the bounds may not be valid.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The log-sum-exp (LSE) decomposition yields provably tighter bounds than the exponential-reciprocal approach.
- Mechanism: Since -LSE(·) is concave, it can be used directly as an upper bound on the intermediate variable r1, and its negative can be bounded below by convex functions (e.g., chords), leading to tighter overall bounds on softmax.
- Core assumption: The concavity of -LSE(·) allows it to serve as an upper bound without further relaxation, and the convex lower bounds on -LSE(·) are tighter than those from the ER decomposition.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We prove that the LSE upper bound is always tighter than the exponential-reciprocal upper bound, and that the LSE lower bound is tighter than the ER lower bound for the case of K = 2 inputs."
  - [section 4] "Since it is concave in x, we may use it as the upper bound on its output r1: r1 ≤ -LSE(˜x)."
  - [corpus] Weak; related work on sigmoid relaxations (Achieving the Tightest Relaxation of Sigmoids, 2024) uses LSE-like techniques but not for softmax.
- Break condition: For K > 2, the LSE lower bound may not always be tighter than ER, though empirically it usually is.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Linearizing the nonlinear bounds via tangent planes preserves soundness while improving scalability in bound-propagation frameworks.
- Mechanism: Any tangent plane to a convex lower bound is also a valid lower bound; similarly for concave upper bounds. This allows integration into existing bound-propagation methods (e.g., CROWN/DeepPoly) without losing correctness.
- Core assumption: The original nonlinear bounds are differentiable almost everywhere, so tangent planes exist and are valid relaxations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5] "Any tangent plane to a convex lower bound is also a sound lower bound; and any tangent plane to a concave upper bound is also a sound upper bound."
  - [section 7.2] "we use the CROWN/DeepPoly framework (Singh et al., 2019b; Zhang et al., 2018), which requires that each neuron is over-approximated with one linear upper bound and one linear lower bound."
  - [corpus] Weak; no direct evidence in corpus for tangent-plane linearization of softmax bounds.
- Break condition: If the nonlinear bounds are not smooth (e.g., at boundaries), tangent planes may not exist or may be poor approximations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input logits from neural network layers
  - Decomposition: Softmax via exponential-reciprocal (ER) or log-sum-exp (LSE)
  - Bounds: Convex lower bounds (Llin, LER, LLSE, LLSE*) and concave upper bounds (Ulin, UER, ULSE)
  - Verification: Bounds used in convex optimization or bound-propagation for properties like robustness
  - Integration: Bounds linearized via tangent planes for CROWN/DeepPoly framework

- Critical path:
  1. Given input bounds [l,u], compute intermediate bounds [˜l,˜u] on differences
  2. Apply ER or LSE decomposition to derive nonlinear bounds
  3. Optionally linearize bounds via tangent planes
  4. Use bounds in convex optimization or bound-propagation for verification

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Tighter bounds (LSE vs ER) come at higher computational cost in solving resulting convex problems
  - Linear bounds are faster to compute and integrate but may be looser
  - Tangent-plane linearization improves scalability but sacrifices some tightness

- Failure signatures:
  - If input bounds [l,u] are too wide, bounds may become trivial (e.g., Ulin = 1)
  - If decomposition is applied incorrectly, bounds may be invalid
  - Numerical instability may occur if exponentials overflow/underflow

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement ER and LSE bounds for K=2 (logistic sigmoid) and verify tightness numerically on synthetic data
  2. Compare runtime and verification precision of nonlinear vs linear bounds on a small MNIST model with softmax output
  3. Integrate linearized bounds into CROWN/DeepPoly and test on a transformer model with self-attention

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the nonlinear bounds be further improved to achieve even tighter bounds on the softmax function, especially for cases where K > 2?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the log-sum-exp lower bound LLSE2 is provably tighter than LER for K = 2, but for K > 2, while LLSE and LLSE* are sometimes better than LER, they are not always superior. The paper also suggests the possibility of a more smoothly integrated lower bound.
- Why unresolved: The current bounds, while tighter than linear bounds, still leave room for improvement, particularly for higher-dimensional cases (K > 2). The paper suggests that the two LSE bounds (LLSE and LLSE*) are complementary but do not provide a unified, always-tighter bound.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a new bound that consistently outperforms LER for K > 2, or proving that such a bound cannot exist, would resolve this question. Numerical experiments comparing the tightness of new bounds against LER across various K values would also provide evidence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the computational trade-offs between using nonlinear bounds (LER, UER, LLSE, LLSE*, U LSE) versus linearized bounds (tangent planes) in large-scale neural network verification tasks?
- Basis in paper: The paper presents both nonlinear and linearized bounds and shows that the linearized bounds can be integrated into existing bound-propagation frameworks. However, the paper also notes that the tangent planes are strictly less tight than their nonlinear counterparts.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the benefits of nonlinear bounds in terms of tightness, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost of using these bounds versus linearized bounds in large-scale settings. The trade-off between precision and computational efficiency is not fully explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that measure the runtime and memory usage of both nonlinear and linearized bounds on large-scale neural networks would provide evidence. Additionally, developing heuristics for deciding when to use nonlinear versus linearized bounds could be valuable.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the softmax bounds be adapted to handle more complex neural network architectures, such as those with skip connections or recurrent layers?
- Basis in paper: The paper focuses on feedforward neural networks and transformers, which have specific structures. It does not address how the softmax bounds would apply to other architectures like those with skip connections or recurrent layers.
- Why unresolved: The current bounds are derived based on the properties of feedforward and transformer architectures. Adapting these bounds to handle other architectures would require new techniques to account for the additional complexities introduced by skip connections or recurrent layers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the current bounds to handle skip connections or recurrent layers and demonstrating their effectiveness through experiments on networks with these architectures would resolve this question. Alternatively, proving that such extensions are not feasible for certain architectures would also provide clarity.

## Limitations
- The claim about LSE bounds being universally tighter for all K > 2 is not proven, only validated for K = 2.
- The paper lacks detailed empirical comparisons of runtime between different bound approaches, particularly for large-scale models.
- The results may depend on implementation details of specific convex optimization solvers (SCS) and bound-propagation frameworks (CROWN/DeepPoly).

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The mathematical derivations of convex/concave bounds using ER and LSE decompositions are rigorous and well-supported by the literature on convex optimization.
- **Medium Confidence**: The empirical validation on MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets demonstrates the practical utility of the bounds, but the sample size and diversity of experiments could be expanded.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim about LSE bounds being universally tighter for all K > 2 is not proven, and the paper lacks comprehensive runtime comparisons between different bound approaches.
