# SMART: Self-supervised Multi-task pretrAining with contRol Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09816
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09816
- Reference count: 40
- The paper proposes SMART, a self-supervised multi-task pretraining framework for sequential decision making using Control Transformer (CT).

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SMART, a self-supervised multi-task pretraining framework that learns reward-agnostic representations for sequential decision making using Control Transformers. SMART addresses the challenge of pretraining control representations that can handle high-dimensional perceptual inputs and long-term control across diverse tasks. The method uses a control-centric objective combining forward/inverse dynamics prediction and random masked hindsight control, enabling it to learn representations transferable across tasks without reward supervision. Experiments on DeepMind Control Suite demonstrate significant improvements in learning efficiency for both seen and unseen tasks under imitation and reinforcement learning, with strong generalization to unseen domains and robustness to low-quality pretraining data.

## Method Summary
SMART pretrains a Control Transformer (CT) using self-supervised objectives on multiple control tasks without reward signals. The method collects offline datasets from multiple pretraining tasks, then pretrains the CT to predict forward and inverse dynamics while learning controllable representations through random masked hindsight control. After pretraining, the model is fine-tuned on downstream tasks using either reinforcement learning with reward-to-go (RTG) or behavior cloning (BC). The pretraining process deliberately excludes rewards to create reward-agnostic representations that transfer better across tasks with different reward functions.

## Key Results
- SMART significantly improves learning efficiency in both seen and unseen downstream tasks and domains on DeepMind Control Suite
- The method demonstrates strong generalization to unseen domains and robustness to low-quality pretraining data (random vs exploratory datasets)
- SMART outperforms existing representation learning methods like contrastive predictive coding (CPC) and action token control (ATC)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Control Transformer learns reward-agnostic representations by excluding reward signals during pretraining.
- Mechanism: By omitting reward from the control sequence input, the model is forced to learn representations based purely on state-action transitions, which can be adapted to multiple downstream tasks with different reward functions.
- Core assumption: Reward information is task-specific and can introduce distribution shift when transferring between tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SMART encourages the representation to capture the common essential information relevant to short-term control and long-term control, which is transferrable across tasks."
  - [section]: "Differently from Decision Transformer (DT) (Chen et al., 2021), we purposefully do not include the reward signal in the control sequence to keep our representations reward-agnostic..."
- Break condition: If downstream tasks require reward information for policy learning, the reward-agnostic representation may need additional adaptation steps.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The control-centric pretraining objective combining forward dynamics, inverse dynamics, and masked hindsight control captures both short-term and long-term control-relevant information.
- Mechanism: Forward and inverse dynamics prediction focus on immediate state transitions, while masked hindsight control encourages the model to learn global temporal dependencies and controllable representations.
- Core assumption: Both short-term and long-term temporal information are necessary for effective control policy learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SMART encourages the representation to capture the common essential information relevant to short-term control and long-term control, which is transferrable across tasks."
  - [section]: "The first two terms focus on local and short-term dynamics, while the third term is designed to capture more global and long-term temporal dependence."
- Break condition: If the task dynamics are primarily short-term or the observation space lacks temporal structure, the long-term component may provide limited benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-task pretraining with diverse control tasks improves generalization to unseen tasks and domains.
- Mechanism: By training on multiple tasks spanning different domains, the model learns shared representations that capture common control-relevant features across tasks.
- Core assumption: There exists common control-relevant information across diverse sequential decision-making tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "SMART encourages the representation to capture the common essential information relevant to short-term control and long-term control, which is transferrable across tasks."
  - [section]: "We show by extensive experiments in DeepMind Control Suite that SMART significantly improves the learning efficiency among seen and unseen downstream tasks and domains..."
- Break condition: If tasks are too dissimilar or have fundamentally different state-action spaces, shared representation learning may be ineffective.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Control sequence (observations and actions, no rewards)
  - Core: Control Transformer with causal attention
  - Tokenization: Separate tokenizers for observations, actions, and returns
  - Output: State and action representations (φ(o_t) and φ(a_t))
  - Pretraining heads: Forward prediction, inverse prediction, and masked hindsight control heads
  - Finetuning head: Policy head (varies by learning method)

- Critical path:
  1. Prepare control sequence input (length 2L)
  2. Tokenize observations and actions
  3. Apply causal attention in transformer layers
  4. Generate state and action representations
  5. Apply appropriate prediction heads for pretraining or policy head for finetuning

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Reward-agnostic vs reward-based pretraining: Reward-agnostic allows better transfer but may require more finetuning
  - Context length (L=30): Longer sequences capture more temporal information but increase computational cost
  - Model capacity (8 layers, 8 heads, 256 dim): Larger models may capture more complex patterns but risk overfitting on smaller datasets

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor downstream performance: Could indicate insufficient pretraining diversity, inadequate model capacity, or distribution shift between pretraining and finetuning
  - Slow convergence: May suggest suboptimal learning rate or need for longer pretraining
  - High variance in results: Could indicate sensitivity to random seeds or need for more stable training objectives

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify pretraining improves single-task performance compared to training from scratch
  2. Test generalization to unseen tasks within the same domain
  3. Evaluate resilience to low-quality pretraining data (random vs exploratory datasets)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does SMART's performance degrade with even larger distribution shifts between pretraining and downstream tasks, beyond what was tested?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper tested generalizability to unseen tasks and domains, but the authors note that performance will eventually decrease as discrepancy increases. They also observed ODT pretraining with random data struggles with online fine-tuning due to large distribution shift.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only tested a limited set of unseen tasks and domains. There's no systematic study of how performance scales with increasing domain/task discrepancy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the similarity between pretraining and downstream tasks (e.g., different physical parameters, observation spaces, action spaces) and measuring performance degradation curves.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does SMART compare to state-of-the-art ResNet-based pretraining methods in terms of sample efficiency during downstream fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares SMART to CPC and ATC (ResNet-based) in terms of final performance, but doesn't explicitly analyze sample efficiency during fine-tuning.
- Why unresolved: The paper reports average rewards after a fixed number of fine-tuning epochs, but doesn't measure how quickly each method reaches a certain performance threshold or how many samples are needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments measuring learning curves (reward vs. number of fine-tuning samples) for SMART vs. ResNet-based methods, reporting metrics like sample efficiency or time to reach 90% of expert performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between short-term and long-term control information in the pretraining objective?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that combining forward/inverse dynamics prediction (short-term) with random masked hindsight control (long-term) performs better than either alone, but doesn't explore the optimal weighting or scheduling of these terms.
- Why unresolved: The pretraining objective uses equal weights for all three terms, and the masking schedule is heuristic. There's no systematic study of how different weightings or schedules affect downstream performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies varying the weights of each pretraining term, and experiments with different masking schedules (e.g., linear vs. exponential increase, different rates) to find optimal configurations.

## Limitations

- The paper demonstrates strong performance on DeepMind Control Suite tasks but the extent to which these results generalize to more complex real-world control problems remains untested
- The impact of different pretraining data qualities (Random vs Exploratory) on final performance is not fully characterized, with some performance gaps potentially attributable to random seed variation
- The specific architectural choices (context length of 30, model dimensions) are not extensively ablated to determine their optimal values

## Confidence

- High confidence in the core mechanism of reward-agnostic pretraining enabling cross-task transfer
- Medium confidence in the generalization benefits of multi-task pretraining, as results show variance across different task combinations
- Medium confidence in the robustness claims, particularly regarding low-quality pretraining data, as the comparison lacks statistical significance testing
