# Creating Large Language Model Resistant Exams: Guidelines and Strategies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12203
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12203
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Proposed strategies can create exam questions that GPT-4 answers correctly only 8% of the time without CoT prompting, rising to 60% with CoT prompting.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the growing challenge of maintaining academic integrity in the face of increasingly sophisticated Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT. The authors propose a comprehensive framework for designing exam questions that resist LLM-based cheating while remaining accessible to human students. Through systematic evaluation with GPT-4, the study demonstrates that carefully crafted questions can exploit the model's limitations in areas such as content moderation, knowledge boundaries, and reasoning capabilities.

The research presents practical strategies including the use of content moderation triggers, deliberate inaccuracies, real-world scenarios beyond the model's knowledge base, effective distractor options, evaluation of soft skills, and incorporation of non-textual information. Initial testing shows promising results, with GPT-4 achieving only 8% accuracy on resistant questions without chain-of-thought prompting, compared to 60% with explicit intermediate reasoning steps provided.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted systematic evaluations of LLM-resistant question design strategies using GPT-4 API. They tested various approaches including content moderation triggers, deliberate inaccuracies, real-world scenarios, and chain-of-thought prompting requirements. Questions were designed to exploit specific LLM limitations while remaining answerable by human students with appropriate domain knowledge. The evaluation measured GPT-4's success rate under different prompting conditions and analyzed response patterns to identify effective resistance mechanisms.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieved only 8% accuracy on LLM-resistant questions without chain-of-thought prompting
- Accuracy improved to 60% when chain-of-thought prompting was provided
- Content moderation APIs effectively blocked sensitive content queries
- Real-world scenario questions exposed LLM limitations in applying theoretical knowledge to practical situations
- Deliberate inaccuracies successfully misled LLM reasoning chains while humans could identify and work around errors

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-4 can be blocked from answering questions containing sensitive or illicit content through moderation APIs.
- Mechanism: The model's built-in moderation API flags queries with dangerous ideologies or instructions for creating illicit substances, preventing response generation.
- Core assumption: The moderation API reliably detects and blocks all sensitive content patterns without false positives.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "GPT-4 is designed to abide by strict ethical guidelines that prioritize user safety and prevent the dissemination of harmful content...Consequently, GPT-4's moderation API will flag this question, preventing the model from generating any response."
  - [corpus] Weak - The corpus contains no direct evidence about moderation API reliability or false positive rates.
- Break Condition: The moderation API fails to detect carefully worded sensitive content or generates false positives on legitimate academic questions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Deliberate inaccuracies in exam questions can mislead LLMs into providing incorrect answers while humans can identify and work around them.
- Mechanism: When presented with false premises, LLMs generate responses based on flawed reasoning chains, while humans can recognize the error and adjust their approach.
- Core assumption: Humans possess superior error detection and reasoning correction capabilities compared to LLMs when presented with false premises.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The query inaccurately posits that jellyfish possess the capacity for photosynthesis...It is worth noting that the most accurate response in this context is [D] Zooplankton."
  - [corpus] Weak - No corpus evidence specifically addresses human vs LLM performance on questions with deliberate inaccuracies.
- Break Condition: The LLM encounters similar false premises frequently enough in training data that it learns to identify and correct them automatically.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Questions requiring real-world scenarios beyond the model's knowledge base expose the LLM's limitations in applying theoretical knowledge to practical situations.
- Mechanism: LLMs generate generic responses based on pattern matching when faced with unfamiliar contexts, while humans can draw on specific experiences and practical knowledge.
- Core assumption: The model's training data lacks sufficient coverage of specific real-world scenarios that students encounter in their coursework.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In this case the model will generate a generic result that is easily detectable, while a student will know that wind turbine in question had various faulty components leading to specific decreases in efficiency."
  - [corpus] Weak - The corpus contains no evidence about the depth or breadth of real-world scenario coverage in LLM training data.
- Break Condition: The model encounters similar real-world scenarios frequently enough in training data that it learns to generate specific rather than generic responses.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Chain of Thought (CoT) Prompting
  - Why needed here: The paper demonstrates that GPT-4 requires explicit intermediate reasoning steps to arrive at correct answers for complex problems.
  - Quick check question: If a student provides GPT-4 with the complete solution steps but not the final answer, can the model reproduce the correct answer?

- Concept: Model Knowledge Boundaries
  - Why needed here: Understanding what the model knows and doesn't know is crucial for designing effective LLM-resistant questions.
  - Quick check question: How would you determine whether a specific topic or scenario is likely to be in the model's training data?

- Concept: Adversarial Example Generation
  - Why needed here: Creating questions that exploit known model weaknesses requires understanding of how to generate adversarial examples.
  - Quick check question: What characteristics make a question more likely to confuse an LLM while remaining clear to human test-takers?

- Component Map:

- Critical Path:
  1. Design question with resistance strategy
  2. Generate multiple prompt variations
  3. Test against GPT-4 API
  4. Analyze response accuracy
  5. Refine question based on results

- Design Tradeoffs:
  - Question complexity vs. student accessibility
  - Resistance strength vs. grading practicality
  - Real-world relevance vs. AI resistance
  - Time investment vs. resistance effectiveness

- Failure Signatures:
  - Model refuses to answer (moderation triggers)
  - Model provides generic responses (knowledge gap)
  - Model follows incorrect reasoning chains (misdirection successful)
  - Model requires CoT prompting to succeed (complexity threshold)

- First 3 Experiments:
  1. Test moderation API by creating questions with progressively subtle sensitive content
  2. Design questions with deliberate inaccuracies and measure human vs LLM performance gap
  3. Create real-world scenario questions with known model knowledge gaps and test response specificity

## Limitations
- Evaluation methodology relies exclusively on GPT-4 testing, limiting generalizability to other LLM architectures
- No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals reported for accuracy rates
- Notably weak corpus evidence base with no supporting literature for core assumptions
- Does not address potential false positive rates when applying resistance strategies to legitimate student responses

## Confidence
**High Confidence:** The basic premise that LLMs can generate sophisticated responses to academic questions is well-established. The observation that GPT-4's accuracy improves significantly with CoT prompting (8% → 60%) is directly supported by the experimental results presented.

**Medium Confidence:** The proposed resistance strategies (moderation triggers, deliberate inaccuracies, real-world scenarios) appear theoretically sound based on the mechanism descriptions, though empirical validation beyond GPT-4 testing is lacking. The claim that humans can better handle deliberate inaccuracies than LLMs is plausible but not empirically supported in the corpus.

**Low Confidence:** Claims about moderation API reliability and the depth of LLM knowledge gaps in real-world scenarios lack empirical support. The assertion that humans will consistently outperform LLMs on these question types requires further validation.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-Model Validation:** Test the proposed LLM-resistant question types against multiple LLM architectures (Claude, Llama, Gemini) to assess generalizability of the resistance strategies and identify model-specific vulnerabilities.

2. **Statistical Significance Analysis:** Conduct formal statistical testing on the accuracy differences between CoT and non-CoT conditions, including confidence intervals and effect size calculations to quantify the robustness of the resistance mechanisms.

3. **Human Performance Benchmarking:** Conduct controlled experiments comparing human test-taker performance on LLM-resistant questions versus traditional questions to validate the assumption that these strategies effectively differentiate between human and machine responses while maintaining assessment validity.
