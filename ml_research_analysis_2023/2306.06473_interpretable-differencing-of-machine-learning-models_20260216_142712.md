# Interpretable Differencing of Machine Learning Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06473
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06473
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a method for interpretable differencing of machine learning models using Joint Surrogate Trees (JSTs) to identify where in feature space two models' predicted outcomes differ

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for interpretable differencing of machine learning models, aiming to identify where in the feature space two models' predicted outcomes differ. The authors formalize this as predicting a dissimilarity function of two models' outputs, subject to human-interpretability constraints. Their solution is a Joint Surrogate Tree (JST), which combines two decision tree surrogates for the models, allowing differences to be shown in the context of the models' decision logic. A refinement procedure improves precision. Experiments on 13 datasets show JSTs yield more concise representations of differences compared to naive approaches, with no loss in fidelity.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes Joint Surrogate Trees (JSTs) as a method for interpretable differencing of machine learning models. JSTs combine two decision tree surrogates for the models, enabling visualization of differences in decision logic context. The approach involves creating surrogate trees for each model, then combining them to highlight differences while maintaining interpretability. A refinement procedure is used to improve precision of the identified differences.

## Key Results
- JSTs yield more concise representations of differences compared to naive approaches
- The method maintains fidelity while improving conciseness
- Enables intuitive visualization and explanation of model differences, aiding user understanding of AI systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The JST approach works by creating surrogate decision trees for each model, then combining them to highlight differences in decision logic. This allows for intuitive visualization of where models disagree in the feature space, while maintaining interpretability through the use of decision trees. The refinement procedure improves precision of identified differences without sacrificing interpretability.

## Foundational Learning
1. Decision tree surrogates - Why needed: To create interpretable approximations of complex models. Quick check: Verify that surrogate trees accurately represent original model behavior.
2. Feature space analysis - Why needed: To identify regions where models' predictions differ. Quick check: Confirm that identified differences correspond to actual model behavior.
3. Interpretability constraints - Why needed: To ensure that the differencing method remains human-understandable. Quick check: Validate that the JST representation is intuitive to human users.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Original models -> Surrogate trees -> Joint Surrogate Tree -> Refinement procedure -> Interpretable difference representation

Critical path: Model -> Surrogate tree creation -> JST construction -> Difference identification -> Refinement -> Final interpretable output

Design tradeoffs: Balancing conciseness of difference representation with fidelity to original models' behavior.

Failure signatures: Loss of fidelity in surrogate trees, inability to scale to high-dimensional feature spaces, loss of interpretability in complex difference representations.

First experiments:
1. Create surrogate trees for simple models on low-dimensional datasets
2. Construct JSTs and identify differences on controlled test cases
3. Evaluate the impact of the refinement procedure on precision and interpretability

## Limitations
- Does not address computational complexity issues when scaling to high-dimensional feature spaces or very deep decision trees
- Claims of "more concise representations" lack quantitative comparison metrics
- Refinement procedure's impact on interpretability versus precision trade-offs is not thoroughly examined

## Confidence
High confidence in the technical soundness of the JST methodology and its basic feasibility. Medium confidence in the empirical claims about conciseness and fidelity. Low confidence in the practical applicability to real-world scenarios without further validation on larger, more complex datasets and models.

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate JST performance on high-dimensional datasets (e.g., 100+ features) to assess scalability and computational efficiency
2. Conduct user studies to validate whether JSTs actually improve human understanding of model differences compared to baseline approaches
3. Test the method on non-tree-based models (e.g., neural networks, ensemble methods) to verify generalizability beyond the current experimental scope
