# Improving Position Encoding of Transformers for Multivariate Time Series Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16642
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16642
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Proposed tAPE and eRPE positional encodings combined with convolutional input encoding achieve highest average rank (1.8125) on 32 multivariate time series classification benchmark datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of encoding positional information in transformers for multivariate time series classification. The authors identify that traditional positional encodings like sine-cosine and learnable embeddings suffer from either poor distance awareness or excessive parameter growth. To solve this, they propose two novel encodings: tAPE (transformed absolute position encoding) that balances distance awareness with isotropy, and eRPE (efficient relative position encoding) that uses scalar representation to avoid parameter explosion. When combined with convolutional input encoding in the ConvTran model, these methods significantly outperform state-of-the-art deep learning models for multivariate time series classification, achieving the highest average rank of 1.8125 compared to 3.4219 for the next best model.

## Method Summary
The authors propose two new position encoding methods for transformers applied to multivariate time series classification. tAPE (transformed absolute position encoding) incorporates series length and embedding dimension into its design to balance distance awareness and isotropy properties. eRPE (efficient relative position encoding) uses a scalar representation to avoid parameter explosion while maintaining effectiveness for relative positioning. These encodings are combined with convolutional input encoding in a model called ConvTran. The convolutional input encoding captures local patterns in the time series before feeding them to the transformer, addressing the limitation of transformers in capturing local dependencies. The complete architecture integrates these components to effectively encode both absolute and relative positional information while preserving local temporal patterns.

## Key Results
- ConvTran achieves the highest average rank of 1.8125 on 32 benchmark datasets, significantly outperforming other models
- The proposed tAPE and eRPE encodings consistently improve classification accuracy compared to sine-cosine and learnable position encodings
- ConvTran shows particularly strong performance on datasets with longer time series where traditional positional encodings struggle

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Transformers lack inherent awareness of input order, making positional encodings essential for time series data. Traditional methods like sine-cosine encodings provide good distance awareness but suffer from isotropy issues (points far apart appear similar), while learnable encodings can capture complex patterns but explode in parameter count for long sequences. The tAPE encoding addresses this by transforming the absolute position using series length and embedding dimension to maintain both distance awareness and isotropy. The eRPE encoding represents relative positions with scalar values rather than full vectors, dramatically reducing parameters while preserving relative ordering information. By combining these with convolutional input encoding, ConvTran captures both local patterns (through convolution) and global positional relationships (through novel encodings), creating a more complete representation of multivariate time series data.

## Foundational Learning

**Positional Encoding in Transformers**: Necessary because transformers are permutation-invariant and need explicit information about input order. Without it, they cannot distinguish temporal sequences.

**Distance Awareness**: The ability of a positional encoding to maintain meaningful distance relationships between positions. Critical for time series where temporal distance matters for classification.

**Isotropy in Embeddings**: A desirable property where vectors maintain consistent geometric relationships. Important for stable learning and meaningful distance comparisons.

**Local Pattern Capture**: The ability to identify short-term dependencies and patterns within time series data. Essential because many time series classification tasks depend on local features.

**Parameter Efficiency**: The ratio of model performance to the number of parameters. Particularly important for relative position encodings where parameters can explode with sequence length.

Quick check: For time series classification, both local patterns and global temporal relationships matter, requiring methods that can capture both effectively.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input -> Convolutional Encoder -> Transformer with tAPE/eRPE -> Classification Head

Critical path: The input time series flows through convolutional layers to capture local patterns, then positional encodings (tAPE for absolute, eRPE for relative) are added before entering the transformer layers. The transformer processes the encoded sequence, and the final classification head produces predictions.

Design tradeoffs: The authors balance between distance awareness (requiring higher embedding dimensions) and isotropy (requiring lower dimensions) in tAPE. For eRPE, they trade off some expressiveness for dramatic parameter reduction. The convolutional input encoding adds computational overhead but significantly improves local pattern capture.

Failure signatures: If positional encodings are inadequate, the model will struggle with sequences of different lengths or fail to capture temporal dependencies. Poor local pattern capture would manifest as degraded performance on datasets with clear local temporal features.

First experiments: 1) Compare tAPE against sine-cosine and learnable encodings on a representative dataset, 2) Test eRPE's parameter efficiency by measuring parameter count versus sequence length, 3) Ablation study removing convolutional input encoding to isolate its contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**: How does the proposed tAPE encoding perform on transformer architectures other than ConvTran?
The paper states "We believe our novel position encodings can boost the performance of other transformer-based TSC models." The experiments only evaluate tAPE within the ConvTran model. The paper explicitly acknowledges this limitation and suggests further research is needed. Testing tAPE on other transformer-based TSC architectures like TST, BENDER, or V2Sa would demonstrate its generalizability across different transformer designs.

**Open Question 2**: What is the optimal balance between distance awareness and isotropy in position encoding for time series with different characteristics?
The paper discusses the trade-off between distance awareness (which requires higher embedding dimensions) and isotropy (which requires lower dimensions), and proposes tAPE to balance these properties. The paper doesn't systematically explore how this balance should change based on time series characteristics like length, dimensionality, or domain-specific properties. Empirical studies varying time series characteristics and measuring classification performance with different position encoding parameter settings would identify optimal configurations for different time series types.

**Open Question 3**: How does the eRPE encoding scale to very long time series (thousands of time points) in terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency?
The paper mentions that eRPE uses a scalar representation to avoid parameter explosion, but doesn't test it on very long sequences. The experiments focus on datasets with relatively moderate time series lengths (8-17,984), but don't specifically examine the scaling behavior for extremely long sequences. Testing eRPE on datasets with time series containing thousands of points while measuring both classification accuracy and computational metrics would reveal its scalability limits and performance characteristics.

## Limitations
- The evaluation focuses primarily on classification tasks, with generalization to regression, forecasting, or anomaly detection untested
- Computational efficiency claims for eRPE are based on theoretical analysis rather than empirical wall-clock time measurements
- It's unclear whether improvements come from the novel positional encodings themselves or from the combination with convolutional layers

## Confidence
High confidence in classification task results showing consistent improvements across multiple datasets with statistical significance. Medium confidence in computational efficiency claims for eRPE due to lack of empirical validation. Low confidence in broader applicability claims to other tasks and domains without additional experiments.
