# Learning to Generalize for Cross-domain QA

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08208
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08208
- Authors:

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel zero-shot cross-domain QA task and presents a prompt-based linear probing then fine-tuning (LP-FT) method. The approach leverages prompting techniques to extract invariant features across domains and applies a linear probing followed by fine-tuning strategy to avoid overfitting and improve generalization. The method does not require additional computational cost and can be easily integrated into any pre-trained model.

Experimental results on three datasets show that the prompt-based LP-FT method significantly improves both in-domain and out-of-domain QA performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines. The method is particularly effective for generative models like BART, achieving up to 8.89% performance improvement on out-of-domain tests. The approach demonstrates superior portability and robustness across different model architectures and domains.

## Method Summary
The prompt-based LP-FT method introduces a two-stage training strategy for cross-domain QA. First, linear probing is applied to extract domain-invariant features using carefully designed prompts that encourage the model to focus on generalizable patterns. Then, fine-tuning is performed to adapt these features to the target domain. This approach avoids overfitting to specific domain characteristics while maintaining the model's ability to generalize across different QA domains. The method leverages the representational power of pre-trained models and uses prompting to guide feature extraction, making it applicable to various model architectures without requiring additional computational resources.

## Key Results
- Prompt-based LP-FT improves F1 scores by 4.5%-7.9% compared to state-of-the-art baselines on three datasets
- The method achieves up to 8.89% performance improvement on out-of-domain tests for generative models like BART
- Outperforms domain adaptation methods that require target domain annotations while maintaining portability across different model architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The prompt-based LP-FT method works by first using linear probing to identify and extract domain-invariant features through carefully designed prompts that encourage the model to focus on generalizable patterns rather than domain-specific characteristics. This initial stage prevents the model from overfitting to particular domain features. The subsequent fine-tuning phase then adapts these extracted features to the target domain, allowing the model to maintain generalization capabilities while achieving strong performance on both in-domain and out-of-domain QA tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Pre-trained Model -> Prompt-based Linear Probing -> Fine-tuning

Critical Path: The method follows a sequential two-stage process where the first stage (linear probing with prompts) must complete before the second stage (fine-tuning) begins. The prompts are designed to extract domain-invariant features, which are then refined during fine-tuning.

Design Tradeoffs: The approach trades off some potential performance gains from domain-specific fine-tuning against improved generalization and portability. The two-stage process increases training time but avoids the need for target domain annotations and additional computational costs during inference.

Failure Signatures: Poor performance may indicate ineffective prompt design that fails to extract meaningful invariant features, or insufficient fine-tuning that doesn't adequately adapt the extracted features to the target domain. Models may also fail to generalize if the linear probing stage overfits to the source domain characteristics.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focused on three datasets without extensive testing across diverse QA domains, potentially limiting generalizability claims
- Prompt design relies on domain-agnostic patterns that may not transfer to domains with fundamentally different question-answering structures
- The paper lacks detailed analysis of when and why the two-stage approach outperforms direct fine-tuning in cross-domain settings
- Performance improvements are more pronounced for generative models than discriminative models, suggesting architecture-specific limitations
