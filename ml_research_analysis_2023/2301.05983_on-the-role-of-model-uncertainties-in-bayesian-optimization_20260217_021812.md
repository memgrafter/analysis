# On the role of Model Uncertainties in Bayesian Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05983
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05983
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: Model family choice matters more than calibration for BO performance; well-calibrated uncertainties are not strictly necessary for good BO results.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the relationship between uncertainty calibration and Bayesian optimization (BO) performance. Through extensive experiments comparing Gaussian Processes, Deep Ensembles, Bayesian Neural Networks, and Random Forests on synthetic and real-world tasks, the authors find that calibration error is only weakly correlated with regret. Surprisingly, when controlling for model type, this correlation vanishes entirely. The study reveals that BO performance is robust to small calibration errors (up to ~0.08 calibration error) but degrades rapidly beyond this threshold. Theoretical analysis shows that empirical calibration curves have high variance for the small sample sizes typical in BO, suggesting that observed calibration errors may not reliably indicate BO performance. The main takeaway is that model family choice is more important than calibration for BO success.

## Method Summary
The study compares four surrogate models (GP, Deep Ensembles, BNN, RF) on synthetic Sigopt benchmark functions and MNIST hyperparameter tuning. BO experiments use Expected Improvement acquisition with 10 random initializations and 90 optimization iterations. Calibration error is measured using 1000 i.i.d. test samples with 20 percentile bins. The researchers also conduct perturbation experiments by scaling predictive uncertainties and test recalibration during BO using a leave-one-out approach. Correlation analysis examines the relationship between calibration error and regret across models and within each model family.

## Key Results
- GPs and Deep Ensembles outperform other models, achieving comparable calibration with GPs being marginally better
- Calibration error correlates weakly with regret across all models, but this correlation disappears when controlling for model type
- Recalibration during BO generally does not improve regret and sometimes harms it
- BO performance is robust to small calibration perturbations but degrades rapidly beyond ~0.08 calibration error
- Empirical calibration curves have high variance for typical BO sample sizes, limiting their reliability as performance indicators

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Gaussian Processes (GPs) and Deep Ensembles (DEs) outperform other surrogate models in Bayesian Optimization (BO) due to their superior uncertainty calibration.
- Mechanism: These models provide well-calibrated uncertainty estimates that effectively balance exploration and exploitation in BO.
- Core assumption: Well-calibrated uncertainties lead to better BO performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our results confirm that Gaussian Processes are strong surrogate models and that they tend to outperform other popular models."
  - [section]: "Table 1 shows that GPs, RFs, and DEs all achieve comparable calibration error with the GPs being marginally better."
  - [corpus]: Weak - The corpus papers focus on robust BO and calibration but don't directly compare GPs/DEs performance.
- Break condition: If the underlying function is highly noisy or discontinuous, the advantage of GPs/DEs may diminish as their assumptions break down.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The choice of model family is more important than calibration for BO performance.
- Mechanism: Different model families capture different aspects of the objective function, and this structural difference has a larger impact on BO performance than the degree of calibration within each model family.
- Core assumption: Structural model properties outweigh calibration effects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the choice of model family is more important than calibration for BO performance"
  - [section]: "when we control for the type of surrogate model, the correlation vanishes...within each model family, BO trials with lower calibration error is generally not associated with better BO performance."
  - [corpus]: Missing - No direct corpus evidence supporting this specific claim.
- Break condition: If all models are equally poor at representing the objective function, calibration might become the dominant factor.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: BO performance is robust to minor changes in calibration, but sufficiently bad calibration leads to poor performance.
- Mechanism: Small perturbations in predictive uncertainty don't significantly affect the exploration-exploitation trade-off, but extreme miscalibration causes the acquisition function to behave like random search.
- Core assumption: The EI acquisition function can tolerate moderate calibration errors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "BO performance is relatively robust to small changes in calibration"
  - [section]: "Figure 2(b) indicates that the GPs are more robust to such perturbations compared to the other methods" and "perturbing the predictive distribution by a small amount does not severely harm the BO performance"
  - [corpus]: Weak - The corpus papers discuss robust BO but don't specifically address calibration perturbations.
- Break condition: If the perturbation magnitude exceeds a threshold (around 0.08 calibration error in the experiments), performance degrades rapidly.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Surrogate model (GP, DE, BNN, or RF) -> Expected Improvement acquisition function -> Objective function evaluation -> Update surrogate model -> Repeat until convergence
- Critical path: 1) Fit surrogate model on observed data, 2) Compute acquisition function values, 3) Select next point to evaluate, 4) Update surrogate model with new observation, 5) Repeat until convergence or budget exhausted
- Design tradeoffs: Using GPs provides good uncertainty estimates but scales poorly with data size. Deep Ensembles offer better scalability but may require more computational resources for training. Random Forests are fast but may provide less informative uncertainty estimates.
- Failure signatures: If the surrogate model is severely miscalibrated (calibration error > 0.08), BO performance degrades rapidly. If the model family is poorly suited to the objective function, calibration improvements won't rescue performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the choice of acquisition function (beyond Expected Improvement) affect the relationship between calibration and regret in Bayesian optimization?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses only Expected Improvement as the acquisition function and finds that over-confident models generally perform better than under-confident ones. This suggests the choice of acquisition function might interact with calibration effects.
- Why unresolved: The study systematically varies only the surrogate model and perturbation level, not the acquisition function itself.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing calibration-regret relationships across multiple acquisition functions (Thompson sampling, Upper Confidence Bound, etc.) while holding other variables constant.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the dimensionality of the optimization problem affect the reliability of calibration curves as performance indicators in Bayesian optimization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that empirical calibration curves have high variance for small sample sizes typical in BO, and conducts experiments across dimensions 1-10, but doesn't specifically analyze how dimensionality affects calibration curve reliability.
- Why unresolved: While dimensionality effects on regret are studied, the specific impact on calibration curve variance and reliability across dimensions remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying problem dimensionality while measuring both calibration curve variance and regret performance, possibly with theoretical analysis of sample complexity requirements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Under what specific conditions does recalibration during Bayesian optimization improve or harm performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that recalibration generally does not improve regret and sometimes harms it, but doesn't identify specific conditions or problem characteristics that determine when it might be beneficial.
- Why unresolved: The experimental results show mixed effects without clear patterns, and the theoretical analysis suggests small sample sizes make recalibration unreliable, but doesn't specify when it might still be useful.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of problem characteristics (noise level, function complexity, sample budget) that correlate with when recalibration helps or hurts, combined with theoretical analysis of when recalibration can be beneficial despite small sample sizes.

## Limitations
- Findings based on synthetic functions and MNIST hyperparameter tuning may not generalize to all BO applications
- Study focuses only on Expected Improvement acquisition function, potentially missing acquisition-specific calibration effects
- Perturbation analysis uses constant scaling of uncertainties, which may not represent all real-world calibration issues

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The observation that GPs and DEs generally outperform other models in BO tasks is well-supported by experimental results across multiple dimensions and problem instances.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that model family choice is more important than calibration for BO performance is supported by correlation analysis but requires further validation across diverse real-world applications.
- **Medium Confidence**: The assertion that BO is robust to small calibration errors (up to ~0.08 calibration error) is demonstrated experimentally but may depend on specific acquisition function choices.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-validation with alternative acquisition functions:** Repeat the main experiments using Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) and Thompson Sampling acquisition functions to verify if the calibration-robustness findings generalize beyond Expected Improvement.

2. **Real-world application test:** Apply the experimental framework to a complex, real-world optimization problem (e.g., molecular design or robotics control) to assess if the calibration robustness findings hold outside synthetic benchmarks.

3. **Alternative perturbation methods:** Instead of constant scaling, implement systematic miscalibration patterns (e.g., increasing variance with prediction magnitude, heteroscedastic noise) to test BO robustness to more realistic calibration errors.
