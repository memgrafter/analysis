# The Hardness of Reasoning about Probabilities and Causality

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.09508
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09508
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Key outcome**: This paper studies the computational complexity of satisfiability problems for formal languages that can express probabilistic reasoning and do-calculus reasoning for causal effects.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the computational complexity of satisfiability problems for formal languages used in probabilistic and causal inference. The authors introduce a new complexity class called succ∃R, which is a succinct variant of the well-studied class ∃R. They demonstrate that satisfiability problems for both probabilistic reasoning and reasoning about causal interventions are complete for succ∃R, establishing even stronger algorithmic limitations than previously known results for restricted variants of these languages.

## Method Summary
The authors establish their results through polynomial-time reductions from known hard problems. For the causal intervention problem, they reduce to a new problem called Σvi-ETR. For the probabilistic reasoning problem, they reduce from a succinct version of a problem called ETR1/8, +, ×[-1/8,1/8]. These reductions involve encoding probabilities and arithmetic terms as variables and equations, showing that the satisfiability problems are at least as hard as problems known to be complete for succ∃R.

## Key Results
- Introduced succ∃R as a new complexity class that is a succinct variant of ∃R
- Showed that SATprob (satisfiability for probabilistic reasoning) is succ∃R-complete
- Showed that SATinterven (satisfiability for causal intervention reasoning) is succ∃R-complete
- Established polynomial-time reductions between these problems and known hard problems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The succ∃R complexity class is essential for capturing the exact hardness of probabilistic and causal reasoning satisfiability problems.
- **Mechanism**: The paper introduces succ∃R as a succinct variant of ∃R, which is closed under polynomial-time reductions to succETR. This succinctness allows encoding exponentially large formulas in polynomial space, making the problems complete for succ∃R.
- **Core assumption**: Succinct encodings accurately represent the exponential growth in formula size due to summation operators.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We introduce a new natural complexity class, named succ∃R, which can be viewed as a succinct variant of the well-studied class ∃R, and show that the problems we consider are complete for succ∃R."
  - [section]: "The main contribution of this paper is to show that deciding the satisfiability of both probabilistic reasoning and reasoning about causal interventions is complete for succ∃R."
- **Break condition**: If the succinct encoding fails to preserve the semantics of the original formula, the complexity classification would be incorrect.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The polynomial-time reduction from SATinterven to Σvi-ETR is the key step in proving membership in succ∃R.
- **Mechanism**: The reduction constructs a system of equations in the language Lprob that is satisfiable if and only if the original formula is satisfiable. This involves encoding probabilities and conditional probabilities using arithmetic terms and summation operators.
- **Core assumption**: The reduction preserves the semantics of the original formula and can be computed in polynomial time.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section]: "We construct a system of equations in the language Lprob and prove that there exists a model which satisfies the equations if and only if the formula (9) is satisfiable."
  - [section]: "The challenging task which remains to be solved is to express the terms qCi(j) in the language Lprob."
- **Break condition**: If the encoding of probabilities or conditional probabilities is incorrect, the reduction would not be valid.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The succ∃R-hardness of SATprob is established by reducing from succETR1/8, +, ×[-1/8,1/8].
- **Mechanism**: The reduction encodes the variables and equations of the succETR1/8, +, ×[-1/8,1/8] instance as probabilities and arithmetic terms in Lprob. The constraints ensure that the probabilities correspond to the real variables in the range [-1/8,1/8].
- **Core assumption**: The encoding of real variables as probabilities and arithmetic terms preserves the solution set.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section]: "We construct a system of equations in the language Lprob and prove that there exists a model which satisfies the equations if and only if the formula (9) is satisfiable."
  - [section]: "The challenging task which remains to be solved is to express the terms qCi(j) in the language Lprob."
- **Break condition**: If the encoding of real variables as probabilities is incorrect, the reduction would not establish hardness.
