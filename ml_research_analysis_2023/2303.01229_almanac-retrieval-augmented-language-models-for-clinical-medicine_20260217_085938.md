# Almanac: Retrieval-Augmented Language Models for Clinical Medicine

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01229
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01229
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Retrieval-augmented LLM achieves 18% mean increase in clinical factuality with citations.

## Executive Summary
Almanac is a retrieval-augmented language model framework designed to improve factual accuracy, completeness, and safety in clinical question answering. By combining a curated medical knowledge database, web search capabilities, and a fine-tuned LLM, Almanac generates responses with in-text citations. Evaluated on a novel dataset of 130 clinical scenarios, Almanac shows significant improvements in factuality compared to standard LLMs while maintaining high standards of completeness and safety.

## Method Summary
Almanac combines a curated medical knowledge database, web-based information retrieval, and a fine-tuned LLM to answer clinical questions. The framework uses a retriever to match queries with relevant documents, a browser for web search, and a fine-tuned LLM for generating responses with citations. Performance is evaluated on a novel dataset of 130 clinical scenarios across multiple specialties, using a rubric assessed by board-certified and resident physicians focusing on factuality, completeness, and safety.

## Key Results
- Almanac demonstrates a mean 18% increase in factuality compared to standard LLMs (p-value < 0.05).
- Physician evaluation confirms high standards of completeness and safety in generated responses.
- The framework shows promise as an effective tool for clinical decision-making by grounding outputs in verifiable external sources.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Retrieval-augmented LLMs reduce hallucination by grounding outputs in external knowledge sources.
- Mechanism: The system uses a retriever to fetch relevant documents from curated medical databases and the web, then a fine-tuned LLM synthesizes answers with citations. This shifts the knowledge base from the model's weights to verifiable external sources.
- Core assumption: External tools (database, browser) can reliably return relevant and accurate medical content for most clinical queries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Almanac employs a framework that combines a database of curated medical knowledge, a browser for web-based information retrieval, a retriever for matching queries with relevant documents, and a fine-tuned LLM for generating responses with in-text citations."
  - [section]: "By utilizing external tools for knowledge retrieval and calculations, we achieve significant improvements on a variety of clinically useful tasks, while mitigating the current limitations of LLMs."
- Break condition: If the retriever fails to find relevant documents or the external sources contain errors, the grounding benefit is lost and hallucinations may return.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Physician-in-the-loop evaluation ensures alignment with clinical safety and completeness standards.
- Mechanism: Outputs are scored by board-certified and resident physicians on factuality, completeness, and safety, using a standardized rubric that checks for accuracy, omissions, and harmful content.
- Core assumption: Human evaluators can reliably assess the clinical validity and safety of LLM-generated responses in real-world scenarios.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Performance on a novel dataset of clinical scenarios (n = 130) evaluated by a panel of 5 board-certified and resident physicians demonstrates significant increases in factuality..."
  - [section]: "our rubric aims to establish a standardized approach to assess outputs with the aim of addressing factuality, completeness, and safety."
- Break condition: If evaluators are inconsistent or the rubric misses edge cases, the safety and completeness guarantees weaken.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning the LLM on instruction-following improves its ability to use retrieved context effectively.
- Mechanism: The language model is fine-tuned with instructions so it can perform in-context and chain-of-thought reasoning over retrieved documents, extracting and synthesizing the most relevant information.
- Core assumption: Instruction fine-tuning transfers well to clinical reasoning tasks when combined with retrieved evidence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The language model is a generative pretrained transformer architecture fine-tuned using instructions... to formulate an answer using a combination of in-context and chain-of-thought reasoning prompts."
- Break condition: If fine-tuning does not generalize beyond the training distribution, the model may still fail to use context properly in novel clinical scenarios.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Vector-based semantic search (HNSW)
  - Why needed here: Enables fast retrieval of relevant documents by comparing embeddings rather than raw text, critical for scaling to large medical corpora.
  - Quick check question: How does cosine similarity between document and query embeddings determine relevance in the retriever?

- Concept: Instruction fine-tuning of LLMs
  - Why needed here: Allows the model to follow structured prompts and use retrieved context effectively, rather than relying solely on its internal parametric knowledge.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between zero-shot and instruction-tuned LLM performance on tasks requiring multi-step reasoning?

- Concept: Human evaluation rubrics for safety and factuality
  - Why needed here: Automated metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) cannot capture clinical accuracy, safety, or completeness; physician feedback fills this gap.
  - Quick check question: What are the three axes (factuality, completeness, safety) used to evaluate clinical LLM outputs, and why is each important?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Retriever (vector database + encoder) → Browser (web search) → LLM (fine-tuned generator) → Citation formatter
- Critical path: Query → Retriever → Context fetch → LLM synthesis → Physician evaluation. The slowest step is usually document retrieval and LLM inference.
- Design tradeoffs: Larger retriever models improve accuracy but increase latency; broader web access increases coverage but risks unreliable sources; more fine-tuning data improves reasoning but requires costly annotation.
- Failure signatures: No citations → hallucination risk; missing clinical contraindications → safety gap; long latency → usability issues in time-critical settings.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run a fixed set of clinical queries through the retriever and verify that retrieved documents are topically relevant (human or automated relevance check).
  2. Compare LLM outputs with and without retrieved context on a sample of queries to measure hallucination reduction.
  3. Conduct a small-scale physician evaluation (2-3 clinicians) on outputs to validate the rubric and detect edge cases in safety/completeness scoring.

## Limitations
- Clinical evaluation based on a small expert panel (5 physicians) and a single novel dataset (130 scenarios).
- Specific architecture details of the retriever, fine-tuned LLM, and citation formatting are not disclosed.
- No ablation study showing the individual contributions of the database, browser, and retriever components.

## Confidence

- **Factuality improvements**: Medium. The claim is supported by physician evaluation showing an 18% mean increase, but the sample size and dataset scope are limited.
- **Safety and completeness gains**: Medium. Human evaluation supports these claims, but rubric consistency and edge case coverage are not fully validated.
- **Mechanism validity (retrieval + fine-tuning)**: Low. Core assumptions about retriever reliability and fine-tuning effectiveness are not experimentally isolated or tested under failure conditions.
- **Clinical applicability**: Low. Real-world deployment factors like latency, source trustworthiness, and integration into clinical workflows are not addressed.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation study**: Remove the retriever and test LLM performance on the same dataset to quantify the isolated impact of retrieval augmentation on factuality, completeness, and safety.

2. **Evaluator agreement and rubric robustness**: Conduct inter-rater reliability analysis among the physician panel and test the rubric on edge cases or adversarial clinical queries to identify potential blind spots.

3. **External source validation**: Audit the top retrieved sources for each query to ensure they are up-to-date, authoritative, and free from bias, and test model performance when some sources are intentionally degraded.
