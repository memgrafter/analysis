# Structure Preserving Cycle-GAN for Unsupervised Medical Image Domain Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.09164
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09164
- **Reference count:** 24
- **Key outcome:** SP Cycle-GAN achieves state-of-the-art Myocardium segmentation Dice score of 0.7435 for MR to CT MM-WHS domain adaptation and excels in nearly all categories for MM-WHS dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses domain shift in medical imaging by introducing the Structure Preserving Cycle-GAN (SP Cycle-GAN), an unsupervised domain adaptation method that enforces structure preservation during image translation. The approach co-trains a U-Net segmentation model with Cycle-GAN generators using a Focal-Tversky loss, encouraging the generators to maintain relevant medical structures during domain translation. The SP Cycle-GAN demonstrates superior performance over baseline approaches and standard Cycle-GAN methods across multiple medical imaging tasks including blood vessel segmentation in retinal images and multi-class heart segmentation in multimodal cardiac images.

## Method Summary
The SP Cycle-GAN extends the standard Cycle-GAN architecture by incorporating a segmentation loss term that promotes structure preservation during image translation. During training, a U-Net segmentation model is co-trained with the Cycle-GAN generators, using Focal-Tversky loss between the predicted segmentation of the recovered source domain image and the original ground truth label. This additional loss term encourages the generators to preserve the structure of interest (e.g., blood vessels, LV, Myocardium) during the image translation process, resulting in translated images that better maintain relevant features for downstream segmentation tasks while reducing domain shift between datasets.

## Key Results
- SP Cycle-GAN achieves state-of-the-art Myocardium segmentation Dice score (DSC) of 0.7435 for MR to CT MM-WHS domain adaptation
- The method excels in nearly all categories for the MM-WHS dataset, showing clear performance increases for both CT to MR and MR to CT domain adaptation
- SP Cycle-GAN demonstrates strong blood vessel structure preservation in DRIVE to STARE domain adaptation, achieving a 4% DSC increase over default Cycle-GAN implementation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The inclusion of a segmentation loss during Cycle-GAN training enforces structure preservation during image translation.
- **Mechanism:** The SP Cycle-GAN co-trains a U-Net segmentation model with the Cycle-GAN generators, using a Focal-Tversky loss between the predicted segmentation of the recovered source domain image and the original ground truth label. This loss term encourages the generators to preserve the structure of interest during the image translation process.
- **Core assumption:** The structure of interest in the source domain image is accurately represented in the ground truth segmentation label, and the U-Net model can effectively segment this structure from the translated image.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "This work introduces the Structure Preserving Cycle-GAN (SP Cycle-GAN), which promotes medical structure preservation during image translation through the enforcement of a segmentation loss term in the overall Cycle-GAN training process."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The SP Cycle-GAN effectively reduces domain shift between medical image datasets, improving segmentation performance.
- **Mechanism:** The Cycle-GAN component of the SP Cycle-GAN translates images from the source domain to the target domain, reducing the domain shift between the datasets. This allows the segmentation model to perform better on the target domain images, as the translated images are more representative of the target domain distribution.
- **Core assumption:** The Cycle-GAN can effectively translate images between the source and target domains, reducing the domain shift between the datasets.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Addressing this problem with a pixel-level machine learning approach, such as the use of Cycle-GANs for image domain adaptation, has been shown to be an effective solution for domain shift in medical images..."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The SP Cycle-GAN outperforms the default Cycle-GAN and baseline methods for medical image segmentation tasks.
- **Mechanism:** The SP Cycle-GAN combines the domain adaptation capabilities of the Cycle-GAN with the structure preservation enforced by the segmentation loss. This results in translated images that not only reduce domain shift but also preserve the structure of interest, leading to improved segmentation performance compared to the default Cycle-GAN and baseline methods.
- **Core assumption:** The structure preservation enforced by the segmentation loss is beneficial for the segmentation task, and the SP Cycle-GAN's combination of domain adaptation and structure preservation outperforms the default Cycle-GAN and baseline methods.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "The SP Cycle-GAN is able to outperform baseline approaches and standard Cycle-GAN domain adaptation for binary blood vessel segmentation in the STARE and DRIVE datasets, and multi-class Left Ventricle and Myocardium segmentation in the multi-modal MM-WHS dataset."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Cycle-GAN generators (G and F) -> U-Net segmentation model (U) -> Focal-Tversky loss -> Cycle-GAN discriminators (DX and DY)
- **Critical path:** 1. Train SP Cycle-GAN on source domain images and labels, and target domain images (unlabeled). 2. Use trained SP Cycle-GAN to translate source domain images to target domain. 3. Train U-Net segmentation model on translated images and original labels. 4. Evaluate segmentation model on target domain test images.
- **Design tradeoffs:** SP Cycle-GAN requires labeled source domain images for structure preservation, while default Cycle-GAN does not. Structure preservation may improve segmentation performance but could potentially introduce artifacts in translated images.
- **Failure signatures:** Poor segmentation performance on target domain images, visible artifacts or distortions in translated images, inconsistent or unstable training process.
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Train SP Cycle-GAN on small subset of source and target domain images, evaluate segmentation performance on held-out validation set. 2. Compare SP Cycle-GAN performance to default Cycle-GAN and baseline methods on held-out test set. 3. Visualize translated images and segmentation results to qualitatively assess structure preservation and segmentation quality.
