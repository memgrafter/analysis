# Automating Style Analysis and Visualization With Explainable AI -- Case Studies on Brand Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03021
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03021
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: BIGNet achieves over 99.8% accuracy on synthetic SVG data for phone brand recognition and 80.6% on real car data, with superior interpretability compared to pixel-based CNNs

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces BIGNet, a two-tier Graph Neural Network architecture designed to automate brand recognition from curve-based SVG representations of product designs. The method addresses the challenge of codifying stylistic features by leveraging SVG's explicit geometric parameterization, enabling more interpretable and human-readable visualizations compared to traditional pixel-based approaches. Through case studies on phone and car design datasets, BIGNet demonstrates high accuracy while producing explainable visualizations of key brand features like lens position, grilles, and headlights.

## Method Summary
BIGNet operates as a two-tier GNN that processes curve-based SVG representations through both curve-level and chunk-level analysis. At the curve level, individual curves serve as nodes with connectivity determined by neighborhood relationships, while at the chunk level, curve groups form nodes connected by learned attention weights. The method includes two data acquisition workflows: parametric modeling from small curve-based datasets and vectorization from large pixel-based datasets. This architecture enables the extraction of explicit geometric features while maintaining interpretability through its graph-based structure.

## Key Results
- Achieved over 99.8% accuracy on synthetic SVG data for phone brand recognition
- Reached 80.6% test accuracy on real vectorized car data across six brands
- Successfully identified key brand features including lens position, height-width ratios, and luxury car elements like grilles and headlights through ablation studies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method's effectiveness stems from leveraging SVG's explicit geometric parameterization, which provides direct access to design features rather than requiring extraction from pixel data. By structuring the GNN at two levels—curves and curve groups—the model captures both local geometric relationships and higher-level design patterns. The graph-based approach allows for attention mechanisms that highlight important feature relationships while maintaining interpretability through its human-readable visualizations.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: SVG Input -> Curve-level GNN -> Chunk-level GNN -> Feature Extraction -> Classification

Critical path: SVG vectorization → Curve graph construction → Message passing through both GNN tiers → Attention-weighted aggregation → Brand classification

Design tradeoffs: Explicit geometric parameterization enables interpretability but requires vectorization; two-tier structure captures multi-scale patterns but increases computational complexity

Failure signatures: Poor vectorization quality degrades performance; insufficient curve connectivity prevents proper feature learning; attention weights become uniform across all nodes

First experiments:
1. Test curve-level GNN on synthetic SVG data with known geometric patterns
2. Validate chunk-level attention weights on simple two-feature designs
3. Compare SVG-based classification accuracy against pixel-based CNN baseline

## Limitations
- Reliance on SVG format limits applicability to datasets primarily available in pixel format
- Performance gap between synthetic (99.8%) and real data (80.6%) suggests generalization challenges
- Focus on standardized product categories raises questions about applicability to diverse design domains

## Confidence

High confidence: The technical framework of BIGNet as a two-tier GNN architecture, and its ability to process curve-based SVG representations with explicit geometric parameterization

Medium confidence: The method's superior interpretability compared to pixel-based CNNs, based on visual comparisons in the case studies

Medium confidence: The specific accuracy metrics reported (99.8% on synthetic data, 80.6% on real data), which depend on dataset characteristics and experimental conditions

Low confidence: The scalability of BIGNet to highly diverse product categories beyond phones and cars
