# Resource-Efficient Federated Hyperdimensional Computing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01339
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01339
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: RE-FHDC reduces network traffic by up to 4x while achieving comparable or higher accuracy on multiple datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RE-FHDC, a resource-efficient framework for federated hyperdimensional computing that addresses the high computational, communication, and energy costs associated with large HDC models in federated learning. The framework trains multiple smaller independent HDC sub-models and refines them using a dropout-inspired procedure, achieving comparable or superior predictive performance while significantly reducing resource consumption. The method demonstrates effectiveness across multiple datasets including MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10, and UCI HAR, with the ability to reduce network traffic by up to 4 times without sacrificing predictive performance.

## Method Summary
RE-FHDC is a two-stage federated learning framework that first partitions a large HDC model into M smaller sub-models, trains them independently, and concatenates them into a single D-dimensional model. The second stage applies a dropout-inspired refinement procedure using random subset selection to improve generalization. The method leverages random projection-based data transforms and prototype-based classification, with the independence of HD space elements ensuring that concatenated sub-models capture complementary information. The framework is compatible with arbitrary feature extractors and operates through a series of global epochs for training and refining stages.

## Key Results
- Achieves maximum accuracy of 0.972 on MNIST, 0.883 on Fashion MNIST, 0.492 on CIFAR-10, and 0.944 on UCI HAR datasets
- Reduces network traffic by up to 4 times compared to baseline federated HDC implementation
- Maintains comparable or higher predictive performance while consuming less computational and wireless resources

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training multiple smaller HDC sub-models and concatenating them achieves similar or higher performance than a single large HDC model while reducing resource usage.
- Mechanism: The random projection-based mapping ensures independence of HD space elements, allowing multiple smaller models to capture complementary information when concatenated, effectively approximating a larger model's behavior without full computational burden.
- Core assumption: The size of each HDC sub-model (D/M) is sufficiently large for accurate distance estimation.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions training multiple smaller independent HDC sub-models; [section] Proposition 3.1 states that concatenation has comparable or higher performance; [corpus] shows weak evidence with no direct comparison found.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The federated refining stage with dropout-like random subset selection improves generalization by preventing overfitting.
- Mechanism: Randomly selecting a subset of D0 prototype positions during refining increases estimation variance, leading to incorrect classifications that trigger prototype updates, acting as regularization similar to dropout in neural networks.
- Core assumption: The refining stage helps the model escape local minima and continue converging to better solutions.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions refining with dropout-inspired procedure; [section] explains how smaller D0 subsets increase variance and incorrect classification likelihood; [corpus] shows weak evidence with dropout's specific application to HDC refinement not well-documented.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: RE-FHDC reduces computational and communication costs by reducing dimensionality of models being transmitted and trained.
- Mechanism: Partitioning the large HDC model into M smaller sub-models reduces data transmitted during each federated learning round and decreases computational complexity of similarity checks and prototype updates.
- Core assumption: Reduction in dimensionality does not significantly impact model's ability to learn and generalize.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] demonstrates comparable performance with less resources; [section] describes the two-stage process with GT and GR global epochs; [corpus] shows weak evidence with resource efficiency mentioned but specific comparisons not provided.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data Preprocessing (RFFM) -> HDC Mapping (Random Projection) -> Federated Training (M sub-models) -> Model Concatenation -> Federated Refining (Dropout-like) -> Inference (Distance computation)

- Critical path: 1. Data preprocessing with RFFM, 2. Federated training of M sub-models, 3. Concatenation of sub-models, 4. Federated refining (optional but recommended), 5. Inference using concatenated model

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of sub-models (M) vs. Model performance: Increasing M reduces resource usage but may impact performance if D/M becomes too small
  - Size of refinement subset (D0) vs. Convergence: Smaller D0 increases regularization but may prevent convergence
  - Number of global epochs (GT, GR) vs. Training time: More epochs may improve performance but increase training time

- Failure signatures:
  - If D/M is too small, the model may fail to converge or have poor performance
  - If D0 is too small, the model may fail to converge during the refining stage
  - If the number of global epochs is insufficient, the model may not fully converge

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that the random projection-based mapping preserves similarity as expected by comparing distances in original and HD spaces
  2. Test federated training of a single HDC sub-model to ensure it works as expected before moving to multiple sub-models
  3. Experiment with different values of D0 during the refining stage to find optimal balance between regularization and convergence

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness is highly sensitive to the dimensionality of HDC sub-models (D/M), with insufficient dimensionality potentially causing poor performance or convergence failure
- The dropout-inspired refinement procedure requires careful tuning of D0 parameter, as values that are too small may prevent model convergence
- Limited exploration of failure modes and edge cases, particularly under extreme non-i.i.d. data distributions among federated participants

## Confidence
- High confidence: The general framework of partitioning HDC models and the resource efficiency benefits are well-supported by numerical results
- Medium confidence: The dropout-inspired refinement procedure's effectiveness is demonstrated but could benefit from more rigorous analysis of its impact on different dataset characteristics
- Low confidence: The generalization performance across diverse datasets is shown but with limited exploration of failure modes or edge cases
