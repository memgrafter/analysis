# Refining Amortized Posterior Approximations using Gradient-Based Summary Statistics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08733
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08733
- Reference count: 25
- Iterative refinement framework for amortized variational inference

## Executive Summary
This work introduces an iterative method to improve amortized variational inference for Bayesian inverse problems by addressing the amortization gap. The approach alternates between constructing training datasets using gradient-based summary statistics and training conditional generative models to refine posterior approximations. Validated on both synthetic and high-dimensional transcranial ultrasound problems, the method demonstrates improved posterior quality without requiring additional training data.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework addresses limitations in standard amortized variational inference by implementing an iterative refinement process. In each iteration, gradient-based summary statistics are computed from data residuals to construct training datasets, which are then used to train a normalizing flow model that serves as a probabilistic update to the unknown parameter. This process alternates between summary statistic computation and model training, progressively refining the posterior approximation. The method leverages the expressive power of normalizing flows while using gradients to focus the training data on regions of high posterior probability.

## Key Results
- Iterative refinement improves posterior approximations in stylized Gaussian inverse problems
- Application to transcranial ultrasound imaging increases average PSNR from 40.45dB to 43.27dB after three iterations
- Method achieves posterior improvements without requiring additional training data beyond initial dataset
- Demonstrates scalability to high-dimensional problems while maintaining computational tractability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by systematically reducing the amortization gap through targeted refinement of the variational approximation. By computing gradient-based summary statistics, the approach identifies regions where the current posterior approximation deviates most from the true posterior. The normalizing flow then learns to correct these deviations, with each iteration progressively improving the approximation. This iterative process effectively combines the efficiency of amortization with the accuracy of more expensive inference methods, while maintaining computational feasibility through the use of gradient information to focus refinement efforts.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Initial VI Approximation -> Gradient Summary Computation -> Training Dataset Construction -> Normalizing Flow Training -> Refined VI Approximation -> (loop back)

Critical Path:
The critical computational path involves computing gradients of the log-posterior with respect to parameters, constructing summary statistics from data residuals, and training the normalizing flow. Each iteration requires forward passes through the model, gradient computation, and flow training, with computational cost dominated by the gradient calculations and flow optimization.

Design Tradeoffs:
The method trades increased computational cost per iteration against improved posterior accuracy. Using normalizing flows provides flexibility but requires careful architecture selection and training. The gradient-based approach focuses refinement but may miss global structure. The iterative nature allows progressive improvement but requires multiple passes through the data.

Failure Signatures:
Performance degradation may occur when gradients are uninformative due to flat posterior regions or when the normalizing flow cannot capture complex dependencies. Convergence issues may arise from poor initialization or when the amortization gap is too large for the flow architecture to bridge effectively.
