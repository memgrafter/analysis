# The False Promise of Imitating Proprietary LLMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15717
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15717
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: Model imitation through fine-tuning on ChatGPT outputs improves style and formatting but fails to close capability gaps on tasks not well-represented in the imitation data

## Executive Summary
This paper critically evaluates the emerging approach of improving weaker language models by fine-tuning them on outputs from stronger proprietary models like ChatGPT. Through extensive experiments across various base model sizes (1.5B-13B parameters) and different imitation datasets, the authors demonstrate that while imitation models can successfully mimic ChatGPT's style and instruction-following ability, they fail to match ChatGPT's underlying capabilities, particularly in factuality and reasoning. The study reveals that imitation learning is most effective for tasks heavily represented in the training data but cannot overcome fundamental limitations of smaller base models, concluding that true capability improvements require better base models through increased scale or architectural innovations rather than imitation alone.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes base language models (GPT-2 1.5B, LLaMA 7B, LLaMA 13B) on imitation datasets collected from ShareGPT, HC3, Discord ChatGPT Bots, and synthetic Natural Questions queries. Models are trained using standard supervised learning with AdamW optimizer (learning rate 2e-3, 1000 warm-up steps, batch size 32) for one epoch. The imitation models are evaluated using both human assessments (crowdworkers and GPT-4) and automatic metrics across multiple benchmarks including MMLU, Natural Questions, and HumanEval. The paper systematically varies base model size and imitation data scale to understand their relative impacts on model performance.

## Key Results
- Imitation models achieve high human ratings but perform poorly on automatic factuality benchmarks
- Model performance improves significantly on tasks heavily represented in imitation data but regresses on underrepresented tasks
- Increasing base model size yields larger performance gains than adding more imitation data
- Style imitation successfully transfers but underlying capabilities remain limited by base model quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Imitation models improve by copying style and formatting from ChatGPT rather than underlying capabilities
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning on ChatGPT outputs teaches the model to reproduce similar text patterns, sentence structures, and formatting choices without transferring the reasoning capabilities
- Core assumption: The base model has sufficient knowledge capacity but lacks the ability to format responses appropriately
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "imitation models are adept at mimicking ChatGPT's style but not its factuality"
  - [section 4.4] "as we add more imitation data, the style of our models' outputs are increasingly similar to those of ChatGPT"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence about mechanism
- Break condition: When base model lacks sufficient knowledge capacity, style copying cannot compensate for missing capabilities

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Imitation data provides task-specific training signals that improve performance on heavily represented tasks
- Mechanism: When imitation dataset contains many examples of specific task types, the model learns to perform those tasks better through supervised learning
- Core assumption: Task performance can be improved through supervised learning on task-specific examples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "training exclusively on ChatGPT responses for Natural-Questions-like queries drastically improves task accuracy"
  - [abstract] "imitation models improve on evaluation tasks that are heavily supported in the imitation training data"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence about task-specific improvements
- Break condition: When imitation data lacks sufficient coverage of target tasks

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Base model quality determines the upper bound of imitation model performance
- Mechanism: Larger base models can capture more knowledge and reasoning capabilities, which are then refined through imitation fine-tuning
- Core assumption: Base model quality is the primary determinant of final model performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "as we scale up the base LM steadily improves results"
  - [abstract] "there exists a substantial capabilities gap between open-source LMs and their closed-source counterparts that cannot be closed by cheaply fine-tuning on imitation data"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence about base model scaling
- Break condition: When base model is too small to capture necessary knowledge regardless of imitation data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How much imitation data would be required to close the capability gap between open-source and proprietary models on tasks not heavily represented in the imitation data?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from the conclusion that "broadly matching ChatGPT using weaker base LMs such as LLaMA-13B would require a concerted effort to collect an extremely large and diverse imitation dataset that is far closer to the scale of pretraining."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a specific estimate of the amount of imitation data needed, only stating that it would be "unwieldy" and "far closer to the scale of pretraining."
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the amount of imitation data on a wide range of tasks not represented in the imitation data, showing the relationship between data size and task performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can active learning methods improve the efficiency and effectiveness of model imitation compared to the simple data collection methods used in this study?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from the discussion of "more advanced methods (e.g., active learning) that may improve the effectiveness or efficiency of model imitation."
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers simple methods for collecting imitation data and does not explore more advanced techniques like active learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of models trained on imitation data collected using active learning versus simple random sampling methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the failure modes of imitation models differ across various task categories, and what are the underlying reasons for these differences?
- Basis in paper: Inferred from the observation that "imitation models do not improve (or even decline) in accuracy on evaluation datasets for which there is little support" and the specific example of Natural Questions.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a few examples of tasks where imitation models fail and does not offer a comprehensive analysis of failure modes across different task categories.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic analysis of imitation model performance across a diverse set of tasks, identifying common failure modes and potential reasons for these failures.

## Limitations

- The study relies on relatively small base models (1.5B-13B parameters) compared to modern large language models, potentially limiting generalizability
- The quality and representativeness of the collected imitation data may not fully capture ChatGPT's capabilities, particularly for complex reasoning tasks
- Human evaluation methods may introduce biases in assessing model capabilities, as evidenced by high human ratings despite poor factuality performance

## Confidence

- High confidence: The finding that imitation models improve on tasks heavily represented in the imitation data
- Medium confidence: The conclusion that imitation learning cannot close the full capability gap between open-source and proprietary models
- Low confidence: The specific claims about the upper bounds of imitation learning effectiveness given the limited scale of experiments

## Next Checks

1. **Scale Testing**: Replicate the experiments using larger base models (e.g., 30B-70B parameters) to determine if the imitation learning limits observed in this study hold at larger scales.

2. **Active Learning Validation**: Implement active learning techniques for collecting imitation data to test whether more sophisticated data collection methods could improve the effectiveness of model imitation.

3. **Long-form Capability Assessment**: Design experiments specifically targeting long-form reasoning and complex problem-solving capabilities to better understand whether imitation learning can transfer these more sophisticated abilities.
