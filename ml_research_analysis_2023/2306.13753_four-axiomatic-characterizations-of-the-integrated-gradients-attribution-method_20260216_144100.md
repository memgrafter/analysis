# Four Axiomatic Characterizations of the Integrated Gradients Attribution Method

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13753

## Executive Summary
This paper presents four distinct axiomatic characterizations of the Integrated Gradients (IG) attribution method, establishing its uniqueness among different classes of attribution methods. The authors introduce new axioms including non-decreasing positivity, affine scale invariance, and symmetric monotonicity, and prove that IG is the only method satisfying these properties in various contexts. The theoretical framework rigorously demonstrates IG's uniqueness through multiple mathematical proofs under different combinations of axioms.

## Method Summary
The paper develops a theoretical framework for axiomatic characterization of attribution methods, introducing new axioms and proving uniqueness results for Integrated Gradients. The authors analyze path methods, establish relationships between different axiom sets, and demonstrate that IG attributions to ReLU and max functions coincide with IG attributions to softplus approximations, providing a form of continuity. The proofs rely on mathematical analysis of path integrals along monotonic paths between baseline and input instances.

## Key Results
- Proves IG is the unique monotone path method satisfying symmetry-preserving and affine scale invariance
- Shows IG is the unique method satisfying linearity, affine scale invariance, completeness, non-decreasing positivity, and proportionality
- Demonstrates IG is the unique method satisfying linearity, completeness, dummy, and symmetric-monotonicity
- Establishes that IG attributions to neural networks with ReLU and max functions coincide with IG attributions to softplus approximations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper works by establishing rigorous mathematical frameworks for characterizing attribution methods through axiomatic properties. By defining new axioms and proving uniqueness results, the authors demonstrate that Integrated Gradients occupies a unique position among attribution methods that satisfy certain combinations of properties. The mechanism relies on analyzing path integrals along monotonic paths and showing that IG satisfies specific mathematical constraints that no other method can satisfy simultaneously.

## Foundational Learning
- Path methods in attribution: These methods compute attributions by integrating gradients along paths from baseline to input. Needed to understand the mathematical foundation of IG and related methods. Quick check: Verify that path methods satisfy completeness and linearity axioms.
- Monotonicity in attribution: Refers to the property that attributions maintain consistent signs along monotonic paths. Needed to establish uniqueness results for path methods. Quick check: Confirm monotonicity holds for ReLU networks under IG.
- Symmetry-preserving properties: These ensure that symmetric inputs receive symmetric attributions. Needed to prove uniqueness among methods satisfying both symmetry and monotonicity. Quick check: Test symmetry preservation on mirrored input pairs.
- Affine scale invariance: The property that attributions scale appropriately under affine transformations of inputs. Needed to establish uniqueness in methods satisfying scaling properties. Quick check: Verify attributions remain consistent under input scaling.

## Limitations
- Theoretical results assume smoothness conditions that may not hold for practical ReLU networks
- Practical implications of new axioms (non-decreasing positivity, symmetric monotonicity) for real-world interpretability are not demonstrated
- Claim that IG attributions to ReLU networks are equivalent to softplus approximations requires extensive empirical validation across different architectures

## Confidence
- Mathematical rigor: High
- Practical relevance of new axioms: Medium
- Softplus approximation claim: Low
