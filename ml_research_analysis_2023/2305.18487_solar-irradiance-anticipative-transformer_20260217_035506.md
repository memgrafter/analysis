# Solar Irradiance Anticipative Transformer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18487
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18487
- Reference count: 40
- Introduces SIAT, a transformer-based model achieving 21.45% forecasting skill for 15-minute ahead solar irradiance prediction using sky images.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SIAT, a transformer-based model for short-term solar irradiance forecasting using sequences of sky images. The model employs a Vision Transformer (ViT) as a backbone to encode features from each image, followed by a GPT-2 based decoder to predict future irradiance values. SIAT is trained using a three-stage procedure, incorporating multiple loss components for strong supervision. The model achieves a forecasting skill of 21.45% on a 15-minute ahead prediction for a newly introduced dataset of all-sky images, outperforming a smart persistence model. Additionally, SIAT demonstrates superior performance compared to competing models on three datasets, highlighting its effectiveness in capturing long-range dependencies between sky images for accurate irradiance forecasting.

## Method Summary
The SIAT model processes sequences of 224x224 sky images through a Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone to extract spatial features, which are then fed into a GPT-2 decoder to capture temporal dependencies. The model is trained in three stages: first training the ViT to map single images to irradiance, then freezing the backbone and training the decoder on encoded features, and finally fine-tuning both components jointly. The training uses a weighted sum of irradiance prediction loss and encoding prediction loss to ensure strong supervision.

## Key Results
- Achieves 21.45% forecasting skill for 15-minute ahead prediction on the Chilbolton dataset
- Outperforms smart persistence baseline on newly introduced all-sky image dataset
- Demonstrates superior performance compared to competing models on three different datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Vision Transformer backbone learns to extract sky-relevant features that correlate with future irradiance by attending to the sun and nearby cloud formations.
- Mechanism: The ViT backbone processes sky images in patches, linearly projects them, and uses self-attention to weight regions based on their relevance for irradiance prediction. The class token output from the backbone serves as a compact feature representation of the image.
- Core assumption: The backbone, pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned on the solar task, learns to attend to the sun and cloud formations because these regions are most predictive of irradiance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our proposed vision transformer encodes features of consecutive images"
  - [section] "The backbone's attention predominantly falls on the sun as well as cloud formations near it"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence on solar-specific attention maps, but general ViT attention mechanisms are well established.
- Break condition: If the dataset contains too much noise or occlusion (e.g., fixed objects blocking the sun), the backbone may attend to irrelevant regions and fail to learn useful features.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The GPT-2 decoder captures long-range temporal dependencies between consecutive sky images to forecast future irradiance.
- Mechanism: The decoder uses masked multi-head attention to process the sequence of encoded image features. This allows it to model temporal changes in the sky (e.g., cloud movement, sun position) without leakage from future timesteps.
- Core assumption: The temporal evolution of sky images is sufficiently predictable to infer future irradiance, and the decoder can learn this mapping from image features alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the proposed anticipative transformer captures long-range dependencies between sky images"
  - [section] "The masked attention of the GPT-2 decoder ensures that the model can only attend to past features to make the prediction"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence on GPT-2 for irradiance, but transformer decoders are proven for sequence modeling.
- Break condition: If irradiance changes are driven by factors not visible in sky images (e.g., sudden weather events), the decoder's predictions will degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The three-stage training procedure ensures strong supervision by first learning image-to-irradiance mapping, then future feature prediction, and finally joint fine-tuning.
- Mechanism: Stage 1 trains the ViT backbone to map a single image to irradiance. Stage 2 freezes the backbone and trains the decoder to predict future image features and irradiance. Stage 3 unfreezes the backbone for joint fine-tuning using both encoding and irradiance losses.
- Core assumption: Separating the training into stages prevents catastrophic forgetting and allows the model to first learn robust image features before learning temporal prediction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We train the model in three stages... the backbone is trained separately... the backbone staying frozen for 10 epochs"
  - [section] "Including the encoding loss component in the supervision of the model brings the largest improvement in FS"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence on this specific three-stage scheme, but staged training is common in transfer learning.
- Break condition: If the intermediate feature predictions are too noisy or the losses are poorly weighted, the staged training may not converge or may overfit.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input: Sequence of 224x224 sky images (5 timesteps) -> ViT backbone (12 layers, 12 heads, 768 dim) -> image features (z) -> GPT-2 decoder (4 layers, 8 heads, 512 dim) -> future features (ˆz) -> Linear head -> irradiance predictions (ˆy)
- Critical path: Image -> ViT -> GPT-2 -> Linear -> Irradiance
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using only images (no auxiliary data) reduces deployment cost but may limit accuracy in complex weather.
  - Three-stage training adds complexity but improves feature learning and forecasting skill.
  - GPT-2 decoder vs LSTM: better long-range modeling but higher computational cost.
- Failure signatures:
  - High RMSE but low MAE: model makes occasional large errors but is generally accurate.
  - Negative forecasting skill: model underperforms the smart persistence baseline, indicating poor temporal modeling.
  - Over-smoothing predictions: model fails to capture sharp irradiance changes, likely due to insufficient attention to transient features.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train only the ViT backbone (single image -> irradiance) and evaluate MAE/RMSE on test set.
  2. Replace GPT-2 decoder with a simple LSTM and compare forecasting skill on 15-minute ahead task.
  3. Remove Lenc from the loss and measure impact on FS and MAE.

## Limitations

- Limited ablation studies on the three-stage training procedure and loss function components
- Reliance on sky images alone may limit accuracy in complex weather scenarios
- Performance improvements over competing models are reported but not fully isolated from other factors

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The model architecture (ViT + GPT-2 decoder) is technically sound and the three-stage training procedure is clearly described. The improvements over the smart persistence baseline on the newly introduced dataset are well-documented.
- **Medium confidence**: The performance improvements over competing models are reported, but the comparison is limited by the availability of results from other methods on the same datasets. The effectiveness of the ViT backbone in extracting sky-relevant features is plausible but not directly verified through attention map visualizations.
- **Low confidence**: The specific contributions of the encoding loss component (Lenc) to overall performance are not fully isolated, and the sensitivity of the model to hyperparameters like loss weights (α, β, γ) is not explored.
