# Multi-hop Commonsense Knowledge Injection Framework for Zero-Shot Commonsense Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.05936
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05936
- **Reference count**: 23
- **Key outcome**: Proposed framework improves zero-shot commonsense QA accuracy by 2.8-5.3% over previous methods using multi-hop knowledge injection

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for zero-shot commonsense question answering that addresses the limitation of previous approaches by incorporating multi-hop knowledge from commonsense knowledge graphs. The framework generates synthetic multi-hop QA datasets using compositive and conjunctive linguistic patterns, then pre-trains a masked language model with contrastive learning using InfoNCE loss. Experiments on five benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, with ablation studies confirming the effectiveness of hard negative sampling and the multi-hop approach.

## Method Summary
The framework synthesizes a multi-hop QA dataset from ConceptNet using two linguistically logical generation methods: compositive (capturing continuous reasoning) and conjunctive (capturing multi-faceted reasoning). It then pre-trains a RoBERTa model with contrastive learning using InfoNCE loss, where the model learns to assign lower MLM loss scores to correct answers compared to semantically related but incorrect distractors. The hard negative sampling strategy selects distractors that are semantically close but violate the multi-hop path to the correct answer, forcing the model to learn fine-grained distinctions in reasoning patterns.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on five commonsense QA benchmarks (CSQA, MC-TACO, a-NLI, PIQA, Wino-Grande)
- Improves accuracy by 2.8-5.3% over previous methods that use only single-hop knowledge triples
- Ablation studies show hard negative sampling contributes 1.3% performance improvement
- Temperature parameter τ between 0.6-0.8 yields optimal results for most datasets
- Compositive and conjunctive methods both contribute significantly to performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-hop QA pairs capture richer reasoning patterns than single-hop triples
- Mechanism: The framework generates questions from two-hop paths in ConceptNet using compositive and conjunctive linguistic patterns that reflect human logical thinking
- Core assumption: Multi-hop reasoning patterns in KGs align with human linguistic logic
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] describes the two generation methods; [section 3.1] details the linguistic logic criteria; corpus shows weak evidence of multi-hop reasoning focus

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Hard negative sampling teaches fine-grained distinctions in multi-hop reasoning
- Mechanism: Distractors are selected from KG under constraints ensuring relevance while violating the correct multi-hop path
- Core assumption: Semantically close but incorrect negatives are more effective than random negatives
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.1] specifies negative selection constraints; [section 4.7] shows 1.3% performance drop when removed

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: InfoNCE contrastive learning optimizes MLM loss as answer plausibility proxy
- Mechanism: QA pairs are scored by MLM losses, with InfoNCE loss minimizing positive scores and maximizing negative scores
- Core assumption: MLM loss correlates with answer plausibility
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.2] describes InfoNCE implementation; [section 4.7] shows slight performance drop with margin loss

  - Quick check question: Given entities A, B, C with relations A→B and B→C, what is the multi-hop path from A to C?

  - Why needed here: Pre-training uses InfoNCE to distinguish correct answers from confusing distractors
  - Quick check question: In InfoNCE, what is the role of the temperature τ in the softmax denominator?

- Concept: Masked Language Model scoring for QA
  - Why needed here: Framework uses MLM loss on masked tokens as proxy for answer plausibility
  - Quick check question: If a token is masked in a QA sequence, how does the model compute its probability during pre-training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: KG loader -> multi-hop path extractor -> QA pair generator (compositive & conjunctive) -> synthetic dataset -> RoBERTa MLM pre-trainer -> contrastive InfoNCE optimizer -> zero-shot evaluator
- Critical path: Synthetic QA generation → pre-training with contrastive loss → evaluation; each stage must complete before the next
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-hop vs multi-hop: Multi-hop adds complexity but richer reasoning; single-hop is simpler but less powerful
  - Number of distractors: More negatives improve robustness but increase computational cost
  - KG choice: ConceptNet has explicit multi-hop relations; others may require implicit path inference
- Failure signatures:
  - Low variance in QA scores → distractors too easy or dataset too small
  - Degraded performance vs single-hop baseline → multi-hop generation not effective or contrastive loss mis-tuned
  - Training instability → temperature τ too low/high or batch size too small
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate synthetic dataset with only compositive method, train with InfoNCE, evaluate on CSQA
  2. Replace InfoNCE with margin loss, keep same dataset, compare performance drop
  3. Remove hard negative sampling, replace with random negatives, evaluate impact on accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal number of multi-hop relationships to consider for effective commonsense reasoning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that their synthetic multi-hop QA generation method applies entities and their relationships within two hops of an entity node as the center, but does not consider the rest of the nodes beyond two hops.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the impact of using more or fewer hops in the knowledge graph on the model's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of the model using different numbers of hops (e.g., one, two, three, etc.) in the knowledge graph.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can implicit multi-hop relationships in knowledge graphs like ATOMIC be leveraged for commonsense reasoning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that they utilize ConceptNet for generating multi-hop QA data due to its explicit multi-hop relationships, but other KGs like ATOMIC do not have explicit multi-hop relationships.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore methods to extract or infer implicit multi-hop relationships from knowledge graphs like ATOMIC.
- What evidence would resolve it: A method or approach that can successfully extract or infer implicit multi-hop relationships from knowledge graphs like ATOMIC and demonstrate improved performance on commonsense reasoning tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of the temperature parameter τ in InfoNCE loss on the model's performance across different commonsense reasoning tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that they conduct experiments with different temperatures τ and observe that the temperature corresponding to the best results of most datasets is between 0.6 and 0.8, except for the WG dataset.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a clear explanation for the optimal temperature range or why the WG dataset behaves differently.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the relationship between the temperature parameter τ and the model's performance on different commonsense reasoning tasks, along with an explanation for the observed patterns.

## Limitations
- Limited to single-relation triples in KGs, potentially missing richer multi-hop patterns involving multiple relations
- Performance gains measured against specific baselines and may not generalize to other zero-shot QA approaches
- Requires pre-training on synthetic data, adding computational overhead compared to direct fine-tuning approaches

## Confidence

**High Confidence (4/5):**
- Synthetic dataset generation methodology using compositive and conjunctive methods is well-defined and reproducible
- InfoNCE loss implementation and its role in contrastive learning is clearly specified
- Hard negative sampling strategy is precisely described with explicit constraints
- Evaluation methodology on five benchmarks is standard and transparent

**Medium Confidence (3/5):**
- Magnitude of performance improvements (2.8-5.3%) is supported by ablation studies but depends on synthetic data quality
- Assumption that MLM loss is valid proxy for answer plausibility is reasonable but not extensively validated
- Claim that multi-hop reasoning is necessary for commonsense QA is supported but not conclusively proven

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset Quality Audit**: Generate a sample of 100 synthetic multi-hop QA pairs using the described methods and evaluate them for linguistic naturalness, logical consistency, and diversity of reasoning patterns. Check whether the compositive and conjunctive methods produce genuinely different types of reasoning.

2. **Alternative Negative Sampling**: Replace the hard negative sampling strategy with random negative sampling and conduct controlled experiments to quantify the exact performance drop. This would validate whether the semantic relatedness of negatives is critical to the framework's success.

3. **Single-Hop vs Multi-Hop Comparison**: Train an identical framework using only single-hop knowledge triples (the original dataset) and compare performance against the multi-hop approach on all five benchmarks. This would isolate the contribution of multi-hop reasoning versus other factors like contrastive learning or dataset size.
