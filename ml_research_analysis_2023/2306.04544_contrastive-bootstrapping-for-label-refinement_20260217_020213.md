# Contrastive Bootstrapping for Label Refinement

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.04544
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04544
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Improves coarse-to-fine text classification by 5.67% Micro-F1 and 5.54% Macro-F1 on NYT dataset, and 3.97% Micro-F1 and 3.04% Macro-F1 on 20News dataset.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses coarse-to-fine text classification where fine-grained labels are unknown but coarse labels and a coarse-to-fine mapping are provided. The authors propose a contrastive clustering-based bootstrapping method that iteratively refines labels by pulling away negative passage-prototype pairs under global and local guidance. The method first uses coarse labels to cluster passages, then refines labels by pulling apart both coarse clusters globally and fine clusters locally within each coarse group. Experiments on NYT and 20News datasets show significant improvements over state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
The method consists of a warm-up phase followed by a bootstrapping phase. In the warm-up phase, two contrastive losses are optimized: Lglobal pulls apart coarse clusters and Llocal pulls apart fine clusters within each coarse group. The bootstrapping phase alternates between fine-tuning the model on a selected dataset and updating the selected dataset based on high-confidence predictions. A modified similarity metric with K-nearest-neighbor normalization prevents hub vectors from dominating similarity scores. Gloss knowledge from Wikipedia is optionally used to augment prototype representations.

## Key Results
- Achieves 5.67% improvement in Micro-F1 and 5.54% in Macro-F1 on NYT dataset
- Achieves 3.97% improvement in Micro-F1 and 3.04% in Macro-F1 on 20News dataset
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods including LOT-Class, X-Class, and C2F
- Ablation studies confirm effectiveness of contrastive losses, bootstrapping, and modified similarity metric

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Contrastive clustering with fine-grained label refinement improves separation between fine-grained categories more than coarse-grained clustering alone. The method first uses coarse labels to coarsely cluster passages, then refines labels by pulling away negative passage-prototype pairs under both global (coarse inter-cluster) and local (fine inter-cluster) contrastive losses. This iterative refinement sharpens boundaries between fine-grained categories.

### Mechanism 2
Bootstrapping with selective high-confidence pseudo-labels reduces noise compared to training on all weakly-labeled data. After initial warm-up clustering, the model selects a subset of passages with high-confidence label predictions and fine-tunes only on these high-quality pairs, updating the selection iteratively. This avoids propagating noise from uncertain pseudo-labels.

### Mechanism 3
Modified similarity metric with hubness reduction improves fine-grained discrimination compared to standard cosine similarity. The method replaces cosine similarity with a metric that subtracts the average similarity to the K nearest neighbors, preventing "hub" vectors from dominating similarity scores and making similarity more discriminative for fine-grained label selection.

## Foundational Learning

- **Hierarchical classification and coarse-to-fine mapping**: The method relies on a known coarse-to-fine mapping to guide both global and local contrastive learning. *Quick check*: Given a coarse label "Sports" and fine labels "Baseball", "Basketball", "Football", how would you construct the candidate fine label set for a passage labeled "Sports"?

- **Contrastive learning and margin ranking loss**: The method uses contrastive losses to pull positive pairs closer and push negative pairs apart in embedding space, both globally (coarse clusters) and locally (fine clusters within coarse groups). *Quick check*: In a margin ranking loss max(sim_pos - sim_neg + margin, 0), what happens to the loss if sim_pos is already greater than sim_neg by more than the margin?

- **Hubness in high-dimensional spaces and similarity metric design**: The modified similarity metric addresses hubness by normalizing similarity scores, which is critical for accurate fine-grained label assignment when many prototypes exist. *Quick check*: Why might cosine similarity alone be problematic when assigning one label from a large set of candidates?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Passage encoder (RoBERTa-base) -> Prototype encoder (same as passage encoder) -> Similarity computation (modified metric with KN N) -> Global contrastive loss (coarse cluster separation) -> Local contrastive loss (fine cluster separation) -> Bootstrapping loop (selection → fine-tune → update) -> Gloss knowledge augmentation (optional)

- **Critical path**: 1. Encode passages and prototypes 2. Compute modified similarity scores 3. Apply global and local contrastive losses 4. Generate high-confidence pseudo-labels 5. Fine-tune on selected data 6. Repeat steps 1-5 for bootstrapping

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using RoBERTa-base vs. larger models (performance vs. computational cost), number of bootstrapping epochs (more epochs may overfit), selection ratio r% (higher ratio includes more samples but more noise), K in KN N (affects hubness correction strength)

- **Failure signatures**: Performance degrades if coarse-to-fine mapping is incorrect, no improvement with bootstrapping suggests selection strategy is too strict or too lenient, high confusion between certain fine labels suggests local contrastive loss is insufficient

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Run with only global contrastive loss (no local, no bootstrapping) to verify coarse clustering works 2. Run with both global and local losses but no bootstrapping to verify fine clustering works within coarse groups 3. Run full method with bootstrapping but without selection strategy to quantify noise reduction benefit

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How does the proposed method perform when extended to multi-level hierarchical classification beyond two levels? The paper only evaluates on two-level hierarchical datasets and states it's worth extending to deeper hierarchies.

- What is the impact of class imbalance in the selected high-quality samples during bootstrapping, and how can it be mitigated? The paper uses a fixed percentage selection strategy without addressing potential class imbalance issues.

- How does the performance change when using different knowledge sources for gloss augmentation beyond Wikipedia? The paper only evaluates one specific approach to knowledge augmentation.

## Limitations

- The method's performance heavily depends on the quality of the coarse-to-fine mapping, which could propagate errors if incorrect.
- The bootstrapping selection strategy assumes high-confidence predictions are correct, but this may not hold in early iterations when the model is still learning.
- The modified similarity metric introduces hyperparameter K that needs tuning, and the paper doesn't thoroughly explore sensitivity to this parameter.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core contrastive clustering mechanism works as described - global loss separates coarse clusters and local loss separates fine clusters within coarse groups.
- **Medium confidence**: The bootstrapping with selective fine-tuning improves performance over training on all pseudo-labels, though the exact mechanism of how the selection threshold is updated could affect results.
- **Medium confidence**: The modified similarity metric with KN N improves fine-grained discrimination, though the paper doesn't thoroughly explore parameter sensitivity.

## Next Checks

1. **Mapping quality analysis**: Create corrupted coarse-to-fine mappings with varying error rates and measure performance degradation to understand robustness to mapping errors.

2. **Selection strategy ablation**: Run experiments with different selection ratios (r%) and fixed vs. adaptive thresholds to isolate the impact of the bootstrapping strategy.

3. **Hubness sensitivity**: Test the method with different K values in the KN N similarity metric (e.g., K=1, 3, 10) and compare with alternative hubness reduction methods to verify the choice of K=5 is optimal.
