# Filter-Aware Model-Predictive Control

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10246
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10246
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Filter-aware MPC improves state estimation accuracy and task success rates by constraining plans to maintain trackability

## Executive Summary
Filter-aware model-predictive control (MPC) addresses the challenge of planning under partial observability by predicting future state estimation accuracy. The method learns a "trackability" network that estimates the expected error of a state estimator for different possible plans. This trackability prediction is then used as a constraint in the MPC optimization to penalize plans that would lead to poor state estimation. By maintaining a desired level of estimation accuracy, filter-aware MPC can outperform both regular MPC and belief-space planning methods in terms of task success and computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The method learns a neural network to predict the expected error of a state estimator (trackability) by training on rollouts of an MPC policy with state estimation. During planning, this trackability network is used to constrain the MPC optimization, forcing plans to maintain a desired level of state estimation accuracy. The approach approximates the expected future tracking error using only the current state and optimal carry, avoiding the computational expense of full belief-space planning. The trackability constraint is implemented as a penalty in the MPC cost function, with a threshold parameter controlling the strictness of the constraint.

## Key Results
- Success rate improved from 39% to 93% in a toy scenario with a "dark zone"
- Success rate increased from 48% to 64% in a visual navigation task in ViZDoom
- Filter-aware MPC outperformed both regular MPC and RNN baselines across all tested environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Filter-aware MPC improves state estimation accuracy by penalizing plans that lead to poor observability in future states.
- Mechanism: The trackability network predicts the expected error of the state estimator under a given plan. This prediction is used as a constraint in the MPC optimization, forcing the planner to avoid trajectories where the state estimator is likely to fail.
- Core assumption: The trackability network accurately approximates the expected future tracking error of the state estimator.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our approach, filter-aware MPC, penalises the loss of information by what we call 'trackability', the expected error of the state estimator."
  - [section 4.1]: "Given D, we can train the neural network by minimising: min φ N∑ i (φ(zi 1) − J err(zi 1; λ, φ))2"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no direct citations to support the specific claim about penalizing observability loss.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Filter-aware MPC maintains a desired level of state estimation accuracy without the computational expense of full belief-space planning.
- Mechanism: By only reasoning about future accuracy through the trackability function, the method avoids the need to predict future beliefs directly, which is computationally expensive.
- Core assumption: The approximation J err(zt, ht) ≈ J err(zt, h* zt) is valid for the problems considered.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We show that model-based simulation allows condensing trackability into a neural network, which allows fast planning."
  - [section 4]: "We make an approximation to arrive at a feasible algorithm. We approximate J err(zt, ht) ≈ J err(zt, h* zt)."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no direct citations to support the computational efficiency claim.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Filter-aware MPC can handle high-dimensional observations like images by operating in state space rather than belief space.
- Mechanism: The trackability network is learned in state space, and the rest of the computations (planning, constraints) are also done in state space, avoiding the need to represent or reason about beliefs over high-dimensional observations.
- Core assumption: The state estimator can provide a reasonable state estimate from high-dimensional observations, and the trackability network can learn the relationship between state and expected error.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "In experiments involving visual navigation, realistic every-day environments and a two-link robot arm, we show that filter-aware MPC vastly improves regular MPC."
  - [section 5.2, 5.3]: Descriptions of experiments with visual navigation in ViZDoom and AI2-THOR using RGB-D images.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no direct citations to support the claim about handling high-dimensional observations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  State Estimator -> Trackability Network -> MPC Optimizer -> Dynamics Model -> Emission Model

- Critical path:
  1. Observe the current state using the state estimator.
  2. Use the trackability network to predict the expected error of the state estimator under different plans.
  3. Use the MPC optimizer to find the optimal plan that minimizes cost while respecting the trackability constraint.
  4. Execute the first control of the optimal plan.
  5. Repeat from step 1.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. computational cost: More accurate state estimation and trackability prediction can improve performance but increase computational cost.
  - Constraint strictness vs. performance: Stricter trackability constraints can improve state estimation accuracy but may lead to suboptimal plans.
  - Model accuracy vs. generalization: More accurate dynamics and emission models can improve performance but may reduce the method's ability to generalize to new environments.

- Failure signatures:
  - The trackability network fails to generalize, leading to incorrect constraints and suboptimal plans.
  - The state estimator fails to provide a reasonable state estimate, leading to poor performance.
  - The MPC optimizer fails to find a feasible plan that satisfies the trackability constraint.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Toy scenario: A 2D particle navigating to a goal while avoiding a "dark zone" with poor observability.
  2. ViZDoom navigation: An agent navigating between two rooms using visual observations, with one corridor having corrupted observations.
  3. AI2-THOR orbiting: A robot following a fixed trajectory in a realistic environment with a reflective surface that can throw off the visual tracker.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of filter-aware MPC scale with increasing state dimensionality and complexity?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that belief planning becomes intractable in high-dimensional settings and that filter-aware MPC can work with any black-box state estimator, suggesting potential scalability advantages.
- Why unresolved: The experiments were limited to relatively low-dimensional problems. Scaling to truly high-dimensional problems with image observations would require further validation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results on benchmark POMDP problems with significantly higher dimensional state and observation spaces, comparing filter-aware MPC against belief planning baselines and other state-of-the-art methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is filter-aware MPC to the choice of the trackability threshold parameter δ?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that thresholds were chosen by hand in each experiment, relying on visualizations, and that in higher-dimensional settings they would need to be picked by hyperparameter search.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic study of the sensitivity to δ or guidance on how to choose it in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation study varying δ across a range of values and measuring the impact on performance and constraint satisfaction rates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the trackability network be learned more efficiently, e.g. by leveraging model-based rollouts or active data collection?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses a passive data collection approach, collecting rollouts of the MPC policy. It is not clear if this is optimal or if more efficient strategies exist.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative data collection strategies or ways to leverage model knowledge for more efficient learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparison of different data collection strategies (e.g. active learning, model-based rollouts) in terms of sample efficiency and final performance.

## Limitations

- The trackability network requires access to ground truth dynamics and emission models, which are typically unavailable in real-world applications.
- The approximation made to avoid full belief-space planning may not hold in scenarios where the carry significantly influences future estimation accuracy.
- The computational efficiency gains come at the cost of potentially suboptimal constraint satisfaction, as the method only reasons about future accuracy through the trackability function.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using a learned trackability network to constrain MPC optimization is well-supported by the experimental results across all three environments (toy scenario, ViZDoom, AI2-THOR).
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim about computational efficiency compared to full belief-space planning is supported by the approximation made in the method, but lacks direct empirical validation.
- **Medium Confidence**: The method's ability to handle high-dimensional observations is demonstrated in experiments, but the scalability to more complex visual tasks remains untested.
