# Benchmarking Large Language Model Capabilities for Conditional Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16793
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16793
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: Finetuned decoder-only models match or exceed encoder-decoder performance in conditional generation tasks

## Executive Summary
This work benchmarks large language models on 27 conditional generation tasks across 14 languages, comparing decoder-only (PaLM, GPT-3.5, LaMDA, ST-MoE) and encoder-decoder (T5, mT5, LongT5) architectures. The study reveals that finetuned decoder-only models like PaLM-540B achieve performance matching or exceeding encoder-decoder models, while few-shot results lag significantly behind. Multilingual generation performance strongly depends on pretraining data composition, with mT5 excelling in non-English tasks. The analysis shows that stable model rankings can often be produced with just 500 examples, enabling more efficient benchmarking.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 27 datasets across data-to-text and text-to-text tasks in 14 languages using both few-shot and finetuning approaches. Few-shot evaluation uses simple prompts with random exemplars and truncation to 2048 tokens, while finetuning employs input-target concatenation. Models are evaluated using ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, SentencePiece-ROUGE-L, ChrF, and BLEURT metrics. Decoder-only models use top-k sampling while encoder-decoder models use beam search for inference.

## Key Results
- Finetuned decoder-only models like PaLM-540B match or exceed encoder-decoder performance across all English generation tasks
- Few-shot generation performance lags significantly behind finetuning due to length normalization issues
- mT5 excels in non-English tasks due to its multilingual pretraining data composition
- Stable model rankings can be achieved with just 500 evaluation examples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Decoder-only PLMs with sufficient scale can match encoder-decoder performance in conditional generation tasks
- Mechanism: Larger model capacity compensates for architectural differences in handling input-output relationships
- Core assumption: Scale effects overcome architectural limitations for generation tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "finetuned decoder-only models like PaLM-540B match or exceed encoder-decoder performance"
  - [section 4] "finetuned decoder-only PLMs, such as PaLM-540B, closely match or exceeds the best reported prior results on all English generation tasks"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Few-shot generation performance lags significantly behind finetuning due to length normalization issues
- Mechanism: Few-shot outputs tend to be much longer than reference targets, causing poor overlap metric performance
- Core assumption: Overlap-based metrics are sensitive to length differences between predictions and references
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "few-shot output lengths are on average 2-3 times the average target length while finetuned model's output average 80% the average target length"
  - [section 5] "Overlap metrics used in generation are sensitive to length (Sun et al., 2019) making a natural disadvantage for few-shot learners"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multilingual generation performance strongly depends on pretraining data composition
- Mechanism: Models pretrained on multilingual corpora generalize better to non-English generation tasks
- Core assumption: Exposure to diverse languages during pretraining improves cross-lingual generation capabilities
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "The PLMs evaluated are mostly pretrained on English corpora... contrarily mT5 is explicitly pretrained in a multilingual corpus"
  - [section 4] "the much smaller mT5 bests the other models in 10 out of 14 non-English summarization tasks"

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the fundamental architectural differences between decoder-only and encoder-decoder models that lead to performance differences in few-shot vs fine-tuning settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares decoder-only (PaLM, GPT-3.5, LaMDA, ST-MoE) and encoder-decoder (T5, mT5, LongT5) architectures, noting that finetuned decoder-only models like PaLM-540B match or exceed encoder-decoder performance, while few-shot results lag significantly behind fine-tuning.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates performance differences, it doesn't deeply analyze the underlying architectural reasons for why decoder-only models excel with fine-tuning but struggle in few-shot scenarios compared to encoder-decoder models.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed architectural analysis comparing attention mechanisms, pretraining objectives, and their impact on few-shot vs fine-tuning performance across different task types.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do multilingual generation capabilities scale with model size and pretraining data composition beyond the studied models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that multilingual generation performance strongly depends on pretraining data composition, with mT5 excelling in non-English tasks due to its multilingual pretraining, and notes that English-centric PLMs are better at processing non-English input than generating non-English output.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluates a limited set of model sizes and pretraining compositions. It's unclear how much larger models or different pretraining data ratios would impact multilingual generation performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic scaling studies testing various model sizes and pretraining data compositions (e.g., 10% vs 50% vs 90% non-English data) across more languages and task types.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the optimal evaluation subset sizes and selection strategies for efficient benchmarking across different task complexities and model performance gaps?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper finds that stable model rankings can often be produced with just 500 examples, enabling more efficient benchmarking, but notes that tasks where models achieve very similar scores tend to require more test examples.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies that smaller subsets can work, it doesn't provide guidance on how to select these subsets or determine optimal sizes for different scenarios (e.g., tasks with large vs small performance gaps).
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies testing different subset selection strategies (random vs stratified vs difficulty-based) and their impact on ranking stability across tasks with varying performance gaps and complexities.

## Limitations

- The evaluation focuses primarily on tasks where input and output are roughly comparable in length, potentially biasing results toward decoder-only architectures
- The study relies heavily on overlap-based metrics for evaluation, which may not fully capture generation quality
- The few-shot evaluation methodology may not fully explore the potential of few-shot learning due to simple prompt design

## Confidence

**High Confidence Claims:**
- Decoder-only models with sufficient scale can match encoder-decoder performance in many conditional generation tasks when finetuned
- Multilingual generation performance is strongly influenced by pretraining data composition
- Few-shot generation performance lags significantly behind finetuning, particularly when measured with overlap-based metrics

**Medium Confidence Claims:**
- The observed performance differences between few-shot and finetuned models are primarily due to length normalization issues
- Model rankings stabilize with as few as 500 examples, enabling more efficient benchmarking
- mT5's superior multilingual performance is primarily attributable to its multilingual pretraining

## Next Checks

1. **Architecture-Specific Task Analysis**: Design and evaluate tasks that specifically stress the architectural differences between decoder-only and encoder-decoder models, particularly those with significant input-output length disparities or complex input encoding requirements, to validate whether scale alone can truly overcome architectural limitations.

2. **Metric Calibration Study**: Conduct a comprehensive analysis comparing overlap-based metrics with length-normalized alternatives across both few-shot and finetuned settings, and correlate these with human judgments to determine if the observed performance gaps are metric artifacts or genuine capability differences.

3. **Pretraining Data Composition Analysis**: Systematically vary the multilingual composition of pretraining data for decoder-only models to isolate the specific aspects of multilingual pretraining that contribute to improved non-English generation performance, beyond simple language exposure.
