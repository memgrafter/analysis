# Biologically inspired structure learning with reverse knowledge distillation for spiking neural networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09500
- Reference count: 31
- One-line primary result: Reverse knowledge distillation with pruned or virtual teachers improves SNN accuracy while reducing connection redundancy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitations of spiking neural networks (SNNs) by proposing biologically inspired structure learning methods with reverse knowledge distillation. The core idea is to dynamically optimize the synaptic connections in SNNs through knowledge distillation and connection pruning, enabling the construction of deep but sparse network topologies. The method involves two approaches: sparse-KD, which uses pruned SNN models as teachers, and teacher default-KD, which uses a virtual teacher with 100% accuracy. Experiments on CIFAR100 and DVS-Gesture datasets demonstrate that the proposed methods achieve improved performance while reducing connection redundancy.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses reverse knowledge distillation to train student SNNs using pruned or virtual teacher models. The process involves training an initial SNN, pruning it to create a sparse teacher model, and then training a student SNN using knowledge distillation loss that combines KL divergence between teacher and student outputs with cross-entropy against true labels. The surrogate gradient method handles the non-differentiability of spikes during training.

## Key Results
- Sparse-KD improves accuracy by up to 1.60% on CIFAR100 and 2.44% on DVS-Gesture
- Teacher default-KD achieves accuracy improvements of 1.60% on CIFAR100 and 1.74% on DVS-Gesture
- Both methods reduce connection redundancy while maintaining or improving performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Sparse-KD improves student SNN accuracy by transferring hidden knowledge from pruned teacher models.
- Mechanism: Pruning removes redundant connections from a pre-trained SNN, creating a sparse teacher model. The student SNN learns from the teacher's output (soft labels) via KL divergence, capturing distilled knowledge without interference from redundant connections.
- Core assumption: A pruned SNN retains sufficient representational power to act as an effective teacher, and its sparsity reduces interference in the knowledge transfer process.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Teacher default-KD enables knowledge transfer without requiring a pre-trained teacher model.
- Mechanism: A virtual teacher with 100% accuracy is created by manually setting a probability distribution (high confidence for correct class, low for others). The student SNN learns from this ideal distribution via KL divergence, avoiding interference from teacher model errors.
- Core assumption: A manually designed probability distribution can effectively guide student SNN training without requiring actual teacher model computation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Reverse knowledge distillation allows student SNNs to learn from weaker teacher models while improving performance.
- Mechanism: Unlike traditional KD where strong teachers guide weak students, reverse KD uses pruned (weaker) or virtual (ideal) teachers to guide student SNN training. This challenges the assumption that only strong models can be effective teachers.
- Core assumption: Teacher strength isn't the only factor in effective knowledge transfer; the structure and characteristics of the teacher model also matter.

  - Why needed here: Pruning creates sparse teacher models and is fundamental to the sparse-KD approach.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Teacher models: Pruned SNNs or virtual probability distributions
  - Student models: SNNs with same architecture as teachers
  - Knowledge distillation layer: Computes KL divergence between teacher and student outputs
  - Loss function: Combines KL divergence with cross-entropy against true labels
  - Surrogate gradient layer: Handles SNN training with non-differentiable spikes

- Critical path:
  1. Train initial SNN model
  2. Prune connections to create sparse teacher (sparse-KD) or create virtual teacher (default-KD)
  3. Train student SNN using knowledge distillation loss
  4. Evaluate student performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pruning ratio vs. teacher model accuracy
  - Temperature parameter T vs. smoothness of probability distributions
  - α weighting vs. balance between distillation and true label loss
  - Student model depth vs. training complexity and resource requirements

- Failure signatures:
  - Student accuracy lower than teacher accuracy
  - No improvement when increasing pruning ratio
  - Training instability or divergence
  - Excessive resource consumption during distillation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare student SNN accuracy with and without knowledge distillation using the same architecture
  2. Test different pruning ratios on teacher model and measure impact on student performance
  3. Evaluate virtual teacher effectiveness by varying the confidence parameter α in the probability distribution

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed reverse knowledge distillation method perform on other neuromorphic datasets beyond CIFAR100 and DVS-Gesture?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that future work will expand structure learning methods to utilize the advantages of spiking signals on more types of datasets.
- Why unresolved: The current experiments are limited to two datasets, which may not be representative of all neuromorphic data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the method on a diverse range of neuromorphic datasets (e.g., event-based, audio, or time-series data) and comparing performance against existing methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the reverse knowledge distillation approach be extended to handle cases where the teacher model is severely impaired or completely disabled?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention exploring more situations when the teacher model is "ill or disabled" as future work.
- Why unresolved: The current framework assumes a functional (if weak) teacher model, but real-world scenarios may involve broken or degraded teacher models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstrating the method's effectiveness when the teacher model is corrupted, has missing weights, or is non-functional.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of different pruning strategies (e.g., structured vs. unstructured pruning) on the performance of the sparse-KD method?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors use weight-based pruning with a mask to remove low-weight connections, but do not explore alternative pruning strategies.
- Why unresolved: The choice of pruning method could significantly affect the quality of the teacher model and the resulting student performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the performance of sparse-KD using various pruning strategies (e.g., magnitude pruning, structured pruning, or gradient-based pruning) and analyzing the trade-offs between sparsity and accuracy.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the reverse knowledge distillation method scale to larger and deeper spiking neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors evaluate their method on relatively small architectures (ResNet18, WRN16-4, VGG16) and suggest exploring deeper structures in future work.
- Why unresolved: The scalability of the method to very deep or wide networks is unknown, which is critical for practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on larger architectures (e.g., ResNet50, ResNet101) and analyzing computational efficiency, memory usage, and accuracy gains.

## Limitations

- Limited dataset scope: Experiments only cover CIFAR100 and DVS-Gesture, limiting generalizability
- Unclear pruning methodology: The specific pruning approach for creating teacher models isn't detailed
- Unverified biological claims: Energy efficiency and biological inspiration claims lack quantitative analysis

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The basic mechanism of knowledge distillation in SNNs using KL divergence is well-established in the literature, and the performance improvements on the tested datasets are clearly demonstrated.

**Medium confidence**: The reverse knowledge distillation concept (using pruned or virtual teachers) is innovative but requires more rigorous ablation studies to confirm that the improvements aren't simply due to standard KD effects.

**Low confidence**: Claims about biological inspiration and energy efficiency improvements are not supported by any quantitative analysis in the abstract.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study**: Test student SNN performance with standard KD (strong teacher) vs. reverse KD (pruned teacher) to isolate the specific benefits of the proposed approach.

2. **Pruning sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary pruning ratios and measure the trade-off between teacher sparsity and student performance to identify optimal pruning thresholds.

3. **Generalization testing**: Evaluate the method on additional datasets beyond CIFAR100 and DVS-Gesture to assess broader applicability and identify potential dataset-specific limitations.
