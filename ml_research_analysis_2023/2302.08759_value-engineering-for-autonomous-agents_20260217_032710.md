# Value Engineering for Autonomous Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08759
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08759
- Reference count: 32
- One-line primary result: Proposes a novel approach to building artificial moral agents by grounding values in computational goals and connecting them to normative reasoning

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to building artificial moral agents (AMAs) by grounding values in computational goals and connecting them to normative reasoning. The approach treats values as context-dependent goals that agents evaluate using fuzzy logic, allowing for nuanced, continuous satisfaction measures. These goals serve as the basis for reasoning about norms, enabling agents to assess the value-alignment of norms by predicting and evaluating the outcomes they incentivize. The paper also proposes mechanisms for agents to collectively agree on the best set of norms that align with their instilled values, integrating agreement technologies like computational social choice and argumentation.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves representing human values as fuzzy logic goals with satisfaction thresholds that vary by context, enabling agents to evaluate norms based on their predicted outcomes and alignment with these value-grounded goals. Agents use agreement technologies such as computational social choice, argumentation, and negotiation mechanisms to collectively select norms that best align with their instilled values. This approach moves beyond treating values as inert labels by connecting them to normative reasoning and social functionalities needed for value-aware agents in hybrid human-computer societies.

## Key Results
- Values are encoded as context-dependent goals using fuzzy logic statements, allowing continuous satisfaction evaluation
- Agents can evaluate norms by predicting outcomes and measuring alignment with value-grounded goals
- Agreement technologies enable collective norm selection among agents with potentially conflicting individual values

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Value-aware agents can evaluate norms by predicting their incentivized outcomes and measuring the satisfaction of value-grounded goals using fuzzy logic.
- Mechanism: Values are encoded as context-dependent goals using fuzzy logic statements, allowing continuous satisfaction evaluation. Agents use these goals to assess norms by predicting the outcomes norms incentivize and measuring how well those outcomes satisfy the value-grounded goals.
- Core assumption: Agents have reliable mechanisms for predicting norm outcomes and can accurately evaluate fuzzy satisfaction of goals.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "These goals serve as the basis for reasoning about norms, enabling agents to assess the value-alignment of norms by predicting and evaluating the outcomes they incentivize."
  - [section 3.3] "Ensuring AI is aligned with human values has been argued to be one of the main concerns for ethical AI. To address this problem, and given the role of norms in driving behaviour, some works on automated norms synthesis have emerged, focusing on selecting norms on the basis of the moral values they support."
- Break condition: Prediction mechanisms for norm outcomes become unreliable, or fuzzy evaluation fails to capture the nuances of value satisfaction.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Agents can collectively agree on the best set of norms that align with their instilled values using agreement technologies.
- Mechanism: Agents use agreement technologies such as computational social choice, argumentation, and negotiation mechanisms to collectively select norms that best align with their value-grounded goals.
- Core assumption: Agents can effectively use agreement technologies to reach consensus on norm selection despite potentially conflicting individual value-grounded goals.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This capability paves the way for agents to align the norms enforced in their societies with respect to the human values instilled in them, by complementing the value-based reasoning on norms with agreement mechanisms to help agents collectively agree on the best set of norms that suit their human values."
  - [section 3.3] "Agreement technologies could be applied here, making use of computational social choice, argumentation, and negotiation mechanisms."
- Break condition: Agreement technologies fail to produce consensus, or individual value conflicts become irreconcilable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fuzzy logic encoding of value-grounded goals allows for nuanced representation of value importance and satisfaction thresholds.
- Mechanism: Value-grounded goals are encoded using fuzzy logic statements with satisfaction thresholds, allowing agents to pursue goals to satisfactory extents rather than absolute achievement.
- Core assumption: Fuzzy logic can adequately capture the nuances of value satisfaction and importance in different contexts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "Traditionally, the term 'goal' in AI denotes a hard goal with a clear-cut definition that is evaluated to either true or false. We believe that this kind of dichotomous goals are not nuanced enough to reﬂect the complexities of values. Hence, we propose to encode value-motivated goals using fuzzy logic statements that can capture levels of satisfaction on a continuous scale."
  - [section 3.1] "Furthermore, this approach can also handle the relative importance of values by assigning satisfaction thresholds to their corresponding goals."
- Break condition: Fuzzy logic fails to adequately represent value satisfaction in complex scenarios, or satisfaction thresholds become too difficult to determine.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Value elicitation interface -> Value-grounded goal encoder (fuzzy logic) -> Norm outcome predictor -> Value satisfaction evaluator -> Agreement technology module -> Norm enforcement

- Critical path: Value elicitation → Goal encoding → Norm evaluation → Collective agreement → Norm enforcement

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fuzzy logic vs. crisp logic for goal encoding (nuance vs. computational simplicity)
  - Analytical vs. simulation-based norm outcome prediction (accuracy vs. computational cost)
  - Centralized vs. distributed agreement mechanisms (efficiency vs. scalability)

- Failure signatures:
  - Inconsistent value satisfaction evaluations
  - Prolonged norm agreement processes
  - Mismatch between predicted and actual norm outcomes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How can we formally define and operationalize the concept of "value awareness" in artificial agents?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes a new artificial moral agent paradigm that allows for human-selected values to be embedded in an artificial agent's reasoning process, aiming to create value-aware agents.
- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests using goals grounded in values and fuzzy logic to capture levels of satisfaction, it does not provide a formal definition or operationalization of value awareness.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal definition and operationalization of value awareness, along with a method to measure the level of value awareness in artificial agents.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can artificial agents reason about the moral implications of norms and align them with instilled values?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes that agents should be able to reason about norms from the perspective of value-alignment, enabling them to analyze a set of norms in terms of the outcomes they promote and their alignment with desired values.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests using analytical or simulation-based approaches to evaluate the value-alignment of norms, but does not provide a specific method for agents to reason about the moral implications of norms.
- What evidence would resolve it: A concrete method or algorithm that allows artificial agents to reason about the moral implications of norms and align them with their instilled values.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can artificial agents collectively agree on the best set of norms that align with their human values?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes using agreement technologies, such as computational social choice, argumentation, and negotiation mechanisms, to help agents collectively agree on the best set of norms that suit their human values.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a specific implementation or evaluation of these agreement technologies in the context of norm selection and value alignment.
- What evidence would resolve it: An implementation and evaluation of agreement technologies, demonstrating how artificial agents can collectively agree on the best set of norms that align with their human values.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of fuzzy logic in capturing the nuanced nature of value satisfaction remains largely theoretical, with limited empirical validation shown
- The proposed agreement technologies are mentioned but not concretely specified, making it difficult to assess their practical feasibility
- The mechanism for predicting norm outcomes - crucial for evaluating value alignment - is not detailed enough to determine its reliability

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The general framework connecting values to goals and using these for norm evaluation is conceptually sound and builds on established AI principles
- **Medium Confidence**: The use of fuzzy logic for value representation is plausible but requires empirical validation for complex moral scenarios
- **Low Confidence**: The specific agreement mechanisms for collective norm selection and their ability to handle conflicting values are not sufficiently specified
