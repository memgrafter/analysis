# Don't Retrain, Just Rewrite: Countering Adversarial Perturbations by Rewriting Text

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16444
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16444
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ATINTE R, a T5-based text rewriter, improves adversarial accuracy by over 4% on SST-2 with only 0.5% clean accuracy drop

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ATINTE R, a text rewriter model that defends downstream text classifiers by rewriting adversarial inputs to their original, non-adversarial form. Unlike existing defense methods that rely on random word replacements or model retraining, ATINTE R uses a T5-based encoder-decoder model trained to reverse the adversarial changes made to the input text. Experiments on four text classification datasets and five adversarial attack methods show that ATINTE R consistently outperforms existing defense approaches, achieving higher adversarial accuracy with smaller drops in clean accuracy. The model also demonstrates strong transferability, improving adversarial accuracy across different models and datasets without retraining.

## Method Summary
The method employs a T5-based encoder-decoder model (ATINTE R) that intercepts adversarial inputs and rewrites them to remove perturbations before classification. The rewriter is trained on pairs of adversarial and original text, including intermediate steps in the attack chain, to learn to reverse all types of perturbations. Additionally, the model is pretrained on Wikipedia sentences with artificial perturbations to improve generalization. ATINTE R acts as a pluggable component that can transfer to other models and datasets without retraining, improving their adversarial accuracy.

## Key Results
- ATINTE R improves adversarial accuracy by over 4% on SST-2 compared to the best baseline, with only 0.5% decrease in clean accuracy
- The method achieves 16.6% average improvement in adversarial accuracy across three different models and 10.9% across three different datasets
- Pretraining with Wikipedia sentences improves ATINTE R's performance by more than 2.5% in adversarial accuracy without significant clean accuracy decrease

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial perturbations are undone by training the rewriter on intermediate adversarial examples.
- Mechanism: The rewriter is trained on pairs (ˆxi, x) where ˆxi is any adversarial step in the attack chain and x is the original input. This ensures it learns to reverse all intermediate perturbations, not just the final adversarial form.
- Core assumption: Each intermediate adversarial change is itself adversarial and can be reversed independently.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Instead, we employ a separate model that is explicitly trained to remove adversarial perturbations."
  - [section 3.2] "Therefore, in addition to undoing ˆxk, the rewriter should also be able to reverse each intermediate step."
  - [corpus] Weak: neighbors focus on rewriting tasks but not specifically on adversarial reversal.
- Break condition: If adversarial steps are too large or non-reversible, or if the rewriter overfits to training perturbations and fails on unseen attack patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The rewriter acts as a pluggable component that can transfer to other models and datasets without retraining.
- Mechanism: By dissociating robustness from the classifier, the rewriter trained on one dataset and model can generalize to others, improving their adversarial accuracy.
- Core assumption: Adversarial perturbations follow similar patterns across models and tasks, enabling cross-model transferability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Moreover, we show that ATINTE R generalizes across multiple downstream tasks and classifiers without having to explicitly retrain it."
  - [section 5.2] "We use ATINTE R which was trained to remove adversarial perturbations for the BERT classifier on the SST-2 dataset and employ it, without retraining, to remove adversarial perturbations for other classifiers on the same dataset."
  - [corpus] Weak: neighbors discuss rewriting but not adversarial transferability.
- Break condition: If attack patterns differ drastically across models, or if the rewriter overfits to specific dataset characteristics.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pretraining the rewriter on artificial perturbations improves its ability to handle real adversarial examples.
- Mechanism: Wikipedia sentences are perturbed by random word substitutions, and the rewriter is trained to restore the original text. This pre-training helps the rewriter generalize to real adversarial perturbations.
- Core assumption: Random perturbations from Wikipedia mimic the structure of adversarial perturbations enough to provide useful pretraining.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.5] "We sample 100k sentences from the English Wikipedia and randomly substitute 15% of the words in each of those sentences with one of the neighbors from the GloVe embedding space."
  - [section 5.5] "We observe that this pre-training improves the ATINTE R by more than 2.5% in terms of adversarial accuracy without any significant decrease in clean accuracy."
  - [corpus] Weak: neighbors discuss rewriting but not adversarial perturbation pretraining.
- Break condition: If real adversarial perturbations differ significantly from random perturbations, pretraining may not help or could even harm performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Victim classifier -> Rewriter (ATINTE R) -> Adversarial attack generator -> Pipeline: Rewriter -> Classifier

- Critical path:
  1. Generate adversarial examples on the training set.
  2. Train the rewriter on pairs (adversarial, original) including intermediate steps.
  3. At inference, pass inputs through the rewriter before classification.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a large language model for the rewriter provides strong generalization but increases latency.
  - Training on intermediate adversarial steps improves robustness but requires more training data and compute.
  - Pretraining on artificial perturbations can boost performance but may not perfectly mimic real attacks.

- Failure signatures:
  - Significant drop in clean accuracy when using the rewriter.
  - Minimal improvement in adversarial accuracy despite training.
  - Rewriter fails to generalize to new models or datasets.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the rewriter on SST-2 adversarial examples and evaluate its ability to improve BERT's adversarial accuracy.
  2. Test the rewriter's transferability by applying it to RoBERTa and DistilBERT on SST-2 without retraining.
  3. Pretrain the rewriter on Wikipedia perturbations and measure its impact on SST-2 performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- The paper mentions that experiments were restricted to text classification tasks and states "It therefore remains unexplored if our conclusions transfer beyond the text classification tasks."

- The paper mentions that ATINTE R relies on T5, which is trained on large amounts of English text, and states "It is possible that our approach is not as effective for languages where such a model is not freely available."

- The paper mentions that one limitation is the slower inference time due to using two neural models and states "One possible approach to reduce inference time could be to use more efficient text generation models like non-autoregressive text generation."

## Limitations
- Limited exploration of the method's effectiveness beyond text classification tasks
- Potential reduced effectiveness for languages without large pretrained models like T5
- Significant computational overhead due to using two neural models (rewriter + classifier)

## Confidence
- High confidence: ATINTE R's effectiveness on the tested datasets (SST-2, MR, AGNews, MNLI) against the five specific attack methods
- Medium confidence: The claim of transferability across models and datasets without retraining
- Low confidence: The pretraining mechanism's general applicability to diverse attack patterns
