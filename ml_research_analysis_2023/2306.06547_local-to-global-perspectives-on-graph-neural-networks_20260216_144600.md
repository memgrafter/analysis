# Local-to-global Perspectives on Graph Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06547

## Executive Summary
This thesis presents a comprehensive study of graph neural networks (GNNs) from both local and global perspectives. The work establishes theoretical foundations for understanding GNN behavior through three main contributions: convergence analysis of Invariant Graph Networks (IGNs), approximation power between Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs) and Graph Transformers, and a novel data-driven graph coarsening method. The research bridges theoretical guarantees with practical applications, providing insights into when and how different GNN architectures can be effectively deployed.

## Method Summary
The thesis employs a multi-faceted approach combining theoretical analysis and empirical validation. For IGN convergence, the work analyzes stability under edge weight continuous and edge probability discrete models, introducing edge smoothing techniques. The MPNN-Graph Transformer connection is established through approximation theory, examining self-attention mechanisms under various width and depth constraints. The graph coarsening method leverages neural network parametrization to learn optimal weight assignments, trained unsupervised to maximize coarsening quality across different metrics and reduction ratios.

## Key Results
- IGN-small converges under edge probability discrete model after edge smoothing and can approximate spectral GNNs arbitrarily well
- MPNN + VN with O(1) depth and O(1) width can approximate self-attention in Performer and Linear Transformer
- Data-driven graph coarsening method significantly improves common techniques across various metrics, reduction ratios, and graph types

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

The theoretical foundations work because they address fundamental limitations in GNN understanding. The convergence analysis of IGNs provides stability guarantees under realistic edge weight models, while the approximation results connect seemingly disparate architectures through shared computational primitives. The graph coarsening approach succeeds by learning domain-specific weight assignments rather than relying on heuristic methods, allowing the model to adapt to graph-specific structures.
