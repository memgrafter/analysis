# Supervised Hierarchical Clustering using Graph Neural Networks for Speaker Diarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.12716
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12716
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Key outcome:** GNN-based supervised hierarchical clustering achieves 53% and 44% relative DER reductions on AMI and Voxconverse datasets respectively

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel supervised hierarchical clustering approach for speaker diarization using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). The method treats speaker embeddings as graph nodes with edges based on similarity scores, allowing the GNN to learn optimal edge linkages and node densities across hierarchical levels. By enabling direct supervision of the clustering process, the approach significantly improves upon traditional unsupervised methods. The framework is further extended to an end-to-end system that jointly optimizes both the embedding extractor and GNN model.

## Method Summary
The approach constructs a graph where speaker embeddings serve as nodes and edges represent similarity scores between them. A GNN is trained to learn edge linkages and node densities across multiple hierarchical levels, effectively creating a supervised hierarchical clustering algorithm. This differs from traditional spectral clustering by allowing direct supervision of the clustering process. The method is extended to an end-to-end approach where both the embedding extractor and GNN model are jointly updated, potentially improving the quality of both speaker embeddings and clustering decisions.

## Key Results
- 53% relative DER reduction on AMI dataset compared to baseline systems
- 44% relative DER reduction on Voxconverse dataset
- Significant improvements demonstrate the effectiveness of supervised hierarchical clustering for speaker diarization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The GNN-based approach works by leveraging the hierarchical structure of the clustering problem, where speakers can be grouped at multiple levels of granularity. By learning edge linkages and node densities through supervised training, the model can better capture the underlying speaker structure in the data. The end-to-end extension further improves performance by ensuring that speaker embeddings are optimized specifically for the downstream clustering task, rather than being generic representations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Graph Neural Networks**: Used to process graph-structured data where speaker embeddings are nodes and similarities are edges
  - Why needed: Traditional neural networks cannot directly process graph-structured data
  - Quick check: Verify the GNN can propagate information between connected nodes effectively

- **Hierarchical Clustering**: Organizing data into tree-like structures with multiple levels of granularity
  - Why needed: Speaker diarization often requires identifying speakers at different hierarchical levels
  - Quick check: Confirm the hierarchical structure captures meaningful speaker groupings

- **Speaker Embeddings**: Fixed-dimensional representations of speaker characteristics extracted from audio
  - Why needed: Provide the input features for the clustering algorithm
  - Quick check: Validate embeddings capture speaker identity information effectively

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Audio -> Embedding Extractor -> Speaker Embeddings -> Graph Construction -> GNN -> Hierarchical Clustering -> Diarization Output

**Critical Path:** The critical path is the joint optimization of the embedding extractor and GNN, where improvements in embedding quality directly impact clustering performance through the GNN's learned linkages.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The approach trades computational complexity (due to GNN processing and hierarchical clustering) for improved accuracy through supervised learning. The end-to-end extension adds training complexity but potentially yields better-optimized embeddings.

**Failure Signatures:** Potential failures include poor generalization if the GNN overfits to training data, degraded performance with very large numbers of speakers, and sensitivity to the quality of initial speaker embeddings.

**First Experiments:** 1) Test on a small dataset to verify basic functionality; 2) Compare against traditional spectral clustering on the same data; 3) Perform an ablation study removing the end-to-end component to isolate its contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several implicit ones emerge from the approach, including how to scale the method to very long recordings, how robust the approach is to varying recording conditions, and whether alternative graph topologies might yield better performance.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses primarily on DER reduction without extensive ablation studies isolating component contributions
- Limited analysis of failure cases or robustness to varying recording conditions
- Scalability to very long recordings or large numbers of speakers is not discussed
