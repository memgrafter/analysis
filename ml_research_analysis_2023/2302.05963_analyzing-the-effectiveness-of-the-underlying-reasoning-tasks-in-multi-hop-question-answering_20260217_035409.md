# Analyzing the Effectiveness of the Underlying Reasoning Tasks in Multi-hop Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05963
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05963
- Reference count: 28
- Multi-task model improves QA performance from 77.9 to 79.4 F1 on 2WikiMultiHopQA and from 66.4 to 69.4 F1 on HotpotQA-small

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how incorporating underlying reasoning tasks—specifically sentence-level supporting facts prediction and entity-level reasoning prediction—affects the performance of multi-hop question answering models. The authors develop a multi-task learning framework that jointly performs these auxiliary tasks alongside answer prediction. Experimental results demonstrate that the multi-task approach yields significant performance improvements on two datasets, while also mitigating reasoning shortcuts. However, the underlying tasks do not enhance model robustness against adversarial questions, suggesting their benefits are limited to specific aspects of QA performance.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a multi-task learning framework that jointly performs answer prediction, sentence-level supporting facts prediction, and entity-level reasoning prediction. The model architecture builds upon a standard transformer-based QA model, augmented with additional output heads for the auxiliary tasks. During training, the model is optimized with a combined loss function that balances performance across all three tasks. The approach is evaluated on 2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA-small datasets, with additional analysis on debiased versions of these datasets to assess reasoning shortcut mitigation.

## Key Results
- Multi-task learning improves F1 scores from 77.9 to 79.4 on 2WikiMultiHopQA dataset
- Multi-task learning improves F1 scores from 66.4 to 69.4 on HotpotQA-small dataset
- Underlying reasoning tasks reduce performance drops on debiased datasets, indicating mitigation of reasoning shortcuts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The underlying reasoning tasks work by forcing the model to explicitly consider and represent intermediate reasoning steps. By requiring the model to predict which sentences support the answer and identify relevant entities across documents, the multi-task framework encourages the model to develop more structured representations of the reasoning process. This explicit reasoning supervision helps the model avoid simply memorizing surface-level patterns and instead focus on the actual logical connections needed to answer multi-hop questions.

## Foundational Learning
- Multi-hop reasoning: Why needed - enables answering complex questions requiring multiple inference steps; Quick check - model must correctly identify intermediate reasoning steps
- Supporting facts identification: Why needed - helps model focus on relevant evidence; Quick check - model should highlight sentences that directly support the answer
- Entity-level reasoning: Why needed - captures relationships between entities across documents; Quick check - model should identify key entities and their connections
- Debiased evaluation: Why needed - reveals whether models rely on superficial patterns; Quick check - performance should drop less on debiased datasets if shortcut mitigation is effective
- Adversarial robustness: Why needed - tests model's ability to handle perturbed questions; Quick check - performance should remain stable across different question formulations

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
BERT-based encoder -> Answer prediction head -> Sentence-level prediction head -> Entity-level prediction head

**Critical Path**
Input question and context -> BERT encoder -> Shared transformer layers -> Three parallel output heads for answer, sentence, and entity predictions

**Design Tradeoffs**
The multi-task architecture trades increased model complexity and computational overhead for improved reasoning capabilities and shortcut mitigation. The shared encoder forces the model to learn representations that benefit all tasks simultaneously.

**Failure Signatures**
- Performance degradation when entity-level reasoning is particularly challenging
- Limited improvement on adversarial questions despite gains on standard datasets
- Potential interference between tasks when their objectives conflict

**First 3 Experiments**
1. Ablation study removing sentence-level prediction head to isolate its contribution
2. Training with different loss weight combinations to find optimal balance
3. Evaluation on additional adversarial question types beyond sub-questions and inverted questions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Whether the improvements from underlying reasoning tasks would generalize to other multi-hop QA datasets beyond 2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA-small
- How the model would perform on more diverse adversarial attacks beyond the sub-question and inverted question types tested
- Whether the underlying reasoning tasks could be beneficial for other types of complex reasoning tasks beyond multi-hop QA

## Limitations
- Experimental scope limited to only two datasets (2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA-small)
- Robustness evaluation only covers two specific adversarial question types
- Findings based on English-language datasets only
- Entity-level reasoning evaluated only as entity predictions, not natural language questions

## Confidence

*Multi-task learning improves QA performance* (High confidence): The 1.5-3 point F1 improvements on both datasets are statistically meaningful and consistent across different experimental conditions.

*Underlying reasoning tasks mitigate reasoning shortcuts* (Medium confidence): While the performance drops are smaller on debiased datasets, the analysis does not establish causal relationships between the underlying tasks and shortcut mitigation.

*Underlying reasoning tasks do not improve robustness to adversarial questions* (Low confidence): The analysis is based on only two specific adversarial question types, and the conclusion may not generalize to other forms of adversarial attacks.

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate the multi-task model on additional multi-hop QA datasets (e.g., HotpotQA full dataset, QASC, or StrategyQA) to assess generalizability across different domains and question types.

2. Conduct ablation studies that systematically remove different components of the multi-task model to isolate which specific task (sentence-level vs. entity-level) contributes more to performance improvements.

3. Test the model's robustness against a broader range of adversarial question types, including those involving reasoning chains, temporal reasoning, and numerical reasoning to better understand the limitations of underlying reasoning tasks.
