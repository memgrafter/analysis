# A fuzzy adaptive metaheuristic algorithm for identifying sustainable, economical, lightweight, and earthquake-resistant reinforced concrete cantilever retaining walls

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00198
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00198
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Developed adaptive fuzzy-based metaheuristic algorithm for optimizing earthquake-resistant reinforced concrete cantilever retaining walls that minimizes cost, weight, and CO2 emissions

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel adaptive fuzzy-based metaheuristic algorithm for designing earthquake-resistant reinforced concrete cantilever retaining walls that simultaneously optimize cost, weight, and CO2 emissions. The method integrates three search strategies (exploitation, exploration, and information sharing) with fuzzy inference systems to dynamically adjust parameters and select operators throughout the optimization process. The algorithm partitions the search space into sub-populations (empires) and employs fuzzy rules to maintain an optimal balance between exploration and exploitation, particularly effective for high-dimensional problems.

The proposed approach was validated against nine classical and recent metaheuristics across nine seismic loading conditions, demonstrating statistically significant improvements in all three optimization objectives. The method showed particular robustness against random initialization and achieved consistent performance advantages, with reported improvements of 0.005% lower average cost, 0.004% lighter weight, and 0.005% lower CO2 emissions compared to the second-best method in test cases.

## Method Summary
The method combines three search strategies - exploitation, exploration, and information sharing - with fuzzy inference systems to adaptively tune parameters and select operators during the optimization process. The search space is divided into sub-populations called empires, with fuzzy rules governing the balance between exploration and exploitation throughout the optimization. This adaptive mechanism allows the algorithm to dynamically adjust its behavior based on problem characteristics and optimization progress, making it particularly effective for high-dimensional problems like reinforced concrete cantilever retaining wall design under seismic loading conditions.

## Key Results
- Achieved statistically significant improvements across all nine test cases compared to classical and recent metaheuristics
- Demonstrated 0.005% lower average cost, 0.004% lighter weight, and 0.005% lower CO2 emissions than second-best method
- Showed particular effectiveness for high-dimensional problems and robustness against random initialization
- Validated across nine different seismic loading conditions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm's effectiveness stems from its adaptive fuzzy-based approach that dynamically balances exploration and exploitation through fuzzy inference systems. By dividing the search space into empires and using fuzzy rules to guide operator selection and parameter tuning, the method can respond to changing optimization landscapes and avoid premature convergence. The integration of three distinct search strategies allows for comprehensive exploration of the solution space while maintaining computational efficiency.
