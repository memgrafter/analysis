# Breast Cancer Segmentation using Attention-based Convolutional Network and Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14389
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14389
- Reference count: 11
- Key outcome: Transformer-attention-based UNet achieves 95% training accuracy and 99% validation accuracy for breast cancer segmentation

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a transformer-attention-based U-Net architecture for breast cancer segmentation using infrared thermography images. The authors integrate attention mechanisms with convolutional layers to capture long-range spatial dependencies in tumor boundaries, while employing CLAHE preprocessing to enhance image contrast. The framework demonstrates exceptional performance on a breast ultrasound dataset with 780 images across normal, benign, and malignant classes, achieving training and validation accuracies of 95% and 99% respectively. The system incorporates Grad-CAM for explainable AI to visualize model decision-making and identify potential biases.

## Method Summary
The methodology combines CLAHE preprocessing for image enhancement with a transformer-attention-based U-Net architecture for segmentation. The preprocessing step improves contrast and extracts regions of interest through adaptive histogram equalization, while the proposed UNet model integrates transformer blocks into the encoder-decoder structure to capture long-range spatial dependencies. The framework employs attention gates within the U-Net architecture to focus on relevant features during segmentation. Grad-CAM visualization is used to analyze the model's decision-making process and identify areas of bias or weakness.

## Key Results
- Training accuracy of 95% and validation accuracy of 99% achieved on breast ultrasound dataset
- Framework outperforms existing state-of-the-art deep learning models for breast cancer segmentation
- High performance across three classes: normal, benign, and malignant with 780 total images
- Grad-CAM successfully visualizes model decision regions and identifies potential biases

- Mechanism: Transformer layers capture long-range spatial dependencies between pixels, overcoming local receptive field limitations of standard convolutional layers.
- Core assumption: Breast tumor boundaries exhibit spatially correlated features that span large image regions.

- Break condition: If tumor morphology lacks long-range correlations, attention mechanisms may add computational overhead without accuracy gains.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Histogram Equalization and CLAHE preprocessing enhances image quality and ROI extraction, leading to better segmentation performance.
- Mechanism: CLAHE divides images into tiles, applies adaptive histogram equalization to each, and blends them using bilinear interpolation, improving contrast without amplifying noise.
- Core assumption: Low-contrast and noisy infrared thermography images obscure tumor boundaries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The proposed methodology enhances the image's quality and extracts the ROI by Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE)"
  - [section] "CLAHE works efficiently with less computational time"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence about CLAHE for breast thermography
- Break condition: If CLAHE over-amplifies noise in homogeneous regions, it may introduce artifacts that confuse segmentation models.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Grad-CAM visualization provides explainable AI insights, identifying model biases and weaknesses in segmentation predictions.
- Mechanism: Grad-CAM computes gradients of the target class with respect to feature maps, producing heatmaps that highlight influential regions for predictions.
- Core assumption: Model decisions can be interpreted through gradient-based visualization, enabling diagnostic validation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose... and employ Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) to analyze areas of bias and weakness in the UNet architecture"
