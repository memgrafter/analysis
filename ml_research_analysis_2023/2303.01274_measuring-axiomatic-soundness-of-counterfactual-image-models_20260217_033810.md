# Measuring axiomatic soundness of counterfactual image models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01274
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01274
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Framework for evaluating counterfactual image models using Pearl's axioms (composition, reversibility, effectiveness) without requiring observed counterfactuals

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a general framework for evaluating the quality of image counterfactual models by translating Pearl's counterfactual axioms into measurable distance metrics. The authors propose that counterfactuals can be framed as functional mappings between observations and their counterfactuals, conditioned on parents and counterfactual parents. By leveraging the axioms of composition, reversibility, and effectiveness, the framework derives metrics that assess how closely an approximate model matches the ideal counterfactual function, enabling comparison between different model architectures even in the absence of observed counterfactuals.

## Method Summary
The framework evaluates counterfactual image models by translating Pearl's three counterfactual axioms (composition, reversibility, effectiveness) into distance metrics that measure deviation between approximate and ideal counterfactual functions. Models are trained as conditional VAEs or GANs that generate counterfactual images given intervention parents. Composition is measured as the l1 distance between original and null-transformed images, reversibility as the l1 distance after intervention cycles, and effectiveness is assessed using pseudo-oracles trained to classify parent attributes. Simulated interventions break confounding by resampling parents from their marginal distributions. The framework allows model comparison across synthetic and real datasets without requiring observed counterfactuals.

## Key Results
- Soundness metrics successfully distinguish between poor counterfactual models (identity function, VAE without encoder) and better models (conditional VAE, conditional GAN)
- Simulated interventions improve counterfactual effectiveness in confounded scenarios by breaking spurious correlations between parents
- There exists a trade-off between composition/reversibility and effectiveness when varying abduction depth in conditional VDVAEs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Counterfactual soundness can be evaluated without observed counterfactuals by leveraging axiomatic constraints
- Mechanism: The framework translates Pearl's counterfactual axioms (composition, reversibility, effectiveness) into distance metrics that measure deviation between approximate and ideal counterfactual functions
- Core assumption: The counterfactual function f(x, pa, pa*) exists and can be approximated as a black-box model
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We revisit Pearl's axiomatic definition of counterfactuals to determine the necessary constraints of any counterfactual inference model: composition, reversibility, and effectiveness."
  - [section 3.1.1]: "The soundness theorem states that the properties of composition, effectiveness and reversibility hold true in all causal models."
- Break condition: If the true counterfactual function is not well-defined (e.g., in cyclic models without proper intervention structure)

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Simulated interventions can mitigate confounding effects in counterfactual estimation
- Mechanism: By resampling parents from their marginal distributions, simulated interventions break spurious causal links between parents, allowing models to learn true causal relationships
- Core assumption: The joint distribution of parents has full support over the product of marginals, enabling resampling
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3]: "We sample pak and paK\k according to their respective marginal distributions and then randomly sample an observation conditioned on the sampled parent values."
  - [section 4.1]: "To make all parents independent of each other, following the same logic, we can sample each parent independently according to its marginal distribution."
- Break condition: If the joint distribution lacks full support, simulated interventions cannot properly break all confounding

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different model architectures have inherent biases that affect counterfactual quality, measurable through soundness metrics
- Mechanism: Conditional VAEs enforce composition via likelihood terms, while GANs require explicit composition constraints; effectiveness varies based on how well models can isolate parent effects
- Core assumption: Model architectures can be evaluated independently of their class using the same soundness metrics
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4]: "Unlike VAEs, GANs have no inherent mechanism to enforce composition; thus, we introduce a composition constraint which encourages the null transformed observation to be close to the original observation."
  - [section 4.1]: "For each scenario, we can see how the proposed metrics allow us to compare models independently of the model class."
- Break condition: If models cannot properly encode parent conditioning, effectiveness metrics will degrade regardless of other properties

  - Why needed here: These axioms define the necessary properties any counterfactual function must satisfy, which the framework uses to derive evaluation metrics
  - Quick check question: How does the composition axiom translate into a measurable metric for counterfactual models?

  - Why needed here: Confounding between parents can cause models to learn spurious correlations; simulated interventions break these links by resampling from marginals

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Counterfactual model (VAE/GAN) -> Soundness metrics (composition, reversibility, effectiveness) -> Pseudo-oracles for effectiveness measurement
- Critical path: Generate counterfactuals → Measure composition/reversibility via distance metrics → Measure effectiveness via pseudo-oracles → Compare models across scenarios
- Design tradeoffs: VAEs have built-in composition enforcement but may struggle with effectiveness; GANs need explicit composition constraints but can be more flexible; pseudo-oracles add data requirements but enable effectiveness measurement
- Failure signatures: Poor composition indicates the model doesn't preserve identity under null intervention; poor reversibility suggests the mechanism isn't invertible; poor effectiveness means the model can't isolate parent effects
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test identity function and VAE without encoder on color MNIST to establish baseline poor counterfactuals
  2. Compare Bernoulli VAE (β=1) vs Normal VAE (β=5) on confounded vs unconfounded color MNIST scenarios
  3. Test VDVAE with different numbers of abducted latents on CelebA-HQ to find the effectiveness-composition tradeoff

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of pseudo-oracles trained on biased data compare to those trained on unbiased data when using simulated interventions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses using pseudo-oracles trained on biased data and applying simulated interventions to address confounding, showing improved effectiveness metrics
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows relative improvements, it doesn't provide a direct comparison of absolute effectiveness scores between biased and unbiased oracle training with simulated interventions
- What evidence would resolve it: A head-to-head comparison of oracle performance metrics (accuracy, F1-score) trained on biased vs. unbiased data, both with and without simulated interventions

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between abduction depth (number of latent variables) and counterfactual effectiveness for conditional VDVAEs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates a trade-off between composition/reversibility and effectiveness when varying the number of abducted latent variables in the CelebA-HQ experiments
- Why unresolved: The paper explores this trade-off but doesn't determine an optimal point or provide guidance on how to choose the abduction depth for different applications
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying abduction depth across multiple datasets and tasks, correlating with downstream performance metrics to identify optimal points

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different distance metrics (e.g., perceptual vs. pixel-wise) affect the measurement of counterfactual soundness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the choice of distance metrics for composition and reversibility, noting the limitations of perceptual distances due to their non-metric properties
- Why unresolved: The paper uses l1 distance for its simplicity and interpretability but doesn't explore how alternative metrics might affect soundness measurements or model comparisons
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing soundness metrics using different distance functions (l1, l2, perceptual, SSIM) on the same models and datasets to assess consistency and sensitivity

## Limitations

- The framework assumes the counterfactual function exists as a well-defined mapping between observations and counterfactuals, which may not hold in complex real-world scenarios
- The effectiveness of simulated interventions depends critically on the joint distribution having full support over marginals, a condition difficult to verify in practice
- Soundness metrics rely on pseudo-oracles for effectiveness measurement, which themselves may introduce bias if they don't capture the true counterfactual relationship

## Confidence

- High confidence: The theoretical framework linking Pearl's axioms to distance metrics is mathematically sound and well-established in causal inference literature
- Medium confidence: The empirical results showing trade-offs between model architectures are convincing, but the synthetic datasets may not fully capture real-world complexity
- Low confidence: The generalizability of simulated interventions to all confounding scenarios, particularly where joint distributions lack full support

## Next Checks

1. Test framework on real-world confounded datasets (e.g., healthcare imaging with hidden confounders) to verify if simulated interventions effectively mitigate confounding effects
2. Evaluate metric sensitivity by systematically varying the degree of confounding in synthetic data and measuring how well the soundness metrics detect degradation
3. Compare against alternative evaluation methods such as human perceptual studies or downstream task performance to validate that higher soundness scores correlate with better counterfactual quality
