# Training Transformers with 4-bit Integers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11987
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11987
- Reference count: 40
- This work proposes a novel 4-bit integer training method for transformers that is compatible with contemporary hardware.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel 4-bit integer training method for transformers that addresses key challenges in ultra-low precision training: activation outliers and sparse gradients. The method employs a Hadamard quantizer to suppress activation outliers by spreading information across dimensions, and a bit splitting plus leverage score sampling technique to efficiently handle sparse gradients during backpropagation. Experiments demonstrate competitive accuracy on diverse tasks including language model fine-tuning, machine translation, and image classification, while achieving up to 35.1% speedup in training throughput compared to FP16 baselines. The implementation shows that INT4 matrix multiplications can be 2.2× faster than FP16 equivalents on modern GPUs.

## Method Summary
The proposed method addresses the challenges of 4-bit integer training through two key techniques. First, a Hadamard quantizer (HQ) is used for forward propagation to suppress activation outliers by spreading information across dimensions. Second, a bit splitting plus leverage score sampling method (LSS) is employed for backpropagation to efficiently handle sparse gradients. The method implements INT4 matrix multiplications using CUDA and Cutlass, and integrates these operators into existing transformer training codebases. The approach is tested on various transformer models (BERT, GPT2, ViT) across multiple tasks, demonstrating competitive accuracy and significant speedups compared to FP16 baselines.

## Key Results
- Achieves competitive accuracy on language model fine-tuning, machine translation, and image classification tasks
- Demonstrates up to 35.1% speedup in training throughput compared to FP16 baselines
- Shows INT4 matrix multiplications can be 2.2× faster than FP16 equivalents on modern GPUs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by addressing two fundamental challenges in ultra-low precision training: activation outliers and sparse gradients. The Hadamard quantizer suppresses outliers by spreading information across dimensions, which reduces the impact of extreme values on quantization error. The bit splitting plus leverage score sampling method exploits gradient sparsity to compute backpropagation efficiently, focusing computation on the most important gradient components. This combination allows for effective 4-bit training while maintaining model accuracy and achieving significant speedups.

## Foundational Learning
- **INT4 quantization**: Reducing numerical precision to 4 bits per value, which can significantly improve computational efficiency but introduces challenges in maintaining model accuracy.
- **Hadamard transform**: A mathematical operation that spreads information across dimensions, useful for suppressing outliers in quantization.
- **Leverage score sampling**: A technique for approximating matrix products by sampling rows or columns based on their importance, which can be used to efficiently handle sparse gradients.
- **Matrix multiplication optimization**: Techniques for improving the performance of matrix multiplications on GPUs, such as using specialized libraries like Cutlass.
