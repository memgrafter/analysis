# Explain, Adapt and Retrain: How to improve the accuracy of a PPM classifier through different explanation styles

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14939
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14939
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The DECLARE encoding consistently improves F1-score on balanced datasets when combined with automated FEI-based retraining.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to improve predictive process monitoring (PPM) accuracy by leveraging explainable AI techniques to identify and mitigate problematic feature correlations. The method uses SHAP explanations to extract frequent feature-value pairs (FEIs) from incorrectly classified instances, then automatically retrains the model after shuffling these problematic features. A new declarative encoding (DECLARE) is introduced and evaluated alongside traditional PPM encodings. The approach is validated on both synthetic and real-world event logs, demonstrating consistent improvements in F1-score after retraining, particularly with the DECLARE encoding on balanced datasets.

## Method Summary
The method follows a three-phase pipeline: First, extract frequent explanation itemsets (FEIs) using SHAP explanations on a feedback set of misclassified instances. Second, select discriminative FEIs using an M-score metric that measures the ratio of FEI occurrences in misclassified instances to correctly classified instances. Third, shuffle feature values in the training data according to selected FEIs and retrain the classifier. The approach is evaluated across multiple encodings including simple-index, complex-index, and the novel DECLARE encoding, which uses declarative constraints to capture temporal patterns in event logs.

## Key Results
- DECLARE encoding outperforms traditional encodings on balanced datasets when combined with automated FEI-based retraining
- F1-score improvements are consistent across multiple real-world event logs (CLAIMMGMT, BPIC15, PRODUCTION, SEPSIS)
- The M-score metric effectively identifies discriminative FEIs that, when shuffled, lead to improved classifier performance
- Automated FEI selection eliminates the need for manual identification of problematic features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by identifying and breaking spurious correlations that the classifier has learned from training data. SHAP explanations reveal which feature-value combinations are most influential in incorrect predictions, and by shuffling these features during retraining, the model is forced to learn more robust patterns rather than memorizing dataset-specific artifacts. The DECLARE encoding captures temporal relationships through declarative constraints, providing richer feature representations that help the model distinguish between correct and incorrect predictions more effectively.

## Foundational Learning
**Declarative constraints** - Temporal rules expressed as conditions on event ordering (e.g., "A must be followed by B before C occurs")
*Why needed*: Enable capturing complex temporal patterns beyond simple sequential dependencies
*Quick check*: Can the constraint model valid and invalid trace variations in the event log?

**SHAP explanations** - Additive feature attribution method providing consistent, locally accurate explanations for individual predictions
*Why needed*: Quantify feature importance in a way that satisfies key properties like consistency and missingness
*Quick check*: Do SHAP values sum to the difference between model prediction and expected value?

**M-score metric** - Ratio measuring discriminative power of FEIs by comparing their frequency in misclassified vs correctly classified instances
*Why needed*: Identify which feature combinations are most associated with prediction errors
*Quick check*: Does the M-score correctly identify FEIs that appear predominantly in one confusion matrix quadrant?

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map**: Event log → Encoding (Simple/Complex/DECLARE) → Train Classifier → SHAP Explanations → FEI Extraction → M-score Selection → Feature Shuffling → Retrain Classifier → Evaluation

**Critical path**: The most time-consuming steps are constraint discovery for DECLARE encoding and SHAP explanation computation, as both scale with dataset size and feature dimensionality.

**Design tradeoffs**: DECLARE encoding provides richer temporal features but requires more complex constraint mining; SHAP explanations are model-agnostic but computationally expensive for large datasets.

**Failure signatures**: Poor performance on imbalanced datasets with many activities suggests overfitting to minority class patterns; minimal improvement after retraining indicates FEIs may not capture the most problematic feature correlations.

2. Vary the k parameter in M-score selection to find optimal number of FEIs to shuffle
3. Apply the approach to a dataset with known temporal patterns to verify DECLARE constraint discovery

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the DECLARE encoding compare to other encodings on multi-class prediction tasks beyond binary classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the approach can be generalized to multi-class predictions but does not evaluate it beyond binary classification.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on binary outcome predictions and does not explore multi-class scenarios, which would require handling a more complex confusion matrix and potentially different FEI selection strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing DECLARE encoding performance on multi-class prediction tasks against other encodings, showing improvements in accuracy metrics like F1-score.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal value for the hyperparameters t (SHAP threshold) and k (FEI filter threshold) for different types of datasets and encodings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that hyperparameter optimization for t and k is a future research direction and only reports empirical evidence for specific values used in experiments.
- Why unresolved: The paper fixes t = 10 and reports results for k = 3, 5, and 10, but does not systematically investigate how these values affect performance across different dataset characteristics or encoding types.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying t and k across diverse datasets and encodings, identifying patterns or rules for optimal hyperparameter selection.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does combining DECLARE and complex-index encodings (e.g., data-aware temporal patterns) affect the accuracy and interpretability of predictions compared to using either encoding alone?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests this as future work, noting that features based on "data-aware" temporal declarative patterns could be investigated.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates DECLARE encoding separately from traditional encodings and does not explore hybrid approaches that might leverage the strengths of both.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing a hybrid DECLARE-complex index encoding against individual encodings on various datasets, measuring both prediction accuracy and explanation quality.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What are the computational costs and scalability limitations of the proposed pipeline when applied to very large event logs with millions of traces and thousands of activities?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates the approach on datasets with thousands of traces but does not discuss scalability challenges or computational complexity, particularly for the DECLARE constraint discovery and FEI selection steps.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation uses relatively small datasets, and the computational complexity of frequent constraint mining and FEI aggregation is not analyzed for large-scale scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance benchmarks showing execution time and memory usage as dataset size scales, identifying bottlenecks and potential optimization strategies.

## Limitations
- Performance degrades on highly imbalanced datasets with many activities, suggesting overfitting to minority class patterns
- The DECLARE encoding's constraint discovery mechanism is not fully specified, limiting reproducibility
- The approach is validated only on binary classification tasks, leaving multi-class extension effectiveness unverified

## Confidence
- Core claim validation: Medium confidence based on reported F1-score improvements across multiple datasets
- DECLARE encoding effectiveness: Medium confidence, though implementation details are incomplete
- SHAP-based FEI selection: High confidence in methodology, but hyperparameter sensitivity not fully explored
