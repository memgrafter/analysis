# Distinguishing Human Generated Text From ChatGPT Generated Text Using Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01761
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01761
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: 77% accuracy achieved using Extremely Randomized Trees classifier to distinguish ChatGPT from human text

## Executive Summary
This study presents a machine learning approach to distinguish text generated by ChatGPT from human-written text using a dataset of 10,000 samples. The authors employ TF-IDF vectorization to convert text into numerical features and test 11 different machine learning and deep learning algorithms. The Extremely Randomized Trees (Extra Trees) classifier achieves the highest performance with 77% accuracy, demonstrating the effectiveness of ensemble methods for this classification task. The research addresses the growing challenge of identifying AI-generated content in an era of widespread language model deployment.

## Method Summary
The study uses a balanced dataset of 10,000 text samples (5,204 human-generated from news/social media, 4,796 ChatGPT-generated using GPT-3.5). Text preprocessing includes stop words removal and TF-IDF vectorization to create numerical features. The dataset is split 80/20 for training and testing. Eleven algorithms are compared including logistic regression, SVM, decision tree, random forest, and neural networks like MLP and LSTM. The Extra Trees classifier with 50 trees and gini criterion achieves the best performance at 77% accuracy with MCC of 0.54.

## Key Results
- Extra Trees classifier achieves 77% accuracy in distinguishing ChatGPT-generated text from human-written text
- TF-IDF vectorization effectively captures discriminative features between the two text classes
- Ensemble methods demonstrate superior performance compared to individual models in this classification task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Extra Trees classifier outperforms other tested algorithms in distinguishing ChatGPT-generated from human-written text.
- Mechanism: Extra Trees builds multiple decision trees by randomly splitting data at random thresholds rather than optimal ones, reducing overfitting and capturing diverse patterns in text features. The ensemble voting aggregates these diverse trees to improve classification accuracy.
- Core assumption: The TF-IDF features contain sufficient discriminative information between human and ChatGPT-generated texts, and random splits can effectively capture these differences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The best performance is achieved using an Extremely Randomized Trees (Extra Trees) classifier, which yields an accuracy of 77% in identifying ChatGPT-generated text."
  - [section] "Form the results, it is clearly visible that extra tree classifier outperforms all the classifiers with an accuracy of 77%."
- Break condition: If the feature space (TF-IDF vectors) lacks discriminative power between the two text classes, random splits cannot produce meaningful distinctions, causing performance to degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: TF-IDF vectorization captures word importance differences between human and ChatGPT-generated texts, enabling classification.
- Mechanism: TF-IDF computes term frequency and inverse document frequency, highlighting words that are frequent in a document but rare across the corpus. This weighting emphasizes distinctive vocabulary patterns in human vs. AI-generated text.
- Core assumption: Human and ChatGPT-generated texts have systematically different word usage patterns that TF-IDF can detect.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "They employ TF-IDF vectorization to convert sentences into numerical features."
  - [section] "The speciality of this vectorizer over other methods is its ability to capture the importance of a word."
- Break condition: If both text sources use very similar vocabulary or if stop words removal significantly reduces discriminative features, TF-IDF may fail to differentiate classes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble methods (Extra Trees) mitigate overfitting and variance compared to single models, improving generalization on this text classification task.
- Mechanism: By training many trees on random subsets and random splits, Extra Trees reduces model variance and sensitivity to noise in the dataset, leading to more stable predictions on unseen data.
- Core assumption: The dataset contains enough variability and noise that ensemble averaging improves performance over single models like logistic regression or SVM.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The study highlights the effectiveness of ensemble methods in this task."
  - [section] "Extra Trees classifier splits data randomly" and outperforms other models including Random Forest and Gradient Boosting.
- Break condition: If the dataset is perfectly separable or very small, the benefits of ensemble averaging may be minimal or even harmful due to increased complexity.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - TF-IDF vs. embeddings: TF-IDF is simpler and interpretable but may miss semantic nuances that transformer embeddings capture
  - Extra Trees vs. deep learning: Extra Trees is faster and requires less data but may underperform on very large or complex datasets
  - Balanced dataset vs. natural distribution: Undersampling ensures balanced classes but may reduce real-world applicability
- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy across all models indicates weak discriminative features
  - High training accuracy but low test accuracy suggests overfitting
  - Poor precision or recall imbalance indicates class-specific weaknesses

## Limitations

- The 77% accuracy, while above random chance, indicates substantial room for improvement in detection capabilities
- The dataset originates from Quora and CNN, potentially limiting applicability to other domains and text types
- No comparison is made against state-of-the-art transformer-based detection methods that might achieve higher performance

## Confidence

- **Medium confidence**: TF-IDF vectorization effectively captures discriminative features between human and ChatGPT text
- **Medium confidence**: Ensemble methods provide robustness advantages over single models
