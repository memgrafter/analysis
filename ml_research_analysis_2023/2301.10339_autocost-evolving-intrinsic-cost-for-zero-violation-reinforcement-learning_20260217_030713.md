# AutoCost: Evolving Intrinsic Cost for Zero-violation Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10339
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10339
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: AutoCost framework automatically discovers intrinsic cost functions enabling zero-violation policies in safe RL

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of achieving zero-violation safety in reinforcement learning by automating the design of cost functions. The authors identify that standard safe RL methods often fail to achieve zero constraint violations due to inadequate cost function design. They propose AutoCost, an evolutionary search framework that automatically discovers intrinsic cost functions which, when combined with extrinsic costs, enable safe RL algorithms to achieve zero-violation performance while maintaining reward objectives. The learned cost function (IC4SRL) demonstrates strong generalization across different tasks, constraints, and robot types in the Safety Gym benchmark.

## Method Summary
AutoCost formulates intrinsic cost function design as a bi-level optimization problem. An evolutionary algorithm searches the parameter space of a 1-hidden-layer MLP (4 neurons) to find cost functions that enable safe RL algorithms (CPO and PPO-Lagrangian) to achieve zero-violation policies. The framework trains RL agents with each candidate intrinsic cost function, evaluates their safety performance, and evolves the population through selection and mutation. The discovered IC4SRL is then tested across various Safety Gym environments to verify its generalization capabilities.

## Key Results
- IC4SRL achieves zero-violation performance where standard safe RL methods fail
- The learned intrinsic cost function generalizes across different tasks, constraints, and robot types
- AutoCost successfully enables both CPO and PPO-Lagrangian to satisfy zero-violation constraints
- IC4SRL maintains competitive reward performance while ensuring safety

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - AutoCost -> Evolutionary Search -> Intrinsic Cost Function -> Safe RL Algorithm -> Safety Performance

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize a population of intrinsic cost function candidates.
  2. Evaluate each candidate by training RL agents with the corresponding intrinsic cost function.
  3. Select the top-performing candidates based on their ability to achieve zero-violation performance.
  4. Apply mutation to the selected candidates to generate a new population.
  5. Repeat steps 2-4 until a satisfactory intrinsic cost function is found.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost vs. search effectiveness: Increasing the population size or the number of generations can improve the search effectiveness but also increases the computational cost.
  - Generalization vs. specificity: The learned intrinsic cost function should be general enough to work in different environments but specific enough to capture the local unsafe regions in each environment.

- Failure signatures:
  - If the intrinsic cost function fails to guide the RL agent to avoid unsafe states, the zero-violation constraint may not be achieved.
  - If the evolutionary search gets stuck in local optima, it may fail to find proper intrinsic cost functions.
  - If the learned intrinsic cost function does not generalize well to different environments, it may not achieve zero-violation performance in those environments.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train an RL agent with a randomly initialized intrinsic cost function and observe its performance in terms of safety and rewards.
  2. Train an RL agent with a hand-designed intrinsic cost function and compare its performance to the randomly initialized one.
  3. Run the AutoCost framework to search for a proper intrinsic cost function and evaluate its performance in terms of safety and rewards.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can AutoCost be extended to discover intrinsic cost functions for safe RL algorithms beyond CPO and PPO-Lagrangian?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that IC4SRL was only searched with two safe RL algorithms (CPO and PPO-Lagrangian), and notes that "It is possible that some other safe RL methods require even more conservative intrinsic cost functions to achieve zero violation."
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluated IC4SRL with CPO and PPO-Lagrangian, leaving open the question of whether it generalizes to other safe RL algorithms with different theoretical foundations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of IC4SRL and AutoCost-discovered cost functions with a diverse set of safe RL algorithms (e.g., Lagrangian methods with different multipliers, safeguard-based methods, Lyapunov-based methods) across multiple environments.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the properties of intrinsic cost functions (like local awareness and density) and their effectiveness in enabling zero-violation policies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper analyzes that "some vital properties of cost functions like local awareness and dense signals are beneficial to safe RL methods" and visualizes that IC4SRL "assigns intrinsic cost values to the states next to the hazard."
- Why unresolved: While the paper empirically demonstrates these properties in IC4SRL, it does not provide a formal theoretical analysis of why these properties are necessary or sufficient for zero-violation performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis establishing formal connections between cost function properties (e.g., local awareness, density, Lipschitz continuity) and safety performance metrics, potentially through Lyapunov-like analysis or constraint satisfaction theory.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is AutoCost's performance to its hyperparameter choices (population size, mutation strategies, selection criteria)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper describes AutoCost's evolutionary algorithm with specific parameters (population size of 50, top-10% selection, two mutation types) but does not explore the sensitivity of results to these choices.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents results with fixed hyperparameters without ablation studies or sensitivity analysis, leaving open questions about robustness and optimal configuration.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive hyperparameter sensitivity analysis showing how performance metrics (zero-violation rate, reward performance, convergence speed) vary with population size, mutation rates, selection thresholds, and evaluation duration across multiple environments.

## Limitations
- Computational expense of evolutionary search requiring multiple training runs for each candidate
- Limited validation to Safety Gym environments, which may not capture all real-world complexities
- Unclear generalizability of IC4SRL to safe RL algorithms beyond CPO and PPO-Lagrangian
