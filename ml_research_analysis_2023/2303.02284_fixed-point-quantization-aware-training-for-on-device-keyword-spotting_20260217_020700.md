# Fixed-point quantization aware training for on-device keyword-spotting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02284
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02284
- Reference count: 0
- 4-bit FXP models achieve accuracy parity with 32-bit FLP models

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a fixed-point quantization aware training (FXP-QAT) method for keyword spotting (KWS) systems that enables training fixed-point models using standard floating-point workflows. The approach combines FXP training with two QAT techniques - squashed weight distribution and absolute cosine regularization - and extends these techniques to transient variables. Experiments demonstrate that 4-bit FXP models can match the accuracy of 32-bit floating-point models on the Google Speech Commands v2 dataset, while achieving 68% faster inference execution times. On in-house datasets, 8-bit FXP-QAT models show 4-6% improvements in false discovery rates compared to full-precision models.

## Method Summary
The authors propose training fixed-point KWS models by integrating quantization-aware techniques into standard floating-point training workflows. They combine squashed weight distribution with absolute cosine regularization and extend these QAT techniques to handle transient variables during training. This approach eliminates the need for q-format normalization during inference while maintaining model accuracy. The training process leverages floating-point arithmetic for gradient computation and weight updates, then applies quantization to simulate fixed-point behavior during forward passes. The method is evaluated on both the Google Speech Commands v2 dataset and an in-house keyword spotting dataset, comparing 4-bit and 8-bit fixed-point models against their 32-bit floating-point counterparts.

## Key Results
- 4-bit FXP models achieve accuracy parity with 32-bit FLP models on Google Speech Commands v2 dataset
- 8-bit FXP-QAT models show 4-6% improvement in false discovery rate on in-house KWS dataset
- 68% reduction in inference execution time without accuracy loss

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: The combination of squashed weight distribution and absolute cosine regularization effectively mitigates quantization noise by maintaining weight vector consistency and utilizing full dynamic range. The extension to transient variables ensures that quantization effects are properly accounted for throughout the network, preventing error accumulation during inference.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Quantization-aware training (QAT)** - Simulates quantization effects during training to mitigate accuracy loss. Needed because naive quantization typically degrades model performance. Quick check: Verify quantization noise is properly modeled during training.
2. **Squashed weight distribution** - Compresses weight values to utilize the full dynamic range of fixed-point representation. Needed to prevent underutilization of bit-width. Quick check: Confirm weight histograms span the available fixed-point range.
3. **Absolute cosine regularization** - Encourages weight vectors to maintain consistent directionality during quantization. Needed to preserve feature representations across bit-width reduction. Quick check: Measure cosine similarity between full-precision and quantized weights.
4. **Transient variable handling** - Extends QAT techniques to intermediate activations and gradients. Needed because quantization effects propagate through the network. Quick check: Monitor activation distributions through network layers.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Critical path:** Feature extraction and classification layers benefit most from FXP-QAT optimization, as these contain the majority of computation and memory access patterns.
