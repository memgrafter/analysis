# Optimal Best-Arm Identification in Bandits with Access to Offline Data
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09048
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09048
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Achieves optimal sample complexity for best arm identification in bandits using both offline and online data

## Executive Summary
This paper studies best arm identification (BAI) in stochastic multi-armed bandits where learners have access to both offline data (from an unknown policy) and online data (from sequential arm pulls). The goal is to identify the arm with the highest mean in as few online samples as possible, with confidence at least 1-δ, while optimally utilizing the offline data. The authors derive a lower bound on the expected number of online samples needed and propose a computationally efficient Track-and-Stop algorithm that matches this lower bound for small δ values.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a Track-and-Stop (TaS) algorithm that solves an optimization problem at each step to determine optimal proportions for pulling each arm. The algorithm then uses these proportions to guide online sampling. The key innovation is how the algorithm leverages offline data to inform the optimal sampling strategy, significantly reducing the number of required online samples. The algorithm is shown to be δ-correct and achieves an amortized computational complexity of O(K) per sample.

## Key Results
- Offline data can significantly reduce the number of online samples needed, even when the offline data poorly explores the arm space
- The TaS algorithm matches the lower bound on sample complexity for small δ values, up to lower order terms
- The algorithm is computationally efficient with O(K) amortized complexity per sample and maintains δ-correctness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm works by optimally combining offline and online data through a principled optimization framework. The offline data provides initial estimates of arm means, which the algorithm uses to solve a continuous optimization problem that determines the optimal sampling proportions. By following these proportions in the online phase, the algorithm efficiently allocates samples to reduce uncertainty about which arm is best. The Track-and-Stop approach allows for adaptive refinement of these proportions as more online data is collected.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: Offline Data -> Arm Mean Estimation -> Optimization Problem -> Sampling Proportions -> Online Sampling -> Arm Identification

Critical Path: The algorithm's critical path flows from offline data processing through the optimization step to determine sampling proportions, which then guide the online sampling phase until the best arm can be identified with confidence 1-δ.

Design Tradeoffs: The algorithm trades computational complexity (solving an optimization problem at each step) for optimal sample complexity. This is justified by the O(K) amortized complexity per sample and the significant reduction in required online samples.

Failure Signatures: The algorithm may fail if the offline data is extremely biased or if the optimization problem becomes intractable for large K. The δ-correctness guarantee may also degrade for larger δ values.

## Limitations
- Assumes knowledge of arm means from offline data, which may not hold in practice
- Lower bound derivation relies on specific assumptions about offline policy behavior
- Computational efficiency claims depend on efficient solution of optimization problem for large K
- Strongest results are for small δ values, with less precise guarantees for larger δ

## Confidence
High confidence in theoretical framework and lower bound derivation. Medium confidence in computational efficiency claims and practical applicability. Low confidence in generalization to cases where offline arm means must be estimated.
