# What Makes Entities Similar? A Similarity Flooding Perspective for Multi-sourced Knowledge Graph Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02622
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02622
- Reference count: 12
- Key outcome: Proposes similarity flooding perspective showing embedding-based EA methods seek fixpoint of pairwise similarities; introduces TransFlood and GCNFlood achieving comparable or better performance than baselines (e.g., TransFlood achieves 0.347 Hits@1 on FR-EN, outperforming MTransE by 0.103)

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a theoretical perspective on entity alignment (EA) by showing that embedding-based EA models are fundamentally similarity flooding algorithms seeking fixpoints of pairwise entity similarities. Through mathematical analysis, the authors prove that both TransE-based and GCN-based EA models can be reformulated to compute entity compositions whose similarity matrices converge to a fixpoint. Based on this insight, they propose two methods: TransFlood which uses similarity flooding via entity compositions, and GCNFlood with self-propagation to prevent over-smoothing. Experiments on DBP15K and OpenEA benchmarks show the proposed methods achieve comparable or better performance than existing models, with TransFlood outperforming MTransE by 0.103 on Hits@1 for FR-EN alignment.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a similarity flooding perspective for multi-sourced knowledge graph embeddings. It proves that embedding-based EA methods are variants of similarity flooding, seeking a fixpoint of pairwise entity similarities through embedding learning. The authors propose two methods: similarity flooding via entity compositions (TransFlood) and self-propagation in GCNs (GCNFlood). TransFlood reformulates TransE-based EA to be independent of relations by using entity composition matrices, while GCNFlood adds self-propagation connections between GCN layers to maintain local information and prevent over-smoothing. Both methods compute lambda matrices from entity compositions, initialize similarity matrices using seed alignment, iteratively compute fixpoints, and perform alignment search via kNN.

## Key Results
- TransFlood achieves 0.347 Hits@1 on FR-EN dataset, outperforming MTransE by 0.103
- GCNFlood with self-propagation shows stable improvement on DBP15K, especially on Hits@1 and MRR
- Theoretical proof establishes equivalence between TransE-based EA and fixpoint computation of entity similarities
- Entity alignment pairs induce isomorphic structures in entity composition space

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Embedding-based EA models are fundamentally similarity flooding algorithms that seek a fixpoint of pairwise entity similarities. The embedding learning process computes entity representations that enable iterative similarity propagation, where the learned embeddings represent entity compositions that converge to a similarity matrix fixpoint. This works because entity similarity can be expressed as an inner product of learned embeddings, forming a convergent iterative system.

### Mechanism 2
Aligned entities have isomorphic structures in the entity composition space, making them mathematically equivalent to graph matching problems. Entity alignment pairs induce a mapping function that makes the composition matrices of different KGs identical, revealing structural isomorphism between aligned entities. This works because the entity composition matrices capture sufficient structural information to enable alignment through isomorphism.

### Mechanism 3
Self-propagation in GCNs improves entity alignment by maintaining local focus and preventing over-smoothing. Adding a direct connection between entity representations and aggregated neighbor representations allows embeddings to retain local information while benefiting from neighborhood aggregation. This works because the entity representation itself contains important local information that should not be lost during multi-layer aggregation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Entity composition computation → lambda matrix computation → similarity initialization → iterative fixpoint computation → alignment search
- Critical path: For TransFlood: entity composition → lambda matrix computation → similarity initialization → iterative fixpoint computation → alignment search. For GCNFlood: entity composition → lambda matrix computation → similarity initialization → iterative fixpoint computation → alignment search. For GCN with self-propagation: GCN layers with self-propagation → alignment learning → alignment search.
- Design tradeoffs: The algorithm trades matrix manipulation (memory intensive) for avoiding embedding learning. It requires relation alignment for TransE but not for GCN. Self-propagation adds a hyperparameter but improves robustness against over-smoothing.
- Failure signatures: Slow convergence or divergence in the fixpoint iteration suggests the composition matrices are not well-formed. Poor alignment performance despite good fixpoint convergence suggests the isomorphism assumption is violated. Over-smoothing in deep GCNs despite self-propagation suggests the hyperparameter needs tuning.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the lambda matrix computation for a small KG and verify it matches the entity composition formulation.
  2. Run the fixpoint iteration on a toy example with known alignment to verify convergence and correctness.
  3. Implement self-propagation in a simple GCN and compare performance with and without it on a small EA benchmark.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the fixpoint computation in similarity flooding affect the scalability of entity alignment models when dealing with very large knowledge graphs? The paper mentions that the proposed similarity flooding via entity compositions algorithm has the disadvantage of requiring matrix manipulation, which could consume a lot of memory for large-scale KGs. This question is unresolved because the paper does not provide experimental results or analysis on the performance of the proposed methods when applied to very large knowledge graphs. Conducting experiments on larger datasets or providing theoretical analysis on the computational complexity and memory requirements of the proposed methods would help address this question.

### Open Question 2
How do the proposed self-propagation and similarity flooding via entity compositions methods compare in terms of performance and efficiency when applied to different types of knowledge graph schemas (e.g., homogeneous vs. heterogeneous)? The paper mentions that different KGs usually have heterogeneous schemata, which can affect the performance of the proposed methods. This question is unresolved because the paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison of the proposed methods across different types of knowledge graph schemas. Conducting experiments on various datasets with different KG schemas and comparing the performance and efficiency of the proposed methods would help address this question.

### Open Question 3
Can the proposed methods be extended to handle entity alignment in temporal knowledge graphs, where entities and relations may change over time? The paper focuses on static knowledge graphs and does not discuss the application of the proposed methods to temporal knowledge graphs. This question is unresolved because the paper does not explore the potential extension of the proposed methods to temporal knowledge graphs. Developing and evaluating extensions of the proposed methods for temporal knowledge graphs would help address this question.

## Limitations
- Limited experimental scope to small benchmark datasets, preventing assessment of scalability to large-scale KGs
- Modest improvements over baselines despite theoretical advantages
- Computational complexity concerns for large matrices requiring matrix manipulation
- Lack of ablation studies on the self-propagation mechanism
- No comparison with more recent state-of-the-art EA methods

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Theoretical equivalence between TransE-based EA and similarity flooding fixpoint computation; mathematical proofs of isomorphism between aligned entities; self-propagation mechanism preventing over-smoothing.
- **Medium confidence**: Practical effectiveness of TransFlood and GCNFlood on benchmark datasets; computational efficiency claims; overall improvement over baseline models.

## Next Checks
1. **Scalability test**: Evaluate the proposed methods on large-scale KGs (e.g., DBpedia, Wikidata) to assess computational efficiency and memory requirements for matrix manipulations.

2. **Ablation study**: Systematically remove self-propagation from GCNFlood and measure the impact on performance to quantify its contribution beyond standard GCN aggregation.

3. **Parameter sensitivity**: Conduct extensive experiments varying the self-propagation hyperparameter λ to determine optimal values and assess robustness across different KG pairs and sizes.
