# Calibrating a Deep Neural Network with Its Predecessors

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06245
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06245
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: PCS significantly improves calibration, achieving innately calibrated models without temperature scaling for some architectures

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical problem of confidence calibration in deep neural networks, particularly for safety-critical applications. The authors propose Predecessor Combination Search (PCS), a novel regularization method that improves calibration by searching for optimal combinations of block predecessors from earlier training stages. PCS treats each network block as an independent unit and searches for the best-fitting predecessor combination using a differentiable approach inspired by neural architecture search. The method achieves state-of-the-art calibration performance across multiple datasets and architectures, both before and after temperature scaling, while also improving model robustness under dataset distribution shift.

## Method Summary
PCS improves calibration by identifying the optimal predecessor combination for each block in a neural network. The method first trains a base model and saves weights from multiple training epochs as candidate predecessors. It then uses a differentiable search framework with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation to sample predecessor combinations, fine-tuning each combination for one epoch and evaluating on validation data. A proxy estimator (LSTM + linear layers) predicts classification error and ECE for each combination, enabling gradient-based optimization of the non-differentiable calibration objective. The search iteratively refines predecessor selections until convergence, ultimately selecting the combination that minimizes the weighted sum of classification error and ECE.

## Key Results
- PCS significantly outperforms previous calibration methods, achieving lower ECE on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet
- Multiple models achieve innately calibrated results (temperature T=1.0) without requiring temperature scaling
- PCS improves robustness to dataset distribution shift, showing better performance on SVHN and CIFAR-10-C
- The method consistently outperforms focal loss and standard early stopping across all tested architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
PCS improves calibration by searching for optimal predecessor combinations at the block level rather than the whole network. The method identifies the "sweet point" for each block independently, combining predecessors from earlier training epochs to mitigate overfitting in individual blocks while maintaining performance. Different blocks overfit at different stages during training, and optimal calibration requires block-specific regularization.

### Mechanism 2
PCS uses a differentiable search framework with a proxy estimator to optimize the non-differentiable calibration objective. By relaxing discrete predecessor selection into continuous space using Gumbel-Softmax and training a proxy estimator (LSTM + linear layers) to predict ECE and classification error, PCS enables gradient-based optimization of the calibration objective through the proxy predictions.

### Mechanism 3
PCS achieves innately calibrated models that require no temperature scaling for some architectures. By finding optimal predecessor combinations that balance overfitting and underfitting at the block level, PCS creates models where the softmax temperature is naturally close to 1, eliminating the need for post-hoc temperature scaling adjustments.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base model training -> Predecessor collection -> PCS search (sampling -> fine-tuning -> evaluation -> estimator update) -> Best predecessor combination selection
- Critical path: Training → Predecessor collection → PCS search (sampling → fine-tuning → evaluation → estimator update) → Best predecessor combination selection
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Storage vs. search quality: Storing weights from all training epochs provides better search coverage but requires significant memory
  - Search efficiency vs. accuracy: Random sampling of predecessors is simpler but may miss optimal combinations compared to more sophisticated sampling strategies
  - Proxy estimator complexity vs. search performance: More complex estimators may better capture the calibration landscape but require more training data and computation
- Failure signatures:
  - If PCS produces predecessor combinations that don't improve calibration, the proxy estimator may be poorly trained or the search space may be insufficient
  - If searching becomes prohibitively slow, the population size or sampling strategy may need adjustment
  - If temperature scaling is still required after PCS, the method may not have found optimal block-level regularization

  2. Test different sampling strategies (random, uniform, Laplace) for predecessor selection to evaluate their impact on calibration performance
  3. Compare PCS against standard early stopping and focal loss on multiple architectures to validate improvements in calibration and robustness to dataset shift

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the optimal combination of predecessors differ across various network architectures (e.g., transformers, recurrent networks) and whether the PCS approach generalizes to these architectures? The paper demonstrates PCS effectiveness on CNNs but does not explore other architectures, leaving open whether the predecessor combination concept applies to architectures with different block structures or training dynamics.

### Open Question 2
What is the theoretical relationship between block-wise overfitting patterns and the overall network's calibration performance, and can this relationship be formalized into a predictive framework? While empirical evidence shows different blocks overfit at different stages, the paper does not establish theoretical justification for why this relationship exists or how to mathematically model it.

### Open Question 3
How sensitive is the PCS performance to the choice of candidate sampling strategy, and what is the optimal strategy for different training regimes and architectures? The paper only evaluates sampling strategies on ResNet-50 with CIFAR-10, leaving open whether these findings generalize to other architectures, datasets, or training regimes.

## Limitations
- PCS requires storing multiple model checkpoints from training, significantly increasing memory requirements
- The method's effectiveness depends critically on the quality of the proxy estimator and representativeness of validation data
- PCS assumes block-level overfitting patterns are consistent enough across different architectures to benefit from block-specific regularization

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core mechanism of block-level predecessor selection for calibration
- Medium confidence in the differentiable search framework's ability to consistently find optimal predecessor combinations
- Medium confidence in the robustness claims under dataset shift

## Next Checks
1. Remove the proxy estimator and evaluate PCS performance using only ground truth ECE evaluations to quantify the impact of the differentiable search approximation
2. Test PCS with reduced checkpoint storage (e.g., saving only every 25 epochs instead of every epoch) to evaluate the tradeoff between memory usage and calibration performance
3. Apply PCS to vision transformer architectures (e.g., ViT) to validate whether the block-level overfitting patterns and PCS effectiveness generalize beyond convolutional networks
