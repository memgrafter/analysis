# Bayesian Quadrature for Neural Ensemble Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08874
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08874
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: BQ-NES improves test accuracy, ECE, and test log likelihood on ImageNet16-120 and shows robustness to dataset shift across multiple corruption types.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Bayesian Quadrature for Neural Ensemble Search (BQ-NES), a novel approach to automatically constructing ensembles of neural networks with different architectures. The method frames ensemble construction as a Bayesian quadrature problem, using uncertainty sampling to select a candidate set of architectures and then applying posterior recombination or re-weighted stacking to select and weight the final ensemble members. The approach is evaluated on multiple benchmark datasets and search spaces, demonstrating improved performance in terms of test accuracy, expected calibration error (ECE), and test log likelihood compared to state-of-the-art baselines like NES-RE and NES-BS. Specifically, BQ-NES achieves better ECE and test log likelihood on ImageNet16-120, and shows superior robustness to dataset shift across various corruption types and severity levels. The method's effectiveness stems from its ability to explore the architecture likelihood surface more thoroughly than alternative approaches, particularly in larger search spaces.

## Method Summary
BQ-NES treats ensemble construction as a Bayesian quadrature problem, where the goal is to approximate the integral of architecture likelihood over a search space. The method begins by using uncertainty sampling to select a diverse candidate set of architectures. These candidates are then evaluated to estimate their individual performance distributions. Finally, posterior recombination or re-weighted stacking is applied to select the final ensemble members and assign optimal weights, leveraging Bayesian quadrature to quantify and minimize integration error. This approach allows for a principled exploration of the architecture space while maintaining computational efficiency compared to exhaustive search.

## Key Results
- BQ-NES achieves better expected calibration error (ECE) and test log likelihood on ImageNet16-120 compared to NES-RE and NES-BS baselines.
- The method demonstrates superior robustness to dataset shift across multiple corruption types and severity levels on benchmark datasets.
- BQ-NES shows consistent improvements in test accuracy across various search spaces, including NAS-Bench-101 and NAS-Bench-201.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BQ-NES works by framing ensemble construction as a Bayesian quadrature problem, which allows for a principled exploration of the architecture space. By using uncertainty sampling to select candidate architectures, the method ensures diversity in the candidate pool, which is crucial for robust ensemble performance. The Bayesian quadrature framework then provides a way to quantify the uncertainty in the integration of architecture likelihoods, leading to more reliable weight assignments and better overall ensemble performance. This approach is particularly effective in larger search spaces where traditional methods may struggle to explore the full space efficiently.

## Foundational Learning
1. **Bayesian Quadrature**: A method for numerical integration using Bayesian inference, particularly useful when the integrand is expensive to evaluate. Why needed: Forms the mathematical foundation of BQ-NES. Quick check: Understand how Bayesian quadrature differs from traditional numerical integration methods.
2. **Neural Architecture Search (NAS)**: The process of automating the design of neural network architectures. Why needed: BQ-NES operates within the context of NAS. Quick check: Familiarize yourself with common NAS benchmarks like NAS-Bench-101 and NAS-Bench-201.
3. **Ensemble Methods**: Techniques that combine multiple models to improve overall performance. Why needed: BQ-NES aims to construct optimal ensembles. Quick check: Understand the difference between bagging, boosting, and stacking.
4. **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)**: A metric for evaluating the reliability of model predictions. Why needed: BQ-NES shows improvements in ECE. Quick check: Be able to calculate and interpret ECE values.
5. **Dataset Shift**: Changes in the data distribution between training and testing. Why needed: BQ-NES is evaluated on its robustness to dataset shift. Quick check: Understand common types of dataset shift (e.g., covariate shift, prior probability shift).

- Scalability concerns when applying BQ-NES to very large search spaces with thousands of candidate architectures
- Limited evaluation of robustness to dataset shift, focusing on specific corruption types and severity levels
- Potential computational overhead of Bayesian quadrature, which may impact practical applicability in resource-constrained environments
