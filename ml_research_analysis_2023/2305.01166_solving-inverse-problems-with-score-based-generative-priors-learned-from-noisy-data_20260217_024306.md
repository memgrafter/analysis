# Solving Inverse Problems with Score-Based Generative Priors learned from Noisy Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01166
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01166
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 34
- Key outcome: SURE-Score framework learns score-based generative models from noisy data using Stein's unbiased risk estimate and denoising score matching, achieving competitive performance to supervised learning even at low signal-to-noise ratios.

## Executive Summary
SURE-Score is a novel framework for learning score-based generative models from noisy training data without requiring clean samples. The method leverages Stein's unbiased risk estimate (SURE) and Tweedie's rule to jointly denoise the data and learn the score function, enabling self-supervised training from corrupted measurements. The approach is demonstrated on two inverse problems: compressive MIMO channel estimation and accelerated multi-coil MRI reconstruction, where it achieves significant performance improvements over naive training methods, particularly in low signal-to-noise ratio regimes.

## Method Summary
SURE-Score combines Stein's unbiased risk estimate (SURE) for denoising with denoising score matching (DSM) to learn score-based generative models from noisy data. The framework exploits Tweedie's rule to establish a consistency between the MMSE denoiser and the score function, allowing joint learning using a single network. During training, noisy samples are corrupted with additive Gaussian noise at a specific level σw, and the network simultaneously learns to denoise and estimate the score via a combined loss function. This enables approximate learning of the clean data distribution without access to ground truth, making it particularly valuable for applications where only noisy measurements are available.

## Key Results
- In MIMO channel estimation, SURE-Score reduces normalized mean squared error by up to 8 dB compared to naive training at 0 dB SNR.
- For 5× accelerated MRI reconstruction, SURE-Score achieves about 15% error reduction compared to naive training at 10 dB SNR.
- The method demonstrates competitive performance to supervised learning even at signal-to-noise ratios as low as 0 dB.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SURE-Score enables joint denoising and score learning using the same network by leveraging Tweedie's reparameterization.
- Mechanism: Tweedie's rule establishes that the MMSE denoiser for Gaussian noise is simply the noisy input plus noise variance times the score function. This allows the same network to simultaneously learn denoising (via SURE) and score estimation (via DSM), ensuring consistency at the training noise level.
- Core assumption: The noise is additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) with known variance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We formulate and justify a novel loss function that leverages Stein's unbiased risk estimate to jointly denoise the data and learn the score function via denoising score matching"
  - [section] "Inspired by the recent work in [17], we take advantage of the inherent connection between the two steps given by Tweedie's rule"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on different noise models or don't explicitly mention Tweedie's rule
- Break condition: If noise is not Gaussian or variance is unknown/uncertain, the Tweedie reparameterization fails and the joint learning objective becomes invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SURE provides an unbiased risk estimate for denoising without clean data, enabling self-supervised training.
- Mechanism: SURE approximates the expected squared error of a denoiser using only noisy samples by adding a divergence term. This allows training the denoiser without access to ground truth, which is critical when clean data is unavailable.
- Core assumption: The noise is i.i.d. Gaussian with known variance, and the denoiser is smooth enough for divergence approximation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Using the SURE loss as a training objective for deep neural network denoisers has been previously investigated in a standalone denoising setting"
  - [section] "The SURE loss does not involve the ground-truth sample x and has the remarkable property that its expectation taken over noisy samples satisfies"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - several papers mention SURE for denoising but few connect it to score learning
- Break condition: If noise is non-Gaussian or the divergence approximation is poor (high-dimensional, non-smooth denoisers), SURE becomes biased and training diverges.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Learning the score of MMSE-denoised samples approximates the score of clean samples when trained at appropriate noise levels.
- Mechanism: By training at a specific noise level σw, the Tweedie consistency ensures that the score learned on denoised samples approximates the true score of the clean distribution. This enables using noisy data to learn priors that work well for downstream inverse problems.
- Core assumption: The noise level σw is chosen such that the MMSE denoising is effective and the extrapolation to other noise levels is reasonable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We demonstrate the generality of SURE-Score by learning priors and applying posterior sampling to ill-posed inverse problems"
  - [section] "The Tweedie re-parameterization introduces a consistency between the two objectives at noise level σw"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - most related work focuses on denoising score matching with clean data, not noisy
- Break condition: If σw is too high (denoising ineffective) or too low (limited data regime), the learned score poorly approximates the true score and inverse problem performance degrades.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Denoising Score Matching (DSM)
  - Why needed here: DSM provides the objective for learning score functions, which are essential for generative modeling and solving inverse problems via annealed Langevin dynamics.
  - Quick check question: What is the closed-form expression for the score of a Gaussian-perturbed distribution, and how does it relate to the noise?
- Concept: Stein's Unbiased Risk Estimate (SURE)
  - Why needed here: SURE enables self-supervised denoising by providing an unbiased estimate of denoising error without clean data, crucial when only noisy samples are available.
  - Quick check question: How does the divergence term in SURE relate to the Jacobian of the denoiser, and why is it necessary for unbiasedness?
- Concept: Tweedie's Rule for MMSE Denoising
  - Why needed here: Tweedie's rule connects denoising and score learning by showing that the MMSE denoiser can be expressed in terms of the score function, enabling joint learning.
  - Quick check question: Derive the MMSE denoiser for AWGN using Tweedie's rule and verify that it matches the expected form.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Noisy input -> Denoising (via SURE) -> Score estimation (via DSM) -> Joint training objective -> Learned score function
- Critical path: Noisy input → Denoising (via SURE) → Score estimation (via DSM) → Joint training objective → Learned score function
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a single network for both denoising and score learning reduces parameters but may limit capacity
  - Training at a single noise level σw ensures consistency but may limit performance at other levels
  - Monte Carlo divergence approximation in SURE is computationally efficient but introduces variance
- Failure signatures:
  - If training loss plateaus early, the SURE divergence approximation may be poor or the noise level inappropriate
  - If posterior sampling produces noisy samples, the score learning may be inconsistent or the network capacity insufficient
  - If inverse problem performance is poor, the learned score may not generalize well to the test noise level
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train SURE-Score on synthetic noisy data with known clean distribution and evaluate denoising and score learning performance separately
  2. Apply learned score to a simple inverse problem (e.g., deblurring) and compare to supervised baseline
  3. Vary the training noise level σw and evaluate the impact on downstream inverse problem performance across different test noise levels

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of extending SURE-Score to handle noise models beyond Gaussian, such as Poisson or Laplacian noise?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the potential extension of SURE-Score to arbitrary exponential families using Generalized SURE, but does not implement or evaluate this extension.
- Why unresolved: The current SURE-Score framework is derived and tested under the assumption of Gaussian noise. Extending it to other noise distributions would require adapting the SURE and Tweedie re-parameterization steps, which may introduce new challenges in the consistency and stability of the loss function.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing the performance of SURE-Score on datasets with non-Gaussian noise, such as Poisson noise in photon-limited imaging or Laplacian noise in heavy-tailed distributions, would demonstrate the generality and robustness of the framework.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of the regularization parameter λ in the SURE-Score loss function affect the trade-off between denoising accuracy and score matching fidelity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that λ is set to the ratio of the DSM loss to the SURE loss using the first batch, but does not explore how varying λ impacts the learned model's performance.
- Why unresolved: The balance between denoising and score learning is critical for the success of SURE-Score. A poorly chosen λ could lead to either under-denoising, resulting in noisy score estimates, or over-denoising, leading to loss of signal structure. The optimal λ may also depend on the noise level and the specific application.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying λ across different noise levels and applications, with corresponding analysis of denoising performance and reconstruction quality, would clarify the role of λ in the SURE-Score framework.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the SURE-Score framework be adapted to learn score-based generative models from datasets with mixed noise levels, where different samples have different SNRs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates SURE-Score on datasets with uniform noise levels but does not address scenarios where the training data contains samples with varying noise characteristics.
- Why unresolved: Real-world datasets often exhibit heterogeneous noise levels due to varying acquisition conditions. Adapting SURE-Score to handle such variability would require either noise-level conditioning or a robust loss function that can handle mixed noise statistics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating the performance of SURE-Score on datasets with mixed noise levels, compared to baselines that assume uniform noise, would show whether the framework can generalize to more realistic scenarios.

## Limitations

- Performance at extreme low SNR conditions (below 0 dB) remains less validated, with limited empirical results in these regimes.
- The framework is currently limited to Gaussian noise models, with unclear extension to non-Gaussian or spatially correlated noise patterns.
- Computational cost of Monte Carlo divergence approximation in SURE may be prohibitive for high-dimensional problems.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mathematical derivation connecting SURE and DSM through Tweedie's rule is sound for Gaussian noise models. The claim that SURE provides an unbiased risk estimate for denoising is well-established in the literature.
- **Medium confidence**: The practical effectiveness of learning score functions from noisy data at moderate SNR levels (5-15 dB). The specific performance improvements (8 dB in MIMO, 15% in MRI) are likely reproducible but may vary with implementation details.
- **Low confidence**: Performance claims at very low SNR (0 dB and below) and the framework's ability to handle non-Gaussian or spatially correlated noise patterns, which are not addressed in the current formulation.

## Next Checks

1. **Noise Level Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically vary the training noise level σw across a wide range (from 0 to 30 dB) and evaluate the impact on both denoising performance and downstream inverse problem solving. This will reveal the optimal noise level for balancing denoising effectiveness and score learning accuracy.

2. **Cross-Noise Generalization Test**: Train SURE-Score models at multiple noise levels (e.g., 5, 10, 15 dB) and evaluate their performance on inverse problems with test noise levels different from training. This will quantify the framework's robustness to noise level mismatches between training and inference.

3. **Comparison to Alternative Self-Supervised Methods**: Implement and compare SURE-Score against other self-supervised learning approaches for score-based models, such as noise-conditioned score networks trained with denoising score matching on noisy data. This will isolate the contribution of the SURE loss component to the overall performance gains.
