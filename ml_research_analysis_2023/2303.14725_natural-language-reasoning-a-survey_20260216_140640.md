# Natural Language Reasoning, A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14725
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14725
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This survey clarifies natural language reasoning in NLP by proposing a distinct definition, discussing reasoning tasks, and reviewing methodologies. It categorizes reasoning into deductive and defeasible inference, highlighting the challenges of defeasible reasoning due to probable conclusions and complex inference relations.

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive framework for understanding natural language reasoning in NLP by distinguishing between deductive and defeasible inference types. The paper argues that while deductive reasoning produces certain conclusions from premises, defeasible reasoning deals with probable conclusions that can be retracted with new evidence, making it more challenging. The survey reviews transformer-based approaches including end-to-end reasoning, forward reasoning (chain-of-thought prompting), and backward reasoning (question decomposition), with particular emphasis on the potential of backward reasoning for efficiency due to smaller search spaces. It also identifies defeasible reasoning as a critical future direction and evaluates existing benchmarks, noting significant gaps in research on interpretable, faithful reasoning paths.

## Method Summary
The survey employs a literature review methodology, collecting and analyzing over 200 papers on natural language reasoning. It systematically categorizes reasoning types (deductive vs defeasible), reasoning directions (forward vs backward), and model approaches (end-to-end, modular frameworks). The authors provide a distinct definition of NLP reasoning based on both philosophical foundations and NLP-specific concerns, then apply this framework to review existing methodologies and benchmarks. The survey explicitly excludes neuro-symbolic techniques and mathematical reasoning to maintain focus on natural language reasoning with transformers. The authors identify research gaps and propose future directions, particularly emphasizing the need for better defeasible reasoning capabilities and more interpretable reasoning paths.

## Key Results
- The survey establishes a clear distinction between deductive reasoning (truth-preserving, monotonic) and defeasible reasoning (probable conclusions, non-monotonic), explaining why certain NLP tasks are harder than others.
- Backward reasoning is identified as a powerful but underexplored paradigm that offers efficiency advantages through smaller search spaces compared to forward reasoning.
- The paper highlights defeasible reasoning as a critical future direction, noting that current models struggle with tasks requiring non-deductive reasoning such as induction and abduction.
- Existing benchmarks are evaluated, revealing significant gaps in defeasible reasoning research and the need for more interpretable, faithful reasoning paths.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The survey distinguishes deductive from defeasible reasoning, which is critical for understanding why certain NLP reasoning tasks are harder than others.
- Mechanism: By categorizing reasoning into monotonic (deductive) and non-monotonic (defeasible), the paper explains why some conclusions are truth-preserving while others are only probable and can be retracted with new evidence.
- Core assumption: That philosophical definitions of reasoning can be directly mapped to NLP task characteristics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "categorizes reasoning into deductive and defeasible inference, highlighting the challenges of defeasible reasoning due to probable conclusions and complex inference relations."
  - [section] "While knowledge has been well categorised in NLP (e.g. explicit world knowledge and implicit commonsense knowledge), we find that there is still a lack of reasonable taxonomy for inference."
- Break condition: If the distinction between monotonic and defeasible inference does not align with actual model performance patterns, the taxonomy becomes less useful.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The paper identifies backward reasoning as a powerful but underexplored paradigm that is more efficient than forward reasoning due to smaller search space.
- Mechanism: Backward reasoning starts from the problem and breaks it into sub-problems, reducing the combinatorial explosion that occurs in forward reasoning.
- Core assumption: That the efficiency gain from smaller search space translates into practical performance improvements in NLP models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The paper also identifies and views backward reasoning, a powerful paradigm for multi-step reasoning, and introduces defeasible reasoning as one of the most important future directions."
  - [section] "Compared to forward chaining, backward chaining has a smaller search space and thus is more efficient [71]."
- Break condition: If the search space reduction does not lead to measurable improvements in practice, or if the backward reasoning approach cannot be scaled to complex problems.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The survey provides a clear definition of natural language reasoning that combines philosophical concepts with NLP-specific concerns, enabling better task identification and methodology selection.
- Mechanism: By defining NLP reasoning as integrating multiple knowledge sources to derive new conclusions that are semantically different from premises, the paper creates a framework for distinguishing reasoning tasks from non-reasoning tasks.
- Core assumption: That the definition captures the essential characteristics of reasoning tasks across different NLP domains.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Conceptually, we provide a distinct definition for natural language reasoning in NLP, based on both philosophy and NLP scenarios, discuss what types of tasks require reasoning, and introduce a taxonomy of reasoning."
  - [section] "Correspondingly, we propose the definition of NLP reasoning in Definition 2.4 and suggest 'what isn't reasoning in NLP' and 'what NLP reasoning can do' in Description 2.3 and Description 2.4."
- Break condition: If practitioners cannot consistently apply the definition to classify new tasks, or if the definition proves too broad or too narrow for practical use.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do emergent reasoning capabilities in large language models differ from those in medium-sized models, and what underlying mechanisms drive this difference?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the emergent abilities of large language models (LLMs) like GPT-3 and GPT-4, particularly in performing multi-step reasoning tasks, and contrasts this with the limitations of medium-sized PLMs.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies that LLMs exhibit reasoning capabilities that are not present in smaller models, but the exact mechanisms or features of the architecture that enable this are not fully understood.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed comparative studies of model architectures, training data, and prompting strategies that isolate the factors contributing to emergent reasoning in LLMs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective strategies for improving the generalizability of defeasible reasoning in natural language processing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights defeasible reasoning as a critical area for future research, noting that current models struggle with tasks requiring non-deductive reasoning, such as induction and abduction.
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses the challenges of defeasible reasoning, it does not provide concrete solutions or methodologies for improving model performance in this area.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results demonstrating improved performance on defeasible reasoning tasks through novel training techniques, model architectures, or prompting strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can backward reasoning be further developed and applied to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of multi-step reasoning tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces backward reasoning as a powerful paradigm for multi-step reasoning, emphasizing its efficiency due to smaller search spaces compared to forward reasoning.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests potential benefits of backward reasoning but does not explore specific implementations or evaluate its effectiveness across a wide range of tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies and benchmarks showing the performance of backward reasoning against forward reasoning and other methods in various multi-step reasoning tasks.

## Limitations

- The survey focuses primarily on transformer-based approaches and explicitly excludes neuro-symbolic methods and mathematical reasoning, which may limit its applicability to domains where symbolic reasoning is crucial.
- The categorization of reasoning tasks into deductive and defeasible inference, while theoretically sound, may not always align perfectly with practical NLP task characteristics.
- The survey's treatment of defeasible reasoning as an underexplored area may underestimate recent advances in commonsense reasoning that incorporate probabilistic inference.

## Confidence

- High: The distinction between deductive and defeasible reasoning, and the characterization of backward reasoning as more efficient due to smaller search space.
- Medium: The survey's proposed definition of NLP reasoning and its application to benchmark analysis.
- Medium: The identification of defeasible reasoning as a key future direction, given the rapidly evolving nature of commonsense reasoning research.
