# Integrating Item Relevance in Training Loss for Sequential Recommender Systems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.10824
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10824
- **Reference count:** 38
- **Primary result:** Proposes new evaluation protocol and relevance-aware loss function for SRS, showing ~1.63% NDCG@10 and ~1.5% HR improvements

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses limitations in Sequential Recommender Systems (SRSs) by introducing a novel evaluation protocol that considers multiple future items as potentially relevant, moving away from the traditional single-relevant-item assumption. The authors also propose a relevance-aware loss function designed to train SRS models more effectively when dealing with multiple relevant items, aiming to improve robustness to noise in the training data. The proposed approach demonstrates performance improvements over existing methods in both traditional and new evaluation protocols.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces a new evaluation protocol for SRSs that acknowledges multiple future items can be relevant to a user's next action, rather than assuming only one item is relevant. This is paired with a novel relevance-aware loss function that incorporates item relevance information during training, allowing the model to better handle scenarios where multiple items in the ground truth are actually relevant. The method aims to provide a more realistic evaluation framework and a more robust training objective for SRS models.

## Key Results
- Traditional evaluation protocol: ~1.2% improvement in NDCG@10 and 0.88% improvement in baseline metrics
- New evaluation protocol: ~1.63% improvement in NDCG@10 and ~1.5% improvement in HR metrics
- Relevance-aware models show consistent improvements over baseline models across both evaluation protocols

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed approach works by addressing the inherent limitation of assuming only one relevant item in SRS evaluation and training. By recognizing that multiple items can be relevant to a user's next action, the new evaluation protocol provides a more realistic assessment of model performance. The relevance-aware loss function then leverages this multiple-item relevance information during training, allowing the model to optimize for a more nuanced understanding of user preferences rather than being constrained by the single-relevant-item assumption.

## Limitations
- The multi-item relevance assumption, while potentially more realistic, lacks empirical validation showing it better reflects actual user behavior
- Performance improvements, while statistically significant, may have limited practical impact in real-world applications
- The relevance-aware loss function's robustness claims need validation across diverse datasets and noise patterns

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Technical correctness of mathematical formulation and implementation of relevance-aware loss function
- **Medium confidence:** Validity of new evaluation protocol and performance improvements due to limited empirical evidence supporting multi-item relevance assumption
- **Medium confidence:** Generalization of reported improvements across different domains and datasets
