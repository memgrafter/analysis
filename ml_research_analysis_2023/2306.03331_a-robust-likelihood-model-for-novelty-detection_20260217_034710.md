# A Robust Likelihood Model for Novelty Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03331
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03331
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: RLND achieves improved F1 score and AUROC metrics on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and COIL-100 under ǫ-attacks compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a robust likelihood model for novelty detection that integrates a robust prior into the training objective to make the likelihood test statistic resistant to predefined perturbations. The method builds on a generative probabilistic novelty detection approach with isometric adversarial autoencoders, leveraging its geometric properties for efficient implementation. The key idea is to maximize the likelihood of perturbed inliers and minimize the likelihood of perturbed outliers using closed-form approximations of worst-case perturbations. Experiments show significant improvements in detection performance under adversarial attacks compared to baseline methods.

## Method Summary
RLND extends the GPNDI framework by adding a robust prior Lr that generates synthetic perturbed inliers and outliers during training. The method uses an isometric adversarial autoencoder where the decoder defines a smooth manifold M and the orthogonal component determines likelihood. For each training sample, synthetic inliers are generated by sampling ν from U([0,ν0)) and applying f(z) ± (ν+ǫ)1x, while synthetic outliers are generated by sampling ν from E(λ) and applying f(z) ± (ν+ν0−ǫ)1x. The model is trained with a combined loss including the robust prior, using Adam optimizer with learning rates 0.0002 (MNIST/Fashion-MNIST) or 0.0003 (COIL-100), batch size 128, λr=0.001, ǫ=0.5×ν0, λ=5.0.

## Key Results
- RLND shows significant improvements in F1 score and AUROC metrics when subjected to ǫ-attacks on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and COIL-100
- The method achieves improved performance even in the absence of attacks compared to the baseline GPNDI
- Computational efficiency is maintained through closed-form perturbation optimization instead of iterative PGD

- Claim: Synthetic outlier generation via exponential sampling of ν ensures diversity beyond the decision boundary.
- Mechanism: By sampling ν from an exponential distribution with offset ν0, the method creates outliers at increasing distances from the manifold. This encourages the model to learn a sharp likelihood drop beyond the inlier region, improving discrimination.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Closed-form perturbation optimization avoids costly projected gradient descent while still targeting worst-case perturbations.
- Mechanism: Instead of iterative PGD to find worst-case δ in the ǫ-ball, the method uses the manifold geometry to approximate min and max likelihood perturbations as simple ±ǫ shifts along 1x. This yields the same robust objective but at much lower computational cost.
- Core assumption: The worst-case perturbations are exactly ±ǫ along the orthogonal direction, which holds because likelihood drops fastest when moving away from the manifold.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Given the properties of the autoencoder defined by g and f, f (z) is the orthogonal projection of x onto the manifoldM, and 1x is perpendicular to the tangent plane T. Therefore, (18) stems from the fact that it is reasonable to expect that the largest drop of the likelihood pX will be due to a perturbation that moves x away fromM the furthest possible, and this should happen along the direction orthogonal toM. Similarly, (19) stems from the fact that we are expecting to observe the highest increase of pX when x moves the closest towardsM."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Encoder g -> Latent z -> Decoder f -> Manifold M; Discriminator Dx; Discriminator Dz
- Critical path: 1. Sample minibatch of inliers 2. For each, compute ν0 from validation threshold γ 3. Generate synthetic inliers via f(z) ± (ν+ǫ)1x 4. Generate synthetic outliers via f(z) ± (ν+ν0−ǫ)1x 5. Update Dx, f, g using adversarial and robust losses 6. Repeat until convergence
- Design tradeoffs: Closed-form perturbation vs. PGD: speed vs. exactness; Exponential vs. uniform outlier sampling: tail coverage vs. simplicity; Robust prior weight λr: regularization strength vs. overfitting
- Failure signatures: Training loss plateaus early: synthetic data generation may be degenerate; AUROC degrades with larger ǫ: robust prior may be too weak or closed-form assumption breaks; Discriminator loss diverges: adversarial training instability
- First 3 experiments: 1. Train RLND on MNIST with 10% outliers, no attacks, measure F1 vs. GPNDI 2. Apply ǫ=0.5 attack on same model, compare precision/recall 3. Vary λr across {0.001, 0.01, 0.1}, observe effect on robustness curve

## Limitations
- The robustness claims rely on closed-form approximation of worst-case perturbations along orthogonal direction, which lacks rigorous verification across diverse perturbation patterns
- Synthetic outlier generation introduces hyperparameters (ν0, λ) that could significantly affect performance but are not fully explored
- The geometric assumptions about manifold learning may not hold for complex real-world data distributions

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Orthogonal perturbation focus): Medium - Supported by geometric reasoning and empirical results, but the closed-form approximation's accuracy under arbitrary perturbations is not validated.
- Mechanism 2 (Synthetic outlier diversity): Low - The exponential sampling approach is described but not compared against simpler alternatives or analyzed for sensitivity to ν0, λ.
- Mechanism 3 (Computational efficiency): High - The claim of avoiding PGD is clearly stated and aligns with the geometric simplifications used.
