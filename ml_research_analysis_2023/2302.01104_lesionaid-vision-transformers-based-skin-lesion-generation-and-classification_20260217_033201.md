# LesionAid: Vision Transformers-based Skin Lesion Generation and Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01104
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01104
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: ViT-based skin lesion classification achieves 99.2% training and 97.4% validation accuracy on HAM10000 dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces LesionAid, a framework for skin lesion classification that combines Vision Transformers with ViTGAN for synthetic image generation to address class imbalance. The system generates synthetic skin lesion images to balance underrepresented classes, then applies a Vision Transformer classifier with GradCAM-based explainable AI. The framework is deployed as a web application and demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods in terms of computational efficiency, predictive accuracy, and explainability.

## Method Summary
LesionAid is a four-phase framework addressing skin lesion classification. First, ViTGAN generates synthetic images to balance class distribution in the HAM10000 dataset. Second, image processing includes data augmentation techniques. Third, a Vision Transformer classifies the balanced dataset. Finally, GradCAM provides explainable visualizations of model decisions. The system is implemented as a Flask-based web application for user-friendly deployment.

## Key Results
- Training accuracy: 99.2%
- Validation accuracy: 97.4% on HAM10000 dataset
- Seven skin lesion types classified (NV, MEL, and others)
- Synthetic image generation addresses class imbalance
- GradCAM provides interpretable model decision visualizations

- Mechanism: ViTGAN generates realistic synthetic skin lesion images for underrepresented classes, balancing the dataset before training the ViT classifier.
- Core assumption: Generated images are sufficiently realistic to improve model performance without introducing noise.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Vision Transformers achieve superior classification accuracy by processing image patches as sequential embeddings rather than using convolutional layers.
- Mechanism: Images are divided into patches, linearly embedded, and processed through transformer encoders with self-attention mechanisms.
- Core assumption: Patch-based processing with self-attention captures relevant features better than traditional CNNs for skin lesion classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Vision transformers-based GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks) are utilized to tackle the class imbalance."
  - [section] "Vision transformers are used in modern methodological approaches to classify images appropriately. Without using the framework's convolution layers, the ViT framework uses the transformer model with a series of picture speckles."

- Break condition: If patch size or embedding strategy fails to capture critical lesion features.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Explainable AI through GradCAM provides interpretable visualizations of model decision regions, increasing clinical trust.
- Mechanism: GradCAM computes gradients from the output concerning feature maps in the last convolutional layer to generate heatmaps highlighting important regions.
- Core assumption: Heatmap visualizations accurately represent the model's reasoning for classification decisions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The authors also implemented GradCAM for explainable AI and developed a web application for user-friendly deployment."
  - [section] "GradCAM is a technique used to visualize the input region to predict the lesion with the ViT model."

- Break condition: If heatmaps don't correlate with clinically relevant features or mislead clinicians.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the LesionAid framework perform when tested on real-time, in-the-wild skin lesion images captured by non-dermatologists?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions the framework's deployment as a web application for user-friendly access but does not provide validation on real-world images or user testing.
- Why unresolved: The current evaluation is based on the HAM10000 dataset, which may not fully represent real-world conditions or capture images taken by non-experts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the framework on a diverse dataset of real-time images taken by patients or non-dermatologists and comparing performance metrics like accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity with expert diagnoses.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the limitations of using ViT and ViTGAN for skin lesion classification, particularly regarding interpretability and potential biases in the generated synthetic data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the use of GradCAM for explainable AI but does not discuss potential limitations or biases in the synthetic data generated by ViTGAN or the interpretability of ViT models.
- Why unresolved: While GradCAM provides some interpretability, it does not fully explain the model's decision-making process or address potential biases in the synthetic data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a thorough analysis of the synthetic data for biases and performing ablation studies to understand the contribution of each component (ViT, ViTGAN, data augmentation) to the overall performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the LesionAid framework perform on skin lesions of different ethnicities and skin tones, which may have varying presentations and characteristics?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper does not mention any evaluation or consideration of the framework's performance on diverse skin tones or ethnicities.
- Why unresolved: Skin lesions can present differently across various skin tones, and the HAM10000 dataset may not be representative of all populations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the framework on a diverse dataset that includes images of skin lesions from individuals with different ethnicities and skin tones, and analyzing performance metrics for each group.

## Limitations

- Missing ViTGAN architecture and training parameter details
- No comparative analysis with baseline approaches to verify superiority claims
