# Optimization and Optimizers for Adversarial Robustness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13401
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13401
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces PWCF, a novel algorithmic framework for solving constrained optimization problems in adversarial robustness evaluation, addressing reliability and generality limitations of existing methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PWCF (PyGRANSO with Constraint-Folding), a novel algorithmic framework for solving constrained optimization problems in adversarial robustness evaluation. PWCF addresses two key limitations of existing numerical methods: lack of reliability in assessing solution quality and lack of generality in handling distance metrics beyond ℓ1, ℓ2, and ℓ∞. The framework combines the general-purpose constrained optimization solver PyGRANSO with a Constraint-Folding technique to improve efficiency and handle a wide range of distance metrics.

The authors demonstrate PWCF's effectiveness through experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-100 datasets, showing competitive attack performance in solving max-loss and min-radius forms. They also investigate the distinct patterns in solutions found using various combinations of losses, perturbation models, and optimization algorithms, highlighting implications for robustness evaluation and adversarial training. PWCF's reliability is ensured through stationarity measures and feasibility tests, while its generality is demonstrated by handling non-ℓp metrics like ℓ1.5, ℓ8, and perceptual distance.

## Method Summary
PWCF is a novel algorithmic framework that combines PyGRANSO, a general-purpose constrained optimization solver, with Constraint-Folding, a technique that reduces the number of constraints and improves efficiency. The framework reformulates ℓ∞ constraints and decouples objective and radius for ℓ1 and ℓ∞ distances to handle general distance metrics. PWCF provides reliable solutions through stationarity measures and feasibility tests, and can handle a wide range of distance metrics, including non-ℓp metrics.

The method involves adapting PyGRANSO with Constraint-Folding to solve constrained optimization problems in adversarial robustness evaluation, including max-loss form (maximize loss subject to perturbation constraints) and min-radius form (minimize perturbation radius subject to decision boundary crossing). The authors validate PWCF on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-100 datasets using ℓ1, ℓ2, ℓ∞ distances, comparing against APGD and FAB methods for max-loss and min-radius forms respectively. They also extend PWCF to handle general distance metrics and evaluate performance on RE problems.

## Key Results
- PWCF provides reliable solutions with stationarity measures and feasibility tests, addressing the lack of reliability in existing methods
- PWCF can handle a wide range of distance metrics, including non-ℓp metrics like ℓ1.5, ℓ8, and perceptual distance, addressing the lack of generality in existing methods
- Experiments show PWCF offers competitive attack performance in solving max-loss form with diverse solutions and provides competitive solutions to min-radius form

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PWCF works by leveraging the general-purpose constrained optimization solver PyGRANSO and introducing Constraint-Folding, which reduces the number of constraints and improves efficiency. This combination allows PWCF to handle general distance metrics beyond ℓ1, ℓ2, and ℓ∞, addressing the lack of generality in existing methods. Additionally, PWCF provides reliability through stationarity measures and feasibility tests, ensuring the quality of solutions found.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** PWCF (PyGRANSO + Constraint-Folding) -> Max-loss and min-radius forms -> ℓ1, ℓ2, ℓ∞, and general distance metrics -> CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-100 datasets

**Critical path:** Reformulate constraints and decouple objective and radius -> Apply PyGRANSO with Constraint-Folding -> Obtain solutions with stationarity measures and feasibility tests -> Handle general distance metrics

**Design tradeoffs:** PWCF trades off the specialization of existing methods for the generality and reliability of a general-purpose solver with Constraint-Folding. This allows handling a wider range of distance metrics but may be less efficient for specific cases.

**Failure signatures:** Poor scaling of objective and constraints leading to slow convergence, premature termination due to insufficient MaxIter, and suboptimal solutions due to the complexity of general distance metrics.
