# CitePrompt: Using Prompts to Identify Citation Intent in Scientific Papers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12730
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12730
- Reference count: 39
- State-of-the-art accuracy: 78.42% on ACL-ARC dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CitePrompt, a prompt-based learning framework for citation intent classification in scientific papers. The method leverages a manual prompt template to convert citation sentences into a natural language interface, which is then processed by SciBERT to predict citation intent labels. A verbalizer maps label words from an external knowledge base (S2ORC) to the label space. The framework achieves state-of-the-art results on ACL-ARC (78.42% accuracy, 68.39% F1) and competitive results on SciCite (87.56% accuracy, 86.33% F1). The paper also explores few-shot and zero-shot settings, demonstrating the model's ability to generalize with limited training data.

## Method Summary
CitePrompt employs a prompt-based learning approach where citation sentences are mapped to a natural language interface using a manual prompt template: "[X] It has a citation of type [MASK]." The masked position is processed by SciBERT, and a verbalizer selects the most probable label word from a set derived from the S2ORC knowledge base. The label words are selected based on their co-occurrence with specific citation types in scientific paper sections. The framework is evaluated on ACL-ARC (1,941 instances, 6 labels) and SciCite (11,020 instances, 3 labels) datasets, with training conducted over 5 epochs using batch size 40. The method also explores few-shot and zero-shot settings using knowledge-based prompt tuning (KPT) refinement techniques.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 78.42% and F1 of 68.39% on ACL-ARC dataset
- Achieves competitive accuracy of 87.56% and F1 of 86.33% on SciCite dataset
- Demonstrates zero-shot performance of 53.86% F1 and 10-shot performance of 66.99% F1 on SciCite dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: The prompt-based approach works by leveraging the pre-trained language understanding capabilities of SciBERT to map citation sentences to a natural language interface, where the masked position allows the model to predict citation intent labels. The verbalizer then translates these predictions into the target label space using label words selected from an external knowledge base (S2ORC) based on their co-occurrence patterns in scientific text. This combination of prompt-based learning and knowledge-based verbalization enables effective citation intent classification, particularly in few-shot and zero-shot settings where traditional fine-tuning would struggle.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Input Citation Sentences -> Prompt Template -> SciBERT -> Masked Prediction -> Verbalizer -> Label Output

**Critical Path**: The most critical path is from input citation sentences through the prompt template to SciBERT's masked prediction, as this directly determines the classification output. The verbalizer's role in mapping predictions to labels is also critical.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The manual prompt template design requires domain expertise but offers flexibility, while automated template strategies might reduce human effort but could compromise performance. The choice of SciBERT over BERT provides domain-specific advantages but limits generalizability to non-scientific domains.

**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation when using BERT instead of SciBERT (accuracy drops by 4.75% and F1 by 12.02% on ACL-ARC), and poor results in few-shot/zero-shot settings due to class imbalance on ACL-ARC dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of template design affect performance in prompt-based citation intent classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that prefix prompts outperform cloze prompts, but does not extensively explore alternative template designs or their impact on performance.
- Why unresolved: The study uses a fixed prefix prompt template without comparing it to other potential prompt designs or automated template strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing various prompt template designs (e.g., different prefix structures, cloze variants, automated template strategies) across multiple datasets would clarify the optimal template design.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the size and composition of the label word set on classification performance in different data regimes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a comparatively large set of label words selected from an external knowledge base, but does not systematically analyze how the size or composition of this set affects performance, particularly in few-shot and zero-shot settings.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions using a large label word set to ensure coverage and reduce bias, it does not explore how varying the number of label words or their selection criteria impacts model performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the size and composition of the label word set across different training data sizes would reveal optimal label word selection strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of prompt-based learning for citation intent classification generalize to other scientific domains or languages?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper tests on ACL-ARC and SciCite datasets (NLP and broader CS domains) and uses SciBERT (trained on CS and biomedical papers), but does not explore performance on other scientific domains or languages.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to English scientific papers in computer science and biomedical domains, leaving questions about cross-domain and cross-lingual generalizability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the prompt-based approach on citation intent datasets from other scientific domains (e.g., physics, social sciences) and languages would demonstrate its generalizability.

## Limitations
- Manual prompt template design requires domain expertise and may not transfer well to other domains
- Performance varies significantly between datasets, with ACL-ARC showing challenges in few-shot/zero-shot settings due to class imbalance
- Reliance on external knowledge bases (S2ORC) limits applicability to domains without similar resources

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Reported accuracy and F1 scores on standard settings (ACL-ARC and SciCite datasets)
- **Medium confidence**: Few-shot and zero-shot results due to limited specification of KPT refinement techniques
- **Low confidence**: Generalizability of manual prompt template and verbalizer construction process
