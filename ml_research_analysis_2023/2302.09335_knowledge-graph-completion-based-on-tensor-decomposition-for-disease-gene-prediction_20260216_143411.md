# Knowledge Graph Completion based on Tensor Decomposition for Disease Gene Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09335
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09335
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: KDGene achieves over 20% improvement in HR and MAP metrics compared to conventional KGE methods for disease gene prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of disease gene prediction by leveraging knowledge graph completion techniques. The authors construct a biological knowledge graph centered on diseases and genes, and propose a novel method called KDGene. KDGene introduces an interaction module between entity and relation embeddings to tensor decomposition, which enhances information interaction in biological knowledge and enables more precise representations of entities and relations. The primary results show that KDGene significantly outperforms state-of-the-art algorithms, achieving an average improvement of over 20% on HR and MAP metrics compared to conventional KGE methods.

## Method Summary
The KDGene method constructs a biological knowledge graph integrating multiple biomedical databases including DisGeNet for disease-gene associations, STRING for protein-protein interactions, GO for gene ontology, KEGG for pathways, and SymMap for disease-symptom relationships. The core innovation is an interaction module that applies LSTM-style gating mechanisms to enhance the information flow between entity and relation embeddings during tensor decomposition. Specifically, KDGene uses CP-N3 decomposition to represent the knowledge graph as a sum of rank-one tensors, with the interaction module treating head entity embeddings as hidden states and relation embeddings as inputs to LSTM cells. The model is trained end-to-end using multiclass log-loss with N3 regularization, and evaluated using 10-fold cross validation on the DisGeNet dataset.

## Key Results
- KDGene achieves over 20% improvement in HR@N and MAP@N metrics compared to conventional KGE methods
- Performance is particularly strong for diseases with limited known gene associations in the training data
- Case study on diabetes mellitus demonstrates KDGene's ability to identify new and accurate candidate genes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The gating mechanism-based interaction module enables more precise representations of entities and relations in the knowledge graph.
- Mechanism: By treating the head entity embedding as a hidden layer and the relation embedding as input to an LSTM cell, the interaction module allows the model to selectively retain relevant information while filtering out noise, similar to how LSTM gates control information flow in sequence models.
- Core assumption: The biological knowledge graph contains complex dependencies between entities and relations that benefit from gated information processing.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "KDGene introduces an interaction module between the embeddings of entities and relations to tensor decomposition, which can effectively enhance the information interaction in biological knowledge."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Tensor decomposition allows capturing higher-order interactions between entities and relations in the knowledge graph.
- Mechanism: CP-N3 decomposes the entity-relation tensor into a sum of rank-one tensors, which can capture complex, non-linear relationships between diseases and genes that simple linear methods miss.
- Core assumption: Disease-gene relationships are not simply pairwise but involve higher-order interactions that can be modeled through tensor decomposition.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.2] "CP-N3 [Lacroix et al., 2018] is based on CP decomposition [Hitchcock, 1927], which decomposes a high-order tensor X ∈ R^n1×n2×n3 into several r rank one tensors u_i ∈ R^n1, v_i ∈ R^n2, w_i ∈ R^n3."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using a biological knowledge graph with multiple relation types improves prediction accuracy by providing richer context.
- Mechanism: By incorporating diverse biological relationships (disease-symptom, protein-protein interactions, protein-GO, protein-pathway) beyond just disease-gene associations, the model can learn more comprehensive representations that capture the full biological context.
- Core assumption: Different types of biological relationships provide complementary information that helps disambiguate true disease-gene associations.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.1] "To learn more comprehensive representations of diseases and genes, we introduce the knowledge of different relation types to construct a biological KG."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Biological Knowledge Graph Construction -> Entity and Relation Embedding Layer -> Interaction Module -> Scoring Function -> Training Pipeline -> Prediction Layer

- Critical path: Knowledge Graph Construction → Embedding Learning → Interaction Module Processing → Scoring → Training → Prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. Expressiveness: Higher tensor ranks capture more complex relationships but require more memory
  - Training Time vs. Accuracy: The interaction module adds computational overhead but improves performance
  - KG Complexity vs. Noise: More relation types provide richer context but may introduce noise

- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: Poor performance on test set despite good training performance
  - Vanishing gradients: Training stalls or progresses very slowly
  - Mode collapse: Model consistently predicts the same genes regardless of input disease

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train KDGene on a simplified KG with only disease-gene relations to establish baseline performance
  2. Add disease-symptom relations and measure improvement in prediction accuracy
  3. Compare different interaction module variants (LSTM vs. GRU vs. RNN) to identify optimal gating mechanism

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of KDGene change when incorporating external biomedical knowledge sources beyond those used in the current study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that "When others use it, the disease-gene relation facts can be added or subtracted from the KG to complete the training according to the demand" and "As the amount of knowledge in biomedical databases grows, abundant facts about new types of relations can be continuously added to the KG."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical results demonstrating the impact of incorporating additional knowledge sources beyond the ones already used.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments evaluating KDGene's performance with various combinations of additional knowledge sources, comparing metrics like HR and MAP against the baseline performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between the number of different relation types and the confidence levels of the facts in the KG for maximizing KDGene's performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors evaluate KDGene's performance with different combinations of relations (KG1-KG6) and different confidence levels of PPI scores, showing that performance varies based on these factors.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how to optimally balance the number of relation types and their confidence levels for the best performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying both the number of relation types and their confidence levels, identifying the optimal combination for maximizing KDGene's performance metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of KDGene compare to other state-of-the-art methods when predicting disease-gene associations for diseases with limited known associations in the training data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates KDGene's performance on the DisGeNet dataset, but does not specifically address its performance on diseases with limited known associations.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of KDGene's performance on diseases with sparse training data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing KDGene's performance to other methods on a subset of diseases with limited known associations, analyzing metrics like HR and MAP to assess relative performance.

## Limitations
- The computational complexity of the interaction module may limit scalability to larger knowledge graphs
- Limited validation across diverse disease categories, with case study focused primarily on diabetes mellitus
- Lack of extensive ablation studies to isolate the contribution of individual components

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The experimental methodology and evaluation metrics (HR@N and MAP@N) are well-established in knowledge graph completion literature. The implementation of CP-N3 tensor decomposition and the overall model architecture appear technically sound based on the detailed descriptions provided.

**Medium Confidence:** The claim that KDGene outperforms state-of-the-art methods by 20% on average is supported by experimental results, but the paper lacks extensive ablation studies to isolate the contribution of individual components. The effectiveness of the LSTM-based interaction module compared to simpler alternatives remains partially validated.

**Low Confidence:** The paper's assertion that specific relation types (particularly disease-symptom associations) are universally beneficial for all disease gene prediction tasks is based on limited experimental evidence. The case study on diabetes mellitus, while illustrative, represents a single disease category and may not generalize to rare diseases or those with different biological mechanisms.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation Study on Relation Types:** Conduct experiments systematically removing each relation type from the knowledge graph to quantify their individual contributions to prediction performance, particularly testing whether disease-symptom associations consistently improve results across multiple disease categories.

2. **Scalability Assessment:** Evaluate KDGene's performance and training time on progressively larger knowledge graphs (10K, 100K, 1M entities) to determine practical limitations and identify at what scale the interaction module's computational overhead becomes prohibitive.

3. **Cross-Domain Validation:** Apply KDGene to knowledge graphs from different biological domains (e.g., drug-target interactions, protein function prediction) to assess whether the interaction module provides similar benefits outside the specific disease gene prediction task.
