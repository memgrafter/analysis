# How Robust is GPT-3.5 to Predecessors? A Comprehensive Study on Language Understanding Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00293
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00293
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GPT-3.5 experiences up to 35.74% robustness degradation in NLI and 43.59% in sentiment analysis under adversarial transformations

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates GPT-3.5's robustness across 21 datasets and 9 NLU tasks using 66 text transformations from TextFlint. While GPT-3.5 outperforms fine-tuned models on some tasks, it shows significant robustness degradation under adversarial perturbations. The research identifies key challenges including prompt sensitivity, number sensitivity, and inconsistent few-shot learning gains across tasks. The findings highlight the importance of instruction consistency and suggest directions for improving model generalization through better prompting strategies.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates GPT-3.5 models (text-davinci-003, text-davinci-002) across 21 datasets covering 9 NLU tasks including machine reading comprehension, relation extraction, natural language inference, semantic matching, aspect-based sentiment analysis, sentiment classification, named entity recognition, part-of-speech tagging, and the Winograd Schema Challenge. Performance is measured using task-specific metrics (F1, accuracy, EM score) in both zero-shot and few-shot scenarios (1/3/6/9-shot). Robustness is assessed using TextFlint's 66 text transformations, with results compared against GPT-3 and fine-tuned models.

## Key Results
- GPT-3.5 shows up to 35.74% performance drop in NLI and 43.59% drop in sentiment analysis under adversarial transformations
- Few-shot learning improves performance in most cases but robustness gains vary significantly by task
- GPT-3.5 exhibits high sensitivity to prompt variations, particularly for sentiment classification where neutral responses increase with prompt length
- The model is more sensitive to numerical inputs than fine-tuned models, with NumWord transformations causing disproportionate performance drops

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-3.5's robustness degrades significantly on adversarial transformations, especially for NLI and sentiment tasks.
- Mechanism: TextFlint transformations (AddSent, NumWord, SwapAnt) introduce perturbations that challenge the model's ability to maintain task-specific understanding, leading to up to 35.74% drop in NLI and 43.59% drop in sentiment analysis.
- Core assumption: The model's instruction tuning stage makes it sensitive to prompt and task label consistency.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "significant robustness degradation, such as its average performance dropping by up to 35.74% and 43.59% in natural language inference and sentiment analysis tasks"
  - [section 4.3.1] "the AddSent transformation shows slightly lower robustness in the 0/1/3-shot models compared to the fine-tuned models"
- Break condition: If instruction tuning incorporates robustness to label and prompt variations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Few-shot learning improves performance but robustness gains are task-dependent.
- Mechanism: In-context examples help the model learn task patterns, but the effectiveness varies—strong for ABSA but weaker or even negative for NLI/SM.
- Core assumption: The number of examples and their quality influence the model's ability to generalize under perturbations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Few-shot learning improves performance in most cases, but robustness gains vary"
  - [section 4.4.1] "in-context learning has been demonstrated to significantly enhance the model's robustness and ability to handle transformed data"
- Break condition: If prompt examples are irrelevant or noisy, robustness may degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-3.5 is more sensitive to numerical inputs than fine-tuned models.
- Mechanism: Transformations like NumWord (replacing words with numbers) cause disproportionate performance drops, suggesting less robustness to numeric variations.
- Core assumption: The model's pretraining or instruction tuning did not adequately cover numeric robustness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "number sensitivity" as a specific robustness challenge
  - [section 4.3] "GPT-3.5 is more sensitive to numerical inputs than pre-training fine-tuning models"
- Break condition: If robustness to numeric perturbations is explicitly targeted in future training.

  - Why needed here: Evaluating model stability under perturbed inputs (TextFlint) is key to assessing real-world reliability.

  - Why needed here: GPT-3.5's performance is highly dependent on prompt wording, affecting both accuracy and robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different prompt construction strategies affect the robustness of GPT-3.5 models across NLU tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that prompt sensitivity is a significant robustness challenge and that changes in input prompts have a significant impact on results. It suggests further research into more effective prompting strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper tested multiple prompts per dataset but did not systematically analyze how different prompt construction methods (e.g., prompt phrasing, example selection, label formatting) specifically impact robustness outcomes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing robustness metrics across systematically varied prompt construction approaches (e.g., different prompt phrasings, label formats, or example selection strategies) while holding model and task constant.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between instruction consistency during instruction tuning and robustness in zero-shot/few-shot scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes that ensuring consistency of task labels and label types between instruction tuning and application stages is crucial, and suggests designing effective prompts that guarantee this consistency.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies this as important, it does not empirically investigate how mismatches between instruction tuning data and application prompts affect robustness, or what specific aspects of instruction consistency matter most.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing model robustness when using prompts/instructions that match vs. mismatch the instruction tuning stage, and analysis of which specific aspects of instruction consistency (label types, task framing, output formats) have the strongest effects.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the robustness of GPT-3.5 models vary across different types of text transformations and what underlying factors explain these differences?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that robustness varies across different transformations (e.g., AddSent, NumWord, SwapAnt) and that GPT-3.5 exhibits different sensitivity levels to numerical inputs versus other perturbations.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies that robustness varies but does not provide a systematic framework for understanding why certain transformations affect robustness more than others, or what linguistic/semantic properties of transformations drive these differences.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis correlating transformation characteristics (e.g., syntactic vs. semantic changes, local vs. global modifications, perturbation severity) with robustness degradation patterns across multiple tasks and models.

## Limitations
- Prompt variability and lack of fully specified prompt templates make exact replication challenging
- Evaluation focuses on 66 TextFlint transformations which may not capture all real-world adversarial scenarios
- Comparison between GPT-3.5 and fine-tuned models could be influenced by differences beyond instruction tuning
- Does not address potential dataset-specific biases that might affect robustness measurements

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that GPT-3.5 experiences significant robustness degradation under adversarial transformations (up to 35.74% for NLI and 43.59% for sentiment analysis) is well-supported by the reported experimental results across multiple datasets and transformations.

**Medium Confidence**: The finding that few-shot learning improves performance but with task-dependent robustness gains is reasonably supported, though the specific conditions under which robustness improves or degrades need more detailed analysis. The claim about GPT-3.5's sensitivity to numerical inputs is supported but could benefit from more extensive testing across diverse numeric scenarios.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that GPT-3.5 is more sensitive to prompt variations than fine-tuned models, while observed, lacks direct comparative analysis with fine-tuned models under identical prompt variations. The study shows prompt sensitivity exists but doesn't establish it's worse than alternatives.

## Next Checks

1. **Prompt Sensitivity Replication**: Test IMDB sentiment classification with systematically varied prompt lengths and phrasings to quantify how often "neutral" responses occur and whether this pattern holds across different sentiment datasets.

2. **Transformation Impact Analysis**: Isolate and test the specific TextFlint transformations (particularly NumWord and AddSent) that cause the largest performance drops in NLI tasks to determine if these effects are consistent across different NLI datasets beyond the ones tested.

3. **Few-Shot Robustness Consistency**: Conduct a controlled experiment comparing zero-shot, 1-shot, and 3-shot performance on the same dataset with identical transformations to verify that the reported task-dependent robustness gains hold when shot count is the only variable changed.
