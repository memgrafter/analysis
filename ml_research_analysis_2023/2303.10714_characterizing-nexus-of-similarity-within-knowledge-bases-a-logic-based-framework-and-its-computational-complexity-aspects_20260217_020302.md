# Characterizing Nexus of Similarity within Knowledge Bases: A Logic-based Framework and its Computational Complexity Aspects

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10714
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10714
- Reference count: 33
- Characterizes similarity between entity tuples in knowledge bases using logic-based framework

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a formal logic-based framework to characterize the nexus of similarity between tuples of entities within knowledge bases. The framework introduces nearly connected formulas as a novel explanation language to formally and automatically express shared properties between entity tuples. The authors analyze computational complexity aspects of this framework, showing that key reasoning tasks range from NP-complete to coNEXP-complete depending on input size and structure.

## Method Summary
The framework consists of selective knowledge bases (SKB) composed of a dataset and summary selector, a nexus explanation language (NXL) using nearly connected formulas to characterize similarity, expansion graphs to classify tuples by similarity to an initial unit, and various reasoning tasks with analyzed computational complexity. The core method builds canonic characterizations (BuildCan) and core characterizations (BuildCore) to formally represent shared properties between entity tuples.

## Key Results
- Introduced nearly connected formulas as a formalism to express nexus of similarity between entity tuples
- Developed expansion graphs to provide a structured taxonomy of similarity characterizations
- Analyzed computational complexity showing tasks range from NP-complete to coNEXP-complete
- Established formal reference semantics for evaluating similarity approaches on weakly similar entities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The logic-based framework provides formal, machine-readable characterizations of similarity via nearly connected formulas.
- **Mechanism**: Nearly connected formulas encode semantic connections between entities by ensuring each atom has a (direct or indirect) link to a free variable, capturing shared properties while excluding irrelevant ones.
- **Core assumption**: The summaries selected by the summary selector accurately reflect the relevant properties of entities for the task at hand.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "developing a novel logic-based framework to formally and automatically characterize nexus of similarity between tuples of entities within a knowledge base."
  - [section]: "Nearly connected formulas are the formalism we adopt to express nexus of similarity between the tuples of a unit (i.e., properties shared by such tuples) w.r.t. their summaries."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; this is a novel contribution not reflected in neighbors.
- **Break condition**: If the summary selector fails to capture relevant properties, the characterizations become incomplete or misleading.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The expansion graph provides a structured taxonomy of similarity, enabling comparison and expansion of entity sets.
- **Mechanism**: The expansion graph classifies tuples by their similarity to an initial unit, with nodes representing characterizations and arcs showing refinement. This structure allows navigation to find more/less similar entities.
- **Core assumption**: The characterization function can be computed and is unique for each unit, providing a well-defined taxonomy.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Furthermore, we analyze computational complexity aspects of this framework."
  - [section]: "Expansion graphs provide a natural bridge from nexus characterizations to semantic similarity and set expansion."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; expansion graphs are novel to this work.
- **Break condition**: If characterizations are not unique or cannot be computed efficiently, the expansion graph becomes intractable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The framework enables fair evaluation of similarity approaches by providing a formal reference semantics.
- **Mechanism**: By providing a formal characterization of similarity, the framework allows comparison of different approaches against a well-defined standard, addressing the lack of consensus on evaluating weakly similar entities.
- **Core assumption**: The formal characterization accurately reflects human judgments of similarity.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "As a first step towards filling these gaps, we aim to complement existing literature by developing a novel logic-based framework to formally and automatically characterize nexus of similarity between tuples of entities within a knowledge base."
  - [section]: "Accordingly, machines are largely able to deal with both similarity measures and set expansions."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; evaluation fairness is a novel contribution.
- **Break condition**: If the formal characterization diverges significantly from human similarity judgments, the evaluation framework loses validity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the computational complexity of characterizing nexus of similarity scale when the arity of the unit exceeds the typical values used in semantic similarity tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses complexity analysis for different assumptions (general, medial, agile) and notes that arity is typically small in semantic similarity tasks, but doesn't provide specific complexity results for higher arity cases
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on cases where arity is bounded by constants, leaving open the question of how complexity grows as arity increases beyond these bounds
- What evidence would resolve it: Complexity analysis specifically targeting higher arity cases (e.g., k > 5) showing whether the problem remains in P or becomes NP-hard

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the explanation language NXL be extended to handle controlled forms of disjunction while maintaining tractability?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses design choices for NXL, explicitly noting that disjunction is avoided as it tends to go beyond semantic connections, but suggests investigating controlled forms of disjunction as future work
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as a potential direction but doesn't provide formal analysis of what controlled disjunctions might be tractable
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal complexity analysis of extended NXL variants with controlled disjunction, showing whether they remain in P or become harder

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of the proposed framework compare to existing semantic similarity measures when applied to real-world knowledge graphs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the theoretical framework and complexity but doesn't provide empirical evaluation on real knowledge graphs like DBpedia or BabelNet
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on theoretical foundations and computational complexity without implementing or benchmarking the framework on actual data
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the framework's performance to existing similarity measures on standard knowledge graphs, including runtime and accuracy metrics

## Limitations

- Computational complexity of reasoning tasks ranges from NP-complete to coNEXP-complete, suggesting significant scalability limitations for large knowledge bases
- The framework lacks empirical validation on real-world knowledge graphs, making practical utility uncertain
- The impact of different summary selector strategies on characterization quality is not systematically evaluated

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The formal logic framework and definition of nearly connected formulas
- **Medium confidence**: The expansion graph taxonomy and its properties
- **Medium confidence**: The complexity analysis and classification of reasoning tasks
- **Low confidence**: The practical applicability and scalability of the framework for large knowledge bases
