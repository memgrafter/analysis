# A step towards the applicability of algorithms based on invariant causal learning on observational data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02286
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02286
- Reference count: 29
- One-line primary result: ICP with decision tree environment generation achieves 100% TPR and ≤4% FDR on simpler datasets, outperforming traditional methods

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of causal discovery from observational data by generating "environments" that represent different interventions, enabling application of invariant causal prediction (ICP) algorithms. The proposed method uses supervised clustering via decision trees to create environments with dataset shifts between them, satisfying conditions for invariant causal property testing. The method is applied to simulated datasets to evaluate its effectiveness, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional causal discovery methods like FCI and Lingam.

## Method Summary
The method generates environments by applying supervised clustering via decision trees, where each tree is built to predict a target variable from covariates. The resulting leaf nodes represent environments with distinct joint distributions P(X,Y). For each environment, the invariant causal property is tested by examining whether the conditional distribution P(Y|X) remains stable across environments. ICP is then applied to identify causal parents by testing all subsets of variables for invariance across the generated environments. An improved variant (ICPv2) is introduced for complex datasets with multiple covariates, using a voting mechanism to select causal parents.

## Key Results
- ICP with the proposed method achieves 100% True Positive Rates and low False Discovery Rates (≤4%) on simpler datasets
- The method outperforms traditional methods like FCI and Lingam on simpler datasets
- For complex datasets with multiple covariates, ICPv2 maintains high performance (TPR: 73-100%, FDR: 8-21%) while standard methods degrade

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Supervised clustering via decision trees can generate environments with dataset shifts between them, satisfying conditions for invariant causal property testing.
- Mechanism: Decision trees maximize heterogeneity between nodes by optimizing splits based on the joint distribution of inputs and target variable. Each final node represents an environment with distinct P(X,Y) from other nodes.
- Core assumption: The invariant causal property holds - conditional distribution of target given causal parents remains identical across environments despite changes in joint distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proposed method uses supervised clustering via decision trees to create environments with dataset shifts between them, satisfying conditions for invariant causal property testing."
  - [section] "The environments are in other words latent clusters that are unknown. Since we have addressed the necessity to generate a dataset shift between them we propose a supervised clustering approach..."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers mention invariant causal prediction and environment generation but don't directly validate the decision tree approach for this specific purpose.
- Break condition: If the decision tree cannot find splits that create sufficiently different joint distributions between environments, the method fails to generate valid environments for causal discovery.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ICP with the proposed environment generation method achieves high True Positive Rates (100%) and low False Discovery Rates (≤4%) on simpler datasets.
- Mechanism: The method generates valid environments, then ICP tests all subsets of variables for invariance across these environments. Variables present in all invariant subsets are selected as causal parents.
- Core assumption: The environments generated by the decision tree method satisfy the invariant causal property assumptions required by ICP.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show ICP with the proposed method achieves 100% True Positive Rates and low False Discovery Rates (≤4%) on simpler datasets, outperforming traditional methods like FCI and Lingam."
  - [section] "Looking at the results in table 1 we can see how ICP can compete with the other methods with True Positive Rates of 100%..."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers discuss invariant causal prediction performance but don't specifically validate the combination with decision tree environment generation.
- Break condition: If the environment generation fails to create valid environments, ICP will not find the correct causal parents, leading to low TPR and high FDR.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: For complex datasets with multiple covariates, the improved ICP variant (ICPv2) maintains high performance (TPR: 73-100%, FDR: 8-21%) while standard methods degrade.
- Mechanism: ICPv2 limits subset size to N=5, applies voting approach with confidence intervals to select causal parents, making it more robust to the increased complexity and conservatism of standard ICP.
- Core assumption: The voting approach with limited subset size and confidence intervals can effectively filter out spurious causal candidates while retaining true causal parents in complex settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For complex datasets with multiple covariates, an improved ICP variant (ICPv2) maintains high performance (TPR: 73-100%, FDR: 8-21%), while standard methods degrade."
  - [section] "Since we have performed multiple checks we propose a voting approach. It consists of counting the number of each possible candidate..."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers mention improvements to invariant causal prediction but don't specifically validate the voting approach for complex datasets.
- Break condition: If the voting threshold is too strict, true causal parents may be missed; if too lenient, false positives increase, degrading FDR.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Decision tree environment generation -> Environment validation -> ICP subset testing -> Causal parent selection
- Critical path: Decision tree environment generation → Environment validation → ICP subset testing → Causal parent selection
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of environments: More environments provide better validation but increase computational cost
  - Tree depth: Deeper trees may create more homogeneous environments but risk overfitting
  - Voting threshold: Higher thresholds reduce false positives but may miss true causal parents
- Failure signatures:
  - Low TPR: Decision trees failing to create environments with sufficient dataset shift
  - High FDR: Environments not truly representing different interventions, violating invariant property
  - Slow performance: Excessive number of environments or subsets being tested
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test environment generation on simple linear dataset with known causal structure, verify dataset shift between environments using KS test
  2. Apply standard ICP to the generated environments, compare TPR/FDR against ground truth
  3. Apply ICPv2 to complex dataset with multiple covariates, compare performance against standard ICP and other causal discovery methods

## Limitations

- The method relies heavily on the assumption that decision tree clustering can reliably generate environments with sufficient dataset shift while maintaining the invariant causal property, which is not extensively validated across diverse data distributions.
- The paper does not address scalability concerns when dealing with high-dimensional data or the sensitivity of results to hyperparameter choices like tree depth and number of environments.
- The reliance on synthetic datasets with known causal structures limits the generalizability of findings to real-world observational data with complex, unknown causal relationships.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The decision tree-based environment generation mechanism and its application to ICP are clearly described and implemented.
- Medium Confidence: Performance claims for ICPv1 on simpler datasets (TPR: 100%, FDR ≤4%) are supported by results but may not generalize to more complex scenarios.
- Low Confidence: The effectiveness of ICPv2's voting mechanism for handling multiple covariates is demonstrated on synthetic data but lacks validation on real-world datasets or ablation studies to isolate the impact of individual components.
