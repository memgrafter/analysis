# Exploring Large Language Models for Classical Philology

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13698
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13698
- Reference count: 35
- This paper introduces four pre-trained language models for Ancient Greek and Latin, including the first encoder-decoder models for these languages, and demonstrates their superiority on morphological and syntactic tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces four pre-trained language models for Ancient Greek and Latin, including the first encoder-decoder models for these languages. The models vary in architecture (BERT vs. T5) and training data (monolingual vs. trilingual). The authors evaluate the models on morphological and syntactic tasks like POS tagging, dependency parsing, and lemmatization, as well as two semantic probing tasks. Results show that the proposed models significantly outperform prior state-of-the-art models for Ancient Greek and Latin. T5-based models excel at lemmatization and semantic probing, while monolingual models perform best on monolingual morphological and syntactic tasks.

## Method Summary
The study pre-trains four base-sized models: ROBERTA variants (GRεBERTA, PHILBERTA) and T5 variants (GRεTA, PHILTA) on curated Ancient Greek, Latin, and English corpora. Pre-training uses MLM objectives with ROBERTA's dynamic masking and T5's encoder-decoder architecture. Models are fine-tuned on downstream tasks using Universal Dependencies treebanks and EvaLatin datasets, employing multi-task learning for morphological tasks and sequence-to-sequence approaches for lemmatization.

## Key Results
- T5-based models significantly outperform ROBERTA variants on lemmatization and semantic probing tasks
- Monolingual models achieve better performance on monolingual morphological and syntactic tasks
- Multilingual models provide competitive performance while enabling cross-lingual transfer between Ancient Greek and Latin

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: T5-based models excel at lemmatization due to their encoder-decoder architecture enabling sequence-to-sequence transformation
- Mechanism: The encoder processes the inflected word in context while the decoder generates the lemma directly, allowing the model to handle irregular forms through learned editing rules rather than fixed patterns
- Core assumption: The model can learn the complex morphological transformations from context without explicit rule programming
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "T5-based models excel at lemmatization and semantic probing"
  - [section 3.2] "we propose a novel T5-based lemmatization model that is (i) contextualized... (ii) The model works end-to-end"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence, but the model's architecture enables this capability
- Break condition: If the encoder-decoder architecture fails to capture the morphological patterns or if the decoder cannot generate accurate lemmas from context

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multilingual models provide competitive performance despite capacity splitting across languages
- Mechanism: Cross-lingual transfer allows knowledge from related languages (Latin to Ancient Greek) to improve performance, while the shared vocabulary space enables semantic alignment
- Core assumption: Ancient Greek and Latin share enough linguistic features for beneficial transfer learning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "PHIL BERTA and PHILTA are their trilingual counterparts pre-trained on Greek as well as Latin and English data"
  - [section 4.2] "we explore the implications of multilinguality for AG language models"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence, but the multilingual pre-training data supports this mechanism
- Break condition: If the language pairs are too dissimilar or if capacity splitting significantly degrades monolingual task performance

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ROBERTA's dynamic masking strategy provides better utilization of pre-training data compared to BERT's static masking
- Mechanism: Dynamic masking ensures different tokens are masked in each epoch, preventing the model from overfitting to specific masking patterns and improving generalization
- Core assumption: Static masking in BERT leads to suboptimal learning due to repeated exposure to the same masking patterns
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we chose ROBERTA over BERT: its dynamic masking strategy exploits the pre-training data better"
  - [section 3.3] "ROBERTA (Liu et al., 2019) and T5 (Raffel et al., 2020) as strong model types"
  - [corpus] Strong - Liu et al. 2019 provides empirical evidence for ROBERTA's advantages
- Break condition: If the dynamic masking doesn't provide significant benefits over static masking or if it introduces instability in training

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Encoder-only models (ROBERTA): Input → Encoder → Classification head(s)
  - Encoder-decoder models (T5): Input → Encoder → Decoder → Output
  - Multilingual models: Shared vocabulary space with language-specific adaptations

- Critical path: Pre-training → Fine-tuning → Evaluation
  1. Pre-training on curated Ancient Greek corpus
  2. Fine-tuning on downstream tasks (POS tagging, parsing, lemmatization)
  3. Evaluation against baselines and state-of-the-art

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Monolingual vs. multilingual: Performance vs. versatility
  - Encoder-only vs. encoder-decoder: Simplicity vs. task flexibility
  - Model size vs. training data availability: Base models chosen for resource constraints

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on syntactic tasks: Encoder may not capture sufficient context
  - Inaccurate lemmatization: Decoder may not learn correct morphological transformations
  - Multilingual interference: Capacity splitting may degrade monolingual performance

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare ROBERTA and T5 performance on lemmatization task to validate encoder-decoder advantage
  2. Test multilingual models on monolingual Ancient Greek tasks to measure cross-lingual interference
  3. Evaluate knowledge probing tasks to assess semantic understanding capabilities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the encoders in encoder-decoder models like T5 compare to those in encoder-only models like BERT when trained on the same data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that GRETA-ENC performs slightly worse than GRεBERTA despite similar architecture, suggesting the encoder-decoder architecture may impact encoder performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a direct comparison between encoders from different architectures trained under identical conditions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing encoders from T5 vs. BERT models trained on the same data with the same objectives would clarify if the encoder-decoder architecture inherently affects encoder performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does multilingual training improve semantic and world knowledge acquisition for Ancient Greek models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors compare monolingual and multilingual models on semantic probing tasks but note the datasets are too small to draw firm conclusions.
- Why unresolved: The probing tasks used are limited in size and scope, making it difficult to assess the full impact of multilingual training on knowledge acquisition.
- What evidence would resolve it: Larger, more diverse semantic and knowledge probing datasets for Ancient Greek would allow for more definitive conclusions about multilingual training effects.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does character-level information impact lemmatization performance for Ancient Greek?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors find that providing individual characters to GRεTA slightly improves lemmatization performance, suggesting character information is valuable.
- Why unresolved: The study only explores this for T5-based models and doesn't compare different tokenization approaches systematically.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of wordpiece-based models with and without character embeddings, as well as byte-based models, would clarify the impact of character-level information on lemmatization.

## Limitations

- Claims about T5 superiority rely on comparison against non-contextualized baselines rather than modern contextualized models
- Multilingual model benefits may reflect increased training data rather than genuine cross-lingual transfer
- Limited analysis of which specific linguistic features enable transfer between Ancient Greek and Latin

## Confidence

- High confidence: Basic architectural comparisons and evaluation framework are methodologically sound
- Medium confidence: Architectural advantage claims lack direct ablation studies
- Low confidence: Cross-lingual transfer benefits are primarily correlational rather than causal
