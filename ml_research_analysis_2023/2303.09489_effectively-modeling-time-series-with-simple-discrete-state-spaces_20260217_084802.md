# Effectively Modeling Time Series with Simple Discrete State Spaces

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09489
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09489
- Reference count: 40
- SpaceTime achieves state-of-the-art results on 14/16 Informer forecasting tasks and 6/7 ECG and speech classification tasks

## Executive Summary
SpaceTime is a state-space model specifically designed for time series modeling that achieves state-of-the-art performance on forecasting and classification benchmarks. It introduces three key innovations: a companion matrix parameterization that enables direct modeling of autoregressive processes, a closed-loop SSM variant for flexible long-horizon forecasting, and an efficient inference algorithm that reduces computational complexity. The model outperforms prior methods on diverse benchmarks while demonstrating better generalization to new horizons and faster training than Transformers and LSTMs.

## Method Summary
SpaceTime uses companion matrix state-space models (SSMs) to directly model autoregressive processes fundamental to time series. The companion matrix enables the SSM to represent discrete-time AR processes through simple state-space recursion without nonlinear transformations. For long-horizon forecasting, SpaceTime employs a closed-loop SSM variant where the decoder jointly models output predictions and future inputs via a K matrix, enabling recursive generation of future steps. The model achieves O(d+ℓ) computational complexity through an efficient inference algorithm that leverages Fourier domain computation via Woodbury's formula, reducing the memory and compute of forward passes compared to naive O(dℓ) approaches.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on 14/16 Informer forecasting tasks with best MSE
- Outperforms prior methods on 6/7 ECG and speech classification tasks with best AUROC
- Demonstrates better generalization to new horizons compared to fixed-horizon models
- Trains faster than Transformers and LSTMs while maintaining superior accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The companion matrix parameterization allows direct modeling of autoregressive (AR) processes fundamental to time series.
- Mechanism: The companion matrix enables the SSM to represent discrete-time AR processes via simple state-space recursion without needing nonlinear transformations.
- Core assumption: Autoregressive processes are a foundational building block for modeling many time series behaviors including seasonality and trends.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We prove that the companion matrix SSM recovers fundamental autoregressive (AR) and smoothing processes modeled in classical techniques such as ARIMA and ETS"
  - [section] "To allow (3) to express (4), we need the hidden state xk to carry information about past samples"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The closed-loop SSM variant enables flexible long-horizon forecasting without requiring separate training for each horizon.
- Mechanism: The decoder layer jointly models both output predictions and future inputs via a K matrix, allowing recursive generation of many future steps from a single constant-size model.
- Core assumption: Future inputs can be predicted from current state, enabling autoregressive generation through the network.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For long horizon forecasting, we introduce a 'closed-loop' variation of the companion SSM, which enables SpaceTime to predict many future time-steps by generating its own layer-wise inputs"
  - [section] "We thus train the decoder SpaceTime layer to explicitly model its own next time-step inputs with A, B, K"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The efficient inference algorithm reduces computational complexity from O(dℓ) to O(d+ℓ) through Fourier domain computation.
- Mechanism: The companion matrix structure (shift + rank-1) enables Woodbury's formula to compute resolvents in the Fourier domain rather than directly powering the matrix.
- Core assumption: The companion matrix's special structure allows algebraic simplifications not available for general matrices.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For efficient training and inference, we introduce an algorithm that reduces the memory and compute of a forward pass with the companion matrix. With sequence length ℓ and state-space size d, we go from O(dℓ) naïvely to O(d + ℓ)"
  - [section] "This resolvent can be shown analytically to be a lower-triangular matrix consisting of roots of unity"

- Design tradeoffs:

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific theoretical limitations prevent continuous-time LSSL SSMs from representing AR(p) processes, and can these limitations be overcome with alternative continuous-time representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proves in Proposition 2 that continuous-time LSSL SSMs cannot represent noiseless AR(p) processes due to the non-existence of matrix logarithms for singular matrices.
- Why unresolved: The proof demonstrates impossibility but does not explore whether modified continuous-time formulations or hybrid discrete/continuous approaches could overcome this fundamental limitation.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical proof showing either (1) all possible continuous-time SSM representations cannot capture AR processes, or (2) a specific continuous-time formulation that can represent AR processes while maintaining practical trainability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the companion matrix representation scale to extremely high-dimensional time series (e.g., high-frequency financial data with thousands of features), and what are the practical computational bottlenecks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates O(d + ℓ) complexity for the companion matrix SSM but does not extensively evaluate performance on very high-dimensional data or discuss memory constraints in such settings.
- Why unresolved: The theoretical complexity analysis assumes moderate state dimensions, but real-world high-dimensional time series may encounter GPU memory limitations or require specialized optimizations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical scaling studies showing memory usage and training times for companion SSMs on datasets with 100+ features, along with benchmarks comparing different implementation strategies (e.g., sparse vs dense representations).

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between the companion matrix SSM's autoregressive modeling capability and its performance on non-stationary time series with structural breaks or regime changes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows the companion SSM can represent ARIMA and exponential smoothing processes, but does not explicitly test performance on time series with sudden regime changes or structural breaks.
- Why unresolved: While the companion SSM captures autoregressive dynamics, real-world time series often exhibit non-stationarity that may require adaptive or hierarchical modeling approaches not explored in the current work.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments on datasets with known structural breaks (e.g., economic indicators during financial crises) showing whether the companion SSM maintains accuracy or requires augmentation with change-point detection mechanisms.

## Limitations

- The companion matrix approach's expressivity for complex non-linear time series dynamics remains uncertain, with limited analysis of failure cases where AR process assumptions break down
- The efficient inference algorithm's practical performance may degrade for small sequence lengths or when state-space dimension approaches sequence length
- The closed-loop forecasting mechanism's effectiveness in highly stochastic environments where future inputs cannot be predicted from state is not thoroughly evaluated

## Confidence

**Low Confidence** in the O(d+ℓ) complexity claims without detailed implementation specifics
