# Curriculum-guided Abstractive Summarization for Mental Health Online Posts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00954
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00954
- Reference count: 17
- This paper proposes a curriculum learning approach to improve abstractive summarization of mental health online posts, achieving relative improvements of 3.5% (ROUGE-1), 10.4% (ROUGE-2), and 4.7% (ROUGE-L), and 1.5% (BERTSCORE) over BART baseline.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a curriculum learning strategy for abstractive summarization of mental health-related posts from Reddit. The approach uses a confidence-aware SUPER LOSS function that dynamically upweights easier training samples based on task loss and a confidence parameter, thereby improving the learning efficiency and summarization quality. Evaluated on the MENT SUM dataset, the method shows significant improvements over a strong BART baseline in multiple metrics, highlighting the value of curriculum learning for domain-specific summarization tasks involving mental health content.

## Method Summary
The approach builds on BART and applies a curriculum learning strategy via SUPER LOSS, which dynamically reweights training samples to emphasize easier examples. The confidence parameter σ and regularization λ control the weighting, and the model is trained for 8 epochs using AdamW. The method is evaluated on the MENT SUM dataset, a collection of 24k post-summary pairs from 43 mental health subreddits, using ROUGE and BERTScore metrics.

## Key Results
- Relative improvement of 3.5% in ROUGE-1 over BART baseline
- Relative improvement of 10.4% in ROUGE-2 over BART baseline
- Relative improvement of 4.7% in ROUGE-L and 1.5% in BERTScore over BART baseline

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by dynamically adjusting the importance of training samples based on their difficulty, as measured by the task loss and confidence. This reweighting focuses the model's attention on easier samples, which can accelerate learning and improve generalization. The confidence-aware SUPER LOSS formulation helps the model adapt its training focus over time, which is particularly beneficial for the complex, nuanced language found in mental health posts.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Dataset (MENT SUM) → Preprocessing → BART Model → SUPER LOSS Wrapper → Optimizer (AdamW) → Validation Metrics (ROUGE/BERTScore)
- **Critical Path**: Data loading → Preprocessing → Model forward pass with SUPER LOSS weighting → Backpropagation → Validation checkpointing
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Using dynamic sample weighting can speed convergence but requires careful tuning of σ and λ; simpler static weighting would be less adaptive but easier to tune.
- **Failure Signatures**: Loss divergence if σ* or λ mis-specified; poor metric gains if weighting is too uniform or too aggressive; overfitting if validation monitoring is disabled.
- **First Experiments**: (1) Verify SUPER LOSS reweights samples as intended during a single training step; (2) Compare training loss curves with and without curriculum weighting; (3) Evaluate impact of different λ values on validation performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: How does curriculum learning effectiveness vary across different mental health subreddits with distinct linguistic patterns and severity levels?
  - The paper doesn't analyze performance differences across the 43 subreddits, missing potential insights into community-specific benefits.
- **Open Question 2**: What is the optimal confidence parameter σ for different mental health post types, and how does it affect faithfulness vs. conciseness?
  - The study uses a single λ value and doesn't explore σ tuning or its impact on summarization qualities.
- **Open Question 3**: How does curriculum learning impact handling of novel mental health terminology not present in training data?
  - The paper doesn't test generalization to unseen terminology, focusing instead on overall metric improvements.

## Limitations
- Exact values for SUPER LOSS hyperparameter λ and training batch size are unspecified, affecting reproducibility.
- Improvements in ROUGE-1 and ROUGE-L are modest, with largest gains in ROUGE-2, suggesting the method may better capture fine-grained details than overall coherence.
- The dataset is domain-specific and requires a usage agreement, limiting broader experimental validation.

## Confidence
- Medium: Clear quantitative gains over BART baseline are demonstrated, but exact hyperparameter settings are missing, and validation is limited to automated metrics without deeper qualitative or generalization analysis.
