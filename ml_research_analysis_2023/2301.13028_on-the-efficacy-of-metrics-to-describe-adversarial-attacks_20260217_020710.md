# On the Efficacy of Metrics to Describe Adversarial Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13028
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13028
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 1
- Key outcome: Image quality metrics can predict adversarial detector outputs with 0.94 accuracy, outperforming L-norms alone

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the conventional use of Lp norms as the primary metric for characterizing adversarial attacks, arguing that they provide insufficient information about perturbation characteristics. Through extensive experiments on CIFAR-10 using 10 different attack methods and two state-of-the-art detectors (MagNet and Squeezer), the authors demonstrate that a combination of 12 metrics - including 4 Lp norms and 8 image quality metrics - can predict detector responses with approximately 0.94 accuracy. The study reveals that attacks can be grouped based on similar perturbations and detection outcomes, potentially enabling more efficient defense evaluation by selecting representative attacks from each group.

## Method Summary
The authors generate adversarial images using 10 different attack methods (7 white-box, 3 black-box) on CIFAR-10 test images, resulting in 18,062 adversarial images. They compute 12 metrics (4 Lp norms and 8 image quality metrics) for each image, then train two detectors (MagNet and Squeezer) on CIFAR-10. A Random Forest classifier is trained using the computed metrics as features and detector outputs as labels. The study performs leave-one-out analysis to classify attacks based on perturbation patterns and evaluates the predictive power of different metric combinations.

## Key Results
- Image quality metrics combined with Lp norms predict adversarial detector outputs with 0.94 accuracy
- L2 and L∞ norms provide complementary information when used together
- Attack grouping based on similar perturbations and detection outcomes is feasible
- PSNR-B and VIFP are the most informative metrics for predicting detector responses

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Image quality metrics can predict adversarial detector outputs with high accuracy because they capture perturbation characteristics that L-norms miss. The Random Forest classifier uses multiple image quality metrics (MSE, PSNR-B, VIF, etc.) as features to correlate with detector outputs. These metrics provide richer information about the perturbation's perceptual impact and spatial distribution than single L-norms. Core assumption: The 12 selected metrics are sufficiently descriptive to capture all relevant perturbation characteristics that affect detector behavior. Evidence anchors: Strong correlation observed between metrics and detector outputs (0.94 accuracy).

### Mechanism 2
Different attacks can be grouped based on similar perturbations and detection outcomes, enabling efficient defense evaluation. The leave-one-out experiment shows that when certain attacks are unknown to the RF classifier, it can still accurately predict detector responses for those attacks, indicating similar perturbation patterns exist across attack groups. Core assumption: Attacks with similar perturbation patterns will produce similar detector responses, allowing for meaningful grouping. Evidence anchors: RF classifier maintains accuracy when predicting responses for unknown attacks.

### Mechanism 3
Combining multiple metrics provides more informative evaluation than using L-norms alone for adversarial defense assessment. The experimental results show that accuracy increases as more metrics are combined (from 0.695 with L0 alone to 0.937-0.947 with combined metrics), demonstrating that different metrics capture complementary information about perturbations. Core assumption: Each metric captures unique aspects of the perturbation that others miss, and these aspects are relevant to detector behavior. Evidence anchors: Accuracy improvement from single L-norms to combined metrics.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can adversarial attacks be effectively grouped based on image perturbation metrics to create representative attack sets for defense evaluation? The paper identifies the potential for grouping attacks but doesn't provide a concrete methodology for systematically creating these groups or selecting representatives from each group. Resolution would require a proposed framework with empirical validation.

### Open Question 2
Can the Random Forest classifier trained on image perturbation metrics reliably predict the strength and novelty of unseen adversarial attacks against various target models? The paper suggests this possibility but doesn't comprehensively evaluate the RF classifier's performance across different target models or datasets. Resolution would require extensive testing on multiple models and datasets.

### Open Question 3
How do the effectiveness and predictive power of image quality metrics compare to L-norms when evaluating adversarial attacks on different types of image classifiers beyond ConvNet12? The study's experiments are limited to a single classifier and dataset. Resolution would require a comparative study across diverse classifiers and datasets.

## Limitations
- Results may be dataset-specific, as only CIFAR-10 with 100 images was used
- The study focuses on a single classifier (ConvNet12), limiting generalizability
- Effectiveness of the proposed metrics on newer, more sophisticated detectors is not established

## Confidence
- High: The correlation between image quality metrics and detector outputs (0.94 accuracy) is well-supported by experimental results
- Medium: The claim that L-norms alone are insufficient is demonstrated but could benefit from testing against newer detectors
- Medium: The attack grouping mechanism is supported by leave-one-out experiments but needs validation on additional datasets
- Low: The generalizability of the proposed metrics to other domains (medical imaging, text) is not established

## Next Checks
1. **Dataset Generalization**: Repeat the experiments on ImageNet or other diverse datasets to verify if the correlation between metrics and detector outputs remains consistent
2. **Detector Diversity**: Test the proposed metrics against a broader range of defense mechanisms, including gradient-based, detection-based, and certified defenses
3. **Metric Redundancy Analysis**: Conduct feature importance analysis to determine if all 12 metrics are necessary or if a smaller subset provides equivalent predictive power
