# Extracting Thyroid Nodules Characteristics from Ultrasound Reports Using Transformer-based Natural Language Processing Methods

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00115
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00115
- Reference count: 28
- Key outcome: Developed transformer-based NLP pipeline extracting 16 clinically relevant thyroid nodule characteristics with GatorTron achieving F1-scores of 0.8851 (strict) and 0.9495 (lenient) for concept extraction, and 0.9321 for linking characteristics to nodules.

## Executive Summary
This study developed a transformer-based NLP pipeline to extract 16 clinically relevant thyroid nodule characteristics from ultrasound reports. A multidisciplinary team created annotation guidelines and annotated 490 ultrasound reports. Five transformer models were compared, with the GatorTron model achieving the best performance with strict and lenient F1-scores of 0.8851 and 0.9495 for concept extraction, and 0.9321 for linking characteristics to nodules. The study provides a foundation for assessing documentation quality of thyroid ultrasound reports and examining outcomes of patients with thyroid nodules using electronic health records.

## Method Summary
The study employed a transformer-based NLP pipeline for extracting thyroid nodule characteristics from ultrasound reports. A multidisciplinary team developed annotation guidelines for 16 clinically relevant characteristics. The GatorTron model, pretrained on over 90 billion words of clinical text, was fine-tuned on 490 manually annotated reports using BIO format for named-entity recognition. The pipeline extracted individual characteristics and linked them to specific nodules through relation extraction. Performance was evaluated using strict and lenient F1-scores for concept extraction and an F1-score for characteristic-to-nodule linking.

## Key Results
- GatorTron achieved the best performance with strict F1-score of 0.8851 and lenient F1-score of 0.9495 for concept extraction
- The model achieved an F1-score of 0.9321 for linking characteristics to nodules
- The study successfully extracted 16 clinically relevant thyroid nodule characteristics from ultrasound reports

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The GatorTron transformer model achieves superior performance in extracting thyroid nodule characteristics due to its large-scale pretraining on over 90 billion words of clinical text.
- Mechanism: By pretraining on a vast corpus of clinical narratives from multiple healthcare settings, GatorTron develops rich representations of medical terminology and context, enabling more accurate extraction of clinical concepts from ultrasound reports.
- Core assumption: Larger pretraining corpus size leads to better performance in domain-specific clinical NLP tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our GatorTron model, a transformer-based large language model trained using over 90 billion words of text, achieved the best strict and lenient F1-score of 0.8851 and 0.9495"
  - [section]: "GatorTron was developed by training from scratch using >90 billion words of text (including >82 billion words of de-identified clinical text from UF Health)"
- Break condition: If the pretraining corpus is not representative of the target clinical domain or if the model is overfitting to specific institutional reporting styles.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The multidisciplinary collaboration between NLP experts and thyroid specialists improves the quality and clinical relevance of the annotation guidelines and extracted concepts.
- Mechanism: Domain experts identify clinically important thyroid nodule characteristics based on current guidelines, while NLP experts ensure proper technical implementation of concept extraction and linking.
- Core assumption: Combining clinical expertise with technical NLP knowledge produces more comprehensive and accurate annotation guidelines.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "a multidisciplinary team of NLP experts and thyroid specialists, identified thyroid nodule characteristics that are important for clinical care, composed annotation guidelines"
  - [section]: "We conveyed a multidisciplinary research team including expert NLP researchers and thyroid specialists to develop annotation guidelines"
- Break condition: If the collaboration process is inefficient or if there's significant disagreement between clinical and technical perspectives that cannot be resolved.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using transformer-based models with BIO annotation format enables effective extraction of both individual thyroid nodule characteristics and their relationships to specific nodules.
- Mechanism: The BIO format captures the beginning, inside, and outside positions of clinical concepts, while relation extraction links characteristics to their corresponding nodules through candidate pair generation and classification.
- Core assumption: Transformer models can effectively learn the complex patterns of clinical concept boundaries and their relationships in medical text.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We approach the thyroid nodule characteristics extraction as a clinical concept extraction (or named-entity recognition [NER]) task and adopted the standard beginning-inside-outside (BIO) annotation format"
  - [section]: "After the nodules and nodule characteristics were identified, we linked the characteristics to the nodules to form a nodule profile using relation extraction"
- Break condition: If the relation extraction becomes too complex with multiple nodules and overlapping characteristics, leading to ambiguous linkages.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data preprocessing pipeline (tokenization, sentence boundary detection, BIO transformation) -> Transformer-based NER models for concept extraction -> Relation extraction module for linking characteristics to nodules -> Evaluation framework with strict and lenient metrics -> Annotation guideline development and quality monitoring system

- Critical path:
  1. Data preprocessing and tokenization
  2. Concept extraction using transformer models
  3. Relation extraction and entity linking
  4. Evaluation and performance comparison
  5. Clinical validation and deployment planning

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model complexity vs. interpretability: More complex models may achieve better performance but are harder to interpret clinically
  - Pretraining data diversity vs. specificity: Balancing broad clinical knowledge with domain-specific expertise
  - Annotation granularity vs. practical utility: More detailed annotations may improve accuracy but increase annotation burden

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on specific characteristic types (e.g., Echogenic foci or Vascularity)
  - Inconsistent entity linking across multiple nodules in the same report
  - Overfitting to institutional reporting styles when generalizing to new datasets
  - Difficulty handling negation and uncertainty expressions in clinical text

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare strict vs. lenient evaluation metrics on a held-out test set to understand performance differences
  2. Analyze error patterns by characteristic type to identify model weaknesses and potential annotation improvements
  3. Test model generalization by evaluating performance on reports from different time periods or institutions

## Limitations
- The dataset consists of 490 annotated reports from a single institution (University of Florida Health), limiting generalizability to other healthcare systems
- The study does not provide detailed error analysis by characteristic type, making it difficult to assess which specific nodule characteristics remain challenging for extraction
- While the lenient evaluation metric provides insight into model performance, it may overestimate real-world clinical utility by being overly permissive in matching extracted concepts to ground truth annotations
