# Beyond the limitations of any imaginable mechanism: large language models and psycholinguistics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00077
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00077
- Reference count: 2
- Key outcome: LLMs are successful at language modeling but differ from human processing in hierarchical parsing and error patterns, serving as tools for surprisal quantification, comparative modeling, and philosophical exploration of language-thought relationships.

## Executive Summary
Large language models excel at their primary task of language modeling but differ fundamentally from human linguistic processing in several key aspects. While they can effectively estimate surprisal and serve as practical tools for psycholinguistic experimentation, they struggle with hierarchical parsing rules like Principle C and produce different error patterns than humans in tasks like subject-verb agreement. The paper argues that despite these limitations, LLMs provide three valuable contributions to psycholinguistics: as quantitative tools for measuring linguistic phenomena, as comparative models to understand computational strategies, and as philosophical instruments to explore the relationship between language and thought.

## Method Summary
The paper provides a theoretical framework for understanding the relationship between large language models and psycholinguistics, drawing on existing literature rather than implementing new experiments. It discusses how LLMs can be used to quantify surprisal, compares their performance to human language processing on various linguistic tasks, and explores the philosophical implications of machines that can generate human-like language without apparent understanding. The analysis focuses on identifying where LLMs succeed and fail relative to human processing, particularly in areas requiring hierarchical parsing versus those amenable to statistical learning.

## Key Results
- LLMs can effectively estimate surprisal values that correlate with human cognitive processing times, serving as practical tools for psycholinguistic experimentation
- LLMs fail at hierarchical parsing rules like Principle C while succeeding at long-range agreement tasks, revealing computational differences from human processing
- The ability of LLMs to generate coherent language without apparent understanding challenges traditional assumptions about the relationship between linguistic competence and cognition

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs serve as a practical tool for quantifying linguistic phenomena like surprisal
- Mechanism: LLMs are trained on next-word prediction, making them naturally suited to estimate surprisal values, which correlate with human cognitive processing times (reaction times, gaze duration, EEG responses)
- Core assumption: The statistical probabilities derived from LLM training distributions accurately reflect the surprisal experienced by human language processors
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "LLMs are typically trained on a task akin to quantifying surprisal and are superior to n-grams in estimating word probabilities"
  - [section]: "Differences between LLM-derived estimates and neural perception of surprisal may quantify which linguistic structures, perhaps poorly represented in the statistical evidence, the brain privileges during processing"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct citations about surprisal quantification in the corpus
- Break condition: If the statistical distributions learned by LLMs diverge significantly from the linguistic exposure patterns of human learners, the surprisal estimates may become unreliable indicators of human cognitive processing

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LLMs provide a comparative model to understand computational strategies underlying language
- Mechanism: By comparing LLM performance on language tasks to human performance, we can identify which aspects of language are easily learned through statistical patterns versus which require specialized cognitive mechanisms
- Core assumption: Differences in error patterns and task performance between LLMs and humans reveal fundamental computational distinctions in language processing strategies
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "LLMs are capable of long range number and gender agreement... but they are not successful in implementing another long-range rule: Principle C... Thus, LLMs allow us to recognize those aspects of language which require special consideration"
  - [abstract]: "they struggle with hierarchical parsing rules like Principle C and differ from human error patterns in tasks like subject-verb agreement"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct citations about Principle C or hierarchical parsing failures in the corpus
- Break condition: If LLMs eventually master hierarchical parsing rules through architectural improvements or larger training data, this comparative mechanism would lose its distinguishing power for identifying uniquely human language capabilities

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LLMs serve as a philosophical tool to recast the relationship between language and thought
- Mechanism: The ability of LLMs to generate coherent language without apparent understanding challenges traditional assumptions about the connection between linguistic competence and cognition
- Core assumption: If machines can produce human-like language without possessing thought or understanding, this suggests language competence may be separable from cognitive processes
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Either we must claim that, despite recent progress, machine-generated language will remain unlike human language in vital respects, or we must defy our intuition and consider machines to be as capable of thought as we are, or we must codify our intuition to specify why a machine able to produce language should, nonetheless, be considered lacking in thought"
  - [abstract]: "Large language models are not detailed models of human linguistic processing. They are, however, extremely successful at their primary task: providing a model for language"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct citations about the philosophy of language and thought in the corpus
- Break condition: If future evidence demonstrates that LLM language generation is fundamentally dependent on implicit world knowledge or understanding, the philosophical distinction between language and thought would be undermined

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Token embeddings -> Transformer blocks -> Self-attention mechanisms -> Feed-forward networks -> Output probability distribution
- Critical path: Tokenization and embedding lookup -> Multi-head self-attention computation -> Feed-forward neural network processing -> Layer normalization and residual connections -> Output probability distribution calculation -> Loss computation and backpropagation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Depth vs. width: Deeper networks capture more complex patterns but risk overfitting and computational inefficiency
  - Attention mechanism design: Full attention vs. sparse attention for computational efficiency
  - Context window size: Larger windows capture more context but increase computational complexity
  - Training data size: More data improves generalization but increases training costs
- Failure signatures: Principle C violations, relative clause errors, context forgetting, surface-level generation patterns
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Subject-verb agreement task: Test LLM performance on sentences with varying levels of syntactic complexity and embedded clauses
  2. Principle C test: Evaluate whether the model correctly blocks coreference between a pronoun and a c-commanding antecedent

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can LLMs be modified to successfully implement Principle C and other hierarchical parsing rules that they currently struggle with?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that LLMs are not successful in implementing Principle C, which depends on hierarchical parsing
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a limitation of current LLMs but does not explore whether this limitation is fundamental or could be overcome with architectural changes
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementation of Principle C in LLMs through architectural modifications, or theoretical arguments proving why such implementation is impossible given current LLM frameworks

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific aspects of human language processing are captured by LLMs' success with long-range agreement but failure with Principle C?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that LLMs can handle long-range number and gender agreement but fail at Principle C, suggesting they capture some but not all aspects of human language processing
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this discrepancy but doesn't provide a detailed analysis of what this tells us about the differences between LLM and human processing mechanisms
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies showing which specific linguistic phenomena LLMs can and cannot handle, and theoretical frameworks explaining why certain phenomena require hierarchical processing while others don't

### Open Question 3
- Question: How should we define the relationship between language generation and thought, given that LLMs can produce human-like language without apparent understanding?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the philosophical challenge of reconciling LLMs' language capabilities with the intuitive belief that they lack thought or understanding
- Why unresolved: This is a fundamental philosophical question that the paper frames but doesn't attempt to answer, noting we must either claim machines will always differ from humans, defy our intuitions, or codify why language alone doesn't indicate thought
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies on the nature of human thought and language, or theoretical frameworks that clearly delineate what constitutes "understanding" versus mere language production

## Limitations

- The comparative claims between LLM behavior and human linguistic processing rest on empirical assumptions that remain unverified
- The philosophical framing about language and thought is presented as consequence rather than testable hypothesis
- The paper lacks methodological rigor typically required for scientific claims about cognition

## Confidence

- High Confidence: LLMs serve as practical tools for quantifying surprisal - Well-supported by extensive prior work demonstrating correlations with human behavioral measures
- Medium Confidence: LLMs reveal computational strategies through comparative failure patterns - Compelling qualitative argument but requires more rigorous empirical validation
- Low Confidence: LLMs fundamentally challenge the relationship between language and thought - Intriguing philosophical claim but remains speculative without empirical evidence
