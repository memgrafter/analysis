# Lottery Tickets in Evolutionary Optimization: On Sparse Backpropagation-Free Trainability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00045
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00045
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: ES lottery tickets outperform magnitude-based pruning and transfer across tasks and optimization methods

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether the lottery ticket hypothesis, which posits that sparse, trainable neural network initializations exist, extends beyond gradient-based training to evolutionary optimization methods. The authors introduce a novel signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) pruning procedure that incorporates loss curvature information into the network pruning step for evolution strategies (ES). They demonstrate that winning lottery tickets exist for various ES, tasks, and architectures, with SNR-based iterative pruning consistently discovering initializations that outperform magnitude-based pruning across all sparsity levels. Furthermore, they show that ES tickets are transferable across tasks, ES algorithms, and to gradient descent-based training, and that ES tickets tend to converge to flatter optima compared to gradient descent-based tickets.

## Method Summary
The authors use iterative magnitude pruning (IMP) and a novel SNR pruning procedure to discover sparse initializations for evolution strategies (ES). They evaluate these initializations on a range of control tasks (using Brax environments) and image classification tasks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST) with MLPs and CNNs. The ES algorithms used include Sep-CMA-ES, SNES, PGPE, and DES. They compare the performance of ES-based lottery tickets to magnitude-based pruning, SNR-based pruning, and random pruning baselines. Additionally, they test the transferability of tickets across tasks, ES algorithms, and to gradient descent training, and analyze the properties of the local optima found by ES and GD.

## Key Results
- Winning lottery tickets consistently exist for various ES, tasks, and architectures
- SNR-based iterative pruning discovers initializations that outperform IMP tickets across all sparsity levels
- ES tickets transfer across tasks, ES algorithms, and to gradient descent-based training
- ES tickets tend to converge to flatter optima compared to gradient descent-based tickets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The lottery ticket phenomenon generalizes to evolution strategies (ES), not just gradient-based training.
- Mechanism: ES can discover sparse, trainable subnetworks by evolving the sufficient statistics of a search distribution (mean and variance), analogous to how gradient descent prunes and resets weights.
- Core assumption: Sparse trainability is a property of the network initialization and task, not dependent on the specific optimization algorithm's update rule.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we establish the existence of highly sparse trainable initializations for evolution strategies (ES)"
  - [section] "Winning lottery tickets consistently exist for various ES... The LTH phenomenon, therefore, does not depend on gradient-based training"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighboring papers focus on pruning at initialization or lottery ticket rewinding, not ES.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SNR-based pruning incorporates loss curvature information and discovers sparser trainable tickets than magnitude-based pruning.
- Mechanism: By pruning weights based on the ratio of mean magnitude to standard deviation (|θ|/σ), SNR pruning implicitly uses the ES search covariance to identify weights in flat loss directions that can be removed without harming trainability.
- Core assumption: Weights with low SNR are in flat directions of the loss surface and contribute little to performance; ES covariance encodes this curvature information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "SNR pruning implicitly incorporates information about the loss geometry/curvature into the sparsification"
  - [section] "SNR-derived tickets are consistently trainable at higher degrees of sparsity"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighboring papers focus on pruning heuristics but not ES-specific SNR pruning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ES lottery tickets transfer across tasks, ES algorithms, and to GD-based training, capturing moderately task-general inductive biases.
- Mechanism: The sparse subnetworks found by ES encode useful architectural inductive biases (e.g., which input dimensions are task-relevant) that generalize beyond the specific task and optimization algorithm used to discover them.
- Core assumption: The lottery ticket mask captures task-relevant information that is not overly specific to the training paradigm or exact task instance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "ES tickets outperform sparse random initializations when transferred to related tasks... They can be transferred between different evolutionary strategies and to GD-based training"
  - [section] "ES tickets do not overfit to their training paradigm or task setting. Instead, they capture moderately task-general inductive biases"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighboring papers focus on lottery ticket transferability but not in the context of ES.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP)
  - Why needed here: IMP is the procedure used to discover lottery tickets in both GD and ES settings; understanding it is crucial for interpreting the results.
  - Quick check question: In IMP, after training a network and pruning a fraction of weights, what is the next step before the next iteration?

- Concept: Evolution Strategies (ES)
  - Why needed here: ES is the gradient-free optimization algorithm studied as an alternative to GD for finding lottery tickets; knowing how ES works is essential for understanding the SNR pruning mechanism.
  - Quick check question: In ES, how is the search distribution updated based on the fitness evaluations of the candidate solutions?

- Concept: Linear Mode Connectivity
  - Why needed here: Linear mode connectivity is used to compare the local optima found by GD and ES; understanding it helps interpret the differences in their loss landscape exploration.
  - Quick check question: If two networks are linearly mode connected, what does that imply about the performance when interpolating between their parameters?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Tasks (control + vision) -> Network architectures (MLPs + CNNs) -> ES algorithms (Sep-CMA-ES, SNES, PGPE, DES) -> Pruning procedures (IMP, SNR) -> Transfer settings (task-to-task, ES-to-ES, ES-to-GD) -> Analysis tools (linear mode connectivity, loss surface projections, curvature estimates)

- Critical path:
  1. Run ES-adapted iterative pruning (IMP or SNR) to find sparse initializations
  2. Evaluate trainability of found tickets vs. random pruning baseline
  3. Test transferability of tickets to related tasks, other ES, and GD
  4. Compare local optima properties (connectivity, curvature) between GD and ES

- Design tradeoffs:
  - ES vs. GD: ES can find sparser trainable tickets but is more computationally expensive; GD is faster but struggles with high sparsity
  - IMP vs. SNR pruning: SNR can find sparser tickets by incorporating curvature info, but requires estimating ES covariance; IMP is simpler but may prune too aggressively
  - Task similarity for transfer: Higher similarity enables better transfer, but exploring lower similarity transfers can reveal the limits of ticket generalization

- Failure signatures:
  - Tickets not outperforming random pruning: Task/network may be too complex for the given sparsity level
  - SNR pruning not outperforming IMP: ES covariance may not accurately reflect loss curvature, or the task may not benefit from curvature-aware pruning
  - Transfer performance close to random: Tickets may be too task-specific, or the source and target tasks may be too dissimilar

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run ES-IMP on a simple control task (e.g., Pendulum) with an MLP and verify that tickets outperform random pruning
  2. Compare ES-IMP vs. ES-SNR on the same task to see if SNR pruning discovers sparser trainable tickets
  3. Test transferability of the found tickets to a related control task (e.g., different mass in Ant) to verify the transfer results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the results suggest several directions for future research:
- Understanding the underlying mechanism that causes SNR pruning to outperform magnitude-based pruning in ES
- Investigating the qualitative differences between ES and GD lottery tickets
- Exploring the limits of ES ticket transferability across more diverse tasks and architectures

## Limitations
- The study focuses on relatively shallow MLPs and CNNs, leaving open the question of scalability to deeper architectures and more complex tasks.
- The SNR pruning heuristic requires careful estimation of the ES search covariance, and its effectiveness may degrade if this estimation is inaccurate.
- The transfer experiments are limited to related tasks within the same domain, not exploring transfer to more diverse task domains.

## Confidence
- High confidence: ES can discover sparse, trainable initializations (lottery tickets) and SNR pruning can find sparser tickets than magnitude-based pruning
- Medium confidence: ES tickets transfer across tasks, ES algorithms, and to gradient descent training
- Low confidence: Broader implications for deep learning with more complex networks and tasks

## Next Checks
1. Scale up the experiments to deeper architectures (e.g., ResNets) and more complex tasks (e.g., CIFAR-10) to test the limits of ES-based lottery tickets.
2. Investigate the sensitivity of SNR pruning to the quality of the ES search covariance estimation by comparing results using ground-truth vs. estimated covariances.
3. Conduct a systematic study of ES ticket transferability across more diverse task domains (e.g., control → vision, or language → vision) to better understand the generality of the captured inductive biases.
