# Interpretation of Time-Series Deep Models: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14582
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14582
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Systematic review of post-hoc and inherently interpretable methods for time-series deep learning models

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive review of interpretation methods for time-series deep learning models, organizing them into post-hoc and inherently interpretable approaches. Post-hoc methods include backpropagation-based techniques (gradient, LRP, DeepLIFT), perturbation-based methods (erasure, ablation, permutation), and approximation-based methods (LIME, SHAP). Inherently interpretable models integrate human-understandable components directly into the architecture through attention mechanisms, disentangled representation learning, shapelet learning, symbolic representation learning, causality-based models, and physics rule-based models. The survey also discusses evaluation metrics and highlights current challenges in the field.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically categorizes time-series interpretation methods into two main approaches: post-hoc methods that analyze trained models to generate explanations, and inherently interpretable models that build interpretability into the architecture during training. For post-hoc methods, it reviews backpropagation-based approaches that use gradients to highlight important features, perturbation-based methods that measure prediction changes when inputs are modified, and approximation-based techniques that use surrogate models. For inherently interpretable models, it examines approaches that incorporate attention mechanisms, learn disentangled representations, identify shapelets, use symbolic representations, incorporate causality, or apply physics rules. The survey also discusses evaluation metrics and current challenges in the field.

## Key Results
- Post-hoc interpretation methods include backpropagation-based (gradient, LRP, DeepLIFT), perturbation-based (erasure, ablation, permutation), and approximation-based (LIME, SHAP) techniques
- Inherently interpretable models incorporate attention mechanisms, disentangled representation learning, shapelet learning, symbolic representation learning, causality-based models, and physics rule-based models
- Current challenges include developing more intuitive explanations for non-experts and establishing systematic comparisons between different interpretation methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Post-hoc interpretation methods provide model-agnostic explanations for time-series deep models by analyzing the relationship between input features and predictions after model training.
- Mechanism: These methods work by either backpropagating gradients through the model, perturbing input features to observe output changes, or approximating feature importance using surrogate models.
- Core assumption: The pre-trained model's internal representations contain sufficient information to explain its predictions through external analysis.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "post-hoc methods include backpropagation-based (gradient, LRP, DeepLIFT), perturbation-based (erasure, ablation, permutation), and approximation-based (LIME, SHAP) techniques"
  - [section] "post-hoc interpretation methods for time series models based on backpropagation, perturbation, and approximation"
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbor papers focus on recent developments but don't directly validate these mechanisms
- Break condition: When the model's internal representations are too complex or non-linear for external analysis to capture meaningful relationships, or when the model architecture prevents proper gradient flow.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Inherently interpretable models integrate human-understandable information directly into the model architecture during training.
- Mechanism: These models use attention mechanisms, disentangled representation learning, shapelet learning, symbolic representation learning, causality-based models, or physics rule-based models to create transparent decision-making processes.
- Core assumption: Designing interpretable components into the model architecture during training creates more reliable and meaningful explanations than post-hoc analysis.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "inherently interpretable models include attention mechanisms, disentangled representation learning, shapelet learning, symbolic representation learning, causality-based models, and physics rule-based models"
  - [section] "human-understandable information is designed within the models"
  - [corpus] Moderate - neighbor papers discuss self-interpretable networks and causality-based approaches that support this mechanism
- Break condition: When the interpretability constraints significantly degrade model performance or when the human-understandable components cannot capture the complexity of the task.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Evaluation metrics for time-series interpretability must account for the sequential nature of the data and temporal dependencies.
- Mechanism: Quantitative evaluation uses perturbation analysis to measure how feature removal affects predictions, while qualitative evaluation relies on domain expert validation of explanations.
- Core assumption: The quality of explanations can be measured through systematic perturbation of input features and comparison with ground truth or expert judgment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "evaluation metrics used for the explanations"
  - [section] "perturbation analysis in the image domain could be adapted to evaluate time series interpretations"
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbor papers mention evaluation but don't provide specific metrics for time-series interpretability
- Break condition: When temporal dependencies make simple perturbation ineffective, or when domain expertise is unavailable to validate qualitative assessments.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Time-series model architectures (CNN, RNN, Transformer, GNN) → Interpretation methods (post-hoc backpropagation-based, perturbation-based, approximation-based / inherently interpretable attention, disentangled, shapelet, symbolic, causality-based, physics rule-based) → Evaluation metrics (quantitative perturbation analysis, qualitative domain expert validation)

- Critical path: Understand time-series model architecture → Select interpretation method based on post-hoc vs. inherent needs → Apply chosen method → Evaluate using domain-appropriate metrics

- Design tradeoffs: Post-hoc methods offer flexibility but may lack fidelity, while inherently interpretable methods provide transparency but might sacrifice performance. The choice depends on application requirements and user expertise.

- Failure signatures: Poor explanations often result from mismatched interpretation methods and model architectures, insufficient domain knowledge for evaluation, or overly complex models that resist interpretation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can attention mechanisms in time series models provide reliable and intuitive explanations for non-expert users?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the controversy around whether attention mechanisms provide meaningful insights for model predictions, noting that while some argue attention weights weakly correlate with feature importance derived by conventional methods, others believe attention weights can demonstrate meaningful interpretation.
- Why unresolved: The interpretability issue of model attention weights is still being debated across the community, with no clear consensus on whether attention weights can be considered faithful explanations of model behaviors.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies directly comparing attention-based explanations with gradient-based and perturbation-based methods on the same time series tasks, measuring both technical fidelity metrics and user comprehension for non-expert audiences.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can inherently interpretable models be designed to balance interpretability and performance effectively for time series tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces inherently interpretable models as a novel category where human-understandable information is designed within the models, but notes the need for more research on how these models perform compared to post-hoc methods.
- Why unresolved: While the paper presents several types of inherently interpretable models (attention, disentangled representations, shapelets, causality-based, physics rule-based), there is a lack of systematic comparisons between these approaches and their effectiveness across different time series tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies benchmarking various inherently interpretable models against both post-hoc methods and black-box models across multiple time series tasks, measuring both performance metrics and interpretability scores.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What quantitative evaluation metrics can be developed specifically for time series interpretation that incorporate domain-specific context?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that quantitative evaluation is preferred over qualitative evaluation for accuracy, but most current quantitative metrics lack domain-specific context, making them less effective for real-world applications.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions some quantitative metrics like perturbation analysis and orthogonal metrics, it highlights the need for more metrics that can capture the nuances of different time series domains while maintaining objectivity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of new evaluation metrics that combine domain expert knowledge with quantitative measures, tested across multiple time series domains to demonstrate their effectiveness in distinguishing between high and low quality interpretations.

## Limitations

- The survey doesn't address the computational cost of various interpretation methods or their real-time applicability
- Limited validation of more recent inherently interpretable approaches like DSANet and SAITS due to insufficient empirical studies
- Evaluation framework primarily adapts image domain metrics to time series without sufficient domain-specific validation

## Confidence

- Post-hoc interpretation mechanisms: Medium confidence - basic principles established but adaptation to time-series data introduces complexities
- Inherently interpretable models: High confidence for attention mechanisms and shapelet learning, Low confidence for DSANet and SAITS
- Evaluation framework: Medium confidence - adapts image metrics to time series without sufficient domain-specific validation
