# Better Zero-Shot Reasoning with Self-Adaptive Prompting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14106
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14106
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: COSP improves zero-shot reasoning accuracy by up to 15% over baselines and matches few-shot methods on arithmetic and logical reasoning tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Consistency-based Self-adaptive Prompting (COSP), a method that improves zero-shot reasoning in large language models without handcrafted examples or ground-truth labels. COSP works by selecting in-context demonstrations from the LLM's own outputs based on consistency, diversity, and repetitiveness criteria. The method generates a pool of LLM-generated rationales and answers, then prunes and selects the most suitable examples using outcome entropy as a proxy for correctness and redundancy measures. These selected examples are used to augment test queries, with final predictions made via majority voting. Experiments demonstrate that COSP significantly improves performance over zero-shot baselines and achieves competitive results with few-shot methods.

## Method Summary
COSP operates in two stages: first, it collects LLM responses to test questions via zero-shot Chain-of-Thought prompting to build a pool of rationales and answers; second, it performs another pass over test samples using selected question-reasoning pairs as in-context demonstrations. The method filters responses to retain only those leading to majority predictions and removes repetitive or fragmented ones, then selects K demonstrations using a scoring function combining entropy and repetitiveness. Final predictions are made via majority voting across both stages. The approach requires no ground truth labels and can be applied to any task where zero-shot CoT is applicable.

## Key Results
- COSP improves accuracy by up to 15% over zero-shot baselines on arithmetic and logical reasoning tasks
- Performance matches or exceeds few-shot methods without requiring handcrafted examples
- Method generalizes to few-shot settings and remains robust across different model sizes
- Entropy proves to be a reliable proxy for correctness, with low entropy correlating positively with correct answers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Self-consistency approximates model uncertainty and selects more reliable demonstrations
- **Mechanism**: Multiple sampling paths produce varying answers; lower entropy across these paths correlates with correctness. The algorithm selects only rationales that lead to the majority prediction, filtering out likely incorrect ones.
- **Core assumption**: Majority predictions are more likely to be correct; outcome entropy is a reliable proxy for correctness.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "using only LLM predictions, COSP improves performance up to 15% compared to zero-shot baselines"
  - [section]: "Fig. 3, we indeed find that the normalized entropy is a good proxy over a number of different tasks where low entropy is positively correlated with correctness"
- **Break condition**: When model predictions are poorly calibrated or when reasoning tasks have very small label spaces (e.g., binary classification), entropy may not reliably indicate correctness

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Diverse demonstrations improve generalization better than repetitive ones
- **Mechanism**: Repetition penalty term in scoring function discourages semantically similar demonstrations. Diversity encourages exposure to varied question types and reasoning patterns.
- **Core assumption**: Repetitive demonstrations create spurious patterns that LLMs overfit to, harming performance
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "COSP selects and builds the set of examples from the LLM zero-shot outputs via carefully designed criteria that combine consistency, diversity and repetition"
  - [section]: "repetitive demonstrations often lead to worse performance... we hypothesize that repetitive responses acting as demonstrations create a strong but spurious pattern on which LLMs are prone to overfit"
- **Break condition**: When the most consistent demonstrations are inherently repetitive (e.g., similar arithmetic structures), diversity may trade off with consistency

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Stage-wise processing with self-consistency enables unsupervised demonstration selection
- **Mechanism**: Stage 1 generates pool of responses via zero-shot CoT; Stage 2 uses selected demonstrations as context. This creates a bootstrapping loop where LLM's own outputs guide improvement without ground truth labels.
- **Core assumption**: LLM can generate useful demonstrations from its own outputs when guided by consistency and diversity criteria
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "COSP selects and builds the set of examples from the LLM zero-shot outputs via carefully designed criteria"
  - [section]: "In the first stage, COSP collects the pool of LLM responses to test questions via Zero-shot CoT... In the second stage, we perform another pass over all test samples, but with the selected question-reasoning pairs as in-context demonstrations"
- **Break condition**: When initial zero-shot outputs are predominantly incorrect, the pool may lack quality demonstrations to select from

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Entropy as uncertainty measure
  - Why needed here: Used to approximate correctness of LLM predictions without ground truth labels
  - Quick check question: If an LLM outputs the same answer across 10 sampled reasoning paths, what does this indicate about the outcome entropy and likely correctness?

- **Concept**: In-context learning sensitivity
  - Why needed here: Demonstration selection critically affects performance; poor examples can mislead rather than help
  - Quick check question: Why might showing an LLM multiple similar demonstrations sometimes hurt performance more than showing diverse ones?

- **Concept**: Greedy forward selection
  - Why needed here: Efficiently selects K demonstrations from large candidate pool while balancing consistency and diversity
  - Quick check question: What's the computational complexity difference between evaluating all possible K-demonstration combinations versus greedy forward selection?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Test question input → Zero-shot CoT sampling (Stage 1) → Pool building → Consistency/diversity scoring → Demonstration selection → Context augmentation → Re-query with demonstrations (Stage 2) → Majority vote
- **Critical path**: Zero-shot CoT generation → Majority vote filtering → Entropy calculation → Greedy demonstration selection → Context-augmented querying → Final majority vote
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - More sampling paths → better entropy estimates but higher compute cost
  - Higher diversity weight → potentially less consistent demonstrations
  - Larger K → more guidance but risk of including erroneous examples
- **Failure signatures**:
  - No improvement over zero-shot baseline → likely poor demonstration quality or insufficient diversity
  - Performance worse than zero-shot → probably overfitting to repetitive or misleading demonstrations
  - High variance across runs → entropy estimates unstable, may need more sampling paths
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Baseline zero-shot CoT with m=7 paths, no demonstration selection
  2. COSP with K=5 demonstrations, m=7 paths per stage, λ=0.2
  3. COSP with adaptive K based on entropy (COSP+), same other parameters

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does COSP's performance scale with the number of sampled reasoning paths beyond the 14 paths tested in the paper?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper states "we also analyze the effect of the number of sampling paths in Fig. 4b, where it is clear that the performance of all methods increases with number of paths" and suggests "there is potential for further performance gain by scaling COSP when additional computational resources are available."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests up to 21 paths and does not explore the upper limits of scaling or diminishing returns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing COSP with significantly more paths (e.g., 50, 100, or 1000) to identify performance plateaus and computational cost-benefit tradeoffs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can COSP's selection mechanism be extended to tasks with small label spaces (e.g., binary classification) where outcome entropy is less informative?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper acknowledges in the Limitations section that "a potential remedy... is to not only consider the consistency over outcomes but also over the intermediate rationales which are generated but not currently used for self-consistency computation."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests COSP on tasks with large label spaces (arithmetic, multi-choice) and does not implement the proposed intermediate-rationale consistency mechanism.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying COSP to binary/multi-class classification tasks with and without intermediate-rationale consistency metrics, comparing performance against baseline methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is COSP to noise in the LLM's zero-shot outputs when the task is extremely challenging?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The Failure Analysis section notes that on GSM-8K with GPT-3, "due to the extreme difficulty of the task to the model most of the generated demonstrations are wrong," and discusses cases where Auto-CoT leads to performance deterioration more frequently.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides qualitative examples but lacks systematic analysis of performance degradation thresholds and mitigation strategies for highly challenging tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying task difficulty and measuring the point at which COSP's selection mechanism fails, along with ablation studies testing different selection heuristics or filtering thresholds.

## Limitations
- Entropy-based correctness proxy assumes well-calibrated models and sufficient label space diversity
- Repetitiveness penalty relies on approximate semantic similarity measures that could introduce noise
- Experiments focus primarily on PaLM and GPT-3 models without systematic testing across diverse model families or sizes

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using outcome entropy to filter demonstrations is well-supported by experimental results
- **Medium confidence**: Diversity and repetitiveness criteria effectively improve performance, though optimal weighting appears task-dependent
- **Low confidence**: Claims about COSP generalizing well to few-shot settings lack systematic validation across multiple few-shot scenarios

## Next Checks

1. **Calibration testing**: Systematically evaluate COSP's performance across models with known calibration differences (e.g., comparing GPT-3.5 vs GPT-4) to verify entropy remains a reliable correctness proxy across model families.

2. **Ablation on diversity weight**: Conduct controlled experiments varying the diversity weight λ across different task types to establish optimal settings and test whether the hypothesized overfitting to repetitive demonstrations consistently explains performance patterns.

3. **Few-shot robustness**: Test COSP across a wider range of few-shot scenarios (K=1, 3, 10, 30 examples) on the same reasoning tasks to verify claimed generalization and identify breaking points where demonstration selection quality degrades.
