# Two Stage Contextual Word Filtering for Context bias in Unified Streaming and Non-streaming Transducer

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06735
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06735
- Reference count: 0
- Over 20% relative character error rate reduction compared to baseline

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a two-stage contextual word filtering approach for unified streaming and non-streaming transducer models to improve recognition of rare words like named entities. The method uses phone-level streaming output to filter a predefined contextual word list before fusing it into the non-causal encoder and decoder. Experiments show over 20% relative character error rate reduction compared to baseline, with real-time factor stabilized under 0.15 even when the contextual word list exceeds 6,000 items.

## Method Summary
The approach employs a cascaded Conformer-Transducer architecture with both streaming (causal) and non-streaming (non-causal) encoders. Phone-level streaming outputs are used to filter contextual word lists through two stages: Posterior Sum Confidence (PSC) eliminates most irrelevant words based on phone posterior probabilities, followed by Sequence Order Confidence (SOC) refinement using dynamic programming. The filtered words are then fused into the non-streaming encoder/decoder via cross-attention bias modules to improve recognition accuracy of rare words.

## Key Results
- Over 20% CER reduction compared to baseline systems
- Real-time factor stabilized under 0.15 even with contextual word lists exceeding 6,000 items
- Improved Entity Recall Rate (ERR) while maintaining computational efficiency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Phone-level streaming outputs provide broader acoustic coverage for rare words through homophone matching. In languages like Mandarin with many homophones, phone-based matching captures more relevant contextual word candidates than character-based matching, especially in large, noisy lists.

### Mechanism 2
Two-stage filtering (PSC then SOC) efficiently reduces contextual word list size while maintaining high recall. PSC eliminates most irrelevant words by computing posterior sum confidence, while SOC further refines by checking sequence order confidence on surviving candidates.

### Mechanism 3
Cross-attention fusion of filtered contextual words into non-streaming encoder/decoder improves rare word recognition. Contextual word embeddings are integrated via cross-attention, allowing the model to attend to relevant contextual information during final character-level prediction.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input features → Cascaded Conformer encoders (causal → non-causal) → Phone predictor (streaming) → Contextual word list filtering (PSC + SOC) → Char predictor with context bias (non-streaming) → Final output
- **Critical path**: Streaming inference (phone-level) → Two-stage filtering → Non-streaming inference with context bias
- **Design tradeoffs**: Streaming vs non-streaming trade-off enables efficient filtering but adds architectural complexity; phone units vs character units for matching accuracy
- **Failure signatures**: Large RTF despite filtering indicates insufficient list pruning; poor CER reduction suggests filtering retains too many irrelevant words or cross-attention integration is ineffective
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement basic transducer with cascaded encoders and measure baseline CER without any context bias
  2. Add full contextual word list to context bias module and measure performance degradation on large lists
  3. Implement PSC-only filtering and compare CER and RTF to baseline with full list

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to Mandarin datasets, restricting generalizability to other languages
- Filtering thresholds not extensively explored or justified, suggesting potential hyperparameter sensitivity
- Architectural complexity may not justify performance gains in all scenarios, particularly with resource constraints

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Two-stage filtering architecture is technically sound; cross-attention integration mechanism is consistent with established methods
- **Medium Confidence**: 20% CER reduction supported by experimental results but may be dataset-dependent; phone-level matching superiority lacks comparative experiments
- **Low Confidence**: Generalization claims to other languages not supported by experiments; robustness to speech variations not demonstrated
