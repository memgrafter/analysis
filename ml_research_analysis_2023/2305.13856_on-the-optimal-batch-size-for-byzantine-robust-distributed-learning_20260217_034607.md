# On the Optimal Batch Size for Byzantine-Robust Distributed Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13856
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13856
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Optimal batch size increases with Byzantine worker fraction when total gradient computations are fixed

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a fundamental relationship between batch size and Byzantine resilience in distributed learning systems. The authors prove that when gradient computation resources are constrained, increasing batch size improves robustness against Byzantine workers by reducing gradient variance. They propose ByzSGDnm, a normalized momentum approach that maintains convergence guarantees for non-convex problems under Byzantine attacks. The method outperforms existing approaches under sophisticated attack scenarios while maintaining competitive performance against simpler failure modes.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces ByzSGDnm, which combines normalized momentum with Byzantine-robust aggregation. The method normalizes momentum vectors to unit length before aggregation, preventing Byzantine workers from amplifying their influence through large momentum values. This normalization is applied element-wise across worker gradients before aggregation using robust mean estimators. The approach maintains the benefits of momentum-based optimization while providing theoretical convergence guarantees for non-convex problems under Byzantine attacks.

## Key Results
- Optimal batch size increases monotonically with Byzantine worker fraction
- ByzSGDnm achieves better convergence under deliberately crafted Byzantine attacks compared to existing methods
- The normalized momentum approach maintains performance under bit-flipping failures
- Theoretical convergence proof established for general non-convex cases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on the observation that larger batch sizes reduce stochastic gradient variance, which helps robust aggregation methods better distinguish between legitimate and Byzantine gradients. When Byzantine workers introduce large errors, smaller gradient variance from larger batches makes these attacks more detectable. The normalized momentum prevents Byzantine workers from exploiting momentum accumulation to amplify their influence over time.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Larger batch sizes improve Byzantine resilience but reduce model accuracy per iteration; momentum normalization improves robustness but may slow convergence
**Failure Signatures**: Byzantine workers can cause divergence through momentum amplification; small batch sizes increase vulnerability to gradient variance-based attacks

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several areas remain unexplored including scalability to larger systems, performance under heterogeneous data distributions, and behavior with different neural network architectures.

## Limitations
- Theoretical results depend on idealized assumptions about Byzantine behavior
- Empirical validation limited to two datasets with small-scale distributed setups
- Does not explore performance across diverse failure modes beyond tested scenarios
- Scalability to larger systems and different data modalities not examined
