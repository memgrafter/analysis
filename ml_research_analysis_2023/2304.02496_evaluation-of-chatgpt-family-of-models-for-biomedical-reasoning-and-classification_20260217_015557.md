# Evaluation of ChatGPT Family of Models for Biomedical Reasoning and Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02496
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02496
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Fine-tuned BioBERT consistently outperformed GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 on biomedical classification and reasoning tasks, with chain-of-thought prompting improving LLM performance but requiring significantly more development time.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically compares large language models (GPT-3.5 and GPT-4) with traditional NLP approaches (bag-of-words with logistic regression and fine-tuned BioBERT) on two fundamental biomedical tasks: health advice classification and causal relation detection. The researchers found that while LLMs showed promise, fine-tuning BioBERT remained the superior approach, achieving macro-F1 scores of 0.800-0.902 for classification and 0.851 for reasoning. Surprisingly, the simple bag-of-words model outperformed all LLMs without chain-of-thought prompting and performed comparably to the best GPT-4 approach for certain tasks. The study highlights that developing effective LLM prompts was resource-intensive, requiring approximately 100x more time than fine-tuning BioBERT.

## Method Summary
The researchers evaluated multiple approaches on two biomedical NLP tasks using the HealthAdvice dataset (10,000+ sentences categorized into "no advice", "weak advice", and "strong advice") and the CausalRelation dataset (3,000+ PubMed conclusion sentences labeled as "correlational", "conditional causal", "direct causal", and "no relationship"). They fine-tuned BioBERT on 20%, 50%, and 100% of development data, used bag-of-words with logistic regression as a baseline, and evaluated GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 using zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot prompting with and without Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Performance was measured using macro-F1 scores, and time/cost metrics were tracked using the OpenAI API for LLM evaluation.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned BioBERT achieved the highest performance with macro-F1 scores of 0.800-0.902 for classification and 0.851 for reasoning
- Bag-of-words model outperformed all LLMs without chain-of-thought prompting and matched GPT-4 performance on structured abstracts and reasoning tasks
- Chain-of-thought prompting with multiple exemplars improved LLM performance compared to zero-shot and non-CoT approaches
- Developing effective LLM prompts required approximately 100x more time than fine-tuning BioBERT

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the performance differences between LLMs and fine-tuned BioBERT models change when evaluated on actual clinical narrative text from electronic medical records rather than proxy biomedical literature?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that LLMs cannot be evaluated on clinical datasets containing PHI, and uses biomedical literature as a proxy for clinical NLP tasks. It also notes that LLM performance is known to decrease on out-of-domain tasks and that classic language models trained on clinical text have been shown to outperform LLMs.
- Why unresolved: HIPAA restrictions prevent the use of patient data with public LLM APIs, making direct evaluation on clinical EMR text impossible with current LLM deployment methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative evaluation of GPT-4 and BioBERT on the same classification and reasoning tasks using de-identified clinical narrative text from EMR systems, once safe methods for LLM access to PHI are established.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific prompt engineering strategies or chain-of-thought templates could reduce the sensitivity of LLMs to small variations in exemplar prompts while maintaining or improving performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper found that LLM performance was highly sensitive to the choice of exemplar prompts, with small changes like typos significantly impacting outcomes. It notes that simply providing more exemplars does not solve the challenge of LLM robustness.
- Why unresolved: Current prompt engineering approaches appear fragile and resource-intensive, yet the paper does not identify specific techniques to improve robustness beyond adding more CoT exemplars.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of different prompt regularization techniques, exemplar selection methods, or prompt tuning approaches that demonstrate improved consistency across variations while maintaining competitive performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of fine-tuned smaller models compare to zero/few-shot LLMs when both are trained/finetuned on the same limited amount of task-specific data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that fine-tuning BioBERT on just 20-50% of the development set consistently outperformed the best GPT settings, but does not directly compare this to LLMs that have been fine-tuned rather than used in zero/few-shot mode.
- Why unresolved: All LLM evaluations in the paper used zero/few-shot settings without fine-tuning, while BioBERT was fine-tuned. A direct comparison of fine-tuned smaller models versus fine-tuned LLMs on the same data would provide clearer insights into the value proposition of each approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative study where both BioBERT and an LLM (like GPT-3.5 or GPT-4) are fine-tuned on identical subsets of the development data for each task, measuring performance, training time, and computational requirements.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focused on only two specific biomedical tasks, limiting generalizability to other biomedical NLP applications
- Resource-intensive prompt engineering required approximately 100x more time than fine-tuning BioBERT, suggesting practical scalability challenges
- Study did not explore alternative fine-tuning strategies or hyperparameter optimization for BioBERT that might further improve performance

## Confidence
- High confidence: Comparative performance results between BioBERT fine-tuning and LLM prompting approaches
- Medium confidence: Generalizability of prompt engineering findings across different tasks
- Medium confidence: Resource efficiency conclusions regarding development time differences
