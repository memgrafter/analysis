# A Static Pruning Study on Sparse Neural Retrievers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12702
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12702
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Static pruning achieves 2× speedup with ≤ 2% effectiveness loss and 4× speedup with ≤ 8% effectiveness loss on sparse neural retrievers

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether static pruning techniques can improve the efficiency of sparse neural retrievers without significantly hurting effectiveness. The authors experiment with three pruning strategies—term-centric, document-centric, and agnostic—on models like DeepImpact, uniCOIL, and SPLADE, as well as BM25. They find that static pruning can speed up retrieval by 2× with less than 2% loss in effectiveness, and by 4× with less than 8% loss. The approach also generalizes to other datasets (e.g., TripClick) and works even in zero-shot scenarios. Additionally, neural rerankers remain robust to candidates from pruned indexes. When combined with quantization and model distillation, static pruning further improves the efficiency-effectiveness trade-off.

## Method Summary
The authors apply three static pruning strategies to sparse neural retrievers: term-centric (removing low-scoring terms), document-centric (removing low-scoring documents), and agnostic (removing low-scoring postings regardless of origin). They evaluate these approaches on DeepImpact, uniCOIL, SPLADE, and BM25 models using datasets like MSMARCO, TREC-DL (2019 and 2020), and TripClick. The experiments measure speedup and effectiveness metrics (MRR@10, Recall@1k) to determine the optimal pruning threshold. The study also examines the impact on reranking and combines pruning with quantization and model distillation.

## Key Results
- Static pruning achieves 2× speedup with negligible effectiveness loss (≤ 2% drop)
- Pruning can achieve 4× speedup with minimal impact on effectiveness (≤ 8% drop)
- Neural rerankers remain robust to candidates from pruned indexes
- The approach generalizes to other datasets (e.g., TripClick) and works in zero-shot scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Static pruning removes low-scoring terms/documents from inverted indexes, thereby reducing the number of postings accessed during query processing and speeding up retrieval.
- Mechanism: By eliminating postings with low impact scores, the search algorithm processes fewer postings per query, which directly reduces computational overhead.
- Core assumption: The removed postings have negligible impact on retrieval effectiveness, so their elimination does not significantly affect ranking quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "static pruning achieves 2× speedup with negligible effectiveness loss (≤ 2% drop) and, depending on the use case, even 4× speedup with minimal impact on the effectiveness (≤ 8% drop)."
  - [section 2] Describes pruning methods (term-centric, document-centric, agnostic) that target low-scoring postings.
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no corpus citations provided.
- Break condition: If the pruned postings contain significant relevance signals, effectiveness will drop sharply.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Learned sparse retrievers alter document representations, changing the statistical distribution of posting scores and making pruning more effective than with traditional models.
- Mechanism: Sparse neural retrievers assign learned impact scores to terms, concentrating high scores on semantically important terms and creating a clear score distribution that pruning can exploit.
- Core assumption: The learned scoring captures term importance more precisely than bag-of-words models, so pruning low scores does not harm retrieval much.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the new document representations learned by these systems alter the actual content of documents, and the statistical properties of the corresponding postings lists."
  - [section 2] Mentions learning term importance and document expansions in sparse neural retrievers.
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no corpus citations provided.
- Break condition: If the scoring model assigns importance uniformly, pruning will not provide much benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pruning generalizes across datasets and retrieval models because the underlying inverted index structure and pruning logic remain valid regardless of training domain.
- Mechanism: Static pruning operates on the inverted index structure, not on query semantics, so it applies equally to models trained on different corpora and in zero-shot settings.
- Core assumption: Index-level pruning does not rely on dataset-specific term distributions beyond what the learned model has already encoded.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that neural rerankers are robust to candidates from statically pruned indexes."
  - [section 3.2] Experiments show pruning effectiveness on TripClick and zero-shot scenarios.
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no corpus citations provided.
- Break condition: If pruning thresholds are tuned to a specific dataset's distribution, they may fail on others.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Index construction -> Sparse neural retriever model -> Posting list generation -> Static pruning filter -> Inverted index storage -> Query processing -> Reranking (optional)
- Critical path: Model inference -> Index building -> Pruning -> Query execution
- Design tradeoffs: Pruning aggressiveness vs. effectiveness loss; index size vs. retrieval speed; model complexity vs. pruning adaptability
- Failure signatures: Sudden drop in MRR/Recall after pruning; inconsistent speedup across query sets; failure to generalize to new datasets
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply term-centric pruning to a small test set and measure speedup and MRR@10
  2. Vary pruning thresholds (quantiles) and plot effectiveness vs. speedup curves
  3. Test pruning on a zero-shot dataset to confirm generalization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can static pruning be effectively combined with other efficiency techniques like quantization and model distillation for different sparse neural retrievers beyond SPLADE?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that combining static pruning with quantization and model distillation improves efficiency-effectiveness trade-offs for SPLADE models, suggesting potential generalizability.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows positive results for SPLADE, it does not explore whether similar combinations would work as effectively for other models like DeepImpact or uniCOIL.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments applying the same combination techniques to different sparse neural retrievers and comparing their efficiency-effectiveness trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of pruning strategy (term-centric, document-centric, or agnostic) affect retrieval performance across different query distributions and document collections?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests three pruning strategies but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how each strategy performs under varying query and collection characteristics.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to specific datasets (MSMARCO and TripClick) and do not explore the impact of diverse query distributions or document collections.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive experiments across diverse datasets with varying query and document characteristics to identify which pruning strategy performs best under different conditions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the long-term impact of static pruning on the robustness and adaptability of sparse neural retrievers to evolving query patterns and document collections?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on immediate efficiency gains and effectiveness retention but does not address how static pruning affects the model's ability to adapt to changing query patterns over time.
- Why unresolved: Static pruning involves removing information from the index, which could potentially limit the model's ability to capture new or evolving query patterns without retraining or re-indexing.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking the performance of pruned indexes over time as query patterns and document collections evolve, assessing the need for re-indexing or retraining.

## Limitations

- Generalizability of pruning thresholds across domains remains untested beyond the limited datasets examined
- Interaction between static pruning and dynamic pruning techniques is not explored in depth
- Computational overhead of determining pruning thresholds is not discussed

## Confidence

- High confidence: Static pruning can achieve 2× speedup with ≤ 2% effectiveness loss (supported by multiple experimental results)
- Medium confidence: 4× speedup with ≤ 8% effectiveness loss is consistently achievable (appears sensitive to pruning aggressiveness and dataset characteristics)
- Medium confidence: Generalization claims to other datasets and zero-shot scenarios (evidence provided but sample size of test domains is limited)
