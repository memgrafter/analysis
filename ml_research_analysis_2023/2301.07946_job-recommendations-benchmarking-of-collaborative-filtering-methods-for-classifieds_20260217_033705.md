# Job recommendations: benchmarking of collaborative filtering methods for classifieds

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07946
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07946
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: RP3beta, SLIM, and ALS significantly outperform Prod2Vec and LightFM in job recommendation accuracy; ALS and RP3beta also increase user engagement in online A/B tests

## Executive Summary
This paper benchmarks five collaborative filtering methods (ALS, LightFM, Prod2Vec, RP3beta, SLIM) for job recommendations on OLX, a classifieds platform. The study finds that RP3beta, SLIM, and ALS outperform Prod2Vec and LightFM in laboratory tests, with ALS and RP3beta also showing increased user engagement in online A/B tests. The paper introduces a large dataset of 65 million job interactions by 3.3 million users on 185,395 job ads, published on Kaggle. The research aims to improve conversion rates and user satisfaction in job classifieds by identifying the most effective recommendation methods.

## Method Summary
The paper benchmarks five collaborative filtering methods using a large dataset of 65 million job interactions from OLX Jobs. The dataset was preprocessed to protect privacy, with only the first interaction counted per user-item pair and timestamps adjusted by a constant. The methods compared are ALS, LightFM, Prod2Vec, RP3beta, and SLIM. Hyperparameters were tuned using Bayesian optimization. Models were evaluated in laboratory settings using metrics like precision@10, recall@10, nDCG, and diversity measures. Top-performing models (ALS and RP3beta) were then validated in online A/B tests to measure their impact on user engagement. The study focuses on implicit feedback data and addresses challenges specific to highly sparse datasets in job classifieds.

## Key Results
- RP3beta, SLIM, and ALS significantly outperform Prod2Vec and LightFM in laboratory tests for job recommendations
- ALS and RP3beta increase user engagement in online A/B tests, with RP3beta showing a 20% greater impact than ALS
- The study introduces a large dataset of 65 million job interactions by 3.3 million users on 185,395 job ads, published on Kaggle

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RP3beta and SLIM outperform LightFM and Prod2Vec because they leverage deterministic item-item similarity matrices, which are more robust in highly sparse datasets.
- Mechanism: In highly sparse datasets, user representations learned via matrix factorization (LightFM) or item representations learned via neural embeddings (Prod2Vec) suffer from high variance and poor generalization for users/items with few interactions. RP3beta computes item-item similarity using paths of length 3 on a user-item bipartite graph, while SLIM learns a sparse similarity matrix directly. These deterministic approaches avoid the noise inherent in learned embeddings.
- Core assumption: The deterministic item-item similarity is a more reliable signal than learned user/item embeddings when interaction data is extremely sparse.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "RP3beta, SLIM, and ALS perform significantly better than Prod2Vec and LightFM when tested in a laboratory setting."
  - [section] "We believe that this is related to the high sparsity of our dataset. RP3Beta calculates the recommendations in a deterministic way... All other approaches utilize machine learning techniques for finding user representations... which might be challenging in case of users or items with a low number of interactions."

- Break condition: If the dataset becomes denser, learned representations may outperform deterministic similarity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ALS performs well because it is a well-established matrix factorization method optimized for implicit feedback and scalable to large datasets.
- Mechanism: ALS optimizes the implicit feedback objective by alternating between fixing user and item factors, leading to stable and accurate recommendations. Its scalability allows it to handle millions of users and items efficiently.
- Core assumption: The implicit feedback objective function is well-suited for the interaction data in classifieds.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Online A/B tests also demonstrated that sending messages with recommendations generated by the ALS and RP3beta models increases the number of users contacting advertisers."
  - [section] "The ALS model (Alternating Least Squares)... is an example of a matrix factorization method designed for implicit feedback datasets. We chose this method because of its proven performance, scalability and popularity in research and industry."

- Break condition: If the implicit feedback assumption is violated (e.g., if negative feedback is available).

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Online A/B tests validate the laboratory results because they measure the actual business impact (user engagement) in a real-world setting.
- Mechanism: Laboratory metrics like precision@10 correlate with online engagement metrics. By testing the top-performing models (ALS and RP3beta) in an A/B test, the study confirms that improved precision translates to increased user activity.
- Core assumption: Laboratory precision@10 is a good proxy for online user engagement in the classifieds domain.

## Limitations
- The paper's findings are based on a single classifieds domain (job recommendations), limiting generalizability to other domains
- The study relies on laboratory experiments and a single online A/B test, which may not fully capture real-world performance
- Privacy-preserving preprocessing steps introduce uncertainty about the exact nature of the training data

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The laboratory comparison of collaborative filtering methods (ALS, LightFM, Prod2Vec, RP3beta, SLIM) and their relative performance in terms of accuracy and diversity. The A/B test results showing that ALS and RP3beta increase user engagement are also highly reliable.
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism explanations for why RP3beta and SLIM outperform other methods in sparse datasets, as they rely on assumptions about the robustness of deterministic item-item similarity matrices.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the findings to other classifieds domains or denser datasets, as the study is limited to the OLX Jobs dataset and its specific characteristics.
