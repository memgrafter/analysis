# Que2Engage: Embedding-based Retrieval for Relevant and Engaging Products at Facebook Marketplace

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11052
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11052
- Authors: Multiple Facebook Marketplace researchers
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: 4.5% increase in user engagement with neutral NDCG in A/B test

## Executive Summary
Que2Engage addresses the challenge of balancing semantic relevance with user engagement in Facebook Marketplace search. The paper proposes a multimodal and multitask embedding-based retrieval system that incorporates contextual product features and trains jointly on relevance and engagement objectives. This approach enhances the retrieval stage with richer signals and balances different business goals. Offline evaluations show significant improvements in engagement prediction while maintaining relevance, and a two-week A/B test demonstrates a 4.5% increase in user engagement with neutral NDCG.

## Method Summary
Que2Engage is a two-tower neural network with query and document towers that process text, images, and contextual features through multimodal fusion. The model uses multitask learning with contrastive loss for relevance and binary cross-entropy for engagement prediction. Training incorporates modality dropout to prevent overfitting to specific modalities. The system is trained on 150M query-product pairs with engagement labels and evaluated on both relevance and engagement datasets using ROC-AUC metrics.

## Key Results
- 4.5% increase in user engagement in two-week A/B test
- Significant improvements in engagement prediction offline while maintaining relevance
- Neutral NDCG (no significant change in relevance ranking quality)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multitask training with contrastive learning and engagement prediction improves retrieval performance by directly optimizing for user engagement while maintaining relevance.
- Mechanism: By combining relevance loss (contrastive learning) and engagement loss (binary cross-entropy on displayed but non-engaged pairs), the model learns to distinguish engaging products from relevant but unengaging ones.
- Core assumption: Products that are semantically relevant may not be engaging, and engagement signals can be learned from user interaction data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multimodal fusion with contextual information as a modality improves engagement prediction by incorporating product attributes like price and condition into the retrieval model.
- Mechanism: Contextual features (price, category, condition) are encoded as a "context token" and fused with text and image tokens through a transformer, allowing the model to consider product attributes when ranking results.
- Core assumption: Contextual information is a distinguishing factor between engaging and non-engaging products among relevant results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Modality dropout prevents overfitting to specific modalities and improves model robustness.
- Mechanism: Randomly masking out text, image, and context tokens during training forces the model to learn robust representations that don't overly rely on any single modality.
- Core assumption: The model should perform well even when some modalities are missing during inference.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Query text → Query tower (XLM encoder + EmbeddingBag) → Document tower (text, image, context features) → Multimodal fusion → Cosine similarity → Ranking
- Critical path: Query → Query tower → Document tower → Multimodal fusion → Cosine similarity → Ranking
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using pre-computed image embeddings vs. fine-tuning image encoders (memory vs. performance)
  - Early fusion vs. late fusion for multimodal information
  - Batch size vs. training stability and memory consumption
- Failure signatures:
  - High relevance but low engagement scores: Model is learning relevance but not engagement
  - Low relevance scores: Contrastive learning is not working properly
  - Memory errors during training: Batch size too large for available GPU memory

## Limitations
- Dataset composition and quality details are limited, with engagement dataset relying on displayed-but-not-engaged pairs that may not represent true negatives
- Generalizability is constrained as the approach is only evaluated on Facebook Marketplace data
- Trade-off analysis is incomplete - long-term user satisfaction, diversity impacts, and computational costs are not discussed

## Confidence
**High Confidence Claims:**
- The Que2Engage architecture with multimodal fusion and multitask learning is technically sound
- Offline evaluation improvements in engagement prediction are measurable and statistically significant
- The two-week A/B test showing 4.5% engagement lift provides strong real-world validation

**Medium Confidence Claims:**
- The claim that Que2Engage achieves "neutral" NDCG while improving engagement
- The effectiveness of modality dropout in preventing overfitting

**Low Confidence Claims:**
- The exact contribution of each individual component to the overall performance improvement
- The scalability of the approach to different marketplaces or product categories
