# Automated stance detection in complex topics and small languages: the challenging case of immigration in polarizing news media

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.13047
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13047
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Zero-shot ChatGPT achieves comparable stance detection accuracy to fine-tuned BERT models for immigration-related sentences in Estonian news.

## Executive Summary
This study tackles automated stance detection for the complex socio-political topic of immigration in the low-resource language of Estonian. Using a manually annotated dataset of 7,345 sentences from two Estonian news sources, the authors fine-tune multiple large language models and compare their performance to a zero-shot approach using ChatGPT. The results show that both approaches achieve similar accuracy, with the best supervised model (Est-RoBERTa) reaching an F1-macro score of 0.66 and ChatGPT achieving 0.65. When applied to seven years of news data, the method reveals that one outlet is generally anti-immigration while the other maintains a neutral stance, with shifts correlating to real-world events like the UN migration pact and the Ukraine war.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune multiple BERT-based models (Est-RoBERTa, EstBERT, XLM-RoBERTa, mBERT) on 7,345 manually annotated Estonian sentences classified as anti-immigration, neutral, or pro-immigration. They use 5-fold cross-validation with class weights to address imbalance. A zero-shot approach using ChatGPT is implemented with a specific prompt format. Both methods are evaluated on the same test set using F1-macro scores, and the best model is applied to a larger corpus of 89,011 immigration-related sentences from 2015-2022 to analyze diachronic stance trends across two news outlets.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned Est-RoBERTa achieves an F1-macro score of 0.66 for stance classification
- Zero-shot ChatGPT achieves comparable performance with an F1-macro score of 0.65
- Applied to seven years of data, the model reveals one news outlet as generally anti-immigration while the other maintains neutral stance
- Stance shifts in the data correlate with real-world events including the UN migration pact and the Ukraine war

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Low-resource language stance detection is feasible with fine-tuned LLMs.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained multilingual or language-specific BERT models can be fine-tuned on a small annotated dataset to classify immigration stance.
- Core assumption: Sentence-level text is sufficient for capturing stance in morphologically complex languages like Estonian.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Supervised achieves acceptable performance, and ChatGPT yields similar accuracy."
  - [section]: "The best-performing fine-tuned model was based on Est-RoBERTa, achieving an acceptable F1-macro score of 0.66"
- Break condition: Insufficient annotated data or overly ambiguous sentences without enough context.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Zero-shot classification with ChatGPT can match fine-tuned models.
- Mechanism: ChatGPT can be instructed via prompt to classify stance without fine-tuning, leveraging its general language understanding.
- Core assumption: The model's training includes sufficient socio-political context to infer stance from Estonian text.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "ChatGPT yields similar accuracy... potentially simpler and cheaper alternative"
  - [section]: "comparable performance of zero-shot ChatGPT with the best models shows it could serve as a viable but cheaper alternative"
- Break condition: Prompt engineering fails to disambiguate complex sarcasm or context-dependent expressions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Media stance shifts correlate with real-world events.
- Mechanism: Applying the stance model to historical news data reveals temporal patterns aligning with events like elections and geopolitical crises.
- Core assumption: Stance changes in news reflect actual shifts in editorial or societal positions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "shifts correlating to real-world events like the UN migration pact and the Ukraine war"
  - [section]: "qualitative correspondences between changes in stance and relate them to events such as Estonian parliamentary elections in 2019 and the start of the Russian invasion to Ukraine of 2022"
- Break condition: Stance classifier accuracy is too low to detect subtle shifts, or media events are not well-represented in the dataset.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the performance characteristics of zero-shot ChatGPT compare to fine-tuned models when applied to even lower-resource languages than Estonian?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "We expect zero-shot accuracy will improve as generative AI models are being improved and developed" and that "We are making our annotated dataset of 7345 sentences public, which we foresee could be of interest to the Estonian NLP as well as media and communications studies communities, as well as applications of multilingual NLP and crosslingual transfer learning."
- Why unresolved: The study only tested ChatGPT on Estonian. Performance on languages with even fewer resources and less digital content is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison studies applying zero-shot ChatGPT to languages with fewer speakers and less digital content than Estonian.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would using longer text units (paragraphs) instead of sentences affect stance detection accuracy for complex political topics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that "The length of articles varies greatly, as does the length of a paragraph across articles, and some articles lack paragraph splits. Secondly, longer text sequences may include multiple stances, which may confuse both human annotators and machine classifiers."
- Why unresolved: The study used sentences as the unit of analysis, but notes that "paragraph level analysis does have the limitation of missing potentially important contextual information across sentences."
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing paragraph-level vs sentence-level classification on the same dataset.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would different prompt engineering approaches affect ChatGPT's zero-shot classification accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "Accuracy and model bias should still be evaluated. For example, in our case it could have been used to further classify sentences as expressing opinions, factual descriptions, and direct quotes."
- Why unresolved: The study used a specific prompt format but notes that "using generative models requires careful prompt engineering" and that "slightly different instructions can lead to differences in classification performance."
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of different prompt formulations on the same dataset to measure performance variations.

## Limitations
- Modest classification accuracy (F1-macro 0.66) suggests challenges in handling Estonian's morphological complexity
- Zero-shot performance may not generalize to other low-resource languages without careful prompt adaptation
- Correlation between stance shifts and real-world events is qualitative rather than statistically validated

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The general feasibility of stance detection in low-resource languages using fine-tuned BERT models and zero-shot approaches
- **Medium confidence**: The specific F1-macro scores (0.66 for Est-RoBERTa, 0.65 for ChatGPT) and their relative ranking
- **Low confidence**: The real-world event correlation claims, which lack rigorous statistical validation

## Next Checks
1. Calculate inter-annotator reliability metrics (Cohen's kappa or Krippendorff's alpha) on a subset of the annotated data to establish consistency of human stance judgments
2. Retrain the best model using paragraph or article-level units instead of sentences to determine if modest F1 scores result from insufficient context
3. Apply the same ChatGPT zero-shot approach to a different socio-political topic in Estonian (e.g., climate policy) using identical prompt engineering to test generalizability beyond immigration
