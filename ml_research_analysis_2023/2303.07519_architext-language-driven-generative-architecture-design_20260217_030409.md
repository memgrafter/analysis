# Architext: Language-Driven Generative Architecture Design

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07519
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07519
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Large language models can generate valid and diverse residential layouts at over 99% validity rate

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that fine-tuned large language models can consistently generate valid and diverse residential architectural layouts from natural language prompts. The Architext system achieves near-perfect geometric validity while maintaining semantic correctness across various prompt types, with larger models (GPT-J) significantly outperforming smaller ones. The work establishes language models as viable generators for architectural design exploration, opening new possibilities for integrating large language models into generative design workflows.

## Method Summary
The approach fine-tunes large language models on synthetic datasets where residential floor plans are represented as sequences of coordinate tuples and semantic annotations. The system uses a geometric-to-language encoding strategy, treating rooms as labeled polygons with adjacency and location constraints. The synthetic dataset contains 250,000 residential layouts generated through deterministic algorithms. The models are evaluated using 58 natural language prompts covering various design scenarios, with metrics including validity (geometric correctness), correctness (semantic alignment with prompts), and diversity (out-of-distribution generation).

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned language models generate valid residential layouts at near 100% rate
- Large-scale GPT-J (6B parameters) achieves 25-83% correctness across various prompt types
- Scaling model size improves semantic accuracy while maintaining design diversity
- Architext successfully generates out-of-distribution designs by prompting near training distribution boundaries

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Large language models can learn to generate valid architectural layouts by treating geometric shapes as language tokens
- **Mechanism:** The models are fine-tuned on a synthetic dataset where each room is represented as a sequence of coordinate tuples and labels, effectively converting geometric constraints into sequential language patterns
- **Core assumption:** The geometric relationships between rooms can be encoded as valid language sequences that the model can learn to reproduce
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Architext models are able to learn the specific design task, generating valid residential layouts at a near 100% rate"
  - [section] "Fine-tuning these PLMs on semantic representations of floor plans is the underlying method featured in Architext"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't contain direct evidence about geometric-to-language encoding
- **Break condition:** If the coordinate representation fails to capture essential geometric constraints, the model will generate invalid layouts

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Scaling model size improves correctness by enabling better understanding of complex spatial constraints
- **Mechanism:** Larger models (like GPT-J) have more parameters to capture nuanced relationships between room types, adjacency requirements, and spatial constraints expressed in natural language prompts
- **Core assumption:** The increased parameter count allows the model to learn more complex patterns in the data
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Large-scale GPT-J (6B parameters) outperforms smaller models, achieving 25-83% correctness across various prompt types"
  - [section] "Accuracy shows great improvement when scaling the models, with the largest model (GPT-J) yielding impressive accuracy ranging between 25% to over 80% for different prompt categories"
  - [corpus] Missing - corpus neighbors don't discuss scaling effects on architectural generation
- **Break condition:** If the training data lacks diversity in spatial configurations, even large models cannot learn to generalize beyond seen patterns

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Out-of-distribution generation is possible when prompts target layout configurations near the boundary of the training distribution
- **Mechanism:** By prompting for extreme values (like maximum or minimum room counts), the model accesses latent space regions containing diverse, unseen designs
- **Core assumption:** The model's latent space contains meaningful representations of layouts beyond those explicitly in the training data
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "A strategy that could assist OOD layout generation is to prompt the model near the 'boundary' of its training distribution"
  - [section] "GPT-J yields higher Wasserstein distance for out-of-distribution generations across all prompt categories"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't contain evidence about OOD generation strategies
- **Break condition:** If the model overfits to training data distributions, it will fail to generate valid designs in OOD regions

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Synthetic data generator -> Fine-tuning pipeline -> Evaluation scripts -> Sampling interface
- **Critical path:** 1. Generate synthetic layouts → 2. Create semantic annotations → 3. Fine-tune PLM → 4. Generate layouts from prompts → 5. Evaluate validity and correctness
- **Design tradeoffs:** Language representation vs. direct geometric representation: Language enables PLM usage but may lose precision; Synthetic vs. real data: Synthetic ensures quality but may lack real-world diversity; Fine-tuning vs. prompt engineering: Fine-tuning requires more resources but enables better performance
- **Failure signatures:** High validity but low correctness: Model learns geometry but not semantics; Low validity: Geometric representation or post-processing is flawed; No OOD generation: Model overfits to training distribution
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Generate 100 layouts with a simple prompt ("a house with 5 rooms") and check validity rates; 2. Compare validity/correctness between GPT-2 and GPT-J on the same prompts; 3. Test OOD generation by prompting for extreme configurations (e.g., 10 rooms) and measuring success rate

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Question:** How do Architext models perform when generating architectural designs for building types other than residential layouts?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper focuses specifically on residential layouts and mentions that the semantic representation approach could be generalized to other design tasks, but does not provide experimental evidence for this
- **Why unresolved:** The study only evaluated Architext on residential floor plans, leaving the performance on other architectural domains unexplored
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Testing Architext models on commercial, industrial, or institutional building designs and comparing performance metrics across different building types

- **Question:** What is the relationship between the complexity of natural language prompts and the correctness of generated designs?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses various prompt categories but does not systematically analyze how prompt complexity (length, number of constraints, syntactic structure) affects generation accuracy
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper evaluates different prompt categories, it doesn't investigate the direct relationship between prompt complexity and model performance
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting controlled experiments varying prompt complexity while keeping other factors constant, and measuring the impact on correctness rates

- **Question:** How do Architext models perform in real-world design workflows compared to traditional parametric design tools?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper discusses potential applications but acknowledges the need for human user studies in real-world settings
- **Why unresolved:** The study is limited to synthetic data and controlled experiments without deployment in actual architectural practice
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting longitudinal studies where architects use Architext in their daily workflow, comparing productivity, design quality, and user satisfaction against traditional tools

## Limitations
- The synthetic dataset generation process is not fully reproducible from the paper
- Semantic correctness evaluation relies on author-defined criteria without external validation
- No comparison to alternative architectural generation methods (rule-based or GAN-based)

## Confidence
- High confidence: Validity metric (>99%) and its underlying geometric validation process
- Medium confidence: Correctness scores and their correlation with prompt types
- Low confidence: Out-of-distribution generation claims due to limited testing of truly novel layouts
