# Low Rank Optimization for Efficient Deep Learning: Making A Balance between Compact Architecture and Fast Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13635
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13635
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Effective rank achieves up to 4× higher compression ratios than other sparsity measures for network compression

## Executive Summary
This survey paper explores low-rank optimization techniques for efficient deep learning, addressing the critical challenges of high computational complexity and storage costs in modern neural networks. The authors categorize model compression approaches into pre-train, pre-set, and compression-aware methods, while examining integration with pruning, sparsification, quantization, and entropy coding. The work identifies effective rank as the most efficient sparsity measure for network compression and demonstrates a fundamental trade-off between spatial efficiency (model compression) and temporal efficiency (subspace training) in tensorized neural networks.

## Method Summary
The paper provides a comprehensive survey of low-rank optimization techniques in deep learning, systematically categorizing compression methods and analyzing their integration with other efficiency techniques. The authors establish a theoretical framework for understanding the trade-offs between spatial and temporal efficiency, proposing a balanced approach that combines model compression with subspace training. Through extensive literature review and experimental analysis, they demonstrate that effective rank serves as the most efficient sparsity measure for network compression, enabling significant improvements in compression ratios while maintaining model performance.

## Key Results
- Effective rank outperforms other sparsity measures with up to 4× higher compression ratios
- Spatial efficiency (model compression) and temporal efficiency (subspace training) are inversely related in tensorized neural networks
- The balanced approach combining model compression with subspace training optimizes both memory and training time under moderate compression regimes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of low-rank optimization stems from exploiting the inherent low-rank structure present in many neural network weight matrices and tensors. By decomposing high-dimensional weight tensors into lower-rank components, the technique reduces both storage requirements and computational complexity while preserving essential information flow. The mechanism works by identifying and retaining the most informative components of the network, allowing for aggressive compression without significant performance degradation.

## Foundational Learning
- **Tensor Decomposition**: Breaking down high-dimensional tensors into lower-rank components
  - Why needed: Reduces storage and computation while preserving essential information
  - Quick check: Verify rank preservation through singular value decomposition analysis

- **Effective Rank**: A sparsity measure that captures the intrinsic dimensionality of weight matrices
  - Why needed: More efficient than traditional sparsity measures for network compression
- **Subspace Training**: Training in lower-dimensional subspaces to reduce computational overhead
  - Why needed: Enables faster training while maintaining model expressiveness
  - Quick check: Monitor training stability and convergence speed

- **Compression-Aware Training**: Training methods that explicitly account for compression during optimization
  - Why needed: Ensures models are optimized for both performance and efficiency
  - Quick check: Validate end-to-end performance after compression

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Low-Rank Optimization -> Model Compression -> Subspace Training -> Tensor Decomposition

**Critical Path**: Data flows through compressed low-rank layers during both training and inference, with subspace training providing the temporal efficiency gains.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The primary tradeoff is between compression ratio (spatial efficiency) and training speed (temporal efficiency). Higher compression leads to faster inference but potentially slower training due to increased complexity in maintaining low-rank structures.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Training instability when effective rank is set too low
- Performance degradation beyond certain compression thresholds
- Suboptimal convergence when subspace dimensions are mismatched
