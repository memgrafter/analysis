# Boosting Adversarial Transferability using Dynamic Cues

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12252
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12252
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: Temporal prompts significantly improve adversarial transferability from image models to both image and video models

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a novel approach to boost adversarial transferability by inducing dynamic temporal cues within pre-trained image models. The core idea is to learn task-specific temporal prompts through a learnable transformation that captures motion dynamics without affecting the original spatial representations. These temporal prompts are then used to generate adversarial perturbations that effectively fool both image and video models. The proposed method significantly improves adversarial transferability from image-to-video and image-to-image models compared to current state-of-the-art approaches. For example, an iterative attack from a DeiT-B model with temporal prompts reduces the top-1 accuracy of a video model by 35% on the Kinetics-400 dataset. Additionally, the method improves adversarial transferability to image models by 9% on ImageNet.

## Method Summary
The method introduces temporal prompts into pre-trained image models to capture motion dynamics. A learnable transformation block applies self-attention over video frames to generate temporal tokens, which are then concatenated with spatial tokens and class tokens for a frozen ViT. During training, the model learns to use both spatial and temporal information for video classification while preserving the original spatial representations. Adversarial attacks are generated by optimizing perturbations based on gradients from both spatial and temporal branches, with the loss aggregated from both supervised and self-supervised objectives. The approach also explores multi-resolution training as a regularization technique.

## Key Results
- Temporal prompts reduce video model top-1 accuracy by 35% on Kinetics-400 using DeiT-B as surrogate
- 9% improvement in adversarial transferability to image models on ImageNet
- Improved black-box attack success rates across multiple attack methods (FGSM, PGD, MIM, DIM)
- Demonstrates image models can effectively attack video models without specialized architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Temporal prompts inject motion dynamics into static image models by learning a self-attention transformation that captures inter-frame relationships.
- **Mechanism**: A frozen ViT processes concatenated spatial and temporal tokens. The temporal tokens come from a learnable transformation block that applies self-attention over all video frames, then pools spatially to produce one prompt per frame. These temporal prompts interact with the image class token through the model's self-attention layers, allowing the model to represent both spatial and temporal cues without modifying the original architecture.
- **Core assumption**: Self-attention over concatenated video frames can model motion dynamics effectively, and the resulting temporal prompts can be used in adversarial attacks to fool video models.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "We optimize temporal prompts through frozen image models to capture motion dynamics."
  - [section] "Our transformationT is a single self-attention block... It learns the relationship between the video frames."
  - [corpus] Weak; no direct evidence found.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Adversarial attacks optimized using temporal prompts transfer better to video models because they exploit both spatial and temporal cues within the same model.
- **Mechanism**: During attack optimization, gradients are aggregated from both the spatial branch (using the original image class token) and the temporal branch (using the average of refined temporal tokens). This dual-objective loss (supervised + self-supervised) maximizes the attack's effectiveness against video models by leveraging both spatial and temporal features.
- **Core assumption**: Video models rely heavily on temporal information, so perturbations that include temporal cues will be more effective against them than purely spatial perturbations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Attacking such prompts maximizes the adversarial transferability from image-to-video and image-to-image models using the attacks designed for image models."
  - [section] "Our attack exploits the spatial and temporal cues to generate transferable perturbations for both image and video models."
  - [corpus] Weak; no direct evidence found.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Training temporal prompts at lower spatial resolutions acts as a regularization effect that improves adversarial transferability.
- **Mechanism**: By adapting image models to different spatial scales (e.g., 56×56, 96×96, 224×224), the model learns to mimic dynamic behavior even on static images. This scale-space approach creates prompts that are more robust to resolution variations and improves generalization during adversarial attacks.
- **Core assumption**: Low-resolution training provides regularization that enhances model robustness and transferability, similar to findings in supervised learning.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "We observe that fine-tuning scale-space prompts at different low resolutions... also has a complimentary regularization effect on the transferability of adversarial perturbations."
  - [corpus] Weak; no direct evidence found.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  - Video frames → Transformation (self-attention block) → Temporal prompts
  - Single frame → Spatial tokens
  - Concatenate spatial tokens + temporal prompts + class tokens → Frozen ViT → Refined tokens
  - Average refined temporal tokens → Temporal head → Video classification output
  - Refined spatial class token → Spatial head → Image classification output

- **Critical path**: Video frames → Transformation → Temporal prompts → ViT → Temporal head (for video tasks)
  - Alternative path: Single frame → Spatial tokens → ViT → Spatial head (for image tasks)

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using a single transformation layer limits computational complexity but may restrict temporal modeling capacity.
  - Freezing the ViT preserves original spatial representations but may limit adaptation to video tasks.
  - Training at multiple resolutions improves regularization but increases training time.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Low temporal prompt quality: Poor adversarial transferability to video models
  - Overfitting to specific resolutions: Poor generalization across different input sizes
  - Ineffective transformation: No improvement over baseline attacks

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify that temporal prompts can be learned by training the transformation on a small video dataset and checking output quality
  2. Test adversarial transferability from the adapted model to a known video model using a simple attack (FGSM)
  3. Compare transferability with and without temporal prompts to quantify improvement

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does the number of temporal prompts affect adversarial transferability across different attack types?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states "We observe non-trivial gains in adversarial transferability as we increase our temporal prompts" (Table 11) and shows results for 8, 16, and 32 prompts
- **Why unresolved**: While the paper demonstrates gains from 8 to 32 prompts, it doesn't explore the full range or identify an optimal number. The relationship between prompt count and transferability efficiency (computational cost vs. performance gain) remains unclear.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic experiments varying prompt numbers (e.g., 4, 8, 16, 32, 64) across multiple attack methods and datasets, measuring both transferability improvement and computational overhead.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: What is the theoretical relationship between spatial resolution changes and temporal dynamic modeling effectiveness?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper shows that "fine-tuning scale-space prompts at different low resolutions...has a complimentary regularization effect on the transferability" (Section 2.2.1) and demonstrates improvements when using multiple resolutions
- **Why unresolved**: The paper demonstrates empirical benefits but doesn't provide theoretical justification for why spatial resolution changes effectively mimic temporal dynamics, or how this relates to the frequency content of adversarial perturbations.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A theoretical framework explaining the connection between spatial scale-space and temporal dynamics, possibly through Fourier analysis or information theory, combined with experiments testing whether this principle holds for other types of dynamics beyond motion.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does the proposed temporal prompt approach compare to specialized video architectures in terms of attack transferability efficiency?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper claims that "Image models are effective surrogates to optimize an adversarial attack to fool black-box models in a changing environment over time" and shows competitive results against TimesFormer, ResNet3D, and MVCNN, but doesn't directly compare computational efficiency
- **Why unresolved**: While the paper shows that temporal prompts achieve good transferability, it doesn't quantify the computational trade-off between using adapted image models versus specialized video architectures for attack generation.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Direct computational efficiency comparisons measuring attack generation time, GPU memory usage, and transferability rates between the temporal prompt approach and various specialized video architectures across multiple attack scenarios.

## Limitations
- The learnable transformation implementation details are underspecified, making exact reproduction challenging
- Limited theoretical justification for why spatial resolution changes effectively model temporal dynamics
- No direct computational efficiency comparison with specialized video architectures

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1: Medium - Theoretical soundness but limited direct evidence
- Mechanism 2: Medium - Reasonable assumption but not empirically verified
- Mechanism 3: Low - Empirical observation without theoretical backing
