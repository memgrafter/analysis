# Improved knowledge distillation by utilizing backward pass knowledge in neural networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12006
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12006
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Up to 4% improvement on MNIST, 1% on CIFAR-10, and average 1.5% on GLUE benchmark

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes an improved knowledge distillation method that utilizes backward pass knowledge in neural networks. The key innovation is generating auxiliary training samples that maximize divergence between teacher and student networks by perturbing data samples along the gradient of the difference between the two networks. This approach augments the training data with samples from regions where the student and teacher differ most, leading to better matching across the entire input space. The method is evaluated on computer vision and NLP tasks, showing significant performance improvements over standard knowledge distillation approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed method improves knowledge distillation by generating auxiliary training samples through a two-step process. First, a maximization step defines a backward KD loss measuring the L2 norm between teacher and student outputs, then perturbs training samples in the gradient direction to maximize this divergence. Second, a minimization step trains the student network using both original and auxiliary samples with the standard KD loss. The process iterates for multiple hyper-epochs, with pre-training and fine-tuning phases using the original KD loss. For NLP tasks, the method transforms gradients between embedding spaces using a transformation matrix to handle discrete tokens.

## Key Results
- MNIST: Up to 4% improvement in classification accuracy compared to baseline KD
- CIFAR-10: 1% improvement in classification accuracy with inception v3 teacher and mobilenet v2 student
- GLUE benchmark: Average 1.5% improvement across NLP tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The method improves knowledge distillation by generating auxiliary samples in regions where student and teacher diverge most. The backward KD loss measures divergence using the L2 norm between student and teacher outputs. By taking the gradient of this scalar loss w.r.t. the input, we can perturb training samples toward the direction that maximizes divergence, generating new auxiliary samples in those regions.

### Mechanism 2
The iterative min-max procedure (maximization step followed by minimization step) leads to better student-teacher matching over the entire input space. The maximization step identifies worst-case divergence regions and generates auxiliary samples there. The minimization step then trains the student using both original and auxiliary samples, forcing the student to match the teacher in these previously uncovered regions.

### Mechanism 3
The method works for NLP applications by transforming gradients between different embedding spaces. For NLP tasks with discrete tokens, the method takes gradients with respect to student embedding vectors and uses a transformation matrix Q to map these to teacher embedding vectors, allowing gradient-based perturbation in the embedding space.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher network -> Student network -> Loss functions (original KD, backward KD) -> Perturbation mechanism -> Auxiliary samples -> Augmented dataset
- Critical path:
  1. Initialize student network
  2. Pre-train student with original KD for e epochs
  3. For h hyper-epochs:
     - Generate auxiliary samples by perturbing training samples along gradient of LBKD
     - Combine auxiliary samples with training data
     - Train student with original KD on augmented dataset for e epochs
  4. Fine-tune student with original KD on original training data for e epochs
- Design tradeoffs: Number of hyper-epochs (h) vs. training time, perturbation rate (η) - too small yields insufficient exploration, too large yields invalid samples, whether to keep or discard auxiliary samples between iterations, computational cost of gradient computation for large models
- Failure signatures: Student performance degrades compared to baseline KD, auxiliary samples fall outside valid input space, training becomes unstable with oscillating losses, NLP embeddings diverge after transformation
- First 3 experiments: 1) Synthetic experiment with polynomial functions to visualize the method, 2) MNIST classification with extremely small student network (160x smaller than teacher), 3) CIFAR-10 classification with inception v3 teacher and mobilenet v2 student

## Limitations
- Effectiveness relies heavily on assumption that gradient-based perturbations accurately identify meaningful divergence regions, with limited empirical validation
- Perturbation rate hyperparameter (η) is not thoroughly explored and likely depends on specific task and network architectures
- Computational overhead of generating auxiliary samples through gradient ascent may be significant, particularly for large models

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (gradient-based auxiliary sample generation): Medium confidence - The theoretical foundation is sound, but empirical validation is limited
- Mechanism 2 (min-max procedure): Medium confidence - The iterative approach is logical, but the optimal hyperparameters are unclear
- Mechanism 3 (NLP embedding space transformations): Low confidence - The transformation is derived theoretically but lacks empirical validation
