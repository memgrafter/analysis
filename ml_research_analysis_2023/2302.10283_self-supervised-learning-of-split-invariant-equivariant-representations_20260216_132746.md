# Self-supervised learning of Split Invariant Equivariant representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10283
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10283
- Reference count: 18
- SIE achieves R²=0.73 on rotation prediction versus 0.60 for previous approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SIE (Split Invariant-Equivariant), a self-supervised learning method that learns representations split into invariant and equivariant parts using a hypernetwork-based predictor. The authors create 3DIEBench, a dataset of 2.5 million images from 55 classes of 3D objects with controllable transformations, to evaluate both invariant (classification) and equivariant (rotation prediction) tasks. SIE achieves significant performance gains on equivariant tasks compared to existing methods, with R²=0.73 on rotation prediction versus 0.60 for previous approaches.

## Method Summary
SIE combines split representations with a hypernetwork-based predictor to learn both invariant and equivariant features. The method uses VICReg loss with separate invariance and equivariance criteria, where the hypernetwork generates predictor weights based on transformation parameters to prevent collapse to invariance. The representation y is split into y_inv (invariant information) and y_equi (equivariant information), with separate projection heads applied to each part before the hypernetwork predictor transforms the equivariant component.

## Key Results
- SIE achieves R²=0.73 on rotation prediction task, compared to 0.60 for previous approaches
- Significant performance gains on equivariant tasks while maintaining competitive performance on invariant classification
- The hypernetwork predictor prevents collapse to invariance that occurs in traditional predictor architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The hypernetwork-based predictor prevents collapse to invariance by forcing the predictor to depend on transformation parameters.
- **Mechanism**: The hypernetwork H: G → R^w takes group elements g as input and produces predictor weights ψ = H(g). Since the predictor is a linear transformation with weights ψ, it cannot ignore g without setting all weights to zero, which would result in a degenerate predictor.
- **Core assumption**: The transformation parameters g contain sufficient information to parameterize the predictor and prevent trivial solutions.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We further introduce a predictor architecture based on hypernetworks to learn equivariant representations with no possible collapse to invariance."
  - [section 4.2]: "To ensure that the transformation parameters are taken into account by the predictor, we introduce a predictor architecture based on hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2016)."
- **Break condition**: If the transformation parameters g are not informative enough about the transformation, the predictor may still collapse or fail to learn meaningful equivariant representations.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Splitting representations into invariant and equivariant parts improves performance on equivariant tasks.
- **Mechanism**: By separating the representation y into y_inv (invariant information) and y_equi (equivariant information), the model can focus on preserving transformation-related information in y_equi while maintaining invariance in y_inv. This separation allows for more targeted optimization of each part.
- **Core assumption**: The information in the representation can be meaningfully separated into invariant and equivariant components.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We introduce SIE (Split Invariant-Equivariant) which combines the hypernetwork-based predictor with representations split in two parts, one invariant, the other equivariant, to learn richer representations."
  - [section 4.2]: "We separate y (resp. y′) as y_inv which contains information that is preserved by the transformation, i.e. invariant information, and y_equi which contain information that was changed by the transformation, i.e., equivariant information."
- **Break condition**: If the information in the representation is not separable or if the split is not optimal, the performance gains may not materialize or could even degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The VICReg loss function with split representations provides both invariance and equivariance while avoiding representational collapse.
- **Mechanism**: The VICReg loss combines variance regularization (ensuring all dimensions are used) and covariance regularization (decorrelating dimensions) with invariance and equivariance similarity criteria. The split representations allow these criteria to be applied separately to invariant and equivariant parts.
- **Core assumption**: The VICReg loss function is suitable for learning both invariant and equivariant representations when combined with split representations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.2]: "We use VICReg (Bardes et al., 2021) as our basis since it lends itself well to split representation."
  - [section 4.2]: "In order to adapt its invariance criterion, we define our similarity criterion L_sim as L_sim(u, v) = ||u - v||^2_2."
- **Break condition**: If the VICReg loss parameters are not properly tuned or if the split representations are not optimal, the loss function may not effectively balance invariance and equivariance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Encoder (ResNet-18) -> Split representations (y_inv, y_equi) -> Projection heads (h_φ,inv, h_φ,equi) -> Embeddings (z_inv, z_equi) -> Predictor (p_ψ,g) -> Loss function (LSIE)

- **Critical path**:
  1. Encode input images to obtain representations y.
  2. Split y into invariant (y_inv) and equivariant (y_equi) parts.
  3. Apply separate projection heads to obtain embeddings z_inv and z_equi.
  4. Use the hypernetwork-based predictor to transform z_equi based on transformation parameters g.
  5. Compute the VICReg loss with separate invariance and equivariance criteria.
  6. Backpropagate the loss to update the encoder, projection heads, and hypernetwork.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Splitting representations allows for targeted optimization but may introduce information loss if the split is not optimal.
  - Using a hypernetwork-based predictor prevents collapse but increases model complexity and computational cost.
  - The VICReg loss function provides both invariance and equivariance but requires careful tuning of its parameters.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor performance on equivariant tasks may indicate that the predictor is not effectively learning the transformation or that the split representations are not optimal.
  - Collapse to invariance may occur if the predictor ignores the transformation parameters or if the loss function is not properly balancing invariance and equivariance.
  - Overfitting to the training data may result in poor generalization to unseen transformations or objects.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train the SIE model on the 3DIEBench dataset and evaluate its performance on rotation prediction and classification tasks.
  2. Compare the performance of the SIE model with and without the hypernetwork-based predictor to assess its impact on equivariant task performance.
  3. Analyze the learned representations by visualizing the nearest neighbors of encoded images and examining the quality of the predictor's transformations.

## Limitations
- The controlled environment of 3DIEBench may not capture all failure modes of the hypernetwork predictor
- VICReg loss parameters are not thoroughly explored, raising questions about robustness to hyperparameter choices
- The claim that split representations are "optimal" rather than "sufficient" lacks extensive ablation studies

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The SIE architecture design and implementation details (sections 3 and 4.2) are well-specified and reproducible. The 3DIEBench dataset creation methodology is clearly documented.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that SIE achieves "significant performance gains" on equivariant tasks is supported by the reported R²=0.73 versus 0.60 for previous approaches, but this comparison relies on a single dataset and specific metrics.
- **Medium confidence**: The assertion that hypernetwork-based predictors prevent collapse to invariance is plausible given the architectural constraints, but alternative failure modes are not fully explored.

## Next Checks

1. **Robustness Testing**: Evaluate SIE on out-of-distribution transformations and datasets (e.g., natural images with complex deformations) to assess whether the hypernetwork predictor maintains performance when transformation parameters are noisy or incomplete.

2. **Ablation Studies**: Systematically test alternative split strategies (different dimensional allocations, adaptive splitting) and VICReg parameter settings to determine the sensitivity of performance to these design choices.

3. **Theoretical Analysis**: Develop formal guarantees or bounds on when the hypernetwork architecture prevents collapse to invariance, connecting the empirical results to theoretical understanding of group representations and predictor architectures.
