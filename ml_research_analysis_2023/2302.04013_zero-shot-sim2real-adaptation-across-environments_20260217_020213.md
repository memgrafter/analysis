# Zero-shot Sim2Real Adaptation Across Environments

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04013
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04013
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: RAT achieves 0.228 ± 0.0016 average step-wise cumulative reward in Ant environment, outperforming Transfer (-0.0363 ± 0.0021) and DR (-0.0372 ± 0.0019) baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Reverse Action Transformation (RAT), a novel approach for zero-shot adaptation of reinforcement learning policies from simulation to real-world environments. The method learns to correct simulated actions so that real-world trajectories match simulated ones, enabling seamless transfer across different environment configurations without retraining. By combining domain randomization with reinforcement learning, RAT becomes robust to various reality gaps. The approach is evaluated on six continuous control tasks and demonstrates significant improvements over competing baselines in zero-shot transfer performance.

## Method Summary
The approach trains a Universal Policy Network (UPN) across a range of simulation parameters, then learns a RAT policy that predicts action corrections needed to make real-world trajectories match simulated ones. Domain randomization is used during RAT training to create a robust initial policy that can handle various hypothetical reality gaps. Once trained, RAT can transform UPN policies for adjacent environments with similar reality gaps without additional training, achieving zero-shot transfer capability.

## Key Results
- RAT achieves 0.228 ± 0.0016 average step-wise cumulative reward in Ant environment
- Outperforms Transfer policy (-0.0363 ± 0.0021) and DR policy (-0.0372 ± 0.0019) baselines
- Shows improved performance in adjacent environments with slightly different latent parameters
- Demonstrates effective bridging of the sim-to-real gap across six continuous control tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RAT learns to correct simulated actions so that the resulting real-world trajectory matches the simulated trajectory.
- Mechanism: A trained UPN produces actions in simulation; RAT predicts the difference (∆a) between simulated and real-world actions. This correction is applied to the simulated action before executing in the real-world. The reward is the negative squared Euclidean distance between the simulated and real-world next states.
- Core assumption: The reality gap is relatively fixed across adjacent environment configurations (modellable parameters).
- Break condition: If the reality gap changes significantly between adjacent environments, RAT corrections learned in one environment will not generalize.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Robust RAT initialization via domain randomization improves generalization to unknown reality gaps.
- Mechanism: During RAT training, a range of hypothetical reality gaps is sampled and applied in simulation to learn a policy robust to different gap values. This creates a good initial policy that can be deployed in real-world without retraining.
- Core assumption: The actual reality gap lies within the sampled range during robust initialization.
- Break condition: If the actual reality gap is outside the sampled range, the robust initialization will be poor.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: RAT enables zero-shot adaptation across environments with similar reality gaps by transforming UPN policies conditioned on new parameters.
- Mechanism: Once RAT is trained for a specific modellable parameter configuration, it can transform UPN policies for adjacent configurations (within εmax) without retraining, as long as the reality gap remains consistent.
- Core assumption: The reality gap (unmodellable parameters) remains consistent across adjacent environments.
- Break condition: If the reality gap changes between environments, zero-shot transfer fails.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Universal Policy Networks (UPNs)
  - Why needed here: UPNs provide a framework for learning policies across a continuous range of simulation parameters, enabling quick adaptation when environment parameters change.
  - Quick check question: How does a UPN's state representation differ from a standard RL policy?

- Concept: Domain Randomization
  - Why needed here: Domain randomization is used to create a robust RAT initial policy by training on a range of simulated reality gaps, improving generalization to unknown real-world conditions.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between using domain randomization for training the main policy vs. using it for RAT initialization?

- Concept: Action Transformation vs. State Transformation
  - Why needed here: RAT learns to transform actions to match simulated trajectories in the real world, rather than trying to transform the simulator's dynamics to match the real world.
  - Quick check question: Why might learning an action transformation be more sample-efficient than adapting each task-specific simulation policy to the real-world?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: UPN -> RAT policy -> Simulator with modellable parameters (θ) -> Real-world environment with unmodellable parameters (U) -> Domain randomization module

- Critical path:
  1. Train UPN on simulator with range of modellable parameters
  2. Train robust RAT initial policy using domain randomization to simulate various reality gaps
  3. Deploy UPN + RAT in real-world for zero-shot transfer
  4. (Optional) Fine-tune RAT with real-world interactions for few-shot adaptation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - UPN vs. separate policies for each parameter setting: UPN provides adaptability but may sacrifice some performance
  - RAT vs. direct policy transfer: RAT adds complexity but enables zero-shot transfer
  - Domain randomization range: Too narrow limits robustness, too wide may hurt performance

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance in real-world despite good simulation results: Reality gap outside training distribution
  - RAT corrections causing instability: Learning rate too high or reward shaping incorrect
  - Zero-shot transfer fails on adjacent environments: Reality gap changed between environments

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify UPN learns reasonable policies across parameter ranges in simulation
  2. Test RAT on single environment with known reality gap to confirm it learns correct corrections
  3. Evaluate zero-shot transfer performance on adjacent environments with consistent reality gap

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several important questions arise from the work:

1. How does the performance of RAT scale with increasing complexity of the reality gap?
2. What is the impact of different RL algorithms on the training efficiency and final performance of RAT?
3. How does RAT perform in environments with non-stationary reality gaps?

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to six MuJoCo tasks with only one fixed reality gap configuration (+400% mass and friction)
- Zero-shot transfer assumes consistent reality gap across adjacent environments, which may not hold in practice
- Comparison against Domain Randomization baseline appears limited and may not represent state-of-the-art domain adaptation methods

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of RAT learning action corrections is well-defined and the simulation results show clear improvements over baselines within the tested conditions
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about zero-shot transfer across adjacent environments are supported by experiments but limited by the narrow range of parameter deviations tested
- **Low confidence**: Generalization claims to arbitrary reality gaps and complex real-world scenarios are not sufficiently validated given the limited experimental scope

## Next Checks

1. **Reality gap robustness test**: Systematically evaluate RAT performance across a wider range of reality gap magnitudes (e.g., 100% to 1000% parameter changes) to determine the actual limits of zero-shot transfer capability.

2. **Adjacent environment stress test**: Test transfer performance on adjacent environments with non-linear parameter changes and combinations of multiple latent parameter deviations to validate the assumption of consistent reality gaps.

3. **Real-world deployment validation**: Implement RAT on a physical robot system with multiple tasks to verify that simulation-based performance gains translate to real-world operation, including measuring the actual reality gap versus the assumed gap.
