# Towards Trust of Explainable AI in Thyroid Nodule Diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04731
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04731
- Reference count: 35
- Key outcome: XAI methods (especially D-RISE and Grad-CAM++) enhance model transparency and build user trust in AI-assisted thyroid nodule diagnosis, with KDE and DM being particularly effective for explaining negative cases.

## Executive Summary
This paper applies state-of-the-art explainable AI (XAI) methods to interpret the decisions of a deep learning model for thyroid nodule diagnosis in ultrasound images. It evaluates multiple XAI methods including LIME, RISE, Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, LRP, D-RISE, and introduces two new statistic-based methods: Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) and Density Map (DM). The study compares these methods both qualitatively and quantitatively across different nodule detection cases using metrics like EBPG, IoU, Bbox, Drop, and Increase. User trust evaluation shows that saliency map-based methods (especially D-RISE and Grad-CAM++) achieve higher understandability and trust among radiologists, medical students, patients, and AI scientists, while KDE and DM are particularly effective for explaining cases where no nodule is detected.

## Method Summary
The study applies multiple XAI methods to a two-stage object detection model (Faster R-CNN/RetinaNet) for thyroid nodule diagnosis. It evaluates eight methods: LIME, RISE, Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, LRP, D-RISE, plus two proposed statistic-based methods (KDE and DM). KDE and DM explain cases where no nodule is detected by analyzing the distribution of predicted box centers from the first stage. The methods are evaluated on a Vietnamese thyroid ultrasound dataset (14,171 images, 970 patients) using quantitative metrics (EBPG, IoU, Bbox, Drop, Increase) and qualitative user trust surveys with 16 participants across different user groups.

## Key Results
- Saliency map-based XAI methods (D-RISE and Grad-CAM++) showed higher understandability and trust among all user groups compared to other methods
- KDE and DM methods were particularly effective for explaining negative cases (no nodule detected) with better plausibility than other methods
- D-RISE uniquely provides separate explanations for each detected object, showing the highest fidelity among all methods
- User trust was consistently higher for XAI methods using saliency maps across all nodule detection cases (TP, FP, TN, FN)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: KDE and DM provide interpretable visualizations of nodule detection behavior, especially for cases where no nodule is detected.
- Mechanism: KDE estimates the density of predicted box centers in the first stage, producing a continuous probability map. DM counts the frequency of model attention per pixel across predicted boxes, producing a heat map.
- Core assumption: The spatial distribution of first-stage predictions reflects the model's focus and uncertainty.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose new statistic-based XAI methods, namely Kernel Density Estimation and Density map, to explain the case of no nodule detected."
  - [section 3.2.1] "KDE creates a distribution density map by weighting distances of all data points for each specific location along the distribution."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct mention of KDE/DM in neighbor corpus, indicating novelty.
- Break condition: If first-stage predictions are highly variable or uniformly distributed, KDE and DM become uninformative.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: D-RISE produces more faithful explanations by directly linking to the localization stage's outputs.
- Mechanism: D-RISE perturbs the input at the superpixel level, then uses the model's bounding box scores to weight the perturbations, producing a saliency map per detected object.
- Core assumption: Bounding box scores accurately reflect the importance of input regions for localization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "D-RISE is the only method to show the explanation solely for the correct and incorrect boxes, while others explain both."
  - [section 3.1.1] "D-RISE is the only method having access to the localization block, it can give end-users separate explanations for each detected object."
  - [corpus] Weak: No mention of D-RISE in neighbors; novel application.
- Break condition: If the localization stage outputs many low-confidence boxes, D-RISE saliency maps become noisy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Saliency map-based XAI methods (Grad-CAM++, LIME, RISE, Ada-SISE) improve user trust because they are visually interpretable and highlight clinically relevant regions.
- Mechanism: These methods generate pixel-level importance maps that overlay on the image, allowing radiologists to visually correlate AI focus with their clinical judgment.
- Core assumption: Visual alignment between AI explanations and human expertise increases trust.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "methods using saliency maps (especially D-RISE and Grad-CAM++) showed higher understandability and trust among radiologists, medical students, patients, and AI scientists."
  - [section 4.3] "XAI methods using a saliency map as the explanation have consistently higher understandability with higher medians towards humans than others in all cases."
  - [corpus] Moderate: Related work on dermatology XAI shows similar trust gains (paper 205190).
- Break condition: If saliency maps frequently highlight irrelevant regions, trust may decrease.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Region Proposal Network (RPN) behavior in object detectors.
  - Why needed here: KDE and DM explain the RPN's proposal distribution, so understanding RPN mechanics is essential.
  - Quick check question: What does the RPN output in the first stage of Faster R-CNN, and how does it influence the second stage?
- Concept: Backpropagation and gradient-based saliency methods.
  - Why needed here: Grad-CAM and Grad-CAM++ rely on gradients from the final convolutional layer; knowing how gradients flow is key to interpreting their maps.
  - Quick check question: How do Grad-CAM and Grad-CAM++ differ in computing class-specific activation weights?
- Concept: Perturbation-based explanation principles.
  - Why needed here: LIME, RISE, and D-RISE perturb inputs to measure prediction changes; understanding this helps evaluate faithfulness.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between RISE and D-RISE in terms of the model stage they explain?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data pipeline → Faster R-CNN model (RPN → ROI pooling → classifier/regressor) → XAI module (per-stage) → evaluation → user survey
- Critical path:
  1. Load thyroid ultrasound image
  2. Run through two-stage detector
  3. Generate KDE/DM from RPN outputs
  4. Generate saliency maps from classifier stage
  5. Generate D-RISE maps from localization stage
  6. Evaluate quantitatively and qualitatively
- Design tradeoffs:
  - KDE/DM: computationally light but coarse; good for no-detection cases
  - D-RISE: high fidelity and per-box explanation but expensive
  - Grad-CAM++: fast and plausible, good default
- Failure signatures:
  - KDE/DM: uniform distribution → no insight
  - D-RISE: many low-score boxes → noisy maps
  - Saliency methods: explanations not overlapping with nodules → low plausibility
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run KDE/DM on a true negative case and verify the map is sparse at image center
  2. Run Grad-CAM++ on a true positive case and confirm the map covers the nodule region
  3. Run D-RISE on a mislocalization case and verify it produces separate maps for each detected box

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different XAI methods impact the predictive performance and confidence of doctors and radiologists in real clinical settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "very little is known about the influence of XAI on the predictive performance, confidence, and model trust of doctors and radiologists in an artificial setting, and nothing is known about its effects in a clinical setting."
- Why unresolved: While the paper surveyed user trust in an artificial setting, it did not evaluate the impact of XAI methods on actual diagnostic accuracy or confidence levels of medical professionals in clinical practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a clinical study where radiologists use XAI-augmented systems for actual thyroid nodule diagnosis, measuring changes in diagnostic accuracy, confidence levels, and decision-making time compared to standard practices.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the computational efficiency of XAI methods affect their practical adoption in real-time clinical settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "the computational time of D-RISE and RISE in specialized and perturbation-based methods, in general, are the highest due to their perturbation process" and mentions this as a limitation.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides computational time metrics but doesn't explore how these time constraints impact clinical workflow or whether faster methods (like Grad-CAM++) are sufficiently effective to replace slower but potentially more accurate methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing XAI methods in a simulated clinical workflow to measure the trade-off between explanation quality and computational time, and determining acceptable thresholds for real-time clinical use.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Do XAI methods improve patient understanding and trust in AI-assisted thyroid nodule diagnosis across different cultural and educational backgrounds?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper conducted a survey with participants from different countries (Vietnam, European countries, North American countries) but notes the need for "a more comprehensive survey that spans multiple countries and continents involving more various user subjects."
- Why unresolved: The current study had limited sample size and diversity, and did not specifically measure how cultural background or health literacy affects understanding and trust in XAI explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting large-scale, cross-cultural studies with diverse patient populations, measuring both comprehension of XAI explanations and resulting trust levels, while controlling for cultural and educational variables.

## Limitations

- The study lacks detailed implementation specifics for the proposed KDE and DM methods, particularly regarding bandwidth selection and frequency calculation
- User trust evaluation relies on surveys with a relatively small sample size (16 participants), limiting generalizability
- The paper does not provide ablation studies to isolate the impact of individual XAI methods on trust or diagnostic accuracy

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Saliency map-based XAI methods (Grad-CAM++, LIME, RISE, Ada-SISE) improve user trust due to their visual interpretability and alignment with clinical judgment
- **Medium confidence**: KDE and DM methods effectively explain negative cases by visualizing the distribution of predicted box centers
- **Low confidence**: D-RISE produces more faithful explanations by directly linking to localization stage outputs

## Next Checks

1. Conduct an ablation study to quantify the individual contribution of KDE and DM methods to user trust and diagnostic accuracy in negative cases
2. Expand the user trust survey to include a larger and more diverse sample size, including clinicians with varying levels of experience
