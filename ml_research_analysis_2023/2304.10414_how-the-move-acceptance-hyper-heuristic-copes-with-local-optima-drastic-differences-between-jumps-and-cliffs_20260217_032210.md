# How the Move Acceptance Hyper-Heuristic Copes With Local Optima: Drastic Differences Between Jumps and Cliffs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10414
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10414
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: MAHH has runtime Ω(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!) on jump functions with m = O(√n), much worse than O(n^m) for (1+1) EA

## Executive Summary
This work analyzes the Move Acceptance Hyper-Heuristic (MAHH) on jump functions, a key multimodal benchmark in runtime analysis. While MAHH performs remarkably well on cliff functions, we prove that for jump functions with gap size m = O(√n), its expected runtime is at least Ω(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!), much worse than the O(n^m) runtime of typical elitist evolutionary algorithms. We also show that MAHH with global bit-wise mutation achieves a runtime of O(min{m n^m, n^(2m-1)/(m! Ω(m)^(m-2))}), essentially the minimum of the classic MAHH and the (1+1) EA. This suggests combining multiple strategies for coping with local optima can be beneficial.

## Method Summary
The paper uses mathematical runtime analysis to study the Move Acceptance Hyper-Heuristic (MAHH) on jump functions. The analysis employs drift analysis techniques, Wald's equation, and recurrence relations to derive both upper and lower bounds on expected runtime. The authors analyze two variants: MAHH with local one-bit mutation and MAHH with global bit-wise mutation. The jump function is defined as having a local optimum at Hamming distance m from the global optimum, with a fitness valley between them.

## Key Results
- MAHH with one-bit flips has Ω(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!) runtime for jump functions with m = O(√n)
- MAHH with global bit-wise mutation achieves O(min{m n^m, n^(2m-1)/(m! Ω(m)^(m-2))}) runtime
- The hybrid approach combining MAHH and global mutation essentially achieves the minimum of both strategies
- MAHH performance degrades significantly compared to (1+1) EA on jump functions with m = O(√n)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The MAHH with global bit-wise mutation achieves a runtime essentially equal to the minimum of the classic MAHH and the (1+1) EA on jump functions.
- Mechanism: The global mutation operator enables two complementary escape strategies: (1) small jumps through the valley via classic MAHH behavior, and (2) direct jumps to the global optimum via bit-wise mutation flipping the right bits.
- Core assumption: The global mutation operator does not significantly increase the time to return to the local optimum from the valley compared to one-bit flips.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We also show that the MAHH with the global bit-wise mutation operator instead of the local one-bit operator optimizes jump functions in time O(min{m n^m, n^(2m-1)/(m! Ω(m)^(m-2})}), essentially the minimum of the optimization times of the (1+1) EA and the MAHH."
  - [section]: "Noting the signiﬁcant performance gap between, e.g., the (1+1) EA and the MAHH, we propose to use the MAHH with bit-wise mutation... This global mutation operators renders the analysis of the MAHH signiﬁcantly more complex..."
- Break condition: If the probability of moving from right to left of the local optimum becomes too high, the return time to the local optimum could dominate the runtime.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The MAHH with one-bit flips has O(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!) runtime for jump functions with gap size m = O(√n).
- Mechanism: The one-bit flip operator limits movement to adjacent states, creating a long escape path through the valley that the MAHH must navigate using its acceptance strategy.
- Core assumption: The MAHH's selection parameter p does not significantly accelerate escape from the local optimum for jump functions with m = O(√n).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We prove that for any choice of the MAHH selection parameter p, the expected runtime of the MAHH on a jump function with gap size m = O(√n) is at least Ω(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!)."
  - [section]: "Our lower bound is relatively tight. We prove that the MAHH with p = m/n has a runtime of O(n^(2m-1)/(m! Ω(m)^(m-2)))."
- Break condition: If the jump size m becomes sublinear but not O(√n), the runtime behavior may change significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The MAHH with one-bit flips has exponential runtime for jump functions with gap size m linear in n.
- Mechanism: When the valley width becomes linear in problem size, the MAHH cannot efficiently escape using its acceptance strategy, leading to exponential runtime.
- Core assumption: The MAHH's acceptance mechanism cannot compensate for the increased difficulty of escaping a valley that spans a constant fraction of the search space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We also show that the MAHH with the global bit-wise mutation operator instead of the local one-bit operator optimizes jump functions in time O(min{m n^m, n^(2m-1)/(m! Ω(m)^(m-2})}), essentially the minimum of the optimization times of the (1+1) EA and the MAHH."
  - [section]: "We prove that for any choice of the MAHH selection parameter p, the expected runtime of the MAHH on a jump function with gap size m = O(√n) is at least Ω(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!)."
- Break condition: If the valley width is sublinear in n but not O(√n), the runtime behavior may transition from polynomial to exponential.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: For which values of the jump size m does the Move Acceptance Hyper-Heuristic (MAHH) outperform simple elitist evolutionary algorithms (EAs) on jump functions?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper proves that for jump sizes m = O(√n), the MAHH has a runtime of Ω(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!), which is much worse than the O(n^m) runtime of typical EAs.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides bounds for specific ranges of m (O(√n) and linear in n). The exact performance crossover point between MAHH and EAs remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: A complete runtime analysis of MAHH for all values of m, or empirical studies comparing MAHH and EAs on jump functions with varying m.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does combining the MAHH with global bit-wise mutation consistently improve performance on multimodal benchmarks beyond jump functions?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper shows that MAHH with global mutation achieves a runtime of O(min{m n^m, n^(2m-1)/(m!Ω(m)^(m-2))}) on jump functions, essentially the minimum of the (1+1) EA and classic MAHH.
- Why unresolved: The analysis is limited to jump functions. The effectiveness of this hybrid approach on other multimodal benchmarks like cliff functions or real-world problems is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Runtime analyses of MAHH with global mutation on other multimodal benchmarks, or empirical studies comparing it to other algorithms on diverse problem sets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal selection parameter D for the MAHH on jump functions?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper analyzes MAHH with D = m/n and shows it achieves a runtime of O(n^(2m-1)/(m!Ω(m)^(m-2))). However, the general performance of MAHH for arbitrary D values is not fully characterized.
- Why unresolved: The paper only proves a lower bound that holds for all D values, but doesn't determine which D value minimizes the runtime.
- What evidence would resolve it: A complete characterization of the MAHH runtime as a function of D, or empirical studies optimizing D on various jump function instances.

## Limitations
- The runtime bounds depend critically on the mixing parameter D, which is not explicitly specified in the abstract
- The distinction between local one-bit and global bit-wise mutation operators may have subtle implementation-dependent effects
- The assumption that the global mutation operator doesn't significantly increase return time from the valley needs empirical validation

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The MAHH with one-bit flips has O(n^(2m-1)/(2m-1)!) runtime for jump functions with m = O(√n) - supported by explicit mathematical proof
- **Medium Confidence**: The global bit-wise mutation achieves O(min{m n^m, n^(2m-1)/(m! Ω(m)^(m-2))}) runtime - theoretical bounds provided but implementation details matter
- **Medium Confidence**: Combining MAHH with global mutation is beneficial - follows logically from the two mechanisms but requires empirical verification
