# Stanford MLab at SemEval-2023 Task 10: Exploring GloVe- and Transformer-Based Methods for the Explainable Detection of Online Sexism

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04356
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04356
- Reference count: 4
- The paper explores GloVe- and transformer-based methods for detecting online sexism, achieving best results with pre-trained transformers and ensembles.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting online sexism through explainable models, focusing on binary and multi-class classification across three subtasks. The authors explore a range of approaches including GloVe-based logistic regression as a baseline, transformer-based models (BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, HateBERT, SBERT), ensemble methods, and model blending. Key findings show that pre-training transformers on unlabeled domain-specific data significantly improves performance, particularly in subtask B where pt-RoBERTa achieved an F1 score of 0.62. Ensemble models and blending provided slight improvements in robustness and macro F1 scores, especially for subtask C.

## Method Summary
The methodology combines data cleaning, baseline GloVe logistic regression, fine-tuning of multiple transformer architectures, domain-specific pre-training, ensemble methods through embedding concatenation, and model blending through weighted prediction averaging. Data cleaning included lowercasing, punctuation removal (except apostrophes), and slang expansion. The approach was evaluated across three subtasks: binary classification (A), multi-class classification (B), and explainable detection (C), using macro F1 score as the primary metric.

## Key Results
- Pre-training RoBERTa on unlabeled Reddit and Gab posts significantly improved performance, with pt-RoBERTa achieving F1 0.62 in subtask B.
- Ensemble models combining transformer embeddings provided slight improvements in macro F1 scores, particularly for subtask C.
- Data cleaning proved essential, with uncleaned text resulting in a significant F1 score drop (0.571 vs. cleaned version).
- The best overall performance was achieved using pre-trained transformers and ensembles, demonstrating their effectiveness for explainable sexism detection.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-training transformer models on unlabeled domain-specific data significantly improves detection performance compared to off-the-shelf pre-trained models.
- Mechanism: Domain-specific pre-training allows the model to learn linguistic patterns, slang, and context unique to online sexism discourse, which are not well captured by general-purpose pre-training corpora.
- Core assumption: The unlabeled data distribution closely matches the target task's data distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Pre-training transformer models yielded significant improvements in performance, and ensembles and blending slightly improved robustness in the F1 score."
  - [section]: "In Subtask B, we see that pt-RoBERTa outperformed all single-transformer models, including DeBERTa. This shows that the success of pt-RoBERTa model can be primarily attributed to the more robust embeddings created after pre-training the RoBERTa embeddings on the unlabeled dataset."
  - [corpus]: Weak or missing direct evidence; relies on F1 score comparison.
- Break condition: If the unlabeled dataset does not match the target domain or contains significant noise/outliers, pre-training may not yield improvements.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Ensemble models that concatenate embeddings from multiple transformers capture complementary linguistic features, improving macro F1 scores.
- Mechanism: Different transformer architectures (e.g., RoBERTa, DeBERTa, SBERT) may specialize in different aspects of language modeling (contextual embeddings, sentence embeddings, disentangled attention). Concatenating their embeddings allows the model to leverage these complementary strengths.
- Core assumption: The concatenated embedding space retains discriminative power and does not introduce noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Ensembles and blending slightly improved robustness in the F1 score."
  - [section]: "For Subtasks B and C, creating ensemble-type models by concatenating embeddings from different transformer models produced slight improvements. Ensemble 2 was middle-of-the-pack in Subtask B since it involved concatenating embeddings from a poorer-performing transformers mode (SBERT). For Ensemble 1, while the improvement was only slight for Subtask B, there is a clear difference in performance between the pt-RoBERTa model's F1 score and Ensemble 1's F1 score for Subtask C."
  - [corpus]: Weak or missing direct evidence; relies on comparative F1 scores.
- Break condition: If one model in the ensemble is significantly weaker, it may introduce noise and degrade overall performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Data cleaning (lowercasing, removing punctuation except apostrophes, expanding slang) is essential for consistent model performance.
- Mechanism: Standardizing text formatting reduces vocabulary size and ensures consistent tokenization, while slang expansion improves semantic clarity for the model.
- Core assumption: The cleaning process does not remove meaningful stylistic cues or distort the text semantics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "To prove the importance of data cleaning, we ran an experiment with our pt-RoBERTa model on the uncleaned input text, as seen in Table 2. The resulting F1 score of 0.571 was a significant decrease from the score on the cleaned input text."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data cleaning pipeline -> GloVe baseline model -> Transformer models -> Ensemble modules -> Blending module -> Training loop with early stopping

- Critical path:
  1. Clean and preprocess input text.
  2. Load and tokenize using appropriate transformer tokenizer.
  3. Fine-tune transformer model(s) on labeled data.
  4. Optionally pre-train transformer on unlabeled domain data.
  5. Apply ensemble or blending if needed.
  6. Evaluate using macro F1 score.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pre-training on unlabeled data vs. computational cost and time.
  - Ensemble complexity vs. marginal performance gains.
  - Data cleaning strictness vs. preservation of stylistic cues.

- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: Training loss decreases but validation F1 plateaus or drops.
  - Class imbalance: Macro F1 drops despite high accuracy; minority class F1 is low.
  - Poor generalization: Large gap between validation and test set performance.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare GloVe baseline vs. fine-tuned BERT on cleaned data to establish baseline improvement.
  2. Test pre-trained RoBERTa vs. standard RoBERTa on the same task to measure domain pre-training impact.
  3. Build an ensemble of RoBERTa and DeBERTa embeddings and compare macro F1 to individual models.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of domain pre-training hinges on the unlabeled dataset's similarity to the target task's data distribution, which is not thoroughly validated.
- Ensemble improvements are marginal and not consistently demonstrated across all subtasks or ensemble configurations.
- The importance of data cleaning is shown through a single experiment without robustness checks or validation of the cleaning process accuracy.

## Confidence
- Pre-training transformers on domain-specific data improves performance - High
- Ensemble models improve macro F1 scores - Low
- Data cleaning is essential for consistent performance - Medium

## Next Checks
1. Analyze the unlabeled pre-training dataset: Perform a qualitative and quantitative analysis of the Reddit and Gab posts to ensure they represent the linguistic and topical diversity of online sexism. Check for noise, outliers, or domain mismatches.
2. Conduct ablation studies on ensemble composition: Systematically remove or replace individual models in the ensemble to measure the impact on macro F1 scores. Identify if weaker models (e.g., SBERT) degrade performance.
3. Validate data cleaning robustness: Test the impact of aggressive cleaning (e.g., removing punctuation, expanding slang) on a held-out test set with varied stylistic cues. Compare performance with and without cleaning across multiple runs to ensure consistency.
