# Toward Degree Bias in Embedding-Based Knowledge Graph Completion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05044
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05044
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: KG-Mixup improves performance on low tail-relation degree triples by 9.8% MRR on ConvE without compromising high-degree performance

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses degree bias in knowledge graph completion (KGC), where triples with low tail-relation degrees suffer from poor performance. The authors propose KG-Mixup, a data augmentation method that generates synthetic triples for low-degree entity-relation pairs by adapting the Mixup strategy to KGs. Through extensive experiments and theoretical analysis, KG-Mixup is shown to act as a regularization mechanism that smooths embeddings and improves calibration, particularly benefiting low-degree triples while maintaining performance on high-degree triples.

## Method Summary
KG-Mixup is a data augmentation framework that generates synthetic triples for entity-relation pairs with low tail-relation degrees using the Mixup strategy. For each triple (ℎ, 𝑟, 𝑡) where the tail-relation degree is below a threshold, the method creates k synthetic samples by mixing with other triples sharing the same tail entity using weighted embeddings. These synthetic triples are incorporated into training with additional loss weighting (β parameter). The method can be combined with any KG embedding technique and works by increasing the representation of low-degree entity-relation pairs in the training data.

## Key Results
- KG-Mixup achieves 9.8% MRR improvement on low tail-relation degree triples when combined with ConvE
- Performance improvements observed across multiple datasets (FB15K-237, CoDEx-M, NELL-995)
- Method maintains or slightly improves performance on high-degree triples while significantly boosting low-degree performance
- Theoretical analysis confirms KG-Mixup acts as regularization that smooths embeddings of mixed samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: KG-Mixup acts as a regularization that smooths embeddings of mixed samples, improving performance on low tail-relation degree triples.
- Mechanism: By generating synthetic triples through mixing embeddings of existing triples, KG-Mixup creates additional training samples that regularize the model's learning process, specifically benefiting low-degree entity-relation pairs.
- Core assumption: The synthetic triples created through mixing are informative enough to improve the model's understanding of low-degree entity-relation pairs without introducing significant noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Theoretical analysis reveals KG-Mixup acts as a regularization that smooths embeddings of mixed samples."
  - [section]: "KG-Mixup can be formulated as a form of regularization on the entity and relation embeddings of low tail-relation degree samples."
- Break condition: If the synthetic triples introduce too much noise or are not informative enough, the regularization effect could be negated, leading to worse performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: KG-Mixup improves model calibration by reducing bias between different groups of data, particularly benefiting low tail-relation degree triples.
- Mechanism: By augmenting the training data with synthetic samples for low-degree entity-relation pairs, KG-Mixup ensures that the model learns to make more balanced predictions across all degree ranges, improving overall calibration.
- Core assumption: The model's calibration is significantly influenced by the distribution of training data, and augmenting low-degree samples helps achieve a more balanced representation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Extensive experiments have demonstrated that our method can improve various embedding-based KGC methods... without compromising the performance on triples of higher degree."
  - [section]: "KG-Mixup is effective at improving the calibration of KG embedding models... suggesting another reason for why KG-Mixup works."
- Break condition: If the model's calibration is not significantly influenced by the distribution of training data, or if augmenting low-degree samples introduces significant bias, the calibration improvement may not be realized.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: KG-Mixup improves performance on low tail-relation degree triples by increasing their representation in the training data, effectively increasing their degree.
- Mechanism: By generating synthetic triples for entity-relation pairs with low tail-relation degrees, KG-Mixup effectively increases the number of training samples containing these pairs, improving the model's ability to predict them.
- Core assumption: Increasing the representation of low-degree entity-relation pairs in the training data will lead to better performance on predicting these pairs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our method works by augmenting entity-relation pairs that have low tail-relation degrees with synthetic triples."
  - [section]: "This strategy has the effect of increasing the degree of an entity-relation pair with a low tail-relation degree by creating more shared edges between them."
- Break condition: If increasing the representation of low-degree entity-relation pairs in the training data does not lead to better performance, or if it negatively impacts performance on high-degree triples, the mechanism may not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: KG-Mixup -> Synthetic triples generation -> Training data augmentation -> KG embedding model (ConvE/TuckER)
- Critical path:
  1. Identify low tail-relation degree entity-relation pairs in the training data
  2. Generate synthetic triples by mixing embeddings of existing triples
  3. Incorporate the synthetic triples into the training process
  4. Train the KG embedding model with the augmented data
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Generating more synthetic triples may improve performance on low-degree pairs but could also introduce noise
  - Using a higher degree threshold may include more pairs but could also include pairs that don't need augmentation
- Failure signatures:
  - If performance on low-degree pairs does not improve, the synthetic triples may not be informative enough
  - If performance on high-degree pairs degrades, the augmentation may be introducing too much noise
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test KG-Mixup on a small dataset with a simple KG embedding model to validate the basic functionality
  2. Vary the number of synthetic triples generated per low-degree pair to find the optimal balance
  3. Compare the performance of KG-Mixup with other data augmentation methods on a larger dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does KG-Mixup's regularization effect vary with different knowledge graph embedding architectures beyond ConvE and TuckER?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper only tested KG-Mixup with ConvE and TuckER, but claims it can be combined with any KG embedding technique
- Why unresolved: The theoretical regularization analysis in Section 5 suggests certain properties that may not hold universally across all embedding architectures
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of KG-Mixup across diverse KG embedding architectures (e.g., RotatE, ComplEx, DistMult) with ablation studies of regularization effects

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for selecting which entity-relation pairs to augment when multiple pairs share the same tail entity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 4.2.2 discusses the mixing criteria but acknowledges this is a non-trivial challenge
- Why unresolved: The current approach selects k random samples from triples sharing the same tail, but this may not be optimal for learning
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing different selection strategies (e.g., based on relation similarity, entity similarity, or adaptive sampling) with performance metrics on low-degree triples

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does KG-Mixup perform on knowledge graphs with different degree distributions, particularly those with power-law distributions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments used FB15K-237, NELL-995, and CoDEx-M, but the paper doesn't analyze performance across graphs with varying degree distributions
- Why unresolved: The effectiveness of KG-Mixup depends on the presence of degree bias, which may vary significantly across different KGs
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on KGs with controlled degree distributions (e.g., synthetic graphs with varying power-law exponents) measuring performance gains relative to degree bias severity

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness appears heavily dependent on hyperparameter tuning (degree threshold, mixing coefficient β, and number of synthetic samples)
- Long-tail distribution problem in real-world KGs means many entity-relation pairs may remain underrepresented even after augmentation
- Theoretical analysis of regularization properties lacks rigorous mathematical derivation of convergence properties

## Confidence
- High confidence: The experimental results demonstrating performance improvements on low-degree triples (9.8% MRR increase on ConvE)
- Medium confidence: The theoretical claim that KG-Mixup acts as a regularization mechanism
- Medium confidence: The calibration improvement claims, as ECE metrics show mixed results
