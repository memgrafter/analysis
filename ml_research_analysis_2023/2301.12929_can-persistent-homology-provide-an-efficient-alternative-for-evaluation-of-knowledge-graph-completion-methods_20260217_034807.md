# Can Persistent Homology provide an efficient alternative for Evaluation of Knowledge Graph Completion Methods?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.12929
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12929
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Proposes Knowledge Persistence (KP) method that reduces KG completion evaluation time by ~99.96% while maintaining strong correlation with traditional ranking metrics

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational inefficiency of ranking-based evaluation in knowledge graph completion, which requires quadratic time relative to the number of entities. The authors introduce Knowledge Persistence (KP), a novel method leveraging persistent homology from topological data analysis to evaluate KG completion methods in linear time. KP constructs graphs from sampled positive and negative triples, computes persistence diagrams through filtration, and measures Sliced Wasserstein distance between them. Experiments demonstrate that KP is highly correlated with traditional ranking metrics (Hits@N, MR, MRR), enabling early stopping with minimal relative error while reducing evaluation time from hours to seconds.

## Method Summary
The Knowledge Persistence (KP) method constructs graphs from sampled positive and negative triples, computes persistence diagrams via filtration, and measures the Sliced Wasserstein distance between them to evaluate KG completion methods. This topological approach enables linear-time evaluation compared to the quadratic complexity of traditional ranking-based methods. The method builds on the principle that KG completion algorithms should produce high-quality negative samples that are topologically distinct from positive samples. By leveraging persistent homology, KP captures the structural differences between positive and negative triple sets in a computationally efficient manner while maintaining strong correlation with established ranking metrics.

## Key Results
- KP achieves approximately 99.96% reduction in evaluation time (e.g., from 18 hours to 27 seconds)
- KP shows strong correlation with traditional ranking metrics (Hits@N, MR, MRR)
- KP demonstrates robustness to noise with only 1% false positive injection affecting results

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Knowledge Persistence works by leveraging the mathematical framework of persistent homology to capture topological features that distinguish positive from negative triples in knowledge graphs. The method exploits the principle that high-quality KG completion algorithms should generate negative samples that are topologically distinct from positive samples. By computing persistence diagrams and measuring their Sliced Wasserstein distance, KP quantifies the structural separation between positive and negative triple sets. This topological approach is inherently more efficient than ranking-based methods because it avoids pairwise comparisons between all entities, instead focusing on global structural patterns that emerge from the entire triple distribution.

- **Sliced Wasserstein distance**: A metric for comparing probability distributions that is computationally efficient; needed to measure similarity between persistence diagrams; quick check: confirm that SWD computation scales linearly with sample size.
- **Filtration**: A sequence of simplicial complexes that captures topological features at different scales; needed to understand how KP builds persistence diagrams from KG triples; quick check: verify that filtration preserves relevant topological structures.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Triples -> Sampling -> Graph Construction -> Filtration -> Persistence Diagrams -> Sliced Wasserstein Distance -> Evaluation Score

**Critical Path**: The method processes triples through sampling, constructs graphs for positive and negative sets separately, applies filtration to build simplicial complexes, computes persistence diagrams for each set, then measures their Sliced Wasserstein distance to produce the final evaluation score.

**Design Tradeoffs**: KP trades the fine-grained entity-level ranking information of traditional methods for computational efficiency and topological robustness. While ranking metrics provide precise per-entity scores, KP captures global structural patterns that may be more representative of overall completion quality at scale.

**Failure Signatures**: The method may fail when positive and negative triples have similar topological structures (low separability), when sampling does not adequately represent the triple distribution, or when the KG structure itself lacks meaningful topological features that distinguish positive from negative samples.

**First Experiments**: (1) Verify KP correlation with ranking metrics across multiple KG datasets, (2) Test KP robustness by varying noise injection levels systematically, (3) Compare KP against approximate evaluation methods in terms of both speed and accuracy.

## Limitations
- Experimental validation limited to two benchmark datasets (WN18RR and FB15K-237), potentially limiting generalizability
- Robustness claims based on limited 1% false positive injection experiments
- Does not explore whether persistent homology captures complementary aspects of KG completion quality beyond what ranking metrics measure

## Confidence
- Computational efficiency claims: High confidence (well-supported by experimental results)
- Correlation with ranking metrics: High confidence (strong empirical evidence)
- Noise robustness claims: Medium confidence (limited validation beyond 1% noise level)
- Generalizability across diverse KG domains: Medium confidence (tested on limited dataset variety)

## Next Checks
