# A Relaxed Optimization Approach for Adversarial Attacks against Neural Machine Translation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08492
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08492
- Reference count: 26
- The paper proposes an optimization-based adversarial attack against Neural Machine Translation (NMT) models that generates semantically similar but translation-quality-destroying examples.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel adversarial attack method for NMT models that generates adversarial examples through continuous relaxation of discrete optimization. The attack uses Gumbel-Softmax sampling to find probability distributions over token vocabularies, enabling gradient-based optimization while preserving semantic similarity through contextualized embeddings. The method significantly degrades translation quality across multiple NMT models while maintaining semantic coherence, and extends to black-box settings through reference model optimization.

## Method Summary
The attack formulates a discrete optimization problem in token space and solves it using continuous relaxation with the Gumbel-Softmax trick. For each token position, the method finds a probability distribution over the vocabulary rather than selecting a single token. The optimization minimizes a loss combining adversarial effectiveness (translation quality degradation) and semantic similarity preservation (using contextualized embeddings from a language model). After optimization, multiple adversarial examples are generated by sampling from the optimized distributions. The method uses Adam optimizer for 100 iterations with learning rate 0.3 and batch size 5, and extends to black-box settings by optimizing on reference models and transferring to target models.

## Key Results
- The proposed attack significantly degrades BLEU scores across multiple NMT models while maintaining semantic similarity
- Outperforms baselines in success rate, semantic similarity preservation, and token error rate
- Effective in black-box settings where gradients of the target model are inaccessible
- Maintains perplexity scores indicating generated adversarial examples are fluent and natural

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Continuous relaxation enables gradient-based optimization on discrete text inputs
- Mechanism: Gumbel-Softmax trick converts discrete token selection into continuous optimization over probability matrices
- Core assumption: Gumbel-Softmax approximation sufficiently maintains discrete nature while enabling gradient flow
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "continuous relaxation to solve it... probability distribution for each token"; [section] "continuous relaxation of one-hot vectors using Gumbel-Softmax trick"
- Break condition: Significant deviation from true one-hot vectors could fail semantic similarity constraints

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Semantic similarity preservation through contextualized embeddings
- Mechanism: Cosine similarity between contextualized embeddings of original and adversarial tokens, weighted by inverse document frequency
- Core assumption: Language model embeddings capture semantic similarity more effectively than static embeddings
- Evidence anchors: [section] "contextualized embeddings of an LM to represent tokens... cosine similarity"; [section] "adapt BERTScore for same-length sentences"
- Break condition: Language model failure to capture semantic equivalence (rare words, domain terminology)

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Black-box effectiveness through reference model optimization
- Mechanism: Optimize token distributions using gradients from accessible reference model, transfer to black-box target
- Core assumption: Reference model gradients provide useful signal for transferable adversarial examples
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "black-box extension... sampling from optimized probability distribution for reference model"; [section] "sample from optimized distributions and test against target model"
- Break condition: Significant architectural or training data differences between reference and target models

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Continuous relaxation of discrete optimization problems
  - Why needed here: Direct optimization on discrete sequences is non-differentiable and intractable
  - Quick check question: What mathematical tool allows differentiable sampling from categorical distributions?

- Concept: Gumbel-Softmax trick
  - Why needed here: Enables differentiable sampling from categorical distributions while maintaining sparsity
  - Quick check question: How does Gumbel-Softmax approximation differ from standard softmax?

- Concept: Contextualized embeddings for semantic similarity
  - Why needed here: Captures meaning in context rather than word-level similarity, preventing unrealistic examples
  - Quick check question: Why might static embeddings fail to preserve semantic similarity in adversarial attacks?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the attack's effectiveness vary across different language pairs and translation tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates against English-to-French, English-to-German, and French-to-English datasets
- Why unresolved: Results are limited to specific language pairs; broader exploration across diverse language pairs and translation tasks is needed
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments across wider range of language pairs, translation tasks, and datasets to assess generalizability

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust are NMT models to these adversarial examples and what defenses can mitigate them?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on attack generation without extensively discussing model robustness or defense mechanisms
- Why unresolved: Understanding model robustness and exploring defense mechanisms is crucial for secure translation systems
- What evidence would resolve it: Research investigating defense mechanisms (adversarial training, input preprocessing, model architecture modifications) against the proposed attack

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the attack perform against other NLP tasks beyond machine translation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on NMT applications but underlying techniques could extend to other NLP tasks
- Why unresolved: Exploring effectiveness across broader NLP tasks would provide comprehensive understanding of applicability
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating attack performance on text summarization, question answering, sentiment analysis, and other sequence-to-sequence tasks

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't empirically validate how well Gumbel-Softmax approximation maintains true semantic similarity constraints
- Limited testing across different NMT model architectures beyond Marian NMT and mBART50
- Semantic similarity metric relies on contextualized embeddings without ablation studies comparing alternative metrics

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Core optimization formulation and continuous relaxation techniques are well-established; experimental setup and evaluation metrics are clearly specified and reproducible.

**Medium Confidence**: Claims about outperforming baselines are supported by quantitative results but limited testing scope; black-box extension shows promise but tested in limited settings.

**Low Confidence**: Semantic similarity preservation mechanism relies heavily on language model's ability to capture true semantic equivalence without empirical validation of this assumption.

## Next Checks

1. **Gumbel-Softmax Quality Validation**: Compare generated adversarial examples from Gumbel-Softmax sampling to those from direct discrete optimization or different sampling temperatures, measuring correlation between semantic similarity scores and approximation quality.

2. **Cross-Model Transferability Study**: Test same adversarial examples against diverse NMT models including RNN-based models, models trained on different datasets, and models for different language pairs to quantify transferability variations.

3. **Semantic Similarity Ablation**: Replace contextualized embedding similarity metric with static embeddings and n-gram overlap metrics, comparing how each correlates with human judgments of semantic equivalence for generated adversarial examples.
