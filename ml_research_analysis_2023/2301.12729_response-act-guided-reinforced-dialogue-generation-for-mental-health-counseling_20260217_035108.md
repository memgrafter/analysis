# Response-act Guided Reinforced Dialogue Generation for Mental Health Counseling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12729
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12729
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: READER achieves up to 15.5% improvement in ROUGE-2 recall and 4.05% in BERTScore over baselines for mental health counseling dialogue generation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces READER, a transformer-based model that jointly predicts response-acts and generates contextually appropriate utterances for mental health counseling dialogues. The model uses reinforcement learning with a multi-component reward function (ROUGE, BERTScore, dialogue-act classification accuracy, and relative entropy) to ensure semantic richness and alignment with the intended response-act. READER significantly outperforms competitive baselines on the HOPE dataset, demonstrating both quantitative improvements in standard metrics and qualitative gains in relevance, consistency, and coherence. The model also generalizes well to the Switchboard Dialogue-act dataset, showing promise for broader applications in dialogue systems.

## Method Summary
READER is built on a GPT-2 foundation and uses three heads: a Response-Act Classification (RAC) head, a Language Model (LM) head, and a Value (V) head. The model is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a custom reward function that combines ROUGE scores for n-gram overlap, BERTScore for semantic similarity, dialogue-act classification accuracy, and relative entropy for distribution matching. The model jointly predicts the response-act and generates the utterance, with the reward encouraging both semantic quality and adherence to the predicted dialogue-act. Extensive experimentation with different reward configurations demonstrates that the multi-component reward function yields the best results.

## Key Results
- READER achieves up to 15.5% improvement in ROUGE-2 recall and 4.05% in BERTScore over baselines.
- The model outperforms DialoGPT, GPT-2, and other competitive baselines on the HOPE dataset.
- Qualitative analysis and human evaluation confirm that READER produces more relevant, consistent, and coherent responses.
- The model generalizes well to the Switchboard Dialogue-act dataset, showing broader applicability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation is the use of reinforcement learning with a multi-component reward function that balances semantic quality (via ROUGE and BERTScore) with dialogue-act alignment (via RAC-Head accuracy and relative entropy). This approach ensures that generated responses are both contextually appropriate and adhere to the intended communicative function, which is critical for mental health counseling where the counselor's response must be both empathetic and task-oriented.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
GPT-2 Base -> RAC-Head, LM-Head, V-Head -> Reward Function (ROUGE, BERTScore, RAC-Accuracy, Relative Entropy) -> PPO Update

**Critical Path**
Input dialogue history -> GPT-2 encoding -> Response-act prediction (RAC-Head) -> Utterance generation (LM-Head) -> Reward calculation -> PPO update

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Joint prediction of response-act and generation vs. separate stages: improves coherence but increases model complexity
- Multi-component reward vs. single metric: better balance of semantic and functional quality, but harder to tune
- RL fine-tuning vs. supervised training only: higher quality outputs, but requires careful reward shaping

**Failure Signatures**
- High learning rates cause reward collapse and mode collapse
- Poor RAC-Head accuracy leads to irrelevant responses
- Over-reliance on ROUGE causes repetitive, less diverse outputs

**3 First Experiments**
1. Train with only ROUGE or BERTScore reward: observe degradation in response-act alignment or semantic quality
2. Vary reward weights (λ1, λ2, λ3, λ4): assess impact on ROUGE, BERTScore, and RAC-Head accuracy
3. Test on extended dialogues: evaluate long-term coherence and topic consistency
