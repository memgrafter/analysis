# Parameter-Efficient Sparse Retrievers and Rerankers using Adapters

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13220
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13220
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Parameter-efficient adapters enable effective sparse retrievers without full fine-tuning

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the use of parameter-efficient adapters for sparse retrievers and rerankers, demonstrating that adapter-tuning SPLADE achieves competitive performance with only 2% of training parameters compared to full fine-tuning. The study systematically evaluates adapter configurations on multiple IR benchmarks including MS MARCO, TREC DL, BEIR, and TripClick datasets. Results show that adapter-tuned sparse models achieve lower R-FLOPS while maintaining or improving effectiveness, and that adapter-tuning provides more stable domain adaptation compared to full fine-tuning. The paper also reveals important insights about knowledge transfer between rankers and rerankers, showing that dense models transfer better than sparse ones.

## Method Summary
The method employs Houlsby-style bottleneck adapters inserted between transformer layers of the SPLADE model, where only adapter parameters are trained while the pretrained language model remains frozen. The SPLADE head predicts term weights with L1 and FLOPS regularization to encourage sparsity. Training uses either BM25 triplets or distillation objectives. The approach explores mono-adapter and bi-adapter settings, where bi-adapters have separate query and document adapters for improved efficiency. Adapter layer ablation studies progressively remove layers to understand their contribution to effectiveness. The method is evaluated across multiple IR tasks including first-stage retrieval, reranking, and domain adaptation on various datasets.

## Key Results
- Adapters-SPLADE with 2% training parameters outperforms fully fine-tuned SPLADE and dense IR models on MS MARCO and TREC datasets
- Adapter-tuned models achieve lower R-FLOPS indicating sparser representations than fine-tuned counterparts
- Bi-adapter settings with separate query/document adapters show further efficiency gains
- Adapter-tuning improves domain adaptation stability on BEIR and TripClick datasets compared to full fine-tuning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adapters retain efficiency and effectiveness by training only bottleneck layers while freezing the pretrained language model.
- Mechanism: Small bottleneck layers are inserted between transformer layers, allowing adaptation with minimal parameter changes. The frozen PLM maintains learned representations while adapters optimize for task-specific needs.
- Core assumption: The pretrained model contains general knowledge that can be leveraged without fine-tuning, and bottleneck adapters can capture task-specific patterns efficiently.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Adapters are memory-efficient and scale well with downstream tasks by training small bottle-neck layers added between transformer layers while keeping the large pretrained language model (PLMs) frozen."
  - [section 3.2] "An additive adapter inserts trainable parameters in addition to the aforementioned transformer layers. The added modules form a bottle-neck architecture with a down-projection, an up-projection and a non-linear transformation."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence about bottleneck layer effectiveness in IR.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Sparse retrievers benefit from adapters by achieving sparser representations and lower computational costs compared to full fine-tuning.
- Mechanism: Adapters optimize the model to produce more selective term weights, reducing R-FLOPS (retrieval floating-point operations) while maintaining or improving retrieval effectiveness.
- Core assumption: Sparse representations are more efficient for retrieval, and adapter training can enhance sparsity without sacrificing effectiveness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Adpters-SPLADE, using only 2% of training parameters, outperforms fully fine-tuned SPLADE and dense IR models on MS MARCO and TREC datasets. Sparse representations are more efficient (lower R-FLOPS) than full fine-tuning."
  - [section 4.1] "We also observe that the R-FLOPS are lower for adapter-tuned models indicating sparser representation than the fine-tuned counterparts."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence about R-FLOPS improvements specifically for sparse models.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adapter layer ablation shows that later layers contain more task-specific information, and removing early layers has less impact on effectiveness.
- Mechanism: Task-specific information is stored in later adapter layers, so progressive removal of early layers maintains effectiveness while reducing parameters and training time.
- Core assumption: Information in transformer models is hierarchical, with later layers capturing more task-specific patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "We observe gradual performance drop for MS MARCO and TREC DL datasets as the training parameters decrease with the progressive removal of adapter layers... The drop is significantly higher (a drop of 0.25 MRR score) when layers are removed from the second half of the model (≥ 0− 3)."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence about layer-specific information storage in IR models.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pretrained language model -> Adapter layers -> SPLADE head -> Training pipeline
- Critical path: 1. Initialize frozen PLM and randomly initialize adapter layers 2. Forward pass through PLM → adapter layers → SPLADE head 3. Compute loss (contrastive or MarginMSE) with regularization 4. Backpropagate only through adapter layers 5. Update adapter parameters
- Design tradeoffs: Adapter size vs. effectiveness (smaller bottlenecks reduce parameters but may limit expressiveness), Mono-adapter vs. bi-adapter (sharing adapters is simpler but bi-adapters can optimize for query/document differences), Regularization weights (balance between effectiveness and sparsity)
- Failure signatures: Poor effectiveness (check adapter initialization, regularization weights, and whether PLM knowledge is relevant), High R-FLOPS (verify that sparsity regularizations are active and effective), Training instability (ensure adapter layers are initialized properly and learning rates are appropriate)
- First 3 experiments: 1. Train mono-adapter SPLADE with default settings on MS MARCO; compare to baseline fine-tuned model 2. Perform adapter layer ablation (remove 0-1, 0-2 layers) and measure effectiveness drop and training time reduction 3. Test domain adaptation by fine-tuning on BEIR datasets; compare adapter vs. full fine-tuning stability and effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do sparse retrievers with adapters compare to dense retrievers with adapters in terms of zero-shot effectiveness across diverse domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that sparse models require full fine-tuning for reranking while dense models work better with adapters for knowledge transfer, but doesn't directly compare zero-shot performance between sparse and dense adapter-based retrievers.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on sparse retrievers and only briefly mentions dense models in the context of reranking, without conducting direct comparative experiments on zero-shot performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing sparse and dense retrievers using adapters across multiple zero-shot benchmarks like BEIR, measuring both effectiveness and efficiency metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for adapter layer allocation across transformer layers in sparse retrievers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper performs adapter layer ablation studies showing that removing later layers causes more significant performance drops, but doesn't explore alternative allocation strategies like concentrating adapters in specific layers.
- Why unresolved: The ablation study follows a sequential removal pattern rather than testing different allocation strategies that might better match sparse retriever characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing different adapter allocation patterns (e.g., concentrating adapters in middle layers vs. upper layers) across multiple sparse retriever architectures.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different regularization strategies affect adapter performance in sparse retrievers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses L1 and FLOPS regularization for sparse retrievers but doesn't explore how different regularization approaches might interact with adapter training or affect knowledge transfer.
- Why unresolved: The paper maintains consistent regularization settings across all experiments without investigating alternative regularization strategies that might better complement adapter training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing various regularization combinations (e.g., different L1/FLOPS weightings, additional regularization terms) while training adapters, measuring impact on both in-domain and out-of-domain performance.

## Limitations
- Limited direct corpus evidence for some proposed mechanisms, particularly regarding the effectiveness of bottleneck layers and layer-specific information storage in IR models
- No ablation studies comparing different adapter architectures (Houlsby vs other types) or alternative parameter-efficient methods
- Training details and hyperparameter choices not fully specified, which may impact reproducibility
- Sparse reranker effectiveness claims rely on single model comparisons without ablation of adapter vs full fine-tuning effects

## Confidence
- High: Adapter-tuning effectiveness on sparse retrieval and domain adaptation stability
- Medium: R-FLOPS efficiency improvements and adapter layer ablation findings
- Low: Knowledge transfer claims from first-stage rankers to rerankers, particularly for sparse models
