# Unbiased Learning to Rank with Biased Continuous Feedback

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04335
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04335
- Reference count: 36
- One-line primary result: Novel unbiased LTR method handling continuous feedback achieves 2.08% improvement in online A/B testing

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning to rank from biased continuous feedback by extending the Position-Based Model (PBM) to pairwise learning. The authors propose a method that explicitly models position-based examination bias and trust bias, allowing for separation of these biases from true user relevance. The approach is evaluated both on public LTR datasets and in a large-scale video recommender system at Tencent News, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing debiasing methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed method extends PBM to pairwise learning by introducing pairwise trust bias and separating position bias, trust bias, and user relevance explicitly. The approach uses two propensity estimation models - one for position bias and one for trust bias - and employs a doubly robust estimator to combine these estimates with the learning objective. The method is designed to handle both categorical and continuous feedback, making it applicable to a wider range of real-world scenarios where continuous ratings are common.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art debiasing methods on continuous labels
- Achieves competitive performance on categorical labels
- Demonstrates 2.08% improvement in business metrics through online A/B testing in Tencent News video recommender system

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by explicitly modeling two types of bias that affect user feedback: position-based examination bias (where users are more likely to examine and interact with top-ranked items) and trust bias (where users tend to give higher ratings to items they choose to examine). By separating these biases from the true relevance signal and using propensity weighting techniques, the method can learn an unbiased ranking function even when the observed feedback is systematically biased.

- Position-Based Model (PBM): Explains how users can only interact with items they examine; needed to model position bias; quick check: verify understanding of PBM equation and assumptions
- Doubly Robust Estimator: Combines propensity weighting with regression modeling for more robust estimation; needed to reduce variance in propensity estimation; quick check: understand the bias-variance tradeoff in doubly robust estimation
- Pairwise Learning: Learning to rank by comparing item pairs rather than absolute relevance; needed for more stable optimization compared to listwise approaches; quick check: contrast pairwise vs. listwise ranking objectives
- Propensity Estimation: Estimating the probability of observing feedback given position and trust factors; needed to correct for selection bias in observed data; quick check: verify understanding of inverse propensity weighting

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
PBM -> Pairwise Trust Bias Model -> Propensity Estimators (Position, Trust) -> Doubly Robust Estimator -> Unbiased LTR Objective

Critical Path:
The critical path follows from modeling the bias factors through propensity estimation to the final learning objective. The most critical components are the propensity estimators, as errors here propagate directly to the final ranking quality.

Design Tradeoffs:
- Using separate propensity models for position and trust bias vs. joint modeling: Separate models offer clearer interpretability and easier debugging but may miss complex interactions
- Doubly robust vs. pure inverse propensity weighting: Doubly robust offers better variance properties but requires training an additional regression model
- Pairwise vs. listwise learning: Pairwise is computationally more efficient and often more stable, but may not capture all ranking nuances

Failure Signatures:
- If propensity estimates are inaccurate, the method could overcorrect or undercorrect for biases, leading to degraded ranking performance
- Misestimation of trust bias could lead to overpenalizing items that naturally receive high ratings
- If the separability assumption between position bias, trust bias, and relevance doesn't hold, the debiasing could introduce new biases

First Experiments:
1. Verify that propensity estimates for position bias follow expected patterns (higher for top positions)
2. Check that trust bias estimates are reasonable across different item quality levels
3. Test debiasing effectiveness on synthetic data with known bias parameters before moving to real data

## Limitations
- The separability assumption between position bias, trust bias, and relevance may not hold in all scenarios
- Reliance on public datasets with artificially converted continuous labels raises ecological validity concerns
- Computational complexity of doubly robust estimator and multiple propensity models not thoroughly discussed

## Confidence
High confidence in: The theoretical framework extending PBM to pairwise learning, the mathematical formulation of trust bias, and the core debiasing methodology.

Medium confidence in: The empirical improvements shown on public datasets, the effectiveness of the doubly robust estimator, and the practical significance of the online results.

Low confidence in: The generalizability of results to different domains, the robustness of the method under varying levels of bias, and the computational efficiency in large-scale production systems.

## Next Checks

3. Perform sensitivity analysis on the initialization of propensity models and the impact of different hyperparameter settings on both offline and online performance.
