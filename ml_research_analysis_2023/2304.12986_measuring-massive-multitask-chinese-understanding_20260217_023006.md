# Measuring Massive Multitask Chinese Understanding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12986
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12986
- Reference count: 2
- Models tested: GPT-3.5-turbo (39.5% zero-shot accuracy), ChatGLM 6B, MOSS 16B, Bloom models

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a comprehensive Chinese language model evaluation benchmark spanning four major domains: medicine, law, psychology, and education. The study evaluates multiple models including GPT-3.5-turbo, ChatGLM, MOSS, and Bloom variants across 11,845 multiple-choice questions. Results show that even the best-performing model (GPT-3.5-turbo) achieves only 39.5% average accuracy in zero-shot settings, with legal domain performance particularly weak at 25.9% maximum. The findings suggest that current Chinese language models have significant room for improvement in vertical domain tasks, and that parameter scaling alone is insufficient for achieving high performance in specialized domains.

## Method Summary
The evaluation benchmark consists of 11,845 multiple-choice questions across four major domains (medicine, law, psychology, education) with various subdomains. The study tests several models including GPT-3.5-turbo, ChatGLM 6B, MOSS 16B, and different Bloom configurations in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. Performance is measured using accuracy as the primary metric, with results reported for each domain and subdomain combination. The benchmark aims to provide a comprehensive assessment of Chinese language models' capabilities across diverse knowledge areas.

## Key Results
- GPT-3.5-turbo achieved highest average zero-shot accuracy of 39.5% across all domains
- Clinical medicine showed best performance at 69.3% accuracy
- Legal domain performance was particularly poor, with highest accuracy at 25.9%
- No model exceeded 50% accuracy in most subdomains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The benchmark's effectiveness stems from its comprehensive coverage of 11,845 multiple-choice questions spanning four major Chinese language domains. The standardized multiple-choice format enables consistent evaluation across different model architectures and sizes. The inclusion of both zero-shot and few-shot settings provides insights into models' ability to generalize from prompts versus requiring explicit examples.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Data preparation -> Model selection -> Prompt engineering -> Evaluation -> Result analysis

**Critical Path:** Benchmark construction → Model evaluation → Performance analysis → Insight generation

**Design Tradeoffs:** Multiple-choice format provides standardization but may not capture full reasoning capabilities; Large question set ensures coverage but requires extensive human annotation

**Failure Signatures:** Low accuracy across domains indicates insufficient domain-specific training data; Poor legal performance suggests challenges with formal/legal Chinese text; Limited few-shot improvement shows prompting strategies may be suboptimal

**First Experiments:** 1) Test additional prompt templates for each domain; 2) Evaluate open-ended responses for selected questions; 3) Compare with human expert performance on same questions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper highlights several open questions regarding the limitations of parameter scaling for specialized domain performance, the effectiveness of prompting strategies for Chinese language tasks, and the gap between automated accuracy scores and actual reasoning capabilities.

## Limitations
- Multiple-choice format may not fully capture reasoning capabilities
- Dataset coverage may have gaps across four major domains
- Single accuracy metric may miss nuances in model responses

## Confidence

**High confidence:** Overall performance findings and comparative model rankings are based on systematic evaluation across multiple models and domains

**Medium confidence:** Conclusions about parameter scaling limitations given testing only up to 16B parameters

**Low confidence:** Generalizability to real-world applications due to multiple-choice format constraints

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate model performance on open-ended responses in the same domains to assess reasoning depth beyond multiple-choice accuracy
2. Test larger parameter models (e.g., 30B+) and alternative architectures to determine if scaling laws differ for Chinese language models in vertical domains
3. Conduct human evaluation studies to verify the correctness of model responses and assess alignment between automated accuracy scores and actual knowledge demonstrated
