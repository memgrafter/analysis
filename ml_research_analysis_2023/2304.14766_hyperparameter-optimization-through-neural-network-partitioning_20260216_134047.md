# Hyperparameter Optimization through Neural Network Partitioning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14766
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14766
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Proposes a method to optimize hyperparameters in neural networks by partitioning both the model and data into K chunks, enabling efficient single-training-run optimization without validation data

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to hyperparameter optimization in neural networks by partitioning both the model and data into K chunks. Each partition is optimized only on specific data shards, and combining these partitions into subnetworks allows defining the "out-of-training-sample" loss as the objective for hyperparameter optimization. The method is computationally efficient, requiring only a single training run without validation data, and is significantly cheaper than alternatives aiming to optimize marginal likelihood. Experiments demonstrate the method's effectiveness in optimizing various hyperparameters, including data augmentations, in both centralized and federated learning settings.

## Method Summary
The method partitions both neural network parameters and training data into K chunks. Each parameter partition is trained only on its corresponding data shard, creating subnetworks with different data exposure patterns. The "out-of-training-sample" loss is defined as the loss on data shards unseen by each subnetwork, which serves as the objective for hyperparameter optimization. This approach approximates marginal likelihood without requiring validation data or expensive Hessian computations. The method scales efficiently to large datasets and models, with computational cost roughly corresponding to evaluating subnetworks on the training set.

## Key Results
- On CIFAR-10, the method outperforms baselines like Augerino and Differentiable Laplace, achieving 86.1% accuracy compared to 84.2% and 79.5% respectively
- In federated learning, the method reduces communication overhead while improving model performance, especially when the true data-generating process is known (e.g., rotated datasets)
- The method is robust to partitioning choices and learns useful invariances, particularly in low-data regimes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The method approximates marginal likelihood by defining "out-of-training-sample" loss on data shards unseen by each subnetwork.
- Mechanism: Each subnetwork is trained only on specific data shards and tested on later shards, mimicking cross-validation without requiring a validation set.
- Core assumption: The subnetwork trained on D1:k approximates the posterior over parameters given that data, and its performance on Dk+1:C approximates generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Combining these partitions into subnetworks allows us to define the 'out-of-training-sample' loss of a subnetwork, i.e., the loss on data shards unseen by the subnetwork, as the objective for hyperparameter optimization."
  - [section 3] "Each partition is associated with and optimized only on specific data shards. Combining these partitions into subnetworks allows us to define the 'out-of-training-sample' loss of a subnetwork, i.e., the loss on data shards unseen by the subnetwork, as the objective for hyperparameter optimization."
- Break condition: If the partitioning creates too strong correlations between partitions, the independence assumption breaks and the approximation becomes invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The method enables efficient hyperparameter optimization by requiring only a single training run without validation data.
- Mechanism: Hyperparameters are updated using gradients from subnetworks trained on different data shards, allowing joint optimization of parameters and hyperparameters in one pass.
- Core assumption: Gradients computed from subnetworks can effectively guide hyperparameter updates without needing separate validation data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We demonstrate that we can apply this objective to optimize a variety of different hyperparameters in a single training run while being significantly computationally cheaper than alternative methods aiming to optimize the marginal likelihood for neural networks."
  - [section 2.2] "Our goal is to propose an architecture and a training scheme so that we can easily obtain models trained on only subsets of the data D1:k for all k throughout training."
- Break condition: If the hyperparameter gradients are too noisy or biased, the optimization may converge to poor hyperparameters.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The method scales better than marginal likelihood approaches because it avoids computing or inverting Hessians.
- Mechanism: By using pointwise approximations to posteriors and direct gradient updates, the method eliminates expensive second-order computations required by Laplace approximations.
- Core assumption: The computational savings from avoiding Hessian computations outweigh any potential loss in approximation quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our method is more scalable than previous works that rely on marginal likelihood and Laplace approximations (which require computing or inverting a Hessian (Immer et al., 2021)) and is broadly applicable to any hierarchical modelling setup."
  - [section 3] "Our objective is computationally efficient, with a computational cost roughly corresponding to evaluating subnetworks on the training set. There is no need to compute nor invert a Hessian with respect to the weights, as in the Laplace approximation (Immer et al., 2021; 2022)."
- Break condition: If the network architecture or dataset size makes the Hessian computations manageable, the scalability advantage may diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data partitioning layer: Splits training data into K shards
  - Network partitioning layer: Divides model parameters into K partitions
  - Subnetwork construction module: Creates subnetworks by combining parameter partitions
  - Objective computation engine: Calculates out-of-training-sample loss for hyperparameter optimization
  - Gradient update scheduler: Manages interleaved updates of parameters and hyperparameters

- Critical path:
  1. Partition data into K shards
  2. Partition network parameters into K partitions
  3. For each partition, train subnetwork on corresponding data shards
  4. Compute out-of-sample loss using subnetworks on unseen data
  5. Update hyperparameters using computed gradients
  6. Iterate until convergence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - More partitions provide better approximation but increase complexity
  - Random vs. structured partitioning affects performance and memory usage
  - Different chunk proportions balance exploration vs. exploitation of data

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor hyperparameter optimization if partitions are too correlated
  - Memory issues if default weight values are non-zero
  - Suboptimal performance if partitioning strategy is poorly chosen

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Input selection task with synthetic data to verify correct model identification
  2. MNIST with affine augmentations to test learning useful invariances
  3. CIFAR-10 with varying partition schemes to assess sensitivity to partitioning choices

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of partitioned networks compare to traditional validation set optimization when using very small validation set proportions (e.g., 1%)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares partitioned networks to validation set optimization in Table 2, showing that partitioned networks outperform validation set optimization with fine-tuning across various chunk proportions, but the smallest proportion tested was 10%.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test validation set proportions smaller than 10%, leaving uncertainty about the performance of partitioned networks compared to traditional methods in extremely low-data validation scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing partitioned networks to validation set optimization with validation set proportions as low as 1% would provide clarity on the relative performance in such scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different partitioning strategies (e.g., dynamic vs. fixed) on the performance of partitioned networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that partitioning happens prior to training and remains fixed throughout, and briefly discusses other partitioning schemes like node partitioning in Appendix C, but does not explore dynamic partitioning strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a single partitioning strategy (random weight partitioning) and does not investigate the potential benefits or drawbacks of alternative partitioning approaches, such as dynamic partitioning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of partitioned networks using different partitioning strategies (e.g., fixed vs. dynamic) would provide insights into the impact of partitioning choices on the method's effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of partitioned networks scale with increasing dataset size and model complexity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates the scalability of partitioned networks on datasets like CIFAR-10 and TinyImagenet, and shows that it outperforms baselines on these datasets. However, it does not explicitly explore the scaling behavior with extremely large datasets or very deep, complex models.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows promising results on moderate-sized datasets and models, it does not investigate the limits of partitioned networks' scalability, particularly for massive datasets or state-of-the-art deep learning architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating the performance of partitioned networks on extremely large datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and very deep, complex models (e.g., Vision Transformers) would provide insights into the method's scalability limits and potential challenges in these scenarios.

## Limitations
- The partitioning approach introduces a fundamental tradeoff between approximation quality and computational efficiency
- The method's effectiveness depends heavily on the partitioning strategy, yet the paper does not provide a systematic way to determine optimal partitioning schemes
- The assumption that subnetworks trained on D1:k can approximate the posterior over parameters given that data may break down for highly correlated features or when data shards have significantly different distributions

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The computational efficiency claims are well-supported by the elimination of Hessian computations and the single-training-run requirement. The federated learning communication savings are directly observable and measurable.
- **Medium Confidence**: The approximation quality of the "out-of-training-sample" loss as a proxy for marginal likelihood is theoretically sound but may not hold across all data distributions and network architectures. The effectiveness in learning useful invariances is demonstrated but limited to specific augmentation types.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the method to arbitrary hyperparameter spaces and complex federated learning scenarios without prior knowledge of the data-generating process is not thoroughly validated.

## Next Checks

1. **Partitioning Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically test different partitioning strategies (random vs. structured, varying partition sizes) across multiple datasets to identify conditions where the method's approximation breaks down.

2. **Cross-Distribution Generalization**: Evaluate the method on federated learning benchmarks with heterogeneous client data distributions to verify whether the communication savings and performance improvements persist when the true data-generating process is unknown.

3. **Approximation Error Quantification**: Compare the out-of-training-sample loss estimates against ground-truth marginal likelihood computations on small networks to measure the approximation error and identify scenarios where the method may fail.
