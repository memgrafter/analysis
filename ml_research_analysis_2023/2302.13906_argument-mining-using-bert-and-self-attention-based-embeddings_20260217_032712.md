# Argument Mining using BERT and Self-Attention based Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13906
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13906
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: F1 score of 0.52 for edge prediction and 0.81 for type prediction on CDCP dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach to argument mining in online discourse that addresses the challenge of modeling complex, non-tree argument structures found in forums. The authors present a two-phase methodology using BERT for argument component classification and self-attention-based embeddings for link prediction between argument components. The approach leverages transfer learning with BERT, fine-tuned on the Cornell eRulemaking Corpus, followed by a transformer encoder layer to capture contextual relationships between argument spans.

## Method Summary
The method employs a two-phase approach: first, BERT is fine-tuned on the CDCP dataset to classify argument spans into five types (Value, Policy, Reference, Fact, Testimony). The method then uses a transformer encoder layer with self-attention to capture hierarchical relationships between argument components. Sentence embeddings are created by taking a weighted average of the last n BERT layers, which are then projected to premise and conclusion spaces for link prediction. The model predicts causational links between argument components, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the CDCP dataset.

## Key Results
- Achieved F1 score of 0.52 for edge prediction (causational links)
- Achieved F1 score of 0.81 for type prediction (argument component classification)
- Outperformed most baselines while matching the performance of TSP+PLBA approach
- Successfully handled non-tree argument structures in online discourse

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BERT's contextual embeddings capture semantic relationships between argument components better than non-contextual embeddings.
- Mechanism: BERT uses self-attention to weigh the importance of each token in relation to all others, producing contextualized representations that encode meaning based on surrounding words.
- Core assumption: The semantic meaning of argument components depends on their context within the sentence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a novel methodology for argument mining is proposed which employs attention-based embeddings for link prediction"
  - [section] "The recent trend in NLP is leveraging Transfer Learning on huge pre-trained models for better performance... Since then, transfer learning in natural language processing has aided in solving several tasks with state-of-the-art performance."
- Break condition: If the argument spans are too short or isolated, context may not provide meaningful information.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Transformer encoder layers can model hierarchical relationships between argument components.
- Mechanism: The encoder layer applies self-attention across the sentence embeddings to capture dependencies and hierarchical structures among argument components.
- Core assumption: Argument components in a discourse form hierarchical relationships that can be learned through attention mechanisms.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "employs attention-based embeddings for link prediction to model the causational hierarchies in typical argument structures"
  - [section] "We use the transformer encoder layer and generate embeddings from the encodings that capture the hierarchical relations between argument components."
- Break condition: If the argument structures are too complex or non-hierarchical, the model may fail to capture all relationships.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Weighted averaging of BERT layers captures different levels of abstraction for argument classification.
- Mechanism: Taking a weighted average of the last n layers of BERT's hidden states combines lower-level lexical features with higher-level semantic information.
- Core assumption: Different BERT layers encode different levels of semantic abstraction relevant to argument classification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We take a weighted average of the last n layers of the hidden layer outputs to make the embeddings."
  - [section] "The weights were sampled from an arithmetic progression for best results."
- Break condition: If the layer weights are not optimal for the specific dataset, performance may degrade.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed BERT and self-attention approach scale to much larger argument structures and longer documents?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that the dataset used (CDCP) contains about 731 comments with 4500 propositions, which is relatively small, and the results show performance on this limited dataset.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experiments or analysis on how the approach would perform on larger, more complex argument structures or longer documents with many more propositions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on larger datasets with more propositions per document and longer documents would demonstrate scalability and performance characteristics.

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide ablation studies or sensitivity analysis to understand the impact of hyperparameter choices on the approach's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies and sensitivity analysis on the impact of different hyperparameter choices would provide insights into the approach's robustness and optimal configuration.

- Question: How does the proposed approach handle arguments with complex, non-causational relationships, such as parallel arguments or arguments with multiple conclusions?

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide examples or analysis of the approach's performance on arguments with complex, non-causational relationships.

## Limitations
- Dataset specificity: Approach evaluated only on CDCP dataset from consumer debt collection domain
- Computational complexity: Two-phase approach with BERT fine-tuning and transformer encoder is computationally expensive
- Edge prediction performance: F1 score of 0.52 remains relatively low for sparse link prediction task

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Core methodology of using BERT for component classification and self-attention for link prediction is sound
- Medium Confidence: Reported performance improvements over baselines are credible but limited evaluation scope
- Low Confidence: Specific hyperparameter choices lack systematic justification and optimization
