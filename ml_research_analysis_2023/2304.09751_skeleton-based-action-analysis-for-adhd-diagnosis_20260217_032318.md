# Skeleton-based action analysis for ADHD diagnosis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09751
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09751
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: 88.2% accuracy and 0.83 AUC for ADHD diagnosis using skeleton-based action recognition

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel, cost-effective system for ADHD diagnosis using skeleton-based action recognition. The method captures subjects' movements during attention tasks through multi-camera video, extracts skeleton sequences using pose estimation, and classifies actions using a 3D convolutional network (PoseC3D). By calculating an Attention Deficit Ratio (ADR) based on action change frequency, the system can distinguish ADHD cases from controls with high accuracy, offering a practical alternative to expensive and time-consuming traditional diagnostic methods.

## Method Summary
The system processes multi-modal video recordings of subjects performing attention tasks using three synchronized cameras. Frame sequences are extracted at 25 FPS and fed through a pose detection pipeline combining HRNet and Faster-RCNN to extract 17-joint skeleton coordinates. These skeleton sequences are then classified into three action categories (still, fidgeting, movement) using a 3D CNN called PoseC3D, which takes stacked 2D pose heatmaps as input. The Attention Deficit Ratio (ADR) is calculated from the frequency of action changes, and a threshold-based approach determines ADHD diagnosis.

## Key Results
- Achieves 88.2% accuracy and 0.83 AUC in distinguishing ADHD subjects from controls
- Outperforms conventional RGB-based action recognition methods
- Demonstrates practical potential for low-cost screening in clinical settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Skeleton-based action recognition isolates ADHD-relevant movement patterns by removing environmental noise
- Mechanism: Converting RGB video to skeleton-joint coordinates focuses purely on body movements, reducing interference from background, lighting, and other people
- Core assumption: Human pose estimation accurately tracks all 17 joints across varied sitting positions without occlusion
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "skeleton-based action recognition has gained attention due to the action-focused nature and robustness." [section] "Through these tasks, action-related information is extracted without contextual nuisances, such as background variation and unrelated personnel interference."
- Break condition: If pose estimation fails due to occlusion or extreme angles, the skeleton sequence becomes incomplete, leading to misclassified actions

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Attention Deficit Ratio (ADR) metric effectively differentiates ADHD subjects from controls based on action change frequency
- Mechanism: ADR rewards consistent actions and penalizes frequent changes, capturing the hyperactivity-impulsivity symptom pattern described in DSM-V
- Core assumption: ADHD subjects exhibit significantly higher action change frequency than controls during the attention tasks
- Evidence anchors: [section] "the action change frequency for the ADHD subject is significantly higher than the control." [section] "the average ADR for 7 subjects and 10 controls are 71.7% and 79.8%, respectively."
- Break condition: If control subjects have high natural movement variability, the ADR threshold may not cleanly separate groups

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PoseC3D's heatmap-based input outperforms GCN methods for ADHD action recognition
- Mechanism: By stacking 2D pose heatmaps into 3D volumes, PoseC3D captures both spatial joint relationships and temporal action patterns more robustly than graph-based approaches
- Core assumption: The heatmap representation preserves discriminative action features better than raw skeleton coordinates
- Evidence anchors: [section] "PoseC3D is more robust to the upstream pose estimation and temporal actions due to the 3D structure of heatmap." [section] "the proposed PoseC3D is significantly higher than the C3D, R3D, ST-GCN and MS-G3D in precision, accuracy, F1 Score and AUC."
- Break condition: If heatmap resolution is insufficient, subtle action differences may be lost, reducing classification accuracy

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Multi-camera video input → Frame segmentation (25 FPS) → Pose detection (HRNet + Faster-RCNN) → Skeleton extraction → Action classification (PoseC3D) → ADR calculation → Binary diagnosis
- Critical path: Video recording → Pose estimation → Action classification → ADR computation → Threshold-based diagnosis
- Design tradeoffs: Skeleton-based methods sacrifice environmental context for movement purity; heatmap input trades computational efficiency for robustness against pose estimation noise
- Failure signatures: High false positives may indicate noisy skeleton tracking; low sensitivity may suggest insufficient action categories or poor ADR threshold tuning
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify skeleton extraction accuracy across all three camera angles with a known action sequence
  2. Test ADR calculation on synthetic action sequences with controlled change frequencies
  3. Compare PoseC3D action classification accuracy against ST-GCN and MS-G3D using the same training/validation split
