# Generating medically-accurate summaries of patient-provider dialogue: A multi-stage approach using large language models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05982
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05982
- Authors: Multiple
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Multi-stage LLM approach generates clinically accurate summaries preferred over naive baseline, with consistent improvements in capturing critical medical history sections.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents MEDSUM-ENT, a multi-stage approach for generating medically-accurate summaries of patient-provider dialogues using GPT-3. The method extracts medical entities and their affirmation status (present, absent, unknown) from the dialogue, then uses these extractions as additional input to inform the final summarization step. Evaluation using both human expert reviewers and GPT-derived automated metrics shows that MEDSUM-ENT generates clinically accurate summaries that are preferred over a naive zero-shot, single-prompt baseline, with consistent improvements in capturing "Pertinent Unknowns" and "Medical History" sections crucial for clinical decision-making.

## Method Summary
The approach discretizes medical conversation summarization into sequential dialogue-understanding tasks. First, medical entities and their affirmations are identified within the conversation to serve as building blocks. Few-shot prompts are dynamically constructed by conditioning on relevant patient information. The system uses GPT-3 as the backbone, employing in-context examples selected through semantic similarity based on patient age, sex, and medical context. The multi-stage pipeline includes RFE entity extraction, dialogue entity extraction (turn-by-turn), unknown entity resolution, and summary generation using extracted entities, producing structured medical summaries with six sections.

## Key Results
- MEDSUM-ENT generates clinically accurate summaries preferred over naive zero-shot baseline
- Consistent improvements in capturing "Pertinent Unknowns" and "Medical History" sections
- Random in-context example selection outperformed semantic-similarity-based selection
- 28% of expert-evaluated summaries contained incorrect information that could harm patients

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Multi-stage entity extraction improves medical summarization accuracy by providing structured medical context to the LLM. The approach first extracts medical entities and their affirmation status (present, absent, unknown) from the dialogue, then uses these extractions as additional input to inform the final summarization step through prompt chaining. Providing explicit medical entities and their statuses as structured input helps the LLM generate more accurate and comprehensive summaries than relying solely on raw dialogue text.

### Mechanism 2
Few-shot prompting with dynamically constructed prompts conditioned on relevant patient information improves entity extraction and summarization performance. The system uses in-context examples selected based on semantic similarity to the current patient's age, sex, and medical context, rather than random selection. Examples that are semantically similar to the current case provide better guidance for the LLM than randomly selected examples.

### Mechanism 3
GPT-derived automated metrics that account for paraphrasing and rephrasing provide more accurate evaluation of medical summarization than traditional exact-match metrics. The system uses GPT-3 to extract medical concepts from both predicted and ground-truth texts, then verifies whether concepts extracted from ground truth are present in the predicted text, allowing for different phrasings. Medical concepts can be expressed in multiple equivalent ways, and evaluation metrics should recognize these paraphrases rather than requiring exact string matches.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dialogue -> RFE entity extraction -> Dialogue entity extraction -> Unknown entity resolution -> Summary generation
- Critical path: Dialogue → RFE entity extraction → Dialogue entity extraction → Unknown entity resolution → Summary generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Multiple model calls vs. single call: Multi-stage approach adds latency but improves accuracy
  - Semantic similarity vs. random example selection: Surprisingly, random selection performed better in this case
  - GPT-derived metrics vs. human evaluation: Automated metrics are faster but may miss nuanced clinical errors
- Failure signatures:
  - Entity extraction errors propagating to summary generation
  - Missing or incorrect affirmation statuses (present/absent/unknown)
  - Failure to capture "Pertinent Unknowns" or "Medical History" sections
  - Clinical inaccuracies that could harm patient care
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare naive zero-shot summarization vs. MEDSUM-ENT with 1-shot semantic example selection
  2. Test impact of unknown entity resolver by running with and without this step
  3. Evaluate different k-shot configurations (1, 3, 5-shot) with random example selection to test diminishing returns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do random in-context example selection methods outperform semantic-similarity-based selection for few-shot prompting in medical entity extraction and summarization tasks? The paper found that random in-context example selection performed consistently better than semantic-similarity-based selection across experiments, which aligns with Ye et al. (2022) findings that diversity of in-context samples is more important than similarity. This finding is unresolved because the paper only reports this finding but doesn't investigate the underlying reasons why random selection outperforms semantic similarity.

### Open Question 2
What is the optimal number of shots (k) for few-shot prompting in medical entity extraction tasks using GPT-3, and why does increasing k beyond 1 show diminishing returns? The paper found no correlation between larger k-shot values and increased performance, suggesting diminishing returns for GPT-3 in medical concept extraction. However, the optimal k value was not determined because the experiments only tested k values of 1, 3, and 5 shots, and no systematic analysis was performed to find the optimal k value or understand why performance plateaus.

### Open Question 3
How can the accuracy of medical dialogue summarization be improved to reduce clinically harmful information in generated summaries? The paper acknowledges that 28% of expert-evaluated summaries contained incorrect information that could harm patients, particularly misattributed symptoms and conditions. This represents a critical limitation for clinical deployment because the paper identifies this problem but doesn't propose specific solutions or modifications to the MEDSUM-ENT framework to address this high error rate in critical sections like "Pertinent Positives" and "Pertinent Negatives."

## Limitations

- Prompt engineering details not fully specified, making faithful reproduction difficult
- Clinical validation limited to 20 examples rather than comprehensive accuracy assessment
- GPT-derived metrics may have circularity issues affecting reliability
- 28% of expert-evaluated summaries contained clinically harmful incorrect information

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The multi-stage approach to medical summarization is well-grounded in the literature and the general methodology (entity extraction followed by summarization) is sound. The paper clearly demonstrates improvements over a naive baseline.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific implementation details and the superiority of the MEDSUM-ENT approach over alternatives are reasonably supported, though some surprising findings (random vs. semantic example selection) warrant further investigation.

**Low Confidence**: The clinical safety claims and the reliability of GPT-derived metrics for medical evaluation need more extensive validation before deployment in real clinical settings.
