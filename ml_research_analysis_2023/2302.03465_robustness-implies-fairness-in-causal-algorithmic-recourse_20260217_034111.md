# Robustness Implies Fairness in Causal Algorithmic Recourse

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03465
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03465
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FARO recourse provides adversarial robustness and individual fairness simultaneously, achieving perfect fairness (σFR = 0) across various datasets and classifiers while maintaining comparable performance to traditional methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of achieving both fairness and robustness in algorithmic recourse by proposing a new framework that views protected features as a pseudometric. The authors demonstrate that individual fairness is a special case of adversarial robustness, introducing the fair robust recourse (FARO) problem to achieve both properties simultaneously. They prove the existence of FARO recourse under certain conditions and show empirically that it outperforms traditional robust recourse in terms of fairness metrics.

## Method Summary
The authors propose the FARO framework that operates on linear SCMs with protected categorical features. The method uses counterfactual perturbations based on pseudometrics to generate recourse recommendations that are both robust to small perturbations and fair across protected groups. The approach combines hard interventions for categorical features and soft interventions for continuous features, defining counterfactual perturbations using product metrics. The optimal recourse action is found by solving the FARO problem, which ensures both adversarial robustness and individual fairness simultaneously.

## Key Results
- FARO achieved perfect fairness (σFR = 0) across various datasets and classifiers in numerical simulations
- FARO outperforms traditional robust recourse in terms of fairness metrics while maintaining comparable performance
- The framework successfully demonstrates that individual fairness can be achieved through robustness by treating protected features as a pseudometric

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Individual fairness can be achieved through robustness by treating protected features as a pseudometric.
- Mechanism: By defining protected features using a pseudometric where distances between certain protected groups are zero, the framework ensures that counterfactual explanations for individuals in these groups are symmetric. This symmetry inherently enforces individual fairness.
- Core assumption: Protected features can be meaningfully represented as a pseudometric space where zero distance implies similar treatment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The new setting views the protected feature as a pseudometric and demonstrates that individual fairness is a special case of adversarial robustness."
  - [section] "The variable A is called partially protected if there exist two levels such that their distance is 0: ∃a,a′∈A, s.t. dA(a,a′) = 0∧a̸=a′"
- Break condition: If the pseudometric cannot be defined meaningfully for the protected feature, or if the distance metric does not capture the intended fairness notion.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adversarial robustness implies individual fairness for small perturbation radii.
- Mechanism: The framework uses counterfactual perturbations that decompose into additive perturbations for each protected group. For small enough perturbation radii, this ensures that counterfactual explanations for twins are symmetric, leading to individual fairness.
- Core assumption: The counterfactual perturbation can be decomposed into additive perturbations that maintain symmetry for small radii.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We demonstrate that individual fairness can be viewed as a specific case of robustness in the new recourse problem setting."
  - [section] "Lemma (Perturbation Reduction Lemma). If A is protected and other categorical variables in M are not partially protected, then there exist ∆0 that for all ∆≤ ∆0: BCF∆(v) = ⋃a∈A BMdo(A=a)∆+(¨va)"
- Break condition: If the perturbation radius is too large, or if the decomposition of counterfactual perturbations does not maintain symmetry.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The Fair Robust Recourse (FARO) problem ensures both adversarial robustness and individual fairness simultaneously.
- Mechanism: FARO is defined as the limit of the adversarially fair robust recourse problem as the perturbation radius approaches zero. This limit ensures that the recourse action is both robust to small perturbations and fair across protected groups.
- Core assumption: The limit of the adversarially fair robust recourse problem as the perturbation radius approaches zero exists and is well-defined.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Finally, we introduce the fair robust recourse problem to achieve both desirable properties and show how it can be satisfied both theoretically and empirically."
  - [section] "Deﬁnition(FARO Recourse Problem). Assume (∆n)∞n=1 be sequence of positive values such that ∆n approaches 0. Let the a∗∆n be the optimal action according to the AFRR problem for counterfactual perturbation with radius ∆n. If the limn→∞a∗∆n exist, we say that the fair robust (FARO) recourse problem has a solution for instance v."
- Break condition: If the limit of the adversarially fair robust recourse problem does not exist, or if the perturbation sequence does not converge to zero.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> SCM definition -> Counterfactual computation -> Fairness and robustness evaluation -> Optimization
- Critical path:
  1. Define the SCM and protected features
  2. Compute counterfactual explanations and their twins
  3. Define the counterfactual perturbation using pseudometrics
  4. Solve the FARO problem to find the optimal recourse action
  5. Evaluate the fairness and robustness of the recourse recommendations
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational complexity: Solving the FARO problem may be computationally expensive, especially for large datasets and complex SCMs.
  - Perturbation radius: Choosing an appropriate perturbation radius is crucial for balancing fairness and robustness.
  - Protected feature definition: Defining the pseudometric for protected features requires careful consideration of the fairness notion.
- Failure signatures:
  - Non-convergence of the FARO problem solution
  - Violation of individual fairness constraints
  - Infeasible recourse actions due to actionability constraints
  - High computational cost for large-scale problems
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the existence of the FARO problem solution for a simple linear SCM with a binary protected feature.
  2. Compare the fairness and robustness of FARO recourse with traditional robust recourse on a synthetic dataset.
  3. Apply the FARO framework to a real-world dataset (e.g., Adult Income) and evaluate the fairness and robustness of the recourse recommendations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the proposed FARO framework be generalized to handle more complex SCMs with multiple protected features and non-linear relationships?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper explicitly states "While the conditions outlined in this section may be expanded upon in a more general manner, the complexity of the resulting formulas precludes us from achieving our objective of identifying fair and robust recourse. The generalization of the concepts presented in this chapter will be addressed in future research."
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on linear SCMs with a single protected categorical feature, but real-world scenarios often involve multiple protected features and non-linear relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing explicit formulas for counterfactuals, twins, and recourse costs for non-linear SCMs with multiple protected features, and demonstrating the existence of FARO recourse in such scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the computational complexities of finding FARO recourse actions, and how do they scale with the number of features and protected groups?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using "a brute-force search to find the optimal action" in the experiments, implying potential computational challenges.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not analyze the computational complexity of finding FARO recourse actions or provide insights into how it scales with problem size.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis of the computational complexity of finding FARO recourse actions, along with empirical studies on how the computation time scales with the number of features and protected groups.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed FARO framework perform in real-world applications beyond the loan approval and income prediction scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper presents case studies on the ASCIncome dataset and a semi-synthetic loan approval dataset, but these are limited in scope.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not evaluate the performance of FARO on a diverse set of real-world applications, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the FARO framework to a variety of real-world datasets and domains, such as healthcare, criminal justice, and education, and comparing its performance to other recourse methods in terms of fairness, robustness, and overall effectiveness.

## Limitations

- The framework requires linear SCMs with specific structural assumptions, limiting generalizability to complex real-world scenarios
- The perturbation reduction lemma only applies to partially protected categorical variables, with unclear extension to mixed feature types
- Empirical validation is limited to synthetic datasets and a small number of real datasets with binary classifiers

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The theoretical relationship between robustness and individual fairness (proven via pseudometric framework)
- **Medium confidence**: The existence proof for FARO solutions under specified conditions
- **Low confidence**: Empirical performance claims across diverse real-world datasets
