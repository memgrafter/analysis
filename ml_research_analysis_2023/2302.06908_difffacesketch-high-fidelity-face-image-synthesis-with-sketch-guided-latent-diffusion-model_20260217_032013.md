# DiffFaceSketch: High-Fidelity Face Image Synthesis with Sketch-Guided Latent Diffusion Model

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.06908
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06908
- **Reference count**: 37

## Executive Summary
DiffFaceSketch presents a novel approach for synthesizing high-fidelity face images from monochrome sketches using a Sketch-Guided Latent Diffusion Model (SGLDM). The method addresses the challenge of preserving high-dimensional face features like geometry and color while handling sketches with varying abstraction levels. By employing a Multi-Auto-Encoder to encode facial regions separately and a 2-stage training process with Stochastic Region Abstraction augmentation, SGLDM achieves superior performance compared to existing methods across multiple fidelity metrics.

## Method Summary
DiffFaceSketch uses a 2-stage training approach with a Sketch-Guided Latent Diffusion Model. In the first stage, a Multi-Auto-Encoder with five partial encoders processes different facial regions separately to reduce sketch dimensionality while preserving geometric details. In the second stage, these encoded features condition a latent diffusion model that denoises latent representations into final face images. The method incorporates Stochastic Region Abstraction augmentation to improve robustness to varying sketch abstraction levels, and is trained on a paired sketch-face dataset built from Celeba-HQ images.

## Key Results
- SGLDM achieves FID scores of 43.58, 45.46, and 46.83 for low, mid, and high abstraction levels respectively
- Outperforms Pix2pixHD and other state-of-the-art methods across FID, LPIPS, and recall ratio metrics
- Demonstrates robustness to sketches with varying abstraction levels through SRA augmentation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The 2-Stage training process enables better distribution mapping between sketch and image domains. By first pre-training the Multi-AE to encode sketch features separately, then training the LDM with these pre-encoded features, the model learns a smoother mapping between domains rather than trying to jointly learn both tasks.

### Mechanism 2
Multi-Auto-Encoder architecture preserves local face details while reducing sketch input dimensionality. The five partial encoders process sketches separately, encoding them into latent space while maintaining geometric information, allowing the model to handle complex local details like accessories and expressions.

### Mechanism 3
Stochastic Region Abstraction (SRA) improves robustness to sketches with varying abstraction levels. By generating sketches at different resolutions and randomly combining cropped regions with different abstraction levels, the model learns to handle diverse sketch qualities and styles.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Sketch → Multi-AE → Latent diffusion → Output image
- **Critical path**: Sketch → Multi-AE → Latent diffusion → Output image
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Resolution vs. computational cost: The model works at 256×256 resolution due to LDM computational constraints
  - Separate training vs. joint training: 2-Stage training provides better distribution mapping but requires more training time
  - Local vs. global encoding: Multi-AE preserves local details but may lose some global spatial relationships
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Noisy or distorted output faces indicate issues with the denoising process
  - Loss of sketch consistency suggests problems with the conditioning mechanism
  - Poor generalization to different abstraction levels indicates SRA augmentation issues
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test the Multi-AE on isolated facial regions to verify it preserves geometric details
  2. Evaluate the LDM with pre-encoded features from the Multi-AE to check distribution mapping
  3. Test SRA augmentation by generating sketches at different resolutions and measuring model robustness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of SGLDM compare to other state-of-the-art models when applied to non-face image synthesis tasks, such as those involving the LSUN or AFHQ datasets?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests that a similar framework could be applied to other sketch-image tasks by changing the training dataset, such as LSUN and AFHQ.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates SGLDM on face synthesis tasks, and no comparative analysis is provided for other types of image synthesis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments using SGLDM on datasets like LSUN and AFHQ, and comparing the results with state-of-the-art models on these datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the specific trade-off methods or algorithms that could be implemented to balance the input consistency and output convincibility of SGLDM, especially when dealing with extremely poor sketches?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that noise and artifacts might be generated when inputting extremely poor sketches, indicating a need for trade-off methods or algorithms.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the issue but does not propose specific solutions or methods to address this limitation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing specific algorithms or methods that can effectively balance input consistency and output convincibility, and evaluating their performance on poor-quality sketches.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would the inclusion of color cues in the input sketches affect the performance of SGLDM in terms of color information synthesis, such as for skin and hair regions?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests considering a method of inputting several color cues to handle color information such as skin and hair regions.
- Why unresolved: The current implementation of SGLDM does not include color cues, and the impact of their inclusion on the model's performance is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Modifying SGLDM to accept color cues in the input sketches and conducting experiments to assess improvements in color information synthesis for skin and hair regions.

## Limitations
- Performance claims are based on controlled datasets without extensive real-world sketch testing
- The model operates at 256×256 resolution due to LDM computational constraints
- Limited analysis of edge cases where random region combinations in SRA might create unrealistic sketch patterns

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The technical architecture and 2-stage training approach are well-described and logically sound
- **Medium confidence**: The claim of superior performance is supported by quantitative metrics but lacks comprehensive ablation studies
- **Low confidence**: The generalization capability to real-world sketches outside the controlled dataset is not demonstrated

## Next Checks

3. **Component isolation**: Train a baseline model without Multi-AE (using direct sketch encoding) while keeping other components constant to validate the architectural contribution
