# SI-LSTM: Speaker Hybrid Long-short Term Memory and Cross Modal Attention for Emotion Recognition in Conversation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03506
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03506
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: SI-LSTM framework achieves 70.43% accuracy on IEMOCAP and 66.59% on MELD datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SI-LSTM, a novel framework for Emotion Recognition in Conversation (ERC) that integrates speaker information with LSTM networks and employs both joint modal attention and cross-modal attention mechanisms. The key innovation is tracking speaker-specific emotional states through GRU-updated speaker vectors fed into modified LSTM gates, combined with attention mechanisms that condition modality fusion on speaker context. Experimental results on IEMOCAP and MELD datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, achieving accuracy of 70.43% and 66.59% respectively.

## Method Summary
The SI-LSTM framework processes multimodal conversation data by first extracting text features using fine-tuned RoBERTa (1024-dim) and audio features using OpenSMILE IS13 ComParE1 (100-dim). A GRU-based speaker updater maintains speaker state vectors that are fed into modified LSTM gates alongside modality features. Joint modal attention conditions the fusion of text and audio features on speaker state, while cross-modal attention captures complementary information between modalities. The framework is trained with Adam optimizer (lr=1e-3, decay=0.97), batch size 60, dropout 0.5, and cross-entropy loss, and evaluated using accuracy and weighted F1 score on 6-class IEMOCAP and 7-class MELD datasets.

## Key Results
- SI-LSTM achieves 70.43% accuracy on IEMOCAP dataset
- SI-LSTM achieves 66.59% accuracy on MELD dataset
- Outperforms state-of-the-art baselines including DialogueRNN, COSMIC, and DAG-ERC

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Speaker information integrated into LSTM gates allows tracking of speaker-specific emotional states over time
- Speaker state vectors are updated via GRU at each utterance, then fed into LSTM gates (input, forget, output) alongside modality features
- Core assumption: Speaker emotional states evolve sequentially and can be represented by fixed-length vectors updated with each utterance
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "emotional states of the distinct speaker can be tracked in a sequential way"; [section] "qs,t = GRU(ut,q s,t−1)"

### Mechanism 2
- Joint modal attention conditioned on speaker information improves cross-modal fusion for emotion features
- Attention weights between text and audio modalities are computed using both modality features and speaker state
- Core assumption: The relative importance of audio vs text features for emotion depends on both the content and the speaker
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "joint modal attention mechanism paired with the speaker information into the LSTM"; [section] "zma→mt t = softmax((xmt t ×p)× (xma t ×q))×xmt t"

### Mechanism 3
- Cross-modal attention between final text and audio representations captures complementary emotional cues
- Text and audio features are attended to each other via cross-modal attention, then combined with SI-LSTM outputs for classification
- Core assumption: Important emotional information is distributed across modalities and can be better captured by attending between them
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "cross-modal attention mechanism is employed to extract the important information"; [section] "attn = softmax(Qma √dk × (K mt)T)"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input -> Unimodal feature extraction (RoBERTa + OpenSMILE) -> Transformer encoders -> GRU speaker updater -> SI-LSTM with joint modal attention -> Cross-modal attention -> Weighted fusion -> Classification
- Critical path: Speaker state update -> SI-LSTM processing -> Cross-modal attention fusion -> Classification
- Design tradeoffs: SI-LSTM adds complexity and parameters but allows speaker-aware emotion tracking; cross-modal attention adds another fusion layer but may introduce noise
- Failure signatures: Poor speaker state updates lead to degraded performance; incorrect modality weighting hurts fusion; overfitting on small datasets due to many parameters
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train SI-LSTM without speaker information to confirm its contribution
  2. Train without joint modal attention to isolate its effect
  3. Train without cross-modal attention to test necessity of second-stage fusion

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the SI-LSTM framework perform on other multimodal datasets beyond IEMOCAP and MELD, particularly those with different conversation structures or more participants?
- Basis in paper: The paper only evaluates the SI-LSTM framework on IEMOCAP and MELD datasets, leaving open the question of its generalizability to other datasets
- Why unresolved: The authors did not test the framework on a diverse range of multimodal conversation datasets, limiting the understanding of its broader applicability
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results on additional benchmark datasets with varying conversation structures, participant numbers, and emotion categories would provide insights into the framework's generalizability

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different text and audio feature extraction methods on the performance of the SI-LSTM framework?
- Basis in paper: The paper uses RoBERTa for text feature extraction and OPENSML toolkit for audio feature extraction, but does not explore the impact of using alternative methods
- Why unresolved: The authors did not conduct experiments with different feature extraction techniques, leaving uncertainty about the optimal feature representation for the SI-LSTM framework
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using various text and audio feature extraction methods, such as different pre-trained language models or audio feature extractors, would reveal the impact on the framework's performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the SI-LSTM framework handle real-time emotion recognition in conversations, considering the computational complexity of the model?
- Basis in paper: The paper focuses on offline emotion recognition and does not address the framework's suitability for real-time applications
- Why unresolved: The authors did not discuss the computational efficiency of the SI-LSTM framework or its potential for real-time emotion recognition, which is crucial for practical deployment
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments measuring the inference time of the SI-LSTM framework on various hardware setups and comparisons with other real-time emotion recognition models would provide insights into its practical applicability

## Limitations

- Model complexity with multiple attention mechanisms and speaker state tracking may lead to overfitting on small datasets
- Limited evaluation on only two benchmark datasets (IEMOCAP and MELD) restricts generalization assessment
- Lack of detailed ablation studies to quantify individual contributions of SI-LSTM, joint modal attention, and cross-modal attention components

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Speaker information integrated into LSTM gates allows tracking of speaker-specific emotional states
- Medium Confidence: Joint modal attention conditioned on speaker information improves cross-modal fusion
- Medium Confidence: Cross-modal attention mechanism's contribution is supported but less detailed
