# Decoding the Underlying Meaning of Multimodal Hateful Memes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17678
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17678
- Reference count: 6
- Key outcome: Introduces HatReD dataset for explainable hateful meme detection with challenging conditional generation task

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HatReD, the first multimodal hateful meme dataset annotated with underlying hateful contextual reasons. The authors address the lack of explainable hateful meme methods by collecting free-text explanations for memes from Facebook's Fine-Grained Hateful Memes dataset. They establish a new conditional generation task where models generate reasons to explain hateful memes. The authors fine-tune state-of-the-art pre-trained language models on HatReD and conduct extensive experiments, finding that the task is challenging even for large PLMs. Human evaluation shows that while generated reasons are fluent, they often lack relevance compared to ground truth explanations. The authors also demonstrate domain adaptation potential by evaluating models on an unseen misogynous meme dataset.

## Method Summary
The authors collect free-text explanations for hateful memes from Facebook's Fine-Grained Hateful Memes dataset to create HatReD. They establish a new conditional generation task where models must generate explanations for why memes are hateful. The approach involves fine-tuning state-of-the-art pre-trained language models on this dataset, using the meme's text and image as input to generate explanations. The methodology includes human evaluation of generated explanations across fluency and relevance dimensions, as well as domain adaptation experiments on misogynous memes.

## Key Results
- Human evaluation shows generated explanations have reasonable fluency (7.32/10) but lower relevance (6.11/10)
- The task proves challenging even for large pre-trained language models
- Domain adaptation experiments show promising transferability to unseen misogynous meme datasets
- Dataset contains 2,895 annotated memes with free-text explanations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism leverages human-annotated explanations to create a supervised learning framework for hateful meme explanation generation. By providing ground truth explanations as training targets, the approach enables models to learn the relationship between multimodal hateful content and the contextual reasons that make them hateful. The conditional generation framework allows models to generate explanations conditioned on both visual and textual meme components, capturing the multimodal nature of hateful content.

## Foundational Learning
- Multimodal hate detection: Why needed - to identify hateful content that combines visual and textual elements; Quick check - models must process both image and text modalities effectively
- Conditional text generation: Why needed - to produce explanations based on input memes; Quick check - generated text should be fluent and contextually relevant
- Human evaluation metrics: Why needed - to assess explanation quality beyond automated metrics; Quick check - evaluations should show reasonable inter-annotator agreement
- Domain adaptation: Why needed - to test transferability of explanation generation across hate types; Quick check - models should perform reasonably on unseen hate categories

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Input memes (text+image) -> Multimodal encoder -> Language model decoder -> Generated explanations

Critical Path: The core pipeline processes multimodal inputs through an encoder to create representations, then conditions a language model decoder to generate explanations. The human evaluation pipeline assesses generated explanations for fluency and relevance.

Design Tradeoffs: The approach trades off between explanation quality and generation complexity, using large PLMs that require substantial training data. The decision to focus on free-text explanations rather than structured formats provides flexibility but increases generation difficulty.

Failure Signatures: Common failure modes include generating explanations that are fluent but irrelevant, missing key contextual elements, or failing to capture the multimodal nature of the hate. Models may also struggle with novel hate types not well-represented in training data.

2. Test domain adaptation on misogynous memes to assess transferability
3. Analyze explanation quality differences between text-only, image-only, and multimodal input conditions

## Limitations
- Dataset size (2,895 memes) may limit generalizability for fine-tuning large language models
- Evaluation framework relies on subjective human judgment with limited metrics
- Domain adaptation results based on relatively small subset (331 misogynous memes)
- Quality and diversity of explanations may vary based on annotator backgrounds

## Confidence
- High confidence: Technical framework for dataset collection and baseline model training is sound
- Medium confidence: Characterization of task as challenging for PLMs based on moderate performance and human evaluation results
- Medium confidence: Domain adaptation potential given limited evaluation data
