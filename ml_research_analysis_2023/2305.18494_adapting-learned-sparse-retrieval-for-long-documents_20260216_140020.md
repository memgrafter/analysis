# Adapting Learned Sparse Retrieval for Long Documents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18494
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18494
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: ExactSDM adaptation of SDM outperforms other aggregation methods for LSR with long documents, while SoftSDM provides no additional benefit

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of adapting learned sparse retrieval (LSR) methods for long documents. LSR encodes queries and documents into sparse vectors aligned with a vocabulary, but these methods typically struggle with long documents due to position-agnostic scoring. The authors propose two adaptations of the Sequential Dependence Model (SDM) for use with LSR: ExactSDM and SoftSDM. ExactSDM assumes only exact query term dependence, while SoftSDM uses potential functions that model the dependence of query terms and their expansion terms. Experiments on the MSMARCO Document and TREC Robust04 datasets demonstrate that both ExactSDM and SoftSDM outperform existing LSR aggregation approaches for different document length constraints. Surprisingly, SoftSDM does not provide any performance benefits over ExactSDM, suggesting that soft proximity matching is not necessary for modeling term dependence in LSR.

## Method Summary
The paper adapts the Sequential Dependence Model (SDM) for learned sparse retrieval (LSR) by proposing two variants: ExactSDM and SoftSDM. Documents are split into up to 10 segments of 400 tokens each, and segment-level term scores are aggregated using various methods including max, sum, and mean pooling. The authors use a Splade-based LSR architecture with DistilBERT, fine-tuning three weighting factors of potential functions on MSMARCO Document training triplets. ExactSDM uses only self-translation logits (no expansion) for exact phrase and proximity matching, while SoftSDM uses potential functions that model the dependence of query terms and their expansion terms. The methods are evaluated on MRR@10 and NDCG@10 metrics across MSMARCO Document and TREC Robust04 datasets.

## Key Results
- Max score aggregation outperforms sum and mean aggregations because it captures local proximity between query terms and document segments
- ExactSDM provides competitive performance to SoftSDM without requiring soft matching
- Both ExactSDM and SoftSDM outperform existing LSR aggregation approaches for different document length constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Max score aggregation outperforms sum and mean aggregations because it inherently captures local proximity between query terms and document segments.
- Mechanism: By taking the maximum relevance score across document segments, Score-max naturally favors segments where query terms co-occur closely, preserving the proximity signal that sum/mean aggregations dilute.
- Core assumption: Local term proximity is a stronger relevance signal than global term frequency in long documents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "proximal scoring is crucial for LSR to handle long documents"
  - [section] "The approach of summing segment scores or representations, Rep(Score)-sum, suffers from the most severe decrease in performance on the MRR@10 and NDCG@10 measures."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no directly cited studies on proximity in LSR.
- Break condition: If query terms are highly distributed across the document without local clusters, max score may miss relevant segments that sum aggregation would capture.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ExactSDM outperforms SoftSDM because exact phrase and proximity matching is sufficient for modeling term dependence in LSR.
- Mechanism: ExactSDM uses only self-translation logits (no expansion), focusing on exact matches within local windows. This avoids noise from over-expansion while still capturing local dependencies.
- Core assumption: Soft matching via expansion terms does not add value for term dependence in LSR.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Surprisingly, SoftSDM does not provide any performance benefits over ExactSDM"
  - [section] "ExactSDM assumes only exact query term dependence, while SoftSDM uses potential functions that model the dependence of query terms and their expansion terms"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence from other studies confirming this claim.
- Break condition: If expansion terms consistently improve relevance matching in other contexts, ExactSDM may underperform.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LSR's term-position-agnostic scoring during dot product makes proximity aggregation necessary for long documents.
- Mechanism: LSR encodes queries and documents into sparse vectors without positional information. Aggregation strategies must therefore inject proximity signals, which max score and SDM variants do by focusing on local windows.
- Core assumption: Positional information is critical for inferring phrase semantics and proximity relevance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Splade uses term positional information when estimating query and document term weights, but is position-agnostic when scoring documents"
  - [section] "documents that contain phrases from the query (or query terms in close proximity) are more relevant than those with query terms scattered throughout"
  - [corpus] No corpus studies cited on positional effects in LSR.
- Break condition: If LSR encoding methods start preserving positional information in scoring, proximity aggregation may become unnecessary.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Query encoder (MLP or MLM head) → sparse query vector
  Document encoder (MLM head) → sparse document vector
  Segmentation module → document → segments
  Aggregation module → segment vectors/scores → document score
  SDM adaptation layer → injects proximity/phrase matching

- Critical path:
  Query → Query Encoder → Sparse Query Vector → Dot Product with Document Vector → Score → Rank
  Document → Segment → Encode Segments → Aggregate → Document Vector → Dot Product → Score

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Exact vs. Soft matching: Exact is simpler and more compatible but may miss relevant expansions.
  - Proximity window size: Larger windows capture more context but increase noise.
  - Aggregation method: Max preserves proximity but may ignore globally relevant segments.

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance drops as segment count increases → aggregation strategy issue.
  - Inconsistent results across datasets → over-fitting to dataset-specific patterns.
  - Slow inference → inefficient aggregation or encoding.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare max score vs. sum vs. mean aggregation on a small dataset to confirm proximity importance.
  2. Test ExactSDM vs. SoftSDM with varying window sizes to find optimal configuration.
  3. Evaluate compatibility of ExactSDM with non-MLM LSR methods (e.g., uniCOIL) to confirm generalizability.

## Limitations
- The finding that ExactSDM matches SoftSDM performance is based on only two datasets, limiting generalizability
- No ablation studies on the expansion terms' individual contributions in SoftSDM
- Limited scope of LSR architectures tested (only Splade-based methods)

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The empirical demonstration that max score aggregation outperforms sum and mean aggregations for LSR with long documents is well-supported by the experimental results across both datasets.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that ExactSDM provides competitive performance to SoftSDM without requiring soft matching is supported by the experimental results, but the lack of deeper analysis into why soft matching fails limits confidence in the broader implications.

**Low Confidence**: The broader conclusion that soft proximity matching is unnecessary for term dependence modeling in LSR extends beyond what the experimental evidence directly supports, particularly given the limited scope of LSR architectures and datasets tested.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on expansion terms**: Implement a controlled experiment comparing ExactSDM with variations that selectively enable different expansion components (unigram, bigram, ordered/unordered) to isolate which aspects of SoftSDM fail to provide benefits.

2. **Cross-architecture validation**: Test both ExactSDM and SoftSDM adaptations with at least two additional LSR methods (e.g., uniCOIL, docT5query) to determine whether the findings generalize beyond Splade-based systems.

3. **Window size sensitivity analysis**: Conduct experiments with varying proximity window sizes (beyond the fixed 10-token window used) to determine whether soft matching might provide benefits at different granularities that were not captured in the current evaluation.
