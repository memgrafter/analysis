# SkinCon: A skin disease dataset densely annotated by domain experts for fine-grained model debugging and analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00785
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00785
- Reference count: 13
- Dense annotation by dermatologists across 48 clinical concepts for skin disease images

## Executive Summary
SkinCon is a densely annotated skin disease dataset created by domain experts to enable fine-grained model debugging and analysis. The dataset contains 3,886 images annotated with 48 clinical concepts across two source datasets, providing critical infrastructure for interpretable dermatology AI. By providing dense clinical concept annotations, SkinCon enables researchers to move beyond black-box predictions to understand which specific clinical features drive model decisions.

## Method Summary
SkinCon combines images from the Fitzpatrick 17k skin disease dataset (3,230 images) and the Diverse Dermatology Images dataset (656 images), creating a total of 3,886 images densely annotated by dermatologists. The annotation process involved two dermatologists selecting 48 clinical concepts based on standard descriptor terms used to describe skin lesions. The dataset focuses on providing fine-grained clinical annotations that can be used across multiple disease processes, making it uniquely valuable for interpretable model development and error analysis in dermatology AI.

## Key Results
- First medical dataset densely annotated by domain experts with annotations useful across multiple disease processes
- 22 concepts have at least 50 images each, enabling statistically meaningful analysis
- Demonstrated use cases include debugging existing dermatology AI models and developing interpretable post-hoc concept bottleneck models
- Model performance can be recovered or exceeded using SkinCon concepts for interpretability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by providing granular clinical concept annotations that map directly to how dermatologists think about and describe skin diseases. By breaking down complex skin conditions into 48 distinct clinical features (color, texture, morphology, etc.), the dataset enables models to learn interpretable representations that align with clinical reasoning rather than just image-to-diagnosis mappings.

## Limitations
- Dataset size (3,886 total images) may be insufficient for training highly generalizable deep models by modern standards
- Only 22 concepts have ≥50 images each, limiting statistical power for some analyses
- Skin tone distribution quantification is not provided despite claims of diversity
