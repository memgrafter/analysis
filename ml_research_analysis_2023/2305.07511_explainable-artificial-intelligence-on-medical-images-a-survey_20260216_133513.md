# eXplainable Artificial Intelligence on Medical Images: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07511
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07511
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Survey of 32 recent studies applying XAI to medical imaging, highlighting techniques like LIME, SHAP, and Grad-CAM to improve model interpretability and clinical trust.

## Executive Summary
This survey comprehensively reviews the application of eXplainable AI (XAI) methods to medical imaging, addressing the critical challenge of transparency in deep learning models used for medical diagnosis. The review covers 32 recent studies that employ various XAI techniques, including LIME, SHAP, Grad-CAM, and Concept Activation Vectors, to interpret model predictions and identify important image regions for diagnosis. The survey highlights the widespread use of CNNs across diverse medical imaging tasks such as COVID-19 detection, skin lesion classification, and diabetic retinopathy diagnosis. Results demonstrate that XAI methods significantly improve interpretability, aiding clinicians in understanding model decisions and potentially increasing clinical trust and adoption.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes findings from 32 recent studies that apply XAI techniques to medical imaging tasks. Researchers reviewed papers implementing various XAI methods (LIME, SHAP, Grad-CAM, CAVs, etc.) with deep learning models, particularly CNNs, on medical image datasets. The studies span multiple imaging modalities and diagnostic tasks, evaluating model performance using standard metrics like accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, AUC, sensitivity, and specificity. XAI methods were applied post-hoc to trained models to generate interpretable explanations, with results analyzed in terms of both technical performance and clinical relevance.

## Key Results
- XAI methods like Grad-CAM and SHAP effectively highlight important image regions for diagnosis, improving model interpretability
- CNNs remain the dominant architecture, achieving high accuracy (e.g., 97.9% for OCT image classification) while maintaining explainability
- Clinical adoption is hindered by lack of standardized XAI evaluation metrics and limited availability of large, diverse medical image datasets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can standardized evaluation metrics for XAI methods in medical imaging be developed and validated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "A standard metric or procedure to evaluate the effectiveness of XAI methods does not exist."
- Why unresolved: The lack of standardized metrics hinders reproducibility and comparison of XAI methods across different medical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of a set of universal metrics that can be applied to evaluate XAI methods in various medical imaging tasks, with demonstrated effectiveness in improving model interpretability and clinical utility.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can large, diverse medical image databases be created to address the data scarcity issue in training deep learning models for medical imaging tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Since the field of medicine, to the best of our knowledge, does not contain a common, expansive and condensed database for such applications, every work has to spend valuable time gathering data."
- Why unresolved: The lack of large, diverse medical image databases limits the development and generalization of deep learning models for medical imaging tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Creation of a publicly available, large-scale, and diverse medical image database that includes a wide range of pathologies and imaging modalities, with demonstrated improvements in model performance and generalization.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the explainability of deep learning models in medical imaging be improved to increase trust and adoption by clinicians?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Giving such important decisions to machines that could not be held responsible would be comparable to absolutely avoiding human responsibility, representing an ethical issue and a fragility to malicious intents."
- Why unresolved: The "black box" nature of deep learning models in medical imaging raises concerns about accountability, trust, and potential misuse, hindering their adoption in clinical practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of XAI methods that provide clear, interpretable explanations for model predictions, with demonstrated improvements in clinician trust, decision-making, and patient outcomes.

## Limitations

- Lack of standardized evaluation metrics for XAI methods in medical imaging
- Limited availability of large, diverse medical image databases for training deep learning models
- Minimal empirical evidence on clinical adoption and real-world impact of XAI-enabled medical imaging models

## Confidence

This survey demonstrates **High confidence** in the core finding that XAI methods improve interpretability of medical imaging models, as evidenced by multiple studies showing clinicians' ability to verify model predictions through techniques like Grad-CAM and SHAP. The survey's **Medium confidence** in claims about XAI improving clinical adoption stems from limited empirical data on actual clinical integration, with most studies focusing on technical validation rather than real-world implementation. **Low confidence** exists around claims of XAI outperforming black-box models, as the corpus evidence shows minimal citations and limited comparative studies.
