# A Survey of Diffusion Models in Natural Language Processing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14671
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14671
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Diffusion models show promise for NLP but require adaptation for discrete text data, with two main approaches: discrete diffusion models and embedding diffusion models.

## Executive Summary
Diffusion models, which have achieved remarkable success in image generation, are being adapted for natural language processing tasks. The fundamental challenge lies in the discrete nature of text data, which differs significantly from the continuous data these models were originally designed for. The survey identifies two main approaches: discrete diffusion models that operate directly on categorical tokens, and embedding diffusion models that convert text to continuous embeddings before applying diffusion. These models offer several advantages over autoregressive approaches, including parallel generation capabilities and better token-level control, though they face challenges in training complexity and interpretability.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes research on diffusion models for NLP, categorizing approaches into discrete diffusion models (operating directly on categorical tokens using Markov transition matrices) and embedding diffusion models (converting text to continuous embeddings before applying diffusion). Both approaches follow a similar framework: a forward process that corrupts the input through noise, and a reverse process that learns to denoise iteratively. Discrete models use techniques like multinomial diffusion and edit-based reconstruction, while embedding models leverage continuous-space techniques including classifier-based guidance and self-conditioning. The survey covers applications in text generation, machine translation, controllable generation, summarization, and dialogue generation, evaluating models using metrics like BLEU, ROUGE, perplexity, and FID.

## Key Results
- Diffusion models enable parallel text generation, unlike sequential autoregressive models
- Two distinct approaches exist: discrete diffusion (token-level) and embedding diffusion (continuous-space)
- Embedding models can leverage classifier guidance for improved sample quality
- Diffusion models show greater robustness to input corruption compared to autoregressive models
- Training diffusion models is more complex and less interpretable than autoregressive alternatives

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Discrete diffusion models operate directly on token-level categorical distributions while embedding diffusion models lift text into continuous space first.
- Mechanism: Discrete models use Markov transition matrices to corrupt tokens, whereas embedding models corrupt continuous embeddings and round back to tokens during sampling.
- Core assumption: Token-level corruption is equivalent to continuous-space corruption after an embedding step.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Discrete diffusion models that work directly on categorical tokens, and embedding diffusion models that first convert text to continuous embeddings"
  - [section] "Discrete diffusion models operate with discrete variables, such as text or categorical data"
  - [corpus] Weak - survey cites specific architectures but not empirical comparison of equivalence
- Break condition: If the embedding rounding step introduces significant information loss, the equivalence breaks.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Diffusion models can generate text in parallel, unlike autoregressive models which generate tokens sequentially.
- Mechanism: At each denoising step, the model predicts the entire sequence simultaneously rather than conditioning on previous tokens.
- Core assumption: Parallel prediction at each timestep preserves sequence coherence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Compared to AR models, diffusion models have significant advantages for parallel generation"
  - [section] "diffusion models adopt a parallel generation approach, enabling simultaneous generation of all output tokens"
  - [corpus] Weak - no quantitative evidence of speedup or quality comparison provided
- Break condition: If parallel generation leads to severe coherence issues, the advantage disappears.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Embedding diffusion models can leverage classifier-based guidance for better sample quality.
- Mechanism: Continuous embeddings can be conditioned on classifier outputs, which discrete tokens cannot directly access.
- Core assumption: Classifier guidance works equally well in continuous and discrete spaces.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "embedding diffusion models can integrate classifier-based guidance, enhancing the quality of generated samples"
  - [section] "embedding diffusion models can integrate classifier-based guidance, enhancing the quality of generated samples by leveraging additional information from a classifier"
  - [corpus] Moderate - survey mentions this capability but provides no direct evidence
- Break condition: If classifier guidance fails to improve quality in continuous space, the advantage vanishes.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text -> Forward Diffusion -> Denoising Network -> Output Text (discrete models) OR Text -> Embedding -> Forward Diffusion -> Denoising Network -> Rounding -> Output Text (embedding models)

- Critical path:
  1. Encode text to embeddings (embedding models only)
  2. Apply forward diffusion to corrupt sequence
  3. Iteratively denoise using neural network
  4. Round embeddings to discrete tokens (embedding models only)
  5. Output final sequence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Discrete models: Simpler token handling, but cannot leverage continuous-space techniques
  - Embedding models: Can use classifier guidance and continuous-space refinements, but require embedding/rounding steps
  - Sampling speed vs quality: More denoising steps generally improve quality but slow generation

- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse: Model generates very similar outputs
  - Rounding errors: Embedding models produce invalid tokens
  - Slow convergence: Training loss plateaus early

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic forward diffusion on a small text dataset
  2. Train a simple denoising network and compare discrete vs embedding approaches
  3. Add classifier guidance to embedding model and measure quality improvements
