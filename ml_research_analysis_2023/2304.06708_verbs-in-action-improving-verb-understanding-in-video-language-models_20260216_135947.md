# Verbs in Action: Improving verb understanding in video-language models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06708
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06708
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: State-of-the-art zero-shot performance on verb-focused downstream tasks while maintaining strong noun-focused performance

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical limitation in video-language models where verb understanding lags behind noun understanding. Current models struggle to distinguish between similar actions (e.g., "brushing hair" vs. "curling hair"), often relying heavily on nouns for video-text matching. The authors propose the Verb-Focused Contrastive (VFC) framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate hard negative captions and extract verb phrases, significantly improving verb reasoning without sacrificing general language understanding.

## Method Summary
The Verb-Focused Contrastive (VFC) framework uses LLMs in two key ways: (1) generating hard negative captions where only the verb changes while keeping context constant, and (2) extracting verb phrases to enforce fine-grained alignment losses. A calibration strategy balances verb occurrences between positive and negative pairs to prevent feature space distortions. The approach is evaluated on three verb-focused downstream tasks—video-text matching, video question-answering, and video classification—achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot performance, particularly on tasks requiring nuanced verb understanding.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on verb-focused downstream tasks
- Significantly outperforms baselines on VerbH benchmark for distinguishing similar actions
- Maintains strong performance on noun-focused benchmarks while improving verb reasoning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Calibrated hard negative training with LLM-generated verb-focused negatives improves verb understanding by creating a more balanced contrastive learning objective. The calibration strategy filters generations to have equal concept occurrences in positives and negatives, preventing feature space distortions. Break condition: If Gω ≈ Sω is not achieved, the model will over-attract or over-repel certain verb concepts, degrading performance.

### Mechanism 2
Verb phrase alignment loss isolates verb understanding by contrasting videos against isolated verb phrases rather than full captions. This forces the model to focus on action understanding rather than general language understanding. Break condition: If verb phrase extraction is inaccurate or too coarse, the model may not receive proper verb-focused supervision.

### Mechanism 3
LLM-generated hard negatives are more effective than random or antonym-based negatives because they maintain semantic and linguistic plausibility while changing only the verb. Break condition: If LLM generations become too creative and change more than just the verb, they may become easier negatives that don't provide the intended contrastive learning signal.

  - Why needed here: LLMs are used both for generating hard negative captions and extracting verb phrases
  - Quick check question: How does the prompt template affect the quality of generated hard negatives?

- **Concept**: Feature space calibration and concept balance
  - Why needed here: The calibration mechanism relies on understanding how concept frequencies affect feature space geometry
  - Quick check question: What happens to the feature space when certain concepts appear only in negative pairs?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Video encoder (CLIP ViT-B/32 + temporal transformer blocks) -> Text encoder (CLIP text encoder) -> Similarity calculator (mean pooling + cosine similarity) -> VFC additions (LLM module for hard negative generation and verb phrase extraction)

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Generate hard negatives and extract verb phrases using LLM
  2. Apply calibration to balance concept frequencies
  3. Train with three loss components: standard contrastive, calibrated hard negative, and verb phrase loss

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using LLMs adds computational overhead but provides superior hard negatives compared to rule-based methods; calibration reduces usable hard negatives but prevents feature space distortions; verb phrase loss requires additional LLM calls but provides more focused supervision

- **Failure signatures**: Performance drops on Kinetics without calibration indicate concept imbalance; poor verb phrase extraction results in degraded performance on verb-focused tasks; LLM generation failures manifest as linguistically incorrect captions

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement basic VFC with PaLM hard negative generation (no calibration) and measure performance on VerbH
  2. Add calibration mechanism and verify it prevents Kinetics performance drop
  3. Implement verb phrase loss and measure impact on both verb-focused and noun-focused benchmarks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
What is the optimal number of hard negative captions to sample per video during training to maximize verb understanding without overfitting? The paper only tests up to N_hard=5 and does not explore the impact of larger numbers or potential diminishing returns.

### Open Question 2
How do different LLM architectures (e.g., GPT-3, LLaMA, PaLM) compare in their ability to generate effective hard negative captions for verb-focused contrastive learning? The paper only uses two LLM architectures and does not provide a comprehensive comparison.

### Open Question 3
Can the verb phrase loss be further improved by incorporating semantic similarity or dependency parsing to ensure more accurate and contextually relevant verb phrase extraction? The paper relies on PaLM's ability to extract verb phrases but does not investigate whether additional linguistic analysis could improve the quality.

## Limitations
- Reliance on LLM-generated data introduces potential biases based on specific model and prompt engineering choices
- Calibration mechanism validated primarily through performance differences rather than direct feature space analysis
- VerbH benchmark has limited size (1,000 videos) and may not fully capture real-world verb understanding complexity

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Overall performance improvements on downstream benchmarks (Kinetics-400, MSR-VTT, NEXT-QA) are well-documented and reproducible
- **Medium confidence**: Calibration mechanism's effectiveness is supported by ablation studies, but underlying feature space analysis could be more thorough
- **Medium confidence**: Superiority of LLM-generated hard negatives over baseline methods is demonstrated, but evaluation of alternative generation strategies is limited

## Next Checks
1. Conduct t-SNE or UMAP visualization of video embeddings to empirically verify that calibration prevents feature space distortions and creates balanced concept distributions
2. Evaluate VFC on additional verb-focused datasets beyond VerbH to assess robustness and generalization of verb understanding improvements
3. Systematically compare performance across different LLM providers (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) and prompt engineering strategies to isolate the contribution of the LLM component
