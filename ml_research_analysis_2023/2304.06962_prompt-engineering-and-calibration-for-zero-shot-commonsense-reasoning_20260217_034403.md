# Prompt Engineering and Calibration for Zero-Shot Commonsense Reasoning
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06962
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06962
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: Prompt engineering and calibration do not universally benefit smaller language models in zero-shot commonsense reasoning tasks.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether prompt engineering and calibration strategies can improve the performance of smaller language models on zero-shot multiple-choice commonsense reasoning tasks. The research evaluates these techniques across five benchmarks using models including GPT-2, T5, and Flan-T5. The findings reveal that while larger Flan-T5 models benefit from prompt engineering, smaller models do not show consistent improvements. Calibration is effective for GPT-2 but produces inconsistent results for other models, and combining both strategies often leads to negative effects.

## Method Summary
The research team conducted experiments on five different commonsense reasoning benchmarks using multiple language models of varying sizes. They applied prompt engineering techniques to optimize the input format and calibration methods to adjust model outputs. Performance was measured across different model families including GPT-2, T5, and Flan-T5 to assess the generalizability of these techniques. The study focused on zero-shot settings where models had no task-specific fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- Prompt engineering improves performance for larger Flan-T5 models but not for smaller ones
- Calibration is effective for GPT-2 but shows inconsistent results across other model types
- Combined application of prompt engineering and calibration mostly produces negative effects
