# Hotelling Deflation on Large Symmetric Spiked Tensors

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.10248
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10248
- **Reference count**: 17
- **Key outcome**: Asymptotic analysis of Hotelling deflation on symmetric spiked tensors with Gaussian noise, deriving limiting equations for estimated components' alignment and weights.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the Hotelling deflation algorithm for estimating low-rank components of a symmetric spiked tensor corrupted by Gaussian noise in the large-dimensional limit. The authors characterize the asymptotic behavior of successive rank-one approximations by deriving a system of equations governing the alignment between estimated and true spike components, as well as their estimated weights. The analysis leverages random matrix theory applied to partial tensor contractions, providing theoretical predictions validated through numerical experiments. The results offer insights into the performance and limitations of deflation-based tensor decomposition methods in high-dimensional settings.

## Method Summary
The paper studies the symmetric spiked tensor model where a rank-one tensor is corrupted by additive Gaussian noise. The Hotelling deflation algorithm iteratively computes rank-one approximations via tensor unfolding and eigenvalue decomposition, then subtracts the estimated component from the tensor. The authors analyze this process in the asymptotic regime where tensor dimensions grow large with fixed ratios. They derive a system of equations describing the limiting alignment between estimated and true components by examining the spectral properties of random matrices formed from partial tensor contractions. The analysis uses tools from random matrix theory, including concentration inequalities and deterministic equivalents, to characterize the limiting behavior of the deflation iterates.

## Key Results
- Derived a system of equations characterizing the asymptotic alignment between estimated and true spike components in the deflation algorithm
- Established deterministic equivalents for the limiting values of estimated weights through random matrix analysis
- Validated theoretical predictions with numerical experiments showing close agreement between finite-dimensional performance and asymptotic predictions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Hotelling deflation algorithm works by iteratively extracting the dominant rank-one component of the residual tensor after subtracting previously estimated components. In the large-dimensional regime with Gaussian noise, the partial contractions of the tensor converge to deterministic equivalents described by random matrix theory. This allows the authors to track how each deflation step's output aligns with the true spike components through a recursive system of equations. The key insight is that the noise structure and tensor symmetry enable exact characterization of the spectral properties governing the estimation error at each iteration.

- **Gaussian noise assumptions in spiked models**: Critical for deriving closed-form expressions for limiting behavior; quick check: test sensitivity to non-Gaussian noise distributions.

## Limitations
- Analysis restricted to symmetric tensors with additive Gaussian noise, limiting applicability to asymmetric or structured noise scenarios
- Asymptotic regime assumes dimensions grow large with fixed ratios, which may not accurately reflect finite-dimensional performance in all cases
- Relies heavily on random matrix theory tools and specific distributional assumptions, potentially limiting generalizability to non-Gaussian settings

## Confidence
- Asymptotic predictions for Gaussian spiked tensor model: High
- Applicability to finite-dimensional practical scenarios: Medium
- Robustness to non-Gaussian noise or asymmetric tensors: Low
