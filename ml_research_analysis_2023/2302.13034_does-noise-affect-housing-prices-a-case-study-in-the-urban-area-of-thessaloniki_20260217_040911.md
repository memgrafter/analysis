# Does Noise Affect Housing Prices? A Case Study in the Urban Area of Thessaloniki

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13034
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13034
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Noise significantly influences residential property prices in Thessaloniki, with positive effects in city center and negative effects in suburbs

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the impact of urban noise pollution on residential property prices in Thessaloniki, Greece. Since official noise pollution data were not publicly available, the authors reconstructed a comprehensive noise dataset using georeferencing and color-to-noise mapping techniques on published heatmaps from the Hellenic Ministry of Environment and Energy. They combined this reconstructed noise data with property listings from the Openhouse platform and trained XGBoost and LightGBM models to evaluate noise's effect on housing prices. The study found that noise significantly influences property values, with a positive price effect in the city center and a negative effect in suburban areas. Incorporating noise features improved model prediction accuracy by up to 5% in some areas, demonstrating the importance of environmental factors in real estate pricing models.

## Method Summary
The authors reconstructed noise pollution data by georeferencing published heatmaps using QGIS and assigning noise values to pixels based on RGB color matching with predefined decibel ranges. They used the CIEDE2000 color difference metric to improve accuracy for transitioning pixels. The dataset was then tessellated into 10-meter tiles by averaging noise values within each tile. This noise data was combined with 2,014 residential property listings from Openhouse for Thessaloniki. XGBoost and LightGBM models were trained on three distinct areas of the city using noise values averaged within 50m and 100m radii around each property. Model interpretability techniques including feature importance, partial dependence plots, permutation importance, LIME, and SHAP were used to analyze noise's impact on price predictions.

## Key Results
- Noise significantly influences residential property prices in Thessaloniki
- XGBoost models with noise features improved prediction accuracy by up to 5% in some areas
- Noise has a positive price effect in the city center and a negative effect in suburban areas
- LightGBM models with noise features performed better than XGBoost models in certain areas

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Noise data reconstruction using georeferencing and color-to-noise mapping accurately approximates the original noise levels.
- Mechanism: The authors align published heatmaps with geographic coordinates using QGIS, then assign noise values to each pixel by matching its RGB color to predefined decibel ranges. Transitioning pixels are assigned values using the CIEDE2000 color difference metric to reduce perceptual error.
- Core assumption: The original heatmap color-to-noise mapping is accurate and the spatial alignment preserves true geographic relationships.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "reconstruct and make publicly available a general purpose noise pollution dataset based on published studies", [section] "The aforementioned studies did not make public the underlying data that were used to create the provided heatmaps"
- Break condition: Misalignment in georeferencing or inaccurate CIEDE2000 threshold would introduce systematic noise value errors.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Aggregating noise values via tessellation into 10-meter tiles creates a manageable dataset without losing critical spatial detail for price modeling.
- Mechanism: After pixel-by-pixel noise assignment, coordinates are rounded to four decimal places (10-meter resolution). Noise values are averaged within each tile, reducing data volume while retaining sufficient geographic granularity.
- Core assumption: A 10-meter resolution is adequate to capture noise variations that affect housing prices in urban areas.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "To minimize the density of information to more practical levels, we utilize tessellation... We chose to tessellate the map by keeping only the four decimal points of the coordinates"
- Break condition: If price sensitivity to noise exists at finer scales than 10 meters, aggregation would smooth out important variations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: XGBoost models trained with noise features improve housing price prediction accuracy more in areas where noise is a stronger differentiator of property value.
- Mechanism: Noise values within a radius around each property are averaged and used as features. XGBoost captures non-linear relationships between noise and price, with interpretability methods revealing area-specific effects (positive in city center, negative in suburbs).
- Core assumption: The relationship between noise and price is non-linear and varies systematically by urban area characteristics.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "XGBoost models incorporating noise features improved prediction accuracy by up to 5% in some areas", [section] "The experiments showed that noise significantly influences property prices, with a positive effect in the city center and a negative effect in suburban areas"
- Break condition: If noise-radius averaging is too coarse or noise-price relationship is actually linear, model improvements may not materialize.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data reconstruction pipeline (heatmap → georeferenced coordinates → pixel noise assignment → tessellation) → Property dataset integration → XGBoost/LGBM training → Interpretability analysis
- Critical path: Accurate georeferencing → Correct color-to-noise mapping → Proper tessellation → Effective noise-radius averaging → Model training with noise features
- Design tradeoffs: Higher tessellation resolution preserves detail but increases computational cost; larger noise-radius averaging smooths noise but may dilute local effects
- Failure signatures: Systematic prediction bias in specific areas, high variance in noise feature importance, or poor model performance when noise features are included

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the influence of noise on housing prices vary across different noise frequency ranges or decibel thresholds?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how noise affects prices differently in city centers versus suburban areas, but does not analyze specific decibel thresholds or frequency ranges in detail.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on overall noise levels and general trends but lacks granular analysis of how different noise intensities or frequency components impact pricing.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of price changes across specific decibel ranges (e.g., 50-55dB vs 65-70dB) and frequency bands, with corresponding price data for each threshold.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do non-traffic noise sources (aviation, industrial, nightlife) differentially impact housing prices compared to traffic noise?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions aviation noise in Kalamaria specifically and notes it has different effects than road noise, but doesn't compare multiple noise source types systematically.
- Why unresolved: The study primarily focuses on aggregated noise measurements without separating different noise source types to understand their individual impacts on prices.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of housing prices relative to proximity to airports, industrial zones, nightlife districts versus main roads, controlling for other variables.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the temporal variability of noise (daily patterns, seasonal changes) affect its impact on housing prices?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses average day and night noise values but doesn't examine how fluctuations throughout the day or seasonal variations might affect price sensitivity.
- Why unresolved: The study treats noise as a static variable using averaged values rather than exploring how temporal patterns might influence buyer preferences and price premiums.
- What evidence would resolve it: Time-series analysis of noise patterns and corresponding price data across different times of day and seasons, with regression models incorporating temporal noise variability.

## Limitations

- Area segmentation into three distinct zones may affect model performance and observed price effects
- Results based on a single city and specific time period may not generalize to other urban contexts
