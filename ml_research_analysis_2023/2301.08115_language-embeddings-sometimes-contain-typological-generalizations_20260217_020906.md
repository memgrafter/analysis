# Language Embeddings Sometimes Contain Typological Generalizations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08115
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08115
- Reference count: 13
- Most models do not appear to have made linguistically meaningful generalizations, though some evidence exists for word order and morphology features

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether neural network models trained on multilingual data capture linguistically meaningful generalizations about language structure. The authors evaluate language representations from various neural models trained on 1295 languages using classifiers to predict typological features. Their findings suggest that most models do not encode typological generalizations as clearly as previous work claimed, though some models show evidence of capturing word order and morphological features. The study introduces a rigorous evaluation framework using constrained cross-validation to control for genealogical and areal correlations between languages.

## Method Summary
The authors train neural models on massively multilingual Bible translations and evaluate whether the resulting language representations encode typological features from linguistic databases. They use a constrained leave-one-out cross-validation scheme that excludes related, geographically close, or potentially contact languages from training folds. Language representations are extracted from various models (word-based and character-based language models, reinflection models, machine translation models) and evaluated using logistic regression classifiers to predict typological features. The evaluation framework controls for spurious correlations by shuffling labels and comparing against baseline representations.

## Key Results
- Most neural models tested do not clearly encode linguistically meaningful typological generalizations
- Some evidence found for encoding typological features related to word order and morphology
- Results are less clear than suggested by previous work, with more rigorous evaluation methodology
- Constrained cross-validation is crucial to avoid spurious results from genealogical and areal correlations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Language embeddings sometimes contain typological generalizations because the models are exposed to parallel text across many languages, and linguistic structures are shared across translations.
- Mechanism: The neural models trained on massively multilingual Bible translations learn language representations that encode typological features through the shared syntactic and morphological structures present in the parallel text.
- Core assumption: Parallel text preserves linguistic structure across languages, allowing neural models to extract typological generalizations.
- Evidence anchors:

  - [section]: "Thus, for data-driven NLP methods at a scale of more than about a hundred languages one has to rely on different types of data. Although the literature on multilingual NLP is vast, two types of data are generally used for low-resource languages: (a limited selection of) parallel texts, and information on language structure encoded in databases on linguistic typology."

Top related titles: Entropy2Vec: Crosslingual Language Modeling Entropy as End-to-End Learnable Language Representations, Grammatical information in BERT sentence embeddings as two-dimensional arrays, Less is More: The Effectiveness of Compact Typological Language Representations.
- Break condition: If the parallel text does not preserve linguistic structure (e.g., if translations are very free or use different syntactic constructions), the model may not learn typological generalizations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Language embeddings encode typological generalizations because the evaluation framework uses linguistically sound cross-validation that controls for genealogical and areal correlations.
- Mechanism: The evaluation framework uses constrained leave-one-out cross-validation, ensuring that languages in the test fold are not related to, geographically close to, or in a potential contact situation with any of the languages in the training fold.
- Core assumption: Controlling for genealogical and areal correlations is necessary to avoid spurious results due to language similarity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "To avoid general language similarity from affecting the results, we use a cross-validation scheme that ensures languages in the test fold are not related to, geographically close to, or in a potential contact situation with any of the languages in the training fold."
  - [section]: "The basis of our evaluation framework consists of typological feature classification, using constrained leave-one-out cross-validation."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence for typological generalizations in language embeddings.
- Break condition: If the cross-validation scheme is not strict enough, or if genealogical and areal correlations are not properly controlled, the evaluation may yield spurious results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Language embeddings encode typological generalizations because the models are trained on a diverse set of tasks that require analysis at different levels of language.
- Mechanism: The models are trained on tasks such as word-level language modeling, character-based language modeling, morphological reinflection, and machine translation, which require the models to learn different aspects of language structure.
- Core assumption: Different tasks require different levels of linguistic analysis, and the language representations learned by the models will reflect this.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Since different tasks require analysis at different levels of language, and given the results of Bjerva and Augenstein (2018b), we expect that the language embedding spaces will mainly capture properties relevant to the task at hand."
  - [section]: "We use the following models, which generate the language representations whose labels are in bold: Word-based language model with multilingual word embeddings (WordLM), Character-based language model (CharLM), Morphological reinflection of noun paradigms (Reinflect-Noun) or verb paradigms (Reinflect-Verb), Word form encoder from characters of a word form to the multilingual word embedding space (Encoder), Neural machine translation models: many-to-English (NMTx2eng) and English-to-many (NMTeng2x), Baseline representations from pairwise lexical similarity (Lexical and ASJP)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence for typological generalizations in language embeddings.
- Break condition: If the tasks are not diverse enough, or if they do not require analysis at different levels of language, the language representations may not encode typological generalizations.

## Limitations
- The study relies on Bible translations as primary data source, which may not be representative of general language use
- Evaluation depends on quality and coverage of typological databases which may have incomplete or inconsistent annotations
- Constrained cross-validation may be overly strict and potentially mask real signal
- Analysis focuses primarily on word order and morphological features, potentially missing other typological dimensions

## Confidence
- Claims about most models not containing linguistically meaningful generalizations: Medium
- Claims about some evidence for word order and morphological features: Low-Medium
- Claims that results are "less clear than suggested by previous work": Medium

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the analysis with a more diverse corpus beyond Bible translations, such as news articles or Wikipedia data, to test whether the findings generalize across different text genres and domains.
2. Conduct ablation studies on the cross-validation constraints by systematically relaxing the genealogical, geographic, and contact constraints to determine how sensitive the results are to these restrictions and whether some signal is being excluded unnecessarily.
3. Extend the typological feature set beyond word order and morphology to include phonological, lexical, and syntactic features to determine whether certain typological dimensions are more readily captured by language embeddings than others.
