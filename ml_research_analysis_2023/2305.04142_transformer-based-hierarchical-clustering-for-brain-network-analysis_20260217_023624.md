# Transformer-Based Hierarchical Clustering for Brain Network Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04142
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04142
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a novel end-to-end approach that combines transformer encoders with hierarchical clustering layers to learn globally shared cluster assignments for brain network analysis, achieving up to 3.2% higher AUROC compared to state-of-the-art transformer and GNN models.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of identifying functional brain network communities within brain networks for improved pathological prediction and analysis. The proposed method, Transformer-based Hierarchical Clustering (THC), introduces a novel end-to-end approach that combines transformer encoders with hierarchical clustering layers to learn globally shared cluster assignments. By integrating stochastic noise into the attention mechanism and incorporating readout layers at each clustering level, THC enhances both model performance and interpretability. Experimental results on two real-world datasets (ABCD and ABIDE) demonstrate that THC achieves up to 3.2% and 1.7% higher AUROC compared to state-of-the-art transformer and GNN models. Additionally, the clustering assignments produced by THC align well with ground-truth functional module labels, with purity scores reaching 88.9%, surpassing existing clustering methods.

## Method Summary
The Transformer-based Hierarchical Clustering (THC) method processes brain networks represented as weighted adjacency matrices. It uses multiple Brain Clustering (BCluster) layers, each containing a Stochastic Transformer (StoTrans) encoder that computes attention scores and node embeddings. A globally shared clustering assignment matrix (Ai) is learned and optimized jointly with the model through backpropagation. The method introduces stochastic noise into the attention mechanism to prevent trivial clustering solutions and includes readout layers at each clustering level for hierarchical predictions. The model is trained in two stages: first learning the clustering assignments, then jointly optimizing all parameters with sparsity and entropy regularization to encourage meaningful cluster structures.

## Key Results
- THC achieves up to 3.2% and 1.7% higher AUROC compared to state-of-the-art transformer and GNN models on ABCD and ABIDE datasets respectively
- Clustering assignments produced by THC align well with ground-truth functional module labels, with purity scores reaching 88.9%
- The method reduces computational complexity while providing plausible insights into the functional organization of brain regions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Stochastic noise in the attention matrix helps the model avoid trivial clustering solutions where the learned clusters simply replicate the raw attention scores.
- Mechanism: By adding a random perturbation drawn uniformly from (0,1) to each element of the attention matrix and taking the logarithm of the ratio with ones, the model is forced to learn a clustering assignment that is robust to small variations in the attention scores. This encourages the model to discover more meaningful cluster structures rather than defaulting to the identity mapping.
- Core assumption: The raw attention scores alone are insufficient to capture the desired hierarchical clustering structure, and a degree of stochasticity is necessary to push the model toward learning more abstract and generalizable cluster assignments.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] mentions "stochastic noise" as one of the three main contributions of the THC model.
  - [section 3] describes the attention mechanism redesign with stochastic noise: "S(i,m)′ = S(i,m) + log( Bi,m / (I - Bi,m) )".
  - [corpus] has no direct mention of stochastic noise in related works, indicating this is a novel contribution.
- Break condition: If the stochastic noise term is removed and the model's clustering performance degrades or collapses to trivial solutions, this would invalidate the claim.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Hierarchical clustering layers enable the model to learn progressively more abstract representations of brain network communities by grouping lower-level clusters into higher-level ones.
- Mechanism: Each BCluster layer takes the adjacency matrix from the previous layer, applies a transformer encoder to learn attention scores and node embeddings, then uses a globally shared clustering assignment (Ai) to aggregate nodes into clusters. The resulting cluster embeddings become the input to the next layer, allowing the model to build a hierarchy of cluster representations. Readout layers at each level enable the model to utilize information from all clustering levels.
- Core assumption: Brain networks exhibit a natural hierarchical structure where functional modules are composed of sub-modules, and this hierarchy can be effectively captured by progressively aggregating nodes into clusters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states that the method "provides plausible insights into the functional organization of brain regions" through hierarchical clustering.
  - [section 3] describes the hierarchical structure: "The model further groups the cluster embeddings from the previous layer into new, larger clusters, generating a hierarchical structure."
  - [corpus] mentions related works like DiffPool and HoscPool that use hierarchical pooling/clustering, suggesting this is a recognized approach for graph data.
- Break condition: If the model's performance significantly drops when using only a single clustering layer (no hierarchy), this would challenge the importance of the hierarchical structure.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The globally shared clustering assignment, which is learned jointly with the model and optimized through gradient descent, allows the model to adapt the clustering structure to the specific classification task at hand.
- Mechanism: Unlike traditional clustering methods that assign nodes to clusters based on pre-defined similarity metrics, the BCluster layer learns a soft clustering assignment matrix (Ai) that is shared across all samples in a batch. This assignment is optimized jointly with the rest of the model parameters through backpropagation, allowing the clustering structure to be tailored to the downstream classification task. The soft assignment also enables differentiable clustering, which is necessary for end-to-end training.
- Core assumption: The optimal clustering structure for a given brain network classification task may differ from generic community detection methods, and learning the clustering in an end-to-end manner allows the model to discover task-specific cluster structures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] highlights "joint hierarchical cluster identification and brain network classification" as the main goal of the model.
  - [section 3] explains the learning process: "Each assignment Ai ∈ (Ci, Ci+1) reduces Xi ∈ (Ci, Ci) into Xi+1 ∈ (Ci+1, Ci+1)" and "The Assignment is Globally Shared...optimized jointly with the model."
  - [corpus] mentions existing clustering methods like Louvain and Lloyd's algorithm, but notes they "fail to capture higher-order connectivity patterns," suggesting the need for a more adaptive approach.
- Break condition: If the model's performance does not improve when using the learned clustering assignments compared to using fixed, pre-computed clusterings, this would question the value of the joint learning approach.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Weighted adjacency matrix X (brain network)
  - Stochastic Transformer (StoTrans) encoder: Learns attention matrix S(i,m) and node embeddings Xi'
  - Brain Clustering (BCluster) layer: Computes globally shared clustering assignment Ai and reduces Xi to Xi+1
  - Readout layers: Attached to each BCluster layer to produce predictions yi
  - Output: Final prediction y (average of yi) and hierarchical cluster assignments (A1, ..., Ak)

- Critical path:
  1. Input X is fed into the first BCluster layer
  2. StoTrans encoder computes attention matrix S(i,m) and node embeddings Xi'
  3. Clustering assignment Ai is computed from S(i,m)
  4. Xi+1 is computed by aggregating Xi' using Ai
  5. Steps 2-4 are repeated for each layer in the hierarchy
  6. Readout layers produce predictions at each level
  7. Final prediction is computed as the average of all yi

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of clustering layers (k) vs. model complexity and interpretability
  - Cluster sizes (Ci) at each layer vs. granularity of the learned representations
  - Amount of stochastic noise added to attention vs. stability of the learned clusters
  - Use of readout layers at each level vs. computational cost and potential overfitting

- Failure signatures:
  - If the learned clustering assignments are random or do not align with ground-truth functional modules, the model may be failing to capture meaningful community structure.
  - If the model's performance is significantly worse than baselines that do not use clustering, the hierarchical clustering approach may not be beneficial for the given task.
  - If the model is very sensitive to the choice of hyperparameters (k, Ci, noise level), it may indicate overfitting or instability in the learning process.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare the model's performance with and without the hierarchical clustering layers to isolate the effect of the clustering on the classification accuracy.
  2. Vary the number of clustering layers (k) and the cluster sizes (Ci) to find the optimal configuration for the given datasets.
  3. Visualize the learned cluster assignments and compare them to ground-truth functional module labels to qualitatively assess the interpretability of the model's clustering.

## Limitations
- The stochastic noise mechanism's impact is theoretically sound but requires ablation studies to confirm its necessity for avoiding trivial clustering solutions.
- The claim that hierarchical clustering provides interpretability needs more validation - while purity scores are high (88.9%), the connection between these clusters and actual neurological understanding of brain function remains largely qualitative.
- The computational complexity reduction claim (O(n²) to O(n·k·c)) is derived from theoretical analysis but would benefit from empirical runtime measurements across different dataset sizes.

## Confidence

**Major uncertainties:**
- The stochastic noise mechanism's impact is theoretically sound but requires ablation studies to confirm its necessity for avoiding trivial clustering solutions. The current results show improved performance but don't definitively prove the noise prevents identity mapping collapse.
- The claim that hierarchical clustering provides interpretability needs more validation - while purity scores are high (88.9%), the connection between these clusters and actual neurological understanding of brain function remains largely qualitative.
- The computational complexity reduction claim (O(n²) to O(n·k·c)) is derived from theoretical analysis but would benefit from empirical runtime measurements across different dataset sizes.

**Confidence labels:**
- **High confidence**: The overall classification performance improvements (3.2% and 1.7% AUROC gains) are well-supported by experimental results across two independent datasets.
- **Medium confidence**: The interpretability claims are supported by quantitative metrics (purity, NMI) but the neurological meaningfulness of the clusters requires domain expert validation.
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism explanations for stochastic noise and hierarchical clustering are logically sound based on the architecture description, but direct ablation studies are needed for definitive proof.
