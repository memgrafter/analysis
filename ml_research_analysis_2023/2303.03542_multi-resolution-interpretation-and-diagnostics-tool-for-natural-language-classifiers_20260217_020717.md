# Multi-resolution Interpretation and Diagnostics Tool for Natural Language Classifiers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03542
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03542
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: Multi-resolution aggregation of local explainability reveals segment-specific model behaviors and biases in NLP classification

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a multi-resolution interpretation framework that improves understanding of NLP classifier decisions by aggregating local explainability scores at segment and cluster levels. The approach addresses the high dimensionality and binary nature of NLP inputs by grouping observations based on topic, message length, OOV proportion, POS, sentiment, or semantic embeddings. Applied to a balanced Yelp review dataset, the method reveals distinct patterns in model behavior across restaurant, hotel, and beauty segments, identifies gender bias through embedding similarity analysis, and uncovers root causes of False Positive and False Negative errors. The framework demonstrates that segment-level aggregation provides more nuanced and actionable interpretability insights than purely global or local explanations.

## Method Summary
The method trains a CNN model on balanced Yelp reviews (complaint vs non-complaint) across three topics, then uses Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) to generate local explainability scores for each token. These local scores are aggregated by segments defined through various criteria: topic (Restaurant/Hotel/Beauty), message length, OOV proportion, POS tags, sentiment lexicons, and semantic embeddings. The aggregated token importance reveals which words drive predictions within each segment, enabling root cause analysis of model errors and bias detection. The approach requires preprocessing text, generating word embeddings, training the CNN, applying LRP, and aggregating explanations across multiple segmentation strategies.

## Key Results
- Segmenting by topic reveals distinct top explanatory tokens for each domain (Restaurant, Hotel, Beauty)
- Aggregating local explainability by message length shows error rate patterns: longer messages have higher False Positive rates
- POS-based aggregation demonstrates adjectives are the most influential token category in model predictions
- Gender bias detection using embedding similarity identifies gender-associated words in model explanations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Segmenting observations by topic reveals different model behaviors and error patterns
- Mechanism: Grouping reviews into natural categories allows aggregation of local explainability scores within each segment, uncovering segment-specific biases and weaknesses
- Core assumption: Different domains have distinct vocabulary and sentiment patterns that influence model predictions differently
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "... illustrate, using a Yelp review data set with three segments (Restaurant, Hotel, and Beauty), that exploiting group/cluster structures in words and/or messages can aid in the interpretation of decisions made by NLP models..."
  - [section] "The top words should be manually reviewed and it's not always intuitive to decide if a top word is reasonable in the model. Table II is an example of variable importance at the overall level."
- Break condition: If topics share too much vocabulary overlap, segmentation may not reveal meaningful differences

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Clustering words by semantic meaning can detect model bias
- Mechanism: Using word embeddings to calculate similarity to gender-associated word groups reveals potential gender bias in model predictions
- Core assumption: Word embeddings capture semantic relationships that correlate with demographic attributes like gender
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "... can be utilized to assess the model's sensitivity or bias towards gender, syntax, and word meanings."
  - [section] "In order to segment the words based on gender or race, we can use the word embeddings (e.g. word2Vec, glove) which contain information about word meanings such as race and gender."
- Break condition: If word embeddings do not capture gender distinctions accurately, bias detection will fail

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Clustering words by Part of Speech shows which syntactic categories drive model predictions
- Mechanism: Aggregating token importance by POS categories reveals whether adjectives, nouns, or other parts of speech are most influential
- Core assumption: Different POS categories have distinct impacts on model predictions in NLP tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "... can be utilized to assess the model's sensitivity or bias towards gender, syntax, and word meanings."
  - [section] "Segmentation based on syntax/POS can help to understand the NLP model better. For example, the local explainability can be combined with the POS segmentation to see which syntax category of the speech has largest impact to the model prediction."
- Break condition: If POS tagging is inaccurate, the syntactic analysis will be unreliable

  - No meaningful differences between segments
  - Inconsistent POS tagging across different text domains
  - Word embeddings fail to capture semantic distinctions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the explainability insights from multi-resolution summaries differ when using separate topic-specific models versus a single joint model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that a joint model for all topics had slightly better performance, but separate models showed more gender-related bias in some topics. It suggests that fitting one large model can help decrease gender-related bias, but doesn't quantify the interpretability differences between these approaches.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the utility of multi-resolution explainability but does not directly compare how topic-specific versus joint model training affects the interpretability findings at segment and cluster levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: A direct comparison of interpretability patterns (e.g., top token importance, FP/FN error root causes) between separate and joint models for the same dataset would clarify how model architecture influences interpretability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent can syntactic (POS-based) segmentation improve NLP model interpretability compared to semantic or sentiment-based grouping?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper applies POS tagging and shows that adjectives dominate top explanatory tokens, but does not evaluate whether this syntactic grouping yields more actionable or accurate interpretability insights than meaning- or sentiment-based groupings.
- Why unresolved: While POS segmentation is implemented, the paper does not compare its interpretability value or diagnostic power against other grouping strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing interpretability clarity, bias detection, or error root cause identification using POS versus semantic/sentiment groupings would reveal relative utility.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can multi-resolution explainability be effectively scaled to much larger, more diverse text corpora without losing granularity or interpretability quality?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study uses a balanced, limited-size Yelp dataset with three predefined segments; the paper notes challenges of high dimensionality but does not test scalability or robustness of the method to larger, noisier, or more varied data.
- Why unresolved: The method's applicability and performance on broader, real-world datasets remain untested, particularly regarding computational efficiency and interpretability degradation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the method to significantly larger, more heterogeneous datasets and measuring interpretability metrics, computational cost, and diagnostic accuracy would demonstrate scalability.

## Limitations
- The method assumes meaningful segment-level patterns exist and requires sufficient sample size within each segment for reliable aggregation
- Gender bias detection relies on word embedding accuracy, which may vary across cultural contexts and embedding methods
- The framework's effectiveness on larger, more diverse datasets beyond the controlled Yelp review dataset remains unverified

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: The core mechanism of aggregating local explainability scores at segment and cluster levels is technically sound and well-supported by the methodology. The CNN model architecture and LRP-based explanation generation are standard approaches with established validity.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that segment-level aggregation reveals distinct model behaviors across topics (Restaurant, Hotel, Beauty) is plausible given domain differences, but the paper provides limited quantitative evidence comparing segment-level insights against global patterns. The error rate analysis by message length shows some differences but requires more rigorous statistical validation.

**Low Confidence**: The gender bias detection claims are the most speculative. While the approach of using word embedding similarity to gender-associated words is methodologically reasonable, the paper does not provide systematic evaluation of whether detected patterns represent actual bias versus legitimate domain differences. The claim that this method "reduces" gender bias is not directly tested or validated.

## Next Checks
1. **Statistical validation of segment differences**: Conduct statistical tests (e.g., permutation tests, confidence intervals) to determine whether the observed differences in explainability patterns across segments are significant or could arise by chance.

2. **Cross-domain generalization**: Apply the multi-resolution analysis to a different NLP dataset with different topics or domains to verify that the approach consistently reveals meaningful segment-level patterns beyond the Yelp review domain.

3. **Bias detection validation**: Create a controlled experiment with synthetic data where known gender biases are introduced into a model, then apply the embedding-based bias detection method to verify it can correctly identify the introduced biases at expected levels.
