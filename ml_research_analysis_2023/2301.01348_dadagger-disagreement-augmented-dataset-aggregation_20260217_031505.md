# DADAgger: Disagreement-Augmented Dataset Aggregation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01348
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01348
- Authors: Not specified in input
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: DADAgger reduces expert queries by 60-90% while maintaining convergence rates

## Executive Summary
DADAgger is an imitation learning algorithm that addresses the excessive expert query problem in DAgger by selectively querying the expert only on out-of-distribution (OOD) states. The method uses dropout layers to simulate an ensemble of policies and measures variance in their action predictions to identify states warranting expert input. On the Car Racing benchmark, DADAgger achieved 100% convergence rate with α=0.2 versus 40% for random sampling, using 20-40% of expert queries compared to standard DAgger. The approach also demonstrated the ability to construct compact, balanced datasets when run without initial data, enabling efficient one-shot training.

## Method Summary
DADAgger extends the DAgger algorithm by replacing uniform expert querying with selective querying based on ensemble disagreement. The policy network includes dropout layers that are active during inference to generate multiple stochastic forward passes (M times). Prediction variance across these passes serves as a proxy for uncertainty, with high variance indicating OOD states. The algorithm selects the top α percent of states by variance for expert querying, aggregates the resulting state-action pairs, and retrains the policy. This process repeats until convergence, significantly reducing expert query burden while maintaining learning performance.

## Key Results
- On Car Racing: 100% convergence rate with α=0.2 versus 40% for random sampling (p < 0.003), using 20-40% of DAgger's expert queries
- On Half Cheetah: All DADAgger variants reached similar performance to DAgger with only 10% of queries
- Constructed compact dataset: 746 samples vs 1139 for DAgger through iterative DADAgger with no initial data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dropout approximates an ensemble of policies to identify OOD states via disagreement
- Mechanism: Dropout layers during inference generate multiple stochastic forward passes, simulating M different models. Variance in predictions serves as uncertainty proxy; high variance indicates OOD states
- Core assumption: States outside training distribution induce higher disagreement than in-distribution states
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section 3 explicitly describe using dropout to simulate ensemble uncertainty for OOD detection
- Break condition: Correlated errors or strong bias among predictors may cause agreement on OOD states, masking true uncertainty

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Selecting top α percent of states by variance reduces expert queries while preserving convergence
- Mechanism: After collecting trajectories, compute prediction variance for each state, keep top α fraction with highest variance, and query expert for those states
- Core assumption: Expert queries are most valuable on states with highest prediction disagreement; lower-variance states are sufficiently covered
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section 4.1 report performance for different α values, showing convergence with reduced queries
- Break condition: If α is too low, critical OOD states may be missed, leading to failure to converge or policy collapse

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DADAgger can construct a compact, balanced dataset when run without initial data
- Mechanism: Starting with empty dataset, agent explores and only queries expert on states of highest disagreement, growing dataset only with informative samples
- Core assumption: Disagreement-based sampling yields high-quality, diverse data that generalizes well even with fewer samples
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section 5.3 describe constructing 746-sample dataset versus 1139 for DAgger with no initial data
- Break condition: Agent may repeatedly visit similar OOD states without diversity, creating biased dataset despite low sample count

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Covariate shift in imitation learning
  - Why needed here: DAgger addresses covariate shift by re-querying expert on encountered states. DADAgger builds on this by selectively querying only when policy encounters states outside training distribution
  - Quick check question: What happens if policy encounters state distribution not seen during training without correction?

- Concept: Uncertainty estimation via ensembles
  - Why needed here: Identifying OOD states relies on measuring disagreement among models. Dropout provides computationally cheap way to simulate ensemble for this purpose
  - Quick check question: Why does high variance in predictions suggest state is out-of-distribution?

- Concept: Active learning and selective querying
  - Why needed here: Instead of querying expert on every state (as in DAgger), DADAgger uses acquisition function (prediction variance) to choose most informative states, reducing expert effort
  - Quick check question: What trade-off exists between fraction α of states queried and risk of missing important data?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Policy network with dropout -> M forward passes -> Variance computation -> Top-α selector -> Expert query interface -> Dataset aggregator -> Trainer

- Critical path:
  1. Generate trajectory with current policy
  2. For each state, run M dropout passes, compute variance
  3. Select top α% by variance
  4. Query expert for selected states
  5. Aggregate new data
  6. Retrain policy
  7. Repeat until convergence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - M vs. computational cost: Higher M gives better uncertainty estimate but more forward passes
  - α vs. sample efficiency: Lower α reduces expert queries but risks missing critical states
  - Dropout rate vs. ensemble diversity: Higher dropout increases variance but may destabilize training

- Failure signatures:
  - Policy never improves: likely α too low or ensemble poorly calibrated
  - High variance in low-stakes states: dropout rate too high or network underfitting
  - Convergence only after many iterations: initial dataset too small or α too conservative

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run DADAgger on Car Racing with α=0.2, M=10, measure convergence rate vs. DAgger and random sampling
  2. Vary α (0.1, 0.2, 0.4) and measure dataset size and action distribution balance
  3. Run DADAgger from empty dataset, track dataset growth and convergence speed, compare to DAgger with initial data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Optimal α value across different environments and tasks
- Comparison to other query-efficient imitation learning algorithms beyond random sampling
- Impact of predictor independence assumption on practical performance

## Limitations
- Assumes sufficient independence between dropout-based ensemble members for reliable uncertainty estimation
- Empirical results based on limited sample sizes (5 runs per condition) despite statistical significance
- One-shot training from empty dataset may be fragile without clear diversity guarantees

## Confidence
- Medium: Dropout-based variance reliably identifies OOD states assuming ensemble independence
- Medium: 20-40% query reduction claims robust but sample size concerns exist
- Low: One-shot training result intriguing but mechanism ensuring diversity unclear
