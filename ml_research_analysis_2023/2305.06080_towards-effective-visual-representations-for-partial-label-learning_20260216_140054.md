# Towards Effective Visual Representations for Partial-Label Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06080
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06080
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PaPi significantly outperforms other PLL methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance with up to 4.57% accuracy improvement on CIFAR-100.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes PaPi, a partial-label learning method that learns effective visual representations through a guided prototypical classifier. PaPi addresses the challenge of partial-label learning, where training instances have ambiguous candidate labels containing the true label. The core idea is to guide the optimization of a prototypical classifier by a linear classifier, explicitly encouraging the representation to reflect visual similarity between categories. PaPi eliminates the contrastive learning module used in prior work, which introduces noise and computational overhead, and instead uses a prototypical alignment loss term to align the similarity distribution over classes with the disambiguated probability from the linear classifier. The method also uses a cross-entropy loss to learn the linear classifier in a self-teaching fashion. Extensive experiments on various image classification tasks demonstrate that PaPi significantly outperforms other PLL methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

## Method Summary
PaPi is a partial-label learning method that learns effective visual representations by guiding a prototypical classifier with a linear classifier. The method uses a shared feature encoder for both classifiers and a projector to map representations to a low-dimensional space for prototype computation. The linear classifier produces disambiguated probabilities that are aligned with similarity distributions computed from class-specific prototypes via a prototypical alignment loss. The linear classifier is self-taught using a cross-entropy loss with current and previous stage predictions. PaPi eliminates the contrastive learning module used in prior work, reducing noise and computational overhead. Prototypes are updated via a moving average mechanism to stabilize training.

## Key Results
- PaPi significantly outperforms other PLL methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance on various image classification tasks.
- On CIFAR-100, PaPi improves accuracy by 4.57% compared to the previous best method.
- The learned representations show better intra-class compactness and inter-class separability.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PaPi guides the optimization of a prototypical classifier by a linear classifier to encourage representations to reflect visual similarity between categories.
- Mechanism: The linear classifier produces disambiguated probabilities, which are aligned with similarity distributions computed from class-specific prototypes in a projected low-dimensional space via a prototypical alignment loss.
- Core assumption: The linear classifier can produce more accurate disambiguated probabilities than the prototypical classifier in early training stages, allowing it to guide the prototypical classifier effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "PaPi guides the optimization of a prototypical classifier by a linear classifier with which they share the same feature encoder, thus explicitly encouraging the representation to reflect visual similarity between categories."
  - [section] "PaPi produces a similarity distribution over classes for each sample based on a softmax function over distances to class-specific prototypes... Afterwards, PaPi aligns the distribution with the disambiguated probability post-processed from one linear classifier prediction."
- Break condition: If the linear classifier's predictions become consistently worse than the prototypical classifier's, the guidance direction may become counterproductive.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Eliminating the contrastive learning module reduces noise and computational overhead from unreliable pseudo-positive samples.
- Mechanism: By removing the contrastive learning module that uses noisy pseudo-labels to select positive samples, PaPi avoids introducing unreliable similarity information and the need for large batches or momentum encoders.
- Core assumption: The contrastive learning module's reliance on noisy pseudo-labels for positive sample selection introduces more noise than benefit to representation learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Without access to true labels, positive points are predicted using pseudo-labels that are inherently noisy, and negative points often require large batches or momentum encoders, resulting in unreliable similarity information and a high computational overhead."
  - [section] "PaPi directly eliminates the contrastive learning module which introduces noisy positives, and adopts the opposite direction of disambiguation guidance compared to PiCO."
- Break condition: If a more reliable method for selecting positive samples becomes available that doesn't introduce significant noise.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Self-teaching the linear classifier with current and previous stage predictions stabilizes training and improves label disambiguation.
- Mechanism: The linear classifier is updated using a cross-entropy loss with disambiguated probabilities that are computed from its own predictions using a soft and moving-average mechanism.
- Core assumption: The linear classifier can gradually improve its predictions through self-teaching, even with initially noisy disambiguated probabilities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Meanwhile, the linear classifier performs self-teaching wherein each stage of learning is guided by the current and previous stages."
  - [section] "We further adopt a soft and moving-average mechanism to update the probability, which also stabilizes the training process: pi = λpi + (1 − λ)ui"
- Break condition: If the moving-average mechanism causes the disambiguated probabilities to converge too slowly or get stuck in poor local optima.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Encoder (f) -> Projector (g) -> Prototypical Classifier and Encoder (f) -> Linear Classifier -> Alignment Loss
- Critical path: Encoder → Projector → Prototypical Classifier (for similarity distribution) and Encoder → Linear Classifier (for disambiguated probability) → Alignment Loss → Backpropagation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Eliminating contrastive learning reduces noise but loses potential benefits of contrasting same/different classes
  - Guiding prototypical classifier by linear classifier assumes linear classifier is more accurate in early stages
  - Using moving average for disambiguated probabilities stabilizes training but may slow convergence
- Failure signatures:
  - If prototypes don't meaningfully represent class centers, alignment loss won't be effective
  - If linear classifier predictions are too noisy, self-teaching won't converge properly
  - If temperature τ is poorly chosen, similarity distributions may be too sharp or too flat
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that eliminating the contrastive module doesn't hurt performance on a simple dataset like Fashion-MNIST
  2. Test different temperature values (τ) for the softmax in similarity distribution computation
  3. Compare performance with and without the moving-average mechanism for updating disambiguated probabilities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of PaPi compare to supervised learning when the true label is included in the candidate set with a certain probability (e.g., 90% vs 100%)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that PaPi can achieve comparable results against supervised learning in many cases, but doesn't explore scenarios where the true label is not guaranteed to be in the candidate set.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers the standard PLL setting where the true label is always included in the candidate set. Exploring scenarios with lower probabilities of the true label being present would provide insights into the robustness of PaPi.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on datasets with varying probabilities of the true label being included in the candidate set and comparing PaPi's performance to supervised learning.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can PaPi be extended to handle multi-label learning scenarios where each instance can have multiple true labels?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on partial-label learning where each instance has only one true label among multiple candidates. Extending this to multi-label learning would be a natural progression.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't discuss or explore the applicability of PaPi to multi-label learning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Modifying PaPi to handle multi-label learning and evaluating its performance on datasets with multiple true labels per instance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of PaPi scale with increasing dataset size and number of classes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates PaPi's effectiveness on various benchmark datasets, but doesn't explicitly explore its scalability with larger datasets and more classes.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating PaPi's performance on existing benchmark datasets without explicitly addressing its scalability properties.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on larger datasets with more classes and analyzing the computational complexity and performance of PaPi as the dataset size and number of classes increase.

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't provide a detailed ablation study comparing performance with and without the contrastive learning module on the same datasets, making it difficult to definitively attribute performance gains to the removal of contrastive learning versus the novel alignment mechanism.
- While the method shows strong performance on standard benchmarks, the paper doesn't thoroughly evaluate its robustness to extreme noise levels in the candidate labels or its scalability to very large-scale datasets.
- The method's performance in scenarios where the true label is not guaranteed to be in the candidate set is not explored, limiting insights into its robustness to lower probabilities of true label presence.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The core mechanism of guiding a prototypical classifier with a linear classifier's disambiguated probabilities is well-supported by the mathematical formulation and implementation details provided.
- Medium confidence: The claim that eliminating contrastive learning reduces noise and computational overhead is plausible but not fully validated through comprehensive ablations.
- Medium confidence: The assertion that self-teaching the linear classifier stabilizes training and improves label disambiguation is supported by the proposed mechanism but could benefit from more empirical evidence, particularly in terms of convergence analysis.
