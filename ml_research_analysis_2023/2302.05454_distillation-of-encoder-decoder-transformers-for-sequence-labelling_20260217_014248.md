# Distillation of encoder-decoder transformers for sequence labelling

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.05454
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05454
- **Reference count**: 25
- **Primary result**: SenTScore enables knowledge distillation from encoder-decoder transformers to BiLSTM sequence taggers, outperforming prior approaches and setting new SOTA on 7 datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SenTScore, a novel inference algorithm that enables knowledge distillation from large encoder-decoder transformer models to smaller sequence tagging models. The key innovation is modifying decoder inference to generate tag-level probability distributions rather than just pseudo-labels, allowing effective distillation via KL divergence between teacher and student tag distributions. Experiments on 7 sequence labeling datasets demonstrate that SenTScore significantly outperforms prior approaches, particularly in few-shot settings, with a BiLSTM student achieving 85.1 F1 on ATIS with 100 gold labels versus 79.9 without distillation.

## Method Summary
The method involves fine-tuning T5-base encoder-decoder models using an sBIO tagging format with sentinel tokens, then applying SenTScore decoding with beam search to generate probability distributions over tag sequences. These distributions are used to train BiLSTM students via a loss combining cross-entropy with pseudo-labels and KL divergence between teacher and student distributions. The approach is evaluated on 7 English sequence labeling datasets, comparing direct tagging performance and knowledge distillation effectiveness against baseline methods.

## Key Results
- SenTScore BiLSTM student achieves 85.1 F1 on ATIS with 100 gold labels (vs 79.9 without KD, 82.4 with KD term removed)
- SenTScore sets new state-of-the-art results for T5-base on most datasets
- Consistent trend showing KD outperforms training with only pseudo-labels across multiple datasets
- Up to 4× speed-up over standard beam-search decoding while maintaining hallucination-free outputs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SenTScore enables direct distillation from encoder-decoder transformers by providing tag-level probability distributions
- Mechanism: The decoder scores complete tag sequences in sBIO format, producing normalized probability distributions over output tags at each position
- Core assumption: Sentinel tokens act as EOS markers that allow proper normalization of the probability distribution
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This paper presents a new inference algorithm called SenTScore that enables knowledge distillation (KD) from large encoder-decoder transformer models to smaller sequence tagging models like BiLSTM"
  - [section 4.1] "The encoder-decoder model can be used to calculate the log-likelihood of each of such strings log Lθ(Sout;Sin)"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The modified sBIO tagging scheme combined with SenTScore provides hallucination-free decoding
- Mechanism: Beam search finds the highest-scoring tag sequence constrained to follow the sBIO format, preventing generation of spurious tokens
- Core assumption: Finite state machine constraints on valid output sequences are correctly implemented and enforced during decoding
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "The valid output strings that can be generated by the decoder are the |T |L sequences of the form Sout = s0t1s1...t LsL"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knowledge distillation with SenTScore outperforms training with pseudo-labels alone
- Mechanism: KL divergence term captures full probability distribution over tags rather than just one-hot pseudo-labels, allowing student to learn teacher's uncertainty patterns
- Core assumption: Temperature scaling parameter τ is properly set to smooth probability distributions for effective distillation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "The contribution of each of the tags to the distillation objective that we use to train the student sequence tagger is −∑k(y∗i)k log (p∗i)k +λKLKL(p∗i||qi)"

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SenTScore performance scale with increasingly larger teacher models (e.g., GPT-3, PaLM) beyond T5-base?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses T5-base (220M parameters) and mentions future work exploring larger models, but does not test this scaling relationship
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates SenTScore with a single, relatively small teacher model. Performance could plateau or improve dramatically with larger models
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying teacher model size from T5-base through GPT-3-scale models, measuring F1 gains from SenTScore vs baseline approaches

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of SenTScore's inference speed compared to constrained decoding methods for hallucination-free sequence labeling?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions SenTScore could slow inference time but doesn't provide timing comparisons with constrained decoding approaches
- Why unresolved: While SenTScore provides tag-level probabilities for distillation, its computational efficiency relative to alternative hallucination-free methods remains unknown
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head timing benchmarks comparing SenTScore's beam search against constrained decoding implementations across various dataset sizes and model architectures

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does SenTScore perform on non-English sequence labeling tasks with morphologically rich languages?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] All experiments use English datasets, though the paper mentions mT5 as a multilingual model and future work exploring cross-lingual applications
- Why unresolved: The effectiveness of SenTScore's tag-level scoring approach may vary significantly with tokenization complexity and morphological variation in other languages
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating SenTScore on established multilingual sequence labeling benchmarks (e.g., CoNLL multilingual NER) and comparing performance across languages with varying morphological complexity

## Limitations
- Limited to English datasets, raising questions about cross-lingual generalization
- Computational overhead of SenTScore vs standard greedy decoding not fully characterized
- Temperature parameter sensitivity not thoroughly explored across datasets

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of SenTScore as a modified inference algorithm that enables encoder-decoder transformers to score complete tag sequences is well-defined and the experimental results showing improvements over baselines are robust
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that SenTScore provides "hallucination-free" decoding is supported by the constrained output format but lacks direct empirical validation against hallucination metrics
- **Low Confidence**: The paper's broader claims about enabling "better utilization of encoder-decoder transformers" for "wide range of sequence labeling tasks" and setting "new state-of-the-art results" are based on limited experimental scope
