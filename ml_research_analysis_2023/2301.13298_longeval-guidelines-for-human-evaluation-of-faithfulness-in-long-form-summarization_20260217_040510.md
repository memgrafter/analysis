# LongEval: Guidelines for Human Evaluation of Faithfulness in Long-form Summarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.13298
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13298
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: 73% of long-form summarization papers don't perform human evaluation due to annotation challenges

## Executive Summary
Human evaluation of long-form summaries remains challenging due to their length and subjective nature. Through a survey of 162 papers, the authors found that 73% do not perform human evaluation, and those that do face issues like low inter-annotator agreement and lack of standardization. To address these challenges, the authors propose LongEval, a set of guidelines for faithfulness evaluation that includes: (1) fine-grained clause-level annotations instead of full-summary ratings, (2) partial annotation of a subset of summary units to reduce workload, and (3) automated alignment of summary units to source sentences. Experiments on SQuALITY and PubMed datasets show that fine-grained annotations improve inter-annotator agreement (std-dev from 18.5 to 6.8), partial annotations maintain high correlation with full annotations (0.89 Kendall's tau at 50% workload), and automated alignment provides limited benefit unless summaries are highly extractive. The authors release their data, templates, and software for future research.

## Method Summary
The LongEval framework addresses human evaluation challenges in long-form summarization through three key innovations. First, it shifts from holistic summary ratings to fine-grained clause-level annotations, breaking down summaries into smaller, more manageable units. Second, it introduces partial annotation where only a subset of summary units are annotated, reducing annotator workload while maintaining evaluation quality. Third, it employs automated sentence alignment to map summary units to source sentences, though this proves most effective for extractive summaries. The framework was validated on two datasets (SQuALITY and PubMed) using multiple annotators per summary to measure inter-annotator agreement and correlation between different annotation approaches.

## Key Results
- Inter-annotator agreement improved significantly with fine-grained clause-level annotations (std-dev reduced from 18.5 to 6.8)
- Partial annotations maintain high correlation with full annotations (0.89 Kendall's tau at 50% workload reduction)
- Automated alignment shows limited benefit unless summaries are highly extractive

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The LongEval guidelines work by breaking down the complex task of evaluating long summaries into more manageable components. By focusing on individual clauses rather than entire summaries, annotators can make more precise judgments about faithfulness. The partial annotation approach recognizes that not every part of a summary needs equal scrutiny, allowing for efficient resource allocation. The automated alignment component attempts to reduce cognitive load by pre-matching summary units to source content, though its effectiveness depends on the extractive nature of the summaries being evaluated.

## Foundational Learning
**Faithfulness vs. other evaluation dimensions**: Understanding that faithfulness (accuracy to source) is distinct from coherence and fluency, and requires different evaluation approaches. *Why needed*: Different aspects of summary quality need different annotation strategies. *Quick check*: Can you explain why a summary might be faithful but incoherent?

**Clause-level vs. summary-level annotation**: Recognizing that breaking down summaries into smaller units improves precision and reduces cognitive load. *Why needed*: Long summaries overwhelm annotators when evaluated holistically. *Quick check*: What's the optimal unit size for annotation?

**Inter-annotator agreement metrics**: Understanding how to measure and interpret agreement between different annotators. *Why needed*: Low agreement indicates unclear guidelines or overly subjective tasks. *Quick check*: What std-dev values indicate good vs. poor agreement?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Summary -> Clause extraction -> (Optional) Automated alignment -> Annotator interface -> Agreement metrics

**Critical path**: The most important workflow is clause extraction followed by human annotation, as this directly impacts the quality and reliability of the evaluation.

**Design tradeoffs**: The framework trades off completeness (evaluating every clause) for efficiency (partial annotation), accepting some loss of coverage for significant workload reduction.

**Failure signatures**: Low inter-annotator agreement suggests either unclear guidelines or inherently subjective evaluation criteria. Poor correlation between partial and full annotations indicates the sampled clauses aren't representative.

## Limitations
- Clause-level annotations may miss global coherence and fluency issues that emerge from examining the summary as a whole
- Automated alignment shows limited improvement in inter-annotator agreement, suggesting summary-level evaluation remains challenging
- The study focuses primarily on faithfulness, leaving other aspects like coherence and relevance less explored

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Inter-annotator agreement improvements (std-dev reduction from 18.5 to 6.8) and correlation between partial and full annotations (0.89 Kendall's tau)

**Medium confidence**: Practical benefits of automated alignment, given its limited effectiveness unless summaries are highly extractive

**Medium confidence**: Workload reduction claims, as the study demonstrates correlation but not definitive time savings

## Next Checks
