# Transfer Learning in Deep Learning Models for Building Load Forecasting: Case of Limited Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10663
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10663
- Reference count: 40
- Key result: Building-to-building transfer learning with Transformer achieves 56.8% improvement in forecasting accuracy for buildings with limited data

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of load forecasting for buildings with limited historical data by proposing a Building-to-Building Transfer Learning framework. The approach leverages pre-trained deep learning models from older buildings with abundant data to enhance forecasting performance on new or data-scarce buildings. The Transformer model is specifically adapted with transfer learning to improve medium-term load forecasting accuracy, achieving significant performance gains over conventional deep learning methods trained from scratch.

## Method Summary
The method involves pre-training deep learning models (Transformer, LSTM, RNN) on an older building with abundant data, then transferring the learned weights to a new building with limited data (20% of total samples). The approach includes feature engineering with calendar-based features (day-of-week, seasons, holidays) and fine-tuning on the target building. The study evaluates performance using MSE, RMSE, and MAPE metrics on hourly data from two commercial buildings over one year.

## Key Results
- 56.8% improvement in forecasting accuracy using transfer learning vs. training from scratch
- Transformer model achieves RMSE of 0.009, outperforming LSTM (0.011) and RNN (0.051)
- Transfer learning effective with only 20% of total data samples (336 hourly points)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Transfer learning enables accurate load forecasting for buildings with limited data by leveraging pre-trained weights from buildings with abundant data. A pre-trained Transformer model trained on older buildings captures general patterns and trends in load consumption. These learned weights are then transferred to a new building with limited data, providing a strong starting point for fine-tuning rather than training from scratch. Core assumption: Load consumption patterns across similar building types share common temporal and environmental dependencies that can be generalized through transfer learning.

### Mechanism 2
Transformer architecture outperforms sequential models like LSTM and RNN in load forecasting by processing inputs in parallel and capturing long-term dependencies more effectively. The Transformer uses self-attention mechanisms to weigh the importance of different time steps simultaneously, avoiding the sequential processing bottleneck of RNNs and LSTMs. This allows it to maintain information over longer time horizons without the vanishing gradient problem. Core assumption: The temporal dependencies in building load data are complex and benefit from the global attention mechanism rather than sequential processing.

### Mechanism 3
Feature engineering (day-of-week, seasons, holidays) combined with transfer learning significantly improves forecasting accuracy for buildings with limited historical data. Engineered features provide additional context that helps the model distinguish between different consumption patterns (e.g., weekdays vs weekends, seasonal variations). When combined with transfer learning, these features allow the model to quickly adapt to the target building's specific patterns. Core assumption: The temporal patterns in building load consumption are strongly influenced by calendar-based features that can be explicitly encoded.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing → Feature engineering → Model training (source building) → Weight transfer → Fine-tuning (target building) → Evaluation
- Critical path: The transfer learning pipeline is critical - without successful weight transfer and fine-tuning, the model cannot achieve the reported 56.8% improvement over training from scratch
- Design tradeoffs: Transformer complexity vs. dataset size (risk of overfitting with limited data), transfer learning benefit vs. domain dissimilarity between source and target buildings
- Failure signatures: Overfitting on limited target data (high training accuracy but poor validation performance), poor convergence during fine-tuning (weights don't adapt well), performance similar to or worse than baseline models
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train Transformer from scratch on target building data only (baseline for comparison)
  2. Transfer weights from source building and fine-tune on target building (main proposed approach)
  3. Transfer weights but freeze early layers, only fine-tune later layers (investigate layer-wise transfer effectiveness)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the minimum amount of historical data required for transfer learning to be effective in building load forecasting?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions future work will focus on finding the limitations of the approach, specifically the least amount of data used with transfer learning to increase performance
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific thresholds or experiments determining the minimum data requirements for effective transfer learning in this context
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the amount of training data from small to moderate sizes, showing when transfer learning begins to outperform training from scratch

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed transfer learning approach perform on different building types and sizes beyond the large commercial building studied?
- Basis in paper: The conclusion states future work will apply the same methodologies to different building domains and sizes to investigate different outcomes
- Why unresolved: The current study only evaluates performance on one large commercial building, limiting generalizability
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive testing across multiple building types (residential, industrial, small commercial) and sizes with varying energy consumption patterns

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why do the models perform poorly under light loading conditions during weekends, and can this be improved?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that "the three models do not seem to perform well under light loading conditions for the case of weekends as shown after hour 125 in Fig. 6"
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this issue but does not investigate the underlying causes or potential solutions
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of model behavior on weekends, identification of specific failure modes, and development of targeted improvements for low-load scenarios

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the proposed transformer model compare to other state-of-the-art deep learning architectures for time series forecasting beyond RNN and LSTM?
- Basis in paper: The paper only compares the transformer model to RNN and LSTM, but mentions that transformers are state-of-the-art for many problems
- Why unresolved: The study lacks comparison with other modern architectures like Temporal Fusion Transformers, Informer, or other specialized time series models
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmark studies comparing multiple modern architectures on the same dataset using identical evaluation protocols

## Limitations

- Limited validation across multiple building pairs - results based on a single case study of one source-target building pair
- Lack of detailed architectural specifications for the Transformer model and specific training hyperparameters
- No comparison with traditional statistical forecasting methods (ARIMA, Prophet) or other modern deep learning architectures

## Confidence

- High confidence in the general effectiveness of transfer learning for building load forecasting with limited data
- Medium confidence in the specific superiority of Transformer over LSTM/RNN architectures due to lack of architectural details
- Medium confidence in the 56.8% improvement claim without access to full experimental methodology and comparison with multiple baselines

## Next Checks

1. Replicate the study with multiple source-target building pairs across different building types and climates to assess generalizability
2. Conduct ablation studies to isolate the contributions of transfer learning vs. feature engineering vs. Transformer architecture
