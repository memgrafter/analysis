# HiPrompt: Few-Shot Biomedical Knowledge Fusion via Hierarchy-Oriented Prompting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05973
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05973
- Authors: Multiple
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 90.79% Hits@1 in zero-shot biomedical knowledge fusion, significantly outperforming conventional and neural baselines

## Executive Summary
HiPrompt addresses the challenge of aligning biomedical knowledge graphs to hierarchical index systems in few-shot settings where labeled training data is scarce. The framework uses large language models with hierarchy-oriented prompts to align entities from knowledge graphs to terms in biomedical hierarchies. By combining BM25-based retrieval with attribute and structural expansion and LLM re-ranking using prompt-based learning that incorporates hierarchy context, HiPrompt achieves state-of-the-art performance across strict and lenient metrics on two benchmark datasets derived from real biomedical knowledge graphs and disease hierarchies.

## Method Summary
HiPrompt employs a retrieve-and-re-rank pipeline for few-shot biomedical knowledge fusion. The method first uses BM25 with attribute and structural expansion to retrieve coarse candidate alignments between knowledge graph entities and hierarchical terms. Then, an LLM re-ranks these candidates using prompt-based learning that incorporates hierarchy context through carefully designed prompt templates. The approach is specifically designed for scenarios where labeled training data is limited, leveraging the few-shot capabilities of large language models to achieve high alignment accuracy without extensive supervision. The framework is evaluated on two benchmark datasets created from real biomedical knowledge graphs and disease hierarchies, demonstrating significant improvements over both conventional unsupervised models and neural embedding approaches.

## Key Results
- Achieves 90.79% Hits@1 in zero-shot settings on MedDRAType dataset
- Achieves 88.01% Hits@1 in zero-shot settings on BioCDX dataset
- Outperforms LogMap, BM25, Edit Dist, SapBERT, MTransE, and SelfKG across strict (Hits@1, MRR) and lenient (nDCG@1, WuP) metrics
- Demonstrates consistent performance gains across 3-shot, 5-shot, 10-shot, and zero-shot settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method leverages LLMs' ability to understand contextual relationships within hierarchies while BM25 provides efficient initial retrieval. The hierarchy-oriented prompts guide the LLM to consider parent-child relationships and semantic proximity within the hierarchy, enabling more accurate alignment than flat similarity measures alone. This combination exploits both the efficiency of traditional information retrieval and the contextual reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Knowledge Graph Entities -> BM25 Retrieval -> LLM Re-ranking -> Aligned Hierarchy Terms

**Critical Path**: The retrieve-and-re-rank pipeline is the critical path, where BM25 retrieval provides candidate set and LLM re-ranking produces final alignment decisions.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Uses simple BM25 for efficiency in candidate generation rather than neural retrieval methods, then applies computationally expensive LLM re-ranking only on a smaller candidate set rather than the full search space.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance when entity names are highly ambiguous without sufficient hierarchical context, or when hierarchies lack clear parent-child relationships that can guide alignment.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can HiPrompt be adapted to dynamically grow the biomedical hierarchy with newly aligned entities, rather than relying on a fixed existing hierarchy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The conclusion mentions "expanding the scope of biomedical knowledge fusion to allow the hierarchy to dynamically grow with the aligned entities."
- Why unresolved: The current framework assumes a fixed hierarchy and does not address how to handle new terms or entities that may not exist in the current hierarchy structure.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating HiPrompt's ability to suggest new hierarchical levels or restructure the hierarchy when encountering novel entities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the performance impact of using automated prompt generation techniques for hierarchy-oriented prompting instead of manual template design?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "We exclude the use of automatic prompt generation techniques [33, 49] due to the limited availability of training data."
- Why unresolved: The authors acknowledge this as a potential direction but did not explore it due to data scarcity constraints.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing retrieval and ranking performance using automatically generated prompts versus manually designed templates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does HiPrompt's performance scale with larger knowledge graphs and hierarchies beyond the tested biomedical domains?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments were conducted on specific biomedical KGs (SDKG, repoDB) and a disease hierarchy, with no evaluation on other domains or larger-scale graphs.
- Why unresolved: The framework's effectiveness in other domains and with significantly larger graphs remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance evaluation on diverse KGs (e.g., general knowledge graphs, other scientific domains) and comparison with baselines across different scales.

## Limitations
- High performance claims lack confidence intervals and statistical significance testing
- Evaluation limited to small hierarchies (28-29 top-level categories) raising generalizability concerns
- Assumes hierarchical alignment is optimal, which may not hold for all biomedical entity relationships
- Complex LLM-based approach adds computational overhead without clear necessity for all use cases

## Confidence
- **Core Innovation**: Medium - Results are compelling but comparison focuses on supervised baselines rather than fully unsupervised alternatives
- **Evaluation Methodology**: Low - Exceptional zero-shot performance lacks statistical validation and confidence intervals
- **Scalability Claims**: Low - Framework not tested on larger graphs or diverse domains beyond specific biomedical cases
- **Generalizability**: Medium - Assumes hierarchy-based alignment works universally, untested on non-hierarchical relationships

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies to quantify the contribution of each component (BM25 vs LLM re-ranking, hierarchy context vs entity-only prompts) using statistical significance testing with confidence intervals.
2. Test the method on larger, more diverse biomedical hierarchies with hundreds of top-level categories to assess scalability and whether performance degrades as hierarchy complexity increases.
3. Evaluate on datasets where entity relationships are inherently non-hierarchical (e.g., protein-protein interactions, drug-drug interactions) to determine whether the hierarchy-oriented approach remains effective outside tree-structured knowledge.
