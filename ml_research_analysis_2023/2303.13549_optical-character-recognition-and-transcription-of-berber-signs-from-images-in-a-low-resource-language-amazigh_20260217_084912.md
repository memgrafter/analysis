# Optical Character Recognition and Transcription of Berber Signs from Images in a Low-Resource Language Amazigh

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13549
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13549
- Reference count: 8
- One-line primary result: VGG-16 CNN achieves over 92% accuracy for recognizing Tifinagh characters from natural environment images

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DaToBS, a deep learning approach for detecting and transcribing Amazigh (Berber) characters from images in natural environments. The work addresses the scarcity of machine learning research on low-resource languages like Amazigh, whose Tifinagh script consists of pictorial symbols requiring computer vision techniques rather than language models. Using a self-curated dataset of 1862 character images from 120 photographs, the VGG-16 CNN model achieves over 92% character recognition accuracy. This research opens pathways for automated translation from Berber and development of pedagogical applications for indigenous language outreach.

## Method Summary
The DaToBS approach combines computer vision and deep learning to recognize Tifinagh characters from natural environment photographs. The method involves collecting 120 images containing Berber road signs and business signage, manually annotating characters with bounding boxes to create a corpus of 1862 images, preprocessing these images to 50x50px grayscale format, and training a VGG-16 CNN model. The model architecture features progressively increasing filter depths from 64 to 512 filters across convolutional layers, trained with an 80/20 split and learning rate of 0.001. The approach specifically targets the challenge of character recognition in uncontrolled outdoor environments with varying lighting, perspectives, and background conditions.

## Key Results
- VGG-16 CNN model achieves over 92% character recognition accuracy on test set
- Self-curated dataset of 1862 character images from 120 natural environment photographs successfully trains the model
- Character imbalance exists in the corpus, with top three characters comprising over one-third of all samples
- VGG-16 outperforms VGG-19 on this dataset size, validating architecture choice

## Limitations

- Dataset availability: Lack of publicly available code and dataset makes independent verification impossible
- Limited evaluation: Evaluation metrics limited to overall accuracy without detailed per-character analysis or robustness testing
- Relative performance: No comparison with existing OCR approaches for Amazigh or similar low-resource languages
