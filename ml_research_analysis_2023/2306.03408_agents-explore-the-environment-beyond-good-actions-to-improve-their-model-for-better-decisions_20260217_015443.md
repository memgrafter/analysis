# Agents Explore the Environment Beyond Good Actions to Improve Their Model for Better Decisions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03408
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03408
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: The paper addresses how MuZero can fail to explore parts of the decision tree it would not visit in self-play.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how MuZero can fail to explore parts of the decision tree it would not visit during normal self-play. The authors propose a hybrid policy approach where the agent randomly deviates from its planned policy at the start of each training episode and then switches back to the planned policy partway through. This allows the agent to experience rewards associated with the planned policy even when starting from sub-optimal states, improving model learning. The primary result shows this approach reduced bad decisions in Tic-Tac-Toe by a factor of 435±190 compared to normal training.

## Method Summary
The method involves using a hybrid policy that starts with an exploring policy and switches to a normal policy at a random time step during each training episode. The normal policy is the improved policy from Gumbel MuZero. The exploring policy uses a temperature T > 1 applied to the normal policy's softmax output. The value target for the non-intermediate reward case is set to keep its existing value before the switch and to the measured reward after the switch. Additionally, Dirichlet noise is added to the policy before tree search to prevent unwarranted action bias.

## Key Results
- The hybrid policy approach reduced bad decisions in Tic-Tac-Toe by a factor of 435±190 compared to normal training
- Using Dirichlet noise improved decisions by a factor of 3.6±1.2
- Playing out eagerly by setting Gumbel value to 0 reduced bad decisions by a factor of 2.1±0.3

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hybrid policy exploration improves model learning by forcing experience in decision paths that would otherwise be unexplored
- Mechanism: The agent uses a two-phase action selection where it first follows an exploring policy (high temperature softmax over normal policy) for a random number of steps, then switches to the normal policy
- Core assumption: The model can learn correct value expectations from experiencing rewards in novel states, even if those states were reached via exploration rather than planned actions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The agent achieves this, first by normal planning to come up with an improved policy. Second, it randomly deviates from this policy at the beginning of each training episode. And third, it switches back to the improved policy at a random time step to experience the rewards from the environment associated with the improved policy"
  - [section 6.1]: "Phybrid = {Pexploring if t < tstartNormal, Pnormal if t ≥ tstartNormal}" with "value target for the non-intermediate reward case is then given by vtarget t = {vinitialInference,t if t < tstartNormal, rmeasured tend if t ≥ tstartNormal}"
- Break condition: If the model cannot learn from the experienced rewards in the novel states, or if the exploration temperature is too high causing the agent to rarely switch back to the normal policy

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adding Dirichlet noise to policy entering tree search prevents unwarranted action bias and improves exploration
- Mechanism: Dirichlet noise is added to the prior probabilities in the root node before starting tree search, forcing the agent to consider actions without unfair preference when actions would not differ in value under perfect strategy
- Core assumption: Without Dirichlet noise, the agent may develop unwarranted bias toward certain actions that prevents it from gaining experience in parts of the decision tree after unfavorable actions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.2]: "it adds a force toward choosing actions without unfair preference if the actions would not differ in value under a perfect strategy. If no force is added, one such action may be favoured by the agent, potentially preventing the agent from gaining experience from the parts of the decision tree after the unfavourable actions"
  - [section 7.4]: "we see an improvement by a factor 3.6 ± 1.2 using Dirichlet noise" compared to models without Dirichlet noise
- Break condition: If the environment has clear optimal actions from the start, Dirichlet noise may unnecessarily slow down learning by forcing exploration of clearly suboptimal actions

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Eager playout without Gumbel noise improves decision quality by emphasizing exploitation over exploration
- Mechanism: During test playout, setting Gumbel value to 0 instead of sampling from Gumbel distribution makes action selection deterministic, allowing the agent to fully exploit learned policy rather than explore
- Core assumption: During evaluation/inference, the agent benefits more from exploiting learned knowledge than from maintaining exploration noise that was useful during training
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 7.3]: "Playing out eagerly by setting the Gumbel value to 0 during planning reduces the number of bad decisions. In the exploration-on case, we see an improvement by a factor 2.1 ± 0.3"
  - [section C.4]: "In the limit T → 0 pT is improved by setting the Gumbel value to 0 in planning"
- Break condition: If the model is not sufficiently trained, removing Gumbel noise may cause premature exploitation of poor policies

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Softmax temperature scaling
  - Why needed here: Temperature scaling is used to control exploration vs exploitation in policy selection, with T > 1 for exploration and T → 0 for exploitation
  - Quick check question: What happens to the probability distribution when you increase temperature from T=1 to T=5 in softmax?

- Concept: Off-policy value target correction
  - Why needed here: When using exploration policy that differs from training policy, value targets must be handled carefully to avoid corrupting learning with off-policy rewards
  - Quick check question: Why does the hybrid policy keep initial value for t < tstartNormal but use measured reward for t ≥ tstartNormal?

- Concept: Tree search with Dirichlet noise
  - Why needed here: Dirichlet noise in root node ensures proper exploration of action space by preventing bias toward actions that appear optimal due to limited experience
  - Quick check question: How does adding Dirichlet noise to policy before tree search differ from adding it during tree search?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: What is the optimal exploration temperature strategy that balances exploring new parts of the decision tree while still learning from experienced rewards?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions "a strategy needs to be found on how to best set the exploration level to improve decisions" and uses a fixed exploration temperature in experiments
  - Why unresolved: The paper uses a fixed exploration temperature but doesn't explore dynamic temperature strategies or optimization methods for setting it
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing different temperature scheduling strategies (e.g., decay over time, adaptive based on uncertainty) and their impact on decision quality metrics

- Question: How does the hybrid policy approach perform on more complex games like Go, Chess, or Atari games compared to Tic-Tac-Toe?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges this as a limitation: "It would be interesting to see how the approaches tested here for Tic-Tac-Toe pay off for Go, Chess, Shogi, and the Atari games"
  - Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to Tic-Tac-Toe, and the paper doesn't demonstrate the approach on more complex environments
  - What evidence would resolve it: Applying the hybrid policy approach to Go, Chess, Shogi, and Atari games and comparing performance metrics (win rates, decision quality) against baseline MuZero implementations

- Question: How does the entropy-based intrinsic reward compare to Dirichlet noise in terms of improving exploration and decision quality?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests entropy as an intrinsic reward "could remove the need for Dirichlet noise" and mentions experiments showing Dirichlet noise improves decisions
  - Why unresolved: The paper only tests Dirichlet noise but speculates about entropy rewards without experimental validation
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing models trained with entropy rewards versus Dirichlet noise, measuring both exploration coverage and decision quality metrics

## Limitations

- Results are derived from a single game (Tic-Tac-Toe) and may not generalize to more complex environments
- Exact implementation details of the exploring policy derivation and value target handling are not fully specified, creating potential reproducibility issues
- The claimed improvement factor of 435±190 is based on a relatively small sample size and may have high variance

## Confidence

- High confidence: The core mechanism of using a hybrid policy to explore decision paths that would otherwise be unvisited is well-defined and the theoretical justification is sound
- Medium confidence: The experimental results showing improvement in Tic-Tac-Toe are convincing but limited to a single environment, making generalization uncertain
- Low confidence: The specific implementation details for Dirichlet noise application and the exact temperature values for exploration are not fully specified, creating potential reproducibility issues
