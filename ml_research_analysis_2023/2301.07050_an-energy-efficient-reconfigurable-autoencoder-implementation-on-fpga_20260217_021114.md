# An Energy-Efficient Reconfigurable Autoencoder Implementation on FPGA

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07050
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07050
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: FPGA implementation achieves 80% accuracy and 21.12 GOP/s throughput with 5.93 W power consumption at 100 MHz for image noise reduction

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a convolutional autoencoder implementation on FPGA for image noise reduction, featuring three convolution and pooling layers for encoding and four convolution layers with up-sampling for decoding. The authors compare their FPGA implementation against GPU platforms, demonstrating advantages in energy efficiency and design flexibility. The work targets autonomous robotics applications where noise reduction from camera inputs is critical.

## Method Summary
The authors designed a reconfigurable convolutional autoencoder architecture with three encoding layers (convolution + pooling) and four decoding layers (convolution + up-sampling). The implementation was developed for FPGA and GPU platforms, with performance measured across accuracy, throughput, and power consumption metrics. The FPGA implementation operates at 100 MHz and achieves 21.12 GOP/s throughput while consuming 5.93 W of power.

## Key Results
- FPGA implementation achieves 80% accuracy for image noise reduction
- Throughput of 21.12 GOP/s with power consumption of 5.93 W at 100 MHz
- Demonstrates energy efficiency advantages compared to other FPGA and GPU implementations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The energy efficiency stems from FPGA's ability to implement custom dataflow architectures optimized for convolutional operations, avoiding the overhead of general-purpose processors. The reconfigurable design allows adaptation to different noise patterns and image characteristics, while the deep convolutional structure effectively learns noise reduction patterns through multiple encoding-decoding stages.
