# Training Private Models That Know What They Don't Know

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18393
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18393
- Reference count: 27
- One-line primary result: Differential privacy degrades selective classification performance beyond utility loss by reducing confidence in correct predictions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the interplay between selective classification and differential privacy, revealing that DP-SGD's noise injection not only reduces model utility but also impairs the confidence calibration necessary for effective selective classification. The authors demonstrate that standard selective prediction methods fail under DP due to privacy budget exhaustion, while their proposed Selective Classification Training Dynamics (SCTD) method consistently achieves the best trade-offs. A key contribution is the introduction of an accuracy-normalized evaluation metric that fairly compares selective classification performance across different privacy levels by accounting for the fundamental tension between accuracy alignment and privacy budgets.

## Method Summary
The authors train ResNet-18 models on CIFAR-10, FashionMNIST, SVHN, and GTSRB using DP-SGD with varying privacy budgets (ε = ∞, 7, 3, 1). They evaluate multiple selective classification methods including softmax response, Monte Carlo dropout, and SCTD, which uses intermediate model checkpoints without additional privacy cost due to DP's post-processing property. The evaluation employs a novel accuracy-normalized selective classification score that accounts for the impossibility of aligning accuracy across different privacy levels without violating privacy budgets.

## Key Results
- DP noise reduces confidence in correct predictions and increases confidence in incorrect predictions, preventing performant selective classification
- SCTD consistently outperforms other selective classification methods under DP constraints
- Standard selective classification evaluation metrics are fundamentally flawed under DP due to the impossibility of accuracy alignment across privacy levels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Differential privacy harms selective classification performance beyond loss of model utility.
- Mechanism: DP injects noise into gradient updates, reducing the confidence in correct predictions and increasing confidence in incorrect predictions, especially for minority or outlier data points.
- Core assumption: The noise introduced by DP-SGD affects the model's ability to rank predictions by correctness, not just accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "stronger levels of DP lead to significantly decreased confidence in the correct class... This reduction effectively prevents performant selective classification."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No direct corpus paper confirms this exact DP+SC interaction; must be inferred from general DP effects on model outputs.
- Break condition: If DP noise is reduced below a certain threshold or if selective classification is applied only to high-confidence predictions that are not significantly affected by DP noise.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Selective classification training dynamics (SCTD) is particularly suitable under DP because it leverages intermediate model checkpoints without additional privacy cost.
- Mechanism: SCTD uses only the predictions from a single DP training run at intermediate epochs, avoiding the composition privacy cost of ensemble methods.
- Core assumption: Post-processing of DP outputs does not increase privacy loss, so SCTD's ensemble-like behavior is privacy-safe.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "only a single training run is used to obtain this ensemble. As a result of the post-processing property of DP, the original (ε, δ)-DP guarantee can be maintained."
  - [section 4] Empirical results show SCTD performs best across privacy levels.
- Break condition: If the model checkpoint frequency is too low to capture meaningful disagreement signals, or if DP noise dominates the intermediate predictions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The standard selective classification evaluation metric fails under DP because it assumes accuracy alignment across privacy levels, which is not possible without violating privacy budgets.
- Mechanism: Accuracy alignment via early stopping in DP training results in models with similar (not different) privacy guarantees, invalidating cross-ε comparisons.
- Core assumption: Privacy loss accumulates with training iterations, so stopping early gives stronger privacy than intended.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4] "early-stopping a DP training algorithm in fact gives a model with greater privacy than the targeted privacy level."
  - [section 4] Authors propose a novel accuracy-normalized metric to fix this.
- Break condition: If a new evaluation method is developed that can align accuracy without affecting privacy budgets, or if DP training is modified to allow controlled early stopping without privacy leakage.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Differential Privacy (DP) and DP-SGD
  - Why needed here: The entire paper's analysis hinges on understanding how DP-SGD's noise injection and clipping affect both model utility and selective classification.
  - Quick check question: What are the two main modifications DP-SGD makes to standard SGD, and how do they affect privacy and utility?

- Concept: Selective Classification (SC) and uncertainty quantification
  - Why needed here: SC methods rely on model uncertainty scores to decide whether to accept or reject predictions; understanding these mechanisms is crucial for analyzing their behavior under DP.
  - Quick check question: How does the softmax response (SR) method compute its selection score, and why might it be overconfident under DP?

- Concept: Composition and post-processing properties of DP
  - Why needed here: The paper distinguishes between SC methods that require multiple data accesses (composition) versus those that only process DP outputs (post-processing), which determines their privacy cost.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between sequential composition and the post-processing property in DP, and how does this affect ensemble-based SC methods?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data pipeline → DP-SGD training → Model checkpoints → Selective classification scoring → Evaluation
- Critical path:
  1. Train DP model with checkpointing
  2. Apply selective classification method to generate coverage/accuracy trade-off
  3. Evaluate using accuracy-normalized SC score
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Checkpoint frequency vs. storage/compute cost
  - Noise multiplier vs. privacy budget vs. utility
  - Selection threshold vs. coverage/accuracy balance
- Failure signatures:
  - Low coverage with no accuracy gain → SC method not effective under DP
  - High coverage but low accuracy → SC threshold too low or DP noise overwhelming
  - Evaluation scores inconsistent across ε → Metric not properly normalized
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run DP-SGD with varying ε on a simple dataset, apply SR, plot coverage/accuracy curves.
  2. Implement SCTD, compare its coverage/accuracy trade-off to SR under same DP settings.
  3. Compute accuracy-normalized SC scores for both methods across ε levels to verify metric behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the fundamental theoretical limits of selective classification performance under differential privacy, and how do these limits vary with privacy parameters and dataset characteristics?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states "future work can expand on the intuition provided here to obtain fundamental bounds on selective classification performance in a DP setting" and discusses the need for deeper theoretical analysis.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides empirical results showing degradation of selective classification under DP but does not establish theoretical bounds or analyze how these limits depend on factors like dataset size, dimensionality, or class imbalance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal proofs establishing upper bounds on selective classification accuracy-coverage trade-offs under DP constraints, potentially using tools from information theory or statistical learning theory.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the interplay between differential privacy and selective classification affect fairness across sensitive subgroups, particularly in terms of coverage disparities and accuracy degradation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "we did not carefully investigate fairness implications beyond class imbalance" and references prior work showing both DP and SC can negatively affect sensitive subgroups.
- Why unresolved: The experimental analysis focuses on overall performance metrics rather than subgroup-specific fairness measures, and the paper acknowledges this limitation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies measuring coverage and accuracy disparities across demographic groups under varying DP levels, along with theoretical analysis of how DP composition affects fairness guarantees in selective classification.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can selective classification methods be designed or adapted specifically to mitigate the overconfidence problem in DP models, thereby improving selective classification performance under strong privacy constraints?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that DP increases confidence in incorrect predictions, which prevents effective selective classification, but does not explore whether selective classification methods can be modified to address this issue.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation focuses on applying existing selective classification methods to DP models rather than developing new approaches that explicitly account for DP-induced overconfidence.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of selective classification methods that incorporate DP-specific uncertainty quantification or use alternative confidence measures that are less affected by DP noise.

## Limitations

- Analysis focuses on image classification with ResNet-18 architectures, limiting generalizability to other domains and model architectures
- Mechanism explanations rely on observed correlations rather than establishing causal links between DP noise patterns and selective classification failures
- The proposed accuracy-normalized metric's sensitivity to dataset characteristics remains untested

## Confidence

- **High**: DP-SGD noise reduces model confidence in correct predictions (Section 3.3 empirical observations are direct and reproducible)
- **Medium**: SCTD's superior performance stems from its checkpoint-based ensemble approach without additional privacy cost (mechanism is logical but could have alternative explanations)
- **Medium**: Standard SC evaluation metrics are fundamentally flawed under DP (valid criticism, though alternative metrics may exist)
