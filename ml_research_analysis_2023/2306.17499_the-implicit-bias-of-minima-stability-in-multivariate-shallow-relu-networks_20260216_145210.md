# The Implicit Bias of Minima Stability in Multivariate Shallow ReLU Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17499
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17499
- Reference count: 40
- This work shows that in multivariate shallow ReLU networks, the step size used during stochastic gradient descent (SGD) has a strong effect on the smoothness of the obtained predictor function.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how step size in stochastic gradient descent (SGD) affects the implicit bias of multivariate shallow ReLU networks. The authors prove that larger step sizes bias SGD towards solutions with smoother predictors by favoring minima with small weighted L1 norm of the Laplacian. This extends prior univariate results to the multivariate case and establishes a depth separation between single and two-hidden-layer networks in terms of stable approximation. The work bridges stability analysis with function smoothness, providing theoretical justification for empirical observations about learning rate effects.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze the linear stability of minima in multivariate shallow ReLU networks by examining the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix. They establish a connection between the stability of critical points and the weighted L1 norm of the Laplacian of the learned predictor function. The analysis leverages results from dynamical systems theory and optimization, particularly focusing on how different step sizes during SGD influence which minima are reached. The theoretical framework is validated through experiments on synthetic data and MNIST, measuring both the empirical stability of trained networks and their generalization performance across different learning rates.

## Key Results
- Large step sizes bias SGD towards smoother predictors by favoring minima with small weighted L1 norm of the Laplacian
- Depth separation result: functions that cannot be well-approximated by stable single-hidden-layer networks can be realized as stable two-hidden-layer networks
- Sufficiently smooth functions (in a Sobolev sense) can be arbitrarily well-approximated using stable single-hidden-layer ReLU networks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on the interplay between optimization dynamics and the geometry of the loss landscape. During SGD with large step sizes, the algorithm is more likely to escape unstable minima characterized by high curvature or oscillatory behavior. The stability criterion, measured through the Hessian's eigenvalues, correlates with the smoothness of the resulting predictor function. When the step size is large enough, the optimization trajectory preferentially finds minima where the weighted L1 norm of the Laplacian is small, effectively selecting smoother solutions from the hypothesis space.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input Data -> Shallow ReLU Network -> Loss Function -> SGD Optimizer -> Trained Model -> Stability Analysis

**Critical Path**: The theoretical analysis hinges on connecting the stability of minima (via Hessian eigenvalues) to the smoothness of the predictor (via Laplacian norm). This relationship is established through careful mathematical bounds that depend on the step size parameter.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The paper balances theoretical tractability with practical relevance. While the multivariate analysis is more complex than the univariate case, it reveals that the step size-smoothness relationship holds more generally. However, the analysis assumes idealized conditions (fixed step size, full-batch gradients for stability analysis) that may not fully capture practical training dynamics.

**Failure Signatures**: The theoretical bounds may become loose when: (1) networks are wide enough that the stability condition becomes trivial, (2) the step size is so large that convergence becomes problematic, or (3) the target function lacks sufficient smoothness for the Sobolev approximation results to apply.

## Limitations
- The stability analysis relies on theoretical bounds that may not fully capture real-world training dynamics
- The depth separation result uses an adversarial construction that may not reflect realistic function classes
- The Sobolev smoothness approximation assumes very strong conditions on the target function
- Empirical validation is limited to synthetic data and MNIST, which may not generalize to more complex datasets
