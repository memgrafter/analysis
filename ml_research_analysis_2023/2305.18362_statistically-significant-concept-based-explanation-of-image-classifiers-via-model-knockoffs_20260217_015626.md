# Statistically Significant Concept-based Explanation of Image Classifiers via Model Knockoffs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18362
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18362
- Authors: Not specified in source
- Reference count: 3
- Key outcome: The paper proposes a method combining Knockoff sampling with concept learning to select statistically significant concepts while controlling False Discovery Rate (FDR), effectively reducing false positives in concept-based explanations for image classifiers.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical problem of false positives in concept-based explanations for image classifiers, where unrelated concepts are mistakenly identified as important for predictions. The authors propose a method that combines Knockoff sampling with concept learning to select statistically significant concepts while controlling the False Discovery Rate (FDR). They introduce Concept Sparsity Regularization (CSR) to encourage sparse linear models, improving interpretability. The method works with both supervised and unsupervised concept learning approaches and demonstrates effectiveness on synthetic and real datasets.

## Method Summary
The proposed method learns image concepts using deep learning models and then applies Knockoff sampling to select important concepts for prediction while controlling FDR. The approach involves two main components: (1) concept learning using either supervised methods like Concept Bottleneck Models or unsupervised methods like Variational Autoencoders, and (2) Knockoff filter-based selection of statistically significant concepts. The authors introduce Concept Sparsity Regularization (CSR) to encourage sparse linear models by adding an L1 penalty on the coefficients, which improves interpretability by reducing the number of selected concepts. The method uses Model-X Knockoff generation with FDR control at a prescribed level q.

## Key Results
- The method effectively controls FDR at the prescribed level (q=0.1) while maintaining high power in selecting truly important concepts
- CSR regularization successfully reduces the number of selected concepts without sacrificing prediction accuracy
- The approach works with both supervised (CBM) and unsupervised (VAE) concept learning methods, showing consistent performance across different learning paradigms

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Knockoff filter can control the False Discovery Rate (FDR) under a prescribed level when applied to concept selection for image classifiers.
- Mechanism: The method generates synthetic "knockoff" concepts that mimic the statistical properties of real concepts but are independent of class labels. By comparing importance statistics of real concepts versus their knockoffs, the method identifies truly important concepts while controlling FDR through a thresholding procedure.
- Core assumption: The relationship between concepts and class labels follows a linear model, and knockoff samples satisfy the exchangeability property with original concepts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our goal is to find the statistically significant concept for classification to prevent misinterpretation... using the Knockoff samples to select the important concepts for prediction by controlling the False Discovery Rate (FDR) under a certain value."
  - [section 3.1]: "Theorem 1. [Candes et al., 2018] Given Y and X following the linear model... the FDR of Ŝ is controlled as the prescribed level, i.e., E[|Ŝ ∩ H0|/max(1, |Ŝ|)] ≤ q."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Concept Sparsity Regularization (CSR) improves interpretability by encouraging sparse linear models in concept-based classifiers.
- Mechanism: CSR adds an L1 penalty on coefficients of the linear classifier mapping concepts to labels. This encourages the model to use fewer concepts for prediction, making selected concepts more interpretable and reducing false positives.
- Core assumption: A sparse model using fewer concepts can achieve comparable prediction accuracy to a dense model, and L1 regularization will effectively promote sparsity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "They introduce Concept Sparsity Regularization (CSR) to encourage sparse linear models, improving interpretability."
  - [section 4]: "From the interpretability perspective, we want to describe the response variable with a relatively small number of concepts... we introduce a concept learning algorithm so that the linear model trained with the resulting concepts (features) becomes sparse, that is, βϕ is sparse."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The method works with both supervised and unsupervised concept learning approaches.
- Mechanism: The framework is designed to be modular, accepting concept embeddings from either supervised methods (like Concept Bottleneck Models) or unsupervised methods (like Variational Autoencoders). The knockoff selection procedure operates on resulting concept embeddings regardless of how they were learned.
- Core assumption: Both supervised and unsupervised concept learning methods can produce meaningful, interpretable concepts that capture relevant information for the classification task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The method works with both supervised and unsupervised concept learning approaches."
  - [section 2.1]: "We introduce two settings of the concept-based classification, unsupervised and supervised concept-based explanation."

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the CSR regularization perform in high-dimensional concept spaces with complex dependencies between concepts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions CSR helps suppress false positives but doesn't extensively analyze its behavior in high-dimensional spaces.
- Why unresolved: The experiments primarily focus on moderate-dimensional concept spaces; performance in extremely high-dimensional settings with complex concept dependencies remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments comparing CSR performance across varying concept dimensions and dependency structures, particularly in scenarios where concepts have intricate non-linear relationships.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical guarantee of FDR control when combining CSR with Knockoff in cases where the underlying data distribution deviates significantly from the assumptions of the Knockoff method?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proves FDR control for Model-X Knockoff under certain assumptions, but the interaction between CSR and Knockoff in non-ideal distributions is not fully explored.
- Why unresolved: The theoretical analysis focuses on standard Knockoff assumptions without addressing how CSR regularization might affect the validity of these assumptions in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal theoretical analysis or extensive empirical studies examining FDR control in scenarios where the data distribution significantly violates Knockoff assumptions, both with and without CSR.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of hyperparameters (α values in CSR) affect the trade-off between interpretability, FDR control, and prediction accuracy in real-world applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses hyperparameter effects on concept selection rate and accuracy but doesn't provide a systematic framework for choosing optimal hyperparameters.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates hyperparameter effects, it lacks a principled method for selecting hyperparameters that balance all three objectives in practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive sensitivity analysis or automated hyperparameter selection method that optimally balances interpretability, FDR control, and prediction accuracy across diverse real-world datasets.

## Limitations
- The method assumes linear relationships between concepts and labels, which may not hold in complex real-world scenarios
- Effectiveness of knockoff generation depends on accurate estimation of concept distribution, which can be challenging in high-dimensional spaces
- CSR regularization may reduce prediction accuracy if the underlying task genuinely requires many concepts

## Confidence
- High confidence: The theoretical foundation of FDR control using knockoffs is well-established (Theorem 1 reference to Candes et al., 2018)
- Medium confidence: The experimental results on synthetic and real datasets demonstrate the method's effectiveness, but the sample sizes and hyperparameter settings are not fully specified
- Medium confidence: The claim that the method works with both supervised and unsupervised concept learning approaches is supported by the paper's methodology but lacks extensive empirical validation across diverse concept learning methods
