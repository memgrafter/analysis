# User Adaptive Language Learning Chatbots with a Curriculum

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05489
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05489
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Multi-turns grid beam search method improves curriculum-aligned vocabulary usage in language learning chatbots, with 39 out of 267 incorrect answers corrected and 21 out of 155 students improving test scores

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a multi-turns grid beam search method for lexically constrained decoding in language learning chatbots. The approach forces chatbots to include curriculum-aligned words and phrases during generation by using a dynamic constraint threshold that increases with dialog turns. Evaluated with 155 middle school students learning English as a second language, the chatbot demonstrated improved understanding of target vocabulary, increased student interest in practicing English, and encouraged more frequent use of target words in both user utterances and system responses.

## Method Summary
The method adapts BlenderBot-3B with a custom constrained decoder using multi-turn grid beam search. A dynamic constraint threshold T = T0 * 2^(a*t) starts low for early turn flexibility and increases exponentially to ensure constraint coverage before dialog ends. Constraint words are removed from the list after use to prevent redundancy while maintaining attention-based reuse. The system uses a two-beam structure (satisfied/unsatisfied) to distribute constraint usage across dialog turns naturally. Curriculum words from textbooks are appended during decoding, and the dynamic constraint list updates throughout conversation.

## Key Results
- 39 out of 267 incorrect answers were corrected after speaking with the chatbot
- 21 out of 155 students improved their overall test scores
- System and users used words for "season" more frequently than other constraint types, demonstrating user adoption of target vocabulary

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dynamic constraint threshold allows early flexibility and later enforcement of target words.
- Mechanism: Threshold T starts low and increases exponentially with dialog turn number, computed as `T = T0 * 2^(a*t)` where t is turn number and a is a scaling factor.
- Core assumption: Increasing pressure to include constraints over time prevents unnatural early generation while ensuring coverage before dialog ends.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] mentions "dynamic constraint threshold" to "force our model to generate constraint words before a dialog ends"
  - [section 3.1] provides the exact formula and reasoning for why early turns need more flexibility
- Break condition: If the threshold grows too quickly relative to the language model's fluency capacity, early turns may become stilted or unnatural.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Constraint word removal after use prevents redundant generation while maintaining attention-based reuse.
- Mechanism: After each turn, the system checks if any constraint words were used and removes them from the constraint list for subsequent turns.
- Core assumption: Once a word is used, forcing its repetition is unnecessary because attention mechanisms will still encourage reuse.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] describes this "dynamic constraint list" where words are "removed from the constraint list" after being used
  - [section 4.2] shows that system usage frequency is less than 100%, suggesting words are removed after use
- Break condition: If attention mechanisms fail to maintain word usage after removal, students may not get sufficient practice with target vocabulary.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-turns approach distributes constraint usage across dialog turns for natural conversation flow.
- Mechanism: Instead of generating all constraints in one turn, the system targets one constraint per turn using a simplified beam structure with two beams (satisfied/unsatisfied).
- Core assumption: Natural conversation requires gradual introduction of vocabulary rather than front-loading all constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] explains this as "we do not require systems to generate all constraint words in one single turn" and describes the two-beam approach
  - [section 4.2] shows constraint usage spreads across "seasons, months, or days" rather than concentrating in early turns
- Break condition: If dialog length is too short relative to constraint count, some constraints may never be naturally introduced.

## Limitations
- Small sample size of 155 students with short-term intervention limits generalizability
- Lack of control group makes it difficult to isolate chatbot effect from testing effect
- Constraint word selection limited to seasons, months, and days restricts generalizability to broader vocabulary

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Technical mechanism of multi-turns grid beam search with dynamic constraint thresholds is well-defined and theoretically sound. Claim that forcing system constraint word generation increases user constraint word usage is directly supported by frequency analysis data.
- Medium Confidence: Claims about improved understanding and increased interest rely on short-term test scores and survey responses without control for testing effects or long-term retention data.
- Low Confidence: Generalizability to broader vocabulary domains beyond seasonal/time-related words is not empirically supported by the study's narrow constraint type focus.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a randomized controlled trial with chatbot group and matched control group receiving traditional instruction, with pre/post tests administered blind to treatment condition to isolate chatbot effect from testing effects.

2. Implement a 3-month follow-up assessment to measure vocabulary retention and sustained improvement in test scores, addressing the current limitation of short-term evaluation.

3. Test the multi-turns grid beam search approach with more diverse and complex constraint types including grammar structures, idioms, and academic vocabulary to validate generalizability beyond the current seasonal/time-related vocabulary focus.
