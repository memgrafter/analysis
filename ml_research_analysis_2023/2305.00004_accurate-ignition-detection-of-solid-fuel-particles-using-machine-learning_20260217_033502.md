# Accurate ignition detection of solid fuel particles using machine learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00004
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00004
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: ResNet-18 with 35% training data provides most accurate ignition detection, outperforming traditional SAS method

## Executive Summary
This work presents a machine learning approach for accurate ignition detection of solid fuel particles using high-speed optical measurements. The study visualizes coal particle ignition in a laminar flow reactor at 10 kHz using OH-LIF and DBI measurements under both air and oxy-fuel conditions. Traditional threshold-based methods showed significant deviations from manually labeled ground truth, prompting the development of deep learning architectures. The ResNet-18 classification model demonstrated superior performance with only 35% of available data for training, highlighting the effectiveness of hierarchical feature extraction in convolutional networks for combustion diagnostics.

## Method Summary
The research employs high-speed optical measurements (10 kHz) of coal particle ignition using OH-LIF and DBI techniques in a laminar flow reactor. Ignition delay times were first evaluated using a threshold-based signal and structure (SAS) method, then compared against manually labeled ground truth data. Two deep learning architectures were developed: ResNet (classification) and FPN (detection), both trained on the ground truth labels. The models were evaluated across different particle sizes and combustion conditions (air vs oxy-fuel), with ResNet-18 demonstrating the most accurate and precise ignition detection capabilities.

## Key Results
- ResNet-18 with 35% training data provided most accurate ignition detection
- ResNet-18 outperformed traditional SAS method significantly
- Method showed robustness across air and oxy-fuel combustion conditions
- Hierarchical feature extraction in convolutional networks enables accurate ignition detection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical feature extraction capability of convolutional neural networks allows for multi-scale analysis of combustion dynamics. The ResNet architecture specifically leverages residual connections to preserve spatial information across layers, enabling the network to identify subtle ignition signatures that threshold-based methods miss. The optical measurement at 10 kHz provides sufficient temporal resolution to capture the rapid changes during ignition, while the combination of OH-LIF and DBI measurements provides complementary chemical and structural information.
