# Leveraging User-Triggered Supervision in Contextual Bandits

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03784
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03784
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper studies a novel contextual bandit setting with biased, user-triggered supervised feedback, establishing O(T^{2/3}) regret lower bounds and developing algorithms to achieve them, with O(√T) regret possible under favorable problem structures.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a new contextual bandit framework where users occasionally provide supervised feedback on the optimal action, but only in a subset of rounds. The feedback is biased and not under the algorithm's control, presenting a unique challenge. The authors develop a constrained optimization framework that balances reward maximization with fidelity to the supervised signals. They establish fundamental lower bounds showing O(T^{2/3}) regret is unavoidable, and develop algorithms including an explore-first approach (EFBO) and an Exp4-based method that can achieve O(√T) regret under favorable conditions.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a constrained optimization framework where the learner seeks to maximize CB reward while maintaining fidelity to supervised signals. The key algorithms include: (1) an explore-first algorithm (EFBO) that performs initial exploration to gather data for both rewards and constraints, then optimizes a blended objective leveraging any alignment between them; (2) an Exp4-based algorithm that incorporates supervised feedback into the reward signal and maintains a set of approximately feasible policies, achieving O(√T) regret under favorable problem structures such as when feedback is triggered by suboptimal actions.

## Key Results
- Establishes O(T^{2/3}) regret lower bounds for both reward and constraint violation in the user-triggered supervision setting
- Develops EFBO algorithm that blends rewards and constraints to leverage alignment between them
- Presents Exp4-based algorithm achieving O(√T) regret under favorable problem structures (e.g., when feedback is withheld for near-optimal actions)
- Provides comprehensive analysis of trade-offs between exploration, exploitation, and constraint satisfaction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The constrained optimization framework allows the learner to balance reward maximization with fidelity to supervised signals, even when the latter is biased and user-triggered.
- Mechanism: By treating supervised feedback as a constraint in an optimization problem, the algorithm can leverage these signals without being overly influenced by their potential bias. The constraint ensures reasonable fidelity while still optimizing for rewards.
- Core assumption: The user's feedback, when provided, is informative about the optimal action and not systematically misleading.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We develop a new framework to leverage such signals, while being robust to their biased nature."
  - [section] "To overcome this issue, we develop a constrained optimization framework, where the learner seeks to optimize its CB reward while also trying to do well under the expected supervised learning error."
- Break condition: If the user's feedback is systematically misleading or if supervised signals are too sparse to provide meaningful constraints.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The explore-first algorithm (EFBO) achieves balance between exploration for rewards and constraints by blending rewards and constraints in a way that leverages any alignment between them.
- Mechanism: EFBO performs initial exploration to gather data for both rewards and constraints, then optimizes a blended objective that combines both sources of information, allowing it to take advantage of any correlation between rewards and supervised feedback.
- Core assumption: There is some alignment between the reward distribution and the distribution of supervised feedback.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "They develop an explore-first algorithm (EFBO) that blends rewards and constraints in a way that leverages any alignment between them."
  - [section] "Since we expect practical settings to be somewhere between these two extremes, we leverage ideas from Zhang et al. (2019) to take advantage of any available (unknown) alignment between the rewards and the constraints."
- Break condition: If there is no meaningful alignment between rewards and supervised feedback, or if alignment is so strong that one signal dominates the other.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The Exp4-based algorithm can achieve better regret bounds under favorable problem structures, such as when the user's feedback is triggered by suboptimal actions or when active learning can be used to estimate optimal actions.
- Mechanism: By incorporating supervised feedback into the reward signal and maintaining a set of approximately feasible policies, the Exp4-based algorithm can leverage the structure of when the user provides feedback to improve its learning.
- Core assumption: The user's feedback is more likely to be provided for actions that are significantly suboptimal under the true reward function.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "To achieve this bound, they develop an explore-first algorithm (EFBO) that blends rewards and constraints in a way that leverages any alignment between them. They also present an Exp4-based algorithm that can achieve O(√T) regret under favorable problem structures..."
  - [section] "We develop an Exp4-based algorithm that can benefit from favorable conditions on the user, such as feedback from the user is only withheld if the selected action has small supervised learning error."
- Break condition: If the user's feedback is not correlated with the suboptimality of the chosen action, or if active learning is not effective in estimating optimal actions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Context space X and action space [K] -> Reward distribution Db -> Supervised feedback distribution -> Constrained optimization framework -> Explore-first algorithm (EFBO) -> Exp4-based algorithm -> Active learning component

- Critical path:
  1. Receive context xt
  2. Choose action at based on current policy
  3. Observe reward rt or supervised feedback ¯a(xt)
  4. Update estimators for rewards and constraints
  5. Optimize policy based on blended objective
  6. Repeat until convergence or time limit

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: Balancing the need to gather data with the desire to maximize rewards
  - Fidelity to supervised signals vs. reward maximization: Determining the appropriate weight for the constraint in the optimization
  - Complexity of the algorithm vs. regret bounds: More complex algorithms may achieve better theoretical guarantees but be harder to implement and tune

- Failure signatures:
  - High regret on rewards: The algorithm may be overly conservative in its exploration or too focused on the supervised signals
  - High constraint violation: The algorithm may be ignoring the supervised feedback or the estimators for the constraint may be biased
  - Slow convergence: The algorithm may be exploring too much or the problem structure may not be favorable for the chosen algorithm

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Synthetic data with known alignment between rewards and supervised feedback: Test the ability of EFBO to leverage this alignment
  2. Synthetic data with no alignment between rewards and supervised feedback: Test the robustness of the algorithm when supervised signals are not informative
  3. Real-world data from a recommendation system: Test the algorithm's performance in a practical setting and evaluate the quality of the learned policy

## Limitations

- The algorithms assume the user's feedback is informative about the optimal action, but this may not always hold in practice
- The explore-first strategy used by EFBO may lead to high regret in initial rounds, especially if rapid adaptation to changing contexts is required
- The paper focuses on the case where user feedback is unbiased, but practical scenarios may involve difficult-to-account-for biases

## Confidence

- High: The constrained optimization framework provides a principled approach to balancing rewards and supervised feedback, and the theoretical analysis of regret bounds is sound.
- Medium: The explore-first algorithm (EFBO) is a practical approach that can leverage alignment between rewards and supervised feedback, but its performance may be sensitive to the choice of parameters and problem structure.
- Low: The Exp4-based algorithm's improved regret bounds under favorable problem structures are promising, but the conditions under which these structures hold may be difficult to verify in practice.
