# PITS: Variational Pitch Inference without Fundamental Frequency for End-to-End Pitch-controllable TTS

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12391
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12391
- Reference count: 0
- Best architecture achieves MOS of 4.01 and EER of 0.926

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PITS, a variational pitch inference method for end-to-end pitch-controllable text-to-speech synthesis. PITS leverages variational inference over Yingram latent space to model pitch information without directly modeling fundamental frequency, avoiding the low variance issues common in pitch-controlled speech. The system incorporates Yingram encoding/decoding, adversarial training of pitch-shifted synthesis, and optional Q-VAE components based on the VITS architecture. Experimental results demonstrate that PITS generates high-quality speech indistinguishable from ground truth while maintaining strong pitch controllability without quality degradation.

## Method Summary
PITS extends VITS with a variational pitch inference framework that avoids direct fundamental frequency modeling. The method encodes speech into Yingram features, applies variational inference to model pitch as a latent distribution, and uses scope-shifting of latent variables for pitch control. The architecture includes STFT and Yingram encoders, a decoder, normalizing flows for duration prediction, and discriminators for adversarial training. Pitch-shifted synthesis is trained with adversarial loss to improve naturalness of pitch-controlled outputs. The system can optionally incorporate Q-VAE on STFT encoder outputs to improve disentanglement between linguistic and pitch information.

## Key Results
- PITS (A+D) achieves MOS of 4.01, comparable to ground truth speech
- EER of 0.926 demonstrates strong pitch controllability without speaker identity degradation
- Q-VAE degrades quality and intelligibility, increasing CER significantly
- Adversarial pitch-shifted loss improves naturalness of pitch-shifted synthesis without affecting normal speech quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Variational inference over Yingram latent space decouples pitch from linguistic content, avoiding direct fundamental frequency modeling
- Mechanism: PITS encodes Yingram features into a variational posterior distribution, then shifts this distribution's channel scope during training. The decoder reconstructs the shifted Yingram, enabling pitch control without modeling f0 directly
- Core assumption: The Yingram representation contains pitch-related information (including harmonics) that can be isolated from linguistic content when using a variational latent space

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adversarial training on pitch-shifted waveforms enforces perceptual pitch control without degrading naturalness
- Mechanism: During training, PITS synthesizes pitch-shifted waveforms and applies least-squares adversarial loss against real paired waveforms. This encourages the model to generate plausible pitch-shifted speech
- Core assumption: Paired adversarial training between pitch-shifted synthesized and real waveforms provides reliable feedback for pitch controllability

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text -> STFT encoder -> Q-VAE (optional) -> concatenate with Yingram encoder output -> normalizing flow -> decoder -> waveform output
- Critical path: Text → STFT encoder → Q-VAE → concatenate → normalizing flow → decoder → waveform output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Q-VAE improves disentanglement but causes training instability and quality degradation (as observed in experiments)
  - Yingram decoding loss enforces translation equivariance but requires careful scope shifting
  - Adversarial pitch-shifted loss improves controllability but adds training complexity
- Failure signatures:
  - CER increase indicates linguistic information leakage into pitch space
  - EER increase suggests pitch control affects speaker identity
  - Mel-spectrogram reconstruction loss increase indicates decoder struggles with shifted pitch

## Limitations
- Q-VAE component introduces training instability with diverging codebook and commit losses
- Effectiveness of adversarial pitch-shifted training relies on paired discriminators that may not reliably distinguish pitch-shifted speech
- Yingram representation's ability to fully capture pitch-related information without linguistic leakage is assumed but not rigorously proven
- Architectural complexity increases training difficulty and may limit scalability

## Confidence
- Pitch controllability mechanism (High): The scope-shift approach and experimental EER results provide strong evidence
- Quality preservation (Medium): MOS scores are competitive but not substantially higher than baselines
- Disentanglement effectiveness (Low): Q-VAE shows theoretical promise but practical implementation issues suggest limited effectiveness
- Adversarial training stability (Medium): While included, the specific impact on pitch control versus overall stability is not clearly delineated
