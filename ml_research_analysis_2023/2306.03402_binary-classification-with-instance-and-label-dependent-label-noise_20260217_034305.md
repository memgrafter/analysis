## Executive Summary
This paper studies binary classification under instance- and label-dependent label noise (ILN), where the probability of label flipping varies with both the feature instance and the true label. The key challenge is that the noise rate is unknown and instance-dependent, making accurate estimation difficult. The authors provide a novel theoretical analysis by deriving matching upper and lower bounds for the excess risk of empirical risk minimization (ERM). They show that without additional assumptions, ERM achieves an excess risk bound proportional to the noise level ρ, with an irreducible error term of 3Cρ/2. They complement this with a minimax lower bound showing that the 0-1 risk is at least ρ/16, implying this bound is tight.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes binary classification under ILN by deriving risk bounds for empirical risk minimization (ERM) using noisy samples. The analysis applies to general loss functions satisfying mild boundedness assumptions and covers linear and non-parametric settings. The method involves comparing clean and noisy estimators, deriving excess risk bounds, and establishing a minimax lower bound using Fano's method to show the fundamental impossibility of learning with ILN without additional assumptions.

## Key Results
- ERM on noisy samples achieves optimal excess risk bound under ILN without additional assumptions
- Excess risk bound decomposes into irreducible error term (3Cρ/2) and estimation error term (2Gδ(n))
- Minimax lower bound of 0-1 risk ≥ ρ/16 establishes fundamental impossibility of learning with ILN
- Results apply to general loss functions with boundedness assumptions and cover both linear and non-parametric settings

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Is there an approach, apart from ERM, that can remove the irreducible error term 3Cρ in Corollary 3.1 while relying exclusively on noisy samples?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors explicitly pose this question in the discussion following Corollary 3.1, noting that Menon et al. (2018) showed it's possible to learn η(x) through minimizing empirical risk with purely instance-dependent noise, but asking whether this is possible under the more general ILN model.
- Why unresolved: The authors address this by studying the minimax risk lower bound in Section 4, showing that learning with ILN is not possible under typical assumptions, but they don't provide an alternative approach that removes the irreducible error.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proposed algorithm that achieves excess risk bound better than 3Cρ + 4Gδ(n) without access to clean samples or additional assumptions beyond those typically made in the CCN model.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What additional assumptions are required to make learning with ILN possible, and what justification exists for using clean samples?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors conclude that their findings validate works on the ILN model that assumed stronger assumptions and used human experts to clean noisy samples, suggesting this is an open direction for future research.
- Why unresolved: While the authors show that learning with ILN is not possible under typical assumptions (margin assumption and anchor point assumption), they don't identify what specific additional assumptions would make learning possible.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal analysis showing that under specific additional assumptions (e.g., bounded noise rates, specific structure in the noise functions, or assumptions about the distribution), learning with ILN becomes possible, along with empirical validation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the upper bound in Theorem 1 be improved for specific hypothesis classes or loss functions beyond what's shown in Examples 3.1 and 3.2?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors provide applications of their results to linear hypothesis classes and RKHS, but note that their theoretical findings can be applied to a wide range of settings, suggesting potential for tighter bounds in specific cases.
- Why unresolved: The examples given use general bounds that may not be tight for specific hypothesis classes or loss functions commonly used in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Sharper upper bounds for specific hypothesis classes (e.g., neural networks, decision trees) or loss functions (e.g., exponential loss, logistic loss with specific regularization) that improve upon the general bound 3Cρ + 2Gδ(n).

## Limitations

- Theoretical results rely heavily on Assumption 3.1 (bounded loss difference), which may exclude some commonly used loss functions
- Minimax lower bound requires specific distributional assumptions that may not hold in all real-world scenarios
- Analysis focuses on optimality of ERM without considering more sophisticated methods that could potentially perform better under ILN

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The optimality of ERM under ILN (Theorem 1 and Corollary 3.1). The proof structure is rigorous and follows standard statistical learning theory arguments.

**Medium Confidence**: The impossibility result (Theorem 2). While the Fano method argument is sound, the specific distributional assumptions required may limit practical applicability.

**Low Confidence**: The generalizability of results to non-bounded loss functions and infinite hypothesis classes. The paper's assumptions are crucial for the theoretical guarantees.
