# Stability Analysis of Sharpness-Aware Minimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06308
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06308
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: This paper analyzes stability issues in Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), identifying convergence instability near saddle points and proving that saddle points can become attractors under SAM dynamics.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive stability analysis of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), revealing critical convergence issues near saddle points. The authors demonstrate that SAM's gradient oscillation mechanism can cause optimization to become stuck when perturbed weights fall into different basins of attraction. Through theoretical analysis and empirical validation, they show that saddle points can become stable attractors under certain conditions, and that SAM escapes saddle points more slowly than SGD due to unfavorable diffusion properties.

## Method Summary
The study employs both theoretical analysis and empirical experiments to investigate SAM's stability properties. Theoretical components include dynamical systems analysis to prove saddle point attraction conditions, and stochastic differential equation modeling to compare SAM's diffusion properties with SGD. Empirical experiments are conducted on the Beale function (2D optimization), synthetic neural networks with quadratic activation, and CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 benchmarks. The analysis systematically varies perturbation radius ρ, batch size, and momentum to characterize their effects on convergence and generalization.

## Key Results
- SAM dynamics exhibit convergence instability near saddle points due to gradient oscillation between basins of attraction
- Theoretical proof that saddle points can become attractors under SAM dynamics when ρ ≥ -1/λ₁ (first negative eigenvalue)
- SAM escapes saddle points more slowly than SGD, with smaller mean squared displacement in parameter space
- Momentum and smaller batch sizes significantly mitigate SAM's convergence issues and improve generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SAM dynamics can cause convergence instability near saddle points due to gradient oscillation.
- Mechanism: When SAM updates parameters, it computes a perturbed weight using the current gradient. Near a saddle point, this perturbed weight can fall into a different basin of attraction than the original weight, causing the optimization to oscillate between basins and become stuck.
- Core assumption: The loss landscape has multiple basins separated by saddle points, and the SAM update rule can push the perturbed weight across basin boundaries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we demonstrate that SAM dynamics can have convergence instability that occurs near a saddle point"
  - [section] "SAM can be hindered by the saddle point d when the perturbed weight wp falls into a different basin of attraction across a basin boundary (Case-III)"

- Break condition: If the perturbation radius ρ is sufficiently small or if the learning rate is adjusted to prevent the perturbed weight from crossing basin boundaries.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Saddle points can become attractors under SAM dynamics.
- Mechanism: For certain values of the perturbation radius ρ, the eigenvalues of the Hessian at a saddle point become positive when combined with ρ, causing the saddle point to become a stable equilibrium point for SAM dynamics.
- Core assumption: The perturbation radius ρ is large enough to make the condition λj + ρλ²j ≥ 0 hold for all negative eigenvalues λj at the saddle point.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we theoretically prove that the saddle point can become an attractor under SAM dynamics"
  - [section] "Theorem 1. Let d be an index-one saddle point... Then, the saddle point d is an attractor of SAM dynamics in (5) if ρ ≥ −1/λ1"

- Break condition: If the perturbation radius ρ is sufficiently small such that λj + ρλ²j < 0 for at least one negative eigenvalue λj.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SAM escapes saddle points more slowly than SGD due to diffusion properties.
- Mechanism: The mean squared displacement of SAM parameters near a saddle point is smaller than that of SGD, indicating that SAM requires more time to escape saddle points.
- Core assumption: The loss function can be approximated by a second-order Taylor expansion near the saddle point, and the diffusion matrix can be approximated using the Fisher Information Matrix.
- Evidence anchors:

- Break condition: If the batch size is sufficiently small or if momentum is sufficiently large to counteract the slower diffusion.

## Limitations

- The theoretical claims about saddle point attraction rely heavily on assumptions about index-one saddle points with specific eigenvalue structures
- Empirical validation demonstrates performance differences but doesn't conclusively prove the proposed mechanisms are solely responsible
- Diffusion analysis assumes small perturbations and may not hold for larger learning rates or non-quadratic loss landscapes

## Confidence
