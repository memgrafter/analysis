# A Semi-supervised Approach for Activity Recognition from Indoor Trajectory Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03134
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03134
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: High classification accuracy with F-scores ranging from 0.81 to 0.95 using minimal labeled data

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a semi-supervised approach for recognizing human activities from noisy indoor trajectory data in manufacturing settings. The method combines Minimum Description Length (MDL) segmentation, constrained hierarchical clustering with Hausdorff distance, and CNN-based classification. By requiring only a small proportion of manually labeled trajectory segments, the approach addresses the challenge of labor-intensive annotation while achieving high accuracy in classifying four types of manufacturing activities.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of three main stages: First, trajectories are partitioned into segments using MDL to identify points where activity changes occur. Second, constrained hierarchical clustering groups similar segments while respecting cannot-link constraints between manually labeled seeds of different classes, generating pseudo labels. Finally, a CNN model is trained on all segments with their pseudo labels to classify activities. The method is evaluated on trajectory data collected from workers performing tricycle assembly tasks.

## Key Results
- Achieved F-scores between 0.81 and 0.95 across different worker trajectories
- Successfully classified four manufacturing activities (WALKING, PARTS_TRANSFER, ASSEMBLING, ADJUSTING) with minimal labeled data
- Demonstrated the effectiveness of combining MDL segmentation with constrained clustering and CNN classification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MDL-based trajectory partitioning balances precision and conciseness to identify activity change points in noisy indoor data.
- Mechanism: MDL selects characteristic points where the cost of encoding the trajectory as segments plus the deviation from original points is minimized, thereby identifying natural boundaries between different activity types.
- Core assumption: Activity homogeneity within segments and rapid statistical changes at transition points.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "first applies an information theoretic criterion to partition a long trajectory into a set of segments such that the object exhibits homogeneous behaviour within each segment"
  - [section] "MDL principle identifies the characteristics points of the trajectories by finding the optimal trade -off between the preciseness and conciseness properties"
- Break condition: If trajectory noise overwhelms the statistical signal of activity changes, MDL may select too few or too many partitions, reducing classification accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Constrained hierarchical clustering with Hausdorff distance generates pseudo labels without merging seeds of different classes.
- Mechanism: By enforcing cannot-link constraints between manually labeled seed segments, the algorithm builds clusters that respect known activity boundaries while using undirected Hausdorff distance to measure segment similarity.
- Core assumption: Seeds are representative samples of their activity classes and Hausdorff distance captures meaningful segment similarity for this application.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we impose cannot-link constraints on the elements of 𝑆𝐿 to ensure that no more than one seed should be present in any cluster"
  - [section] "we apply the Hausdorff distance to compute the distance between pairs of trajectory segments"
- Break condition: If seeds are poorly chosen or too few, pseudo labels may be inaccurate, leading to degraded CNN performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CNNs trained on trajectory segments with pseudo labels can accurately classify activities with minimal labeled data.
- Mechanism: The convolutional layers automatically learn spatial-temporal patterns in trajectory segments, while pooling layers provide translation invariance and noise tolerance.
- Core assumption: The learned features from trajectory segments are discriminative enough to separate the four activity classes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a deep learnin g classification model based on convolutional neural networks is trained on trajectory segments and the generated pseudo labels"
  - [section] "The CNNs used in this paper consist of multiple convolutional and pooling layers"
- Break condition: If the trajectory segments lack sufficient discriminative features or the CNN architecture is too simple, classification accuracy will plateau below acceptable thresholds.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle
  - Why needed here: MDL provides a principled way to partition noisy trajectories by balancing model complexity (number of segments) against data fidelity (fit to original points)
  - Quick check question: If you increase the number of segments in MDL, what happens to the description length of the hypothesis versus the data encoded with the hypothesis?

- Concept: Hausdorff distance for trajectory similarity
  - Why needed here: Standard Euclidean distance fails for trajectory segments of different lengths; Hausdorff distance measures maximum deviation between two sets of points, capturing shape similarity
  - Quick check question: Why is directed Hausdorff distance insufficient for clustering trajectory segments, and how does the undirected version address this?

- Concept: Semi-supervised learning via pseudo-labeling
  - Why needed here: Manual labeling of all trajectory segments is impractical; using a small set of labeled seeds to generate pseudo labels for the majority of data enables training without extensive human annotation
  - Quick check question: What is the risk of propagating errors from pseudo labels to the final CNN model, and how does the paper mitigate this risk?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing → Trajectory segmentation (MDL) → Constrained hierarchical clustering → Pseudo label generation → CNN training → Activity classification
- Critical path: Trajectory segmentation → Clustering → CNN training. Each step must succeed for the final classifier to work.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - MDL partitioning vs. fixed-length windows: MDL adapts to activity boundaries but is computationally heavier
  - Hausdorff vs. DTW distance: Hausdorff is faster but may miss temporal misalignments that DTW captures
  - Number of seeds vs. clustering quality: More seeds improve pseudo label accuracy but reduce semi-supervised benefit
- Failure signatures:
  - MDL produces too many/few segments → Check MDL formulation parameters (perpendicular and angular distance weights)
  - Clustering merges different activities → Verify cannot-link constraints and Hausdorff distance implementation
  - CNN overfits → Increase dropout rate or add more regularization
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run MDL partitioning on a single trajectory and visualize identified change points vs. ground truth to validate segmentation quality
  2. Perform constrained hierarchical clustering with varying numbers of seeds (5%, 10%, 20%) and measure pseudo label accuracy
  3. Train a simple CNN (1-2 conv layers) on one trajectory's segments with pseudo labels and evaluate F-score on held-out test set

## Limitations
- Results validated on single manufacturing environment with four activity types, limiting generalizability
- Assumes relatively clean activity transitions that may not hold in more chaotic environments
- Does not address potential drift in worker behavior over time or varying skill levels

## Confidence
- Trajectory segmentation via MDL: High confidence
- Constrained clustering approach: Medium confidence
- CNN classification performance: Medium confidence
- Generalization to other environments: Low confidence

## Next Checks
1. Test the complete pipeline on at least three diverse manufacturing datasets with different activity types and noise characteristics
2. Compare Hausdorff distance against DTW and other trajectory similarity metrics for clustering quality
3. Evaluate model performance with different seed proportions (1%, 5%, 10%) to quantify the trade-off between manual labeling effort and classification accuracy
