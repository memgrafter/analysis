# Emphasizing Unseen Words: New Vocabulary Acquisition for End-to-End Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09723
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09723
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Word-level loss rescaling with EWC achieves 45.81% OOV recall with 7.8% WER increase on LibriSpeech

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of recognizing out-of-vocabulary (OOV) words in end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems. The authors propose loss rescaling techniques that emphasize OOV words during fine-tuning, applying higher gradient scaling to nodes corresponding to OOV words in the CTC decoding lattice. The approach is combined with model regularization to prevent catastrophic forgetting of existing vocabulary. Experiments on LibriSpeech demonstrate significant improvements in OOV word recall while maintaining reasonable word error rate degradation.

## Method Summary
The proposed method introduces two levels of loss rescaling: sentence-level and word-level. Sentence-level rescaling applies a fixed scaling factor to the loss of sentences containing OOV words, while word-level rescaling applies gradient scaling specifically to CTC decoding nodes corresponding to OOV words. The word-level approach is more targeted, allowing the model to focus on learning OOV word representations without unnecessarily affecting other parts of the network. The method is combined with regularization techniques (L2 and Elastic Weight Consolidation) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting of previously learned vocabulary during the fine-tuning process.

## Key Results
- Word-level loss rescaling with EWC achieves 45.81% recall on OOV word recognition
- Only 7.8% relative increase in WER compared to baseline
- Word-level rescaling outperforms sentence-level rescaling (45.81% vs 41.91% recall)
- Combined approach shows better performance than previous methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by increasing the gradient magnitude for OOV words during training, effectively forcing the model to pay more attention to these underrepresented tokens. By applying this scaling at the word level rather than sentence level, the approach can precisely target the specific CTC nodes responsible for OOV word recognition. The regularization component prevents the model from overfitting to OOV words at the expense of general recognition performance, maintaining a balance between learning new vocabulary and preserving existing capabilities.

## Foundational Learning

### Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC)
- **Why needed**: CTC enables sequence-to-sequence learning without explicit alignment between input and output sequences
- **Quick check**: Verify CTC decoding produces valid probability distributions over output tokens at each time step

### Catastrophic Forgetting
- **Why needed**: Neural networks tend to overwrite previously learned information when trained on new data
- **Quick check**: Monitor performance degradation on original vocabulary during OOV fine-tuning

### Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC)
- **Why needed**: EWC prevents catastrophic forgetting by penalizing changes to important weights
- **Quick check**: Compare parameter importance scores before and after EWC regularization

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Audio Input -> Feature Extractor -> CTC Decoder -> Output Layer
                       ↓
                Loss Rescaling (Word-Level)
                       ↓
                Regularization (EWC/L2)

### Critical Path
The critical path involves the interaction between the CTC decoder and the loss rescaling mechanism, where OOV word nodes receive amplified gradients during backpropagation.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Granularity vs. Complexity**: Word-level rescaling is more precise but requires identifying OOV nodes in the decoding lattice
- **Regularization vs. Learning**: EWC prevents forgetting but may slow OOV word acquisition
- **Computational Overhead**: Loss rescaling adds minimal overhead but requires additional bookkeeping

### Failure Signatures
- Poor OOV recall indicates insufficient gradient emphasis or inadequate regularization
- Excessive WER increase suggests overfitting to OOV words
- Inconsistent performance across different OOV frequencies may indicate sensitivity to word distribution

## Limitations
- Experiments limited to single dataset (LibriSpeech) with small OOV set (58 words)
- Evaluation focuses primarily on recall and WER without examining false positive rates
- Word-level rescaling assumes reliable OOV identification in decoding lattice
- Limited exploration of different OOV word distributions and frequencies
