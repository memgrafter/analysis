# Conflict-Averse Gradient Optimization of Ensembles for Effective Offline Model-Based Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17934
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17934
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: Ensemble methods with MGDA and CAGrad reduce distribution shift in offline MBO, improving design quality

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of distribution shift in offline model-based optimization (MBO), where optimizing against learned proxy models can produce out-of-distribution designs that "fool" the models. The authors propose using ensemble models combined with advanced gradient combination methods—specifically Multiple Gradient Descent Algorithm (MGDA) and Conflict-Averse Gradient Descent (CAGrad)—to mitigate this issue. These methods balance conservatism and optimality by searching for designs that maximize the worst improvement rate across ensemble models, effectively preventing any single model from pulling optimization toward invalid regions.

Evaluated on five diverse computational design tasks, the proposed methods consistently outperform naive baselines including single model, mean gradient, and minimum gradient approaches. MGDA excels in continuous tasks while CAGrad performs better on discrete tasks, demonstrating the importance of task-appropriate algorithm selection. The results suggest these methods can significantly robustify offline MBO without compromising design quality, opening avenues for future work on improved hyperparameter tuning and discrete space optimization techniques.

## Method Summary
The method employs ensemble models (typically 6) with the same architecture but different training subsets to mitigate distribution shift. Two gradient combination approaches are used: MGDA, which finds Pareto-optimal solutions balancing multiple model objectives, and CAGrad, which searches for designs maximizing the worst improvement rate across models. Both are formulated as convex optimization problems with dual formulations that reduce computational complexity from O(d) to O(m) decision variables. The optimization process involves gradient ascent on top designs from the dataset, with performance evaluated against ground truth oracles.

## Key Results
- MGDA and CAGrad outperform naive baselines (single model, mean gradient, minimum gradient) in both max and average ground truth scores
- MGDA excels in continuous tasks while CAGrad performs better on discrete tasks
- Ensemble methods reduce distribution shift by mitigating overestimation errors across models
- Dual formulations enable efficient high-dimensional optimization with reduced computational complexity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Ensemble methods combined with conflict-averse gradient combination reduce distribution shift by mitigating overestimation errors across models. When using an ensemble rather than a single model, it's less likely that all models will overestimate the value of out-of-distribution designs. MGDA and CAGrad specifically optimize for the worst improvement rate across the ensemble, ensuring conservative yet optimal updates.

### Mechanism 2
MGDA and CAGrad balance conservatism and optimality by searching for designs that maximize the worst improvement rate across ensemble models. Both methods formulate convex optimization problems that find update vectors maximizing the minimum improvement rate across all models, preventing any single model from dominating and pulling optimization toward invalid regions.

### Mechanism 3
The dual formulations of MGDA and CAGrad reduce computational complexity from O(d) to O(m) decision variables, where d is input dimensionality and m is ensemble size. By leveraging strong duality, the primal problems with d variables are transformed to dual problems with only m variables, making high-dimensional design optimization computationally feasible.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data pipeline: Static dataset → MBO dataset (bottom K% of target values) → Training/validation splits
  - Model ensemble: 6 models with same architecture, different training subsets
  - Optimization engine: MGDA/CAGrad implementation with dual formulation option
  - Evaluation: Ground truth oracle (exact or learned) → Metrics calculation
  - Hyperparameter tuner: Offline selection based on validation metrics

- Critical path:
  1. Train ensemble models on MBO dataset
  2. For each of 128 top designs, perform gradient ascent using chosen method
  3. Evaluate generated designs using ground truth oracle
  4. Report max, 50th percentile, and average ground truth scores

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Ensemble size vs. computational cost: 6 models provide diversity but increase training time
  - Dual vs. primal formulation: Dual reduces decision variables from d to m but requires solving convex optimization
  - Step size selection: Too large violates Taylor approximation; too small slows convergence
  - CAGrad c parameter: Controls conservatism vs. optimality trade-off

- Failure signatures:
  - Oscillatory behavior: Indicates conflicting gradients or inappropriate step sizes
  - Degradation in average/50th percentile scores while max score improves: Suggests overfitting to outliers
  - Poor performance on discrete tasks: May indicate gradient ascent in discrete space needs adjustment

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reproduce single-model baseline on a simple continuous task to verify optimization pipeline works
  2. Compare mean gradient ensemble vs. single model on a discrete task to confirm ensemble helps
  3. Implement and test MGDA on a small-scale task with known ground truth to verify it finds valid Pareto-optimal solutions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of MGDA and CAGrad compare when using more sophisticated gradient ascent methods for discrete spaces, such as gradient normalization or alternating between soft-space and hard-space updates? The authors mention that certain methods like gradient normalization improved MGDA's performance on discrete tasks significantly, but they used a simple gradient ascent optimizer for consistency.

### Open Question 2
How effective are MGDA and CAGrad when applied to a wider range of computational design tasks, particularly those with unique characteristics that may favor one method over the other? The authors suggest that future work could study CAGrad and MGDA on more tasks, focusing on the unique characteristics of tasks that may make MGDA more suitable than CAGrad, or vice versa.

### Open Question 3
What are the most effective methods for offline hyperparameter tuning in the context of ensemble MBO with MGDA and CAGrad? The authors identify offline hyperparameter tuning as a key challenge and suggest that future work should explore better, more rigorous methods for this purpose.

### Open Question 4
How can the reliability of design algorithms using learned oracles be improved, given that learned oracles often suffer from the same distribution shift problem as proxy design models? The authors mention that using a learned oracle to evaluate the robustness of design algorithms to out-of-distribution designs is unreliable.

## Limitations

- No ablation studies on ensemble size or diversity to quantify the relationship between ensemble diversity and performance improvement
- CAGrad hyperparameter selection (c parameter) not fully explained, making it difficult to reproduce results
- Limited analysis of discrete space gradient optimization behavior, particularly for CAGrad's performance on discrete tasks

## Confidence

- **High**: Ensemble methods improve over single models (empirical results are robust)
- **Medium**: MGDA/CAGrad mechanisms reduce distribution shift (theoretical justification incomplete)
- **Medium**: Dual formulations improve computational efficiency (computational complexity claims not fully verified)
