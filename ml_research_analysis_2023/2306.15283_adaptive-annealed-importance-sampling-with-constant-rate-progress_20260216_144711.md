# Adaptive Annealed Importance Sampling with Constant Rate Progress

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15283
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15283
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes CR-AIS algorithm that adaptively tunes AIS annealing schedules based on target geometry using f-divergence derivatives, achieving comparable or better performance than adaptive AIS with fewer log density evaluations.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Constant Rate Annealed Importance Sampling (CR-AIS), a method that adaptively tunes the annealing schedule of AIS by minimizing the KL divergence between the current particle distribution and the target. Unlike existing adaptive AIS methods that require computationally expensive iterative search loops, CR-AIS uses the derivative of f-divergences to derive a constant rate discretization schedule along the optimal annealing path. The algorithm estimates schedule parameters online using weighted particle estimates, eliminating the need for repeated log-density evaluations during tuning. Experiments demonstrate that CR-AIS achieves comparable or better performance than adaptive AIS while being more computationally efficient.

## Method Summary
CR-AIS adapts AIS annealing schedules by optimizing the derivative of f-divergences between consecutive annealing distributions. The method derives a constant rate discretization schedule that balances the reduction of KL divergence at each step while constraining variance. For α-divergences, this yields closed-form power mean annealing paths. Instead of iterative search, CR-AIS estimates schedule parameters using weighted particle distributions from previous steps, computing the normalization ratio and empirical variance of g(u_t) to determine the next annealing step size. This eliminates the computationally expensive binary search required in adaptive AIS while maintaining similar accuracy.

## Key Results
- CR-AIS achieves comparable or better log Zπ estimation accuracy than Adaptive AIS across synthetic 2D and high-dimensional distributions
- The algorithm requires significantly fewer log density evaluations than Adaptive AIS due to elimination of the iterative search loop
- Performance scales well to high dimensions (d=512), maintaining accuracy while reducing computational cost
- The method adapts the annealing speed inversely to the divergence between particle and target distributions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The constant rate schedule adapts the annealing path to local geometry, reducing estimation bias proportionally at each step
- Mechanism: Optimizes derivative of f-divergence between current particle distribution and target, choosing next annealing distribution that maximally reduces divergence within variance constraint
- Core assumption: Consecutive annealing distributions are sufficiently close for perfect transition regime assumption
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] geometric annealing minimizes KL divergence under feasible change constraint; [section] constant rate enforces balanced difficulty division

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Generalizes to f-divergences with closed-form solutions for α-divergences yielding power mean annealing paths
- Mechanism: Functional derivative of f-divergence objective yields steepest descent direction involving g(u) = u·f'(u) - f(u), solved for α-divergences
- Core assumption: f is convex, differentiable, and lower semicontinuous with f(1)=0
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] extends results to f-divergences and presents CR-AIS implementation; [section] ODE has closed form solution for α-divergences

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: CR-AIS avoids iterative search by estimating schedule online using weighted particle estimates
- Mechanism: Computes estimate of integrand cf_qr,π Z_qr^α / √B Z_π^α using current particle weights, updating βα incrementally
- Core assumption: Particle weights accurately reflect marginal distributions for reliable empirical variance estimates
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] CR-AIS does not rely on searching algorithms and uses derivative information; [section] uses particles and weights to estimate normalization ratio

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Schedule tuner -> Annealing density generator -> Particle transition kernel -> Weight updater
- Critical path: For each AIS step i: estimate Z_qiδ/Z_π and Var_qiδ[g(u_i)] → compute β_(i+1)δ → update annealing density → run transition kernel → update weights
- Design tradeoffs: CR-AIS trades schedule adaptivity for reduced tuning iterations; may need more particles to stabilize empirical estimates versus Adaptive AIS's binary search
- Failure signatures: (1) Empirical variance estimate falls below threshold too early → premature annealing stop; (2) Overestimated variance → very small steps, many iterations; (3) Degenerate weights → inaccurate schedule
- First 3 experiments:
  1. 2D synthetic targets (narrow Gaussian, Gaussian ring) to verify support coverage and schedule adaptation visually
  2. High-dimensional Normal/Laplace/Student-T targets to benchmark log Zπ estimation accuracy vs Adaptive AIS and heuristic schedules
  3. Bayesian logistic regression marginal likelihood estimation to assess computation vs accuracy tradeoff in realistic models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does CR-AIS performance scale with increasing dimensionality beyond d=512, and what are the computational bottlenecks in high dimensions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Tests CR-AIS on d=128 and d=512 dimensional distributions but does not explore higher dimensions
- Why unresolved: Paper only provides experimental results up to d=512
- What evidence would resolve it: Running CR-AIS on synthetic high-dimensional distributions (d >> 512) and analyzing error, ESS, and computational cost as function of dimension

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can CR-AIS be extended to optimize other functional divergences beyond f-divergences, and would this lead to improved performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Extends greedy annealing to f-divergences and α-divergences but does not explore other divergence measures
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on f-divergences, leaving potential benefits of alternative divergences unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing CR-AIS with different divergences (e.g., Wasserstein) and comparing performance on benchmark distributions

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does choice of transition kernel (HMC, MALA, random walk) affect CR-AIS performance, especially in high dimensions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Uses HMC transitions but does not systematically compare different kernels
- Why unresolved: Paper only uses HMC, not exploring how other kernels might impact performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Running CR-AIS with different transition kernels on same benchmarks and comparing accuracy, ESS, and computational cost

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is theoretical justification for relationship between annealing schedule and functional derivative of divergence, and how does this relationship break down in practice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Proves geometric mean path corresponds to steepest descent direction of inverse KL divergence but does not discuss practical limitations
- Why unresolved: Paper provides theoretical justification but does not explore when assumptions break down or robustness to model misspecification
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing CR-AIS performance on distributions where assumptions are violated and identifying practical limitations

## Limitations

- Perfect transition regime assumption may break down for highly multimodal or heavy-tailed targets, causing schedule instability
- Method assumes reliable empirical variance estimates from weighted particles, which can fail when particle degeneracy is severe
- Generalization to f-divergences requires specific differentiability and convexity conditions that may not hold for all divergence choices

## Confidence

- High: Mathematical derivation of constant rate schedule from functional derivatives of f-divergences is rigorous and well-established
- Medium: Empirical validation on synthetic and real-world benchmarks shows improved efficiency, though results are limited to specific distribution families and model sizes
- Low: Claim that CR-AIS "adapts to the geometry" of target distribution requires more thorough investigation across diverse challenging distributions
