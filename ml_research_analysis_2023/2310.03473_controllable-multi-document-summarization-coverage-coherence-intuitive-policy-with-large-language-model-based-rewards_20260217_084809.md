# Controllable Multi-document Summarization: Coverage & Coherence Intuitive Policy with Large Language Model Based Rewards

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2310.03473

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a controllable extract-rewrite approach for multi-document summarization that addresses the challenges of controllability and tractability in neural network-based methods. The core idea is to train a content extraction policy that extracts relevant content based on coverage and coherence objectives, which is then refined by a lightly trained large language model (LLM) into a coherent summary. The extraction policy is rewarded by comparing the refined summary with a reference summary using semantic similarity and N-gram overlap. Experiments on MultiNews and WCEP datasets show that the proposed method yields competitive results in terms of ROUGE metrics and outperforms baselines in coherence, as per human evaluation. The method also demonstrates controllability of coherence by adjusting the associated control parameter.

## Method Summary
The method employs a two-stage extract-rewrite approach. First, a content extraction policy is trained using reinforcement learning to select sentences from source documents based on coverage (how much new information each sentence provides) and coherence (semantic similarity between consecutive sentences). The policy uses submodular functions to estimate coverage and calculates coherence using semantic similarity thresholds. The extracted sentences are then passed to a lightly fine-tuned LLM (specifically flan-t5-xl) that rewrites the extracted content into a fluent summary. The policy is trained using rewards computed from semantic similarity (using SBERT representations) and ROUGE overlap between the LLM-refined summary and reference summaries.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The content extraction policy can control summary coherence by adjusting the coherence control parameter (cl2) in the policy equation.
- Mechanism: The policy selects sentences based on a weighted sum of coverage and coherence scores, where coherence is computed using semantic similarity between consecutive sentences. Increasing cl2 prioritizes coherence over coverage.
- Core assumption: Semantic similarity between consecutive sentences is a valid proxy for discourse coherence.
- Evidence anchors:

  - [section]: "We estimate coherence based on semantic similarity between neighboring sentences in the summary text... for any pair of sentences, if the semantic similarity is below a threshold T 1, it's considered an incoherent pair."

- Break condition: If semantic similarity fails to capture discourse coherence (e.g., semantically similar sentences are topically disconnected), the coherence control would break.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The lightly trained LLM can reliably refine extracted content into coherent summaries without significant hallucination.
- Mechanism: The extraction policy outputs a sequence of sentences optimized for coverage and coherence, which the LLM then rewrites into a fluent summary. The LLM is fine-tuned on aligned summary-source pairs to learn the rewriting task.
- Core assumption: The LLM's rewriting capability is sufficient to handle the extracted content without introducing factual errors or hallucinations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Memory-efficient large language models are good at refining text input for better readability."

- Break condition: If the LLM introduces hallucinations or fails to preserve the extracted content's meaning, the refinement step breaks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Content Extraction Policy (CE) -> Lightly Fine-tuned LLM (Z) -> Reward Computation -> Policy Gradient Trainer
- Critical path: CE → Z → Reward Computation → Policy Gradient Update
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Controllability vs. performance: Explicit coverage/coherence control may limit the model's ability to optimize for other metrics
  - LLM dependency: Heavy reliance on LLM for final refinement means model performance is bounded by LLM quality
  - Computational cost: Two-stage process (extraction + refinement) increases inference time
- Failure signatures:
  - Low coherence scores despite high cl2 values: Semantic similarity metric may not capture discourse coherence
  - Hallucinations in final summaries: LLM may be introducing factual errors during refinement
  - Poor ROUGE scores: Coverage function or reward computation may not be effective
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test coverage function effectiveness: Compare ROUGE scores with and without coverage component in policy
  2. Test coherence control: Generate summaries with varying cl2 values and measure coherence scores
  3. Test LLM refinement: Compare summaries before and after LLM refinement using human evaluation

## Limitations
- The reliance on semantic similarity as a proxy for discourse coherence may not capture all aspects of coherence, particularly discourse relations and narrative flow
- The heavy dependence on LLM quality for the final refinement step means the method's performance is bounded by the LLM's capabilities
- The computational cost of the two-stage process (extraction + refinement) may limit practical applicability for real-time summarization tasks

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The experimental setup and evaluation methodology are clearly described, including dataset selection (MultiNews, WCEP), metrics (ROUGE, human evaluation), and implementation details for the LLM refinement step.
- Medium Confidence: The effectiveness of the coverage-coherence policy is demonstrated, but the specific hyperparameter values and ranges used for control parameters (cl1, cl2) are not provided, limiting reproducibility.
- Low Confidence: The paper does not provide evidence that the proposed coherence metric (semantic similarity between consecutive sentences) aligns with human judgment of discourse coherence. Additionally, the potential for LLM hallucination during the refinement step is acknowledged but not empirically evaluated.
