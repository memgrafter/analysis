# Token-Level Fitting Issues of Seq2seq Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04493
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04493
- Reference count: 28
- Token-level fitting issues occur in seq2seq models during early stopping, with high-frequency tokens overfitting and low-frequency tokens underfitting.

## Executive Summary
Seq2seq models often exhibit token-level fitting issues when trained with early stopping, where some tokens overfit while others underfit. This study proposes two measures to quantify these issues: fitting-offset and potential-gain. Experiments on English-German machine translation datasets reveal that high-frequency tokens tend to overfit while low-frequency tokens underfit. Parts-of-speech and prediction discrepancy also influence fitting behavior, though the effects are less pronounced than frequency-based grouping. The findings suggest that token-level fitting is the result of a complex interaction between multiple factors, and addressing these issues remains an open challenge for future research.

## Method Summary
The study analyzes token-level fitting issues in seq2seq models by training 40 models with different random seeds on English-German machine translation data. Three model configurations are used: Base Transformer, Big Transformer, and mBART25. Tokens are grouped by frequency, parts-of-speech, and prediction discrepancy. For each group, fitting-offset (distance between best fit and early stopping point) and potential-gain (accuracy gap) are calculated. Statistical significance is tested using sign-tests across the 40 runs. The analysis reveals that high-frequency tokens generally overfit while low-frequency tokens underfit, with parts-of-speech and prediction discrepancy modulating these effects.

## Key Results
- High-frequency tokens overfit (average fitting-offset = -3.7 epochs) while low-frequency tokens underfit (average fitting-offset = 5.8 epochs) at early stopping.
- Parts-of-speech affects fitting, with function words and punctuation more prone to overfitting than nouns and verbs.
- Prediction discrepancy strongly correlates with fitting behavior, with high-discrepancy tokens overfit and low-discrepancy tokens underfit.
- Large pretrained models reduce underfitting but are less effective on overfitting compared to base models.

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Token-level fitting issues arise from heterogeneous learning dynamics during seq2seq training.
- Mechanism: Different tokens exhibit varying convergence speeds due to factors like frequency, part-of-speech, and context dependency, leading to some tokens overfitting while others underfit at early stopping.
- Core assumption: The training dynamics of individual tokens can be meaningfully measured and compared using fitting-offset and potential-gain metrics.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Frequency-based grouping is a strong but imperfect predictor of fitting behavior.
- Mechanism: High-frequency tokens generally overfit while low-frequency tokens underfit, but this pattern is modulated by linguistic factors and context dependency.
- Core assumption: Token frequency in training data correlates with its learning difficulty and fitting behavior.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Prediction discrepancy is a strong indicator of fitting behavior, capturing context dependency effects.
- Mechanism: Tokens that rely heavily on long context (high discrepancy) tend to overfit, while those that are more context-independent (low discrepancy) tend to underfit.
- Core assumption: The difference between predictions made with full context versus local context reflects the token's dependence on broader context, which affects its fitting behavior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "As Figure 4 shows, the big-discrepancy tokens have an average fitting-offset of −2.7 with a standard deviation of 3.0. The medium-discrepancy tokens have an average fitting-offset of 1.0 with a standard deviation of 2.1. The small-discrepancy tokens have an average fitting-offset of 8.2 with a standard deviation of 1.8, showing a trend to exceed the boundary of 10."

- Break condition: If discrepancy loses predictive power when combined with other factors or in different model architectures (e.g., pretrained models).

## Limitations

- The study focuses on English-German machine translation and may not generalize to other language pairs or domains.
- The analysis relies on early stopping at fixed checkpoints, which may not reflect optimal stopping strategies for different token groups.
- The prediction discrepancy measure requires training an additional "altered Transformer model with two decoders," but the exact architecture is not fully specified.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core finding that high-frequency tokens tend to overfit while low-frequency tokens tend to underfit at early stopping is well-supported by the data and consistent across multiple model configurations.
- **Medium Confidence**: The relationship between parts-of-speech and fitting behavior is supported by the data, but the effect sizes are smaller than those observed for frequency-based grouping.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim about prediction discrepancy as a strong indicator of fitting behavior is the least robust, with the analysis showing a clear trend but potentially affected by methodological complexity.
