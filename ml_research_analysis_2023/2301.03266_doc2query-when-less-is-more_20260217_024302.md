# Doc2Query--: When Less is More

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03266
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Up to 16% improvement in retrieval effectiveness, 48% reduction in index size, and 30% reduction in query execution time by filtering Doc2Query expansions with a relevance model

## Executive Summary
Doc2Query-- addresses the issue of hallucinated or poor-quality queries generated by the original Doc2Query expansion method. By filtering out irrelevant expansions using a pre-trained relevance model, the approach improves both the effectiveness and efficiency of document retrieval. The method is more effective and efficient than the original Doc2Query, even when accounting for additional indexing compute costs.

## Method Summary
Doc2Query-- improves upon the original Doc2Query by filtering out irrelevant or hallucinated queries using a pre-trained relevance model. The process involves generating candidate queries with Doc2Query, scoring them with the relevance model, and retaining only those above a relevance threshold. This filtering step reduces index size and query execution time while improving retrieval effectiveness by focusing on high-quality expansions.

## Key Results
- Up to 16% improvement in retrieval effectiveness on standard benchmarks
- 48% reduction in index size due to filtering out irrelevant expansions
- 30% reduction in query execution time, improving overall efficiency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by reducing noise in the document expansion process. By filtering out irrelevant or hallucinated queries, the method ensures that only high-quality, relevant expansions are indexed and used during retrieval. This improves the signal-to-noise ratio in the index, leading to better ranking and faster query processing.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component Map**: Document -> Doc2Query Generator -> Relevance Model Scorer -> Filtered Expansions -> Index
- **Critical Path**: Query -> Index -> Retrieval Engine -> Ranked Results
- **Design Tradeoffs**: More aggressive filtering reduces index size but may lose useful expansions; Less filtering retains more content but risks noise.
- **Failure Signatures**: Poor retrieval if threshold is too high (loss of useful expansions); Slow retrieval if threshold is too low (noise retained).
- **First Experiments**:

  3. Conduct manual inspection of filtered vs. unfiltered expansions for relevance.

## Limitations
- No analysis of how filtering thresholds affect recall of relevant expansions
- No discussion of filtering costs relative to indexing time
- Limited comparison beyond the original Doc2Query model
- No statistical significance testing provided

## Confidence
- Retrieval effectiveness improvements: High
- Efficiency gains (index size, query time): Medium
- Generalization to other expansion methods: Low
