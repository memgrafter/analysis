# On the Optimality of Misspecified Kernel Ridge Regression

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07241
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07241
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: KRR is minimax optimal for all smoothness levels in Sobolev RKHS without requiring boundedness of the true function

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the optimality of kernel ridge regression (KRR) in misspecified settings where the true function lies outside the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS). While prior work required the true function to be bounded when the smoothness condition is violated, the authors show that KRR is minimax optimal for all smoothness levels in Sobolev RKHS without this boundedness assumption. They achieve this by replacing the boundedness assumption with a weaker Lq-integrability condition and employing refined analysis using truncation techniques. For Sobolev RKHS with smoothness r > d/2, they prove that KRR achieves the optimal convergence rate n^{-sβ/(sβ+1)} for all s ∈ (0,2], where β = 2r/d is the eigenvalue decay rate. This result closes a longstanding gap in understanding KRR's optimality properties and is verified through experiments.

## Method Summary
The paper replaces the boundedness assumption with a weaker Lq-integrability condition for the true function and employs truncation techniques to handle unbounded functions. The analysis decomposes the generalization error into approximation and estimation errors, using Bernstein-type concentration inequalities and refined error bounds. For Sobolev RKHS with smoothness r > d/2, the authors prove that KRR achieves the optimal convergence rate n^{-sβ/(sβ+1)} for all source conditions 0 < s ≤ 2. The proof involves carefully choosing a truncation threshold that grows with sample size n and applying spectral analysis of the integral operator associated with the kernel.

## Key Results
- KRR achieves minimax optimal rates for all source conditions 0 < s ≤ 2 in Sobolev RKHS without requiring boundedness of the true function
- The boundedness assumption is replaced with a weaker Lq-integrability condition: q > 2(sβ+1)/(2+(s-α0)β)
- For Sobolev RKHS with smoothness r > d/2, KRR achieves optimal convergence rate n^{-sβ/(sβ+1)} for all s ∈ (0,2]

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: KRR achieves minimax optimal rates for all source conditions 0 < s ≤ 2 in Sobolev RKHS without requiring boundedness of the true function.
- Mechanism: By replacing the boundedness assumption with a weaker Lq-integrability condition and using truncation techniques, the analysis can handle unbounded functions while maintaining the same convergence rate.
- Core assumption: The true function f* lies in Lq with q > 2(sβ+1)/(2+(s-α0)β) and the Sobolev RKHS has embedding index α0 = 1/β.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that KRR is minimax optimal for any s ∈ (0, 1) when the H is a Sobolev RKHS"
  - [section 3.1] "We know that KRR is optimal for Sobolev RKHS and all 0 < s≤ 2"
  - [corpus] "On the Optimality of Misspecified Spectral Algorithms" - directly related topic, but specific details differ
- Break condition: If the Sobolev RKHS has embedding index α0 ≠ 1/β, or if the Lq-integrability condition fails for the given s and β.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The approximation error can be bounded by λ^(s-γ)/2 for any 0 ≤ γ ≤ s using the structure of interpolation spaces.
- Mechanism: The proof decomposes the approximation error into a sum over eigenvalues, where each term is weighted by λ^(s-γ)/2 times the source condition norm.
- Core assumption: The true function satisfies the source condition f* ∈ [H]^s with bounded norm.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section A.1] "we have ∥fλ−f*∥[H]γ≤Rλ^(s-γ)/2"
  - [abstract] "For Sobolev RKHS with smoothness r > d/2, they prove that KRR achieves the optimal convergence rate n^(-sβ/(sβ+1)) for all s ∈ (0,2]"
  - [corpus] "Optimal Rates for Vector-Valued Spectral Regularization Learning Algorithms" - related but not directly addressing the boundedness removal
- Break condition: If the source condition norm R is infinite, or if the interpolation space structure changes for non-Sobolev RKHS.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The estimation error can be bounded using a truncation method that exploits the Lq-integrability of the true function.
- Mechanism: By splitting the input space into regions where |f*| ≤ t and |f*| > t, and choosing t to grow with n, the truncation handles unbounded functions while the remaining integral vanishes with high probability.
- Core assumption: The true function has sufficient Lq-integrability and the noise satisfies the moment condition.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section A.2] "we consider two subset of X : Ω1 = {x∈ Ω :|f*ρ (x)|≤ t} and Ω2 =X\ Ω1 where t is allowed to diverge as n→∞"
  - [section D.4] "the truncation method in this proposition is necessary only in the s≤α0 case"
  - [corpus] "Spectral Algorithms in Misspecified Regression: Convergence under Covariate Shift" - related but focuses on covariate shift rather than boundedness
- Break condition: If the Lq-integrability condition fails, or if the noise has heavier tails than assumed.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Kernel ridge regression estimator: f̂λ = (TX + λ)^(-1) gZ
  - Integral operator T: T = ∑ λi ⟨·, ei⟩L2 ei
  - Eigenvalue decay: λi ≍ i^(-β)
  - Source condition: f* ∈ [H]^s with ∥f*∥[H]^s ≤ R
  - Noise model: E(|ε|^m | x) ≤ 1/(2m!σ²L^(m-2))

- Critical path:
  1. Verify Sobolev RKHS properties (eigenvalue decay, embedding index)
  2. Check source condition and Lq-integrability of true function
  3. Choose regularization parameter λ ≍ n^(-β/(sβ+1))
  4. Implement KRR estimator and monitor convergence
  5. Validate error bounds through experiments

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Boundedness vs. Lq-integrability: The paper trades the strong boundedness assumption for a weaker integrability condition, but this requires q > 2(sβ+1)/(2+(s-α0)β)
  - Regularization strength: λ must be chosen carefully as n^(-β/(sβ+1)) to balance approximation and estimation errors
  - Computational complexity: The truncation method adds complexity but is necessary for the unbounded case

- Failure signatures:
  - If q ≤ 2(sβ+1)/(2+(s-α0)β), the truncation argument fails
  - If the Sobolev RKHS has α0 ≠ 1/β, the embedding property needed for the proof may not hold
  - If the noise has heavier tails than sub-Gaussian, the Bernstein inequality may not apply

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement KRR on a Sobolev RKHS with known f* ∈ [H]^s \ L∞ and verify convergence rate n^(-sβ/(sβ+1))
  2. Test the robustness of the result by varying the Lq-norm of f* and checking when the truncation threshold t becomes ineffective
  3. Compare the performance of KRR with boundedness assumption vs. Lq-integrability assumption on synthetic data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the boundedness assumption in the minimax lower bound (Proposition 2.7) be removed or replaced with a weaker integrability condition?
- Basis in paper: The authors explicitly state that their work removes the boundedness assumption for Sobolev RKHS and replaces it with a weaker Lq-integrability condition
- Why unresolved: The paper only proves this for Sobolev RKHS and RKHS with uniformly bounded eigenfunctions. General RKHS cases remain open
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof that the Lq-integrability condition from Theorem 3.1 holds for general RKHS, or a counterexample showing it fails for some RKHS

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the learning curves for kernel ridge regression in the misspecified case with general noise distributions?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention that current results on learning curves rely on strong Gaussian design assumptions, and they believe studying the misspecified case is a crucial step toward removing this assumption
- Why unresolved: Existing work on learning curves (Spigler et al., 2020; Bordelon et al., 2020; Cui et al., 2021) all assume Gaussian design, which is very strong
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof establishing learning curves for KRR under general noise distributions without the Gaussian design assumption

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the saturation effect observed by Li et al. (2023) for s > 2 in Sobolev RKHS relate to the optimal rates proved in this paper for 0 < s ≤ 2?
- Basis in paper: The authors explicitly mention that when s > 2, KRR can't be optimal due to the saturation effect proved in Li et al. (2023), and they ask how this relates to their results
- Why unresolved: The paper establishes optimality for 0 < s ≤ 2 but doesn't fully characterize what happens for s > 2 or explain the transition
- What evidence would resolve it: A complete characterization of KRR's behavior across all s values, explaining why the transition occurs at s = 2 and what the optimal rates are for s > 2

## Limitations

- The Lq-integrability condition q > 2(sβ+1)/(2+(s-α0)β) is a critical bottleneck that may not hold for many interesting functions
- The truncation method's implementation details are somewhat sparse, making it difficult to assess its robustness across different problem settings
- The experimental validation is limited to specific examples and doesn't systematically explore the boundaries of the theory's applicability

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The minimax lower bound (Theorem 3.1) and its proof structure appear sound, as they follow standard techniques for this problem class.
- **Medium confidence**: The upper bound construction and truncation method are mathematically rigorous but depend heavily on the integrability condition being satisfied, which may not hold in practice for many interesting functions.
- **Medium confidence**: The experimental validation is limited to specific examples and doesn't systematically explore the boundaries of the theory's applicability.
