# Causal Unsupervised Semantic Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2310.07379
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07379
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a novel framework, CAUSE, that leverages causal inference to define two-step tasks for unsupervised prediction, achieving state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised semantic segmentation with metrics such as mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and pixel accuracy (pAcc).

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of determining the appropriate level of clustering for segmenting concepts in unsupervised semantic segmentation. It proposes a novel framework, CAUSE, that leverages causal inference to define two-step tasks for unsupervised prediction. The first step involves constructing a concept clusterbook as a mediator, which represents possible concept prototypes at different levels of granularity. The second step uses the mediator to establish an explicit link to concept-wise self-supervised learning for pixel-level grouping. Experiments on various datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of CAUSE, achieving state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised semantic segmentation.

## Method Summary
CAUSE is a two-step causal approach for unsupervised semantic segmentation. First, it constructs a concept clusterbook as a mediator using modularity maximization, which discretizes pre-trained features into concept prototypes while preserving pixel-level locality and connectivity. Second, it employs concept-wise self-supervised learning with positive and negative concept selection based on a pre-computed distance matrix. The framework uses frontdoor adjustment for causal inference, enabling effective semantic grouping without labeled data. The segmentation head is trained using contrastive learning objectives with carefully selected relaxation parameters for positive and negative concepts.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised semantic segmentation across multiple datasets
- Demonstrates effectiveness of causal inference approach with concept clusterbook as mediator
- Shows robustness across different pre-training methods including DINOv2, iBOT, MSN, and MAE

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The discretized concept clusterbook acts as a mediator that enables causal inference by breaking the backdoor path between pre-trained features and semantic groups.
- Mechanism: In unsupervised semantic segmentation, the lack of labeled data creates an unobserved confounder U that disrupts the causal relationship between pre-trained features T and desired semantic groups Y. By constructing a discretized concept clusterbook M that bridges T and Y while being independent of U, the framework can establish a causal association path T → M → Y. This follows the principles of frontdoor adjustment, which allows causal effect estimation even when confounders are unobserved.
- Core assumption: The discretized concept clusterbook M must be constructed in a way that is independent of the unobserved confounder U while maintaining a causal relationship with both T and Y.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we bridge intervention-oriented approach (i.e., frontdoor adjustment) to define suitable two-step tasks for unsupervised prediction"
  - [section]: "building a mediator M can be viewed as a blocking procedure of the backdoor paths induced from U by assigning possible concepts in discretized states"
  - [corpus]: "Weak, only mentions causal inference and frontdoor adjustment in related works without specific implementation details"
- Break condition: If the concept clusterbook M becomes correlated with the unobserved confounder U, the backdoor path cannot be effectively blocked, breaking the causal inference mechanism.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Modularity maximization enables effective discretization of continuous feature representations into concept prototypes that capture pixel-level locality and connectivity.
- Mechanism: The framework uses modularity maximization to construct the concept clusterbook M from pre-trained features T. This method considers both cluster assignments and affinity matrices simultaneously, creating discretized concept prototypes that maintain patch-wise locality and connectivity in the pre-trained representation. This discretization transforms continuous representations into a form suitable for concept-wise self-supervised learning.
- Core assumption: The pre-trained features contain sufficient semantic consistency at the pixel level to enable effective clustering through modularity maximization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we exploit a clustering method that maximizes modularity (Newman, 2006), which is one of the most effective approaches for considering relations among vertices"
  - [section]: "By jointly considering cluster assignments C(T, M ) and affinity matrix A(T ) at a time, in brief, maximizing modularity H constructs the discretized concept clusterbook M taking into account the patch-wise locality and connectivity"
  - [corpus]: "Weak, mentions self-supervised learning and clustering but lacks specific details about modularity maximization approach"
- Break condition: If the pre-trained features lack sufficient semantic consistency at the pixel level, the modularity maximization will produce poor concept prototypes that fail to capture meaningful semantic groupings.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Concept-wise self-supervised learning with carefully selected positive and negative concepts enables effective semantic grouping without labeled data.
- Mechanism: The framework uses concept-wise self-supervised learning where positive and negative concept features are selected based on a pre-computed distance matrix DM. This creates anchor-positive-negative triplets for contrastive learning at the pixel level. The positive relaxation is set softer to facilitate concept consolidation, while negative relaxation is tighter to enhance separation. A concept bank accumulates out-batch features for additional comparison pairs, enabling global view incorporation.
- Core assumption: The concept distance matrix DM accurately reflects semantic similarity between concept prototypes, enabling effective positive/negative sample selection.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we use a pre-computed distance matrix DM that reflects concept-wise similarity between all k concept prototypes such that DM = cos(M, M ) ∈ Rk×k"
  - [section]: "we opt for soft relaxation when selecting positive concept features because the main purpose of our unsupervised setup is to group subdivided concept prototypes into the targeted broader-level categories"
  - [corpus]: "Weak, mentions self-supervised learning and contrastive learning but lacks specific details about concept-wise selection approach"
- Break condition: If the concept distance matrix DM fails to accurately reflect semantic similarity, the positive/negative selection will be ineffective, leading to poor semantic grouping.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Causal inference and frontdoor adjustment
  - Why needed here: The unsupervised nature of the task creates unobserved confounders that prevent direct causal inference between pre-trained features and semantic groups. Frontdoor adjustment provides a way to establish causal relationships without requiring observation of these confounders.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between backdoor adjustment and frontdoor adjustment in causal inference?

- Concept: Modularity maximization in graph theory
  - Why needed here: The framework needs to transform continuous feature representations into discretized concept prototypes while preserving pixel-level locality and connectivity. Modularity maximization is an effective graph clustering method that considers both cluster assignments and vertex relationships.
  - Quick check question: How does modularity maximization differ from traditional k-means clustering in terms of what it optimizes?

- Concept: Self-supervised contrastive learning
  - Why needed here: Without labeled data, the framework must use self-supervised methods to learn semantic groupings. Concept-wise contrastive learning enables the model to distinguish between semantically similar and dissimilar pixel representations.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between instance-level and concept-level contrastive learning?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pre-trained feature extractor (frozen) → Concept clusterbook construction module → Concept-wise self-supervised learning module → Segmentation head
- Concept clusterbook M serves as mediator between pre-trained features T and semantic groups Y
- Concept bank accumulates out-batch features for global view incorporation

- Critical path: Pre-trained features → Modularity maximization → Concept clusterbook construction → Concept-wise self-supervised learning → Segmentation head output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of concepts k in clusterbook vs. computational efficiency
  - Positive vs. negative relaxation parameters affecting semantic grouping granularity
  - Use of concept bank for global views vs. increased memory requirements
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor concept clusterbook quality (too many/few concepts, poor locality preservation)
  - Ineffective positive/negative sample selection (distance matrix inaccuracies)
  - Insufficient semantic consistency in pre-trained features

  1. Validate concept clusterbook construction quality by visualizing retrieved concepts using shared indices
  2. Test different positive/negative relaxation parameters to find optimal semantic grouping
  3. Compare performance with and without concept bank to assess global view contribution

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of CAUSE change with different self-supervised pre-training methods beyond DINO?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests CAUSE with DINOv2, iBOT, MSN, and MAE as alternative pre-training methods
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides mIoU and pAcc results for these methods, but does not analyze how the choice of pre-training method affects CAUSE's performance across different datasets or semantic granularity levels
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed ablation studies comparing CAUSE performance across datasets when using different pre-training methods, including analysis of how pre-training method choice impacts fine-grained vs coarse semantic grouping

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of concept prototypes (k) in the clusterbook for different datasets and semantic granularity levels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper sets k=2048 based on empirical observation but acknowledges a saturation point
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests k=2048 and does not systematically explore how varying k affects performance for different levels of semantic granularity or dataset characteristics
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive ablation studies varying k across a range of values for each dataset, with analysis of how k affects performance for different semantic categories (e.g., Thing vs Stuff categories)

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does CAUSE handle images with complex scenes containing many small objects or intricate backgrounds?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions failure cases with complex scenes but does not provide systematic analysis of CAUSE's limitations in such scenarios
- Why unresolved: The paper only shows qualitative examples of failure cases without quantifying the frequency or severity of these failures across different types of complex scenes
- What evidence would resolve it: Quantitative analysis of CAUSE performance on datasets specifically designed to test fine-grained segmentation (e.g., ADE20K), including metrics for small object detection and complex background handling

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can the positive and negative relaxation parameters (ϕ+ and ϕ−) be learned dynamically rather than set heuristically?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges the challenge of heuristically setting relaxation parameters and mentions this as a limitation
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore methods for learning these parameters adaptively based on image content or semantic category
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing fixed vs learned relaxation parameters, with analysis of how dynamic parameter adjustment affects performance on diverse image types and semantic categories

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of the concept clusterbook construction heavily depends on the quality of pre-trained features and the appropriateness of the number of concepts k
- The framework may struggle with complex scenes containing many small objects or intricate backgrounds
- Hyper-parameters like the number of concepts k and relaxation parameters are set heuristically without systematic optimization
