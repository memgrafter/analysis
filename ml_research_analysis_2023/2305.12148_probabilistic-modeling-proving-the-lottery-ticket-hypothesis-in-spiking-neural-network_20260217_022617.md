## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of proving the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) for Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), which was previously thought to be inapplicable due to the binary spike signals and complex temporal dynamics of SNNs. The authors introduce a novel probabilistic modeling approach that bypasses the Lipschitz condition required in traditional proofs for Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs). By modeling the probability of output changes when weights are pruned, the paper provides theoretical analysis proving that LTH holds in SNNs, demonstrating that sufficiently large SNNs contain sub-networks matching any smaller trained SNN with high probability.

The proposed method not only proves the theoretical validity of LTH in SNNs but also introduces a new pruning criterion based on this probabilistic model. Experimental results validate the existence of good sub-networks in randomly initialized SNNs without weight training, showing improved pruning results compared to baseline methods on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets. This work bridges a significant gap in understanding sparse connectivity in SNNs and opens new avenues for efficient SNN architectures.

## Method Summary

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The probabilistic modeling approach works by capturing the essence of how weight pruning affects the output of SNNs without relying on the Lipschitz condition, which is typically used in ANN-based proofs but is inapplicable to SNNs due to their binary spike signals and complex temporal dynamics. By focusing on the probability of output changes when weights are pruned, the method can effectively model the impact of pruning on SNN performance. This approach allows for a theoretical proof that sufficiently large SNNs contain sub-networks that can match the performance of any smaller trained SNN, thus establishing the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis for SNNs.

## Limitations

- The theoretical guarantees are asymptotic, leaving open questions about performance in finite, practical scenarios.
