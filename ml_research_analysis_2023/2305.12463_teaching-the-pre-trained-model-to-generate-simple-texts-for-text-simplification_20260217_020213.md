# Teaching the Pre-trained Model to Generate Simple Texts for Text Simplification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12463
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12463
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Proposed continued pre-training strategy significantly improves text simplification across lexical, sentence, and document-level tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a continued pre-training strategy to teach pre-trained models to generate simple texts. The approach, called SimpleBART, continues pre-training BART using dynamic masking of simple words and dictionary-based replacement of complex words. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on document-level simplification and comparable performance to advanced models on sentence simplification, while also matching the state-of-the-art LSBert on lexical simplification tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors propose continuing pre-training BART using two main components: (1) dynamically masking simple words in simple texts based on their complexity scores from a lexical complexity predictor, and (2) replacing complex words in ordinary texts with simpler paraphrases from a dictionary while ensuring semantic preservation through BERTScore filtering. The pre-trained models are then fine-tuned on specific simplification tasks including sentence simplification, document-level simplification, and lexical simplification.

## Key Results
- SimpleBART achieves new state-of-the-art results on document-level simplification
- SimpleBART achieves comparable results to advanced models on sentence simplification tasks
- SimpleBART matches state-of-the-art performance on lexical simplification tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dynamic masking of simple words teaches the model to generate simple words during continued pre-training.
- Mechanism: Simple words in simple texts are identified using a lexical complexity predictor (DeepBlueAI). The simpler the word, the higher its masking probability. The model learns to reconstruct these masked simple words.
- Core assumption: The model can learn to generate simpler words if it is trained to reconstruct them from masked positions, especially when simpler words have higher masking probability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We identify simple text spans in simple texts and dynamically replace them with <mask> tokens. Then, the pre-trained model will learn by reconstructing simple words."
  - [section] "We take advantage of the DeepBlueAI model (Pan et al., 2021) that achieves state-of-the-art results on the lexical complexity prediction task (Shardlow et al., 2021)."
- Break condition: If the complexity predictor is inaccurate, the masking will target wrong words, and the model won't learn to generate simple words effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Replacing complex words in ordinary texts with simple words helps the model learn to generate simple words.
- Mechanism: A dictionary (SimplePPDB++) is used to replace complex words in ordinary texts with simpler paraphrases. The model learns to reconstruct these replaced simple words. BERTScore filters out replacements that change the sentence meaning.
- Core assumption: Replacing complex words with simple ones in ordinary texts will give the model more opportunities to learn to generate simple words, provided the meaning is preserved.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We use a dictionary to replace complex words in ordinary texts with simple words. We also ensure the quality of the replaced sentences."
  - [section] "We introduce the dictionary SimplePPDB++ (Maddela and Xu, 2018) to address this issue. It contains millions of paraphrase rules with readability scores."
- Break condition: If the dictionary replacement is poor (high false positives), the model learns to generate incorrect simple words.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Continued pre-training on simplification task data adapts the model to the simplification domain better than random masking.
- Mechanism: BART is continued pre-trained on the training set of the simplification task (ensuring no overlap with test data). This domain-adaptive pre-training teaches the model to generate simple texts.
- Core assumption: Pre-training on task-relevant data (simplification corpora) will adapt the model better than general pre-training, especially for low-resource tasks like lexical simplification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We select the BART-Large model to continue pre-training. We follow the task-adaptive pre-training method (Gururangan et al., 2020)."
  - [section] "We refer to the pre-trained models obtained by our strategy collectively as SimpleBART."
- Break condition: If the continued pre-training data is not representative of the test data domain, the model may not generalize well.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: DeepBlueAI complexity predictor -> Dynamic masking -> SimplePPDB++ dictionary -> BERTScore filtering -> Continued pre-training -> Fine-tuning
- Critical path: Identify simple words in simple texts -> Dynamic masking -> Continue pre-training on masked simple texts and ordinary texts with replaced words -> Fine-tune on specific simplification task data
- Design tradeoffs: Dynamic masking vs. static masking (higher computational cost but potentially better learning), dictionary replacement vs. no replacement (more data but risk of meaning change)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on simplification tasks despite continued pre-training could indicate issues with the complexity predictor, dictionary quality, or insufficient pre-training data
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that the DeepBlueAI model correctly identifies simple words by checking its output on a sample of simple texts.
  2. Test the dictionary replacement process on a sample of ordinary texts to ensure it replaces complex words with appropriate simple ones and that BERTScore filtering works.
  3. Run a small-scale continued pre-training experiment and check if the model learns to generate simple words by evaluating on a held-out simplification validation set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the methodology and results, several natural questions emerge regarding the approach's generalizability, the impact of hyperparameters, and comparisons to other recent models.

## Limitations

- Reliance on external complexity prediction models and paraphrase dictionaries introduces potential failure points
- Focus exclusively on English simplification limits generalizability to other languages
- Evaluation primarily compares against baselines rather than providing comprehensive ablation studies

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the overall effectiveness claim: Consistent improvements across three different simplification tasks compared to strong baselines
- **Medium confidence** in the specific mechanisms: General approach is sound but implementation details and hyperparameters are not fully specified
- **Medium confidence** in the state-of-the-art claim for document-level simplification: Significant improvement but lacks comparison to more recent document simplification models

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study validation**: Run experiments to isolate the contribution of dynamic masking versus dictionary replacement versus task-adaptive pre-training to confirm which mechanism drives improvements.

2. **Cross-linguistic transferability test**: Apply the SimpleBART approach to a non-English simplification task (e.g., German or Spanish) using the same methodology to assess whether the approach generalizes beyond English.

3. **Quality analysis of generated simplifications**: Conduct human evaluation studies on the simplified outputs to verify that the model not only achieves better automatic metrics but also produces genuinely simpler and more readable text that preserves meaning.
