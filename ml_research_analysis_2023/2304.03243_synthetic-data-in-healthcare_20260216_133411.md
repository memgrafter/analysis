# Synthetic Data in Healthcare

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03243
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03243
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Synthetic data is a critical tool for building AI systems in healthcare, enabling systematic generation of training data at scale while addressing privacy, equity, and safety concerns, but requires careful implementation to avoid introducing biases and blind spots.

## Executive Summary
This survey explores the growing role of synthetic data in healthcare and medicine, examining how simulators and generative models can create training data when real patient data is limited, sensitive, or rare. The paper discusses physical and statistical simulation approaches for generating data across various medical modalities including ECG, imaging, and EHR, highlighting both the potential benefits and significant challenges. While synthetic data offers opportunities for privacy preservation, fairness improvement, and safe testing, the authors caution that poorly designed synthetic data can introduce flaws, propagate biases, and fail to capture real-world complexity.

## Method Summary
This survey paper synthesizes existing research on synthetic data generation methods for healthcare applications without presenting original experimental results. The authors examine physical simulation models that parameterize data generation processes based on domain knowledge, statistical approaches like GANs and VAEs that learn data distributions from real samples, and hybrid methods combining both approaches. The survey discusses applications across multiple medical modalities and evaluates the benefits and risks of synthetic data in healthcare contexts, providing a comprehensive overview of the current state of the field without implementing specific algorithms or experiments.

## Key Results
- Synthetic data generation enables systematic creation of training examples for AI models in healthcare where real patient data may be limited due to privacy, rarity, or ethical constraints
- While synthetic data can promote privacy, equity, safety, and causal learning, it also carries risks of introducing flaws, blind spots, and propagating or exaggerating existing biases
- Physical models require domain expertise to parameterize data generation but offer interpretability, while statistical models are easier to create but may amplify biases present in training data

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data sources (real patient data or existing datasets) -> Simulation models (physical or statistical) -> Data generation and augmentation processes -> Evaluation metrics for synthetic data quality and model performance
- Critical path: Accurately defining simulation parameters to reflect real-world complexity, generating diverse and representative synthetic data, rigorously evaluating model performance on both synthetic and real data
- Design tradeoffs: Physical models offer interpretability and known constraints but are complex to create and may not generalize well; statistical models are easier to create but can be less controllable and may amplify biases present in the training data
- Failure signatures: Poor model performance on real data, failure to capture rare events or edge cases, leakage of identifiable patient information
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate synthetic ECG data using a parametric dynamical model and evaluate its similarity to real ECG data in terms of waveform characteristics and diagnostic accuracy
  2. Create synthetic medical imaging data using a GAN and assess its quality by comparing image features and model performance to real imaging data
  3. Generate synthetic patient demographic data with controlled diversity parameters and evaluate the fairness and equity of models trained on this data compared to models trained on real demographic data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we effectively evaluate the utility-privacy tradeoff in synthetic healthcare data generation models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses privacy-utility tradeoff for any simulator, where benefits of privacy protections must be weighed against diminishing utility for the proposed task
- Why unresolved: The paper states that there are no methods for strictly determining whether synthetic data is sufficiently different from real samples to be considered anonymized, and this remains a challenge in the field
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of standardized metrics and evaluation frameworks that can quantitatively measure both the privacy protection and utility of synthetic healthcare data across different applications

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective methods for ensuring synthetic medical data represents diverse patient populations and avoids bias?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses challenges of ensuring synthetic data represents true diversity of patients and conditions, and risks of biases in learned generative models
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions some approaches like minority inclusion for majority enhancement, it states that tools for model creators are still limited and more work is needed to address these biases
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different bias mitigation techniques on synthetic healthcare data, showing improvements in fairness metrics across demographic groups while maintaining model performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we ensure synthetic healthcare data generation models capture unknown unknowns and limitations in the real world?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the problem of unknown unknowns - relationships that may or may not exist but are overlooked by model developers - and states this is the most problematic challenge
- Why unresolved: The paper notes that providing confidence that a simulator has sufficient fidelity that unknown unknowns will be inconsequential is non-trivial and may be impossible to prove
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of systematic approaches for identifying and quantifying unknown unknowns in synthetic data generation, perhaps through human-in-the-loop methods or novel validation frameworks

## Limitations

- The survey lacks empirical validation through concrete experiments, relying primarily on theoretical discussion rather than demonstrated implementations in real-world healthcare settings
- There is insufficient characterization of the domain gap challenges across multiple medical modalities (ECG, imaging, EHR, natural language), making it difficult to assess when synthetic data will successfully transfer to real-world applications
- The paper acknowledges that no standardized methods exist for evaluating whether synthetic healthcare data is sufficiently anonymized or effectively represents true population diversity

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Simulation-based generation): Medium confidence - While the theoretical framework is sound, there's limited evidence that current simulators can capture the full complexity of healthcare scenarios, particularly rare events and edge cases
- Mechanism 2 (Fairness and equity): Low confidence - The claim that synthetic data can improve demographic representation is largely theoretical, with no demonstrated evidence of successful implementation or evaluation of bias reduction
- Mechanism 3 (Safety testing): Medium confidence - The safety benefits are conceptually clear, but there's insufficient data on how well synthetic testing predicts real-world performance, particularly for rare adverse events
