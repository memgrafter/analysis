# Do LLMs Understand User Preferences? Evaluating LLMs On User Rating Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06474
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06474
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Fine-tuned LLMs achieve comparable or better rating prediction performance than traditional CF models using only a small fraction of training data

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can effectively predict user ratings for items, comparing them against traditional collaborative filtering methods. The study evaluates LLMs ranging from 250M to 540B parameters across zero-shot, few-shot, and fine-tuning settings on MovieLens-1M and Amazon-Books datasets. While zero-shot LLMs underperform traditional models, fine-tuning enables them to achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly less training data, demonstrating strong data efficiency. The research highlights the importance of user interaction data for LLMs and shows that larger models (over 100B parameters) perform better in cold-start scenarios.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates multiple LLMs (Flan-T5 variants, GPT-3 models, and Flan-U-PaLM) on user rating prediction using two benchmark datasets. Experiments are conducted in three settings: zero-shot (no training), few-shot (with demonstrations), and fine-tuning (full training). Traditional baselines include matrix factorization, MLP, and Transformer+MLP models. The datasets are split chronologically (90/10), and models are evaluated using RMSE, MAE, and AUC metrics. Fine-tuning uses MSE loss for regression tasks with batch size 64 and learning rate 5e-5 for 50k steps.

## Key Results
- Zero-shot LLMs significantly underperform traditional CF models that have access to user interaction data
- Fine-tuned LLMs achieve comparable or better performance than traditional models using only a small fraction of the training data
- Larger LLMs (over 100B parameters) perform reasonably well in zero-shot cold-start scenarios
- Classification-based fine-tuning may be less effective than regression for rating prediction tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Fine-tuning LLMs on user interaction data enables them to learn dataset-specific preference patterns. Traditional CF methods rely on extensive user-item interaction matrices to capture latent preferences, while zero-shot LLMs lack this grounding. Fine-tuning injects the target dataset's interaction patterns into the LLM, allowing it to approximate CF performance with far less data. The core assumption is that user interaction data contains dataset-specific patterns that cannot be inferred from pre-training alone.

### Mechanism 2
Larger LLMs (over 100B parameters) perform better in zero-shot rating prediction due to richer world knowledge. Scale enables better generalization from pre-training, allowing larger models to leverage world knowledge about items to make reasonable predictions even without user interaction data. The core assumption is that pre-training on internet-scale text provides useful item-level knowledge that transfers to rating prediction.

### Mechanism 3
Classification-based fine-tuning may be less effective than regression-based fine-tuning for LLMs in rating prediction. LLMs are pre-trained on token classification tasks, making the classification objective more aligned with their architecture than regression. The core assumption is that the pre-training task influences the model's ability to adapt to new objectives.

### Open Question 1

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to two specific datasets (MovieLens-1M and Amazon-Books) with particular characteristics
- Does not address computational cost and practical deployment challenges of fine-tuning large LLMs
- Does not explore the impact of item metadata quality or completeness on zero-shot performance

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Fine-tuning LLMs improves rating prediction performance compared to zero-shot settings
- **Medium Confidence**: Larger LLMs (over 100B parameters) provide better zero-shot performance due to richer world knowledge
- **Medium Confidence**: Classification-based fine-tuning may be less effective than regression for rating prediction
