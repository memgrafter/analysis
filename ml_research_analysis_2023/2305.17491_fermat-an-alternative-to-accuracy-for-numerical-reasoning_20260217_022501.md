# FERMAT: An Alternative to Accuracy for Numerical Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17491
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17491
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: FERMAT evaluates numerical reasoning models across number understanding, mathematical operations, and training dependency aspects, revealing weaknesses masked by single-score accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FERMAT, a multi-view evaluation set for numerical reasoning that addresses the limitations of single-score evaluations by assessing models on three key aspects: number understanding, mathematical operations, and training dependency. FERMAT provides a more comprehensive understanding of model strengths and weaknesses by decomposing numerical reasoning into orthogonal sub-skills rather than relying on aggregate accuracy. Experiments demonstrate that fine-tuning smaller models on FERMAT's training data with diverse templates outperforms larger models fine-tuned on various mathematical datasets, highlighting the importance of template diversity and number encoding quality.

## Method Summary
FERMAT evaluates numerical reasoning through three orthogonal aspects: number understanding (various representations and ranges), mathematical operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, combinations), and training dependency (how training exposure affects performance). The evaluation set derives from Illinois and CommonCore datasets with 1111 unique instances. Training data generation uses 100 expert templates plus 155 from GSM8K and 71 from AQUA, producing 200K synthetic examples covering uniform distribution over four common number types. Models are fine-tuned using Huggingface with digit tokenization, learning rate of 5e-5, weight decay of 0.005, warm-up of 100, and 3 generation beams.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning smaller models on FERMAT's Base training data outperforms larger models like BHASKARA fine-tuned on diverse mathematical datasets.
- Template diversity (expert + GSM8K + AQUA) improves performance by over 21% on certain aspects compared to using expert templates alone.
- Models struggle significantly with multi-digit numbers and decimals, even when explicitly trained on mathematical datasets.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FERMAT's multi-view evaluation exposes model weaknesses that single-score accuracy masks.
- Mechanism: By decomposing numerical reasoning into orthogonal aspects, FERMAT reveals performance gaps on specific number ranges, representations, and operations.
- Core assumption: A model's overall accuracy score does not reliably indicate its competence across all numerical reasoning sub-skills.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Instead of reporting a single score on a whole dataset, FERMAT evaluates models on various key numerical reasoning aspects..."
- Break condition: If the model's performance on a single aspect is already near-perfect, further multi-view analysis yields diminishing returns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Template diversity in training data improves numerical reasoning generalization more than scale.
- Mechanism: Using varied expert, GSM8K, and AQUA templates increases linguistic and mathematical diversity, enabling models to generalize across different number encodings and expression forms.
- Core assumption: Exposure to diverse template sources improves the model's ability to map language to mathematical operations.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "fine-tuning smaller commonly used models on this training data outperforms larger models like BHASKARA..."
- Break condition: If the model overfits to template-specific patterns, diversity could degrade performance on unseen problem structures.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Number encoding quality strongly affects performance on multi-digit and decimal operations.
- Mechanism: Models struggle when numbers exceed single digits or involve decimals; improving digit tokenization and magnitude handling boosts accuracy.
- Core assumption: Current tokenizers inadequately represent large numbers and decimals, causing reasoning errors.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "even the larger models that are explicitly trained on maths datasets, i.e., BHASKARA and FLAN-XL, perform poorly on numbers that contain more than one digit..."
- Break condition: If the model uses specialized number-aware tokenization (e.g., NT5), the encoding bottleneck may be less critical.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multi-view evaluation
  - Why needed here: To systematically identify strengths and weaknesses across numerical reasoning sub-skills rather than relying on aggregate accuracy.
  - Quick check question: What are the three orthogonal aspects FERMAT evaluates?

- Concept: Template-based data generation
  - Why needed here: Enables controlled creation of training/evaluation data for each numerical reasoning aspect without manual annotation.
  - Quick check question: From which sources are FERMAT's training templates drawn?

- Concept: Training dependency classification
  - Why needed here: To measure how exposure to specific numbers/operations during training influences test performance.
  - Quick check question: What is the least common class in FERMAT's training dependency taxonomy and why?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - FERMAT evaluation engine -> handles aspect-based scoring and template-driven generation
  - Training data generator -> produces synthetic examples from expert, GSM8K, and AQUA templates
  - Model interface -> wraps zero-shot and fine-tuned inference pipelines
  - Hyperparameter tuner -> manages early stopping and learning rate schedules

- Critical path:
  1. Load evaluation set and templates
  2. Generate training data per aspect
  3. Fine-tune model with digit tokenizer
  4. Evaluate on all FERMAT aspects
  5. Analyze training dependency overlap

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using Illinois/CommonCore limits number diversity but ensures consistent difficulty
  - Restricting output to 12 digits avoids infinite decimals but excludes some real-world cases
  - Prompt-based evaluation introduces variability; averaging over prompts mitigates this

- Failure signatures:
  - Zero-shot accuracy below 5% on most aspects → model lacks basic numerical reasoning
  - High variance across prompts → prompts poorly aligned to task format
  - Strong performance only on "Exact" training dependency class → model overfits to seen numbers

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Zero-shot evaluate all candidate models on FERMAT to establish baselines
  2. Fine-tune FLAN-base on Base set and measure gains per aspect
  3. Compare Base vs. Base Diversified to quantify impact of template diversity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of models on FERMAT's "Same numbers" subset compare when using different number encodings that are invariant to operand permutation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that models' performances on the "Commuted" aspect within the "Same numbers" subset are considerably lower than other aspects, indicating a potential for developing better number encodings.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results comparing different number encodings on this specific aspect.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing model performance on the "Commuted" aspect using various number encoding schemes would clarify if encoding improvements can address this weakness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of linguistic diversity in training templates on model performance for numerical reasoning tasks with complex language requirements?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the impact of template diversity on model performance, noting that linguistic and mathematical diversity seem key to improvement. However, it suggests mathematical variation may be more central than language diversity.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not conclusively determine whether linguistic diversity alone, without mathematical diversity, significantly improves performance on complex language tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments training models on linguistically diverse templates with identical mathematical operations would isolate the effect of linguistic diversity on performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the distribution of numbers in training data affect model performance on numerical reasoning tasks, particularly for rare numbers?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses training dependency classification and notes that seeing numbers during training plays an important role in solving their corresponding operations. It also mentions that the distribution of the Original set is highly skewed towards smaller integers.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide detailed analysis of how the frequency of specific numbers in training data correlates with model performance on those numbers in testing.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive analysis correlating the frequency of each number in training data with model performance on corresponding test instances would clarify the impact of number distribution on numerical reasoning capabilities.

## Limitations
- The evaluation set's reliance on Illinois and CommonCore datasets, which are skewed toward smaller integers, may not adequately represent real-world numerical reasoning challenges.
- The paper does not provide detailed analysis of how FERMAT's performance correlates with downstream mathematical problem-solving capabilities or other numerical domains.
- Major uncertainties remain regarding the generalizability of FERMAT's findings beyond English numerical reasoning tasks.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The mechanism that multi-view evaluation reveals model weaknesses masked by aggregate accuracy scores, supported by direct experimental evidence showing performance gaps across FERMAT's three aspects.
- Medium confidence: The template diversity hypothesis, as the paper shows improved performance but lacks ablation studies isolating the specific contribution of different template sources.
- Low confidence: The claim that digit tokenization quality is the primary bottleneck for multi-digit number handling, as the paper provides only qualitative observations without systematic comparison to alternative tokenization approaches.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct a comprehensive ablation study comparing FERMAT performance when using different template sources (expert-only vs. combined GSM8K/AQUA) to isolate the specific contribution of linguistic diversity versus mathematical diversity.

2. Extend FERMAT evaluation to non-English datasets and larger numerical ranges (10^6+) to assess cross-linguistic and scale generalizability of the findings about template diversity and number encoding effects.

3. Implement a controlled experiment comparing FERMAT-trained models against models trained with specialized number-aware tokenization schemes (like NT5) to definitively assess whether digit tokenization or template diversity drives the observed performance improvements.
