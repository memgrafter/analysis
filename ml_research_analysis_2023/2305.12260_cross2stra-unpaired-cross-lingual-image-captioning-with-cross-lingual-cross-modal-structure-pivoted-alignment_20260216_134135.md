# Cross2StrA: Unpaired Cross-lingual Image Captioning with Cross-lingual Cross-modal Structure-pivoted Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12260
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12260
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Improves English-Chinese cross-lingual image captioning by 3.4-4.1 BLEU scores and 2.0 CIDEr scores through semantic scene graph and syntactic tree guided alignment.

## Executive Summary
Cross2StrA addresses unpaired cross-lingual image captioning by incorporating semantic scene graphs and syntactic constituency trees as pivots. The method improves content relevancy and language fluency by performing cross-modal semantic structure alignment and cross-lingual syntactic structure alignment. Experiments show significant improvements over existing methods on English-Chinese transfers.

## Method Summary
The model consists of two main stages: semantic structure-guided image-to-pivot captioning and syntactic structure-guided pivot-to-target translation. It leverages scene graphs for better image-text alignment and syntactic trees for better pivot-target language alignment. Cross-modal semantic structure alignment and cross-lingual syntactic structure alignment learning are performed via contrastive learning. Cross-lingual and cross-modal back-translation training further aligns the captioning and translation stages.

## Key Results
- Outperforms existing methods by 3.4-4.1 BLEU scores and 2.0 CIDEr scores
- Improves both content relevancy and language fluency
- Effective structure alignment learning through scene graphs and syntactic trees

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Scene graphs and syntactic trees improve cross-modal semantic and cross-lingual syntactic alignment
- These structures serve as pivots for vision-language and language-language alignment
- Core assumption: Scene graph and syntactic tree structures are sufficiently informative to serve as effective pivots

### Mechanism 2
- Contrastive learning aligns scene graphs and syntactic trees by encouraging similar nodes to be closer
- This unsupervised alignment learning mitigates scene inconsistency and language disfluency
- Core assumption: Contrastive learning effectively captures similarity between corresponding nodes

### Mechanism 3
- Cross-lingual and cross-modal back-translation aligns the captioning and translation stages
- Image-to-pivot back-translation generates pseudo pivot captions from predicted target captions
- Pivot-to-target back-translation generates pseudo target captions from gold pivot captions

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image -> Scene graph encoder -> Image-to-pivot captioning -> Pivot caption -> Syntactic constituency tree encoder -> Pivot-to-target translation -> Target caption
- Critical path: Image to Target caption through two-stage pivoting with structure-guided alignment
- Design tradeoffs:
  - External structure parsers introduce dependency on their quality
  - Contrastive learning objectives and back-translation add training complexity
  - Warm-start training requires careful tuning of loss weights
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor visual-language alignment leads to irrelevant captions
  - Poor syntactic alignment leads to disfluent captions
  - Unstable training results in inconsistent quality
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate scene graph and syntactic tree parser quality on held-out data
  2. Train captioning and translation models separately and evaluate individually
  3. Perform ablation studies on alignment learning strategies

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- How do SG and SC structures perform without paired training data in pivot language?
- The paper mentions this fully unsupervised setup but provides no empirical results
- Empirical comparison of model performance with and without pivot language pairs would resolve this

### Open Question 2
- What is the impact of external structure parser quality on model performance?
- The paper notes the method depends on parser quality but doesn't quantify this relationship
- Empirical results showing performance with parsers of varying quality would resolve this

### Open Question 3
- How does the model perform in other language pairs besides English-Chinese?
- The paper only provides results for English-Chinese transfers
- Empirical results for other language pairs would resolve this

## Limitations

- Effectiveness depends heavily on quality of external structure parsers
- Back-translation introduces dependency on external translators and image generators
- Specific contrastive learning implementation details are not fully specified

## Confidence

- High confidence in overall two-stage pivoting framework architecture
- Medium confidence in alignment learning mechanisms
- Medium confidence in improvement claims (3.4-4.1 BLEU, 2.0 CIDEr)
