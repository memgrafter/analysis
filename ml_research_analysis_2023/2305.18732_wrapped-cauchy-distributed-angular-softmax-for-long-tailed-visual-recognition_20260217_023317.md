## Executive Summary
The paper introduces Wrapped Cauchy Distributed Angular Softmax (WCDAS), a novel approach to handle long-tailed visual recognition tasks. WCDAS addresses the challenges of class imbalance by incorporating data-wise Gaussian-based kernels into the angular correlation between feature representations and classifier weights. This method mitigates noise and sparse sampling concerns, leading to improved performance on benchmark datasets. The class-wise distribution of angular representation is approximated using the wrapped Cauchy distribution, with trainable concentration parameters dynamically adjusting the compactness and margin of each class.

## Method Summary
WCDAS is designed to tackle the problem of class imbalance in visual recognition tasks. It incorporates data-wise Gaussian-based kernels into the angular correlation between feature representations and classifier weights, mitigating noise and sparse sampling concerns. The class-wise distribution of angular representation becomes a sum of these kernels, and the wrapped Cauchy distribution is used to approximate the mixed distribution of these kernels. Additionally, WCDAS uses trainable concentration parameters to dynamically adjust the compactness and margin of each class. This approach demonstrates superior performance over state-of-the-art softmax-based methods across multiple benchmark datasets, including CIFAR10-LT/100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist 2018.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
WCDAS works by incorporating data-wise Gaussian-based kernels into the angular correlation between feature representations and classifier weights. This approach mitigates noise and sparse sampling concerns, which are common issues in imbalanced datasets. By using the wrapped Cauchy distribution to approximate the mixed distribution of these kernels, WCDAS can better model the class-wise distribution of angular representation. The trainable concentration parameters allow for dynamic adjustment of the compactness and margin of each class, further improving the model's ability to handle class imbalance.
