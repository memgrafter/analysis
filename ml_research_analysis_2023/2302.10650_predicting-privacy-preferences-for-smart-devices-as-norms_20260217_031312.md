# Predicting Privacy Preferences for Smart Devices as Norms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10650
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10650
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Mean distance from real answers 0.5954 vs 1.0437 for existing methods

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a collaborative filtering approach to predict user privacy preferences for smart devices and encode these as norms to regulate device behavior. The method leverages similarities between users to infer unknown preferences, then converts these into permission/prohibition norms. Using a dataset of 1737 smart assistant users, the approach achieves more accurate predictions than random guessing and existing methods, with a mean distance of 0.5954 from real answers. The predicted norms are also rated as significantly more appropriate by users than randomly generated norms.

## Method Summary
The method uses collaborative filtering to predict unknown user preferences by identifying similar users with known preferences. It employs a cumulative user separation function to find similar users, then averages their preferences to predict unknown ones. The predictions are converted into norms using hard or confidence-based thresholds. The approach requires large quantities of users and partial preferences for each user, with accuracy improving as more data is available. A confidence function based on user separation and preference distribution helps identify reliable predictions for norm generation.

## Key Results
- Prediction accuracy mean distance of 0.5954 vs 1.0437 for existing methods
- Predicted norms rated 24% closer to perfect acceptance than random norms
- Confidence scores show inverse correlation with prediction error

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The paper's collaborative filtering approach improves privacy preference prediction accuracy by leveraging similarity between users.
- Mechanism: The method identifies users with similar known preferences and uses their collective preferences to predict unknown ones, reducing reliance on direct user input which often yields inaccurate or default responses.
- Core assumption: Users with similar known preferences will also have similar unknown preferences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Using a dataset of privacy preferences for smart assistants, the authors test their approach and find that their predictions are more accurate than random guesses and existing methods, with a mean distance from the real answer of 0.5954 (compared to 1.0437 for existing methods)."
  - [section] "Using a dataset of privacy preferences of smart assistant users, we test the accuracy of our predictions."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighbor papers focus on privacy perceptions but do not validate collaborative filtering specifically.
- Break condition: If the assumption that similar known preferences imply similar unknown preferences fails, predictions will degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Norms inferred from predicted preferences regulate smart device behavior more effectively than user defaults.
- Mechanism: Predicted preferences are converted into permission/prohibition norms that guide device interactions with data, aligning device behavior with actual user expectations.
- Core assumption: Numerical preference scores can be reliably mapped to norm structures (permission or prohibition).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "They also show that their predicted norms are rated as significantly more appropriate by users than randomly generated norms, eliminating 24% of the difference between control ratings and perfect acceptance."
  - [section] "Norms can effectively summarise complex privacy preferences into simple sets of regulations, as shown by Abdi et al. [2], who gathered over 800 privacy preferences on data transmissions, yet produced just 17 norms."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; neighbor papers discuss privacy norms conceptually but lack empirical comparison to norms derived from collaborative filtering.
- Break condition: If mapping from preferences to norms introduces significant error, norm appropriateness will drop.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Confidence in predictions correlates inversely with prediction error, enabling selective norm generation.
- Mechanism: The paper defines a confidence function based on user separation and preference distribution, which informs threshold adjustments for norm creation to avoid low-confidence, potentially harmful norms.
- Core assumption: Higher confidence scores reliably indicate closer predictions to true user preferences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "While our confidence formula considers two weights 𝜌 and 𝜇, these depend on each application and could be adjusted at runtime to maximise correlation (i.e. to minimise the correlation coefficient)."
  - [section] "We can therefore detect an inverse correlation between confidence and prediction quality."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; neighbor papers do not discuss confidence metrics for collaborative filtering predictions.
- Break condition: If confidence function does not correlate well with actual accuracy, norm selection may be misguided.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Users → Preference profiles → Known/unknown preference separation → Similarity computation → Prediction function → Confidence evaluation → Norm inference → Device behavior enforcement
- Critical path: User data collection → Similarity assessment → Preference prediction → Confidence scoring → Norm generation → Device regulation
- Design tradeoffs: High user base improves prediction accuracy but increases computational load; confidence thresholds reduce norm errors but may leave some preferences unregulated.
- Failure signatures: High prediction distance from real answers, low confidence scores, user rejection of generated norms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the collaborative filtering approach be adapted to handle multiple users sharing the same smart device?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "we have assumed a single user for each device, but it is unclear how this method would apply when multiple users share the same device (for example, a family sharing a smart speaker)."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any methodology or experimental results for handling multiple users on a single device, leaving this as an open area for future work.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the accuracy of preference predictions and norm inference when applied to devices shared by multiple users, compared to single-user scenarios.

### Open Question 2

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific thresholds or experimental results defining the minimum requirements for the approach to be effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the accuracy of preference predictions and norm inference at different scales of users and preferences, identifying the point at which the approach becomes effective.

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any methodology or experimental results for incorporating rewards or punishments into the norm inference process.

## Limitations
- Dataset represents single cultural context, limiting generalizability
- Assumes linear mapping between numerical preferences and norm structures
- Confidence function effectiveness depends heavily on parameter tuning
- Does not address multi-user device scenarios

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The collaborative filtering approach improves prediction accuracy compared to random guessing and baseline methods
- Medium Confidence: Norms inferred from predicted preferences are rated as more appropriate by users than random norms
- Low Confidence: Confidence scores reliably indicate prediction accuracy
