# Physics-informed radial basis network (PIRBN): A local approximating neural network for solving nonlinear PDEs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06234
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06234
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: PIRBNs achieve superior performance to PINNs for PDEs with high-frequency features or ill-posed domains

## Executive Summary
Physics-informed radial basis networks (PIRBNs) address key limitations in physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for solving partial differential equations (PDEs). PINNs struggle with high-frequency features and ill-posed computational domains due to poor local approximation properties during training. PIRBNs overcome these limitations by using radial basis functions as activation functions, which inherently maintain local approximation properties. The approach demonstrates superior accuracy and faster convergence compared to PINNs across multiple challenging PDE problems, including nonlinear spring equations, wave equations, diffusion equations with ill-posed domains, and viscoelastic Poiseuille flow.

## Method Summary
PIRBNs replace the standard activation functions in neural networks with radial basis functions (RBFs) to maintain local approximation properties during training. The authors analyze PIRBN training dynamics using neural tangent kernel (NTK) theory, showing convergence to Gaussian processes under certain conditions. The method is validated across four challenging PDE problems: a 1D nonlinear spring equation with high-frequency features, a 2D wave equation, a diffusion equation with ill-posed domain, and a viscoelastic Poiseuille flow problem. Comprehensive investigations examine initialization strategies, number of sample points, and selection of radial basis functions to optimize performance.

## Key Results
- PIRBNs demonstrate higher accuracy than PINNs for solving PDEs with high-frequency features
- Faster convergence achieved compared to PINNs across all tested problems
- Superior performance in handling ill-posed computational domains
- NTK analysis shows theoretical convergence to Gaussian processes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PIRBNs work by leveraging the inherent local approximation properties of radial basis functions as activation functions. Unlike standard activation functions in PINNs that can lose local approximation during training, RBFs maintain their localized behavior, allowing the network to better capture high-frequency features and handle complex boundary conditions. The NTK analysis provides theoretical grounding for why this approach converges effectively.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Input -> Radial Basis Activation Layer -> Physics-Informed Loss Function -> Optimization

**Critical Path:** The critical path involves the radial basis activation function maintaining local approximation properties throughout training, combined with physics-informed loss that enforces PDE constraints.

**Design Tradeoffs:** RBFs provide better local approximation but may increase computational complexity compared to standard activations. The choice of specific RBF type impacts both accuracy and training efficiency.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor RBF selection may lead to inadequate approximation of high-frequency features. Insufficient sample points can result in loss of physics constraints. Improper initialization may cause training instability.

## Limitations
- Analysis primarily based on numerical experiments rather than comprehensive theoretical guarantees
- Limited exploration of different radial basis function types across diverse PDE categories
- Scalability to higher-dimensional problems and computational efficiency in large-scale applications not thoroughly investigated
