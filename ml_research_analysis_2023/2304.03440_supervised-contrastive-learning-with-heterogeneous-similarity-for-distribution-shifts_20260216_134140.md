# Supervised Contrastive Learning with Heterogeneous Similarity for Distribution Shifts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03440
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03440
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposed method achieves 86.56% worst-group accuracy on CelebA vs 82.78% for stochastic depth

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of distribution shift, where data distribution changes between training and testing, causing model performance degradation. The authors propose a new regularization method for supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) that uses heterogeneous similarity measures. They extend the cosine similarity to a more general t-vMF similarity and propose using different parameters when comparing samples to positive and negative examples, creating a margin effect in the contrastive loss.

The method is evaluated on benchmark datasets for subpopulation shift (CelebA) and domain generalization (Camelyon17, iWildCam). Results show that the proposed method prevents performance degradation on out-of-distribution data and improves worst-group accuracy compared to standard regularization techniques. The method is particularly effective when there is significant performance degradation on OOD data during training.

## Method Summary
The proposed method extends supervised contrastive learning by using t-vMF similarity instead of cosine similarity, with different parameters (κp, κn) for positive and negative pairs. The t-vMF similarity is a generalization of cosine similarity that depends on a concentration parameter κ. When κp > κn, this creates a margin effect in the contrastive loss that acts as regularization. The method is combined with standard cross-entropy loss and tested with backbone ResNet18 on three datasets: CelebA for subpopulation shift, Camelyon17 for domain generalization, and iWildCam for additional domain generalization evaluation.

## Key Results
- CelebA: Proposed method achieves 86.56% worst-group accuracy vs 82.78% for stochastic depth
- Camelyon17: 80.24% accuracy on OOD splits vs 54.97% without regularization
- iWildCam: Proposed method improves accuracy when performance degradation is observed during training, but shows no significant improvement when no degradation occurs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using different t-vMF similarity parameters (κp, κn) with κp > κn creates a margin effect in contrastive loss.
- Mechanism: The contrastive loss with t-vMF similarity requires positive and negative pairs to be separated by a margin ϵ when κp > κn. This margin acts as regularization that improves robustness to distribution shifts.
- Core assumption: The t-vMF similarity function behaves as described mathematically and the margin ϵ is meaningful in practice.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "extend the cosine similarity in contrastive loss to a more general similarity measure and propose to use different parameters in the measure when comparing a sample to a positive or negative example, which is analytically shown to act as a kind of margin in contrastive loss"
  - [section]: "If we define δ := φκn (fθ(x), fθ(xn))− φκp (fθ(x), fθ(xp)), it is necessary to make δ lower to reduce ˜Lcl. Now we denote the angles that x makes with xp and xn as ψp and ψn, respectively... Therefore, if κp > κn, ψp must be less than ψn by some positive margin ϵ to make δ < 0"
  - [corpus]: "weak evidence - only mentions t-vMF similarity in CREMA paper but no margin discussion"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Supervised contrastive learning with heterogeneous similarity prevents overfitting to majority groups or in-distribution domains.
- Mechanism: By forcing embeddings of the same class to cluster more tightly while maintaining separation from other classes, the model learns representations that are invariant to the specific data distribution used in training.
- Core assumption: The clustering behavior described actually improves generalization rather than just changing the embedding geometry.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Distribution shifts are problems where the distribution of data changes between training and testing... Recent studies suggest that one reason for the degradation is a type of overfitting, and that proper regularization can mitigate the degradation"
  - [section]: "Therefore, one of the major differences between CE and SupCon is whether or not samples of the same class are intentionally being gathered... SupCon tries to learn embeddings that are invariant for the same class, while preserving the features necessary to distinguish classes"
  - [corpus]: "weak evidence - no direct mention of overfitting prevention in corpus papers"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The proposed method is more effective than standard regularization techniques for preventing distribution shift degradation.
- Mechanism: By modifying the contrastive loss structure itself rather than applying external regularization, the method addresses the root cause of distribution shift issues in representation learning.
- Core assumption: The improvement over standard regularization is statistically significant and not due to random variation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Experimental results also show that the proposed method is more effective than the conventional regularization for DNNs"
  - [section]: "Table 2 summarizes the results, evaluating the test accuracy of the worst group at the end of training... The proposed method achieves comparable or better results than existing regularization methods, independent of learning rate and loss type"
  - [corpus]: "weak evidence - no direct comparison to regularization methods in corpus papers"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input -> Backbone (ResNet18) -> Projection head -> Contrastive loss -> Backpropagation
- Critical path: Input → Backbone → Projection head → Contrastive loss → Backpropagation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using t-vMF similarity vs cosine similarity: more expressive but adds hyperparameter κ
  - Separate κp and κn: creates margin effect but requires tuning two parameters
  - Combined with group DRO: addresses both representation learning and group fairness
- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation on OOD data similar to baseline indicates margin effect not working
  - Worsening ID performance suggests over-regularization
  - No improvement over standard SupCon suggests κp = κn configuration
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with standard SupCon (cosine similarity) vs proposed method on CelebA to verify worst-group accuracy improvement
  2. Vary α parameter (0.05, 0.1, 0.15) to find optimal margin strength
  3. Test on iWildCam dataset to verify method works when no degradation is observed during training

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the theoretical basis for why using different parameters (κp and κn) in the t-vMF similarity function is necessary for the proposed method to be effective?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "there was no significant difference in test accuracy between using cosine similarity and t-vMF similarity as long as κp = κn" and that "it is essential to use different κ for the positive and negative samples, which leads to a kind of margin effect."
- Why unresolved: The paper provides some intuition about the margin effect but does not provide a rigorous mathematical proof or theoretical analysis explaining why this difference is necessary.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal mathematical proof or theoretical analysis showing that the margin effect (and thus the improved performance) only occurs when κp ≠ κn, along with empirical evidence demonstrating the difference in performance when κp = κn versus κp ≠ κn.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed method perform on other types of distribution shifts beyond subpopulation shift and domain generalization, such as label shift or covariate shift?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only evaluates the method on subpopulation shift (CelebA) and domain generalization (Camelyon17, iWildCam) datasets, but mentions that distribution shifts can take various forms.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to specific types of distribution shifts, and the paper does not discuss or test the method's performance on other types of shifts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating the proposed method on datasets with different types of distribution shifts (e.g., label shift, covariate shift) and comparing its performance to other methods designed for these specific shifts.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of the proposed method on the model's interpretability and explainability?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on improving model performance under distribution shifts but does not discuss the potential impact on interpretability or explainability.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not address how the proposed method affects the model's internal representations or decision-making process, which are important aspects of interpretability and explainability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of the model's internal representations (e.g., using techniques like feature visualization or attribution methods) to understand how the proposed method affects the model's decision-making process and whether it improves or hinders interpretability.

## Limitations
- The proposed t-vMF similarity with heterogeneous parameters is only validated on three datasets, limiting generalizability claims
- No ablation study showing the individual contribution of κp > κn versus other factors like projection head architecture or temperature scaling
- The mathematical margin analysis assumes specific geometric properties of the t-vMF distribution that may not hold in high-dimensional embedding spaces

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The mathematical formulation of t-vMF similarity and its relationship to margin creation is sound and well-supported by the theoretical analysis
- **Medium Confidence**: The empirical improvements over baseline regularization methods, though consistent, need more extensive validation across diverse domain shift scenarios
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about the method's effectiveness when no performance degradation is observed during training are based on limited evidence (single dataset, iWildCam)
