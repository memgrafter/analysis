# Classifying COVID-19 Related Tweets for Fake News Detection and Sentiment Analysis with BERT-based Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00636
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00636
- Reference count: 18
- Achieved 0.93 accuracy for sentiment analysis and 0.90 for fake news detection

## Executive Summary
This study applies BERT-based models to Arabic sentiment analysis and fake news detection tasks using COVID-19 related tweets. The authors employed fine-tuning techniques with pre-trained BERT models, achieving high performance on both tasks. For sentiment analysis, they used 4,128 tweets with balanced classes, while the fake news detection task used 8,661 tweets with significant class imbalance. The research demonstrates the effectiveness of transfer learning approaches for Arabic NLP tasks in the context of pandemic-related social media content.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tuned pre-trained BERT models for Arabic text classification tasks. For sentiment analysis, they used bert-mini-arabic on 4,128 tweets, while fake news detection employed bert-base-arabic on 8,661 tweets. The preprocessing pipeline removed URLs, punctuation, and emojis while preserving semantic structure and digits. Fine-tuning parameters included batch size of 10, learning rate of 2e-5, AdamW optimizer, 10 training epochs, and maximum sequence length of 256. The dataset for sentiment analysis was balanced across neutral, positive, and negative classes, while fake news detection showed significant class imbalance with 58% in the "can't decide" category.

## Key Results
- Sentiment analysis achieved 0.93 accuracy using bert-mini-arabic
- Fake news detection achieved 0.90 accuracy using bert-base-arabic
- The fake news dataset showed significant class imbalance (58% "can't decide" vs 4% "yes")
- Pre-trained BERT models demonstrated strong performance on Arabic COVID-19 social media content

## Limitations

- The study uses a relatively small dataset (4,128 tweets) that may not capture full complexity of Arabic COVID-19 discourse
- The fake news detection dataset shows significant class imbalance (58% "can't decide" vs 4% "yes") that could bias model performance
- Preprocessing removes emojis and certain punctuation, potentially eliminating important sentiment signals
- The use of BERT-mini for sentiment analysis and BERT-base for fake news detection introduces unexplained architectural inconsistency

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The general approach of using pre-trained BERT models for Arabic NLP tasks is well-established and the methodology follows standard fine-tuning practices
- **Medium Confidence**: The reported accuracy scores (0.93 for sentiment, 0.90 for fake news detection) appear reasonable but lack cross-validation and statistical significance testing
- **Low Confidence**: The preprocessing choices and hyperparameter selections are not validated against alternatives, making it unclear if these represent optimal configurations
