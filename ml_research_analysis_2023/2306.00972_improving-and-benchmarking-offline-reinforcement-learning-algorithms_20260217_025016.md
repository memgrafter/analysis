# Improving and Benchmarking Offline Reinforcement Learning Algorithms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00972
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00972
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: CQL+ and CRR+ achieve state-of-the-art performance on D4RL, with CQL+ improving by 5% and CRR+ by 33.8% over original versions

## Executive Summary
This work systematically studies how low-level implementation choices affect offline RL algorithm performance, identifying 20 critical design decisions. Through careful tuning, the authors develop improved variants CQL+ and CRR+ that achieve state-of-the-art results on D4RL. The study also benchmarks eight popular offline RL algorithms across both D4RL and RL Unplugged datasets under a unified JAX framework, revealing that algorithm success strongly depends on data distribution properties. Algorithms with AWR-style policy improvement generalize best across datasets, while overly conservative methods perform worse on replay datasets.

## Method Summary
The authors implement eight offline RL algorithms (BC, SAC, TD3+BC, CQL, CRR, IQL, OnestepRL, MuZero Unplugged) in JAX with shared network architecture (3-layer MLP, 256 hidden units) and unified data preprocessing. They conduct comprehensive ablation studies on 20 low-level implementation choices including weight initialization, learning rates, activation functions, and normalization schemes. The improved variants CQL+ and CRR+ are developed through careful tuning of these choices. All algorithms are evaluated on both D4RL and RL Unplugged datasets using last step running average return over 100 evaluation episodes and 3 random seeds.

## Key Results
- CQL+ improves over original CQL by 5% on D4RL benchmark
- CRR+ improves over original CRR by 33.8% on D4RL benchmark
- Algorithms with AWR-style policy improvement (IQL, CRR) generalize best across D4RL and RL Unplugged datasets
- SAC performs competitively on replay datasets, while CQL struggles due to overly conservative constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Implementation choices have multiplicative effects on algorithm performance. Specific architectural and training decisions (weight initialization, learning rates, activation functions, normalization) interact with conservative constraints to determine convergence stability and final performance.

### Mechanism 2
Dataset distribution fundamentally determines which algorithmic approaches succeed. Algorithms with implicit conservative constraints (AWR-style policy improvement) generalize better across data distributions because they minimally constrain exploration, while overly conservative methods struggle on replay datasets with continuous exploration spectra.

### Mechanism 3
Point estimation metrics (best/ending return) overestimate true algorithm performance and stability. Using last running average return captures both convergence quality and training stability, preventing selection of unstable but occasionally high-performing algorithms.

## Limitations
- Potential overfitting to specific D4RL and RL Unplugged datasets
- Evaluation framework relies on MLP architectures, potentially missing performance differences with more sophisticated network designs
- Focus exclusively on continuous control tasks limits applicability to discrete action spaces

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Implementation choices): Medium - Strong empirical evidence from ablations, but limited theoretical justification
- Mechanism 2 (Dataset distribution effects): Medium - Clear patterns observed but potential confounding factors not fully controlled
- Mechanism 3 (Evaluation metrics): High - Well-motivated and practically justified
