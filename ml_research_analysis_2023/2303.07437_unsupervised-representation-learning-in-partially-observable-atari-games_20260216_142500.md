# Unsupervised Representation Learning in Partially Observable Atari Games

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07437
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07437
- Authors: Multiple authors (specific names not provided in source)
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: MST-DIM outperforms ST-DIM and supervised learning in partially observable Atari games with 40% masking, achieving ~66% accuracy and ~64% F1 score compared to ~38%/33% for baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MST-DIM, a contrastive representation learning method that extends ST-DIM by incorporating random masking during pretraining to handle partially observable Atari games. The approach applies binomial masks to observations before computing contrastive losses, forcing the model to learn robust latent generative factors even when parts of the observation are missing. Experiments on 20 Atari 2600 games demonstrate that MST-DIM significantly outperforms both the original ST-DIM and supervised learning baselines when environments are not fully observable, with performance improving across different pretraining masking ratios.

## Method Summary
MST-DIM builds upon the ST-DIM framework by introducing random binomial masks during pretraining. For each consecutive observation pair (x_t, x_{t+1}), random masks k_t are drawn from a binomial distribution K and applied to the observations before computing contrastive losses. The method maintains ST-DIM's global-local and local-local contrastive objectives but now operates on masked observations. During probing, the same masking strategy is applied to evaluate representation quality. The encoder architecture remains a CNN-based representation network that maps raw pixels to latent features, with linear probes used to evaluate the learned representations by training classifiers on top of frozen features.

## Key Results
- With 40% masking during probing, MST-DIM achieves mean accuracy of ~66% and mean F1 score of ~64%
- These results considerably exceed supervised learning (~38% accuracy, ~33% F1) and ST-DIM (~38% accuracy, ~34% F1) under the same partially observable setup
- Different pretraining masking ratios (0.2, 0.6, 0.8) all improve results over ST-DIM
- MST-DIM achieves scores close to ST-DIM under fully observable conditions while maintaining strong performance under partial observability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
MST-DIM improves state representation learning in partially observable Atari games by introducing masked pretraining that forces the model to learn robust latent generative factors even when parts of the observation are missing. During pretraining, random masks are applied to observations before feeding them into the contrastive loss, simulating partial observability. The contrastive task (global-local and local-local) maximizes mutual information between consecutive masked and unmasked states. The core assumption is that latent factors needed for downstream tasks remain recoverable even when a fraction of input pixels are randomly masked, provided masking consistency between pretraining and probing.

### Mechanism 2
Masked pretraining with different masking ratios than probing still improves performance compared to no masking in pretraining. Even when pretraining masking ratio differs from probing ratio, the model gains regularization that helps it focus on most stable and generalizable features, improving robustness to missing information. The core assumption is that pretraining distribution with masked inputs overlaps sufficiently with probing distribution so that features learned under masking remain useful when some inputs are masked during probing.

### Mechanism 3
MST-DIM's global-local and local-local contrastive objectives are particularly effective at preserving small object features in partially observable settings. The local-local contrastive task explicitly pulls together representations of nearby spatial patches across time, helping the model learn to represent small objects that might be partially occluded or masked. The global-local task ensures global context is also captured. The core assumption is that contrastive loss can still enforce feature consistency even when parts of observation are masked, as long as enough context remains.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Observation Encoder -> Mask Generator -> Contrastive Loss Modules (Global-Local, Local-Local) -> Probe Classifier

- Critical path:
  1. Generate or load masked observation pairs (x_t ⊙ k_t, x_{t+1})
  2. Pass through encoder φ to get φ(x_t ⊙ k_t) and φ(x_{t+1})
  3. Compute local features φ_m,n for each spatial location
  4. Compute global features via MLP
  5. Calculate global-local and local-local contrastive losses
  6. Backpropagate to update encoder parameters

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Masking ratio during pretraining vs probing: Higher pretraining masking can improve robustness but may degrade performance if too extreme
  - Local-local vs global-local emphasis: Local-local is better for small objects but may miss global context; global-local captures context but may dilute local details
  - Random masks vs structured occlusion: Random masks simulate partial observability but may not reflect real-world occlusion patterns

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy and F1 scores close to supervised baseline but much lower than fully observable ST-DIM → suggests pretraining masking not effective
  - Large drop in small object localization accuracy → suggests local-local contrastive loss not preserving small object features under masking
  - Performance improves with higher masking ratio up to a point, then degrades → suggests optimal masking ratio exists

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run MST-DIM with 0.4 masking in both pretraining and probing; compare accuracy/F1 to ST-DIM and supervised baselines
  2. Vary pretraining masking ratio (0.2, 0.6, 0.8) with fixed 0.4 probing; observe impact on performance
  3. Test MST-DIM with no masking in pretraining but 0.4 masking in probing; verify that masking during pretraining is necessary for improvement

## Limitations
- The paper lacks comparison to other state-of-the-art contrastive methods for partially observable environments beyond ST-DIM
- The ablation study on pretraining masking ratios shows improvements but doesn't explore why specific ratios work or what happens at extremes
- The claim about preserving small object features through local-local contrastive tasks is weakly supported by available evidence

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: MST-DIM outperforms supervised learning and ST-DIM in partially observable settings with 40% masking (accuracy ~66%, F1 ~64% vs ~38%/33% for baselines)
- **Medium confidence**: Different pretraining masking ratios improve robustness, though optimal ratios aren't clearly established
- **Low confidence**: The specific mechanism by which local-local contrastive tasks preserve small object features under masking

## Next Checks

1. Test MST-DIM with pretraining masking ratios at extremes (0.1, 0.9, 0.95) to identify breaking points and optimal ranges

2. Compare MST-DIM against additional contrastive baselines (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo) in partially observable settings to establish relative performance

3. Conduct controlled experiments where specific small objects are systematically masked to quantify the impact on local-local contrastive learning effectiveness
