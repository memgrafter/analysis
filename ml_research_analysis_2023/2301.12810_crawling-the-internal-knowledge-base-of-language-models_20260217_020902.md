# Crawling the Internal Knowledge-Base of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12810
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12810
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: LMCRAWL extracts interpretable knowledge graphs from LMs with 83.3% precision on 1-hop graphs using GPT-3

## Executive Summary
LMCRAWL introduces a novel approach to extract interpretable knowledge graphs from large language models by "crawling" their internal knowledge base. The method decomposes knowledge extraction into sub-problems solved through few-shot in-context learning, generating relations and corresponding objects for seed entities while allowing for uncertainty handling. Evaluated on 100 seed entities with GPT-3, LMCRAWL demonstrates high precision in generating 1-hop knowledge graphs while uncovering facts outside standard KG schemas.

## Method Summary
LMCRAWL approaches knowledge extraction by breaking down the task into two main sub-problems: generating relevant relations for a given entity and then generating corresponding objects for each relation. The method employs few-shot in-context learning with carefully crafted prompts that allow the LM to output "Don't know" when uncertain about a relation or object. To increase recall, LMCRAWL incorporates paraphrasing techniques. The approach starts with seed entities and iteratively expands the knowledge graph by crawling through the LM's internal knowledge base.

## Key Results
- Achieves 83.3% precision for 1-hop knowledge graph extraction
- Generates a reasonable number of facts per entity while maintaining high precision
- Successfully uncovers facts outside standard knowledge graph schemas
- Demonstrates effectiveness with 100 seed entities using GPT-3

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
LMCRAWL works by leveraging the LM's inherent knowledge through structured decomposition of the extraction task. By breaking down knowledge retrieval into relation generation and object generation sub-tasks, the method allows the LM to focus on specific aspects of knowledge representation. The uncertainty handling through "Don't know" responses prevents hallucination and maintains precision, while paraphrasing increases recall by capturing synonymous expressions of the same knowledge.

## Foundational Learning

**Few-shot in-context learning**: Why needed - To enable the LM to understand the task without fine-tuning; Quick check - Test with varying numbers of examples (1-10) to find optimal performance

**Knowledge graph extraction**: Why needed - To transform unstructured LM knowledge into structured, interpretable format; Quick check - Verify extracted facts against ground truth KGs

**Uncertainty handling**: Why needed - To maintain precision by allowing the model to express uncertainty; Quick check - Measure precision drop when forcing predictions on uncertain queries

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Seed entities -> Relation generation -> Object generation -> Paraphrasing -> Knowledge Graph

**Critical path**: The core pipeline follows seed entity selection, relation generation, object generation, and knowledge graph assembly

**Design tradeoffs**: 
- Trade precision for recall through paraphrasing vs. maintaining strict entity-relation-object triples
- Balancing few-shot examples for task understanding vs. prompt length constraints
- Handling uncertainty vs. maximizing knowledge extraction coverage

**Failure signatures**: 
- Low precision due to overconfidence in uncertain relations
- Incomplete knowledge graphs from overly conservative uncertainty thresholds
- Redundancy in extracted facts from insufficient paraphrasing variation

## Limitations
- Relies on GPT-3, raising scalability and cost-effectiveness concerns for larger LMs
- Evaluation limited to 1-hop graphs, leaving multi-hop performance unexplored
- Unclear robustness to prompt variations, LM versions, and different domains
- No details provided on computational resources required for the approach

## Confidence

**High confidence** in the core methodology of decomposing knowledge extraction into sub-problems with uncertainty handling
**Medium confidence** in reported precision and recall metrics due to limited evaluation scope (100 entities, 1-hop graphs)
**Low confidence** in scalability and generalizability without further validation on larger LMs and multi-hop queries
