# Accuracy is not the only Metric that matters: Estimating the Energy Consumption of Deep Learning Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00897
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00897
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Introduces pipeline to estimate DL model energy prior to training, achieving R² > 0.99 for random layers but only 0.352 for real architectures.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method to estimate deep learning model energy consumption before training by decomposing models into layers and predicting per-layer energy from parameters and MAC counts. The approach achieves high accuracy (R² > 0.99) on randomly configured layers but significantly overestimates energy for real architectures (R² = 0.352). The study demonstrates that MAC count is the dominant predictor of energy and that simple regression models suffice for layer-wise prediction. However, generalizing from synthetic to real configurations remains challenging, highlighting the need for more diverse training data.

## Method Summary
The authors collected energy measurements for various layer types (Conv2d, MaxPool2d, Linear, activations) and full architectures (AlexNet, VGG variants) on CPU hardware. They trained separate regression models per layer type using features including layer parameters and MAC counts. For each layer, they built predictors (Linear, Polynomial Regression, Lasso) and summed predictions to estimate total model energy. The target variable was CPU energy consumption, scaled with MinMaxScaler. Models were evaluated on both random test data and real architecture configurations.

## Key Results
- Random layer configurations achieve R² > 0.99 for Conv2d and Linear layers when using MAC count as primary feature
- Real architectures show poor generalization with R² = 0.352, indicating systematic overestimation
- Adding real-layer configurations from AlexNet/VGG improved results to R² = 0.45
- MAC count alone achieves R² = 0.9977 for Conv2d energy prediction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Energy consumption can be decomposed into layer-wise contributions and predicted by regression models.
- Mechanism: Each layer type is modeled independently using regression on features (layer parameters + MAC count), then summed to estimate full-model energy.
- Core assumption: Layer energy contributions are additive and independent of execution order or inter-layer effects.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "capable of predicting the energy consumption of DL models by accumulating their estimated layer-wise energies." [section] "We then built a separate predictor for each layer type to estimate the energy consumption for each instance of that type of layer."
- Break condition: Non-additive effects (e.g., memory pressure, data reuse, parallelization) invalidate independence assumption.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: MAC count is the dominant predictor for energy consumption.
- Mechanism: Including MAC count in regression features dramatically improves prediction accuracy for convolutions and linear layers.
- Core assumption: Energy scales linearly with number of multiply-accumulate operations across hardware.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The study highlights the importance of MAC counts as energy predictors" [section] "the MAC count as the only input feature and achieves an R2 test score of 0.9977" (convolution)
- Break condition: Parallel execution, memory hierarchy, or architectural optimizations decouple MACs from energy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Linear/polynomial regression models suffice because layer energy relationships are nearly linear.
- Mechanism: Simple models (Linear/Polynomial Regression) with MinMaxScaler target yield high accuracy on random layer configs.
- Core assumption: Energy consumption exhibits smooth, low-order polynomial relationships with layer parameters.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "Due to its small scale, a MinMaxScaler is always applied to the target variable 'cpu-energy'." [section] "All models and transforms were taken from scikit-learn" with R² > 0.99 for random configurations.
- Break condition: Non-polynomial or discontinuous energy responses (e.g., cache effects) reduce model fidelity.

  - Why needed here: MAC count is used as a key predictor feature; understanding its relationship to energy is central to the model design.
  - Quick check question: How is MAC count related to FLOPs and why is it preferred for DL energy estimation?

  - Why needed here: Models are built using scikit-learn regressors; scaling normalizes MAC vs parameter magnitudes.
  - Quick check question: What is the effect of MinMaxScaler on the target variable vs StandardScaler on features?

- Concept: Layer-wise decomposition and additive modeling.
  - Why needed here: Full-model energy is computed as sum of predicted layer energies; this assumption underpins the pipeline.
  - Quick check question: Under what hardware conditions might layer-wise energy addition fail?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection -> Energy measurement -> Feature engineering -> Per-layer regression models -> Aggregation layer
- Critical path: 1. Generate random layer configs → 2. Measure CPU energy → 3. Compute MACs → 4. Train per-layer regressors → 5. Parse real model → 6. Predict layer energies → 7. Sum to full estimate
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simple regression vs. complex models: Chosen for interpretability and low training cost
  - CPU vs GPU measurement: CPU chosen for consistency and inference relevance
  - MAC count vs full feature set: MAC count often sufficient, simplifying models
- Failure signatures:
  - Overestimation for real architectures (R² = 0.352) indicates generalization gap
  - Negative R² for ReLU in real configs signals extrapolation failure
  - Large variance in cross-validation suggests unstable feature importance
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train and evaluate per-layer models on random configs only (baseline)
  2. Add real-layer measurements from AlexNet/VGG to training set; retrain and re-evaluate
  3. Replace Linear regressors with Lasso; compare feature importance and performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the impact of using GPUs instead of CPUs for energy estimation in deep learning models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on CPU energy consumption and acknowledges that measuring GPU energy would also be relevant, but does not explore this avenue.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any data or analysis on GPU energy consumption, which is crucial since GPUs are widely used in deep learning applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to measure and compare the energy consumption of deep learning models on both CPUs and GPUs would provide insights into the impact of using different hardware for energy estimation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do parallelization and memory optimizations affect the accuracy of MAC count as a predictor of energy consumption?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests that MAC count not always accurately represents energy consumption due to parallelization and memory optimizations.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not delve into the specifics of how these factors influence the relationship between MAC count and energy consumption.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the energy consumption of deep learning models with varying levels of parallelization and memory optimization, and comparing these results to MAC count predictions, would help understand the impact of these factors.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the inclusion of additional layer configurations from real-world architectures improve the generalization of energy prediction models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that integrating real-layer configurations into training data improved the performance of energy prediction models.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows some improvement, it does not explore the extent to which additional real-world configurations could further enhance model accuracy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically adding more diverse and representative layer configurations from various real-world architectures to the training data and evaluating the impact on prediction accuracy would provide insights into the potential benefits of this approach.

## Limitations
- Poor generalization from random to real architectures (R² drops from >0.99 to 0.352)
- CPU-only measurements may not capture GPU energy behaviors dominant in modern DL
- Layer independence assumption may fail due to hardware effects like memory hierarchy
- Limited training data from real architectures (only AlexNet, VGG variants)

## Confidence
- High Confidence in layer-wise prediction for random configurations: R² > 0.99 consistently achieved
- Medium Confidence in MAC count as primary energy predictor: Strong correlation shown but may not generalize across hardware
- Low Confidence in full-model energy estimation: Dramatic performance drop on real architectures indicates fundamental limitations
