# Explaining Hate Speech Classification with Model Agnostic Methods

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00021
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00021
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: BERT-LIME achieves 82.6% accuracy in classifying hate speech with interpretable explanations

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a methodology for detecting and explaining hate speech in social media texts using BERT for classification and LIME for interpretability. The approach combines a transformer-based classifier with post-hoc explanation techniques to provide both accurate predictions and understandable reasoning for each decision. The model is trained on a Twitter dataset with three classes (Hate, Offensive, None) and demonstrates that combining powerful classification with interpretable methods can effectively identify and explain hate speech content while revealing feature overlaps across classes.

## Method Summary
The methodology employs BERT as the primary classification model, trained on a Twitter dataset with three categories: Hate, Offensive, and None. The dataset was preprocessed by removing emojis, symbols, and stopwords while preserving hashtags, then sampled to 1,430 tweets per class. After training the BERT model to achieve classification, LIME was applied to generate local explanations for individual predictions by highlighting influential features. Manual evaluation of 150 tweets assessed the quality and correctness of both classifications and explanations, providing insights into how well the model handles feature overlap and contextual nuances across classes.

## Key Results
- Achieved 82.6% overall classification accuracy across three classes
- Manual evaluation showed 78% correct classification for Hate, 74% for Offensive, and 84% for None
- LIME successfully provided local explanations by highlighting influential features for individual predictions
- Overall error rate of 21% indicates moderate performance with room for improvement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The BERT-LIME combination works by leveraging BERT's powerful contextual understanding for accurate classification while using LIME's interpretable perturbation-based approach to explain individual predictions. BERT captures complex linguistic patterns and contextual relationships in hate speech, while LIME provides feature-level explanations that make the model's decisions transparent. The methodology addresses the challenge of explaining black-box models by creating a post-hoc interpretability layer that identifies which words or phrases most influenced each prediction, allowing users to understand not just what the model decided, but why it made that decision.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Twitter dataset -> Preprocessing (cleaning, tokenization) -> BERT classifier -> LIME explainer -> Manual evaluation

**Critical path:** The core workflow follows: data preprocessing → BERT training → prediction generation → LIME explanation generation → manual evaluation of explanations

**Design tradeoffs:** The approach prioritizes interpretability over maximum accuracy, accepting a moderate 82.6% accuracy in exchange for understandable explanations. This tradeoff makes sense for applications where trust and transparency are as important as raw performance.

**Failure signatures:** Poor performance likely stems from inadequate preprocessing, imbalanced classes, or insufficient context in short tweets. LIME explanations may be unclear if features are ambiguous or if the underlying BERT model is uncertain about predictions.
