# Unlabeled Imperfect Demonstrations in Adversarial Imitation Learning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UID (Unlabeled Imperfect Demonstrations), a method for imitation learning that can effectively learn from imperfect demonstrations where only a fraction are optimal. The core innovation treats imperfect demonstrations as unlabeled data and dynamically samples demonstrations that best match the current policy's behavior. A discriminator is trained to distinguish between expert demonstrations and policy demonstrations using positive-unlabeled learning principles, while the policy is trained to maximize the discriminator's output. The method is evaluated on MuJoCo and RoboSuite tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to baseline methods when handling imperfect demonstrations.

## Method Summary
UID treats imperfect demonstrations as unlabeled data in an adversarial imitation learning framework. The method uses positive-unlabeled (PU) learning to estimate a discriminator that distinguishes between expert demonstrations and policy trajectories. The discriminator's training objective is based on PU learning principles, treating unlabeled demonstrations as a mixture of optimal and non-optimal samples. The policy is trained to maximize the discriminator's output, effectively learning to "cheat" the discriminator by producing trajectories similar to optimal demonstrations. UID can be extended to various adversarial imitation learning frameworks by defining different f-divergences, such as Jensen-Shannon divergence for GAIL and Total Variation for WAIL.

## Key Results
- UID outperforms baseline methods when dealing with imperfect demonstrations across MuJoCo and RoboSuite tasks
- Performance remains robust across varying ratios of optimal to non-optimal demonstrations (3:1, 1:1)
- UID effectively learns from both stage 1 and stage 2 demonstrations in the staged learning setup

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: UID improves adversarial imitation learning by treating imperfect demonstrations as unlabeled data and dynamically sampling those that match the current policy's behavior
- Mechanism: UID uses positive-unlabeled (PU) learning to estimate a discriminator that distinguishes between expert demonstrations and policy trajectories, while the policy is trained to maximize the discriminator's output
- Core assumption: The optimal demonstrations in the unlabeled set can be dynamically identified and sampled based on their similarity to the current policy's behavior
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "A positive-unlabeled adversarial imitation learning algorithm is developed to dynamically sample expert demonstrations that can well match the trajectories from the constantly optimized agent policy."
  - [section]: "Instead of treating imperfect expert demonstrations as absolutely positive or negative, we investigate unlabeled imperfect expert demonstrations as they are."
- Break condition: If the ratio of optimal to non-optimal demonstrations is too low, the discriminator may not be able to effectively distinguish between them, leading to poor policy learning

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: UID learns from imperfect demonstrations via a self-paced way, gradually covering more demonstrations in the unlabeled set
- Mechanism: At the early training stage, the policy produces demonstrations similar to the non-optimal demonstrations. However, as training progresses, the policy is optimized to "cheat" the discriminator and produce demonstrations resembling those optimal demonstrations
- Core assumption: The policy can effectively learn from the non-optimal demonstrations at the early stage and gradually improve its performance to match the optimal demonstrations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The trajectories of an initial agent policy could be closer to those non-optimal expert demonstrations, but within the framework of adversarial imitation learning, agent policy will be optimized to cheat the discriminator and produce trajectories that are similar to those optimal expert demonstrations."
  - [section]: "Theoretical analysis shows that our method learns from the imperfect demonstrations via a self-paced way."
- Break condition: If the policy fails to improve its performance over time, it may get stuck in a local optimum that resembles the non-optimal demonstrations

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: UID is compatible with various adversarial imitation learning frameworks by defining different f-divergences in Theorem 1
- Mechanism: UID can be extended into more adversarial imitation learning frameworks by defining different f-divergences, such as Jensen-Shannon divergence for GAIL and Total Variation for WAIL
- Core assumption: The choice of f-divergence does not significantly impact the performance of UID, as long as it provides a meaningful measure of the distance between the policy and expert distributions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We also make this technology compatible with various adversarial imitation learning baselines."
  - [section]: "Since UID can be extended into more adversarial imitation learning frameworks by defining different f-divergences in Theorem 1, we test the capacity of UID with two AIL baselines, i.e., GAIL and WAIL."
- Break condition: If the chosen f-divergence is not well-suited for the specific task or dataset, UID's performance may suffer

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Expert demonstrations (unlabeled) -> Policy (agent) -> Discriminator -> PU learning module -> F-divergence module

- Critical path:
  1. Sample trajectories from the policy and expert demonstrations
  2. Update the discriminator using the PU learning objective
  3. Update the policy to maximize the discriminator's output
  4. Repeat steps 1-3 for a fixed number of iterations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Choosing the appropriate f-divergence for the specific task and dataset
  - Balancing the trade-off between exploration and exploitation during policy learning
  - Handling the potential imbalance between optimal and non-optimal demonstrations

- Failure signatures:

## Limitations
- The robustness of PU learning under extreme label noise remains unclear
- Experimental evaluation is limited to specific MuJoCo and RoboSuite environments
- Computational complexity may increase with larger demonstration sets due to dynamic sampling
