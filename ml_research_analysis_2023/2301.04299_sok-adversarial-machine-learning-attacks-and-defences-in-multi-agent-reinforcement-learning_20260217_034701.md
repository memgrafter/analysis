# SoK: Adversarial Machine Learning Attacks and Defences in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04299
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Presents systematic review of adversarial ML attacks and defenses in MARL, proposing new "Attack Vectors" perspective and two novel frameworks (OA-POSG and AR-POSG) to model multiple simultaneous attack vectors

## Executive Summary
This systematic review examines adversarial machine learning attacks and defenses in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) systems. The authors introduce a novel "Attack Vectors" classification perspective that provides a comprehensive framework for understanding how adversaries can exploit vulnerabilities in MARL. They develop two new theoretical frameworks - Observation Adversarial Partially Observable Stochastic Game (OA-POSG) and Action Robust Partially Observable Stochastic Game (AR-POSG) - which extend existing modeling approaches to better capture the complexity of simultaneous attacks. The paper identifies critical research gaps and challenges in the field, particularly around defending against attacks targeting multiple agents simultaneously.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a comprehensive systematic literature review of existing adversarial ML attacks and defenses in MARL systems. They analyzed 40 references to identify patterns in attack methodologies and defense mechanisms. Based on this analysis, they proposed the "Attack Vectors" classification framework to systematically categorize different types of attacks. They then developed two new theoretical frameworks (OA-POSG and AR-POSG) to address limitations in existing MARL modeling approaches, specifically focusing on the ability to model multiple simultaneous attack vectors and communication perturbations. The frameworks provide detailed specifications for modeling attack tempo, magnitude, and location.

## Key Results
- Proposed "Attack Vectors" perspective for classifying adversarial attacks in MARL systems
- Developed OA-POSG and AR-POSG frameworks to model multiple simultaneous attack vectors
- Identified research gaps in studying attacks against multiple agents and quantifying defense effectiveness
- Extended existing modeling frameworks to include Communication Perturbations as an attack vector

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of this approach stems from its systematic classification of attack vectors, which provides a structured framework for understanding adversarial threats in MARL. By explicitly modeling the tempo, magnitude, and location of attacks, the frameworks can capture the nuanced ways in which adversaries manipulate system observations and actions. The inclusion of Communication Perturbations addresses a critical gap in previous models that focused primarily on observation and action manipulation. This comprehensive approach allows researchers to better understand the attack surface and design more robust defenses that account for multiple simultaneous threats.

## Foundational Learning
- **Partially Observable Stochastic Games (POSG)**: Multi-agent extension of POMDPs that models environments where agents have incomplete information - needed for modeling realistic MARL scenarios where agents cannot observe the full state
- **Adversarial Attack Vectors**: Classification of different ways adversaries can manipulate MARL systems - needed to systematically identify and categorize potential threats
- **Observation Manipulation**: Techniques for modifying agent observations to influence decision-making - needed to understand how adversaries can mislead agents
- **Action Manipulation**: Methods for perturbing agent actions to disrupt system behavior - needed to assess vulnerabilities in agent execution
- **Communication Perturbations**: Attacks targeting information exchange between agents - needed to address the growing importance of inter-agent communication in MARL
- **Robust Optimization**: Techniques for designing systems that maintain performance under adversarial conditions - needed to develop effective defense mechanisms

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Agent 1 -> Communication Channel -> Agent 2 -> Environment -> Reward Function
     ↓                          ↓                        ↓
Observation Space         Action Space           State Space
     ↓                          ↓                        ↓
Adversary (Observation)   Adversary (Action)    Adversary (Communication)

Critical Path:
1. Agent observations are collected
2. Adversary applies observation perturbations
3. Agents process modified observations
4. Agents compute actions
5. Adversary applies action perturbations
6. Modified actions are executed in environment
7. Environment returns new state and rewards
8. Communication between agents occurs
9. Adversary applies communication perturbations
10. Agents update policies based on experience

Design Tradeoffs:
- Model complexity vs. computational tractability
- Generality vs. specificity of attack vector definitions
- Theoretical completeness vs. practical implementability
- Centralized vs. decentralized defense mechanisms

Failure Signatures:
- Degraded agent performance despite normal observation quality
- Inconsistent agent behavior across identical situations
- Communication failures or unexpected agent coordination
- Reward function exploitation through strategic action manipulation

First Experiments:

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How to effectively study and defend against attacks targeting multiple agents simultaneously
- Methods for quantifying the effectiveness of defensive mechanisms in MARL systems
- Approaches for modeling and defending against emerging attack methodologies not yet documented
- Techniques for balancing model complexity with practical implementability in real-world systems

## Limitations
- Proposed frameworks lack empirical validation against real-world MARL systems
- Classification may not capture emerging or undocumented attack methodologies
- Focus on theoretical frameworks may overlook implementation-specific vulnerabilities
- Limited discussion of computational complexity and practical deployment considerations
