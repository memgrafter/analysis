# Reward-free Policy Imitation Learning for Conversational Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07988
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07988
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a reward-free conversational search policy imitation learning framework that trains a conversation policy without annotated conversation data or manually designed rewards.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel reward-free policy imitation learning framework for conversational search that addresses the limitations of traditional reinforcement learning approaches. The framework uses generative adversarial imitation learning to train a policy model that decides whether to ask clarifying questions or return results based on conversation context and retrieval results. The authors propose a new evaluation metric called Expected Conversational Reciprocal Rank (ECRR) to assess multi-turn conversational search performance. Experiments demonstrate that the framework outperforms naive baselines and achieves comparable performance to reward-tuning reinforcement learning models, while eliminating the need for manually designed rewards or annotated conversation data.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework employs generative adversarial imitation learning to train a conversation policy without requiring annotated conversation data or manually designed rewards. The policy model learns to make decisions about asking clarifying questions or returning search results based on the current conversation context and retrieval results. The framework operates in a reward-free manner by leveraging expert demonstrations through the imitation learning paradigm, using a discriminator to distinguish between expert and generated trajectories. The Expected Conversational Reciprocal Rank (ECRR) metric is introduced to evaluate multi-turn conversational search systems by considering both the relevance of returned results and the effectiveness of clarifying questions across multiple turns.

## Key Results
- The reward-free framework outperforms naive baselines in conversational search tasks
- Performance is comparable to reward-tuning reinforcement learning models
- Eliminates the need for manually designed rewards and annotated conversation data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework succeeds by leveraging the strengths of generative adversarial imitation learning to learn from expert demonstrations without requiring explicit reward signals. By framing the problem as an imitation task rather than a reward-maximization problem, the framework avoids the heavy reward engineering typically required in reinforcement learning approaches. The discriminator component learns to distinguish between expert and generated conversation trajectories, providing implicit guidance for policy improvement. This approach naturally captures the nuanced decision-making patterns in human conversations, allowing the model to learn when to ask clarifying questions versus when to provide results based on observed expert behavior.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Raw Conversations -> Preprocessor -> Feature Extractor -> Policy Network -> Decision (Ask Question/Return Results) -> Discriminator -> Policy Update

**Critical Path:**
The critical path flows from raw conversation data through preprocessing and feature extraction, to the policy network which makes decisions, with the discriminator providing feedback for policy updates through adversarial training.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The framework trades off the potential performance gains from carefully engineered rewards in traditional RL for the convenience and robustness of learning from demonstrations. This eliminates reward engineering overhead but may limit the ability to optimize for specific reward structures that might be beneficial in certain scenarios.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Discriminator collapse leading to poor policy updates
- Mode collapse in generated conversation trajectories
- Overfitting to expert demonstrations resulting in lack of generalization
- Failure to handle out-of-domain or ambiguous queries effectively

**3 First Experiments:**
1. Validate basic policy learning on a simple conversational dataset with clear expert demonstrations
2. Test discriminator effectiveness in distinguishing expert vs. generated trajectories
3. Evaluate policy decisions on held-out expert conversations to measure imitation quality

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to the Ubuntu Dialog Corpus, raising questions about generalizability to other domains
- The ECRR metric has not been validated against human judgments or established industry benchmarks
- Training stability and convergence challenges of the generative adversarial approach are not extensively discussed
- Framework's handling of ambiguous or out-of-domain queries remains unclear

## Confidence
- Framework efficacy and comparison with RL baselines: **High**
- ECRR metric validity: **Medium**
- Generalizability across domains: **Low**
- Training stability and convergence: **Medium**
