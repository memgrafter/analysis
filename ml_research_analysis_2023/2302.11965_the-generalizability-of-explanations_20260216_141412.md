# The Generalizability of Explanations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11965
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11965
- Authors: [Not specified in source]
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper introduces a novel generalizability-based framework for evaluating explainability methods in machine learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel framework for evaluating explainability methods by measuring the generalizability of generated explanations through autoencoder reconstruction. Instead of relying on sensitivity testing or human evaluation, the approach trains an autoencoder to reconstruct generated explanations from original inputs, measuring both reconstruction quality and distributional similarity. Experiments on MNIST demonstrate that perturbation-based methods like LIME and KernelSHAP outperform gradient-based methods in generalizability, with SEM scores around 0.479 versus 0.196-0.330 for gradient methods. The framework also reveals that SmoothGrad significantly enhances explanation generalizability across all tested methods.

## Method Summary
The evaluation framework trains an autoencoder to learn the joint distribution between inputs and explanations generated by various explainability methods. The autoencoder's ability to reconstruct explanations serves as a proxy for their quality and generalizability. The method measures reconstruction quality using metrics like L1, MSE, SSIM, and correlation coefficients, while also evaluating distributional similarity through variance proximity analysis that compares intra-class and inter-class explanation distributions. The final Generalizability score (SEM) is computed as the product of Distribution Learnability (DL) and Variance Proximity (VP) scores.

## Key Results
- Perturbation-based methods (LIME, KernelSHAP) achieve SEM scores around 0.479, significantly outperforming gradient-based methods (0.196-0.330)
- SmoothGrad improves explanation generalizability across all methods, raising scores from 0.241-0.200 to 0.514-0.426
- Higher n_sample values in LIME and KernelSHAP correlate with improved SEM scores, demonstrating the framework's sensitivity to explanation quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The autoencoder can reconstruct high-quality explanations only if the explanation method captures meaningful patterns from the original data.
- Mechanism: The autoencoder learns the joint distribution between inputs and explanations during training. If explanations contain only noise or random patterns, the autoencoder cannot reconstruct them well, resulting in poor reconstruction metrics.
- Core assumption: Plausible explanations share distributional proximity with the original data and contain learnable regularities that can be captured by a sufficiently complex generative model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We employ an Autoencoder to learn the distributions of the generated explanations and observe their learnability as well as the plausibility of the learned distributional features."
  - [section 3]: "The essential task of the simulation model is to learn the rules of the explainability method. Therefore, the model requires sufficient learning capability (i.e., structural complexity)."
- Break condition: If the autoencoder architecture is too simple to capture the complexity of the explanation distribution, or if the explanation method generates random patterns that appear structured but lack meaningful content.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The variance proximity metric effectively distinguishes between meaningful and uninformative explanations by comparing intra-class and inter-class distributional similarity.
- Mechanism: By computing the difference in Spearman correlation and Fréchet inception distance between same-class and different-class explanation reconstructions, the method captures whether explanations maintain class-specific patterns while being distinguishable between classes.
- Core assumption: Good explanations should show smaller distributional differences within the same class compared to different classes, reflecting that they capture class-relevant features while maintaining class discrimination.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3]: "We exclude these uninformative explanations by evaluating the proximity of the distributions between the reconstructed explanations and the original images. Our assessment is based on the idea that the discrepancies of the reconstructed explanations between the identical classes should be much smaller than those between different categories."
  - [section 4.1]: "The gaps between inter and intra-classes obviously increase with the growth of n_sample" showing the method's sensitivity to explanation quality.
- Break condition: If the classification model itself has poor class discrimination, or if the explanation method generates class-irrelevant features that happen to have small intra-class variance by chance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SmoothGrad improves explanation generalizability by reducing noise and creating more stable distributions that are easier for the autoencoder to learn.
- Mechanism: By averaging gradients over multiple noisy versions of the input, SmoothGrad creates explanations with reduced high-frequency noise, resulting in smoother distributions that have stronger learnability signals and better variance proximity metrics.
- Core assumption: The noise introduced by SmoothGrad is additive and doesn't fundamentally alter the underlying explanation structure, allowing the autoencoder to learn the core patterns while ignoring noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3]: "SmoothGrad significantly strengthens the Variance Proximity (VP) of the explanations. The VPs of the three explainability methods increase by 0.295, 0.468 and 0.475 respectively."
  - [section 4.3]: "SmoothGrad raises the final scores of V, IxG, and IG from 0.241, 0.196, and 0.200 to 0.514, 0.415, and 0.426, respectively."
- Break condition: If the noise level in SmoothGrad is too high, potentially obscuring meaningful patterns, or if the explanation method already produces very smooth explanations where additional smoothing provides no benefit.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input preprocessing pipeline -> Classification model -> Explanation method interface -> Autoencoder architecture -> Evaluation metrics computation module -> Variance proximity analysis module

- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess dataset
  2. Generate explanations using target explainability method
  3. Train autoencoder on (input, explanation) pairs
  4. Evaluate reconstruction quality and distributional metrics
  5. Compute final generalizability scores

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Autoencoder complexity vs. training time: More complex architectures capture better distributions but require more data and training time
  - Metric selection: More metrics provide comprehensive evaluation but increase computational cost and complexity
  - Sampling strategy: Larger sample sizes improve statistical reliability but increase computation time

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor reconstruction but good variance proximity: May indicate explanations capture class-relevant patterns but have complex structure difficult to learn
  - Good reconstruction but poor variance proximity: May indicate explanations are learnable but don't capture meaningful class distinctions
  - Inconsistent metrics across runs: May indicate insufficient training data or unstable explanation generation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement and validate the autoencoder reconstruction on original images to establish baseline performance (target: L1 ≈ 0.01, SSIM > 0.99)
  2. Test the framework with LIME using varying n_samples (10, 30, 50, 100, 500) to verify the expected improvement in generalizability scores
  3. Compare gradient-based vs. perturbation-based methods on MNIST to confirm the reported performance differences (SEM ~0.479 for perturbation methods vs. 0.196-0.330 for gradient methods)

## Limitations
- The framework is currently validated only on MNIST dataset, limiting generalizability to complex real-world applications
- Autoencoder architecture selection lacks systematic justification, potentially affecting evaluation sensitivity
- The method may not extend well to non-image explanations or fundamentally different explanation formats

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The autoencoder reconstruction methodology and its implementation on MNIST dataset are well-defined and reproducible.
- Medium Confidence: The generalizability framework's ability to distinguish between explanation quality across different methods is demonstrated but may not extend to more complex domains.
- Low Confidence: The claim that reconstruction quality directly correlates with explanation faithfulness requires further validation beyond the MNIST experiments.

## Next Checks
1. Test the framework on CIFAR-10 or ImageNet to evaluate performance on more complex, real-world datasets with higher-dimensional inputs.
2. Conduct ablation studies varying autoencoder architecture complexity to determine the impact on evaluation sensitivity and potential biases.
3. Compare framework results with human evaluation studies on explanation quality to validate the reconstruction-based approach against ground truth assessments.
