# A Comprehensive Survey on Applications of Transformers for Deep Learning Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07303
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07303
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: This survey provides a comprehensive overview of transformer-based models across five key application domains, classifying over 650 models and identifying dominant applications including NLP (40%), Computer Vision (31%), and Multi-Modality (15%).

## Executive Summary
This comprehensive survey systematically examines transformer-based models across five major application domains: Natural Language Processing, Computer Vision, Multi-Modality, Audio & Speech, and Signal Processing. The paper analyzes over 650 transformer models from 2017-2022, identifying significant architectures and their performance across diverse tasks. Key findings reveal transformer dominance in NLP applications, growing adoption in computer vision, and emerging applications in multi-modal and signal processing domains. The survey also identifies critical challenges including computational complexity for high-resolution medical images and limited availability of large-scale biomedical signal datasets, while proposing future research directions spanning wireless networks, cloud computing, and reinforcement learning.

## Method Summary
The survey employed a systematic literature review methodology, conducting keyword-based searches across multiple repositories (AAAI, ACM, CVPR, etc.) to identify transformer models from 2017-2022. Models were filtered based on significance, novelty, and performance metrics, then classified according to application domains and specific tasks. The analysis involved qualitative assessment of architectural innovations, performance characteristics, and contributions to each field. A taxonomy was developed based on this classification, with particular attention given to models demonstrating exceptional performance or introducing novel methodologies. Future research directions were identified through analysis of current limitations and emerging trends in transformer applications.

## Key Results
- Transformers demonstrate clear dominance in NLP applications (40% of surveyed models) with architectures like BERT, GPT, and BART achieving state-of-the-art performance
- Computer vision applications represent 31% of surveyed models, with Vision Transformers (ViT) and related architectures showing competitive performance to CNNs
- Multi-modal transformer applications account for 15% of models, enabling cross-domain learning and fusion of different data types
- Critical challenges identified include computational complexity for high-resolution medical images and insufficient large-scale datasets for biomedical signal processing

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transformers outperform RNNs/LSTMs in handling long-range dependencies due to their self-attention mechanism.
- Mechanism: The self-attention mechanism computes attention weights between all pairs of tokens in parallel, allowing the model to capture long-range dependencies without sequential processing bottlenecks.
- Core assumption: Parallel computation is possible and beneficial for sequence modeling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "transformer models excel in handling long dependencies between input sequence elements and enable parallel processing."
  - [section]: "Unlike conventional neural networks or updated versions of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), transformer models excel in handling long dependencies between input sequence elements and enable parallel processing."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains survey papers but no direct performance comparisons.
- Break condition: When sequence length exceeds memory capacity, making self-attention quadratic complexity prohibitive.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-head attention allows transformers to capture diverse feature representations from different attention heads.
- Mechanism: Multiple attention heads learn different aspects of the relationships between tokens, which are then concatenated and linearly transformed to create a richer representation.
- Core assumption: Different attention heads can specialize in different types of relationships.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The utilization of multi-head attention facilitates the neural network in learning and capturing diverse characteristics of the input sequential data."
  - [section]: "Each head denoted by k performs the attention mechanism based on its own learnable weights WkQ, WkK, and Wkv."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus mentions transformer architectures but doesn't provide detailed analysis of multi-head attention performance.
- Break condition: When the number of heads is too large relative to model capacity, leading to redundancy rather than diversity.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pre-training on large corpora followed by task-specific fine-tuning enables transformers to achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse tasks.
- Mechanism: The model learns general language understanding from massive unsupervised data, then adapts to specific tasks with relatively small labeled datasets.
- Core assumption: Language understanding can be transferred from general to specific tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The model is built using two methods: a supervised method and an unsupervised method. The unsupervised method utilizes Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and Casual Language Modeling (CLM) techniques and has shown remarkable effectiveness in translation tasks."
  - [section]: "BART employs a transformer translation model that integrates both the encoder and decoder modules, allowing it to perform various tasks such as text generation, translation, and comprehension with impressive accuracy."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains survey papers but lacks detailed pre-training/fine-tuning performance analysis.
- Break condition: When pre-training data is too dissimilar from target task domain, making transfer learning ineffective.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Self-attention mechanism
  - Why needed here: Self-attention is the core operation that enables transformers to capture relationships between all tokens in a sequence simultaneously.
  - Quick check question: How does self-attention differ from traditional recurrent attention mechanisms in terms of computational complexity and parallelization?

- Concept: Masked Language Modeling (MLM)
  - Why needed here: MLM is a key pre-training objective that teaches the model to understand context by predicting masked tokens.
  - Quick check question: Why is masking 15% of tokens during pre-training more effective than masking fewer or more tokens?

- Concept: Positional encoding
  - Why needed here: Since transformers don't process tokens sequentially, positional encoding provides information about token order.
  - Quick check question: How does sinusoidal positional encoding allow the model to generalize to sequence lengths longer than those seen during training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Token embeddings + positional encodings
  - Encoder stack: Multi-head self-attention + feed-forward network + layer normalization + residual connections
  - Decoder stack (if applicable): Masked multi-head attention + encoder-decoder attention + feed-forward network + layer normalization + residual connections
  - Output: Linear layer + softmax (for classification) or generation head (for sequence generation)

- Critical path: Token embedding → Positional encoding → Multi-head attention → Feed-forward → Output projection
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Depth vs width: Deeper networks capture more complex patterns but require more computation
  - Number of heads: More heads capture more diverse relationships but increase parameter count
  - Sequence length: Longer sequences capture more context but quadratic attention complexity becomes prohibitive

- Failure signatures:
  - Vanishing/exploding gradients: Check residual connections and layer normalization
  - Overfitting: Monitor training vs validation loss gap, consider dropout or weight decay
  - Memory issues: Monitor GPU memory usage, especially for long sequences or large batch sizes

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a small transformer (2 layers, 4 attention heads) on a simple text classification task to verify basic functionality
  2. Compare performance of single-head vs multi-head attention on a small dataset to demonstrate diversity benefits
  3. Test different positional encoding schemes (learned vs sinusoidal) on a sequence prediction task to understand their impact

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can transformer-based models be optimized to handle the computational complexity of high-resolution medical images while maintaining accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies computational complexity in high-resolution medical images as a challenge, noting that the self-attention mechanism becomes demanding for large images.
- Why unresolved: While some models like DI-UNET have introduced enhanced self-attention mechanisms, there is no clear consensus on the optimal approach for balancing computational efficiency and accuracy across different types of medical images.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies demonstrating transformer models' performance on high-resolution medical images with varying architectural modifications and attention mechanisms, along with computational efficiency metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective strategies for improving transformer-based models' performance on small biomedical signal datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that biomedical signal databases are often small, citing a study with only 20 patients that was considered insufficient to establish model effectiveness.
- Why unresolved: Current approaches like ADASYN for data augmentation have limitations, and there is no established best practice for handling small datasets in transformer-based biomedical signal processing.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different data augmentation techniques, transfer learning approaches, and model architectures on small biomedical signal datasets, demonstrating consistent improvements in model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can transformer-based models be effectively integrated with traditional reinforcement learning algorithms to combine the strengths of both approaches?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the potential of integrating transformers with deep reinforcement learning but notes that standard transformer structures applied directly to decision-making tasks may suffer from stability issues.
- Why unresolved: While modified transformer architectures like GtrX have been proposed as alternatives, there is no established framework for effectively combining traditional RL algorithms with sequence modeling using transformers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementations of hybrid models combining traditional RL algorithms with transformer-based sequence modeling, along with comprehensive evaluations demonstrating improved performance over existing methods.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies primarily on qualitative assessment of model significance and performance rather than quantitative benchmarking across all surveyed models.
- The 650+ model count represents identified papers rather than comprehensive coverage, and selection criteria beyond significance, novelty, and performance remain unspecified.
- Future research direction predictions are inherently speculative and may not reflect actual technological developments.

- Medium confidence: Effectiveness claims for specific architectures like BART and ViT, based on cited performance but without standardized benchmarks
- Low confidence: Future research direction predictions, as these are inherently speculative
