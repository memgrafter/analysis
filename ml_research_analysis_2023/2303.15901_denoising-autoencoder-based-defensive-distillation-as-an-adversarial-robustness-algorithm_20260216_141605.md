# Denoising Autoencoder-based Defensive Distillation as an Adversarial Robustness Algorithm

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.15901
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15901
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Key outcome**: DAE-based defensive distillation achieves 76.09% accuracy on adversarial test data against FGSM and I-FGSM attacks

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a novel method that combines defensive distillation with a denoising autoencoder (DAE) to enhance the robustness of deep neural networks (DNNs) against adversarial attacks, specifically data poisoning attacks. The approach involves training a DAE to detect and reconstruct poisonous adversarial inputs in the training data, thereby reducing the sensitivity of the distilled model to such attacks. The proposed method was evaluated using the fast gradient sign method (FGSM) and the iterative-fast gradient sign method (I-FGSM) to create adversarial samples. The results demonstrate that the approach successfully identified and reconstructed the poisonous inputs, improving the DNN's resilience against adversarial poisoning attacks.

## Method Summary
The proposed method combines defensive distillation with a denoising autoencoder to create a robust defense against adversarial attacks. The process begins by training a teacher model with temperature T=5 using the original dataset. Simultaneously, a DAE is trained to detect and reconstruct poisonous adversarial inputs by minimizing reconstruction error. During training, inputs with reconstruction errors above a threshold (0.003) are considered adversarial and reconstructed. The student model is then trained using soft labels produced by the teacher model. The DAE filters poisoned training data while defensive distillation hardens the model against runtime adversarial examples through soft label training.

## Key Results
- The proposed method successfully identified and reconstructed poisonous adversarial inputs during training
- The student model trained with soft labels showed excellent robustness against adversarial attacks with 76.09% accuracy on test data
- The approach demonstrated effective defense against both FGSM and I-FGSM attacks

- Claim: The DAE filters poisonous inputs during training by reconstructing clean versions of adversarial samples, reducing the instructor model's exposure to corrupted data.
- Mechanism: The DAE is trained to minimize reconstruction error between clean data and adversarial samples, enabling it to detect and discard inputs with reconstruction errors above a learned threshold.
- Core assumption: Adversarial perturbations create sufficiently large reconstruction errors that distinguish them from clean data.

- Core assumption: The increased feature modification requirement makes crafting adversarial examples significantly more difficult.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data source → DAE filtering → Teacher training (T=5) → Soft label generation → Student training → Adversarial testing

- Critical path: Data → DAE filtering → Teacher training (T=5) → Soft label generation → Student training → Adversarial testing

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Reconstruction threshold selection balances false positives vs. false negatives in poison detection
  - Temperature parameter affects both defense strength and model accuracy
  - DAE architecture complexity vs. computational efficiency during training

- Failure signatures:
  - High reconstruction error on clean data indicates DAE is too sensitive
  - Low student accuracy on clean data indicates distillation is harming normal performance
  - Student accuracy on adversarial data not significantly higher than baseline indicates DAE filtering is ineffective

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: Train teacher and student without DAE filtering, test on clean and adversarial data
  2. DAE effectiveness: Test DAE reconstruction error distribution on clean vs. adversarial samples to validate threshold selection
  3. Combined defense: Train with DAE filtering and temperature scaling, compare student accuracy on clean and adversarial data vs. baseline

## Limitations

- The reconstruction threshold of 0.003 for detecting adversarial inputs appears arbitrary without clear justification for how this value was determined.
- The method's effectiveness against more sophisticated adversarial attacks beyond FGSM and I-FGSM remains untested.
- The computational overhead introduced by the DAE filtering step during training is not discussed.
