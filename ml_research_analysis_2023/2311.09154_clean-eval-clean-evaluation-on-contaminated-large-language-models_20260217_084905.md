# CLEAN-EVAL: Clean Evaluation on Contaminated Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2311.09154
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09154
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: Introduces Clean-Eval method to mitigate data contamination impact on LLM evaluation using paraphrasing, back-translation, and semantic filtering

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical problem of data contamination in large language model evaluation, where test data has been inadvertently included in training data, leading to inflated performance estimates. Clean-Eval proposes a systematic approach to restore accurate evaluation by generating diverse expressions of potentially contaminated data through paraphrasing and back-translation techniques. The method employs a semantic detector to filter low-quality samples and selects the best candidate based on BLEURT scoring. Human assessment validates that the calibrated data maintains semantic similarity to the original while exhibiting different surface forms, enabling more accurate assessment of LLM capabilities.

## Method Summary
Clean-Eval works by first applying paraphrasing and back-translation techniques to generate multiple diverse expressions of potentially contaminated evaluation data. A semantic detector then filters out low-quality or semantically inconsistent samples. The remaining candidates are scored using BLEURT (a learned metric for text generation evaluation), and the highest-scoring candidate is selected as the final evaluation sample. This process creates semantically equivalent but surface-form-different versions of the original data, reducing the likelihood that contaminated LLMs will recognize exact matches from their training data while preserving the evaluation task's intent and difficulty.

## Key Results
- Clean-Eval effectively restores actual evaluation results for contaminated LLMs under both few-shot learning and fine-tuning scenarios
- Human assessment confirms calibrated data maintains semantic similarity while exhibiting different surface forms compared to original data
- The method successfully mitigates the performance inflation caused by data contamination, providing more accurate capability assessment of LLMs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Clean-Eval exploits the fact that data contamination creates exact or near-exact matches between evaluation data and training data, leading to inflated performance. By generating semantically equivalent but surface-form-different expressions through paraphrasing and back-translation, the method creates evaluation samples that test genuine language understanding rather than memorization. The semantic detector ensures quality by filtering out poorly paraphrased or semantically altered samples, while BLEURT scoring provides an automated way to select the most natural-sounding and semantically faithful paraphrase.

## Foundational Learning
- **BLEURT scoring**: A learned metric for evaluating text generation quality that compares generated text against reference text using a pre-trained BERT model fine-tuned on human ratings. Needed for automated selection of the best paraphrased candidate while maintaining semantic fidelity.
- **Back-translation**: A technique where text is translated to another language and back to the original language, creating paraphrases that preserve meaning while altering surface form. Required for generating diverse expressions of potentially contaminated data.
- **Semantic detection**: Filtering mechanisms that identify and remove low-quality or semantically inconsistent samples. Essential for maintaining evaluation quality and preventing degradation from poor paraphrasing.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Data Sample -> Paraphrasing Engine -> Back-Translation Module -> Semantic Detector -> BLEURT Scorer -> Selected Candidate

Critical Path:
Original sample → Paraphrasing → Back-translation → Semantic filtering → BLEURT scoring → Final evaluation sample

Design Tradeoffs:
The pipeline trades computational efficiency for evaluation accuracy. Each step adds processing overhead but increases the likelihood of generating contamination-resistant evaluation samples. The semantic detector represents a quality control point that may filter out otherwise good candidates, while BLEURT scoring provides automated selection but may introduce bias if the scoring model is itself contaminated.

Failure Signatures:
- If semantic detector is too strict, all candidates may be filtered, requiring fallback to original sample
- Poor paraphrasing quality may result in semantically altered samples that no longer test the intended capability
- BLEURT scoring bias could systematically favor certain types of paraphrases over others

First 3 Experiments:
1. Compare evaluation performance of contaminated LLMs on original vs. Clean-Eval-calibrated data
2. Human evaluation of semantic similarity between original and calibrated samples
3. Ablation study: Evaluate impact of removing semantic detector or using alternative scoring methods

## Limitations
- Reliance on BLEURT scoring may introduce bias if the scoring model has been trained on contaminated data
- Computational overhead of the full pipeline may be prohibitive for large-scale evaluation scenarios
- Effectiveness of semantic detector not independently validated across different domains and languages
- Lack of detailed human assessment methodology (sample size, rater expertise, inter-rater agreement)

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Paraphrasing and back-translation methodology is technically sound and well-established
- **Medium confidence**: Experimental results support claims of restoring actual evaluation results, though lacking statistical significance reporting
- **Low confidence**: Semantic detector effectiveness and BLEURT-based selection robustness across diverse domains

## Next Checks
