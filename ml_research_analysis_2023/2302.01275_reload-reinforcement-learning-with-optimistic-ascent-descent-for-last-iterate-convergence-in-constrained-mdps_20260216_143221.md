# ReLOAD: Reinforcement Learning with Optimistic Ascent-Descent for Last-Iterate Convergence in Constrained MDPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01275
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01275
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ReLOAD introduces optimistic ascent-descent optimization for last-iterate convergence in constrained RL, outperforming baselines on challenging CRL tasks.

## Executive Summary
ReLOAD addresses the critical issue of last-iterate convergence in constrained reinforcement learning (CRL), where standard gradient-based methods often oscillate and fail to converge to an optimal policy that satisfies constraints. By introducing optimistic ascent-descent optimization and leveraging optimistic mirror descent, ReLOAD ensures last-iterate convergence to a saddle point of the CMDP Lagrangian. Theoretical analysis shows ReLOAD's guarantees carry over to the RL setting, and empirical results demonstrate its effectiveness on a range of challenging CRL problems, including discrete MDPs and continuous control.

## Method Summary
ReLOAD combines optimistic mirror descent (OMD) for policy updates with projected gradient ascent for Lagrange multipliers in constrained MDPs. The algorithm uses optimistic gradients (doubling the current gradient and subtracting the previous one) to anticipate the next update direction, preventing the spiraling behavior seen in standard gradient descent-ascent. In function approximation settings, ReLOAD maintains a copy of the previous value network to compute optimistic q-value estimates, while trust regions stabilize learning. The method is implemented with deep RL algorithms like MDPO, IMPALA, and DMPO, and uses different Bregman divergences for policy (negative entropy) and Lagrange multipliers (squared Euclidean norm).

## Key Results
- ReLOAD significantly outperforms baseline methods on the Oscillating Control Suite, demonstrating strong last-iterate convergence.
- On the RWRL Suite, ReLOAD achieves superior performance, especially for the most challenging safety coefficients.
- Theoretical analysis confirms ReLOAD's convergence guarantees carry over to the RL setting with function approximation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Optimistic gradient updates bend the trajectory inward toward the saddle point, ensuring last-iterate convergence.
- Mechanism: By using the optimistic gradient `˜∇Lk = 2∇Lk − ∇Lk−1`, the algorithm anticipates the next gradient and adjusts the update direction, preventing the spiraling behavior seen in standard gradient descent-ascent.
- Core assumption: The Lagrangian objective is convex-concave, and the gradient is monotone and Lipschitz continuous.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ReLOAD introduces optimistic ascent-descent optimization, leveraging optimistic mirror descent to ensure last-iterate convergence."
  - [section] "By adding the current hint ∇Lk and subtracting the previous one ∇Lk−1 to the standard gradient update, the optimistic gradient ˜∇Lk bends inwards towards the optimum."
- Break condition: If the gradient is not monotone or the objective is not convex-concave, the optimistic update may not converge.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Optimistic mirror descent with different Bregman divergences for policy and Lagrange multipliers preserves last-iterate convergence guarantees.
- Mechanism: OMD is formulated as a monotone inclusion problem, allowing different mirror maps for the policy (e.g., negative entropy) and Lagrange multipliers (e.g., squared Euclidean norm), which is critical for scalability in high-dimensional RL.
- Core assumption: The normal cone operators and gradients involved are maximal monotone, and the Bregman divergences are strongly convex.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "OMD can be seen as performing fixed point iteration: xk+1 = PrtΩ ηkNX (∇Ω −ηk∇ℓ)(xk)."
- Break condition: If the Bregman divergences are not strongly convex or the operators are not maximal monotone, convergence is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Policy optimization with optimistic value estimates and trust regions enables last-iterate convergence in function approximation settings.
- Mechanism: By maintaining a copy of the previous value network and using optimistic q-value estimates (`˜qkµ(s,a) = 2qkµ(s,a) − qk−1µ(s,a)`), ReLOAD corrects for the non-stationarity introduced by function approximation, while trust regions stabilize learning.
- Core assumption: Accurate policy evaluation is possible and the policy update is within a trust region to ensure stable learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "For (1), a crucial factor is that with function approximation... the agent can't simply store past gradients to compute optimistic values... Instead, the agent must maintain a copy of the previous value network."
- Break condition: If value estimates are inaccurate or the trust region is too restrictive, oscillations may persist.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Convex-concave optimization and saddle-point problems
  - Why needed here: The constrained RL problem is reformulated as a convex-concave min-max game via Lagrangian relaxation, and understanding saddle-point convergence is key to addressing last-iterate convergence.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between average-iterate convergence and last-iterate convergence in the context of saddle-point optimization?

  - Why needed here: OMD is analyzed via monotone inclusion problems, and Bregman divergences define the geometry of the optimization, enabling different update rules for policy and Lagrange multipliers.
  - Quick check question: How does the choice of Bregman divergence (e.g., negative entropy vs. squared Euclidean) affect the update step in mirror descent?

  - Why needed here: In deep RL, the agent must handle non-stationary data distributions; optimistic value estimates and trust regions are crucial for stable learning.
  - Quick check question: Why can't ReLOAD simply store past gradients for optimistic updates in the function approximation setting?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Base agent (e.g., MDPO, IMPALA, DMPO) -> Optimistic value estimator -> Policy optimizer with trust region -> Lagrange multiplier updater
  Value network (current and previous copy) -> Optimistic q-value computation -> Mixed advantage estimation
  Constraint value estimators -> Optimistic constraint violation estimation -> Lagrange multiplier update via projected gradient ascent

- Critical path:
  1. Collect trajectories using current policy
  2. Compute current and previous value estimates
  3. Form optimistic q-values and constraint violations
  4. Update policy within trust region using optimistic mixed advantages
  5. Update Lagrange multipliers using optimistic constraint violations
  6. Repeat

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Optimistic updates vs. stability: Optimism helps convergence but may introduce variance if value estimates are noisy.
  - Trust region vs. flexibility: Trust regions stabilize learning but may slow convergence if too restrictive.
  - Separate vs. shared networks: Maintaining a previous value network copy increases memory but is necessary for correct optimistic updates.

- Failure signatures:
  - Persistent oscillations in task reward or constraint violation
  - Divergence of value estimates or policy parameters
  - Lagrange multipliers growing without bound or collapsing to zero
  - Performance degradation when switching from tabular to function approximation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run ReLOAD on the paradoxical CMDP with tabular policy iteration to verify last-iterate convergence vs. standard MDPI.
  2. Apply ReLOAD-MDPO to the Catch task and compare oscillations and final performance against µ-MDPO.
  3. Train ReLOAD-IMPALA on Walker with height constraint and visualize policy trajectories to confirm oscillation damping.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does ReLOAD's performance compare to other optimistic optimization methods beyond OGDA in CMDPs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that ReLOAD is part of a broader family of single-call extra-gradient methods, including PEG and RG, which are closely related to OGD. It also notes that ReLOAD's success suggests potential for improving optimization in policy optimization with non-stationary rewards and extending to convex MDPs.
- Why unresolved: The paper primarily focuses on ReLOAD's performance against non-optimistic baseline methods (µ- agents) and OGD. It does not directly compare ReLOAD to other optimistic optimization methods like PEG or RG in the context of CMDPs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results comparing ReLOAD's performance against PEG, RG, and other optimistic optimization methods on the same set of CMDP tasks used in the paper.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can ReLOAD be extended to achieve last-iterate convergence in non-convex CMDPs, or are there fundamental limitations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that ReLOAD's theoretical guarantees are for convex CMDPs, and it acknowledges a lack of theoretical understanding of non-convex ReLOAD. It also mentions potential applications to policy optimization with non-stationary rewards and convex MDPs, suggesting a gap in understanding for non-convex settings.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide theoretical analysis or empirical evidence for ReLOAD's performance in non-convex CMDPs. The convergence guarantee in Theorem 3.6 relies on convexity assumptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis extending ReLOAD's convergence guarantees to non-convex CMDPs, or empirical results demonstrating ReLOAD's performance in non-convex settings, potentially with convergence rates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of Bregman divergence in ReLOAD affect its performance and convergence properties in practice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that ReLOAD is determined by different choices of Ωπ and Ωµ, and it uses the negative entropy for Ωπ and squared Euclidean norm for Ωµ. It also discusses the theoretical analysis of mixed-Bregman OMD via monotone operators.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical results comparing ReLOAD's performance using different Bregman divergences. It also does not discuss the practical implications of the choice of Bregman divergence on convergence properties.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results comparing ReLOAD's performance using different Bregman divergences (e.g., squared Euclidean norm for both Ωπ and Ωµ, or other common choices) on the same set of CMDP tasks. Additionally, theoretical analysis of how the choice of Bregman divergence affects convergence rates and stability.

## Limitations

- The paper's empirical results are mainly focused on the Oscillating Control Suite and RWRL Suite, with specific constraint coefficients, leaving generalizability to other CMDPs unclear.
- The impact of hyperparameters such as initial/final step sizes and trust region parameters on real-world tasks is not fully explored.
- The mechanism of optimistic gradient updates in high-dimensional, non-stationary function approximation settings lacks direct experimental validation in the paper.

## Confidence

- High: Optimistic ascent-descent in convex-concave optimization, supported by both theory and empirical results on synthetic and benchmark CMDPs.
- Medium: Extension to deep RL via optimistic value estimates and trust regions, as the paper provides evidence but the experimental scope is somewhat narrow.
- Low: Claims about scalability to convex MDPs and policy optimization with non-stationary rewards, as these are stated as future directions without empirical backing.
