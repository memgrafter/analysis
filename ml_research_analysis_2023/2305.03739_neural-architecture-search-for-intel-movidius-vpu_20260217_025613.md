# Neural Architecture Search for Intel Movidius VPU

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03739
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03739
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Achieved 1.3x higher FPS (1162 vs 870) than MobileNet-v2-1.4 and 2.2x higher FPS (684 vs 320) than ResNet50 at comparable accuracy for ImageNet classification on Intel Movidius VPU.

## Executive Summary
This paper extends Neural Architecture Search (NAS) to Intel Movidius VPU by incorporating hardware-specific latency modeling into the search process. The authors introduce two latency profiling methods - pre-collected hardware-cost on device and the VPUNN device-specific hardware-cost model - to estimate network latency during architecture search. Using ProxylessNAS with differentiable latency regularization, the method achieves significant speed-accuracy gains over MobileNet-v2-1.4 and ResNet50 for image classification, and outperforms EDSR3 for super-resolution tasks on VPU hardware.

## Method Summary
The method extends ProxylessNAS with differentiable latency regularization by incorporating hardware-specific latency models into the search loss function. Two latency profiling approaches are used: (1) pre-collected hardware cost on device by benchmarking operators on actual VPU hardware through stacking multiple instances to reduce variance, and (2) VPUNN, a neural network-based cost model trained on low-level task profiling data to predict VPU cycle counts. The search space is built on ProxylessNAS with mixed operators per layer, focusing on MBConv blocks for classification and kernel sizes, upsampling methods, and activation functions for super-resolution. The search optimizes for both accuracy and latency, producing models that can be exported as ONNX and converted to OpenVINO IR format for VPU deployment.

## Key Results
- Achieved 1.3x higher FPS (1162 vs 870) than MobileNet-v2-1.4 for ImageNet classification on Intel Movidius VPU
- Achieved 2.2x higher FPS (684 vs 320) than ResNet50 at comparable accuracy
- For super-resolution, achieved 6x higher FPS and 1.08x PSNR over EDSR3

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Hardware-aware NAS improves latency and accuracy by incorporating hardware-specific latency modeling during the search process. The NAS algorithm uses differentiable latency regularization to estimate and optimize network latency on the target VPU during training, allowing direct optimization for hardware efficiency while maintaining accuracy. Core assumption: Latency can be accurately modeled as a continuous function of network dimensions and incorporated into the loss function for optimization.

### Mechanism 2
Pre-collected hardware-cost on device provides accurate latency measurements for operators. Operators are benchmarked on actual VPU hardware by stacking multiple instances to reduce variance, then dividing total latency by the number of operators. Core assumption: Stacking operators reduces measurement variance and provides representative latency for individual operators.

### Mechanism 3
VPUNN neural network-based cost model provides accurate latency estimates without requiring VPU hardware access. VPUNN is a two-layer fully connected neural network trained on low-level task profiling data to predict VPU cycle counts for various operators based on features like operator type, input/output feature map size, and kernel parameters. Core assumption: Operator latency can be predicted from hardware task descriptions and features.

## Foundational Learning

Quick check question: How does ProxylessNAS update weight parameters versus architecture parameters during training?

- **Hardware-aware optimization and differentiable latency**: Why needed here - The key innovation is incorporating hardware latency into the NAS loss function through differentiable regularization, allowing direct optimization for VPU efficiency. Quick check question: How is expected latency calculated from individual operator latencies in the differentiable approach?

- **VPU architecture and operator characteristics**: Why needed here - Understanding VPU constraints (e.g., 16x channel alignment, DSP limitations) is crucial for interpreting the search results and design guidelines. Quick check question: Why should output channels be multiples of 16 for optimal VPU performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Define search space -> Generate latency lookup table (hardware or VPUNN) -> Run NAS with differentiable latency loss -> Export and convert models -> Benchmark on VPU
- **Critical path**: The search process iteratively samples architectures from the supernet, estimates their latency using the chosen profiling method, and updates both weight and architecture parameters to optimize the combined accuracy-latency objective.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Pre-collected hardware cost is more accurate but requires VPU access; VPUNN is less accurate but accessible to all users; more complex operators may improve accuracy but hurt latency.
- **Failure signatures**: Inaccurate latency predictions leading to suboptimal architectures; search getting stuck in local minima; models not converting properly to VPU IR format.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify latency lookup table accuracy by comparing predicted vs actual latency for a small set of randomly sampled networks
  2. Run NAS search on a subset of ImageNet to validate the search process completes and produces architectures
  3. Benchmark a simple searched model on VPU to verify the conversion pipeline works end-to-end

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does the VPUNN latency model handle complex network topologies with non-linear execution patterns (e.g., long residual connections, horizontal fusion) that deviate from the sequential layer-by-layer assumption? The paper acknowledges this limitation but does not provide solutions or validation data showing how VPUNN performs on complex architectures.

- **Open Question 2**: What is the exact methodology for determining the optimal λ1 and λ2 regularization weights in the latency-aware loss function for different tasks? The paper uses these weights in experiments but does not explain the tuning process or whether they vary by task or dataset.

- **Open Question 3**: How does the accuracy of the hardware cost collection pipeline degrade when operators cannot be stacked efficiently (e.g., when input/output feature maps differ significantly)? The paper validates the stacking approach for matching operators but does not provide error analysis or accuracy metrics for the non-matching case.

## Limitations

- Latency model accuracy remains uncertain across diverse network architectures beyond the small set of randomly sampled networks validated in the paper.
- Search results and design guidelines are specific to second generation Movidius VPU (KeemBay) and may not generalize to other hardware platforms.
- The reported performance gains may be partly attributable to the specific architectural choice of MBConv blocks rather than the hardware-aware NAS approach itself.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The basic framework of extending ProxylessNAS to include hardware-aware latency modeling, and the general approach of using differentiable latency regularization. The reported FPS improvements over baseline models are specific and verifiable.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the two latency profiling methods (hardware cost collection and VPUNN) across diverse workloads. The reported accuracy-latency tradeoffs are credible but would benefit from more extensive validation.
- **Low Confidence**: The design guidelines (e.g., output channels as multiples of 16, avoiding Shuffle operations) and their universal applicability across different VPU architectures and workloads.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Architecture Validation**: Test the VPUNN model and search methodology on a different hardware platform (e.g., Intel Core iGPU or NVIDIA GPU) to assess generalizability of the hardware-aware NAS approach beyond Movidius VPU.

2. **Ablation Study on Latency Modeling**: Conduct controlled experiments comparing NAS with differentiable latency regularization against NAS without latency awareness, keeping all other factors constant, to isolate the impact of hardware-aware optimization.

3. **Expanded Search Space Testing**: Run the NAS framework with a more diverse search space including non-MBConv architectures and alternative operations (e.g., different attention mechanisms, novel block types) to determine if the reported improvements are specific to the chosen search space or generalizable.
