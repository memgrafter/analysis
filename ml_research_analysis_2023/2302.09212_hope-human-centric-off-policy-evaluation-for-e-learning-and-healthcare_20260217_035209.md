# HOPE: Human-Centric Off-Policy Evaluation for E-Learning and Healthcare

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09212
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09212
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes HOPE, a method for off-policy evaluation that outperforms state-of-the-art benchmarks in sepsis treatment and intelligent tutoring applications

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HOPE, a novel approach for off-policy evaluation in human-centric environments characterized by partial observability and aggregated rewards. The method reconstructs immediate rewards from historical trajectories using nearest neighbors and applies weighted importance sampling to estimate expected total returns. HOPE demonstrates superior performance compared to existing OPE methods across multiple evaluation metrics in two real-world applications: sepsis treatment and intelligent tutoring systems.

## Method Summary
HOPE addresses the challenge of evaluating policies in human-centric environments where immediate rewards are not directly observable and must be aggregated over time. The method reconstructs immediate rewards by finding nearest neighbor trajectories in historical data and transfers their reward patterns to similar states. These reconstructed rewards are then used with weighted importance sampling to estimate the expected return of target policies. The approach is specifically designed to handle the partial observability and aggregated reward structures common in healthcare and educational settings.

## Key Results
- HOPE outperforms state-of-the-art OPE benchmarks in terms of average absolute error, regret@1, and rank correlation
- The method is the only one that successfully identifies the best policy in both sepsis treatment and intelligent tutoring applications
- HOPE maintains performance using both standard validation methods and human-centric significance tests

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HOPE's effectiveness stems from its ability to reconstruct immediate rewards from historical trajectories using nearest neighbors, addressing the fundamental challenge of partial observability in human-centric environments. By transferring reward patterns from similar trajectories, the method creates a proxy for immediate rewards that can be used in importance sampling estimation. The weighted importance sampling component then leverages these reconstructed rewards to provide unbiased estimates of policy performance, even when the behavior and target policies differ significantly.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Historical trajectories -> Nearest neighbor search -> Reward reconstruction -> Weighted importance sampling -> Policy evaluation

**Critical Path:** The method first identifies nearest neighbor trajectories, reconstructs immediate rewards using patterns from these neighbors, then applies weighted importance sampling to estimate the expected return of the target policy.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The method trades computational complexity for accuracy by performing nearest neighbor searches across all historical trajectories. While this provides more accurate reward reconstruction, it increases computational requirements compared to methods that assume immediate rewards are directly observable.

**Failure Signatures:** Performance degrades when historical trajectories lack diversity, when nearest neighbors cannot be found for certain states, or when the importance sampling weights become too large (high variance). The method may also fail if the reward aggregation patterns differ significantly between historical and target policies.

**First Experiments:** 1) Evaluate nearest neighbor accuracy on held-out trajectories, 2) Test reward reconstruction quality by comparing to known immediate rewards in synthetic data, 3) Measure importance sampling variance as a function of policy similarity.

## Limitations
- Reliance on nearest-neighbor reconstruction assumes sufficient trajectory diversity, which may not hold in specialized medical or educational contexts
- Experimental validation is limited to only two real-world applications, raising questions about generalizability to other human-centric domains
- Lack of comprehensive sensitivity analyses for hyperparameters like number of neighbors or importance sampling horizon

## Confidence
- High confidence in theoretical framework and mathematical formulation
- Medium confidence in empirical results due to limited application scope
- Low confidence in method robustness without additional validation across diverse data distributions
