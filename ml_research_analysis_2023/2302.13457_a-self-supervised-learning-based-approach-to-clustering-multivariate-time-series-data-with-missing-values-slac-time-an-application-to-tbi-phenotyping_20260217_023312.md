# A Self-Supervised Learning-based Approach to Clustering Multivariate Time-Series Data with Missing Values (SLAC-Time): An Application to TBI Phenotyping

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13457
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13457
- Reference count: 38
- A self-supervised learning approach for clustering multivariate time-series data with missing values, applied to TBI phenotyping

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SLAC-Time, a novel self-supervised learning approach for clustering multivariate time-series data with missing values. The method uses time-series forecasting as a proxy task to learn robust representations of unlabeled multivariate time-series data, which are then clustered using K-means. The approach is evaluated on the TRACK-TBI study dataset, where it successfully identifies clinically significant TBI phenotypes that could inform targeted clinical trials and therapeutic strategies.

## Method Summary
SLAC-Time employs a self-supervised learning framework that learns representations of multivariate time-series data through a forecasting proxy task. The method iteratively clusters the learned representations using K-means and utilizes the cluster assignments as pseudo-labels to update the model parameters. This process continues until convergence, producing final cluster assignments that represent distinct phenotypes. The approach specifically handles missing values inherent in clinical time-series data and leverages transformer-based architectures for learning temporal representations.

## Key Results
- SLAC-Time outperforms baseline K-means clustering across multiple internal validation metrics (silhouette coefficient, Calinski Harabasz index, Dunn index, Davies Bouldin index)
- The method identified three distinct TBI phenotypes with clinical significance
- The identified phenotypes have potential applications in developing targeted clinical trials and therapeutic strategies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SLAC-Time leverages the temporal structure of multivariate time-series data through self-supervised learning, where the model learns to predict future time points as a proxy task. This pre-training objective forces the model to capture meaningful temporal patterns and relationships in the data, resulting in robust representations that are better suited for downstream clustering tasks. The iterative refinement process, where cluster assignments are used as pseudo-labels to update the model, creates a feedback loop that progressively improves both the representations and the clustering quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Self-supervised learning**: Why needed - to learn meaningful representations without labeled data; Quick check - can the model generate coherent future time-steps
- **Time-series forecasting**: Why needed - provides a natural proxy task that captures temporal dependencies; Quick check - forecast accuracy on held-out time points
- **Transformer architectures**: Why needed - excel at capturing long-range temporal dependencies; Quick check - attention patterns show coherent temporal relationships
- **K-means clustering**: Why needed - simple, scalable clustering algorithm suitable for large datasets; Quick check - cluster assignments remain stable across iterations
- **Iterative pseudo-labeling**: Why needed - allows model to refine representations based on emerging cluster structure; Quick check - cluster purity improves across iterations

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data preprocessing -> Transformer encoder -> Time-series forecasting head -> K-means clustering -> Pseudo-label assignment -> Model update

**Critical Path**: The core workflow involves (1) preprocessing time-series data with missing values, (2) feeding data through transformer encoder to learn representations, (3) using these representations for time-series forecasting as proxy task, (4) clustering representations with K-means, (5) using cluster assignments as pseudo-labels to update model, and (6) iterating until convergence.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The choice of K-means for clustering provides simplicity and scalability but may limit the ability to capture complex cluster structures. The transformer architecture enables modeling of long-range temporal dependencies but increases computational complexity. The self-supervised learning objective of time-series forecasting is intuitive for temporal data but may not always align with the ultimate clustering goal.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor clustering results may indicate (1) insufficient model capacity to capture complex temporal patterns, (2) convergence to suboptimal local minima in the iterative process, (3) sensitivity to initialization of K-means, or (4) temporal non-stationarity in the data that violates model assumptions.

## Limitations
- Reliance on K-means clustering for pseudo-labels may limit capture of complex cluster structures in TBI data
- Assumes temporal consistency and stationarity in time-series data, which may not hold for all TBI patient trajectories
- Internal validation metrics don't directly assess clinical relevance or external validity of identified phenotypes
- Specific transformer architecture and hyperparameters weren't thoroughly explored through ablation studies

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The overall approach of using self-supervised learning for time-series representation before clustering is technically sound and the methodological framework is well-described.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that SLAC-Time outperforms baseline K-means clustering is supported by the presented metrics, but the clinical significance of the identified phenotypes needs further validation.

**Low confidence**: The generalizability of the identified TBI phenotypes across different datasets and clinical settings has not been demonstrated.
