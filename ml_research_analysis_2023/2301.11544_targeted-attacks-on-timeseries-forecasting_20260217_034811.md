# Targeted Attacks on Timeseries Forecasting

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the gap in literature regarding targeted adversarial attacks on time series forecasting models. While most prior work focuses on untargeted attacks that simply maximize prediction loss, the authors introduce three novel targeted attack types—directional, amplitudinal, and temporal—that manipulate model outputs in specific ways. They adapt existing adversarial attack methods (FGSM, PGD, and a modified AutoPGD) for time series forecasting and demonstrate their effectiveness on real-world datasets. The key finding is that targeted attacks are more statistically similar to legitimate predictions than untargeted attacks, making them harder to detect through statistical methods.

## Method Summary
The authors formulate three targeted attack types for time series forecasting: Directional (forcing predictions to move in a specific direction), Amplitudinal (constraining the magnitude of changes), and Temporal (affecting predictions within specific time windows). They adapt FGSM, PGD, and AutoPGD algorithms for these targeted attacks, creating mAPGD-TSF for regression tasks. The attacks are evaluated on LSTM models trained on Google stock prices and household electric power consumption data, using L2 norms, RMSE, and KS-tests to measure attack effectiveness and statistical similarity.

## Key Results
- Targeted attacks are more statistically similar to legitimate predictions than untargeted attacks, making them harder to detect through statistical methods
- The modified AutoPGD (mAPGD-TSF) algorithm effectively handles targeted regression attacks through adaptive step size selection
- All three attack types (directional, amplitudinal, temporal) successfully manipulate model outputs as intended across different epsilon values

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Targeted attacks manipulate the output in specific ways (directional, amplitudinal, temporal) by optimizing loss functions toward predefined output targets. The attack crafts adversarial perturbations by computing gradients of the loss function with respect to the input, minimizing loss with respect to a target output rather than maximizing it. Core assumption: The forecasting model's loss function is differentiable with respect to the input. Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions novel formulation of targeted attacks; [section] defines targeted attacks using loss functions. Break condition: If the model's loss function is not differentiable or perturbations exceed L∞ constraints.

### Mechanism 2
Targeted attacks are more statistically similar to legitimate predictions than untargeted attacks, making them harder to detect. By constraining attacks to specific output characteristics, perturbations create outputs that remain within the statistical distribution of normal predictions as measured by KS-tests. Core assumption: The statistical distribution of model errors for targeted attacks overlaps more with legitimate predictions than untargeted attacks do. Evidence anchors: [abstract] states targeted attacks are more powerful in terms of statistical similarity; [section] performs KS-tests on output forecasts. Break condition: If statistical tests are not sensitive enough or attack constraints are too loose.

### Mechanism 3
The modified AutoPGD (mAPGD-TSF) algorithm effectively handles targeted regression attacks by adapting step size selection and early stopping conditions. The algorithm replaces confidence maximization with loss minimization toward a target while maintaining momentum-based gradient updates. Core assumption: Adaptive step size conditions from AutoPGD transfer effectively from classification to regression tasks when reformulated. Evidence anchors: [section] describes modifications to AutoPGD for targeted time series applications; mentions specific loss functions for each attack type. Break condition: If step size adaptation conditions are inappropriate for regression loss landscapes.

## Limitations

- Method implementation details are underspecified, particularly for the mAPGD-TSF algorithm's step size adaptation conditions
- Evaluation is restricted to two specific datasets (Google stock prices and household electric power consumption) which may not generalize to other domains
- The paper doesn't address whether statistical tests used are sufficiently sensitive to detect subtle adversarial effects in high-dimensional time series data

## Confidence

**High confidence claims**:
- Formulation of three targeted attack types (directional, amplitudinal, temporal) is well-defined and logically consistent

**Medium confidence claims**:
- Effectiveness of mAPGD-TSF algorithm for targeted regression attacks, as implementation details are incomplete
- Comparative advantage of targeted attacks over untargeted attacks in terms of statistical similarity

**Low confidence claims**:
- Generalizability of results across different time series domains beyond the two tested datasets
- Practical detectability thresholds for targeted attacks in real-world deployment scenarios

## Next Checks

1. **Reproduce directional attack effectiveness**: Implement FGSM-based targeted attack on a simple LSTM model using one of the datasets, then verify that predictions shift in the intended direction by comparing mean predictions before and after attack across multiple samples.

2. **Statistical sensitivity validation**: Conduct additional statistical tests (beyond KS-tests) including Wasserstein distance and maximum mean discrepancy to verify that targeted attacks are indeed harder to detect statistically, and test with varying sample sizes to establish statistical power.

3. **Cross-domain generalization test**: Apply the same attack methodology to a different time series dataset (e.g., meteorological or traffic data) with different characteristics to validate whether the observed patterns of statistical similarity and attack effectiveness hold across domains.
