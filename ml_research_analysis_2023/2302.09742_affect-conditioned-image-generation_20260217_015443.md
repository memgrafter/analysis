# Affect-Conditioned Image Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09742
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09742
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 26
- Key outcome: Method successfully generates images with desired affect content (high/low valence, arousal, dominance) without degrading image quality

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for generating images conditioned on desired emotional content using psychometrically validated three-component affect scores (valence, arousal, dominance). The method involves training a neural network to predict affect scores from CLIP and BERT embeddings, then using these predictions to guide various generative models including VQGAN+CLIP, CLIPDraw, and Stable Diffusion. Results show the approach can effectively generate images with targeted affect while maintaining image quality, though user studies found participants could distinguish high/low valence and arousal images but not dominance images. The work demonstrates potential for creativity support by allowing users to directly target desired emotional content rather than struggling with iterative prompt engineering.

## Method Summary
The approach involves training a neural network to predict affect scores from CLIP and BERT embeddings, then using these predictions to guide various generative models. The affect prediction network learns to map semantic embeddings to VAD scores, enabling generalization from single words to multi-word prompts. For generation-by-optimization methods (VQGAN+CLIP, CLIPDraw), the optimization objective combines semantic loss with affect loss through a regularization term. For conditional models like Stable Diffusion, affect-conditioned embeddings are generated and used during the diffusion process. The method maintains image quality while enabling control over emotional content, with user studies showing participants could distinguish high/low valence and arousal images but not dominance images.

## Key Results
- Affect prediction network achieves 0.06-0.08 mean estimation error on held-out data
- User studies show participants can distinguish high/low valence and arousal images but not dominance images
- Method successfully generates images with desired affect content without degrading image quality or prompt alignment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The model successfully transfers affect scores from words/images to arbitrary text prompts by leveraging the semantic continuity of CLIP and BERT embeddings
- Mechanism: The affect prediction networks learn to map semantic embeddings (CLIP for images/text, BERT for text) to VAD scores. Because semantically similar prompts map to nearby points in the embedding space, the affect prediction generalizes from single words to multi-word prompts
- Core assumption: The latent spaces encode affective information in a continuous and predictable way
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We first train a neural network for estimating the affect content of text and images from semantic embeddings"
  - [section] "Figure 2 shows brief descriptions of four randomly selected images... the affect scores predicted using the image descriptions as prompts, using the same model. Note that these text prompts were written before any predictions were made, without iteration or cherry-picking."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found in corpus
- Break condition: If the semantic spaces don't encode affective information continuously, or if affect is highly context-dependent in ways that can't be captured by embedding proximity

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The regularization term in the optimization successfully steers generated images toward desired affect without degrading image quality or prompt alignment
- Mechanism: The optimization objective combines semantic loss (matching the prompt) with affect loss (matching the target VAD vector). The lambda parameter balances these two objectives, allowing control over affect while maintaining image quality
- Core assumption: The two loss components are orthogonal enough that both can be optimized simultaneously
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show the method can successfully generate images with desired affect content (high/low valence, arousal, or dominance) without degrading image quality"
  - [section] "Figure 3: Quality (i.e. fit-to-prompt) ratings from a survey of 27 participants for images generated using generation-by-optimization methods, with (AC) or without (No AC) incorporating Affect Control. With no observable quality effects between the two we claim that our affect-conditioning approach does not impact subjective image quality."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found in corpus
- Break condition: If the affect loss interferes with semantic loss (e.g., by forcing the generator into semantically incoherent regions), or if lambda is poorly chosen

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Affect conditioning enables creative exploration by pushing generators into regions of the prompt-conditioned space that would be difficult to reach through prompt engineering alone
- Mechanism: By optimizing for specific affect scores rather than descriptive text, the method can discover visual features associated with those affect states that users might not have thought to include in their prompts. This works because affect is a higher-level abstraction than specific visual elements
- Core assumption: The relationship between affect and visual features is discoverable through optimization, even when it's not obvious from semantic analysis alone
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Qualitative analysis demonstrates the method's potential for exploring affect-relevant prompt modifications and design possibilities"
  - [section] "Figure 7... the effect of dominance appears clearly in the images of 'An elephant'... This qualitative difference shows the capacity of affect-conditioning to push generators into diverse and a-priori unlikely regions of the subspaces of images associated with a particular prompt."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found in corpus
- Break condition: If the generator's latent space doesn't contain regions corresponding to the desired affect states, or if the optimization gets stuck in local minima

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Affect prediction network (MLP) trained on CLIP/BERT embeddings → VAD scores → Generation-by-optimization models (VQGAN+CLIP, CLIPDraw) with modified loss function including affect regularization → Conditional generation model (Stable Diffusion) with affect-conditioned embeddings → Survey pipeline for user studies evaluating image quality and affect perception

- Critical path:
  1. Train affect prediction network on word/image VAD data
  2. Generate affect-conditioned embeddings for Stable Diffusion or modify loss for optimization-based models
  3. Generate images using modified models
  4. Evaluate results through user studies or qualitative analysis

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using separate networks for each BERT channel increases parameter count but allows channel-specific affect modeling
  - Balancing lambda in the loss function: too high sacrifices prompt alignment, too low loses affect control
  - Abstract vs. realistic image generation: affect conditioning may work differently across different model types

- Failure signatures:
  - Affect scores not changing significantly between generated images → likely issues with lambda or affect prediction network
  - Images losing semantic coherence → affect loss overpowering semantic loss
  - Affect conditioning only working for some dimensions → potential issues with affect space continuity or training data distribution

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify affect prediction network performance on held-out test data (both words and images)
  2. Generate images with high/low valence for a simple prompt and verify affect scores change as expected
  3. Conduct a small-scale user study comparing affect-conditioned vs. unconditioned images for valence/arousal

## Limitations
- User study results show participants could distinguish high/low valence and arousal images but not dominance images
- Qualitative analysis lacks quantitative validation of whether discovered visual features actually correspond to target affect states
- Method's effectiveness across different types of prompts and subject matter remains unexplored

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The affect prediction network's ability to estimate VAD scores from CLIP and BERT embeddings (supported by reported error rates and examples)
- Medium Confidence: The affect conditioning method's effectiveness for valence and arousal (supported by user study results showing participants could distinguish these states)
- Low Confidence: The method's effectiveness for dominance conditioning (user study showed participants couldn't distinguish dominance states) and the claim that affect conditioning enables creative exploration (largely based on qualitative observations)

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a larger-scale user study with more diverse prompts and participants to verify affect conditioning effectiveness, particularly for the dominance dimension
2. Perform quantitative analysis comparing the visual features discovered through affect conditioning against established affect-relevant image characteristics
3. Test the method's robustness across different generative models and prompt types to establish generalizability beyond the examples shown
