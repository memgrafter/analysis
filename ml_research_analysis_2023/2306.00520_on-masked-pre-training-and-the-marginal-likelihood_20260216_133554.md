# On Masked Pre-training and the Marginal Likelihood

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00520
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00520
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Masked pre-training maximizes the model's marginal likelihood, explaining its strong generalization

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a theoretical understanding of why masked pre-training (MPT) generalizes well by showing it is equivalent to maximizing the model's marginal likelihood, a Bayesian measure of generalization ability. The authors prove that MPT with cumulative scoring corresponds to maximizing marginal likelihood under a latent variable model, even when using fixed masking rates that create biased estimators. Empirical validation using tractable models like Probabilistic PCA and large language models like BERT confirms this theory, demonstrating that MPT implicitly performs integration over the latent space.

## Method Summary
The paper uses a theoretical proof based on the equivalence between log-marginal likelihood (LML) and exhaustive leave-M-out cross-validation, combined with combinatorial analysis of mask orderings. Empirical studies involve training PPCA and VAE models with MPT on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST datasets, comparing MPT loss to LML estimates. BERT experiments analyze the evolution of MPT curves for different masking rates using GLUE datasets and compare to pre-trained BERT models. The method requires implementing the theoretical proof, reproducing empirical studies with tractable models, and validating with large language models.

## Key Results
- MPT with cumulative scoring is mathematically equivalent to maximizing the model's marginal likelihood
- Fixed-rate masking creates a biased but consistent estimator that still maximizes LML during optimization
- BERT experiments show MPT curves evolve similarly to tractable models, supporting implicit latent space integration
- Empirical results confirm the theory across both tractable models and large language models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Masked pre-training (MPT) implicitly maximizes the marginal likelihood by averaging over all possible mask patterns.
- Mechanism: The log-marginal likelihood is invariant to the order of conditional probabilities, allowing it to be rewritten as an expectation over all D! possible mask orderings. Each random mask in MPT samples from this distribution.
- Core assumption: The model's log-marginal likelihood is invariant to the ordering of conditional probabilities in the factorization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This paper shows that masked pre-training with a suitable cumulative scoring function corresponds to maximizing the model's marginal likelihood"
  - [section] "Since the LML is invariant to random choices of M, we can re-write the sum in Eq. 2 as an expectation with a countable set of possible outcomes"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on harmonic mean estimation and Bayesian inference but don't directly address MPT-LML connection
- Break condition: If the invariance property doesn't hold (e.g., non-i.i.d. data or non-factorizable likelihoods), the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fixed-rate masking in MPT creates a biased but consistent estimator of the marginal likelihood that still drives LML maximization.
- Mechanism: When the masking rate is fixed (e.g., 15% in BERT), the cumulative sum is incomplete, creating a constant bias. However, since this bias is fixed during optimization, the model still maximizes LML.
- Core assumption: The bias introduced by fixed-rate masking is constant and doesn't change during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Even if its practical use avoids this cumulative sum, we show that choosing a fixed masking rate... leads to a stochastic biased estimation which still maximizes the log-marginal likelihood"
  - [section] "Since this is usually not affordable... we usually have a biased estimator. However, it is still sufficient to prove that MPT maximizes LML during training"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence - this appears to be a novel theoretical insight
- Break condition: If the bias changes during training (e.g., adaptive masking strategies), the maximization property may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: MPT performs implicit integration over the latent space by averaging self-predictive conditionals across different masking patterns.
- Mechanism: Each random mask pattern creates a different conditional probability, and averaging these is equivalent to integrating over latent variable mappings weighted by prior beliefs.
- Core assumption: Self-predictive conditionals can approximate the true posterior integration even when exact integration is intractable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "masked pre-training drives generalization so well... Is masked pre-training somehow related to the maximization of the marginal likelihood?"
  - [section] "In most cases, performing this integration is extremely difficult or not possible in training time. Therefore, we are interested in finding if alternative approximations qθ to the true self-conditional probabilities still produce accurate estimation"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence - this is a novel theoretical contribution
- Break condition: If the self-predictive approximations are poor (e.g., highly non-linear models with poor encoders), the implicit integration may fail.

- Design tradeoffs:

  - Number of random masks per epoch vs. accuracy in LML approximation
  - Model complexity vs. tractability of self-predictive conditionals
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor generalization despite high training accuracy (possibly due to incorrect masking rate)
  - Unstable training or exploding gradients (likely issues with conditional probability computation)
  - Convergence to suboptimal LML values (possibly due to insufficient random mask coverage)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific architectural or algorithmic components beyond masked pre-training contribute to the strong generalization of large language models like BERT?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that empirical results are impressive but a deeper understanding of why pre-trained models generalize so well is lacking, questioning if it's due to transformer architectures, over-parametrization, or something else entirely.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on linking masked pre-training to marginal likelihood maximization but does not isolate or quantify the contributions of other architectural elements like transformers or over-parametrization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies comparing models with varying architectures (e.g., non-transformer vs. transformer) and parameter counts trained with identical masked pre-training objectives.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of masking rate affect the convergence speed and final performance of masked pre-training, and what is the optimal masking rate for different data modalities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that fixing the masking rate (e.g., 15% as in BERT) leads to a biased estimator of the marginal likelihood, and discusses the role of masking rates but does not provide definitive guidance on optimal rates.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that fixed masking rates still maximize marginal likelihood, it does not explore how different rates impact training dynamics or performance across tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparisons of model performance and convergence speed across a range of masking rates for various datasets and model architectures.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the theoretical framework linking masked pre-training to marginal likelihood maximization be extended to autoregressive modeling of masked tokens, and how would this affect the implicit integration of latent variables?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The discussion section mentions that autoregressive modeling between masked tokens is not analyzed but hypothesizes it could be linked in further development.
- Why unresolved: The current theory assumes independent prediction of masked tokens, but autoregressive modeling is commonly used in practice, potentially altering the relationship with marginal likelihood.
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal proof extending the theory to autoregressive models and empirical validation showing the impact on latent variable integration and marginal likelihood maximization.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the implicit integration of latent variables in masked pre-training scale with increasing model size and data complexity, and what are the limitations of this approach for very large models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that masked pre-training in large language models behaves similarly to tractable models in terms of implicit integration, but does not explore scaling limits or potential issues.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the principle, it does not investigate how implicit integration performs as models grow in size or when dealing with more complex data distributions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of latent space structure and marginal likelihood estimates in increasingly large models trained on diverse, complex datasets, identifying any breakdown in implicit integration.

## Limitations
- Theoretical framework assumes i.i.d. data and relies on invariance of log-marginal likelihood to conditional ordering
- Empirical validation is limited to relatively simple models (PPCA, VAE) and a single large language model (BERT)
- The connection may not hold for non-i.i.d. data or models with complex dependencies

## Confidence
- MPT-LML equivalence theory (High): The mathematical proof is rigorous and well-established using combinatorial arguments and cross-validation equivalence
- Fixed-rate masking bias (Medium): While theoretically sound, the practical impact depends on specific masking strategies and may vary across domains
- BERT empirical validation (Medium): Results show promising trends but are limited by the complexity of large language models and lack of direct LML computation for intractable cases
