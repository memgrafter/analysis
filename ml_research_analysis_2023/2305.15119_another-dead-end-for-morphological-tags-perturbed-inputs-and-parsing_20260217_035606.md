# Another Dead End for Morphological Tags? Perturbed Inputs and Parsing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.15119
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15119
- **Reference count**: 11
- **Primary result**: Morphological tags generally harm parsing robustness under lexical perturbations except for sequence labeling architectures

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether morphological tags help or hinder parsers when faced with character-level perturbations in content words. The authors apply four types of synthetic perturbations—character deletion, swapping, insertion, and QWERTY-adjacent replacement—to test parser robustness across three families of dependency parsers. Results show that while morphological tags are generally harmful for transition-based and graph-based parsers under increasing noise levels, sequence labeling parsers continue to benefit from morphological information even in perturbed conditions. XPOS tags consistently outperform UPOS and FEATS across architectures and perturbation levels.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates three parser architectures—transition-based with pointer networks, graph-based biaffine, and sequence labeling—each trained with and without morphological input (UPOS, XPOS, FEATS). Four character-level perturbation types are systematically applied to content words in 14 UD treebanks. Experiments compare performance degradation under perturbation, contrasting perturbed tags with clean gold-standard tags to isolate the impact of tagger errors. The study measures parsing accuracy across varying perturbation levels to assess the utility of morphological information for robustness.

## Key Results
- Transition-based and graph-based parsers show consistent performance degradation with morphological tags as perturbation increases
- Sequence labeling parsers maintain benefits from morphological tags even under character-level noise
- XPOS tags yield the best overall performance, followed by UPOS, while FEATS generally harm parsing accuracy
- Even with clean gold-standard tags, transition-based models improve only slightly while sequence labeling parsers consistently benefit

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The findings suggest that morphological tags provide useful structural constraints for sequence labeling parsers but introduce harmful noise for transition-based and graph-based architectures when lexical inputs are perturbed. Sequence labeling models may better integrate morphological features as supplementary signals without disrupting their primary character-to-dependency mapping. In contrast, transition-based and graph-based models appear more sensitive to input inconsistencies, where perturbed morphological tags conflict with the corrupted lexical forms. The superior performance of XPOS tags indicates that fine-grained morphological distinctions provide more robust signals than coarse-grained UPOS or feature bundles.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dependency parsing fundamentals**: Understanding how syntactic dependencies are represented and evaluated is essential for interpreting parser performance metrics
- **Morphological tagging schemes**: UPOS (universal POS), XPOS (language-specific POS), and FEATS (morphological features) represent different granularities of morphological information
- **Parser architecture families**: Transition-based, graph-based, and sequence labeling approaches differ in how they construct dependency trees and handle input features
- **Adversarial perturbation testing**: Synthetic noise injection helps isolate model vulnerabilities and measure robustness in controlled conditions
- **Universal Dependencies framework**: Standard annotation scheme providing consistent morphological and syntactic annotations across languages
- **Why needed**: These concepts provide the theoretical and practical foundation for understanding how morphological information interacts with different parsing strategies under lexical noise
- **Quick check**: Can you explain why sequence labeling parsers might be more robust to input perturbations than transition-based parsers?

- **Design tradeoffs**: Morphological richness vs. noise sensitivity; computational overhead of feature extraction vs. potential accuracy gains
- **Failure signatures**: Degradation in parsing accuracy correlates with perturbation level; morphological tags amplify errors in transition/graph-based models

## Limitations
- Results based on English content words may not generalize to morphologically rich languages
- Controlled synthetic perturbations differ from real-world noise patterns in user-generated text
- Limited to three specific parser implementations, may not extend to architectural variations
- Focus on UD treebanks constrains generalizability to other annotation schemes

## Confidence
- **Morphological tags harm transition/graph-based parsers under noise**: High confidence
- **Sequence labeling benefits from morphological tags under perturbations**: Medium confidence
- **XPOS outperforms UPOS/FEATS across conditions**: High confidence for tested setup

## Next Checks
