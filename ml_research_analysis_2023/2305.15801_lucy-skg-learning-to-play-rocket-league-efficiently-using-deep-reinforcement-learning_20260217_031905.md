# Lucy-SKG: Learning to Play Rocket League Efficiently Using Deep Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15801
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15801
- Reference count: 40
- Lucy-SKG demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in 2v2 Rocket League through novel reward shaping and auxiliary tasks

## Executive Summary
Lucy-SKG is a deep reinforcement learning agent designed to efficiently learn complex 3D team-based gameplay in Rocket League. The agent introduces innovative reward shaping techniques, including Kinesthetic Reward Combinations and parameterized distance functions, to guide learning in a sparse-reward environment. By incorporating auxiliary tasks such as state representation and reward prediction, Lucy-SKG significantly improves sample efficiency and performance compared to baseline approaches.

The agent was trained in a self-play 2v2 environment and evaluated against top-tier bots Necto and Nexto. Results show that Lucy-SKG won the majority of matches (300-54 vs Necto, 300-4 vs Nexto), demonstrating its superior gameplay capabilities. Ablation studies confirm that each proposed component—reward shaping, auxiliary tasks, and action stacking—contributes positively to overall performance, with auxiliary tasks providing the most significant sample efficiency gains.

## Method Summary
Lucy-SKG leverages deep reinforcement learning with novel reward shaping techniques to address the challenges of learning complex 3D team-based gameplay in Rocket League. The agent introduces Kinesthetic Reward Combinations, which parameterize distance functions to provide more informative feedback during training. Auxiliary tasks, including state representation and reward prediction, are integrated to improve sample efficiency by encouraging the agent to learn useful representations of the game state. The agent operates in a 2v2 self-play environment, using a 5-action stacking approach to manage the complexity of continuous control in a physics-driven game.

## Key Results
- Lucy-SKG won 300 out of 354 matches against Necto and 300 out of 304 matches against Nexto in 2v2 self-play
- Ablation studies show that reward shaping, auxiliary tasks, and action stacking each contribute positively to performance
- Auxiliary tasks significantly improve sample efficiency, reducing the number of training steps required to achieve competitive performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Lucy-SKG's success stems from its ability to efficiently learn complex behaviors in a sparse-reward environment through targeted reward shaping and auxiliary learning. The Kinesthetic Reward Combinations provide more granular feedback by parameterizing distance functions, allowing the agent to better understand the relationship between actions and outcomes. The auxiliary tasks encourage the agent to learn meaningful state representations and predict future rewards, which improves generalization and reduces the need for extensive exploration.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Observation -> Feature Extractor -> Policy Network -> Action Selection -> Environment

**Critical Path**: Observation -> Feature Extractor -> Policy Network -> Action Selection

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Reward shaping vs. exploration: Shaped rewards guide learning but may bias behavior
- Auxiliary tasks vs. computational cost: Auxiliary tasks improve efficiency but increase training time
- Action stacking vs. complexity: 5-action stacking manages complexity but may limit adaptability

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Poor performance against diverse opponents suggests overfitting to specific strategies
- Slow convergence indicates ineffective reward shaping or insufficient exploration
- Inconsistent behavior across seeds suggests instability in training

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to 2v2 self-play against specific top-tier bots without testing against human players
- Performance claims based on single training runs without cross-validation across different seeds
- Lack of testing in diverse game modes and 3v3 matches to assess scalability

## Confidence
- High confidence: Effectiveness of individual reward shaping techniques and auxiliary tasks within tested framework
- Medium confidence: Sample efficiency improvements attributed to auxiliary tasks
- Low confidence: Generalizability to 3v3 matches, human opponents, or deployment in varied game modes
