# A Neuro-Symbolic Approach for Enhanced Human Motion Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11740
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11740
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: NeuroSyM improves motion prediction accuracy by up to 80% over baseline models using QTC-based interaction weighting

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces NeuroSyM, a neuro-symbolic approach that enhances human motion prediction by weighting interactions based on their reliability. The method leverages Qualitative Trajectory Calculus (QTC) and Conceptual Neighbourhood Diagrams (CND) to assess and weight the reliability of interactions between agents. When integrated into existing neural architectures like SGAN and DA-RNN, NeuroSyM demonstrates significant improvements in prediction accuracy across multiple datasets collected from both fixed and mobile camera perspectives.

## Method Summary
NeuroSyM integrates qualitative spatial reasoning with neural prediction architectures by using QTC to represent agent interactions and CND to assign reliability scores to these interactions. The approach modifies existing architectures by weighting pooled embeddings (in SGAN) or attention weights (in DA-RNN) based on the reliability of interactions at previous time steps. The method is tested on six datasets including ETH, UCY, and JackRabbot, using medium (48 steps) and long (80 steps) prediction horizons with evaluation metrics including ADE, FDE, DE-STD, and FDE-STD.

## Key Results
- NeuroSyM outperforms baseline SGAN and DA-RNN models in most cases
- Relative gain of up to 80% in prediction accuracy metrics
- Effective across both fixed and mobile camera perspectives
- Demonstrates consistent improvement in medium and long-term horizons

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The NeuroSyM approach improves motion prediction by weighting interactions based on their reliability.
- Mechanism: The approach leverages Qualitative Trajectory Calculus (QTC) to assess the reliability of interactions between agents and weights them accordingly in the prediction process. The Conceptual Neighbourhood Diagram (CND) is used to anticipate possible interactions that might influence the future state of an individual agent.
- Core assumption: The reliability of interactions, as assessed by QTC and CND, is a strong predictor of their influence on future states.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method leverages qualitative trajectory calculus (QTC) to assess the reliability of interactions between agents and weights them accordingly in the prediction process."
  - [section] "The likelihood of the next-step interaction state is used for labeling it given the current state of each agent (position and interaction)."
- Break condition: If the CND dictionary fails to accurately represent the reliability of interactions in a new domain, the weighting mechanism will not improve prediction accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: NeuroSyM can be integrated into existing neural architectures for human motion prediction to enhance accuracy.
- Mechanism: The approach modifies the pooling mechanism in architectures like SGAN and the attention weights in DA-RNN by incorporating a-priori information on the reliability of interactions. This is achieved by embedding the relative pose between agents, weighting it based on the CND-inspired label, and then max-pooling the weighted embedding across neighbors.
- Core assumption: The modification of existing architectures with the NeuroSyM approach will not disrupt their core functionality but will enhance their predictive accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We therefore propose a neuro-symbolic approach for human motion prediction (NeuroSyM), which weights differently the interactions in the neighbourhood by leveraging an intuitive technique for spatial representation called Qualitative Trajectory Calculus (QTC)."
  - [section] "NeuroSyM acts mainly on the pooling mechanism of the predictive models, where it represents human-human interactions by (a) embedding first their relative pose in all the observed states of each agent through a dense layer, then (b) weighing the embedded relative pose based on the CND-inspired label (αcnd) associated to the interaction at a previous time step, and finally (c) max-pooling the weighted embedding across neighbours in the global scene."
- Break condition: If the integration of NeuroSyM into existing architectures leads to overfitting or significantly increases computational complexity, it may not be a viable enhancement.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The approach is effective for both fixed and mobile camera perspectives in crowded scenarios.
- Mechanism: The NeuroSyM approach is tested on datasets collected from both fixed top-down cameras (ETH, UCY) and a mobile robot perspective (JackRabbot), demonstrating its generalizability across different data collection methods.
- Core assumption: The spatial interactions between agents, as captured by QTC, are invariant to the perspective of the camera.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proposed approach is experimentally tested on medium and long term time horizons using two architectures from the state of art, one of which is a baseline for human motion prediction and the other is a baseline for generic multivariate time-series prediction. Six datasets of challenging crowded scenarios, collected from both fixed and mobile cameras, were used for testing."
  - [section] "In order to generalise our evaluation to robotics application, we chose another well-known dataset, the JackRabbot (JRDB [26]), which provides multi-sensor data of human behaviours from a mobile robot perspective in populated indoor and outdoor environments."
- Break condition: If the spatial relationships between agents are significantly distorted by the perspective of the camera, the QTC-based weighting mechanism may not be effective.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of interaction radius R affect the performance of NeuroSyM DA-RNN in predicting human motion?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the interaction radius R = 3.7m was selected based on proxemics literature, but does not investigate the effect of different radii on prediction accuracy.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses one fixed interaction radius and does not explore how varying this parameter might impact the model's performance in different scenarios or crowd densities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with multiple interaction radii (e.g., 2m, 3.7m, 5m) and comparing the prediction accuracy (RMSE, MAE) across these different settings would provide insights into the optimal interaction radius for various crowd scenarios.

### Open Question 2

- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that this is a point to be addressed in future work but does not provide any comparative analysis of computational costs between the neuro-symbolic and neural approaches.

- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a detailed comparison of training time, inference time, and memory usage between NeuroSyM SGAN/DA-RNN and their original neural counterparts on the same hardware would provide a complete picture of the computational trade-offs involved.

## Limitations
- Requires extensive hyperparameter tuning for optimal performance across different datasets and scenarios
- Effectiveness depends on availability of QTC representations and appropriate CND dictionaries
- Increased computational complexity may be prohibitive for real-time applications in highly crowded environments

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using QTC and CND for weighting interactions is well-established and theoretically sound. The improvement in prediction accuracy metrics (ADE, FDE, DE-STD, FDE-STD) over baseline models is clearly demonstrated.
- **Medium Confidence**: The integration of NeuroSyM into existing architectures (SGAN and DA-RNN) is plausible and shows promise, but the extent of improvement may vary depending on the specific architecture and dataset.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the approach to datasets and scenarios beyond those tested (ETH, UCY, JackRabbot) is uncertain without further validation.
