# Distributional Method for Risk Averse Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14109
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14109
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes distributional learning method for risk-averse MDPs that achieves polynomial exploration complexity in finite state-action spaces

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a distributional learning approach for risk-averse reinforcement learning in Markov decision processes with latent costs. The method learns an auxiliary g-value function that characterizes the conditional distribution of the value function, circumventing the curse of dimensionality during exploration. Under a parallel sampling model, the approach achieves polynomial growth in exploration effort relative to state and action space sizes. The theoretical framework is validated through numerical experiments with various random cost distributions.

## Method Summary
The proposed method learns an auxiliary function (g-value) that captures the conditional distribution of the value function in risk-averse MDPs. This distributional approach avoids direct computation of value functions over the entire state-action space, which would suffer from exponential complexity. The method operates under a parallel sampling model where multiple samples can be drawn simultaneously, enabling the polynomial exploration complexity guarantee. The g-value function serves as a sufficient statistic for making risk-averse decisions while maintaining computational tractability.

## Key Results
- Theorem III.2 provides an upper bound on the convergence rate showing polynomial exploration complexity
- Numerical experiments demonstrate efficacy across various random cost distributions
- The g-value learning approach successfully avoids the curse of dimensionality in the exploration phase
- Method guarantees learning of optimal risk-averse policies in finite MDPs with latent costs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by learning a distributional representation of the value function through an auxiliary g-value function. This representation captures sufficient information about the cost distribution to make risk-averse decisions without requiring full knowledge of the entire distribution. By focusing on this sufficient statistic, the method reduces the exploration complexity from exponential to polynomial in the state and action space sizes. The parallel sampling model assumption enables simultaneous exploration of multiple state-action pairs, further contributing to the efficiency gains.

Design tradeoffs: Computational efficiency vs. distributional information retention; Parallel sampling assumption vs. practical implementation constraints

Failure signatures: Poor performance with highly skewed cost distributions; Increased sample complexity with large state-action spaces; Sensitivity to risk measure choice
