# Exploring Automatically Perturbed Natural Language Explanations in Relation Extraction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15520
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15520
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Perturbed context achieves competitive performance to annotated explanations while being 20-30× faster for relation extraction

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of natural language explanations in relation extraction tasks, challenging the assumption that their value lies in providing inductive bias. Through systematic experiments, the authors demonstrate that the primary benefit of explanations is actually the expansion of context space available to the model. They propose "perturbed context" - randomly initialized embeddings that serve as fine-tunable context parameters - achieving comparable results to manually annotated explanations but with significantly improved efficiency. The approach works across different training styles (fine-tunable and frozen models) and shows promise for broader NLP applications requiring entity knowledge.

## Method Summary
The method involves concatenating natural language explanations or perturbed contexts with target sentences before encoding them through a pre-trained language model (BERT or RoBERTa). Perturbed contexts are randomly initialized token embeddings that can be either globally shared across all samples or conditioned on individual inputs through learnable functions. The model uses the [CLS] token for classification after encoding. Experiments compare this approach against baselines (BERT without explanations) and ExpBERT (with annotated explanations), testing various corruption strategies for explanations and different variants of perturbed contexts including random, conditional, and factorized versions.

## Key Results
- Perturbed context achieves competitive performance to annotated explanations on Spouse, Disease, and TACRED datasets
- The approach is 20-30 times faster than ExpBERT while maintaining comparable accuracy
- Context space expansion, not inductive bias, is the primary driver of explanation effectiveness
- Perturbed contexts work effectively for both fine-tunable and frozen language models
- The method shows promise for other NLP tasks requiring entity knowledge integration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The primary value of natural language explanations lies in providing additional context space rather than their inductive bias.
- Mechanism: When explanations are concatenated with the original text before encoding, they expand the representation space available to the model, allowing it to learn richer patterns regardless of the semantic validity of the explanation content.
- Core assumption: Language models can effectively utilize additional context space to improve task performance even when that context contains random or corrupted information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find that corrupted explanations with diminished inductive biases can achieve competitive or superior performance compared to the original explanations"
  - [section 3] "Reducing the inductive bias of annotated explanations does not significantly decrease the effect"
- Break condition: If the language model cannot effectively process or utilize the additional context space, or if the context becomes too noisy to be useful for learning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tunable perturbed contexts can achieve competitive results to manually annotated explanations by providing flexible, learnable context.
- Mechanism: Randomly initialized context tokens serve as fine-tunable parameters that the model can adapt during training, effectively replacing the need for manually curated explanations while maintaining representational power.
- Core assumption: The model can learn to use the randomly initialized context tokens as effective features for the downstream task through fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "the proposed perturbed contexts overall achieve competitive performance with annotated explanations"
  - [abstract] "Utilizing the proposed automatic perturbed context, we were able to attain comparable results to annotated explanations"
- Break condition: If the randomly initialized context cannot be effectively optimized or if it interferes with learning meaningful representations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The effectiveness of explanations varies significantly based on training style and dataset characteristics.
- Mechanism: Frozen language models benefit more from explanations because they cannot adapt their internal representations, making external context more valuable, while fine-tunable models can learn similar patterns through parameter updates.
- Core assumption: The training regime fundamentally affects how models utilize external information versus internal learning capacity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3] "the effect of natural language explanations is sensitive to the training style and the downstream dataset"
  - [section 3] "For frozen language models... introducing annotated natural language explanations significantly improves the effect"
- Break condition: If the model architecture or training procedure changes the balance between external context utilization and internal representation learning.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input sentence → Perturbed context generation → Token concatenation → Language model encoding → [CLS] token extraction → Classification layer → Output prediction
- Critical path: The model takes a target sentence with entity placeholders, generates or retrieves perturbed context tokens, concatenates them, encodes through BERT/RoBERTa, extracts the [CLS] token, and passes it through a classification layer
- Design tradeoffs: Fine-tunable contexts provide flexibility but increase training time and risk overfitting; fixed contexts are faster but may be less effective; global contexts are simpler but may miss sample-specific patterns
- Failure signatures: Performance degradation when using perturbed contexts on datasets where explanations were previously effective; inability to converge when corrupted explanations are used with frozen models; minimal improvement over baseline when using conditioned perturbed contexts
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare frozen vs fine-tunable models with and without perturbed contexts on a small relation extraction dataset
  2. Test different variants of perturbed contexts (random, conditional, factorized) to understand their relative effectiveness
  3. Measure training efficiency and parameter count differences between annotated explanations and perturbed contexts

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise relationship between perturbed context and prompt tuning, and how do their mechanisms of leveraging pre-trained language models differ?
- Basis in paper: explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper briefly mentions that perturbed context differs from prompt tuning by using the [CLS] token for classification instead of mapping [MASK] predictions to labels. However, a deeper analysis of how perturbed context exploits contextual representation generalization compared to prompt tuning's focus on [MASK] prediction generalization is lacking.
- What evidence would resolve it: A rigorous mathematical formalization of both perturbed context and prompt tuning, detailing their respective interactions with pre-trained language models, followed by controlled experiments comparing their performance across various few-shot learning scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the effectiveness of perturbed context scale with the size and complexity of the relation extraction task, particularly for tasks with extremely large label spaces?
- Basis in paper: inferred
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates competitive results for perturbed context on three relation extraction datasets. However, the scalability of this approach to tasks with significantly larger and more diverse label spaces, such as open-domain relation extraction, is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive experiments on relation extraction datasets with progressively larger label spaces, comparing perturbed context to state-of-the-art methods and analyzing the trade-off between context size and model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for initializing and training the parameters of the perturbed context to maximize its effectiveness across different NLP tasks?
- Basis in paper: explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions that the initialization of the perturbed context parameters affects the results and suggests using a normal distribution based on token embeddings. However, the impact of different initialization strategies and training schedules on the perturbed context's performance is not thoroughly investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing various initialization methods (e.g., normal distribution, uniform distribution, pre-trained embeddings) and training strategies (e.g., joint training, separate training) for the perturbed context across a diverse set of NLP tasks, analyzing the impact on model performance and training efficiency.

## Limitations
- Experimental scope limited to three relation extraction datasets without extensive validation on other NLP tasks
- Efficiency claims depend on specific implementation details and hardware configurations not fully specified
- Trade-off between efficiency and potential loss of semantic coherence in explanations remains unexplored
- Sensitivity to initialization strategies and context size hyperparameters not thoroughly investigated
- Scalability to tasks with very large label spaces or complex reasoning requirements not demonstrated

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that context space expansion is the primary benefit of explanations has strong experimental support from the corrupted explanations experiments.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of perturbed contexts is well-demonstrated within the paper's experimental framework, but generalizability to other tasks needs validation.

**Medium Confidence**: The observation about training style sensitivity is supported by results but lacks deep theoretical grounding for the underlying mechanisms.

## Next Checks

**Validation Check 1**: Replicate the perturbed context experiments on an additional relation extraction dataset not used in the original paper (such as SemEval or DocRED) to test the generalizability of the context space hypothesis across different dataset characteristics and relation types.

**Validation Check 2**: Conduct ablation studies varying the perturbed context size (m parameter) and initialization strategies to determine the sensitivity of the approach to these hyperparameters and identify optimal configurations for different training scenarios.

**Validation Check 3**: Apply the perturbed context approach to a non-relation-extraction task that requires entity or knowledge integration (such as entity typing or knowledge base completion) to validate the broader applicability of the context space hypothesis beyond the original experimental scope.
