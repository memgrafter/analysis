# Enhancing Fine-Tuning Based Backdoor Defense with Sharpness-Aware Minimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11823
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11823
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: FT-SAM achieves state-of-the-art backdoor defense by leveraging Sharpness-Aware Minimization with adaptive perturbations to shrink backdoor-related neuron norms.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FT-SAM, a novel approach to mitigate backdoor effects in neural networks during fine-tuning. The method leverages Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) with adaptive perturbations to target backdoor-related neurons, which are observed to have larger weight norms. Experiments on CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, and GTSRB demonstrate that FT-SAM significantly reduces attack success rates while maintaining high accuracy. The approach is robust across different datasets, network architectures, and poisoning ratios, and can enhance existing defense methods when combined with them.

## Method Summary
FT-SAM combines fine-tuning with Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) and adaptive perturbations to mitigate backdoor effects. The method identifies backdoor-related neurons by their large weight norms and applies targeted perturbations during fine-tuning to shrink these norms, helping the model escape local minima where backdoor effects persist. The adaptive perturbation budget is based on neuron norms, ensuring larger perturbations are applied to backdoor-related neurons. This approach maintains high accuracy on benign inputs while effectively reducing attack success rates.

## Key Results
- FT-SAM achieves state-of-the-art defense performance, significantly reducing attack success rates across CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet, and GTSRB datasets.
- The method is robust to different datasets, network architectures, and poisoning ratios.
- FT-SAM can enhance existing defense methods when combined with them, improving overall backdoor defense effectiveness.

- Core assumption: Larger perturbations on high-norm neurons lead to more effective backdoor mitigation.

## Limitations
- Core claims rely heavily on empirical observations about backdoor-related neurons having larger weight norms, which may not generalize across all architectures or attack scenarios.
- Effectiveness of adaptive perturbations depends on proper tuning of ρ, with no clear guidelines provided for different datasets.
- Claim that FT-SAM can be easily combined with existing defenses lacks quantitative validation in the paper.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The basic premise that SAM can help escape local minima and that adaptive perturbations can target high-norm neurons.
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of FT-SAM across different datasets and attack types, given the limited scope of experiments.
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of the method to architectures beyond ConvNets and ResNet variants.
