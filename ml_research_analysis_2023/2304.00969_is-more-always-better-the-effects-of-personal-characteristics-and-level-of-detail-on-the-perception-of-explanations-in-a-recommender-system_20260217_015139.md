# Is More Always Better? The Effects of Personal Characteristics and Level of Detail on the Perception of Explanations in a Recommender System

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00969
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00969
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Explanation perception varies by user characteristics and explanation goals, suggesting personalized explanations outperform one-size-fits-all approaches

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how personal characteristics and explanation detail levels affect user perception of explainable recommender systems. The researchers developed RIMA, a transparent recommender system providing explanations at three levels of detail (basic, intermediate, advanced). Through a user study with 31 participants, they found that the perception of explanations varies based on explanation goals and user types. Key findings include: need for cognition affects satisfaction, visualization familiarity impacts trust, personal innovativeness influences scrutability, and technical expertise affects efficiency perceptions. The study suggests designing personalized explanations tailored to user characteristics and specific explanation goals, moving away from one-size-fits-all approaches in explainable recommendation systems.

## Method Summary
The study employed a within-subject design where 31 participants evaluated recommendations with three different explanation levels (basic, intermediate, advanced) across seven explanation goals. Participants first completed questionnaires measuring six personal characteristics (need for cognition, visualization familiarity, personal innovativeness, technical expertise, social media usage, domain knowledge). The RIMA system generated recommendations based on Semantic Scholar publications and provided explanations at varying levels of detail. Participants evaluated each explanation type on seven goals using Likert scales, and qualitative feedback was collected. Repeated measures ANCOVA analyses examined interactions between personal characteristics and explanation levels.

## Key Results
- Need for cognition significantly affects satisfaction with explanation levels
- Visualization familiarity impacts trust perceptions of different explanation levels
- Technical expertise influences efficiency perceptions of basic and intermediate explanations
- Different explanation goals (transparency, scrutability, trust, etc.) show varying levels of user perception across explanation levels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Personalized explanation levels improve user satisfaction more than one-size-fits-all explanations.
- Mechanism: Users with high need for cognition prefer detailed explanations tailored to their data, while those with low need for cognition are satisfied with basic explanations. The study found significant interaction between NFC and satisfaction with explanation level.
- Core assumption: Users with high NFC require detailed, personalized explanations to meet their cognitive needs, while those with low NFC are overwhelmed by too much information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "need for cognition affects satisfaction"
  - [section] "Our analysis revealed that users with high NFC were dissatisfied with the advanced explanation... as they expected it to explain the reasoning behind the recommendations using their actual interest and tweet keywords instead of example values."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on varying levels of detail but don't specifically address NFC-satisfaction relationship.
- Break condition: If users' NFC doesn't actually correlate with their preferred explanation depth, or if personalized content isn't feasible to implement.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Visualization familiarity influences trust in explanations with different levels of detail.
- Mechanism: Users with high visualization familiarity expect more sophisticated visualizations and are disappointed by static, simplified representations, leading to reduced trust. The study found significant interaction between VF and trust with explanation level.
- Core assumption: Users with high VF have higher expectations for data visualization quality and sophistication, making them more critical of static or simplified explanations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "visualization familiarity impacts trust"
  - [section] "users with high VF perceived the advanced explanation of the recommendations as less trustworthy... The qualitative analysis revealed that users with high VF disliked the static appearance of the advanced explanation."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers mention visualization but don't specifically address VF-trust relationship in explanations.
- Break condition: If VF doesn't actually correlate with expectations for explanation sophistication, or if static explanations are acceptable to all users regardless of VF.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Technical expertise affects efficiency perceptions of explanations with different levels of detail.
- Mechanism: Users with high technical expertise find basic and intermediate explanations efficient for quick understanding, while those with low expertise find them overwhelming and less efficient. The study found significant interaction between TE and efficiency with explanation level.
- Core assumption: Users with high TE can quickly parse and understand technical explanations, while those with low TE need more time and guidance to comprehend the same information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "technical expertise affects efficiency perceptions"
  - [section] "users with high TE had higher perceptions of efficiency of the basic and intermediate explanation... users with low TE seemed to have more difficulties in understanding the basic and intermediate explanation."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss explanation efficiency but don't specifically address TE-efficiency relationship.
- Break condition: If TE doesn't actually correlate with ability to efficiently process explanations, or if all users benefit equally from detailed explanations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  RIMA application with three explanation levels -> Interest model inference using Semantic Scholar publications -> Recommendation generation using tweet similarity -> User interface for selecting explanation levels -> Questionnaire system for collecting personal characteristics and evaluation data

- Critical path:
  1. User logs in with Semantic Scholar ID
  2. System infers interest model from publications
  3. System fetches and recommends tweets
  4. User selects explanation level (basic/intermediate/advanced)
  5. System displays explanation with chosen level of detail
  6. User evaluates explanation on seven goals

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Static vs. personalized advanced explanations: Static examples are easier to implement but may disappoint users with high NFC who want personalized data
  - Three levels vs. continuous scale: Three discrete levels are simpler to implement and evaluate but may not capture all user preferences
  - Self-report vs. objective measurements: Self-report is easier to collect but may be biased, while objective measurements are more reliable but harder to implement

- Failure signatures:
  - Users consistently choosing the wrong explanation level for their needs
  - High drop-off rates when accessing advanced explanations
  - Negative correlation between VF and trust that can't be explained by explanation quality
  - Users with high TE reporting low efficiency for basic explanations

- First 3 experiments:
  1. A/B test with personalized vs. static advanced explanations to measure impact on user satisfaction, particularly for high NFC users
  2. Eye-tracking study to measure how users with different VF levels interact with visualizations at each explanation level
  3. Task completion time study to objectively measure efficiency differences for users with varying TE levels across explanation levels

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different levels of explanation detail impact user trust differently for users with varying levels of visualization familiarity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper found a significant interaction between visualization familiarity (VF) and explanation level in terms of trust (F(2,48) = 3.639, p = .034, f = .39), with users with high VF perceiving the advanced explanation as less trustworthy than users with low VF.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identified this interaction, it did not explore the underlying reasons for why users with high VF perceive advanced explanations as less trustworthy, or what specific aspects of the visualization design contribute to this effect.
- What evidence would resolve it: A follow-up study could manipulate specific aspects of the visualization design (e.g., clarity, complexity, use of color) in the advanced explanation and measure their impact on trust for users with different levels of visualization familiarity.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the level of explanation detail impact the efficiency of understanding recommendations for users with different levels of technical expertise?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper found a significant interaction between technical expertise (TE) and explanation level in terms of efficiency (F(2,48) = 3.262, p = .047, f = .37), with users with high TE perceiving the basic and intermediate explanations as more efficient than users with low TE.
- Why unresolved: The paper did not investigate the specific aspects of the basic and intermediate explanations that contribute to their perceived efficiency for users with high TE, or how these aspects could be improved for users with low TE.
- What evidence would resolve it: A follow-up study could conduct a detailed analysis of user interactions with the basic and intermediate explanations, measuring metrics such as time spent, number of clicks, and accuracy of understanding. This could reveal the specific aspects of the explanations that impact efficiency for different levels of technical expertise.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the level of explanation detail impact the scrutability of recommendations for users with different levels of personal innovativeness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper found a significant interaction between personal innovativeness (PI) and explanation level in terms of scrutability (F(2,48) = 3.478, p = .039, f = .38), with users with low PI perceiving the advanced explanation as less scrutable than users with high PI.
- Why unresolved: The paper did not explore the reasons why users with low PI find the advanced explanation less scrutable, or what specific aspects of the explanation design could be improved to enhance scrutability for this group.
- What evidence would resolve it: A follow-up study could conduct interviews with users with low PI to understand their specific needs and preferences for scrutability in explanations. This could inform the design of more scrutable explanations tailored to this group, which could then be evaluated in a user study.

## Limitations
- Small sample size (n=31) may limit statistical power for detecting interaction effects
- Static, example-based advanced explanations may not capture the potential of personalized explanations
- Reliance on self-reported measures for personal characteristics may introduce bias and measurement error

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The finding that different explanation goals (transparency, scrutability, trust, effectiveness, persuasiveness, efficiency, satisfaction) are perceived differently by users is well-supported by the data and consistent with existing literature on explanation design.

**Medium Confidence:** The interaction effects between personal characteristics (NFC, VF, PI, TE, TP, DK) and explanation levels show statistical significance but require larger samples for robust validation. The specific mechanisms described (e.g., why high VF users distrust static advanced explanations) are plausible but not definitively proven.

**Low Confidence:** The practical implications for system design, particularly around personalized explanation generation, are promising but remain largely theoretical given the current implementation constraints.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a larger-scale study (n > 100) to verify the interaction effects between personal characteristics and explanation levels, with particular focus on NFC-satisfaction and VF-trust relationships.

2. Implement and test a version of the advanced explanation that uses personalized data (actual interest keywords, specific publication examples) rather than static examples to assess impact on user satisfaction.

3. Perform a controlled experiment comparing self-reported efficiency measures with objective task completion times to validate the relationship between technical expertise and explanation efficiency perceptions.
