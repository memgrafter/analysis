# Preserving Commonsense Knowledge from Pre-trained Language Models via Causal Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10790
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10790
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: CET improves commonsense QA accuracy by 0.05% to 3.53% over vanilla fine-tuning across six datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning of pre-trained language models by introducing a causal inference-based method called Causal Effect Tuning (CET). The approach preserves commonsense knowledge from pre-trained models while enabling adaptation to new tasks by retrieving missing causal effects from the pre-trained data. CET operates as a parameter-free plug-in module that can enhance existing question-answering models without requiring architectural modifications. Experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements on six commonsense QA benchmarks, validating the effectiveness of the causal approach for knowledge preservation during fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The Causal Effect Tuning method introduces a causal graph-based approach to fine-tuning that explicitly models the relationship between pre-trained knowledge and target task learning. The core innovation involves estimating causal effects between model representations using a heuristic approximation based on K-nearest neighbors from pre-trained data. During fine-tuning, CET retrieves missing causal effects from the pre-trained dataset to compensate for knowledge loss, effectively bridging the gap between old and new knowledge. The method is implemented as a flexible plug-in module that requires no additional learnable parameters, making it compatible with existing fine-tuning pipelines for question-answering tasks.

## Key Results
- CET achieves 3.53% accuracy improvement over vanilla fine-tuning on CommonsenseQA dataset
- Outperforms state-of-the-art fine-tuning methods across all six tested commonsense QA datasets
- Demonstrates consistent improvements ranging from 0.05% to 3.53% across different benchmarks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CET works by explicitly modeling the causal relationships between pre-trained knowledge and target task learning through a causal graph framework. The method retrieves missing causal effects from pre-trained data to compensate for knowledge loss during fine-tuning, effectively preserving commonsense knowledge while adapting to new tasks. By estimating these causal effects using K-nearest neighbor heuristics, CET can identify and maintain important knowledge pathways that would otherwise be lost during standard fine-tuning procedures.

## Foundational Learning
- **Catastrophic forgetting**: The tendency of neural networks to rapidly lose previously learned information when trained on new tasks. Why needed: CET directly addresses this fundamental limitation of fine-tuning approaches.
- **Causal inference in ML**: Using causal graphs and effects to understand and manipulate relationships between variables in machine learning systems. Why needed: Provides theoretical foundation for CET's knowledge preservation mechanism.
- **K-nearest neighbor heuristics**: Approximation methods using similarity to known examples for estimating properties of new data points. Why needed: CET uses this to estimate causal effects efficiently without additional parameters.

## Limitations
- Empirical validation limited to commonsense QA tasks with modest absolute improvements (0.05% to 3.53%)
- K-nearest neighbor heuristic may not scale optimally to larger, more complex models
- Claims about preserving general commonsense knowledge remain theoretical without direct measurement
