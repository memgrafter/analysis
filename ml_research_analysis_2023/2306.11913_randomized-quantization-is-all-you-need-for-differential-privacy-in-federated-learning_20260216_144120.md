# Randomized Quantization is All You Need for Differential Privacy in Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11913
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11913
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: RQM achieves better privacy-accuracy trade-offs than PBM on EMNIST with m=16

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Randomized Quantization Mechanism (RQM) for achieving differential privacy in federated learning without additive discrete noise. The mechanism relies solely on randomized quantization through three steps: enlarging output range, sub-sampling quantization levels, and randomized rounding. Theoretical analysis proves that RQM satisfies local Renyi differential privacy guarantees. Empirical results on EMNIST demonstrate that RQM achieves lower Renyi divergence than the state-of-the-art Poisson Binomial Mechanism (PBM) for the same number of quantization levels, leading to better privacy-accuracy trade-offs when integrated into standard differentially private federated learning frameworks.

## Method Summary
The Randomized Quantization Mechanism (RQM) consists of three key steps. First, it enlarges the output range beyond the input range and establishes evenly spaced quantization bins. Second, it randomly sub-samples feasible quantization levels from these bins. Third, it performs randomized rounding on the sub-sampled levels to produce the final quantized output. The mechanism achieves differential privacy through this quantization process alone, without requiring additive discrete noise. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that RQM satisfies local Renyi differential privacy guarantees, while empirical evaluation shows improved privacy-accuracy trade-offs compared to the Poisson Binomial Mechanism when applied to federated learning on the EMNIST dataset.

## Key Results
- RQM achieves lower Renyi divergence than PBM on EMNIST for m=16, providing better Renyi DP guarantees
- RQM integrated into differentially private federated learning yields higher model accuracy than PBM
- The improved privacy-accuracy trade-off is attributed to RQM's additional hyperparameter flexibility compared to PBM

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RQM works by leveraging the inherent randomness in the quantization process to achieve differential privacy. The mechanism exploits the fact that when quantization bins are properly configured and combined with randomized sub-sampling and rounding, the resulting output distribution provides sufficient privacy guarantees. Unlike traditional approaches that add discrete noise, RQM embeds the privacy protection directly into the quantization operation itself. The key insight is that by carefully controlling the quantization parameters and randomization steps, the mechanism can simultaneously achieve the required privacy guarantees while minimizing information loss that would degrade model accuracy.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input data -> Quantization bins -> Sub-sampling module -> Randomized rounding -> Output
Critical path: The privacy guarantee is established through the combination of sub-sampling and randomized rounding operations, with quantization bins providing the structure for controlled information loss.
Design tradeoffs: RQM trades implementation simplicity (no additive noise) for careful parameter tuning of quantization bins and randomization probabilities. The mechanism must balance bin size (affecting utility) against the number of bins (affecting privacy).
Failure signatures: Poor privacy-accuracy trade-offs manifest as either insufficient privacy guarantees (high Renyi divergence) or degraded model performance (accuracy loss). These typically occur when quantization bins are improperly sized or when randomization probabilities are poorly calibrated.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees depend on assumptions about evenly spaced quantization bins and sufficient randomness in sub-sampling
- Potential bias from randomized rounding on overall privacy guarantees is not fully explored
- Empirical evaluation limited to EMNIST dataset, with generalizability to other federated learning scenarios unclear
- Comparison with PBM focuses on single hyperparameter setting (m=16) rather than comprehensive evaluation
