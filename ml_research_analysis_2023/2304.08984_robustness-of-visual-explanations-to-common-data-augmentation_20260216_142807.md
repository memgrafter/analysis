# Robustness of Visual Explanations to Common Data Augmentation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.08984
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08984
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Explanations are less robust than model predictions under data augmentation, with LRP composites and Guided Backprop showing greater stability

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how common data augmentation transformations affect the stability of post-hoc visual explanations for neural networks. The research compares explanation robustness to model prediction robustness under transformations like brightness, hue, saturation, rotation, scaling, and translation. Experiments were conducted both with and without data augmentation in training to understand how training strategies impact explanation stability. The study evaluates multiple explainability methods including gradient-based approaches and Layer-wise Relevance Propagation variants.

## Method Summary
The researchers conducted controlled experiments comparing visual explanation stability across multiple data augmentation transformations. They evaluated six different explainability methods (Gradients, Input x Gradients, Integrated Gradients, Guided Backprop, Deconvolution, and three LRP variants) on image classification tasks. The experiments were performed with and without data augmentation during training to assess whether training strategies influence explanation robustness. Stability was measured by comparing explanation similarity before and after augmentation using quantitative metrics.

## Key Results
- Visual explanations show significantly lower robustness to augmentation compared to model classification accuracy
- LRP composites and Guided Backprop demonstrate greater stability across augmentation transformations
- Gradients and Input x Gradients methods exhibit lower stability under the same conditions
- Using data augmentation during training does not significantly improve explanation stability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The observed stability differences stem from how each explainability method computes attributions. Methods like LRP composites and Guided Backprop incorporate non-linear operations that create more consistent attribution patterns under small input perturbations. In contrast, pure gradient-based methods (Gradients, Input x Gradients) are more sensitive to local variations in the input space, making them less stable under augmentation. The training strategy doesn't improve explanation stability because the attribution computation process itself remains vulnerable to input transformations regardless of model generalization improvements.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Preprocessing -> Augmentation -> Model Forward Pass -> Explanation Method -> Stability Evaluation

**Critical Path**
Input image → Augmentation → Model inference → Explanation computation → Similarity measurement → Stability assessment

**Design Tradeoffs**
The study prioritizes quantitative stability measurement over qualitative explanation quality assessment, potentially missing practical utility aspects of explanations in real-world applications.

**Failure Signatures**
Low stability scores indicate that small input perturbations cause significant changes in attribution patterns, suggesting the explanation method may not be reliable for robust decision-making.

**First 3 Experiments**
1. Baseline: Compute explanations without any augmentation
2. Single transformation: Apply one augmentation type (e.g., brightness change) and compare explanations
3. Combined transformations: Apply multiple augmentations simultaneously to test cumulative effects

## Limitations
- Experiments focus primarily on image classification, limiting generalizability to other tasks
- Evaluation relies on quantitative similarity metrics that may not capture semantic meaning
- Limited testing across diverse model architectures and datasets

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: LRP composites and Guided Backprop demonstrate greater stability across augmentations
**Medium Confidence**: Explanations are generally less robust than classification performance
**Medium Confidence**: Data augmentation during training doesn't significantly improve explanation stability
