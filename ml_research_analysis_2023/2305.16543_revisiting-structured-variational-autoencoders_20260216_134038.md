# Revisiting Structured Variational Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16543
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16543
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: Structured variational autoencoders (SVAEs) demonstrate advantages over general alternatives in accuracy and efficiency using parallel Kalman filtering and self-supervised masking

## Executive Summary
This paper revisits structured variational autoencoders (SVAEs) and demonstrates their advantages over more general alternatives in terms of accuracy and efficiency. The authors develop a modern implementation using JAX with parallel Kalman filtering and smoothing for significant speed-ups on parallel hardware. They propose a simple self-supervised scheme for learning latent dynamics that leverages SVAEs' strengths in handling missing data. The approach is evaluated on linear dynamical system and synthetic pendulum datasets, showing better predictive performance and learning informative representations about dynamics.

## Method Summary
The paper implements SVAEs using JAX with parallel Kalman filtering and smoothing algorithms. The method leverages the structure of linear Gaussian dynamical systems by using conjugate Gaussian potentials from a recognition network combined with prior parameters via message passing. A self-supervised masking scheme randomly masks observations during training to encourage learning accurate dynamics. The approach is compared against RNN-based recognition networks (RNN-MF, RNN-AR-L, RNN-AR-NL) and CNN-AR-L baselines on synthetic datasets.

## Key Results
- SVAEs with linear Gaussian dynamics achieve competitive performance on synthetic datasets
- SVAEs demonstrate superior predictive performance for high-dimensional observations with nonlinear underlying dynamics
- Parallel Kalman filtering achieves nearly ten-fold speedup over RNN methods
- Self-supervised masking encourages learning accurate dynamics by forcing models to rely on learned dynamics to fill in missing observations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Structure-exploiting amortized inference yields more accurate posterior approximations than unstructured RNN-based inference
- Mechanism: The SVAE uses a recognition network that outputs conjugate Gaussian potentials for each time step. These potentials are combined with the prior parameters via message passing algorithms (e.g., Kalman smoothing) to form the variational posterior. This leverages the known structure of the prior to perform inference more accurately than RNNs which must learn temporal dependencies from scratch.
- Core assumption: The prior distribution belongs to an exponential family (e.g., linear Gaussian dynamical system) and the true posterior can be well-approximated by combining conjugate potentials with this prior
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Second, we show that by exploiting structure in the prior, the SVAE learns more accurate models and posterior distributions, which translate into improved performance on prediction tasks."
  - [section 2.1] "The SVAE exploits structure in the prior distribution by incorporating it into the target and only learning to produce conjugate potentials."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct comparison of SVAE vs RNN posterior accuracy in cited papers
- Break Condition: If the prior is not well-specified or the true posterior has complex dependencies that cannot be captured by combining conjugate potentials with the prior

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Parallel Kalman filtering and smoothing enables significant speedups on modern hardware compared to sequential RNN-based inference
- Mechanism: The forward filtering and backward smoothing operations in Kalman filtering can be cast as an associative operation on Gaussian distributions. This allows the use of parallel prefix sum algorithms to compute the filtering and smoothing in O(log T) parallel time instead of O(T) sequential time.
- Core assumption: The model prior is a linear Gaussian state space model, allowing the filtering and smoothing operations to be expressed as associative operations on Gaussian distributions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "We can leverage the prefix sum algorithm for parallel computational span that is only logarithmic in sequence length."
  - [section 4.2] "The SVAE with parallel scan achieves nearly ten-fold speedup over the faster RNN methods."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations about parallel Kalman filtering speedups
- Break Condition: If the prior is not a linear Gaussian state space model, the filtering and smoothing operations may not be associative and parallelizable in the same way

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Self-supervised masking of observations encourages the model to learn accurate dynamics by forcing it to rely on its learned dynamics to fill in missing observations
- Mechanism: During training, a random continuous subsequence of observations is masked (set to zero). Since the model must reconstruct the original unmasked data, it is incentivized to learn accurate dynamics that can predict the masked observations based on the observed ones.
- Core assumption: The SVAE can naturally handle missing data by simply dropping the corresponding conjugate potentials, which forces it to rely on the prior dynamics
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "The SVAE has a unique advantage with this self-supervised scheme due to its structured approach to inference. Since each observation produces a separate conjugate potential, we can apply the mask by simply dropping the corresponding potentials."
  - [section 4.3] "We found that RNN-based recognition networks failed to learn accurate models when significant masking was used in early stages of training; thus, we gave these architectures full observations for the first 10-100 epochs before applying the masks."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations about self-supervised masking for learning dynamics
- Break Condition: If the masking is too aggressive early in training, it may cause posterior collapse in RNN-based models that cannot handle the missing data as naturally

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Recognition network -> Conjugate Gaussian potentials -> Message passing (Kalman smoothing) -> Variational posterior -> Decoder

- Critical path:
  1. Recognition network processes observations to produce conjugate potentials
  2. Conjugate potentials combined with prior via message passing to form variational posterior
  3. Variational posterior used to compute ELBO and gradients for training
  4. Decoder maps latent states to observations for reconstruction and prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a structured prior (e.g., linear Gaussian) vs. a more flexible prior
  - Parallelizable message passing (e.g., Kalman smoothing) vs. sequential message passing
  - Self-supervised masking to encourage learning of dynamics vs. using full observations

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor predictive performance: Model may not have learned accurate dynamics
  - Posterior collapse: Recognition network may be ignoring latent states and relying too heavily on the prior
  - Slow training: Model may be too complex or not leveraging parallel hardware effectively

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement SVAE with linear Gaussian dynamics on a simple LDS dataset and compare ELBO and predictive performance to an RNN-based model
  2. Implement parallel Kalman filtering and smoothing using prefix sum algorithm and measure speedup compared to sequential implementation
  3. Implement self-supervised masking and evaluate its effect on learning dynamics and predictive performance on a pendulum dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of SVAEs scale to higher-dimensional latent states and observations in real-world datasets beyond synthetic data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows SVAEs achieve competitive performance on synthetic linear dynamical systems and a synthetic pendulum dataset, but scaling to higher dimensions is mentioned as a future direction with only preliminary results in the appendix.
- Why unresolved: The experiments in the main text focus on relatively low-dimensional latent spaces (up to 5 dimensions). The appendix shows some results for 32 and 64 dimensional latent spaces, but real-world datasets often have much higher dimensionality and more complex dynamics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying SVAEs to high-dimensional real-world sequential datasets (e.g., video data, neural recordings, financial time series) with latent dimensions in the hundreds or thousands, comparing performance to state-of-the-art sequential VAEs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the parallel Kalman filtering approach be extended to non-linear dynamical systems or more complex graphical model priors?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that parallel Kalman filtering is leveraged for linear Gaussian dynamical systems, but states that the approach "can also be applied more generally" without providing details or experiments.
- Why unresolved: The parallel filtering approach is only demonstrated for linear Gaussian models. It's unclear how well it would work for non-linear dynamics or more complex priors like switching linear dynamical systems or recurrent switching linear dynamical systems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of parallel message passing algorithms for non-linear dynamical systems (e.g., extended/unscented Kalman filtering) or more complex priors, with benchmarks comparing computational efficiency to sequential implementations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the self-supervised masking approach affect model performance across different data modalities and missing data patterns?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a self-supervised masking scheme that forces models to learn dynamics by randomly masking subsequences, showing benefits for pendulum data but noting RNN-based methods fail with significant masking early in training.
- Why unresolved: The masking approach is only demonstrated on one synthetic dataset with a specific type of missing data (contiguous blocks). It's unclear how robust this approach is to different data modalities (e.g., text, audio) or missing data patterns (e.g., random missing entries, irregular sampling).
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments applying the masking approach to diverse sequential data types with various missing data patterns, analyzing the trade-offs between masking rate, training stability, and predictive performance.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation on real-world datasets, with experiments primarily on synthetic data
- Theoretical advantages over RNN-based approaches lack direct empirical comparison in terms of posterior accuracy
- Self-supervised masking benefits demonstrated only on one synthetic dataset with contiguous masking patterns

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The JAX implementation details and parallel prefix sum algorithm for Kalman filtering are well-specified and technically sound
- **Medium confidence**: The theoretical advantages of structured inference over unstructured RNN approaches are plausible but under-validated empirically
- **Medium confidence**: Self-supervised masking strategy effectiveness is demonstrated on synthetic data but requires validation on more complex real-world scenarios
