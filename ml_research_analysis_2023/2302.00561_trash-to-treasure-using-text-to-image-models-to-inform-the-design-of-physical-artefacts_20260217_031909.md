# Trash to Treasure: Using text-to-image models to inform the design of physical artefacts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00561
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00561
- Reference count: 4
- Participants: 30

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how text-to-image generative models like Stable Diffusion can support early-stage creative design and ideation for physical artifacts. Through a pilot study where participants selected materials, generated up to three AI images, and created sculptures, the research found that most participants (23/30) found the generated images informative for their final designs, and 28/30 reported interest in using such models for future creative tasks. The study identifies distinct prompting strategies (refiner, rephraser, explorer) and demonstrates that semantic distance traveled in prompts correlates with design stage, suggesting text-to-image models can effectively support creativity when prompting aligns with the user's stage in the design process.

## Method Summary
The study involved 30 participants who selected 3-5 physical materials (e.g., foam, pipe cleaners, wires) and were then introduced to Stable Diffusion for generating up to three images via text prompts. Participants created a physical sculpture based on their materials and AI-generated images. The research team analyzed semantic distance between prompts using sentence embeddings, conducted post-interview surveys, and qualitatively identified prompting styles. The analysis correlated semantic distance with design stages and examined how different prompting strategies affected the creative process.

## Key Results
- 23 out of 30 participants reported that generated images informed their sculptures
- 28 out of 30 participants expressed interest in using text-to-image models for future creative tasks
- Three distinct prompting styles identified: refiner, rephraser, and explorer
- Semantic distance traveled in prompts correlated with design stage (lower exploration for those with clear ideas upfront)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Text-to-image models provide lateral conceptual insights during ideation, expanding the creative space beyond the user's initial expectations.
- Mechanism: The generative model produces images with unexpected visual elements that can introduce novel associations or features not originally considered by the user, thereby broadening the conceptual exploration space.
- Core assumption: The model's latent space contains semantically rich variations that can be sampled to yield meaningful, diverse outputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The majority of participants (23/30) reported that the generated images informed their sculptures, and 28/30 reported interest in using text-to-image models to help them in a creative task in the future."
  - [section] "In post-interviews, the reasons participants reported that images were informative were diverse. Some found that the lateral concepts introduced by the images gave them new conceptual ideas..."
- Break condition: If the generated images consistently fail to relate to the user's conceptual intent or materials, the lateral insight mechanism breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Text-to-image models assist in refining and implementing existing ideas by providing visual references that highlight specific features or details.
- Mechanism: The generated images act as a visual scaffold, allowing users to see concrete representations of abstract concepts, which helps them identify and incorporate specific design elements into their physical artifact.
- Core assumption: Users can effectively map visual features from generated images to physical materials and construction techniques.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Others honed existing ideas by getting implementation inspiration from the images, like in Figure 2 perhaps indicating a later design stage focused on execution."
  - [section] "Some found that the lateral concepts introduced by the images gave them new conceptual ideas... Others honed existing ideas by getting implementation inspiration from the images..."
- Break condition: If the visual features in generated images are too abstract or not aligned with the available materials, users cannot effectively translate them into physical form.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Prompting strategy correlates with the user's design stage, influencing the depth and direction of creative exploration.
- Mechanism: Users at different stages of the design process (e.g., early exploration vs. later refinement) exhibit distinct prompting behaviors, which in turn affect the semantic distance traveled in prompt space and the utility of generated images.
- Core assumption: Semantic distance in prompt space is a valid proxy for conceptual exploration depth and can be measured via text embeddings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We found that the amount of conceptual exploration a participant did was lower for participants who said they had a sculpture idea at the visualization stage than those who did not (t = -2.94, p = 0.006)."
  - [section] "We observed that a remaining 7 participants did not fit neatly into these qualitatively defined prompting categories, often using a mix of styles..."
- Break condition: If the semantic distance metric does not accurately reflect meaningful conceptual exploration, or if users' self-reported design stages are unreliable, the correlation breaks down.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Semantic distance and text embeddings
  - Why needed here: The paper uses average cosine distance between prompt embeddings to quantify conceptual exploration.
  - Quick check question: How is semantic distance computed between two text prompts in this study?

- Concept: Design process stages and their relation to creativity
  - Why needed here: The study maps prompting styles to Hwang's taxonomy of creative stages (wandering, hands-on, camera-ready).
  - Quick check question: According to Hwang's taxonomy, what distinguishes the "wandering" stage from the "hands-on" stage?

- Concept: Prompt engineering strategies
  - Why needed here: The paper identifies and categorizes different prompting behaviors (refiner, rephraser, explorer) and their effects on ideation.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between a "refiner" and an "explorer" prompting style?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: User input (text prompts) → Text embedding model (e.g., sentence-transformers) → Semantic distance calculation → Image generation (Stable Diffusion) → User feedback loop → Physical artifact creation
- Critical path: Prompt → Embedding → Distance → Image generation → User interpretation → Physical construction
- Design tradeoffs: Balancing exploration vs. exploitation in prompting; aligning digital image affordances with physical material constraints; managing user expectations vs. model output diversity
- Failure signatures: User frustration due to mismatch between expected and generated images; low semantic distance traveled but high user satisfaction (or vice versa); inability to translate visual features into physical form
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Measure semantic distance traveled with varying numbers of prompts (1, 2, 3) to see if exploration increases with more iterations.
  2. Compare semantic distance and user satisfaction between users with and without pre-existing sculpture ideas.
  3. Test different embedding models (e.g., CLIP vs. sentence-transformers) to see if semantic distance measurement changes meaningfully.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does a participant's semantic distance traveled in prompting correlate with the quality or originality of their final sculpture?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses semantic distance as a measure of conceptual exploration, but does not assess the creative quality or originality of final sculptures.
- Why unresolved: The study did not evaluate the aesthetic or creative merit of the physical sculptures, focusing instead on participant perceptions of informativeness and prompting behavior.
- What evidence would resolve it: A follow-up study that rates sculptures on originality/creativity by independent judges, then correlates those ratings with participants' semantic distance metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would allowing participants to choose materials after the AI visualization phase lead to different prompting behaviors or design outcomes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note this as a potential future work direction, as participants chose materials before generating images, which may have constrained their prompting.
- Why unresolved: The study fixed the material selection order, so the effect of reversing it remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing two groups—one selecting materials before AI generation, one after—measuring differences in prompting style and sculpture design.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does user familiarity with text-to-image tools influence their prompting strategy and satisfaction with the generated outputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention anecdotally that some users were distracted by the technology itself and are unsure how familiarity may have affected prompting journeys.
- Why unresolved: The study did not screen for or measure participants' prior experience with AI image generation tools.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study that controls for prior exposure to text-to-image models and measures changes in prompting strategy, image satisfaction, and perceived usefulness across familiarity levels.

## Limitations

- Findings based on single-session pilot study with 30 participants, limiting generalizability
- Correlation between semantic distance and design stage observed but not experimentally manipulated
- Translation between digital image features and physical materials remains largely anecdotal with no systematic analysis

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The majority finding that 23/30 participants found images informative and 28/30 reported interest in future use. These are direct survey results with clear quantitative support.
- **Medium Confidence**: The identification of three prompting styles (refiner, rephraser, explorer) and their correlation with design stages. While qualitatively observed, the seven participants who didn't fit neatly into these categories suggest the framework may be incomplete.
- **Low Confidence**: The semantic distance metric as a proxy for conceptual exploration depth. The study assumes cosine distance between sentence embeddings meaningfully captures creative exploration, but this relationship is not empirically validated against independent measures of creativity or design quality.

## Next Checks

1. **Replication with controlled design stage manipulation**: Recruit participants with explicitly controlled design stages (e.g., those with pre-existing ideas vs. those without) and measure semantic distance and prompting patterns to test causality.
2. **Material-to-image translation analysis**: Conduct a systematic study mapping specific visual features from generated images to physical construction techniques, quantifying which image elements participants successfully translate.
3. **Embedding model comparison**: Compare semantic distance measurements using different text embedding models (e.g., sentence-transformers vs. CLIP) to assess whether the choice of embedding significantly affects the observed correlations.
