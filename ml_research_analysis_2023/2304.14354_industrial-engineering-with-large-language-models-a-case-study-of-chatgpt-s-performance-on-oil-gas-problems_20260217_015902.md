# Industrial Engineering with Large Language Models: A case study of ChatGPT's performance on Oil & Gas problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14354
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14354
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: ChatGPT generates mathematically sound symbolic solutions but struggles with extrapolation to novel scenarios and computational accuracy for complex PDEs in oil and gas engineering.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates ChatGPT's ability to solve mathematical problems in oil and gas engineering, focusing on tasks like geological modeling and fluid flow in porous media. The authors assess ChatGPT's performance using Spontaneous Quality (SQ) and Reference Test (RT) scores, finding that while the model can generate mathematically sound solutions, it struggles with extrapolation to novel scenarios and computational accuracy for complex partial differential equations. For example, when solving Burger's equation, ChatGPT's results deviate significantly from exact solutions. The paper highlights limitations in handling geometric boundary conditions and suggests that domain-specific software could enhance LLM outputs by incorporating specialized knowledge.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ChatGPT's performance on mathematical problems from oil and gas engineering using Chain-of-Thought reasoning to decompose problems. Solutions are evaluated using Spontaneous Quality (SQ) and Reference Test (RT) scores, comparing LLM-generated solutions against exact solutions where available. The evaluation focuses on problems like Burger's equation, continuity and momentum equations, and various boundary value problems in porous media flow.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT reliably generates mathematically correct symbolic formulations for standard PDEs
- The model fails to adapt boundary conditions when problem geometry changes, leading to incorrect extrapolations
- Numerical solutions show significant deviation from exact solutions due to series truncation errors, particularly for larger time values

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs struggle with extrapolation to novel physical scenarios despite strong performance on canonical problems.
- Mechanism: ChatGPT reliably generates mathematically correct formulations for standard equations (e.g., continuity, momentum, Burger's equation) but fails to adapt boundary conditions or geometry-aware reasoning when problem context shifts slightly.
- Core assumption: Standard Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning yields correct intermediate steps but lacks the symbolic generalization to re-map solutions under modified assumptions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "it struggles with extrapolation to novel scenarios and computational accuracy for complex partial differential equations"
  - [section] "it maintains the same interpretation of the boundary conditions which doesn't reflect the change in the geometry as follows: u(0, y) = u(L, y) = 0"
- Break condition: When the domain-specific geometric transformation cannot be captured by a simple bounding-box substitution, the LLM output remains a trivial extrapolation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Chain-of-Thought decomposition yields correct symbolic steps but computational errors compound in non-trivial numerical evaluation.
- Mechanism: CoT allows step-by-step symbolic manipulation, but floating-point or summation truncation errors in infinite series (e.g., Fourier solutions for Burger's equation) produce inaccurate numeric results.
- Core assumption: LLMs treat infinite series as truncated approximations without adaptive error control, leading to divergence from exact solutions at later time points.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "For u(0.25, t) where t = 0.6, 0.8, 1.0, and 3.0 we have the following results calculated by ChatGPT and paired by the expected exact solution: u(0.25, 0.6) = 0.006006 | Exact Solution : 0.00189"
- Break condition: When series truncation error exceeds acceptable bounds, especially for larger t, the numeric output becomes unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hybrid LLM + domain-specific software can recover accuracy in specialized knowledge gaps.
- Mechanism: Offloading domain-specific knowledge (e.g., geometric boundary condition logic) to an external solver while letting the LLM generate high-level formulation preserves correctness.
- Core assumption: Domain software can validate and correct LLM-generated intermediate forms before final output.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "suggests that domain-specific software could enhance LLM outputs by incorporating specialized knowledge"
- Break condition: If the domain software cannot represent the specialized knowledge compactly, the hybrid approach fails to improve accuracy.
