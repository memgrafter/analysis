# Ontology-based Solution for Building an Intelligent Searching System on Traffic Law Documents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11252
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11252
- Reference count: 11
- 75% correct rate in responding to typical queries

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes an ontology-based method to represent Vietnam road traffic law knowledge using an improved Rela-model called Traffic-Law model. The system organizes legal knowledge into concepts, relations, and inference rules to enable intelligent searching of traffic law documents. Testing shows the system achieves 75% overall accuracy in responding to queries, with 78% accuracy for concept/definition queries and 73% for penalty/fine queries.

## Method Summary
The approach uses the Traffic-Law model (an improved Rela-model ontology) to represent legal knowledge from Vietnam road traffic law documents. The system extracts keywords from user queries, classifies query types, expands keywords with synonyms from legal sources, and applies inference rules to match concepts and retrieve relevant penalties or definitions. The knowledge base is built from official legal documents including the Law on Road Traffic (2008) and Decree 100/2019 on Administrative Penalties.

## Key Results
- 75% correct rate in responding to typical queries
- 78% accuracy for concept and definition queries
- 73% accuracy for penalty and fine queries

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The ontology-based model enables semantic matching between user queries and legal document concepts.
- Mechanism: The Traffic-Law model represents legal knowledge using structured concepts (C), relations (R), and inference rules (Rules). When a user query is input, the system classifies the query type, extracts keywords, and uses inference rules to find related concepts and relations. This semantic matching allows the system to retrieve relevant legal content and penalties even when exact keyword matches are not present.
- Core assumption: Legal concepts and relations can be adequately represented in a formal ontology structure that supports inference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This knowledge model is built by the improvement of ontology Rela-model. In addition, several searching problems on traffic law are proposed and solved based on the legal knowledge base."
  - [section] "Definition 2.1: The knowledge model for representing the legal domain of road traffic is improved from ontology Rela-model, named Traffic-Law model. This model consists of three components as follows: (C, R, Rules)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on general legal ontologies but lack specific validation of semantic matching effectiveness in traffic law contexts.
- Break condition: If the formal representation cannot capture the nuance and context-dependent meaning of legal concepts, or if inference rules are incomplete or incorrect.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The system achieves accurate penalty determination through structured rule-based inference.
- Mechanism: The Rule_offence set contains inference rules that map combinations of concepts and relations to specific offenses and their associated penalties. When the system identifies relevant concepts from a query (e.g., "not wearing helmet" + "motorcycle operator"), it applies these rules to determine the applicable offense and retrieves the penalty information from the legal document structure.
- Core assumption: Traffic law violations follow consistent logical patterns that can be captured in formal inference rules.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The intelligent search system built on this knowledge base can help users find concepts, definitions, and penalties related to traffic violations."
  - [section] "Set Rules is a set of inference rules. Those rules deduce relations between concepts or determine offences based on road traffic law. Each rule r ∈ Rules has the form u(r) → v(r) where, u(r) is the hypothesis facts of rule r and v(r) is the result of rule r."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - several related papers (e.g., FÆRDXEL, DISC-LawLLM) demonstrate rule-based reasoning for legal domains, though primarily in different jurisdictions.
- Break condition: If legal penalties depend on contextual factors not captured in the ontology, or if the rule structure cannot handle complex multi-factor violations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Query classification and keyword expansion improves retrieval accuracy for both concept definitions and penalty information.
- Mechanism: The system first classifies queries into types (concept/definition vs. penalties/fines), then extracts main keywords and finds similar words from legal sources, experts, or dictionaries. This expanded keyword set improves the matching against the knowledge base, allowing the system to find relevant concepts even when users use different terminology.
- Core assumption: Users may phrase queries differently but with similar semantic intent, and these variations can be captured through synonym expansion.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "For solving Problem 1, the inputted query needs to be classified. The input can be classified into two kinds: query about meaning of a concept ('what is?') and query about the penalties & fines of an offence ('how much', 'penalty', 'fines'). After that, from the kind of the query, its main keywords are extracted. In addition, some similar words for extracted keywords are also achieved."
  - [section] "The similar keywords can be collected from legal document sources, experts (as lawyers or legal lecturers), or from dictionaries."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - limited corpus support for keyword expansion techniques specific to Vietnamese legal terminology.
- Break condition: If the synonym expansion mechanism cannot capture domain-specific terminology or if legal concepts have context-dependent meanings that simple keyword matching cannot resolve.

## Limitations

- The 75% accuracy claim relies on a small test set of 160 queries with no information about query diversity or validation methodology.
- The paper provides minimal detail on how the Traffic-Law model was constructed from source legal documents, making it difficult to assess the completeness and correctness of the knowledge base.
- The evaluation focuses only on common query types, potentially overlooking edge cases or complex multi-factor violations that would challenge the system's inference capabilities.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The general approach of using ontology-based knowledge representation for legal document search is well-established in related work (DISC-LawLLM, FÆRDXEL).
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific Traffic-Law model improvements over Rela-model and the 75% accuracy metrics, given limited methodological detail and small test set.
- **Low Confidence**: The system's ability to handle complex, context-dependent legal scenarios not covered in the test queries or described in the methodology.
