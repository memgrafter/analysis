# Knowledge Relation Rank Enhanced Heterogeneous Learning Interaction Modeling for Neural Graph Forgetting Knowledge Tracing

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces NGFKT, a novel knowledge tracing model designed to mitigate the impact of subjective labeling through calibration of skill relation matrices and Q-matrices. The model leverages Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to model heterogeneous interactions between students, exercises, and skills. Experimental results on two public educational datasets demonstrate superior performance compared to baseline models across multiple metrics including AUC, accuracy, and performance stability.

## Method Summary
The NGFKT model addresses limitations in traditional knowledge tracing by incorporating graph-based learning to capture complex relationships between students, exercises, and skills. It employs GCNs to model heterogeneous interactions while simultaneously calibrating the Q-matrix and skill relation matrix to reduce bias from subjective labeling. The model is trained to predict student performance on exercises while maintaining stable performance across different data distributions, with particular attention to capturing knowledge state dynamics including forgetting patterns.

## Key Results
- NGFKT outperforms all baseline models in terms of AUC, ACC, and Performance Stability (PS)
- Achieves better results even with limited training records
- Correctly captures student knowledge state compared to two standard knowledge tracing models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The model's effectiveness stems from its ability to model heterogeneous learning interactions through GCNs, which capture complex dependencies between students, exercises, and skills. By calibrating the Q-matrix and skill relation matrix, the model reduces bias introduced by subjective labeling. The GCN architecture allows for information propagation across different node types (students, exercises, skills), enabling the model to learn richer representations of knowledge states and their evolution over time.

**Design Tradeoffs:**

- Poor calibration leading to biased skill representations

## Limitations
- Reliance on manually constructed Q-matrices may introduce biases
- Modest absolute performance improvements (0.5-1.5% AUC gains) may limit practical significance
- Experimental validation limited to only two public datasets
