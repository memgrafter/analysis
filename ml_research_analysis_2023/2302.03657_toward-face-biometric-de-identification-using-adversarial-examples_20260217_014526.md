# Toward Face Biometric De-identification using Adversarial Examples

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03657
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03657
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: Adversarial examples for face de-identification show that achieving high protection while maintaining imperceptible perturbations is difficult, especially under compression or resizing.

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates adversarial examples for face biometric de-identification using black-box settings with three face recognition models: FaceNet, ResNet-50, and SENet-50. The authors test BIM (untargeted) and ILLC (targeted) methods, finding that BIM generally outperforms ILLC in transferability, contrary to typical expectations. The study reveals that high protection success rates require larger perturbation budgets that degrade image quality, and that transferability depends heavily on architectural similarity between source and target models. The results suggest that simple adversarial methods struggle to balance protection and image fidelity.

## Method Summary
The study uses the VGGFace2 dataset with three pre-trained face recognition models (FaceNet, ResNet-50, SENet-50) in a black-box setting. Adversarial examples are generated using BIM (untargeted) and ILLC (targeted) methods with perturbation budgets {4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128} and niter = min(ϵ+4, 1.25×ϵ). The Protection Success Rate (PSR) is measured as 100 - (100/N ΣN i=1 1(C(Advϵ(xi)) = ytrue)) across Top-1, 5, 10, 25, and 50 accuracies. The evaluation includes testing transferability across models and analyzing effects of compression and resizing preprocessing.

## Key Results
- BIM method outperforms ILLC in transferability and protection success rate
- High protection success rates require perturbation budgets above ϵ = 32, degrading image quality
- Transferability is highly affected by architectural similarity between source and target models
- Compression and resizing significantly reduce the effectiveness of adversarial perturbations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BIM method adversarial perturbations transfer better than ILLC across different face recognition models
- Mechanism: BIM maximizes loss with respect to true class in untargeted fashion, creating generic perturbations less specific to particular feature embeddings. ILLC targets least likely class, making perturbations more tailored to source model's feature space and less transferable.
- Core assumption: Feature space similarity between source and target models is insufficient for targeted perturbations to transfer effectively
- Evidence anchors: [section] "it is not likely to obtain a high protection success rate together with quite imperceptible adversarial perturbation", [section] "Comparing Figure 3 with Figures 4, 5, it can be seen that examples crafted by FaceNet using BIM method at ϵ = 32 reported high transferability"
- Break condition: If source and target models share highly similar architectures or training datasets, targeted perturbations might transfer better

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High protection success rate requires larger perturbation budgets, degrading image quality
- Mechanism: Increasing perturbation budget (ϵ) allows adversarial example to deviate further from original image in feature space, making it harder for target model to classify correctly. Larger perturbations are more visible to human eye, degrading perceptual quality.
- Core assumption: Trade-off between protection success rate and image quality is fundamental constraint of adversarial de-identification methods
- Evidence anchors: [section] "when the perturbation budget increases (i.e. as the quality of the image is decreasing due to adding more noise), the protection success rate increases as well", [section] "These results show that using BIM and ILLC adversarial methods to preserve privacy for face images can only be achievable with ϵ > 32 at the cost of degrading the quality of the image"
- Break condition: If novel adversarial method can create highly effective perturbations with minimal impact on image quality

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transferability of adversarial examples is highly affected by similarity of training parameters between source and target networks
- Mechanism: Adversarial examples are generated based on specific feature embeddings learned by source model during training. Different training parameters (architecture, dataset, loss function) result in different feature embeddings in target model, making adversarial examples less effective.
- Core assumption: Effectiveness of adversarial examples is closely tied to feature space of model that generated them
- Evidence anchors: [section] "Finally, we found out that the transferability of adversarial examples is highly affected by the training parameters of the network with which they are generated", [section] "Therefore, the transferability of generated adversarial examples conforms with the similarity of the attacker network to that of the defender in terms of training parameters"
- Break condition: If adversarial examples can be generated that are agnostic to specific feature space of source model

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does effectiveness of untargeted adversarial methods like BIM significantly outperform targeted methods like ILLC across diverse face recognition architectures and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors observed BIM (untargeted) outperformed ILLC (targeted) in transferability and protection success rate, contrary to expectations
- Why unresolved: Paper only tested two specific methods on three face recognition models trained on VGGFace2; trend may not hold for other adversarial methods, architectures, or datasets
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of multiple untargeted and targeted adversarial methods across varied face recognition models, datasets, and perturbation budgets

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is minimum perturbation budget required to achieve high protection success rates while maintaining acceptable image quality, and how does this threshold vary with different preprocessing operations like compression and resizing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors found high PSR requires larger perturbation budgets (ϵ > 32) which degrades image quality; compression and resizing significantly affect transferability
- Why unresolved: Study didn't systematically explore tradeoff between PSR, image quality, and preprocessing robustness; impact of other preprocessing operations unknown
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical analysis of PSR and image quality across wide range of perturbation budgets and preprocessing operations, including subjective and objective quality metrics

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do training parameters and architectural differences between source and target face recognition models influence transferability of adversarial examples, and can this be predicted or mitigated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors concluded transferability is highly affected by similarity of training parameters between source and target networks; FaceNet examples transferred better than ResNet-50 or SENet-50
- Why unresolved: Paper didn't investigate specific training parameters or architectural features that drive transferability; methods to predict or improve transferability unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of how specific training parameters and architectural choices affect transferability, along with techniques to predict or enhance transferability across diverse models

## Limitations
- Study only evaluates two adversarial attack methods (BIM and ILLC), leaving open questions about whether other attack strategies might perform better
- Evaluation uses only three face recognition models, which may not capture full diversity of real-world systems
- Paper doesn't explore effects of different face datasets or training conditions on transferability
- Black-box setting assumes attacker has no knowledge of target model, but real-world scenarios often involve partial information leakage
- Paper doesn't investigate defense mechanisms that could be deployed by recognition systems to counter these attacks

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core finding that higher protection success rates require larger perturbation budgets, leading to degraded image quality. This tradeoff is well-established in adversarial machine learning literature and the paper provides clear empirical evidence through systematic evaluation across multiple models and perturbation levels.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that BIM outperforms ILLC for transferability in this context. While experimental results support this, the counterintuitive nature (given that targeted attacks typically show better transferability) suggests this might be specific to particular models and settings tested.

**Medium Confidence**: The observation that transferability depends heavily on architectural similarity between source and target models. The paper provides evidence for this, but analysis could be strengthened by including more diverse model architectures and examining specific architectural features that affect transferability.
