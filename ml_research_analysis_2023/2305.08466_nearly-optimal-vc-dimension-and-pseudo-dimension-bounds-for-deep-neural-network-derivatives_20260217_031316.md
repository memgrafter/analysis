# Nearly Optimal VC-Dimension and Pseudo-Dimension Bounds for Deep Neural Network Derivatives

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08466
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08466
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Nearly optimal bounds for VC-dimension and pseudo-dimension of DNN derivatives, leading to optimal Sobolev approximation rates and tight generalization error bounds

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes nearly optimal bounds for the VC-dimension and pseudo-dimension of derivatives of deep neural networks (DNNs). The key insight is a novel approach to estimate the complexity of DNN derivatives by investigating the relationships between multiplied terms in the chain rule. These bounds are then used to prove the optimality of DNN approximation in Sobolev spaces and to characterize the generalization error of loss functions involving derivatives. The results demonstrate that the generalization error of loss functions with derivatives is equivalent to those without derivatives, corresponding to the width and depth of DNNs.

## Method Summary
The paper employs a novel approach to estimate the complexity of DNN derivatives by investigating relationships between multiplied terms in the chain rule. The method involves constructing a partition of the parameter space layer-by-layer, showing that each layer contributes at most a certain number of distinct sign patterns. This approach leads to a nearly optimal bound on the VC-dimension of DNN derivatives, which is then used to obtain bounds on the pseudo-dimension and generalization error.

## Key Results
- Nearly optimal VC-dimension bound: O(N²L²log²Llog²N) for DNN derivatives
- Nearly optimal pseudo-dimension bound of the same order
- Optimal approximation rate: O(N^{-2(n-1)/d}L^{-2(n-1)/d}) for functions in Sobolev spaces
- Tight generalization error bound: O(NL(log²Nlog²L)^{1/2}/√M) for loss functions defined by Sobolev norms

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The chain rule structure of DNN derivatives can be decomposed into independent polynomial terms whose VC-dimension is bounded using combinatorial arguments.
- **Mechanism**: The proof constructs a partition of the parameter space layer-by-layer, showing that each layer contributes at most 2(2emnN_i∑_{i=1}^n W_i)^{∑_{i=1}^n W_i} distinct sign patterns. By applying Lemma 1 (growth function bound for polynomials), the VC-dimension is bounded by O(N²L²log²Llog²N).
- **Core assumption**: The sign patterns of DNN derivatives depend only on the sign of intermediate linear combinations, and these can be partitioned independently layer-by-layer.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "a novel approach to estimate the complexity of DNN derivatives by investigating the relationships between multiplied terms in the chain rule"; [section] "The proof is inspired by [22, Theorem 7]... we obtain the desired partition S_n... the required property 2 is satisfied"
- **Break condition**: If the chain rule introduces correlations between layers that cannot be decomposed, the partition argument fails and the bound no longer holds.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The pseudo-dimension of DNN derivatives equals the VC-dimension up to constant factors, enabling tight generalization bounds for Sobolev loss functions.
- **Mechanism**: The paper uses the relationship Pdim(DΦ) ≤ VCdim(DΦ_N) where DΦ_N is the augmented function class including output values. Combined with the optimal VC-dimension bound, this yields the same order pseudo-dimension bound.
- **Core assumption**: The pseudo-dimension of a function class containing both network outputs and derivatives can be bounded by the VC-dimension of the derivative-only class.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "we utilize our previous work on estimating the VC-dimension of DNN derivatives to obtain an upper bound on the pseudo-dimension of DNN derivatives"; [section] "Based on the definition of VC-dimension and pseudo-dimension, we have that Pdim(DΦ) ≤ VCdim(DΦ_N)"
- **Break condition**: If the relationship between pseudo-dimension and VC-dimension for augmented function classes requires additional constraints not satisfied here, the bound would be invalid.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Sobolev approximation rates for DNNs are optimal because any faster rate would violate the VC-dimension lower bound.
- **Mechanism**: The proof uses a contradiction argument: if approximation rate were faster than O(N^{-2(n-1)/d}L^{-2(n-1)/d}), then the VC-dimension of DNN derivatives would need to be smaller than the established lower bound, contradicting Theorem 1.
- **Core assumption**: Approximation capability is fundamentally limited by the complexity measure (VC-dimension) of the function class.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "we demonstrate the optimality of our DNN approximation, as measured by Sobolev norms"; [section] "Theorem 1 plays a crucial role in our proof of Theorem 4, which is established through a proof by contradiction"
- **Break condition**: If approximation rates depend on factors other than VC-dimension (e.g., specific network architecture properties), the contradiction argument fails.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: Is the estimation of VC-dimension and pseudo-dimension of higher-order derivatives of σ2-NNs optimal?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states that "The optimality of these results and the generalization error of Sobolev training with loss functions containing higher-order derivatives of DNNs remain open problems, as estimating the VC-dimension and pseudo-dimension of higher-order derivatives of σ2-NNs requires further investigation."
  - **Why unresolved**: The paper only provides bounds for the VC-dimension and pseudo-dimension of first-order derivatives of DNNs, but not for higher-order derivatives.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: A proof showing that the obtained bounds for higher-order derivatives are tight or a counterexample demonstrating that they can be improved.

- **Open Question 2**: Can the approximation rate of DNNs in Sobolev spaces be further improved?
  - **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper states that "While recent works have studied the approximation of smooth functions or functions in Sobolev spaces by DNNs measured in the norm of Lp(Ω) or Ws,p(Ω), they typically present results that are not optimal or are measured in Lp-norms."
  - **Why unresolved**: The paper provides an optimal approximation rate for DNNs in Sobolev spaces measured by Sobolev norms, but it is unclear if this rate can be further improved.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: A proof showing that the obtained approximation rate is indeed optimal or a counterexample demonstrating that it can be improved.

- **Open Question 3**: How does the generalization error of Sobolev training with loss functions containing higher-order derivatives of DNNs compare to that without derivatives?
  - **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper shows that the generalization error of loss functions containing first-order derivatives of DNNs is equivalent to that without derivatives, but it does not address higher-order derivatives.
  - **Why unresolved**: The paper only provides bounds for the generalization error of loss functions containing first-order derivatives of DNNs, but not for higher-order derivatives.
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: A proof showing that the generalization error of loss functions containing higher-order derivatives of DNNs is also equivalent to that without derivatives or a counterexample demonstrating that it is different.

## Limitations

- The complexity of the layer-by-layer partition argument relies on specific assumptions about the independence of sign patterns across layers that may not hold for all network architectures.
- The constants in the bounds are not explicitly specified, making it difficult to assess the tightness of the results in practice.
- The paper focuses on ReLU activation functions for VC-dimension analysis, which may limit the generalizability of the results to other activation functions.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The pseudo-dimension bound follows directly from the VC-dimension bound using established relationships.
- **Medium Confidence**: The approximation rate in Sobolev spaces is well-grounded in the VC-dimension bounds but depends on specific construction of DNNs.
- **Medium Confidence**: The generalization error bound for Sobolev loss functions follows standard Rademacher complexity arguments, but the tightness claim depends critically on the VC-dimension bound.
