# FedLP: Layer-wise Pruning Mechanism for Communication-Computation Efficient Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06360
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06360
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: FedLP reduces communication costs by up to 30% and computation complexity while maintaining comparable accuracy in federated learning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FedLP, a novel layer-wise pruning framework for federated learning (FL) that optimizes communication and computation efficiency. The key idea is to prune entire layers rather than individual neurons, enabling more effective model compression while preserving model-agnostic properties. FedLP supports both homogeneous and heterogeneous scenarios, with probabilistic layer-preserving rates controlling the trade-off between efficiency and accuracy. Theoretical analysis shows FedLP achieves (1-p)^K convergence rate decay compared to non-pruned FL, where p is the layer-preserving rate and K is the number of participating clients.

## Method Summary
FedLP implements layer-wise pruning in federated learning by treating entire layers as the smallest pruning unit. The framework operates through two schemes: FedLP-Homo where all clients preserve the same layers probabilistically, and FedLP-Hetero where clients preserve different layer sequences based on device capabilities. During each training round, clients perform local training, prune layers according to their layer-preserving rate (LPR), and upload only the preserved layers to the parameter server. The server aggregates these preserved layers and updates the global model, which is then distributed to all clients. This approach reduces both communication costs (fewer parameters transmitted) and computation complexity while maintaining model accuracy through probabilistic layer preservation.

## Key Results
- FedLP reduces communication costs by up to 30% compared to FedAvg while maintaining comparable accuracy
- FedLP-Homo(0.5) achieves similar accuracy to FedAvg with 25% reduction in communication costs
- The framework demonstrates effectiveness across different data heterogeneity levels (iid, mixed non-iid, Dirichlet non-iid)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Layer-wise pruning reduces communication costs by eliminating entire layers rather than individual neurons.
- Mechanism: By pruning entire layers, FedLP transmits only the preserved layers' parameters, reducing the total number of parameters sent per client.
- Core assumption: The model's performance is not critically dependent on the pruned layers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "FedLP reduces communication costs by up to 30% and computation complexity while maintaining comparable accuracy"
  - [section] "By adopting layer-wise pruning in local training and federated updating, we formulate an explicit FL pruning framework"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related works focus on neuron-level pruning or dropout, not layer-wise.
- Break condition: If critical layers for model performance are pruned, accuracy will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Layer-wise pruning enables model-agnostic compression applicable to different neural network architectures.
- Mechanism: Treating entire layers as the smallest pruning unit allows FedLP to be applied without considering the specific type of layer (e.g., FC or Conv).
- Core assumption: The structure of the neural network allows for layer-wise pruning without disrupting the flow of information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "This is because the whole layer is treated as the smallest pruning unit and the inner-layer structure can be neglected"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related works do not discuss model-agnostic layer-wise pruning.
- Break condition: If the network architecture requires specific layer configurations, layer-wise pruning may not be feasible.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Probabilistic layer-preserving rates control the trade-off between efficiency and accuracy.
- Mechanism: By adjusting the probability that each layer is preserved (LPR), FedLP can balance the reduction in communication/computation costs with the potential loss in model performance.
- Core assumption: The impact of pruning on model performance can be predicted and controlled through LPR.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "probabilistic layer-preserving rates controlling the trade-off between efficiency and accuracy"
  - [section] "we formulate a probabilistic rule for homogeneity FedLP, where layer l of client k contributes to aggregation with probability pl_k"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related works do not discuss probabilistic layer-preserving rates.
- Break condition: If the relationship between LPR and model performance is non-linear or unpredictable, controlling the trade-off becomes difficult.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Clients -> Local Training -> Layer Pruning -> Parameter Upload -> Parameter Server -> Layer Aggregation -> Global Model Update -> Model Distribution -> Clients
- Critical path:
  1. Clients train local models
  2. Clients prune layers based on LPR
  3. Clients upload pruned models
  4. Parameter server aggregates preserved layers
  5. Parameter server sends updated global model to clients
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Higher LPR: Better accuracy, higher communication costs
  - Lower LPR: Lower accuracy, lower communication costs
  - Homogeneous vs. Heterogeneous: Simpler aggregation vs. adaptive to device capabilities
- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy degradation: Too many critical layers pruned
  - Convergence issues: Inconsistent pruning across clients
  - Communication bottlenecks: Insufficient bandwidth for even pruned models
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline: FedAvg without pruning
  2. FedLP-Homo with varying LPRs (e.g., 0.3, 0.5, 0.7)
  3. FedLP-Hetero with different layer counts assigned to clients

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does layer-wise pruning impact model convergence rates under varying participation rates and data heterogeneity levels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Theoretical analysis shows (1-p)^K convergence rate decay, but experiments suggest more complex interactions with data heterogeneity
- Why unresolved: Current theoretical results assume fairness case (ωk ≡ 1/K) and homogeneous LPRs, but real-world scenarios involve heterogeneous participation rates and data distributions
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive theoretical analysis covering heterogeneous participation rates and varying data distributions, validated through extensive experimental comparisons

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the security implications of layer-wise pruning for protecting against model inversion and membership inference attacks in federated learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors mention layer-wise pruning "potentially prevents model attacks in some degrees" and "will protect the FL model from attacks"
- Why unresolved: The paper only briefly mentions security benefits without quantifying the protection level or analyzing specific attack scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical evaluation of layer-wise pruning against various attack vectors, comparison with existing defense mechanisms, and quantification of privacy-utility tradeoffs

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can dynamic layer-preserving rates be optimized based on client device capabilities and network conditions in heterogeneous federated learning scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The current heterogeneous scheme uses fixed layer-sequences based on device capabilities, but real-world scenarios require dynamic adaptation
- Why unresolved: Current implementation uses static pruning assignments without considering temporal variations in device capabilities or network conditions
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of adaptive algorithms that dynamically adjust layer-preserving rates based on real-time device/network conditions, with comparative performance analysis against static approaches

## Limitations
- The paper claims a (1-p)^K convergence rate decay, but the theoretical analysis is not detailed enough to fully verify this claim.
- The experimental results are based on a relatively simple CNN model on CIFAR-10 dataset. The effectiveness of FedLP on more complex models and datasets is not explored.
- The paper does not discuss the impact of layer-wise pruning on model robustness and generalization.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the mechanism of layer-wise pruning reducing communication costs, supported by the experimental results showing up to 30% reduction in communication costs.
- Medium confidence in the model-agnostic property of FedLP, as the paper claims but does not provide extensive evidence across different neural network architectures.
- Low confidence in the convergence rate analysis, as the theoretical proof is not detailed enough to fully verify the claim.
