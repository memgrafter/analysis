# Interdisciplinary Methods in Computational Creativity: How Human Variables Shape Human-Inspired AI Research

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17070
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17070
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: Qualitative study of 22 interviews reveals limited deep engagement with human psychology literature among computational creativity researchers, who often rely on serendipitous encounters or popular culture rather than systematic literature review.

## Executive Summary
This qualitative study examines how human-inspired AI researchers, particularly those focused on creativity, engage with human psychology literature when developing computational systems. Through 22 in-depth interviews, the authors discover that researchers frequently encounter psychological concepts through chance or popular culture rather than systematic academic engagement, leading to potential disconnection between computational applications and their original theoretical contexts. The study reveals a gap between how researchers perceive their engagement with source disciplines and what the interview data suggests about actual practices. The findings point to a need for greater reflexivity and documentation in computational creativity research.

## Method Summary
The study employs qualitative methods through 22 in-depth interviews with human-inspired AI researchers, half of whom specialize in creativity research. Researchers were selected from North American and European institutions, and interviews explored how participants engage with human psychology literature and translate concepts for computational systems. The qualitative analysis focused on identifying patterns in how researchers encounter, interpret, and apply human concepts in their AI work, with particular attention to the sources of their theoretical foundations and the depth of their engagement with original literature.

## Key Results
- Researchers predominantly encounter psychological concepts through serendipitous encounters or popular culture rather than systematic literature review
- Computational applications often become disconnected from their original theoretical contexts in human psychology
- Self-reported research practices may not accurately reflect actual engagement patterns with source disciplines
- Geographic focus on North American and European researchers may introduce cultural biases in concept translation and application

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper highlights the need for greater documentation of decision-making processes when formalizing human concepts for machines, and calls for reflexive commentary on human elements in computational creativity work. It suggests that without systematic engagement with source disciplines, key ideas risk becoming disconnected from their original contexts, potentially limiting the field's theoretical grounding and cross-disciplinary dialogue.

## Limitations
- Small sample size of 22 interviews may not represent the full diversity of computational creativity approaches
- Geographic focus on North America and Europe may introduce cultural biases in how human concepts are translated
- Reliance on self-reported data about research practices may not accurately reflect actual engagement patterns
- Proposed interdisciplinary dialogue mechanisms lack specific implementation strategies

## Confidence
- Findings on limited engagement with source disciplines: Medium
- Claims about popular culture influence: Medium
- Recommendations for documentation practices: High
- Claims about geographical/cultural biases: Low

## Next Checks
1. Survey a larger, more diverse sample of computational creativity researchers across different geographical regions to test the generalizability of findings about literature engagement patterns.
2. Conduct systematic citation analysis of computational creativity papers to empirically verify claims about the depth of engagement with human psychology literature.
3. Implement and evaluate a pilot program for publishing reflexive commentary alongside computational creativity papers to assess practical feasibility and impact on interdisciplinary dialogue.
