# "What if?" in Probabilistic Logic Programming

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15318
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15318
- Reference count: 16
- Primary result: Extends ProbLog with counterfactual reasoning using Pearl's twin network method, enabling "What if" queries with formal semantics and implementation.

## Executive Summary
This paper extends ProbLog with counterfactual reasoning by adapting Pearl's twin network method from causal models to probabilistic logic programming. The authors provide a formal semantics (FCM-semantics) for counterfactual queries in ProbLog and prove its correctness. They show that their approach is consistent with CP-logic's causal semantics for LPAD-programs and implement it using knowledge compilation techniques (aspmc and PySDD). The method is evaluated on synthetic graph traversal benchmarks, demonstrating that top-down compilation outperforms bottom-up compilation, especially when evidence is present.

## Method Summary
The paper adapts Pearl's twin network method to probabilistic logic programming by transforming a ProbLog program with evidence and intervention into a twin program where evidence is handled in one copy and intervention in another. This transformation reduces counterfactual reasoning to standard marginal inference. The approach uses knowledge compilation (top-down with aspmc and bottom-up with PySDD) for efficient evaluation. The authors prove that their counterfactual semantics is consistent with CP-logic's causal semantics for LPAD programs through translations between the two formalisms.

## Key Results
- Top-down compilation (aspmc) consistently outperforms bottom-up compilation (PySDD) for counterfactual queries
- Interventions can both help and hinder performance depending on their nature (positive vs negative evidence)
- The implementation correctly handles counterfactual queries and scales to graphs with hundreds of nodes
- The approach is consistent with CP-logic's causal semantics for LPAD programs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Counterfactual reasoning in ProbLog is enabled by transforming the original program into a twin-network style program where evidence and interventions are handled separately.
- **Mechanism**: The authors define a twin program PK where one copy (Pe) handles evidence and the other (Pi) handles interventions. The intervention is applied to Pi, and the query is evaluated under the evidence constraint on Pe. This separation preserves the causal semantics while reducing the problem to standard marginal inference.
- **Core assumption**: The FCM-semantics of ProbLog is well-defined (unique supported models) and that the twin network transformation correctly simulates the abduction-action-prediction steps of Pearl's counterfactual calculus.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "we extend this ProbLog language by the ability to answer 'What if' queries" and "we adapt the twin-network method of Balke and Pearl (1994) from causal models to probabilistic logic programming"
  - [section 3] "Procedure 2 (Treatment of Counterfactuals)" and "Theorem 3 (Correctness of our Treatment of Counterfactuals)"
- **Break condition**: If the program does not have unique supported models, the FCM-semantics is not well-defined, and the twin network transformation may not yield correct counterfactual probabilities.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The counterfactual semantics defined via twin networks is consistent with CP-logic's causal semantics for LPAD programs.
- **Mechanism**: The authors show that translating a ProbLog program to an LPAD program (and vice versa) preserves the counterfactual probabilities under both the FCM-semantics and CP-logic's counterfactual reasoning. This is done by proving equivalence of the distributions and the behavior under interventions.
- **Core assumption**: The translations Prob(P) and LPAD(P) (Riguzzi 2020) preserve both the distribution semantics and the counterfactual semantics.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "we also show that our approach is consistent with CP-logic, i.e. with the causal semantics for logic programs with annotated with disjunctions"
  - [section 4] "Theorem 4 (Consistency with CP-Logic - Part 1)" and "Theorem 5 (Consistency with CP-Logic - Part 2)"
- **Break condition**: If the translations do not preserve the counterfactual behavior (e.g., due to differences in how interventions are modeled), the consistency result would fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Efficient evaluation of counterfactual queries is possible using existing knowledge compilation techniques because the twin network transformation results in a program amenable to marginal inference.
- **Mechanism**: After transforming the ProbLog program into the twin network form, the resulting program can be evaluated using standard marginal inference techniques (bottom-up or top-down knowledge compilation). The evaluation is equivalent to computing a conditional probability in the transformed program.
- **Core assumption**: The knowledge compilation tools (aspmc, PySDD) can handle the transformed program efficiently, and the performance characteristics of marginal inference carry over to counterfactual queries.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "we provide a full implementation by making use of the aspmc library (Eiter et al., 2021)" and "we investigate the scalability of the two main approaches used for efficient inference"
  - [section 5] "Knowledge Compilation for Evaluation" and "Implementation"
- **Break condition**: If the twin network transformation introduces significant structural complexity that degrades the performance of knowledge compilation, the approach may not scale as expected.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Functional causal models and Pearl's counterfactual calculus (abduction-action-prediction).
  - **Why needed here**: The paper's counterfactual reasoning is built on Pearl's framework, so understanding how interventions and observations interact in causal models is essential to grasp the twin network method.
  - **Quick check question**: What are the three steps of Pearl's counterfactual calculus, and how does the twin network method avoid explicitly performing abduction?

- **Concept**: Distribution semantics of ProbLog and LPAD programs.
  - **Why needed here**: The paper relies on the equivalence between ProbLog and LPAD programs under the distribution semantics to establish consistency with CP-logic's causal semantics.
  - **Quick check question**: How does the distribution semantics of a ProbLog program define the probability of a query, and how is this related to the underlying logic program's models?

- **Concept**: Knowledge compilation and tractable circuit representations (BDDs, SDDs, DNNFs).
  - **Why needed here**: The scalability evaluation uses knowledge compilation techniques, and understanding how these representations enable efficient marginal inference is key to interpreting the results.
  - **Quick check question**: What is the main advantage of using knowledge compilation for marginal inference, and how do bottom-up and top-down approaches differ in their compilation strategy?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Parser -> Transformer -> Knowledge Compiler -> Evaluator
- **Critical path**: Parser → Transformer → Knowledge Compiler → Evaluator. The translator is used for consistency checking but not for the main counterfactual reasoning pipeline.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Top-down vs. bottom-up compilation: Top-down compilation (aspmc) generally scales better but may have higher memory usage; bottom-up compilation (PySDD) is more mature in ProbLog but can struggle with larger instances.
  - Twin network transformation: Introduces redundancy (duplicate atoms) but enables the use of standard marginal inference tools; the tradeoff is between correctness and efficiency.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Non-unique supported models: The FCM-semantics is not well-defined, and the counterfactual reasoning may produce incorrect results.
  - Memory/time limits during compilation: The transformed program is too complex for the knowledge compilation tool, indicating a scalability issue.
  - Incorrect translations: If the ProbLog to LPAD or vice versa translation is buggy, the consistency with CP-logic will fail.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify the twin network transformation: Take a simple ProbLog program with evidence and intervention, apply the transformation, and check that the resulting program correctly encodes the counterfactual query.
  2. Test knowledge compilation on transformed programs: Use a small benchmark instance and compare the performance of top-down (aspmc) and bottom-up (PySDD) compilation on the transformed program.
  3. Check consistency with CP-logic: Translate a simple LPAD program to ProbLog, apply a counterfactual query using both the FCM-semantics and CP-logic, and verify that the results match.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: What is the precise relationship between top-down and bottom-up knowledge compilation performance for counterfactual queries, and can we predict which approach will perform better based on program characteristics?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper compares bottom-up and top-down compilation performance on counterfactual queries and observes significant differences, but does not provide a predictive framework for when each approach will excel.
- **Why unresolved**: The experimental results show varying performance depending on program structure and evidence/intervention patterns, but the authors do not establish clear guidelines for predicting performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A comprehensive analysis mapping specific program features (like treewidth, number of rules, evidence patterns) to compilation approach performance, along with a decision framework for selecting the optimal approach.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How can we extend counterfactual reasoning to programs with stratified negation that do not have unique supported models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The authors note that while their procedure can be applied to programs with stratified negation, there is no established theory of counterfactual reasoning for such programs.
- **Why unresolved**: The authors explicitly state uncertainty about interpreting results for programs without unique supported models, as Pearl's causality theory does not cover this case.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A formal extension of Pearl's causality theory to handle stratified negation, or a formal proof showing why such an extension is not possible.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: What is the optimal strategy for choosing evidence and intervention atoms to maximize performance in counterfactual inference?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The experiments show that evidence and interventions can significantly affect performance, but the effect depends on the type (positive/negative) and the specific program structure.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper observes performance differences but does not provide a systematic approach for selecting evidence/intervention atoms to optimize inference.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: A study analyzing the impact of different evidence/intervention selection strategies on inference performance across diverse program types, potentially leading to heuristic guidelines.

### Open Question 4
- **Question**: How does the size and complexity of the twin network created during counterfactual reasoning affect overall scalability?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The authors use twin network method for counterfactual reasoning but do not analyze the size/complexity of the resulting networks.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper focuses on program-level performance but does not examine the characteristics of the twin networks themselves.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Analysis of twin network size and complexity as a function of the original program parameters, and investigation of whether twin network characteristics correlate with inference performance.

## Limitations
- Requires programs with unique supported models; fails otherwise
- Performance varies unpredictably based on intervention structure
- Evaluation limited to synthetic graph traversal benchmarks
- Twin network transformation can introduce computational overhead

## Confidence
- **High**: Theoretical correctness of twin network method and consistency with CP-logic (formally proven)
- **Medium**: Implementation correctness and knowledge compilation integration (established libraries but limited evaluation)
- **Low**: Scalability claims (single benchmark domain, complex interventions not well-characterized)

## Next Checks
1. Test programs without unique supported models: Verify that the implementation correctly identifies and handles programs that violate the FCM-semantics uniqueness requirement.
2. Evaluate on diverse benchmark domains: Apply the counterfactual reasoning to benchmarks beyond graph traversal (e.g., medical diagnosis, fault localization) to assess generalizability.
3. Analyze intervention impact: Systematically vary the structure and location of interventions in benchmark instances to characterize their impact on compilation and inference performance.
