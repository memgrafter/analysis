# ClArTTS: An Open-Source Classical Arabic Text-to-Speech Corpus

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00069
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00069
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: 12-hour classical Arabic TTS corpus with 10,334 utterances, achieving MOS scores of 3.63-3.84 with end-to-end models

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ClArTTS, a 12-hour single-speaker classical Arabic TTS dataset built from a LibriVox audiobook. The authors manually transcribed and annotated audio segments, filtering for high quality (SNR > 20 dB) and a minimum segment duration of 2 seconds. The corpus includes 10,334 utterances with manual diacritization. They trained two baseline end-to-end TTS models—Grad-TTS and Glow-TTS—using character inputs and evaluated them with subjective MOS tests and objective metrics (PESQ, MCD, Lf0 RMSE, BAP, speaker similarity). ClArTTS-based models achieved MOS scores of 3.63 (Grad-TTS) and 3.84 (Glow-TTS), outperforming models trained on the smaller ASC corpus and showing better speaker similarity.

## Method Summary
The authors built ClArTTS from a 16-hour LibriVox audiobook, manually transcribing and segmenting the audio while filtering for high quality (SNR > 20 dB) and minimum 2-second duration. The corpus includes 10,334 utterances with manual diacritization. They trained Grad-TTS and Glow-TTS end-to-end models using raw character inputs without explicit phoneme conversion. The models were evaluated using subjective MOS tests and objective metrics including PESQ, MCD, Lf0 RMSE, BAP, and speaker similarity. A HiFi-GAN vocoder was used for waveform generation.

## Key Results
- ClArTTS-based models achieved MOS scores of 3.63 (Grad-TTS) and 3.84 (Glow-TTS)
- Outperformed models trained on the smaller ASC corpus (3.4 hours)
- Better speaker similarity compared to ASC-based models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Using a large, single-speaker, high-quality audiobook corpus improves end-to-end TTS performance compared to smaller, multi-speaker datasets. Large, consistent acoustic conditions reduce variability that neural models must learn to normalize, allowing the model to focus on phonetic and prosodic modeling. Single-speaker consistency yields better speaker similarity scores. Core assumption: Quantity of high-quality, consistent data is more valuable than careful phonetic optimization for neural TTS.

### Mechanism 2
Manual diacritization of Arabic text transcripts improves phonetic clarity and pronunciation accuracy. Diacritical marks resolve ambiguities in Arabic orthography, ensuring the TTS model learns correct phoneme-to-grapheme mappings, reducing mispronunciations. Core assumption: The model can effectively leverage diacritized input if provided; otherwise, pronunciation errors will increase.

### Mechanism 3
End-to-end diffusion-based TTS models with raw character inputs achieve competitive quality without explicit phoneme conversion. End-to-end models learn direct text-to-speech mappings, avoiding errors from rule-based G2P and reducing preprocessing complexity. Core assumption: The model architecture can learn robust mappings from characters to acoustic features without intermediate phonetic representation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text preprocessing → Diacritized character encoder → Acoustic model (Grad-TTS/Glow-TTS) → Mel-spectrogram prediction → Vocoder (HiFi-GAN) → Raw waveform generation → Evaluation pipeline (MOS, PESQ, MCD, Lf0 RMSE, BAP, speaker similarity)
- Critical path: Text → Acoustic model → Vocoder → Waveform
- Design tradeoffs: Larger dataset vs. phonetic optimization (ClArTTS vs. ASC); Raw character input vs. phoneme input (simpler preprocessing vs. potential accuracy gains); Single speaker vs. multi-speaker (better consistency vs. speaker diversity)
- Failure signatures: Low MOS but high PESQ → Intelligibility issues but audio quality OK; High MCD/Lf0 RMSE → Spectral/pitch modeling problems; Low speaker similarity → Speaker identity not preserved
- First 3 experiments: 1) Train Grad-TTS on ClArTTS with default parameters; evaluate MOS and speaker similarity. 2) Train Glow-TTS on ClArTTS with default parameters; compare MOS and PESQ to Grad-TTS. 3) Train Grad-TTS on a subset of ClArTTS (e.g., 6 hours) to test data quantity impact.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does increasing the size of the ClArTTS corpus beyond 12 hours significantly improve the naturalness and speaker similarity scores of end-to-end TTS models? The authors note that neural TTS models benefit from larger datasets but do not test the impact of corpus size scaling. Controlled experiments training models on incrementally larger subsets would identify performance plateaus.

### Open Question 2
How does the performance of ClArTTS-based TTS systems compare when synthesizing Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) versus Classical Arabic (CA) text? The authors state they demonstrate synthesis for both but do not report separate quantitative evaluations. Separate MOS and objective metric evaluations for MSA vs. CA test sets would measure differences.

### Open Question 3
Would incorporating phonetic features as input to TTS models trained on ClArTTS improve intelligibility and naturalness compared to raw character input? The authors mention they did not use a G2P module but do not test phonetic input features. Comparative experiments using both character and phonetic inputs would quantify the impact.

### Open Question 4
Can transfer learning from the ClArTTS corpus improve the performance of TTS models for other Arabic dialects or speakers with limited data? The authors propose future work on transfer learning but do not present experimental results. Experiments fine-tuning ClArTTS-trained models on smaller dialect-specific datasets would assess generalization.

## Limitations
- Single male speaker from one audiobook source limits generalizability to other speakers, genders, and speaking styles
- No comparison of character vs. phonetic input performance despite claims about end-to-end character processing
- Limited evaluation scope without hyperparameter tuning, model convergence analysis, or ablation studies

## Confidence
- Single-speaker large corpus improves TTS quality: Medium confidence (supported by MOS and speaker similarity results but limited by single-speaker scope)
- Manual diacritization improves pronunciation: Low confidence (no direct experimental comparison with/without diacritics)
- End-to-end character input achieves competitive quality: Low confidence (claims made but no comparative performance metrics between character and phonetic inputs)
