# Performance of data-driven inner speech decoding with same-task EEG-fMRI data fusion and bimodal models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10854
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10854
- Reference count: 0
- Bimodal fusion strategies improve decoding performance when underlying data structure exists, with late fusion achieving 26.41% average accuracy versus 17.50% unimodal fMRI baseline

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates combining EEG and fMRI data for inner speech decoding using bimodal fusion approaches. The authors propose concatenation of probability vectors from unimodal models and early fusion with feature engineering as two fusion strategies. Tested on same-task inner speech data from four participants, the results show that bimodal fusion can improve performance when data exhibits underlying structure, with late fusion achieving 26.41% accuracy compared to the unimodal fMRI baseline of 17.50%. However, performance benefits were inconsistent across subjects, with only subjects 3 and 5 showing significant improvements.

## Method Summary
The study employs multimodal neural decoding for inner speech using simultaneous EEG-fMRI recordings from four participants. Two bimodal fusion approaches are implemented: late fusion by concatenating probability vectors from unimodal models, and early fusion with feature engineering. The decoding framework uses deep learning models trained on the concatenated or fused feature representations. Performance is evaluated using classification accuracy across four inner speech conditions. The analysis includes non-linear dimensionality reduction techniques to examine underlying data structure that may influence fusion effectiveness.

## Key Results
- Late fusion approach achieved average accuracy of 26.41%, surpassing unimodal fMRI baseline of 17.50%
- Performance improvements were inconsistent across subjects, with only subjects 3 and 5 showing significant gains
- Underlying data structure, as revealed by non-linear dimensionality reduction, plays a crucial role in determining fusion strategy effectiveness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The bimodal fusion improves decoding when EEG and fMRI capture complementary neural information about inner speech that manifests as structured patterns in the joint feature space. When these complementary patterns exist, fusion strategies can leverage information from both modalities that would be missed by unimodal approaches. The success depends on the degree of orthogonality and redundancy between modalities, which is revealed through dimensionality reduction techniques that expose the underlying structure of the combined neural representations.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Multimodal data fusion** - Combining complementary information from different sensor types
   - Why needed: EEG and fMRI capture different aspects of neural activity with distinct temporal and spatial characteristics
   - Quick check: Fusion should outperform individual modalities when complementary information exists

2. **Non-linear dimensionality reduction** - Techniques like t-SNE or UMAP to reveal data structure
   - Why needed: High-dimensional neural data may contain hidden patterns not apparent in original feature space
   - Quick check: Reduced dimensions should separate classes or reveal clustering structure

3. **Late vs early fusion strategies** - Different approaches to combining multimodal information
   - Why needed: Timing of fusion affects how complementary information is integrated
   - Quick check: Late fusion preserves modality-specific processing while early fusion enables joint feature learning

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** EEG data -> EEG encoder -> EEG features
                      fMRI data -> fMRI encoder -> fMRI features
                      [Fusion module] -> Classifier -> Output probabilities

**Critical Path:** Data acquisition → Preprocessing → Feature extraction → Fusion → Classification → Evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs:** 
- Early fusion enables joint feature learning but requires careful alignment of temporal/spatial scales
- Late fusion preserves modality-specific representations but may miss cross-modal interactions
- Deep learning models require sufficient training data to avoid underfitting

**Failure Signatures:** 
- Performance degradation when underlying structure is absent or modalities are highly redundant
- Overfitting with limited training data, especially for high-dimensional EEG features
- Inconsistent improvements across subjects indicating individual differences in neural representations

**First Experiments:**
1. Compare unimodal EEG and fMRI performance to establish baseline complementarity
2. Test fusion performance on subjects with strongest vs weakest unimodal baselines
3. Evaluate dimensionality reduction visualization to confirm underlying structure existence

## Limitations
- Small sample size (N=4 participants) severely constrains generalizability and statistical power
- Inconsistent performance improvements across subjects suggest heavy dependence on individual data characteristics
- Limited training data may cause underfitting in deep learning models, particularly for high-dimensional EEG features

## Confidence
High: Bimodal fusion approaches can improve decoding performance when underlying data structure exists. The late fusion approach achieved higher accuracy than unimodal fMRI baseline in some subjects.

Medium: Inconsistent performance benefits across subjects indicate that fusion effectiveness depends on individual data characteristics. The suggestion that non-linear dimensionality reduction techniques reveal underlying structure is reasonable but requires further validation.

Low: The claim that larger datasets would enable more conclusive evaluations of complementary data in hybrid systems is speculative without demonstrating actual performance gains from increased sample sizes.

## Next Checks
1. Test the fusion models on an independent validation set or through cross-validation to assess generalizability beyond the training data.
2. Conduct ablation studies to determine the contribution of each modality to the bimodal performance by systematically removing EEG or fMRI components.
3. Compare the proposed fusion approaches against alternative multimodal fusion methods, such as attention-based mechanisms or Bayesian model averaging, to establish relative performance.
