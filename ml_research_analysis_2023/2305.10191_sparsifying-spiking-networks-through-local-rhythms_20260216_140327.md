# Sparsifying Spiking Networks through Local Rhythms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10191
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10191
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Up to 60% of spikes can be removed without significant accuracy loss using phase-based sparsification

## Executive Summary
This work demonstrates that spiking neural networks can leverage local rhythmic oscillations to sparsify communication and computation while maintaining performance. By assuming in-phase spikes are redundant, up to 60% of spikes can be removed without significant accuracy loss—a stark improvement over random sparsification. This was shown in a small MLP trained on FashionMNIST, where phase-based sparsification methods preserved 85%+ accuracy while drastically reducing spike traffic. The approach offers a biologically plausible path toward energy-efficient neuromorphic computing by exploiting phase coding and local inhibition dynamics, with plans to extend to deeper, more complex architectures.

## Method Summary
The method uses resonate-and-fire neurons to create phase-encoded spiking networks, where inputs are randomly projected to phasor values and processed through layers with bias inputs. Three sparsification methods were tested: explicit phase-based removal, inhibitory sparsification using local field potentials, and random removal as a baseline. The network was trained using cosine similarity loss and evaluated in spiking inference mode, with phase clustering enabled by bias inputs allowing selective removal of redundant in-phase spikes.

## Key Results
- Phase-based sparsification preserves 85%+ accuracy while removing up to 60% of spikes
- Outperforms random sparsification by 20-30% in accuracy retention
- Demonstrated on FashionMNIST with a small MLP (128, 10 neurons in hidden layers)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: In-phase spikes are redundant because they represent the same phase value within a layer
- Mechanism: When neurons in a layer share a common bias, their spikes cluster around a dominant phase. Removing spikes within a narrow angular range around this phase preserves the essential information while eliminating redundancy.
- Core assumption: Most spikes within a layer are in-phase with each other due to the common bias input
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "assuming in-phase spikes are redundant, up to 60% of spikes can be removed without significant accuracy loss"
  - [section] "This change also led to different behavior in the spiking domain. When angles are viewed as spiking outputs... the shift of distributions from uniform to normal can be seen in the 'bunching' of spikes"
- Break condition: If layer neurons lose their phase clustering (e.g., no bias, diverse inputs), the redundancy assumption fails

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Local field potentials can be used to inhibit redundant spikes without explicit phase decoding
- Mechanism: Each spike contributes to a local field potential. When the rate of change in this field reaches a maximum, neurons are silenced, removing spikes during periods of high in-phase activity.
- Core assumption: Local oscillations and field potentials can serve as implicit reference signals for spike inhibition
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This can reduce the amount of energy required for communication and computation in these networks while preserving accuracy"
  - [section] "I emulate the use of a local field potential to inhibit a neuron's output. Each spike in a layer contributes to this field, and as the rate of change in this field reaches a local maximum, neurons in the layer are silenced"
- Break condition: If the local field potential cannot effectively track the dominant phase, inhibition becomes unreliable

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Biasing neurons creates normally-distributed phase outputs, concentrating information in fewer spikes
- Mechanism: Adding a bias input shifts the phase distribution from uniform to normal around zero. This creates a natural clustering of spikes, making it easier to identify and remove redundant in-phase spikes.
- Core assumption: The bias input effectively shifts the phase distribution without destroying representational capacity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "This change shifted the distribution of activations – each representing an angle - from uniform across the unit circle to normally-distributed around zero"
  - [section] "With so many spikes being produced at the same time in a layer with biased neurons, I propose that neurons which spike out-of-phase produce information which is more valuable to a downstream input"
- Break condition: If the bias becomes too strong, it may collapse the phase distribution to a single point, losing all information

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input layer (768 pixels → 1024 phasors) -> Hidden layer 1 (128 phasor neurons) -> Hidden layer 2 (10 phasor neurons) -> Output with cosine similarity loss
- Critical path:
  1. Forward pass through phasor layers
  2. Spike train generation via R&F dynamics
  3. Sparsification applied to each layer's output
  4. Decoding and accuracy calculation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Phase-based sparsification vs. random sparsification: phase-based preserves accuracy better but requires phase tracking
  - Local vs. global reference: local field potentials are more biologically plausible but potentially less reliable than global timing
  - Bias strength: affects phase clustering but may reduce representational diversity
- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy drops sharply with low sparsification percentages: suggests phase clustering isn't occurring
  - No difference between phase-based and random sparsification: indicates the phase assumption is invalid
  - Network fails to train: may indicate the bias is too strong or the phase representation is broken
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test phase-based vs. random sparsification on varying percentages (0-90%) to confirm the 60% threshold
  2. Remove the bias input to verify that phase clustering is indeed caused by the bias
  3. Apply sparsification to a deeper network (e.g., 5 layers) to test scalability assumptions

## Limitations
- Experimental validation limited to single small-scale FashionMNIST task with only two hidden layers
- Biological plausibility of proposed mechanisms remains largely theoretical with minimal empirical grounding
- Critical implementation details for inhibitory sparsification method are underspecified

## Confidence
- High confidence for FashionMNIST MLP results
- Medium confidence in biological relevance claims
- Low confidence in complete method specification and scalability claims
