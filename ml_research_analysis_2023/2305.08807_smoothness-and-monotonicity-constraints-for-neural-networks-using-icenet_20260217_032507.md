# Smoothness and monotonicity constraints for neural networks using ICEnet

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08807
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08807
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: ICEnet produces smoother and more monotonic predictions than standard neural networks with only minimal loss in predictive accuracy (Poisson deviance of 0.2385 vs 0.2383)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ICEnet, a novel neural network architecture for enforcing smoothness and monotonicity constraints in actuarial modeling. The method augments training data with pseudo-observations to produce Individual Conditional Expectation (ICE) outputs, then applies constraint penalties to these outputs during training. Applied to French motor insurance data, ICEnet produces smoother and more monotonic predictions than standard neural networks with only minimal loss in predictive accuracy. The approach enables actuaries to incorporate commercial and statistical constraints while maintaining the predictive power of deep learning models.

## Method Summary
ICEnet is a neural network architecture that enforces smoothness and monotonicity constraints by augmenting the training data with pseudo-observations. The model generates ICE outputs for constrained covariates, then applies third difference penalties for smoothness and first difference penalties for monotonicity during training. A local approximation variant computes ICE outputs only within a window around observed values to reduce computational overhead. The architecture combines fully connected layers with embedding layers for categorical variables, using a custom loss function that balances predictive accuracy with constraint enforcement.

## Key Results
- ICEnet achieves Poisson deviance of 0.2385 on French MTPL data, comparable to unconstrained networks (0.2383)
- The method successfully enforces smoothness and monotonicity constraints as verified by Partial Dependence Plots
- Local ICEnet approximation reduces computational burden while maintaining constraint quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ICEnet enforces smoothness by applying the squared third difference penalty to ICE outputs
- Mechanism: The model creates pseudo-data varying constrained covariates, applies the same network to these, then penalizes large third differences in the resulting ICE outputs
- Core assumption: Third differences capture meaningful smoothness violations for actuarial pricing
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we implement here as the square of the third difference of the predictions"
  - [abstract] "applies constraint penalties to these outputs during training"
- Break condition: If third differences fail to capture the smoothness notion required by actuaries

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ICEnet enforces monotonicity by penalizing negative first differences in ICE outputs
- Mechanism: The model computes first differences of ICE outputs for monotonicity-constrained variables, then applies L1 penalty on negative components
- Core assumption: First differences adequately capture monotonic behavior for insurance pricing variables
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we add the absolute value of the negative components of the first difference"
  - [abstract] "then applies constraint penalties to these outputs during training"
- Break condition: If first differences fail to capture the monotonic relationships actuaries expect

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Local ICEnet approximation maintains constraint enforcement while reducing computational burden
- Mechanism: Instead of computing ICE outputs for all possible covariate values, only computes within a local window around observed values
- Core assumption: Local ICE behavior approximates global ICE behavior sufficiently for constraint enforcement
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "only produce the ICE outputs in a window of ω−1/2 around the actual value"
  - [abstract] "Applied to French motor insurance data, ICEnet produces smoother and more monotonic predictions"
- Break condition: If local constraints fail to maintain smoothness/monotonicity in extrapolation regions

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input preprocessing → FCN with embeddings → ICE generation layer → Combined loss layer → Output
- Critical path: Data → ICE computation → Constraint penalty → Training loop
- Design tradeoffs: Global ICEnet (more accurate constraints, slower) vs Local ICEnet (faster, potentially less precise)
- Failure signatures: Excessive constraint strength → poor predictive performance; Insufficient strength → rough/monotonic outputs

  1. Train FCN baseline without constraints to establish performance benchmark
  2. Apply ICEnet with only smoothing constraints to test impact on predictive performance
  3. Apply ICEnet with both smoothing and monotonicity constraints using different penalty strengths

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of ICEnet compare to other state-of-the-art neural network architectures that enforce monotonicity constraints (e.g., lattice networks or monotonic gradient boosting machines)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that ICEnet is related to other methods like lattice networks (You et al., 2017) but does not provide a direct comparison
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares ICEnet to standard FCNs and does not benchmark against other specialized monotonic architectures
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of ICEnet against lattice networks and monotonic GBMs on the same dataset using identical evaluation metrics

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the ICEnet smoothing constraints and LASSO/fused LASSO regularization in GLMs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Enforcing the same constraints as those in (3.4) in a GLM will automatically smooth/constrain the GLM coefficients similar to LASSO and fused LASSO regularization" but does not elaborate
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions the connection but does not provide mathematical derivation or empirical evidence of the equivalence
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal mathematical proof of the relationship between ICEnet penalties and LASSO/fused LASSO penalties, plus empirical comparison of coefficient smoothness across both methods

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the Local ICEnet approximation affect constraint enforcement at the boundaries of the covariate domain?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that for extreme values, the Local ICEnet only produces outputs for the first or last ω observations, but does not evaluate the impact on constraint quality
- Why unresolved: The paper shows density plots of constraint enforcement but does not specifically analyze boundary behavior
- What evidence would resolve it: Quantitative analysis of constraint enforcement quality at domain boundaries (e.g., minimum/maximum driver age) comparing global vs. local ICEnet

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for selecting constraint strength parameters across different covariates?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper varies constraints uniformly across all variables but notes "using other techniques, one could also attempt to find an optimal set of constraints where the values of these differed by variables and type of constraint"
- Why unresolved: The paper uses heuristic selection and does not explore variable-specific optimization strategies
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic study of variable-specific constraint optimization using techniques like cross-validation or Bayesian optimization, with performance comparison to uniform constraint selection

## Limitations
- Computational complexity of generating ICE outputs can be prohibitive for high-dimensional data or large training sets
- Local ICEnet approximation may not maintain global constraint properties, especially at covariate domain boundaries
- Optimal penalty strength for constraints remains an empirical tuning challenge without theoretical guidance

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** The fundamental mechanism of using ICE outputs with constraint penalties is well-grounded in the methodology
- **Medium confidence:** The claim of minimal predictive performance loss (Poisson deviance of 0.2385 vs 0.2383) is supported by experimental results but lacks comparison with alternative constraint enforcement methods
- **Low confidence:** The generalizability of results to other actuarial datasets beyond the French MTPL data is uncertain without additional validation
