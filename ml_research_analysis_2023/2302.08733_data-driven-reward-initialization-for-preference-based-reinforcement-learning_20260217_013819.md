# Data Driven Reward Initialization for Preference based Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08733
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08733
- Reference count: 9
- High variability in reward model initialization leads to inconsistent PbRL performance across random seeds

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical challenge in preference-based reinforcement learning (PbRL): the high variability in performance caused by reward model initialization. While PbRL learns reward functions from human preferences over trajectory pairs, standard initialization methods like Kaiming-Uniform and Xavier-Uniform produce "patchy" reward landscapes that vary significantly across random seeds. The authors propose a data-driven initialization method that pretrains the reward model to produce uniform rewards across the state space using unsupervised trajectories, thereby reducing performance variability and improving overall performance without additional human cost.

## Method Summary
The proposed method pretrains the reward model Rhψ to minimize the squared difference between predicted rewards and a constant value ϵ across all states visited during unsupervised exploration. This creates a uniform reward initialization across the state space before the standard PbRL training begins. The pretraining loss is LP = ∑τj∼Dτ i=|τj|∑i=1 ||Rhψ(si)−ϵ||2, where D represents trajectories collected via random exploration. This approach ensures that the initial reward predictions are uniform rather than following the variable patterns produced by standard weight initialization schemes.

## Key Results
- Pretraining reward model to produce uniform rewards (ϵ=0.4) reduces performance variability across random seeds compared to standard initialization methods
- The proposed method improves agent performance on 7x7 and 15x15 gridworld tasks compared to Kaiming-Uniform and Xavier-Uniform initialization
- Uniform initialization does not add additional cost to human in the loop and only requires unsupervised trajectory collection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pretraining reward model to minimize difference from a constant value ensures uniform reward predictions across the state space.
- Mechanism: The pretraining step minimizes the squared difference between predicted rewards and a constant value ϵ across all states visited during unsupervised exploration, effectively flattening the reward landscape.
- Core assumption: Uniform reward initialization reduces the bias in trajectory sampling during preference queries, leading to more diverse and informative queries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a data-driven reward initialization method that does not add any additional cost to the human in the loop and negligible cost to the PbRL agent and show that doing so ensures that the predicted rewards of the initialized reward model are uniform in the state space"
  - [section] "we solve a regression problem by minimizing the following pretraining-initialization loss, LP = ∑τj∼Dτ i=|τj|∑i=1 ||Rhψ(si)−ϵ||2"
- Break condition: If the pretraining trajectories don't adequately cover the state space, the uniform initialization may not extend to all states.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Uniform reward initialization reduces performance variability across random seeds.
- Mechanism: By eliminating the "patchy" reward initialization patterns that vary with random seeds, the method creates a more stable starting point for reward learning.
- Core assumption: The variability in performance is primarily caused by differences in reward initialization rather than other factors in the PbRL pipeline.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we found that high variability in the learned reward model and subsequently the learned agent policy is also seen when we vary the reward function initialization strategy"
  - [section] "we propose a data-driven reward function initialization technique that not only stabilizes the resultant performance across multiple runs but also improves the agent's performance"
- Break condition: If other sources of variability (e.g., exploration strategy, query selection) dominate over initialization effects.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Uniform initialization improves exploration by enabling more diverse trajectory sampling.
- Mechanism: With uniform initial rewards, the agent doesn't get biased toward specific state regions during early exploration, leading to more diverse trajectory collection.
- Core assumption: Diverse trajectory collection leads to more informative preference queries and better reward learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In fact, such initialization is assumed to be a 'feature than a bug' where works consider the initial randomness as a means for the agent to explore in the world"
  - [section] "we propose that the initialization of reward model parameters such that the corresponding reward values on the domain of the state space of the world are uniform will help with reward learning as well as stabilize the performance across various random seeds"
- Break condition: If the exploration strategy itself introduces sufficient diversity regardless of initialization.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: PbRL system consists of (1) RL agent (DQN), (2) reward model Rhψ, (3) human feedback loop providing preference labels, (4) unsupervised pretraining phase for data collection, (5) pretraining-initialization loss LP

- Critical path: 1) Collect unsupervised trajectories via random policy, 2) Pretrain reward model to minimize LP (make rewards uniform), 3) Run PbRL with preference queries and cross-entropy loss, 4) Update both reward model and policy

- Design tradeoffs: The method trades a small amount of pretraining computation for improved stability and performance. Alternative approaches might include curriculum learning or more sophisticated initialization schemes.

- Failure signatures: High variance in performance across seeds despite uniform initialization, poor reward recovery even with good initialization, or the pretraining phase not covering the relevant state space.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare uniform initialization (ϵ=0.4) vs. zeros initialization on 7x7 gridworld, measuring performance variance across seeds
  2. Test different values of ϵ (e.g., 0.1, 0.4, 0.7) to find optimal reward level
  3. Apply the method to a continuous control task (e.g., LunarLander) to verify scalability beyond gridworlds

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does reward initialization impact performance across more complex domains like locomotion or robotic manipulation tasks compared to gridworld domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "We plan to further investigate the impact of reward initialization across more complex domains used in PbRL literature like locomotion tasks, robotic manipulation tasks, and other explicit discrete action domains."
- Why unresolved: The current study only validates the proposed method on 7x7 and 15x15 gridworld domains, which are relatively simple compared to real-world robotic applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the performance of the proposed data-driven reward initialization method on complex continuous control tasks and robotic manipulation benchmarks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What other factors beyond reward initialization contribute to reward function degeneracy in preference-based reinforcement learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "This work calls for a more thorough investigation of potential reasons that worsen the issue of reward function degeneracy."
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies reward initialization as a significant factor, it acknowledges that other unidentified factors may also contribute to degeneracy.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies isolating different components of PbRL algorithms (query strategy, exploration method, temporal augmentation, etc.) to identify which factors most strongly impact degeneracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed data-driven initialization method compare to other initialization strategies across different PbRL algorithms beyond PEBBLE?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "we also plan to study the impact of reward initialization across various PbRL methods like (Park et al. 2022; Verma and Metcalf 2022; Christiano et al. 2017)."
- Why unresolved: The current experiments only compare against PEBBLE with DQN as the backbone, limiting generalizability to other PbRL frameworks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments applying the same initialization method across multiple PbRL algorithms (PEBBLE, SURF, etc.) with different RL backbones (SAC, PPO) to determine algorithm-specific effects.

## Limitations
- The method is only validated on simple gridworld environments (7x7 and 15x15), not on complex continuous control tasks
- Pretraining requires collecting trajectories via random exploration, which may not scale well to high-dimensional state spaces
- The assumption that uniform reward initialization is universally beneficial may not hold for domains where state-specific reward structures are important

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The mechanism showing that uniform initialization reduces variance across random seeds is well-supported by experimental results on 7x7 and 15x15 gridworlds
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that uniform initialization improves performance beyond just reducing variance is supported but limited to simple gridworld domains
- **Medium Confidence**: The assertion that the method adds "negligible cost" is reasonable but the computational overhead relative to the full PbRL pipeline could be more thoroughly characterized

## Next Checks
1. Test the method on continuous control benchmarks (e.g., MuJoCo or PyBullet tasks) to assess scalability and effectiveness in high-dimensional state spaces.

2. Evaluate performance when the true reward function has significant state-dependent structure (e.g., sparse rewards or reward regions) to determine if uniform initialization might hinder learning in such cases.

3. Measure the actual computational overhead of the pretraining phase relative to the full PbRL training time to quantify the "negligible cost" claim across different environment complexities.
