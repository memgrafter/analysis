# A Benchmark of Nested Named Entity Recognition Approaches in Historical Structured Documents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10204
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10204
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Joint labeling with hierarchical loss most suitable for hierarchically structured data; IO tagging format outperforms IOB2

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of nested named entity recognition approaches on historical structured documents, specifically 19th-century Paris trade directories. The study compares three deep learning-based methods: independent NER layers, joint labeling, and a new hierarchical BERT-based approach with semantic weighting of errors. The evaluation considers the impact of supervised fine-tuning, unsupervised pre-training with noisy texts, and variations in IOB tagging formats. Results show that while nested NER approaches enable extracting structured data directly, they do not significantly outperform flat NER methods in terms of F1 scores. The joint labeling approach with hierarchical loss is most suitable for hierarchically structured data. Experiments reveal the superiority of the IO tagging format over IOB2 for this task. The study demonstrates that pre-training BERT models on domain-specific noisy texts improves performance on real-world OCR data, achieving F1 scores between 93.7% and 96.6% for nested entity recognition.

## Method Summary
The study benchmarks three nested named entity recognition approaches: independent NER layers, joint labeling, and a hierarchical BERT-based method. The hierarchical approach introduces semantic weighting of errors to handle nested entities. The evaluation uses 19th-century Paris trade directories as test data, examining the impact of supervised fine-tuning, unsupervised pre-training with noisy texts, and different IOB tagging formats. The research compares nested NER methods against flat NER baselines to assess whether the added complexity of nested approaches provides meaningful performance improvements.

## Key Results
- Joint labeling approach with hierarchical loss most suitable for hierarchically structured data
- IO tagging format outperforms IOB2 for nested NER on historical documents
- Pre-training BERT models on domain-specific noisy texts improves performance on real-world OCR data
- Nested NER approaches do not significantly outperform flat NER methods in terms of F1 scores

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The hierarchical BERT-based approach with semantic error weighting addresses the challenge of nested entity recognition by assigning different importance levels to errors at different hierarchical levels. This allows the model to better handle the complexity of nested structures where entities can contain other entities. The pre-training on noisy texts helps the model become robust to real-world OCR errors common in historical documents, bridging the gap between clean training data and imperfect input text.
