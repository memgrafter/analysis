# Transforming Human-Centered AI Collaboration: Redefining Embodied Agents Capabilities through Interactive Grounded Language Instructions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.10783
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10783
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Dataset of 8,136 pairs of actions and instructions, with 1,056 annotated ambiguous instructions, and baseline models achieving F-1 score of 0.761 and MRR@20 of 0.5960.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of developing embodied agents capable of natural language communication and interaction within complex environments. The authors propose a crowdsourcing tool for collecting interactive grounded language instructions in a Minecraft-like environment and present the largest dataset of human-to-human grounded language instructions with clarifying questions. The dataset consists of 8,136 pairs of actions and instructions, with 1,056 instructions annotated as ambiguous and accompanied by clarifying questions. The authors also establish baselines for predicting clarifying questions, achieving promising results. For the "When" task (clarification need prediction), a textual grid world state approach achieves an F-1 score of 0.761. For the "What" task (clarifying question retrieval), a baseline using BERT-like pre-trained language models achieves a Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)@20 of 0.5960. The contributions provide a foundation for further research in grounded language understanding and human-AI collaboration.

## Method Summary
The authors developed a crowdsourcing tool to collect interactive grounded language instructions in a Minecraft-like environment. The tool gathers multi-modal data (text, images, key-value pairs) for collaborative building tasks. The collected data is processed and cleaned, with ambiguous instructions annotated and accompanied by clarifying questions. The authors establish baselines for two tasks: clarification need prediction ("When" task) and clarifying question retrieval ("What" task). For the "When" task, they use fine-tuned BERT, Text-Grid Cross Modularity, and Textual Grid World State approaches. For the "What" task, they implement BM25 and DeBERTa-v3-based models with various ranking techniques. The models are evaluated using F-1 score for the "When" task and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)@20 for the "What" task.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Human adaptability to new tasks and environments can be modeled by interactive embodied agents that learn through natural language instructions and clarifying questions.
- Mechanism: Agents learn by receiving instructions, executing actions, and requesting clarification when instructions are ambiguous, mimicking how humans learn from demonstrations and verbal guidance.
- Core assumption: Natural language is an effective medium for transmitting generic knowledge and enabling rapid skill acquisition without trial-and-error learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] Human intelligence's adaptability allows swift adjustment to new tasks and environments, with natural language communication being highly effective for transmitting knowledge.
  - [section] The research community pursues embodied agents that can engage in natural conversations and assist with tasks by requesting feedback when instructions are unclear.
- Break condition: If the agent cannot accurately identify gaps in instructions or generate relevant clarifying questions, the learning process will be hindered.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A Minecraft-like environment provides a suitable platform for developing and testing interactive grounded language understanding due to its constraints and regularities.
- Mechanism: The voxel-based grid and simple physics of Minecraft allow for abundant research scenarios and opportunities for experimentation with agents trained by demonstration.
- Core assumption: The controlled environment of Minecraft can simulate real-world complexities sufficiently for research purposes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] The Minecraft world's constraints and regularities allow numerous scenarios for grounded NLU research, and its popularity ensures enthusiastic player interaction.
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers with topics including pragmatic embodied instruction following, multimodal speech recognition, and safety benchmarking.
- Break condition: If the simplified environment fails to capture essential aspects of real-world complexity, the agents' performance may not generalize.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Collecting a large dataset of human-to-human grounded language instructions with clarifying questions is essential for training and evaluating interactive embodied agents.
- Mechanism: The crowdsourcing tool efficiently gathers multi-modal data (text, images, key-value pairs) for collaborative building tasks, enabling the creation of the largest available dataset in this domain.
- Core assumption: Human-generated instructions and clarifying questions provide high-quality, diverse data that captures the nuances of natural language communication.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] The paper presents the largest dataset of grounded language instructions, accompanied by clarifying questions, as a valuable resource for research.
  - [corpus] The dataset consists of 8,136 pairs of actions and instructions, with 1,056 instructions annotated as ambiguous and accompanied by clarifying questions.
- Break condition: If the data collection process does not capture sufficient diversity or quality in instructions and questions, the dataset may not effectively support model training and evaluation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Crowdsourcing Tool -> Data Processing Pipeline -> Model Training Framework -> Evaluation Metrics
- Critical path:
  1. Collect data using the crowdsourcing tool.
  2. Process and clean the collected data.
  3. Train models on the processed data.
  4. Evaluate models using appropriate metrics.
  5. Iterate on model architecture and training based on evaluation results.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model complexity vs. interpretability: More complex models may achieve better performance but are harder to interpret and debug.
  - Data quality vs. quantity: Collecting more data may introduce noise, while focusing on high-quality data may limit the dataset size.
  - Real-time performance vs. accuracy: Models that can operate in real-time may sacrifice some accuracy compared to offline models.

- Failure signatures:
  - High variance in model performance across different tasks or datasets.
  - Inability to handle edge cases or rare scenarios.
  - Models generating irrelevant or nonsensical clarifying questions.
  - Poor generalization to new instructions or environments.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune a pre-trained BERT model on the clarification need prediction task and evaluate its performance using the F-1 score.
  2. Implement the Text-Grid Cross Modularity baseline and compare its performance with the BERT baseline on the clarification need prediction task.
  3. Train a BERT-based model for the clarifying question retrieval task and evaluate its performance using the Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) metric.

- What evidence would resolve it: Successful adaptation and evaluation of the crowdsourcing tool for collecting data in other collaborative task domains, demonstrating its generalizability and effectiveness beyond the Minecraft environment.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the dataset be expanded to include more diverse and complex structures, as well as a wider range of clarifying questions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper presents the largest available dataset of human-to-human grounded language instructions, but acknowledges that it is limited in terms of the diversity and complexity of the structures and clarifying questions.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a clear strategy for expanding the dataset to include a more comprehensive range of structures and clarifying questions, which could enhance its utility for training and evaluating embodied agents.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful collection and integration of a larger and more diverse dataset, including a wider variety of structures and clarifying questions, along with an evaluation of its impact on the performance of embodied agents in understanding and responding to natural language instructions.

## Limitations
- The gap between controlled Minecraft environments and real-world complexity may limit the generalizability of results
- The dataset size (8,136 pairs) may not capture the full diversity of natural language ambiguity and clarification patterns encountered in real applications
- Uncertainty about how well findings translate to less structured, more ambiguous real-world scenarios
