# Unit Scaling: Out-of-the-Box Low-Precision Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11257
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11257
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: BERT-Large trained in FP8 without loss scaling, achieving 85.22% F1 on SQuAD v1.1

## Executive Summary
Unit scaling introduces a principled approach to low-precision deep learning training by ensuring unit variance in weights, activations, and gradients through fixed scaling factors in the computational graph. This method eliminates the need for loss scaling sweeps and per-tensor dynamic scaling while maintaining training stability and accuracy. The technique is validated across multiple architectures including BERT and vision models, demonstrating successful training in both FP16 and FP8 precisions without accuracy degradation. BERT-Large achieves 85.67% F1 in FP16 and 85.22% F1 in FP8 on SQuAD v1.1.

## Method Summary
Unit scaling works by inserting fixed scaling factors into the computational graph to maintain unit variance across weights, activations, and gradients. These scaling factors are determined based on the layer's weight distribution and activation statistics, ensuring that each layer operates within the representable range of low-precision formats. The approach is architecture-agnostic and optimizer-compatible, requiring no per-model hyperparameter tuning. By fusing these scaling factors into the computation, the method achieves minimal overhead while enabling stable training in FP8 without the numerical instability typically encountered in low-precision training.

## Key Results
- BERT-Large achieves 85.67% F1 on SQuAD v1.1 in FP16 without loss scaling
- BERT-Large achieves 85.22% F1 on SQuAD v1.1 in FP8 without loss scaling
- Method validated across BERT, ViT, and ResNet architectures with consistent results
- Eliminates need for loss scaling sweeps while maintaining training stability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Unit scaling works by ensuring that the variance of weights, activations, and gradients remains unit at initialization through carefully designed scaling factors. These fixed scaling factors are integrated into the computational graph during forward and backward passes, preserving the statistical properties needed for stable training. By maintaining unit variance, the method prevents values from underflowing or overflowing in low-precision formats, eliminating the primary source of instability in FP8 training. The scaling factors are derived from layer-wise statistics and remain constant throughout training, providing a deterministic alternative to dynamic scaling approaches.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to BERT and standard vision models; generalization to GPT-style transformers untested
- FP8 results show slight accuracy degradation (85.67% vs 85.22% F1) suggesting precision limitations
- Fixed scaling factors may accumulate numerical error over very deep networks
- Assumes homogeneous scaling across all layers, which may not be optimal for heterogeneous architectures

## Confidence
- High confidence in variance preservation mechanism and basic implementation
- Medium confidence in FP16 results across multiple architectures
- Medium confidence in FP8 results, given the slight accuracy degradation
- Low confidence in generalization to architectures beyond BERT and standard vision models
