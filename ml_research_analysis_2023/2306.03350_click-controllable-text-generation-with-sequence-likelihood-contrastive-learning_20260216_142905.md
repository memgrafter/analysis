# Click: Controllable Text Generation with Sequence Likelihood Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03350
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03350
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: CLICK method outperforms baselines on language detoxification, sentiment steering, and repetition reduction tasks

## Executive Summary
CLICK introduces a novel approach for controllable text generation by leveraging sequence likelihood contrastive learning. The method aims to reduce the generation probability of undesirable attributes in text, such as toxicity, negative sentiment, and unnatural repetition. By constructing contrastive samples from model generations and pairing negative samples with the highest-likelihood positive samples that rank lower, CLICK effectively steers text generation away from unwanted characteristics while maintaining fluency and diversity.

## Method Summary
CLICK employs a contrastive learning framework to guide text generation away from undesirable attributes. The method constructs contrastive samples by generating text and pairing each negative sample with the highest-likelihood positive sample that ranks lower than the former. A contrastive loss is then applied to these pairs, encouraging the model to assign higher probabilities to positive samples and lower probabilities to negative samples. This approach allows CLICK to effectively control the generation of text by reducing the likelihood of undesirable attributes while preserving the quality and diversity of the generated output.

## Key Results
- CLICK outperforms strong baselines on language detoxification, reducing toxic language in generated text.
- The method demonstrates effectiveness in sentiment steering, allowing for control over the emotional tone of generated text.
- CLICK shows significant improvements in repetition reduction, mitigating the generation of unnatural and redundant text patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CLICK works by leveraging the power of contrastive learning to guide text generation towards desired attributes and away from undesirable ones. The method constructs contrastive samples from model generations, creating pairs of negative and positive samples based on their likelihood rankings. By applying a contrastive loss to these pairs, CLICK encourages the model to assign higher probabilities to positive samples (which exhibit desirable attributes) and lower probabilities to negative samples (which exhibit undesirable attributes). This process effectively shapes the probability distribution of the generated text, steering it towards the desired characteristics while maintaining fluency and diversity.

## Foundational Learning
- Contrastive learning: A machine learning technique that learns representations by comparing positive and negative samples. Why needed: Enables the model to distinguish between desirable and undesirable text attributes. Quick check: Verify that the contrastive loss is properly applied to the constructed sample pairs.
- Sequence likelihood: The probability assigned to a sequence of tokens by a language model. Why needed: Forms the basis for constructing contrastive samples and guiding the generation process. Quick check: Ensure that the model's likelihood predictions are accurate and reliable.
- Text attribute control: The ability to steer text generation towards specific attributes, such as sentiment or toxicity. Why needed: Allows for fine-grained control over the characteristics of the generated text. Quick check: Validate that the controlled attributes are effectively incorporated into the generation process.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Text generation model -> Contrastive sample construction -> Contrastive loss application -> Controlled text generation

Critical path:
1. Generate text using the base language model
2. Construct contrastive samples by pairing negative and positive samples based on likelihood rankings
3. Apply contrastive loss to the constructed pairs
4. Use the updated model to generate controlled text

Design tradeoffs:
- Balancing the strength of the contrastive loss to ensure effective control without sacrificing fluency and diversity
- Determining the optimal sample size and diversity for constructing contrastive pairs
- Deciding on the frequency of contrastive learning updates during the generation process

Failure signatures:
- Insufficient control over undesirable attributes due to weak contrastive loss or inadequate sample construction
- Degradation of fluency and diversity in generated text as a result of overly strong contrastive loss
- Ineffectiveness in controlling attributes that are not well-represented in the training data
