# Tensor Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05277
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05277
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: TGCCA outperforms state-of-the-art methods in retrieving true canonical vectors and achieving accurate multi-view matching, especially when canonical vectors have rank greater than one

## Executive Summary
Tensor Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis (TGCCA) extends Regularized Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis (RGCCA) to higher-order tensor data by imposing orthogonal rank-R CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP) decompositions on canonical vectors. Two algorithmic strategies are proposed: separable TGCCA (with Kronecker-structured regularization matrices) and non-separable TGCCA (with orthogonality constraints on rank-1 factors). Both approaches maximize RGCCA's multi-convex objective over compact feasible sets. Experimental results on simulated and real data demonstrate that TGCCA outperforms state-of-the-art methods in retrieving true canonical vectors and achieving accurate multi-view matching.

## Method Summary
TGCCA imposes orthogonal rank-R CP decompositions on canonical vectors to extend RGCCA to tensor data. The method uses block coordinate ascent to maximize a multi-convex objective function. Two formulations are proposed: separable TGCCA exploits Kronecker-structured regularization matrices for computational efficiency, while non-separable TGCCA uses orthogonality constraints on rank-1 factors. The algorithm alternates between updating each canonical vector using mode-wise SVD operations, with convergence guaranteed by the multi-convex structure. Multiple random starts are used to avoid local optima, and deflation is applied to extract additional components.

## Key Results
- TGCCA outperforms state-of-the-art methods (MGCCA, TCCA, RGCCA) in retrieving true canonical vectors and achieving accurate multi-view matching
- The separable version is faster while maintaining accuracy compared to the non-separable version
- TGCCA demonstrates superior performance especially when canonical vectors have rank greater than one
- The method scales well with increasing numbers of blocks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TGCCA extracts canonical vectors with orthogonal rank-R CP decompositions that improve canonical component matching over existing methods
- Mechanism: By imposing orthogonality constraints on the rank-1 factors within each canonical vector's CP decomposition, TGCCA avoids collinearity and degeneracy that can occur in non-orthogonal decompositions like TCCA. This leads to better recovery of true canonical vectors in both synthetic and real data experiments
- Core assumption: The true canonical vectors in the data have orthogonal rank-R CP structure and this structure is exploitable for improved matching
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Tensor Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis (TGCCA) extends Regularized Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis (RGCCA) to higher-order tensor data by imposing orthogonal rank-R CANDECOMP/PARAFAC (CP) decompositions on canonical vectors"
  - [section 3.4]: "To ensure that the solution of (5) does not degenerate in practice, we add orthogonality constraints between the rank-1 factors w(r) l of the CP decomposition"
- Break condition: If the true canonical vectors do not have orthogonal CP structure or if orthogonality constraints are too restrictive relative to the data structure

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The separable and non-separable formulations of TGCCA allow handling both Kronecker-structured and general covariance structures efficiently
- Mechanism: In the separable case, the Kronecker product structure of regularization matrices allows efficient updates via mode-wise SVD operations. In the non-separable case, a relaxed formulation with norm constraints on the CP weights still enables tractable optimization while maintaining accuracy
- Core assumption: Either the covariance structure is Kronecker-separable or the relaxed non-separable formulation provides sufficient accuracy
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4]: "Under the assumption that matrices Ml are separable: Ml = Mld⊗... ⊗ Ml1 with Mlm∈ Rplm×plm form∈ [d], we choose∀l∈ [L], Kl = Ml"

- Break condition: If the covariance structure is neither separable nor well-approximated by the relaxed formulation

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: TGCCA's multi-convex optimization framework with block coordinate ascent guarantees monotonic convergence to stationary points
- Mechanism: The objective function is multi-convex (convex in each block canonical vector when others are fixed). Block coordinate ascent updates each canonical vector to increase the objective while maintaining constraints, ensuring monotonic improvement and convergence

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing: Centering and scaling each tensor block
  - Model specification: Choose separable vs non-separable formulation, rank R
  - Initialization: Random starts for multiple runs
  - Optimization: Block coordinate ascent with mode-wise updates
  - Post-processing: Deflation for multiple components, concentration estimation

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess data (center/scale)
  2. Initialize canonical vectors randomly
  3. Alternate between updating each canonical vector using mode-wise SVD updates
  4. Check convergence (objective change below threshold)
  5. Repeat with different random starts, keep best solution
  6. Apply deflation to extract additional components if needed

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Rank selection: Higher rank captures more complex structure but increases computation and risk of overfitting
  - Separable vs non-separable: Separable is faster but requires Kronecker structure; non-separable is more flexible but computationally heavier
  - Multiple starts: Improves robustness to local optima but increases computation time

- Failure signatures:
  - Slow convergence: May indicate poor initialization or ill-conditioned data
  - Degenerate solutions: Suggests orthogonality constraints are too restrictive or rank is mis-specified
  - High variance across runs: Indicates local optima trapping or insufficient regularization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Synthetic data with known orthogonal CP structure - verify recovery accuracy vs baseline methods
  2. Real data with Kronecker-structured covariances (e.g., chemical mixtures) - compare separable vs non-separable formulations
  3. High-dimensional multi-view data (e.g., facial images) - test scalability and matching performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How can TGCCA be modified to simultaneously extract all sets of canonical vectors instead of using a sequential deflation strategy?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] "A procedure that extracts all sets of canonical vectors simultaneously is currently under investigation"
  - Why unresolved: The current TGCCA implementation relies on deflation, which imposes orthogonality constraints that may not hold in real data, as seen in the chemical mixtures dataset
  - What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of a simultaneous extraction algorithm that can handle non-orthogonal canonical components, with demonstrated performance improvements on datasets with non-orthogonal structures

- **Open Question 2**: How can the optimal rank R for TGCCA be determined in practice, especially when distinguishing between relevant signal and noise?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] "A procedure to find the best rank is of great interest and under investigation"
  - Why unresolved: The paper shows that overestimating rank can lead to modeling noise as additional factors, but no systematic method is provided for rank selection
  - What evidence would resolve it: A cross-validation or information criterion-based method that accurately selects rank R, validated on both simulated and real datasets with known ground truth

- **Open Question 3**: How can TGCCA be extended to incorporate higher-order correlations (beyond pairwise) between more than two blocks?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] "An interesting research line would be defining higher-order correlations and incorporating them in the criterion to optimize"
  - Why unresolved: The current TGCCA framework only considers pairwise correlations through the C matrix, limiting its ability to capture complex multi-block relationships
  - What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical framework and implementation that extends TGCCA to include higher-order correlations, with demonstrated performance improvements on multi-block datasets where such correlations are known to exist

## Limitations
- The orthogonal CP decomposition assumption may be too restrictive for data where true canonical vectors have non-orthogonal or non-CP structure
- The Kronecker-structured separable formulation requires specific covariance structure that may not hold in many real-world applications
- The method's performance at very high dimensions (p ≫ n) or with non-Gaussian noise remains untested

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (orthogonal CP structure): Medium - supported by synthetic experiments but real-world applicability needs validation
- Mechanism 2 (separable vs non-separable): Medium - theoretical justification strong but empirical comparison limited to specific covariance structures
- Mechanism 3 (multi-convex optimization): High - well-established theory with proven convergence guarantees
