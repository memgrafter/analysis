# OPI at SemEval 2023 Task 9: A Simple But Effective Approach to Multilingual Tweet Intimacy Analysis

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.07130
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07130
- **Reference count:** 7
- **Primary result:** Ranked first in 5 out of 10 language subtasks in multilingual tweet intimacy analysis

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a solution for multilingual tweet intimacy analysis in the SemEval 2023 Task 9, which measures the level of intimacy in Twitter posts across ten languages. The approach combines domain adaptation through in-domain pre-training on a large tweet corpus and semi-supervised learning via pseudo-labeling. The method achieved the highest average Pearson correlation score across all languages and demonstrated effectiveness in cross-lingual transfer learning.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of three main steps: First, the team further pre-trained XLM RoBERTa large on a 156 million tweet corpus to adapt it to Twitter domain language patterns. Second, they trained an ensemble of regression models on the original training data, then used this ensemble to pseudo-label 292K additional tweets that met specific confidence criteria (standard deviation threshold of 0.05). Third, they trained a new ensemble on the combined original and pseudo-labeled data for final predictions. The system used five regression models per ensemble with five-fold cross-validation and five different random seeds.

## Key Results
- Achieved highest average Pearson correlation score across all ten languages
- Ranked first in five out of ten language subtasks (English, French, Arabic, Spanish, Italian)
- Demonstrated strong performance on languages not present in the original training set (zero-shot learning)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages domain adaptation to handle the informal, context-specific language of Twitter, while semi-supervised learning expands the training data to improve model robustness across languages. The ensemble approach reduces variance and improves generalization.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Tweet Corpus -> In-domain Pre-training -> Base Ensemble -> Pseudo-labeling -> Expanded Dataset -> Final Ensemble -> Predictions

**Critical Path:** The pseudo-labeling step is critical - it bridges the gap between limited labeled data and the final ensemble training. The standard deviation threshold of 0.05 determines which examples get added to the expanded dataset.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The team chose ensemble averaging over other uncertainty quantification methods, trading computational cost for improved robustness. They prioritized simplicity over complex cross-lingual architectures.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance on unseen languages suggests insufficient cross-lingual transfer; high variance across ensemble models indicates instability in pseudo-labeling quality.

**First Experiments:** 1) Test pre-training impact by comparing with baseline XLM-RoBERTa, 2) Validate pseudo-labeling threshold by testing different standard deviation cutoffs, 3) Verify zero-shot learning performance on languages not in training set.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper doesn't explicitly call out open questions, but raises several implicit ones: How the model performs on languages not included in the pre-training corpus, the impact of different ensemble sizes on performance, and how pseudo-labeling thresholds affect overall results.

## Limitations
- The 156 million tweet corpus used for domain adaptation is not publicly available
- The pseudo-labeling selection criteria beyond standard deviation threshold lack detailed justification
- No ablation study comparing performance with and without pseudo-labeled data

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in domain adaptation effectiveness (consistent top rankings across languages)
- **Medium confidence** in pseudo-labeling contribution (no ablation studies provided)
- **High confidence** in ensemble robustness (multiple model runs with variance reporting)
