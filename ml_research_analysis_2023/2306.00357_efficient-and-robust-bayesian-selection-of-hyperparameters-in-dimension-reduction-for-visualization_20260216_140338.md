# Efficient and Robust Bayesian Selection of Hyperparameters in Dimension Reduction for Visualization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00357
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00357
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: Bayesian optimization framework with subsampling enables efficient hyperparameter tuning for t-SNE and UMAP with computational savings while maintaining visualization quality

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of hyperparameter selection in dimensionality reduction algorithms for visualization by introducing a Bayesian optimization-based framework that leverages subsampling and surrogate modeling. The approach significantly reduces computational cost by tuning hyperparameters on a small subsample of data while maintaining quality through careful normalization and multiple-initialization averaging. The framework supports multi-objective optimization across various performance metrics and demonstrates robust performance across synthetic and real-world datasets.

## Method Summary
The framework uses Bayesian optimization with surrogate models (Gaussian processes or random forests) to efficiently explore the hyperparameter space of dimensionality reduction algorithms like t-SNE and UMAP. It operates by subsampling the original dataset (typically 1/3 or 1/10 of the data), normalizing the hyperparameter space, and using multiple initializations to stabilize performance metrics. The optimal hyperparameters found on the subsample are then transferred to the full dataset. The approach handles multi-objective trade-offs through Pareto optimization and includes sensitivity analysis to understand hyperparameter impacts.

## Key Results
- Bayesian optimization with subsampling achieves comparable visualization quality to full-data tuning at significantly reduced computational cost
- The framework successfully handles both task-independent metrics (AUC, Q-global) and task-dependent metrics (NMI, classification error) through multi-objective optimization
- Optimal hyperparameters found on subsampled data transfer effectively to full datasets, with leverage score sampling showing smaller metric variation than random sampling

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Subsampling preserves the hyperparameter landscape while reducing computational cost
- Mechanism: The framework leverages subsampling to create a smaller dataset that approximates the full dataset's performance landscape for hyperparameter selection. By normalizing the hyperparameter space, the optimal hyperparameters found on the subsample can be transferred back to the full dataset.
- Core assumption: The performance landscape of the subsampled dataset is sufficiently similar to the full dataset for hyperparameter optimization purposes
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We subsample a third of the original dataset under two different sampling schemes: random sampling and leverage score sampling... Under leverage score sampling, the variation for the performance metrics is smaller compared to that under random sampling."
  - [section]: "Using the unbalanced 2-cluster dataset, we illustrate how the surrogate-based tuning methodology works when the evaluation metrics can be directly computed on the full dataset... we use normalized perplexity, calculated by dividing the perplexity by the number of data points."
- Break condition: If the subsampled dataset fails to capture the essential structure of the full dataset, the optimal hyperparameters found on the subsample may not generalize to the full dataset, leading to poor performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Bayesian optimization with surrogate modeling efficiently explores the hyperparameter space
- Mechanism: The framework uses Bayesian optimization with a surrogate model (e.g., Gaussian processes, random forests) to sequentially select promising hyperparameter configurations. This approach balances exploration and exploitation to find optimal hyperparameters with fewer evaluations than exhaustive search.
- Core assumption: The surrogate model can accurately approximate the true performance function and guide the search for optimal hyperparameters
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "By leveraging Bayesian optimization (BO) with a surrogate model, our approach enables efficient hyperparameter selection with multi-objective trade-offs"
  - [section]: "We examine the following approaches, grid exhaustive search (N0 = total sample size), random forest regression, and Gaussian processes (GP-EI, GP-PI, GP-LCB (Louppe, 2017)) as Bayesian surrogates"
- Break condition: If the surrogate model fails to accurately capture the true performance function or the acquisition function leads to poor hyperparameter selections, the framework may not find optimal hyperparameters efficiently.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multiple-initialization averaging improves metric robustness and reduces initialization sensitivity
- Mechanism: The framework addresses the sensitivity of DR methods to initialization by averaging performance metrics across multiple initializations. This averaging process stabilizes the surrogate model and improves the reliability of hyperparameter selection.
- Core assumption: Averaging performance metrics across multiple initializations provides a more stable and representative estimate of the true performance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We proposed to tune empirical means of several repeatsbEF (γ) instead of F (γ) directly due to the observation that metrics computed by DR methods are highly variable with different initializations"
  - [section]: "Given the fact we want to approximateEF (γ) from multiple possible sources of randomness, we propose multiple-initialization averaged metrics for more stable and robust performance"
- Break condition: If the performance metric is highly variable even after averaging across multiple initializations, or if the number of initializations is insufficient to stabilize the metric, the framework may still suffer from initialization sensitivity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Subsampling/Normalization -> Surrogate modeling (GP/RF) -> Bayesian optimization -> Performance metrics evaluation -> Visualization
- Critical path: 1. Preprocess data (subsampling, normalization) 2. Initialize surrogate model with pilot samples 3. Iteratively optimize acquisition function to select promising hyperparameters 4. Evaluate performance metrics on subsample with selected hyperparameters 5. Update surrogate model with new data points 6. Repeat steps 3-5 until convergence or budget exhaustion 7. Apply optimal hyperparameters to full dataset
- Design tradeoffs: Subsampling vs. full dataset: Balancing computational efficiency and accuracy; Surrogate model choice: Gaussian processes vs. random forests (complexity vs. interpretability); Performance metrics: Task-independent vs. task-dependent (comprehensiveness vs. specificity); Multiple-initialization averaging: Stability vs. computational cost
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on full dataset despite good performance on subsample; Surrogate model fails to capture true performance landscape; High variance in performance metrics across initializations; Computational cost exceeds budget due to large number of initializations or surrogate evaluations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the proposed surrogate-based hyperparameter tuning methods scale to datasets with hundreds of hyperparameters or extremely high dimensionality (e.g., n << d cases)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that for UMAP with two hyperparameters they used higher budgets (N2 ∈ {20, 40}) and that for highly structured or ultra-high-dimensional DR tasks, non-trivial modifications of surrogate-based tuning methods need to be developed.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates results with 1-2 hyperparameters (t-SNE perplexity, UMAP n_neighbor and min_distance) and doesn't test the approach on datasets with extreme dimensionality ratios or hundreds of hyperparameters.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing successful hyperparameter tuning on datasets with 10+ hyperparameters or cases where n << d (like microarray data), along with analysis of computational scaling and performance degradation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical guarantee for the transfer of optimal hyperparameters from subsampled data to the full dataset, particularly when the subsampling scheme is non-uniform (e.g., leverage score sampling)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "the optimal hyperparameters learned from the subsample can be transferred back to the large datasets with good visualization" but acknowledge this is heuristic, and mention "graph-theoretic guarantees (Wilkinson et al., 2005)" as future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides empirical evidence of successful transfer but doesn't establish theoretical bounds or conditions under which this transfer would fail, particularly for non-uniform sampling schemes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis proving conditions under which hyperparameters optimized on subsampled data will be optimal (or near-optimal) for full data, or empirical studies systematically varying subsampling schemes and dataset characteristics to identify failure modes.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is the proposed framework to the choice of performance metrics and their combinations in multi-objective optimization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss multi-objective trade-offs and Pareto optimization, noting that "conventional single-objective optimization techniques are insufficient due to conflicting objectives" and that different metrics may have different optimal hyperparameters (e.g., optimal perplexity for AUC vs Q-global in the unbalanced 2-cluster dataset).
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the framework with several metrics, it doesn't systematically explore how metric choice affects results, whether certain metric combinations are more stable or reliable, or how to automatically select appropriate metric combinations for different data types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of hyperparameter tuning results across different metric combinations, analysis of metric stability and correlation properties, and guidelines for selecting appropriate metric sets based on data characteristics.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of subsampling-based hyperparameter transfer may degrade for datasets with complex, non-uniform structures or heavy-tailed distributions
- Computational savings depend heavily on the specific dimensionality reduction algorithm and dataset characteristics, which may vary significantly in practice
- The framework's generalizability to other dimensionality reduction algorithms beyond t-SNE and UMAP requires further validation

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The core methodology of combining Bayesian optimization with subsampling for efficient hyperparameter selection is well-established and theoretically sound
- **Medium Confidence:** The specific implementation details and hyperparameter tuning strategies for t-SNE and UMAP are demonstrated to work on tested datasets but may require adaptation for other DR algorithms
- **Medium Confidence:** The effectiveness of multiple-initialization averaging in reducing metric variance is supported by experimental evidence but may not fully eliminate initialization sensitivity
