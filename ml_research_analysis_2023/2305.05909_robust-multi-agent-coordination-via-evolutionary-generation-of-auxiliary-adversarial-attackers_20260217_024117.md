# Robust multi-agent coordination via evolutionary generation of auxiliary adversarial attackers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.05909
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05909
- **Reference count:** 13
- **Key outcome:** ROMANCE improves robustness of MARL policies against policy perturbations using evolutionary generated adversarial attackers, achieving comparable or better performance than baselines across SMAC scenarios.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical vulnerability of cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) policies to unpredictable policy perturbations during testing, particularly malicious action attacks. The authors propose a novel framework called ROMANCE that formulates the problem as a Limited Policy Adversary Dec-POMDP (LPA-Dec-POMDP) and uses evolutionary generation of auxiliary adversarial attackers to improve robustness. The ego-system is trained against a diverse population of attackers optimized to minimize rewards while promoting behavior diversity, resulting in policies that maintain high performance under various attack conditions.

## Method Summary
ROMANCE introduces a novel adversarial training framework for MARL that addresses policy perturbation vulnerabilities through evolutionary generation of auxiliary attackers. The approach formulates the problem as an LPA-Dec-POMDP where some agents may suffer from malicious action attacks. A diverse population of attackers is maintained and evolved using Quality-Diversity optimization to minimize the ego-system's reward while promoting behavioral diversity. The ego-system is then trained against these evolving attackers using alternating optimization. The framework integrates with value-based MARL algorithms like QMIX, VDN, and QPLEX, and is evaluated across multiple SMAC scenarios with varying attack conditions.

## Key Results
- ROMANCE achieves comparable or better win rates than baseline methods under natural and attack conditions across multiple SMAC maps.
- The method demonstrates improved robustness to different numbers of attacks during testing compared to standard MARL approaches.
- Performance is competitive with or exceeds improved robustness baselines across various SMAC scenarios including 2s3z, 3m, 3s vs 3z, 8m, MMM, and 1c3s5z.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ROMANCE works by creating a robust training environment through evolutionary generation of diverse adversarial attackers. The quality-diversity optimization ensures the attacker population maintains both effectiveness (quality) and behavioral diversity, preventing the ego-system from overfitting to specific attack patterns. By training the ego-system against this evolving population, the method develops policies that can handle various policy perturbations and maintain coordination under attack conditions.

**Critical Path:** The ego-system (MARL policy) trains against the attacker population, with attacker policies evolving through quality-diversity optimization to maintain diversity and effectiveness.

**Design Tradeoffs:** Balancing attacker quality vs. diversity through hyperparameter α; computational cost of maintaining evolving attacker population vs. improved robustness; integration complexity with different MARL algorithms.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How does ROMANCE's performance scale with increasing numbers of agents and complexity of the environment beyond the tested SMAC scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper tests ROMANCE on several SMAC maps but doesn't explore scalability to more complex or larger environments.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to a specific set of SMAC scenarios, leaving questions about performance in more complex, real-world settings with many agents.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing ROMANCE on more complex multi-agent environments with larger numbers of agents and more intricate coordination requirements, comparing its performance to other robust MARL methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can ROMANCE be effectively extended to handle non-adversarial forms of policy perturbation, such as communication delays or partial observability changes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on action-based adversarial attacks but doesn't address other forms of policy perturbation.
- Why unresolved: The problem formulation and solution are tailored to action perturbations, leaving open the question of adaptability to other types of disturbances.
- What evidence would resolve it: Modifying ROMANCE to handle different types of perturbations (e.g., communication delays, observation noise) and evaluating its performance in these new settings.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between the quality and diversity objectives in the attacker population, and how does this balance affect the robustness of the trained policy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a hyperparameter α to balance quality and diversity but notes that its sensitivity varies across scenarios.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides some analysis of α's sensitivity, the optimal balance may depend on the specific environment and task, and a more systematic study is needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive sensitivity analysis of α across a wider range of environments and tasks, potentially with automated methods for setting α based on the problem characteristics.

## Limitations
- Computational overhead from maintaining and evolving a diverse attacker population
- Potential overfitting to specific attack patterns despite diversity-promoting mechanisms
- Dependency on hyperparameters for balancing quality and diversity objectives
- Scalability challenges with larger team sizes or more complex environments
