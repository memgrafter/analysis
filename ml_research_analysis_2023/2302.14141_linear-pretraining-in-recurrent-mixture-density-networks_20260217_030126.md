# Linear pretraining in recurrent mixture density networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14141
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14141
- Reference count: 2
- Pretraining method improves RMDN convergence and prevents NaN values

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a pretraining method for recurrent mixture density networks (RMDN) to address convergence issues and NaN values during training. The method involves pretraining the linear nodes in the hidden layer of the RMDN before including non-linear node updates. This approach improves the model's robustness and ensures it surpasses the performance of the GARCH model, which is the RMDN's linear counterpart. The pretraining method is evaluated on daily returns of 10 stocks from the S&P 500 universe, showing that pretraining leads to better log-likelihoods compared to the GARCH model in most cases.

## Method Summary
The method involves training only the linear nodes in the hidden layer of the RMDN for 20 epochs before including non-linear node updates. The model is implemented in PyTorch and trained using the Adam optimizer for 300 epochs total. Parameters are initialized randomly, except for the output node of the variance recurrent network and the biases of all networks, which are set to 1. The approach is evaluated on daily returns of 10 S&P 500 stocks from September 20, 2017 to September 10, 2021.

## Key Results
- Pretraining method resolves NaN values during RMDN training
- Improves convergence and prevents bad local minima
- Achieves better log-likelihoods compared to GARCH model in most cases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Linear pretraining stabilizes RMDN training by first optimizing the linear submodel that nests within the full non-linear model.
- Mechanism: The RMDN includes a linear component (AR(1)-GARCH(1,1)) that is a special case when all non-linear nodes are disabled. By freezing non-linear nodes and training only linear nodes for several epochs, the model first learns parameters close to the optimal linear submodel. This provides a better initialization for the subsequent full non-linear training phase.
- Core assumption: The optimal parameters for the linear submodel provide a better starting point than random initialization for finding a good local minimum of the full RMDN.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The intuition behind our approach is that the parameters of a linear ELU-RMDN should be a good starting point for the parameters of a non-linear ELU-RMDN."
  - [abstract] "Such procedures allow approaching the predictive behavior of the GARCH model in early training stages and thus to surpass it in later training iterations."
- Break condition: If the linear submodel is not a good approximation of the data, or if the optimization landscape changes significantly between linear and non-linear phases, the pretraining may not provide benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Linear pretraining prevents the persistent NaN problem by avoiding extreme parameter values during early training.
- Mechanism: Mixture density networks are prone to NaN values when parameter updates cause numerical instability, particularly in the variance estimates. By first training with only linear nodes, the model avoids these instabilities. The ELU activation with positive shift ensures variance estimates remain positive, and the controlled linear phase prevents early explosions.
- Core assumption: The NaN problem occurs primarily during early non-linear updates, and a stable linear phase prevents this.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The pretraining method proposed resolves these issues by training the linear nodes in the hidden layer of the RMDN before starting including non-linear node updates."
  - [section] "Such problem consists in frequently obtaining 'Not a number' (NaN) values during training."
- Break condition: If NaN issues are caused by factors unrelated to initialization (e.g., learning rate, data scaling), pretraining may not fully resolve them.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Linear pretraining improves log-likelihood by ensuring the model can at least match the performance of its linear counterpart.
- Mechanism: Since the RMDN includes the GARCH model as a special case, the optimal RMDN should always have log-likelihood at least as good as GARCH. However, without pretraining, the model often converges to solutions worse than GARCH, indicating poor optimization. Linear pretraining ensures the model starts from a state that can at least match GARCH, then improves from there.
- Core assumption: The relationship between RMDN and GARCH is such that any valid RMDN solution should outperform GARCH, and poor convergence indicates getting stuck in bad local minima.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "A consequence of this relationship between the GARCH and the ELU-RMDN is that the optimized log-likelihood of the ELU-RMDN should always be greater or equal to that of a AR-GARCH."
  - [section] "However, in early experiment the ELU-RMDN did not always converge to a minimum with a log-likelihood greater to that of the GARCH."
- Break condition: If the data is not well-modeled by either GARCH or the RMDN family, this mechanism may not provide benefits.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to 10 S&P 500 stocks over a specific 4-year period
- Does not compare pretraining to other initialization or regularization techniques
- Does not quantify computational overhead introduced by the pretraining phase

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The mechanism by which linear pretraining prevents NaN values (Mechanism 2) is well-supported by the paper's empirical observations and the mathematical properties of ELU activation.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that pretraining ensures RMDN performance exceeds GARCH (Mechanism 3) is supported by the experimental results but could benefit from more rigorous statistical testing across different market regimes.

**Medium confidence**: The optimization landscape argument (Mechanism 1) is intuitively sound but not empirically verified through ablation studies that would isolate the pretraining effect from other factors.
