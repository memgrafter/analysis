# Improving Customer Experience in Call Centers with Intelligent Customer-Agent Pairing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08594
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08594
- Reference count: 21
- This work formulated the customer-agent pairing problem in a large call center as a machine learning task, predicting whether a caller seeks a specific service or other services. The proposed method bypasses the interactive voice response stage for correct predictions, reducing average call duration.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the customer-agent pairing problem in large call centers by formulating it as a machine learning task. The proposed approach predicts whether a caller seeks a specific service (SERVICE_A) or other services, enabling the system to bypass the interactive voice response (IVR) stage for correct predictions. By forwarding callers directly to the appropriate department queue when predictions are confident, the method reduces average call duration and improves customer experience.

The authors evaluate multiple machine learning models including Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, XGBoost, and MLP against a rule-based baseline. The MLP model demonstrates superior performance with an F1-GM score that is 4.5 times better than the rule-based method, translating to significant reductions in total waiting time. The work provides a practical framework for implementing intelligent routing in call centers while addressing the challenges of imbalanced classification.

## Method Summary
The method involves extracting customer features from demographics, profile, and interaction history, then engineering features like "number of previous calls in last three months for SERVICE_A." The dataset is split into training (162,563 unique callers), validation (56,317), and test (34,128) sets. Multiple ML models are trained and evaluated on the validation set using F1, GM, and F1-GM metrics. The best-performing model (MLP) is selected and compared against a rule-based method on the test set. The total waiting time reduction is calculated based on assumed stage durations: IVR interaction time (t_stage1), prediction verification time (t_stage_pred), and queue waiting time.

## Key Results
- The MLP model achieves 215% better performance than the rule-based baseline in combined F1-GM metric
- Total waiting time is reduced by over 22 hours compared to rules and over 7 hours compared to no prediction
- The method bypasses IVR stage for correct predictions, reducing average call duration
- F1 and GM-F1 scores are 4.5 and 2.15 times better than rule-based method

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The MLP model reduces total waiting time by predicting the caller's intent before the IVR stage.
- Mechanism: By bypassing the interactive voice response (IVR) stage for correct predictions, callers are forwarded directly to the appropriate department queue, saving time spent on keypad interactions.
- Core assumption: The average time for IVR interaction (t_stage1) is significantly larger than the prediction time (t_stage_pred), making the time saved from skipping IVR greater than the time spent on prediction and verification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed method bypasses the interactive voice response stage for correct predictions, reducing average call duration."
  - [section 3.2]: "The user will use the keypad to make their desired choice that fits most their enquiry... It is assumed that the average duration time of this stage is t_stage1."

- Break condition: If t_stage1 ≤ 2.38 * t_stage_pred, the average call duration would not decrease, and the model would not reduce waiting time.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The MLP model achieves higher accuracy than rule-based methods due to its ability to learn complex patterns from historical data.
- Mechanism: The MLP learns nonlinear relationships between customer features (demographics, profile, interaction history) and the target department, capturing subtle patterns that rule-based methods cannot.
- Core assumption: Historical data contains sufficient signal about caller intent that can be captured by the MLP model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "The MLP appears to be the best performing model based on the combined F1-GM metric; specifically, it produces the highest F1 and the second highest GM."
  - [section 5.2]: "The proposed learning-based method yields an F1 and GM-F1 scores which are 4.5 and 2.15 times better than the rule-based method."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence found.
- Break condition: If historical data is noisy or lacks discriminative features for caller intent, the MLP's performance advantage over rule-based methods would diminish.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combined F1-GM metric provides a balanced evaluation of the model's performance on imbalanced data.
- Mechanism: By taking the geometric mean of F1-score and Geometric Mean (GM), the metric ensures both high precision/recall and high true negative rate, which is crucial for imbalanced datasets where the majority class dominates.
- Core assumption: The dataset is imbalanced, with significantly more calls for services other than SERVIC_E_A than for SERVIC_E_A itself.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3]: "We adopt two widely accepted metrics which are less sensitive to imbalance; these are, F1-score (F1) and Geometric Mean (GM)."
  - [section 5.1]: "The MLP appears to be the best performing model based on the combined F1-GM metric."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence found.
- Break condition: If the dataset becomes balanced, simpler metrics like accuracy might suffice, and the combined F1-GM metric would be less critical.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data extraction: Collects demographics, customer profile, and interaction data.
  - Feature engineering: Processes raw data into engineered features for the model.
  - MLP model: Classifies calls into departments based on engineered features.
  - Prediction verification: Confirms the model's prediction with the caller before routing.
  - Routing system: Forwards the call to the appropriate department queue.

- Critical path: Caller → Data extraction → Feature engineering → MLP prediction → Prediction verification → Routing to department queue

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model complexity vs. interpretability: MLP offers higher accuracy but is less interpretable than rule-based methods.
  - Prediction speed vs. accuracy: Faster predictions might sacrifice accuracy, affecting the overall waiting time reduction.
  - Data privacy vs. feature richness: Collecting detailed customer data improves predictions but raises privacy concerns.

- Failure signatures:
  - High false positive rate: Callers are incorrectly routed, leading to longer calls as they are transferred to the correct department.
  - High false negative rate: Callers are routed to the wrong department, requiring them to repeat the IVR process.
  - Model degradation over time: Changes in caller behavior or service offerings might reduce the model's accuracy, necessitating retraining.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare the average call duration with and without the MLP prediction on a small subset of calls to validate the time-saving mechanism.
  2. Evaluate the model's performance on different time periods to check for temporal degradation or concept drift.
  3. Test the model's sensitivity to feature engineering choices by comparing performance with different feature sets.

## Limitations
- Data dependency: The model's performance heavily relies on the quality and representativeness of historical call data, with no clear strategy for handling data drift.
- Feature engineering opacity: The paper doesn't provide complete details on the feature engineering process or the final feature set, making exact reproduction challenging.
- Rule-based baseline ambiguity: Specific implementation details of the rule-based method are not fully specified, limiting direct comparison.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The core mechanism of using MLP for intent prediction and bypassing IVR for correct predictions is technically sound and well-supported by experimental results.
- Medium Confidence: The claimed waiting time reduction appears mathematically sound based on stated stage durations, but depends on unverified assumptions about these durations.
- Low Confidence: The generalizability of results to different call center environments, customer demographics, and service types remains uncertain without additional validation.

## Next Checks

3. **Sensitivity analysis of stage durations**: Conduct empirical measurement of actual stage durations (IVR interaction time, prediction verification time, queue waiting time) across different call types and times to validate the assumptions used in calculating total waiting time reduction.
