# Coping with low data availability for social media crisis message categorisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17211
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17211
- Reference count: 0

## Executive Summary
This thesis tackles the critical challenge of categorising social media crisis messages when labelled data is scarce. The work proposes three distinct research scenarios - crisis domain adaptation, few-shot learning, and zero-shot learning - each addressing different aspects of the data scarcity problem. Through innovative use of pre-trained language models, text augmentation techniques, and pseudo-labelling strategies, the research demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple crisis communication tasks.

The proposed methods show strong generalisation capabilities, with performance improvements that could significantly enhance emergency response operations. By enabling more accurate and timely categorisation of crisis-related social media messages, these approaches have the potential to improve humanitarian aid delivery and resource allocation during emergency situations.

## Method Summary
The thesis develops three complementary approaches to address low data availability in crisis message categorisation. For domain adaptation, it employs multi-task learning with pre-trained language models to transfer knowledge from past crisis events to ongoing situations. The CAST method leverages sequence-to-sequence models incorporating event information for more effective adaptation. For few-shot learning, the STA and ISA approaches use self-controlled text augmentation to generate high-quality synthetic data. The P-ZSC method addresses zero-shot learning by generating pseudo-labelled data to enable categorisation without any labelled examples. These methods collectively provide a comprehensive framework for crisis message classification under various data constraints.

## Key Results
- Multi-task learning approach for crisis domain adaptation outperforms baselines using pre-trained language models
- CAST method achieves strong performance in both many-to-one and one-to-one domain adaptation scenarios
- Self-controlled augmentation approaches (STA and ISA) significantly improve few-shot learning classification performance
- P-ZSC method effectively pseudo-labels unlabelled data and outperforms other zero-shot approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The methods work by leveraging the semantic richness of pre-trained language models and strategically addressing data scarcity through different learning paradigms. Multi-task learning exploits commonalities across crisis events to improve generalisation. Text augmentation techniques generate realistic synthetic examples that preserve the characteristics of crisis communication. Pseudo-labelling creates bridge data that enables learning from unlabelled sources. These approaches collectively overcome the fundamental challenge of limited labelled data while maintaining the specificity required for effective crisis response.

## Foundational Learning

**Crisis Domain Adaptation**: Transferring knowledge from past events to new, similar events
- Why needed: Past crisis data is abundant but current event data is scarce
- Quick check: Compare performance on target domain with and without adaptation

**Few-shot Learning**: Building models with minimal labelled examples
- Why needed: Initial response periods often have very limited labelled data
- Quick check: Evaluate performance as labelled examples increase from 5 to 50

**Zero-shot Learning**: Categorising without any labelled examples
- Why needed: Some crisis situations may have no available labelled data
- Quick check: Test on completely new crisis types with no prior examples

**Text Augmentation**: Generating synthetic training data
- Why needed: Natural crisis data is limited and expensive to label
- Quick check: Compare augmented vs original training data performance
