# FedCBO: Reaching Group Consensus in Clustered Federated Learning through Consensus-based Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02894
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02894
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: FedCBO achieves 96.51% ± 0.04% accuracy on rotated MNIST while identifying clusters without prior knowledge

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FedCBO, a novel algorithm for clustered federated learning that leverages consensus-based optimization to simultaneously optimize multiple objective functions without prior knowledge of cluster identities. The method uses interacting particles to find global minimizers of loss functions corresponding to different clusters, achieving this through a mean-field limit analysis and convergence guarantees. Experimental results on rotated MNIST demonstrate FedCBO's effectiveness, outperforming baselines like IFCA and FedAvg while successfully identifying agents with similar data distributions through its local aggregation scheme.

## Method Summary
FedCBO operates by employing interacting particles in a consensus-based optimization framework to simultaneously optimize multiple objective functions corresponding to different data clusters in federated learning. The algorithm uses a mean-field limit analysis to establish convergence guarantees, allowing particles to converge to the global minimizers of each cluster's loss function without prior knowledge of cluster identities. Through local aggregation and consensus formation among particles, FedCBO can identify agents with similar data distributions while optimizing their respective models, effectively addressing the challenge of unknown cluster structures in federated learning scenarios.

## Key Results
- Achieves 96.51% ± 0.04% accuracy on rotated MNIST dataset
- Outperforms baselines IFCA and FedAvg in clustered federated learning settings
- Successfully identifies agents with similar data distributions through local aggregation scheme without prior cluster knowledge

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
FedCBO works by using interacting particles that simulate consensus-based optimization to find global minimizers of multiple loss functions simultaneously. The particles interact and reach consensus on their trajectories, effectively grouping themselves according to the underlying data clusters without explicit cluster labels. This approach leverages the mean-field limit to ensure convergence, where the particle system's behavior approximates a continuous distribution that converges to the global optima of each cluster's objective function.

## Limitations
- Theoretical convergence analysis relies on mean-field limit approximations that may not hold in practical finite-particle regimes
- Experimental validation limited to single dataset (rotated MNIST), raising questions about generalizability to complex real-world scenarios
- Privacy implications of consensus-based optimization approach not addressed, potentially leaking information about cluster membership
