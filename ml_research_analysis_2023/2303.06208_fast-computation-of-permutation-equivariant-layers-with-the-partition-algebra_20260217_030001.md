# Fast computation of permutation equivariant layers with the partition algebra

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06208
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06208
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: New basis for permutation equivariant layers using Kronecker products that enables faster multiplication than orbit basis methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a computationally efficient basis for permutation equivariant linear layers in neural networks using the partition algebra framework. The authors demonstrate that their Kronecker product construction allows for faster multiplication operations compared to the orbit basis used in previous work. The proposed basis spans the space of permutation equivariant linear layers and recovers the sum/transfer/broadcast operations described in related research, offering a theoretically sound and practically efficient alternative for implementing permutation equivariant operations.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a new basis construction for permutation equivariant layers that leverages the partition algebra and Kronecker products. This approach constructs a basis that spans the space of permutation equivariant linear layers, with the key innovation being an algorithm for efficient multiplication operations. The method builds upon existing work in permutation equivariant neural networks but replaces the orbit basis with a partition algebra framework, resulting in computational advantages while maintaining the same theoretical coverage of permutation equivariant operations.

## Key Results
- The new basis spans the space of permutation equivariant linear layers
- Kronecker product construction enables faster multiplication than orbit basis methods
- The basis recovers sum/transfer/broadcast operations from related work

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The partition algebra framework provides a natural mathematical structure for representing permutation equivariant operations. By using Kronecker products in the basis construction, the authors exploit the tensor product structure inherent in permutation equivariant layers, which allows for more efficient computation of the linear transformations. The partition algebra captures the combinatorial structure of how elements can be grouped and related under permutations, which directly maps to the symmetries that permutation equivariant layers must preserve.
