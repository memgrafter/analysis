# Who breaks early, looses: goal oriented training of deep neural networks based on port Hamiltonian dynamics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07070
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07070
- Reference count: 38
- Key outcome: Goal-oriented training with adaptive friction switching improves deep neural network performance by 0.5%+ on CIFAR10 and FashionMNIST

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of local minima in deep neural network training by introducing a goal-oriented optimization strategy based on port Hamiltonian dynamics. The method uses momentum-based optimization with adaptive friction control, switching from exploration to exploitation phases when a predefined reduction in loss function is achieved. Applied to standard deep learning problems using LeNet-5 architecture, the approach demonstrates consistent improvements in classification accuracy compared to standard stochastic gradient descent and fixed-parameter momentum methods, while also showing reduced overfitting issues.

## Method Summary
The core method involves interpreting the loss function as potential energy, momentum as kinetic energy, and friction as energy dissipation in a port Hamiltonian framework. Training begins with low friction for exploration, then switches to high friction after achieving a predefined loss reduction goal (65-90%). This adaptive friction control allows the optimization to first explore the loss landscape broadly and then exploit promising local minima. The approach is implemented using standard LeNet-5 architecture without data augmentation, with learning rates of 0.01-0.1, mass parameters of 0.001-100, and friction parameters of 0.001-10.

## Key Results
- Consistent accuracy improvements of around and above 0.5% across various parameter settings
- Better performance compared to standard SGD and fixed-parameter momentum methods
- Reduced overfitting issues with the goal-oriented friction switching approach

- Claim: Port Hamiltonian dynamics with adaptive friction switching from exploration to exploitation improves deep neural network training
- Mechanism: The loss function is interpreted as potential energy, momentum as kinetic energy, and friction as energy dissipation. Low friction enables exploration by maintaining momentum, while high friction enables exploitation by damping momentum and forcing convergence to local minima
- Core assumption: The loss landscape can be effectively navigated by controlling friction based on predefined reduction goals

- Break condition: Predefined reduction of initial loss function (65-90%) triggers friction increase

- Claim: Goal-oriented training with friction switching reduces overfitting compared to standard momentum methods
- Mechanism: By starting with low friction for exploration and switching to high friction after reaching a loss reduction goal, the method avoids prolonged exploration that can lead to overfitting while still finding better local minima
- Core assumption: Overfitting is associated with excessive exploration rather than exploitation of local minima
- Evidence anchors: Weak evidence - no direct corpus citations for overfitting reduction in momentum-based training
- Break condition: Achievement of predefined loss reduction goal triggers switch from exploration to exploitation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the proposed goal-oriented PHS optimization strategy perform on more complex neural network architectures beyond the LeNet-5 based architecture used in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that they chose LeNet-5 for simplicity and to focus on training exclusively, while acknowledging that more sophisticated architectures like ResNet or Transformer exist
- Why unresolved: The paper deliberately uses a simpler architecture to isolate the effects of the optimization strategy, leaving performance on modern architectures untested
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of the goal-oriented PHS method on state-of-the-art architectures (ResNet, Transformer) with various datasets, comparing performance against standard optimizers

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for adjusting the friction parameter after the initial goal-oriented phase, and how does this affect long-term convergence and generalization?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors suggest that "after a first exploitation phase, a re-acceleration could be executed" and mention this as a direction for future research, indicating uncertainty about optimal post-goal strategies
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the initial transition from exploration to exploitation but does not explore subsequent optimization phases or adaptive friction adjustments
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing different post-goal friction adjustment strategies (gradual vs. immediate changes, periodic re-acceleration) on various datasets and architectures, measuring both convergence speed and final generalization performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is the goal-oriented PHS method to the choice of the predefined loss reduction threshold, and is there an optimal way to determine this threshold?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors test various thresholds (65% to 90% reduction) but do not provide a systematic method for choosing the optimal threshold, noting that this parameter selection affects performance
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that the method works with different thresholds, it does not establish guidelines for selecting the most effective threshold for a given problem or architecture
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study analyzing the relationship between threshold selection and performance across diverse datasets and architectures, potentially developing a principled method for threshold determination based on problem characteristics

## Limitations

- The theoretical foundation connecting port Hamiltonian dynamics to deep learning optimization lacks empirical validation and direct corpus citations
- Results are limited to relatively simple architectures (LeNet-5) and datasets (CIFAR10, FashionMNIST), reducing generalizability to modern networks
- The method requires careful hyperparameter tuning of friction parameters and loss reduction thresholds, with no systematic approach provided for optimal selection

## Confidence

- Port Hamiltonian interpretation framework: Low - conceptual analogy without empirical validation
- 0.5% accuracy improvement claims: Medium - consistent improvements shown but on limited architectures
- Overfitting reduction mechanism: Low - observational claims without rigorous statistical validation
