# On Frequency-Wise Normalizations for Better Recording Device Generalization in Audio Spectrogram Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11764
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11764
- Reference count: 23
- Key outcome: Frequency-centering operation improves ASC performance on unseen recording devices by up to 18.2 percentage points

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of acoustic scene classification (ASC) when there is a mismatch in recording devices between training and testing data. The authors investigate how recording device characteristics are encoded in the hidden layer activations of audio spectrogram transformers (ASTs) and propose a frequency-centering operation for spectrograms to remove these device-specific characteristics. They find that most recording device information is initially encoded in the frequency dimension of ASTs but is largely transformed into the token dimension after the first self-attention block. Based on this observation, they conjecture that suppressing recording device characteristics in the input spectrogram is the most effective.

## Method Summary
The proposed method applies frequency-wise centering to the input spectrogram by subtracting the mean frequency magnitude across time for each frequency bin. This operation reduces device-specific frequency bias before the spectrogram enters the transformer layers. A softened version (SFC) uses a convex combination λ·FC(x) + (1-λ)·x to balance device invariance with acoustic scene preservation. The method is evaluated on the TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2020 dataset with ASTs pretrained using Masked Spectrogram Modeling on AudioSet, then fine-tuned on the target dataset with SFC applied at the input level.

## Key Results
- Frequency-centering operation improves ASC performance on unseen recording devices by up to 18.2 percentage points
- SFC with λ=0.9 provides optimal balance between device invariance and scene preservation
- Normalizing only the input spectrogram yields the highest improvements on unseen devices
- The proposed method outperforms baselines without domain adaptation techniques

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Frequency-wise centering of the input spectrogram removes recording device characteristics early, before self-attention layers transform them into token dimensions.
- Mechanism: The proposed frequency-centering operation (FC) subtracts the mean frequency magnitude across time for each frequency bin. This reduces the device-specific frequency bias present in the spectrogram before it enters the transformer layers.
- Core assumption: Most recording device information is initially encoded in the frequency dimension and can be suppressed without harming acoustic scene content.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The authors conjecture that suppressing recording device characteristics in the input spectrogram is the most effective.
  - [section II] "recording device information is initially encoded in the frequency dimension; however, after the first self-attention block, it is largely transformed into the token dimension."
  - [corpus] Weak alignment - no corpus paper explicitly discusses transformer-based frequency centering for ASC, though device variability in audio is a known challenge.
- Break condition: If device information is also heavily encoded in the time dimension or if acoustic scene cues are correlated with device-specific frequency biases, centering could degrade classification performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Softened frequency-centering (SFC) balances device invariance with acoustic scene preservation by mixing original and centered activations.
- Mechanism: SFC uses a convex combination λ·FC(x) + (1-λ)·x to relax the centering operation. This allows partial suppression of device characteristics while retaining some original frequency content.
- Core assumption: The mean over frequency is more predictive of device identity than standard deviation, and a softened approach can reduce device influence without completely removing it.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II] "the mean over the frequency dimension predicts the recording device and the acoustic scene better than the standard deviation. Therefore, we conjecture that centering the spectrogram instead of both centering and whitening will be sufficient."
  - [section IV-E] Grid search over λ in {0.0, 0.1, ..., 1.0} and best results at λ=0.9 for SFC.
  - [corpus] Weak alignment - no corpus paper describes softened normalization specifically, but general domain adaptation techniques use similar interpolation strategies.
- Break condition: If λ is set too high, acoustic scene information correlated with device characteristics may be overly suppressed, harming performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Frequency-wise centering is more effective when applied only to the input spectrogram rather than to hidden activations.
- Mechanism: The self-attention layers transform device information from the frequency dimension into the token dimension after the first block. Normalizing hidden activations post-transformation has minimal impact.
- Core assumption: The first self-attention block disperses frequency-wise device information into token dimensions, making later frequency normalization less effective.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II] "unlike in CNNs, the amount of device information in the frequency statistics drops significantly after the first self-attention block."
  - [section IV-E] "normalizing only the input spectrogram yields the highest improvements on the unseen devices."
  - [corpus] Weak alignment - no corpus paper explicitly discusses when to apply normalization in transformers, though the global nature of self-attention is well-known.
- Break condition: If the self-attention mechanism preserves frequency-wise device information longer than observed, later normalization could still be beneficial.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input spectrogram → Patch extraction (16x16) → Linear projection to tokens → Positional encoding → Self-attention blocks (12 encoder) → Classification head. SFC is applied after positional encoding but before the first self-attention block.
- Critical path: Spectrogram preprocessing → SFC normalization → Patch extraction → Token embedding → Self-attention encoding → Classification. SFC is the key intervention point.
- Design tradeoffs: SFC reduces device variability but requires careful tuning of λ to avoid losing acoustic scene information. Applying SFC only to input simplifies implementation but may miss device characteristics in some cases.
- Failure signatures: Performance drops on seen devices indicate over-suppression of acoustic scene cues. Poor generalization to unseen devices suggests SFC is not removing enough device information.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement SFC with λ=0.9 and compare ASC accuracy on seen vs. unseen devices against baseline.
  2. Sweep λ from 0.0 to 1.0 to find optimal balance between device invariance and scene preservation.
  3. Compare SFC applied to input only vs. SFC applied to input and all hidden layers to validate the hypothesis about self-attention transformation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of frequency-wise normalization strategies vary across different audio domains (e.g., speech, music, general sounds)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that ASTs have outperformed CNNs on a broad variety of tasks and acoustic domains, but the study focuses specifically on acoustic scene classification.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide comparative analysis of frequency-wise normalization strategies across different audio domains, which would help determine their generalizability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with frequency-wise normalization on ASTs across various audio domains (speech, music, general sounds) and comparing the results would provide insights into the strategy's effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal degree of relaxation (λ) for frequency-wise normalization that balances performance on seen and unseen recording devices?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that lower values of λ lead to small improvements on both seen and unseen devices, while higher values increase performance on unseen devices but decrease it on seen devices.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the optimal λ value that balances performance across both seen and unseen devices, which is crucial for practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a comprehensive grid search or optimization to find the λ value that maximizes overall performance across both seen and unseen devices would help identify the optimal relaxation degree.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does pre-training with normalized spectrograms affect the generalization ability of ASTs to unseen recording devices?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors find that pre-training with normalized spectrograms leads to a noticeable gain across unseen devices but with a less significant performance gain compared to training without normalization.
- Why unresolved: The study does not explore the long-term effects of pre-training with normalized spectrograms on the model's ability to generalize to new recording devices.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different pre-training strategies and evaluating the model's performance on a diverse set of unseen recording devices over multiple iterations would provide insights into the effectiveness of pre-training with normalized spectrograms.

## Limitations

- The core claims about frequency-wise centering effectiveness rest on empirical observations from a specific transformer architecture applied to one acoustic scene dataset.
- The self-attention analysis showing device information migration is observational rather than analytical, without theoretical guarantees about when this behavior holds.
- The proposed method requires careful tuning of λ, and the optimal value (0.9) may not generalize across different acoustic domains or model architectures.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The empirical finding that frequency-wise centering improves generalization to unseen devices on the TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2020 dataset.

**Medium confidence**: The mechanism explanation that self-attention transforms device information from frequency to token dimensions after the first block.

**Low confidence**: The claim that SFC is "the most effective" approach without comparison to other domain adaptation techniques like adversarial training or impulse response augmentation.

## Next Checks
