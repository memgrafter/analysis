# SenteCon: Leveraging Lexicons to Learn Human-Interpretable Language Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14728
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14728
- Reference count: 30
- Key outcome: SENTE CON produces interpretable language representations that correlate better with human annotations than traditional lexicons while achieving competitive or superior performance on downstream tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SENTE CON, a method for creating interpretable deep language representations by leveraging traditional lexicons. SENTE CON maps sentences to category relevance scores by computing similarities between sentence embeddings and category centroids in the embedding space. The method outperforms traditional lexicon-based approaches in both human interpretability and empirical performance across multiple datasets. SENTE CON+ extends this by adapting to specific domains using unlabeled reference text, further improving performance.

## Method Summary
SENTE CON creates interpretable language representations by first building a sentence embedding dictionary using pre-trained deep language model embeddings. For each category in a lexicon (like LIWC or Empath), it computes a centroid from the embeddings of words associated with that category. When processing new text, SENTE CON generates an embedding for the sentence and computes cosine similarity between this embedding and each category's centroid, producing interpretable relevance scores. SENTE CON+ improves upon this by using domain-specific sentences from a reference corpus to build category representations, allowing adaptation to different language patterns across domains.

## Key Results
- SENTE CON correlates better with human annotations than traditional lexicon methods (LIWC and Empath) on MELD dataset
- SENTE CON+ outperforms both traditional lexicons and SENTE CON on downstream tasks (SST, MELD, IMDb, MOSI)
- SENTE CON(+) representations achieve competitive performance with fine-tuned deep language models while maintaining interpretability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SENTE CON represents sentences as category centroids in the embedding space, allowing similarity-based scoring
- Mechanism: Uses pre-trained embeddings from a deep language model to compute centroids for each category, then computes cosine similarity between new sentence embeddings and these centroids to produce interpretable scores
- Core assumption: Word embeddings from pre-trained models capture meaningful sentence context, and averaging them approximates the category's centroid
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Given a passage of text, SENTE CON encodes the text as a layer of interpretable categories in which each dimension corresponds to the relevance of a specific category."
  - [section]: "SENTE CON efficiently approximates sentences for each category using the deep embeddings of the words L associates with that category."
- Break condition: If the language model's embeddings do not capture sentence context well or the averaging step loses important distinctions, the category representations will be poor

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SENTE CON+ improves performance by using domain-specific sentences to build category representations
- Mechanism: Maps sentences from a reference corpus to categories based on lexicon word matches, then uses their embeddings to build centroids tailored to the domain
- Core assumption: Language patterns differ across domains, so using domain-specific sentences better captures context-specific usage
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "SENTE CON+ allows our interpretable language representation to further adapt to a particular data domain using only unlabeled text from that domain."
  - [section]: "We can improve how well SENTE CON representations characterize the text in different settings by altering the method by which we construct the sentence embedding dictionary."
- Break condition: If the reference corpus is too small or not representative of the domain, SENTE CON+ will not outperform SENTE CON

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SENTE CON(+) can distinguish word senses based on sentence context
- Mechanism: When a word has multiple meanings, the contextualized embedding of the word in a sentence will be more similar to the embeddings of words matching its intended meaning than to those of its other meanings
- Core assumption: Pre-trained language models encode different word senses in distinct regions of the embedding space based on context
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The ability to distinguish word senses helps SENTE CON(+) to correctly identify relevant categories where traditional lexicons may be not be able to do so."
  - [section]: "When used in a particular sentence context, words with multiple meanings show significantly more similarity to their matching-sense definition than they do to their opposing-sense definition."
- Break condition: If the language model's embeddings do not effectively capture word sense distinctions, the similarity-based matching will fail

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Word embeddings and sentence embeddings
  - Why needed here: SENTE CON uses embeddings from a pre-trained language model to represent words and sentences in a high-dimensional space
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between a word embedding and a sentence embedding?

- Concept: Cosine similarity
  - Why needed here: SENTE CON uses cosine similarity to compute the relevance of each category to a new sentence based on the angle between their embeddings
  - Quick check question: What does a cosine similarity of 1, 0, and -1 represent?

- Concept: Centroid calculation
  - Why needed here: SENTE CON computes the centroid of embeddings for each category to create a representative point in the embedding space
  - Quick check question: How do you compute the centroid of a set of vectors?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Lexicon (LIWC or Empath) -> Deep language model (MPNet, BERT, etc.) -> Embedding dictionary builder -> Similarity scorer -> Output layer

- Critical path:
  1. Load lexicon and reference corpus (if using SENTE CON+)
  2. Generate embeddings for words/sentences using deep language model
  3. Compute centroids for each category
  4. For new sentence, generate embedding and compute similarities to all category centroids
  5. Return category relevance scores

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single centroid per category vs. multiple centroids: Single centroid is simpler but may miss subcategories; multiple centroids can capture more nuance but increase complexity
  - Pre-trained vs. fine-tuned language model: Pre-trained is more interpretable but may underperform; fine-tuned is less interpretable but may perform better
  - Choice of deep language model: Larger models may capture more context but are slower and more resource-intensive

- Failure signatures:
  - All category scores are very similar: May indicate poor separation in embedding space or ineffective centroid calculation
  - Category scores do not correlate with human annotations: May indicate the language model is not capturing relevant context or the lexicon is not appropriate for the domain
  - Performance is worse than lexicon baseline: May indicate the embedding averaging step is losing important information or the similarity computation is not effective

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate embeddings and compute centroids for a small lexicon and a few sentences, then manually inspect the similarities
  2. Compare SENTE CON category scores with human annotations on a small set of sentences to check interpretability
  3. Evaluate SENTE CON on a simple downstream task (e.g., sentiment classification) and compare with lexicon baseline

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of reference corpus domain impact SENTE CON+ performance compared to using no reference corpus at all?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that using any of the three new reference corpora (Switchboard, NYT, PubMed) yields worse performance than using a held-out set from MELD, and worse than not using a reference corpus at all.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores a limited set of reference corpus domains and doesn't investigate the impact of using a reference corpus from a completely unrelated domain.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experiment comparing SENTE CON+ performance using reference corpora from a wide range of domains, including both related and unrelated to the task dataset.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of centroids per category for SENTE CON(+)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the number of centroids per category can be viewed as a tunable hyperparameter and that small improvements are observed for SENTE CON on SST and MOSI with multiple centroids.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores a limited range of centroid numbers (1-4) and doesn't investigate the impact of using a larger number of centroids.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experiment comparing SENTE CON(+) performance across a wider range of centroid numbers, potentially including a very large number of centroids.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does fine-tuning Mθ on the target domain lead to a decrease in agreement with human evaluations for SENTE CON(+)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that when Mθ is fine-tuned on the target domain, SENTE CON(+) correlates much less strongly with human category ratings than the existing lexicon methods do.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a clear explanation for this phenomenon.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis of the differences between pre-trained and fine-tuned Mθ representations, potentially including an investigation of how fine-tuning affects the model's ability to capture sentence-level context and word sense.

## Limitations

- The method relies heavily on pre-trained language models' ability to capture sentence context and word senses, which may not generalize across all domains or languages
- Human interpretability evaluation is based on a single dataset (MELD) with relatively few raters per sentence (6), limiting generalizability
- The paper doesn't address potential biases in the lexicon word lists or how SENTE CON might perform with less common or domain-specific vocabulary

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: SENTE CON's ability to produce category relevance scores that correlate with human annotations better than traditional lexicons
- **Medium confidence**: SENTE CON+ improving performance through domain adaptation, as results are limited to specific datasets
- **Medium confidence**: SENTE CON's ability to distinguish word senses based on context, though the mechanism is theoretically sound
