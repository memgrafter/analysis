# Analyzing Effects of Fake Training Data on the Performance of Deep Learning Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01268
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01268
- Reference count: 26
- Key outcome: Synthetic data generated by cGANs improves model robustness on simple datasets when added in small proportions, with diminishing returns on complex datasets.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the impact of synthetic data on deep learning model performance, particularly for computer vision tasks. Synthetic data is generated using conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (cGANs) for datasets like MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR10. The research explores various ratios of synthetic to real data in training, evaluating model accuracy and robustness to out-of-distribution data. Key findings include improved model performance and robustness when a small proportion of synthetic data is added to the original dataset. For instance, in MNIST, adding synthetic data in a 1:5 ratio improved accuracy on corrupted test sets by up to 700%. However, the benefits were less pronounced for more complex datasets like CIFAR10. The study highlights the potential of synthetic data to enhance model robustness without requiring large amounts of real data, offering insights for future research in cost-effective neural network training.

## Method Summary
The study investigates the impact of synthetic data on deep learning model performance by generating synthetic images using conditional GANs for MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR10 datasets. Models are trained on various combinations of synthetic and real data with different ratios (e.g., 1:1, 1:2, 1:5), and their performance is evaluated on original test sets, synthetic test sets, and corrupted test sets (MNIST-C). The study aims to understand how synthetic data affects model accuracy and robustness to out-of-distribution data.

## Key Results
- Adding small proportions of synthetic data (e.g., 1:5 ratio) significantly improves model robustness to out-of-distribution data on simple datasets like MNIST and Fashion-MNIST.
- Models trained solely on synthetic data can generalize to real data better than models trained only on real data generalize to synthetic data.
- The effectiveness of synthetic data diminishes as dataset complexity increases, with less pronounced benefits observed for complex datasets like CIFAR10.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adding small amounts of synthetic data improves model robustness to out-of-distribution data without significantly harming accuracy on in-distribution data.
- Mechanism: Synthetic data introduces a slight distribution shift that regularizes the model, forcing it to learn features that generalize better across data variations.
- Core assumption: The synthetic data is generated in a way that preserves the underlying class semantics while introducing controlled perturbations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Key findings include improved model performance and robustness when a small proportion of synthetic data is added to the original dataset."
  - [section]: "We notice a significant increase in accuracy from the Original-Synthetic(1:1) to the Original-Synthetic(5:1) training-set category... adding just a small number of synthetic images to the original dataset (say in the 1:5 ratio) yields a dataset that is significantly more robust to out of distribution data."

- Break condition: If synthetic data generation introduces artifacts or biases that do not align with real data distribution, it could harm performance instead of improving robustness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Models trained solely on synthetic data can generalize to real data better than models trained only on real data generalize to synthetic data.
- Mechanism: Synthetic data generation via cGANs captures essential class features while potentially smoothing out noise and irrelevant variations present in real data, leading to more robust feature learning.
- Core assumption: The GAN-generated synthetic data maintains sufficient semantic similarity to real data while reducing overfitting to specific real-world noise patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We notice that on addition of just a small proportion of synthetic images to the original dataset, the model accuracy started improving... However the model still shows an impressive revival of accuracy on adding just a few instances of the synthetic data to the training set."
  - [section]: "We also make an important observation that adding only a small number of synthetic images to the original dataset... yields a dataset that is significantly more robust to out of distribution data."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence available for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the synthetic data generation process introduces significant distribution shifts that the model cannot bridge, generalization from synthetic to real data may fail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The effectiveness of synthetic data diminishes as dataset complexity increases (e.g., from MNIST to CIFAR10).
- Mechanism: Simple datasets have more uniform and less overlapping features across classes, making it easier for synthetic data to capture representative patterns. Complex datasets have more intricate feature distributions that are harder to synthesize accurately.
- Core assumption: The synthetic data generation process (cGAN/StyleGAN) can adequately capture the essential features of simpler datasets but struggles with the complexity and diversity of features in more complex datasets.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "However, the benefits were less pronounced for more complex datasets like CIFAR10."
  - [section]: "We also observe that though our results hold on simple dataset such as Fashion-MNIST and MNIST, they are not as striking on more complex datasets such as CIFAR10, where classes do not possess many overlapping features and are drastically different from one another."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence available for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If synthetic data generation techniques improve to better handle complex feature distributions, this mechanism may no longer hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data Generation -> Model Training -> Evaluation
  - cGAN/StyleGAN2-ADA -> Deep CNN architectures -> Accuracy on original, synthetic, and corrupted test sets

- Critical path:
  1. Generate synthetic data using cGAN/StyleGAN2-ADA
  2. Combine synthetic data with real data in various ratios
  3. Train deep CNN models on the mixed datasets
  4. Evaluate model performance on original, synthetic, and corrupted test sets

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using more synthetic data may improve robustness but could potentially harm performance on in-distribution data if the synthetic data is not representative enough.
  - More complex datasets (like CIFAR10) may require more sophisticated synthetic data generation techniques to achieve similar benefits as simpler datasets.

- Failure signatures:
  - If synthetic data introduces significant distribution shifts or artifacts, model performance on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution data may degrade.
  - If the model overfits to synthetic data, it may perform poorly on real test sets.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a model on only real data and evaluate its performance on synthetic and corrupted test sets to establish a baseline.
  2. Train a model on only synthetic data and evaluate its performance on real and corrupted test sets to assess the generalization capability of synthetic data.
  3. Train models on mixed datasets with varying ratios of synthetic to real data (e.g., 1:1, 1:2, 1:5) and evaluate their performance on all test sets to identify the optimal ratio for balancing accuracy and robustness.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of synthetic data is significantly reduced for complex datasets like CIFAR10, limiting the generalizability of the findings.
- The study lacks detailed implementation specifications for the GAN architectures and CNN models, which would be crucial for faithful reproduction.
- The paper doesn't extensively explore potential failure modes or provide thorough analysis of when synthetic data might actually harm model performance.

## Confidence
- Claims about synthetic data improving robustness on simple datasets: High
- Claims about reduced effectiveness on complex datasets: Medium
- Claims about synthetic-to-real generalization: Low (limited empirical support)
