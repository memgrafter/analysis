# QCNeXt: A Next-Generation Framework For Joint Multi-Agent Trajectory Prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper presents QCNeXt, a next-generation framework for joint multi-agent trajectory prediction in autonomous driving. The model uses a query-centric encoding paradigm with permutation equivariance, roto-translation invariance, and translation invariance properties. A multi-agent DETR-like decoder captures future social interactions among agents. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Argoverse 2 benchmark, ranking first and winning the championship of the Argoverse Challenge at CVPR 2023.

## Method Summary
QCNeXt extends QCNet by introducing a joint prediction framework that estimates the joint distribution of multiple target agents' future trajectories. The model employs a query-centric scene encoder that provides invariance properties for accurate multi-agent forecasting. A multi-agent DETR-like decoder generates joint trajectory proposals and refinements while capturing future social interactions. A scene scoring module aggregates agent embeddings into a single scene-level confidence score. The framework is trained using a combination of classification and trajectory prediction losses.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on Argoverse 2 multi-agent motion forecasting benchmark
- Ranks first and wins the championship of the Argoverse Challenge at CVPR 2023
- Outperforms marginal prediction models even on marginal metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Query-centric encoding provides permutation equivariance, roto-translation invariance, and translation invariance by building local spacetime coordinate systems for each scene element, making representations invariant to global coordinate shifts and rotations.

### Mechanism 2
The multi-agent DETR-like decoder captures future social interactions among agents through a recurrent anchor-free trajectory proposal module followed by an anchor-based refinement module, with row-wise self-attention explicitly modeling social interactions.

### Mechanism 3
The scene scoring module enables joint prediction by aggregating agent embeddings into a single scene-level score using attentive pooling to summarize post-refinement mode embeddings of all target agents.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Query-centric scene encoder -> Multi-agent DETR-like decoder (anchor-free proposal + anchor-based refinement) -> Scene scoring module (attentive pooling + MLP)

- **Critical path**:
  1. Scene elements are encoded in their local coordinate systems by the query-centric scene encoder.
  2. The multi-agent DETR-like decoder generates joint trajectory proposals and refinements, capturing future social interactions.
  3. The scene scoring module aggregates agent embeddings into a single scene-level score.
  4. The model is trained using the combined loss function.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Query-centric encoding vs. global encoding: Query-centric encoding provides invariance properties but may require more complex coordinate transformations.
  - Recurrent vs. non-recurrent trajectory generation: Recurrent generation allows for adaptive trajectory anchors but may be more computationally expensive.
  - Scene-level vs. agent-level scoring: Scene-level scoring captures joint predictions but may be less interpretable than agent-level scores.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - If the model fails to capture social interactions among agents, the joint predictions may not improve over marginal predictions.
  - If the scene scoring module fails to properly aggregate agent embeddings, the scene-level scores may be unreliable.
  - If the training objectives are not well-balanced, the model may overfit to certain aspects of the data.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify that the query-centric encoding provides the desired invariance properties by testing the model's performance on rotated and translated scenes.
  2. Compare the performance of the multi-agent DETR-like decoder with and without the row-wise self-attention module to assess the importance of modeling future social interactions.
  3. Evaluate the impact of different pooling methods (e.g., average pooling, max pooling, attentive pooling) in the scene scoring module on the model's overall performance.

## Limitations
- The exact mechanisms and robustness of the roto-translation invariance and translation invariance properties are not thoroughly validated under extreme transformations.
- The effectiveness of the scene scoring module in capturing complex joint distributions is assumed but not explicitly tested against alternative aggregation methods.
- The claim that QCNeXt outperforms marginal prediction models even on marginal metrics lacks a detailed ablation study to isolate the contributions of each component.

## Confidence
- **High**: The overall framework design and its core components (query-centric encoder, DETR-like decoder, scene scoring module) are well-established and contribute to the model's performance.
