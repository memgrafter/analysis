# Open World Classification with Adaptive Negative Samples

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05581
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05581
- Reference count: 26
- Improves open world classification accuracy on Banking (83.93%), CLINC (92.64%), and StackOverflow (90.88%) datasets with 25% known categories

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses open world classification by proposing an adaptive negative sampling (ANS) method that identifies unknown categories during inference without requiring open category data during training. The approach synthesizes negative samples near decision boundaries for each known category using gradient-based methods, and employs auxiliary one-versus-rest binary classifiers to effectively utilize these samples. The method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art approaches on three benchmark datasets, achieving accuracy improvements of 5.08, 3.11, and 1.42 percentage points respectively when known category proportion is 25%, 50%, and 75%.

## Method Summary
The method uses a two-stage approach: first training a C-way classifier on known categories using BERT as a feature extractor, then training auxiliary one-versus-rest binary classifiers for each known category. Adaptive negative sampling generates synthetic negative samples near decision boundaries using projected gradient ascent, which are combined with real negative samples to train the binary classifiers. During inference, a sample is classified as open if all binary classifiers predict negative; otherwise, the C-way classifier determines the known category. The approach requires no external datasets or prior knowledge of open categories.

## Key Results
- Achieves 83.93% accuracy on Banking dataset with 25% known categories
- Achieves 92.64% accuracy on CLINC dataset with 25% known categories  
- Achieves 90.88% accuracy on StackOverflow dataset with 25% known categories
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods by 5.08, 3.11, and 1.42 percentage points respectively

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Gradient-based synthesis generates negative samples near decision boundaries for each known category by sampling in a spherical shell around each known sample, avoiding other known samples. This exploits the assumption that high-dimensional data lies on low-dimensional manifolds, making Euclidean distance locally meaningful for identifying boundary regions.

### Mechanism 2
One-versus-rest binary classifiers better capture boundaries between known and open categories than (C+1)-way classifiers by creating separate binary classifiers for each known category. This approach handles heterogeneous open category samples more effectively than modeling them as a single "open" class.

### Mechanism 3
Projected gradient ascent finds the hardest synthetic negatives within the constraint region by starting with Gaussian noise around known samples and iteratively moving in the gradient direction to maximize classifier loss. The projection step ensures samples remain within the valid region near decision boundaries.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: BERT encoder (frozen first 10 layers) → Feature extractor fψenc → C-way classifier fψcls + C binary classifiers gθclsm → Classification output

- **Critical path**: Input → BERT encoder → Feature extraction → C-way classifier + C binary classifiers → Classification output. For open detection: If all binary classifiers output negative, classify as open

- **Design tradeoffs**: One-versus-rest approach adds C binary classifiers but improves open detection accuracy; adaptive negative sampling requires gradient computations but eliminates need for external data; fixed radius r=8 simplifies implementation but may not be optimal for all datasets

- **Failure signatures**: High false positive rate on known categories indicates binary classifiers too sensitive or radius r too small; high false negative rate on open categories indicates binary classifiers not discriminative enough or radius r too large; poor performance on specific datasets may indicate dataset-specific characteristics not captured by current parameters

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Implement basic C-way classifier on known categories only, measure baseline accuracy; 2) Add one-versus-rest binary classifiers without synthetic negatives, measure improvement; 3) Add adaptive negative sampling with fixed radius r=8, measure final performance improvement

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper identifies several areas for future work, including investigating the scalability of the ANS method to longer text sequences and larger datasets, exploring the impact of the radius r parameter on performance across different datasets and scenarios, and comparing the ANS method to other open-world classification approaches when external resources are available.

## Limitations

- Performance sensitive to choice of radius r parameter in adaptive negative sampling process
- Geometric assumption that high-dimensional data lies on low-dimensional manifolds lacks empirical validation for text data
- Limited ablation studies on individual components make it difficult to quantify specific contributions

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall methodology architecture (BERT encoder + C-way classifier + one-versus-rest binary classifiers) is well-established and the paper provides clear implementation details
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of adaptive negative sampling is demonstrated empirically but lacks ablation studies and theoretical justification for the specific geometric constraints used
- **Medium Confidence**: The comparative performance improvements are well-documented but the analysis doesn't explore failure cases or dataset-specific limitations
