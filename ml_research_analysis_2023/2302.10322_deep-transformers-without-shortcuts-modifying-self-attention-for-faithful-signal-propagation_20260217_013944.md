# Deep Transformers without Shortcuts: Modifying Self-attention for Faithful Signal Propagation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10322
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10322
- Reference count: 40
- This paper develops methods to train very deep transformers without skip connections or normalization layers by controlling signal propagation through attention mechanisms.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training deep transformers without skip connections or normalization layers, which typically suffer from rank collapse in self-attention layers. The authors develop three methods—Value-SkipInit, Uniform SPA (U-SPA), and Exponential SPA (E-SPA)—that control the evolution of kernel matrices across layers to preserve signal propagation. E-SPA performs best, matching standard transformers' performance after approximately 5× more iterations, and can match standard training speed when combined with skip connections.

## Method Summary
The paper tackles rank collapse in deep skipless transformers by carefully constructing attention matrices to preserve signal propagation. The methods use combinations of parameter initializations, bias matrices, and location-dependent rescaling. Value-SkipInit initializes attention matrices to identity while remaining trainable. U-SPA and E-SPA construct attention matrices from Cholesky factors of specific kernel matrix families with uniform or exponentially decaying off-diagonal elements. These methods ensure the product of attention matrices remains close to the identity matrix across layers, preventing rank collapse and enabling training of deep transformers without traditional shortcuts.

## Key Results
- Deep skipless transformers can be trained using Signal Preserving Attention methods
- E-SPA achieves competitive performance with standard transformers after ~5× more training iterations
- With normalized skip connections, E-SPA matches standard transformer training speed
- Methods validated on WikiText-103 and C4 datasets with depth up to 108 blocks
- E-SPA captures positional information while U-SPA provides simpler baseline

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Kernel Matrix Evolution Control
Controlling the evolution of kernel matrices prevents rank collapse by ensuring the product of attention matrices remains close to the identity matrix across layers. The authors construct attention matrices that satisfy algebraic constraints, allowing them to control kernel evolution without relying on skip connections or normalization layers.

Core assumption: Kernel matrix evolution in transformers can be analyzed and controlled through careful construction of attention matrices.

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract]: "Our methods use combinations of: 1) parameter initialisations, 2) bias matrices, and 3) location-dependent rescaling..."
- [section 3.1]: "We seek instead to tackle this issue directly... derive modifications from our insights to achieve faithful signal propagation in deep transformers..."
- [corpus]: Weak evidence. Related papers discuss rank collapse but lack specific prevention mechanisms.

Break condition: If algebraic constraints are violated or assumptions about kernel matrix evolution no longer hold, rank collapse may reoccur.

### Mechanism 2: Signal Preserving Attention Construction
SPA methods maintain well-behaved kernel matrices by constructing attention matrices from Cholesky factors of specific kernel matrix families. The authors define two families with controlled off-diagonal elements (uniform or exponentially decaying) and ensure elementwise non-negativity and lower triangular structure required for causal masked attention.

Core assumption: Cholesky decomposition can construct valid attention matrices preserving kernel matrix properties.

Evidence anchors:
- [section 3.2]: "We identify two such families... Uniform (U-SPA): Σl(ρl) = (1 − ρl)IT + ρl11⊤. 2. Exponential (E-SPA): (Σl(γl))i,j = exp(−γl|i − j|)."
- [section 3.3]: Construction details for causal masked attention with trainable parameters.
- [corpus]: Weak evidence. Related papers discuss kernel matrices but lack specific construction methods.

Break condition: If Cholesky decomposition cannot be computed or resulting matrices violate causal masked attention constraints, SPA methods fail.

### Mechanism 3: Identity Initialization Baseline
Value-SkipInit initializes attention matrices to identity while remaining trainable, providing a stable starting point for skipless transformers. The method modifies attention to include trainable parameters initialized to 1 and 0, so the attention matrix is the identity at initialization.

Core assumption: Identity initialization provides a stable starting point while trainable parameters allow adaptation during training.

Evidence anchors:
- [section 3.1]: "In Value-SkipInit, we modify the attention operation... to (αI + βA(X)) · V(X) with trainable parameters α and β that are initialised to 1 and 0 respectively."
- [section 3.1]: Connection to Delta-initialization for CNNs and modifications to GNNs.
- [corpus]: Weak evidence. Related papers discuss initialization but lack specific identity initialization methods.

Break condition: If trainable parameters don't allow sufficient adaptation or identity initialization isn't suitable for the task, Value-SkipInit may fail.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input sequence → Token embeddings → Transformer blocks (Attention + MLP) → Output logits
  - Attention block: Query, Key, Value projections → Attention matrix computation → Value matrix multiplication
  - MLP block: Linear layer → Activation function → Linear layer
  - SPA methods modify the attention matrix computation and initialization

- **Critical path**: 
  - Initialization: Set up token embeddings, transformer blocks, and SPA-specific parameters
  - Forward pass: Compute attention matrices using SPA methods, apply attention and MLP blocks
  - Loss computation: Calculate cross-entropy loss between output logits and target tokens
  - Backward pass: Compute gradients and update parameters using Adam optimizer

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - SPA methods vs. standard transformer: SPA methods remove skip connections and normalization layers, which may reduce training speed but enable deeper architectures
  - U-SPA vs. E-SPA: U-SPA has simpler kernel matrix family but no positional encoding, while E-SPA captures positional information but has more complex construction
  - Orthogonal vs. Gaussian initialization: Orthogonal initialization provides exact kernel matrix evolution but may be more computationally expensive

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Rank collapse: Output kernel matrix becomes rank-1, causing poor signal propagation and training instability
  - Vanishing/exploding gradients: Gradients become too small or large, preventing effective parameter updates
  - Numerical instability: Computations become unstable due to large or small values, causing NaN or inf errors

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal way to set the decay rates (ρl)l and (γl)l for U-SPA and E-SPA across different depths?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper mentions they tried different polynomial rates for U-SPA but found linear increase to work well, and for E-SPA they used a specific formula based on maintaining constant diagonal values.
- Why unresolved: The authors only provide empirical findings for specific decay rate choices without theoretical justification or exploration of the full hyperparameter space.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing different decay rate schedules across multiple datasets and model depths, measuring both training speed and final performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do E-SPA and U-SPA perform when combined with strong second-order optimizers like K-FAC or Shampoo?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The authors mention that "second order optimisers for transformers are not well established" and chose to demonstrate training loss gap closure through longer training instead of using second-order methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper deliberately avoids second-order optimizers to maintain focus on signal propagation methods, leaving open the question of potential synergistic effects.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison of E-SPA/U-SPA transformers trained with Adam versus K-FAC/Shampoo, measuring training speed and final performance.

## Limitations

- **Computational overhead**: The Cholesky decomposition and matrix exponential calculations in E-SPA may introduce computational overhead compared to standard attention, which is not quantified in the paper.
- **Empirical scope**: Results are demonstrated primarily on decoder-only transformers with specific architectures, leaving uncertainty about performance on encoder-decoder models or different attention mechanisms.
- **Theoretical completeness**: While the paper provides theoretical analysis of kernel matrix evolution, it doesn't fully characterize training dynamics throughout the entire training process or explain generalization performance.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core claim that rank collapse prevents training of deep skipless transformers is well-supported by theoretical analysis and empirical evidence.
- **Medium confidence**: The SPA methods (particularly E-SPA) show promising results on benchmark datasets, but the performance gap relative to standard transformers (5× more iterations) suggests the approach may not be fully competitive for all applications.
- **Low confidence**: The claim that SPA methods "highlight several intricacies specific to signal propagation in transformers" is somewhat vague and not thoroughly explored in the empirical validation.
