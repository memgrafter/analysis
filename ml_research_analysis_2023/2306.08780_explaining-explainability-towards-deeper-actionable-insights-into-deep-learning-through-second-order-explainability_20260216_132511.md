# Explaining Explainability: Towards Deeper Actionable Insights into Deep Learning through Second-order Explainability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08780
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08780
- Reference count: 12
- The paper introduces SOXAI to automatically identify visual biases in deep learning models by analyzing relationships between quantitative explanations at the dataset level

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces second-order explainable AI (SOXAI) as a method to automatically identify prevalent visual concepts and biases in deep learning models. Unlike first-order explainability methods that require manual review of instance-level explanations, SOXAI groups similar explanation embeddings using t-SNE to reveal patterns in model behavior. The method was validated on both a ResNet-50 classifier (chainsaw class) and a MaskRCNN segmentation model (drills), successfully identifying biases such as earmuffs and logs associated with chainsaws, and text logos with drills. By eliminating these identified biases from training, model performance improved, with the segmentation model's mAP increasing from 0.592 to 0.618 when the logo bias was masked.

## Method Summary
SOXAI generates first-order explanations (using GSInquire) for each image in a dataset, then creates embeddings by computing weighted averages of model feature maps using the explanation scores as weights. These embeddings are reduced to 50 dimensions using PCA, then further reduced to 2D using t-SNE for visualization. The resulting clusters reveal dataset-level patterns and biases that are invisible when examining individual explanations. Once biases are identified, they can be removed from the training set to improve model generalization and performance.

## Key Results
- SOXAI successfully identified earmuffs and logs as spurious associations with chainsaws in ResNet-50
- SOXAI detected text logo bias in drills for MaskRCNN segmentation model
- Model performance improved when identified biases were eliminated: MaskRCNN mAP increased from 0.592 to 0.618 after logo bias masking

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SOXAI groups similar explanation embeddings to reveal dataset-level biases that are invisible at the instance level
- Mechanism: First-order explanations generate quantitative attribution maps per image, converted to fixed-dimensional embeddings via weighted averaging of model feature maps. t-SNE then clusters these embeddings, grouping images with similar explanation patterns and exposing systematic biases.
- Core assumption: Explanation embeddings from similar visual concepts will have similar quantitative signatures that t-SNE can meaningfully cluster.
- Evidence anchors:
  - "SOXAI groups similar explanation embeddings using t-SNE to reveal patterns in model behavior"
  - "SOXAI seeks to automatically unveil these patterns through the analysis of the relationships between quantitative explanations"
- Break condition: If first-order explanations are noisy or inconsistent, the embeddings may not capture meaningful patterns, causing t-SNE to produce arbitrary clusters.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Eliminating identified biases from the training set improves model generalization
- Mechanism: Once biases are identified via SOXAI clustering, targeted removal or masking of these features during training forces the model to rely on more generalizable class-relevant features, reducing overfitting to spurious correlations.
- Core assumption: The identified biases are non-essential for correct classification/segmentation and their removal improves focus on core class features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - "eliminating irrelevant concepts from the training set based on actionable insights from SOXAI can enhance a model's performance"
  - "enhanced model performance may be achieved by better-targeted elimination of biases in future training"
- Break condition: If the "bias" features are actually important cues for the class, removing them could hurt performance or cause catastrophic forgetting.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SOXAI automates bias discovery, avoiding time-consuming and biased manual review
- Mechanism: Instead of inspecting individual explanation maps for thousands of images, SOXAI aggregates and clusters all explanations to provide a dataset-level summary of model behavior in one visualization.
- Core assumption: Human reviewers cannot efficiently or reliably identify systematic biases by inspecting individual instances.
- Evidence anchors:
  - "reviewing these visualizations can be time-consuming... SOXAI automates the analysis of the connections between quantitative explanations and dataset biases"
  - "This was accomplished without the need to manually inspect each example in the validation set"
- Break condition: If clustering is not interpretable or too granular, it may still require manual review, negating the automation benefit.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: t-SNE dimensionality reduction and clustering
  - Why needed here: t-SNE maps high-dimensional explanation embeddings into 2D space for visualization, enabling humans to see groupings of similar explanations.
  - Quick check question: What does t-SNE preserve when reducing dimensions—local structure or global distances?

- Concept: Gradient-based attribution methods (e.g., GSInquire, Grad-CAM)
  - Why needed here: These generate the first-order quantitative explanations that SOXAI analyzes; understanding how they work is key to interpreting SOXAI outputs.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between gradient-based and perturbation-based attribution methods?

- Concept: Overfitting and dataset bias in deep learning
  - Why needed here: SOXAI's value lies in detecting biases that cause overfitting to non-essential features; understanding these concepts frames why bias detection matters.
  - Quick check question: How does learning a spurious correlation between background objects and class labels manifest in model predictions?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input images -> First-order XAI module (GSInquire) -> Per-image attribution maps -> Embedding generator (weighted averaging) -> PCA (50D) -> t-SNE (2D) -> Visualization (2D scatter plot) -> Analysis clusters -> Define bias removal strategy

- Critical path:
  1. Generate first-order explanations for all images
  2. Compute embeddings for each image
  3. Reduce dimensionality (PCA → t-SNE)
  4. Cluster and visualize embeddings
  5. Analyze clusters for biases
  6. Remove/modify biased data → retrain model

- Design tradeoffs:
  - PCA dimension (50) vs. t-SNE perplexity: Too few PCA dims may lose info; too many may hurt t-SNE clustering.
  - First-order XAI choice: GSInquire gives quantitative explanations; others may differ in sensitivity to bias detection.
  - Cluster granularity: Too coarse → miss subtle biases; too fine → noisy, hard to interpret.

- Failure signatures:
  - No clear clusters in t-SNE plot → embeddings not discriminative enough
  - All points cluster in one region → first-order explanations too uniform
  - Clusters don't align with known classes or features → explanation method not capturing relevant signals

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run SOXAI on a small, known-biased dataset (e.g., colored MNIST) to verify it finds color bias clusters
  2. Compare GSInquire vs. Grad-CAM embeddings: do they produce different cluster patterns?
  3. Remove a detected bias (e.g., earmuffs from chainsaw class) and retrain: does mAP/accuracy improve?

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the following remain:

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SOXAI performance vary with different embedding methods beyond t-SNE, such as UMAP or hierarchical clustering, for grouping quantitative explanations?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions using t-SNE for grouping embeddings but doesn't explore alternative dimensionality reduction or clustering methods
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates SOXAI using t-SNE without comparing its effectiveness to other grouping methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparisons of SOXAI results using different embedding/clustering algorithms across multiple datasets and model types

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can SOXAI be extended to identify and quantify intersectional biases involving multiple visual concepts simultaneously rather than examining biases one at a time?
- Basis in paper: The paper identifies single-concept biases (e.g., earmuffs with chainsaws, logos with drills) but doesn't address compound biases
- Why unresolved: The current implementation groups similar explanations but doesn't analyze multi-dimensional relationships between different visual concepts
- What evidence would resolve it: Methods for detecting and visualizing multi-concept bias patterns, along with case studies showing improved model performance after addressing intersectional biases

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal sample size needed for SOXAI to reliably identify biases in large-scale datasets, and how does this scale with the number of classes and model complexity?
- Basis in paper: The paper demonstrates SOXAI on specific cases but doesn't establish sampling requirements or scalability
- Why unresolved: No analysis of how the number of examples needed for effective SOXAI scales with dataset size, number of classes, or model complexity
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic studies showing minimum sample sizes needed for reliable bias detection across different dataset sizes and model architectures

## Limitations
- SOXAI relies on the quality and discriminative power of first-order explanations; poor explanation methods may fail to reveal meaningful biases
- The bias removal strategy is qualitative and lacks systematic methodology for determining which features to mask
- The approach requires sufficient examples per bias to form meaningful clusters; rare biases may be missed

## Confidence
- SOXAI clustering reveals meaningful biases: Medium
- Bias removal improves model performance: Medium
- SOXAI automates bias discovery better than manual review: Medium
