# Using Explainable AI to Cross-Validate Socio-economic Disparities Among Covid-19 Patient Mortality

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08605
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08605
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: XGBoost model with SHAP and LIME cross-validation reveals Medicare financial class, older age, and gender as high-impact factors for COVID-19 mortality prediction

## Executive Summary
This study investigates socioeconomic disparities in COVID-19 patient mortality using explainable artificial intelligence methods. An XGBoost prediction model was built on a de-identified Austin area hospital dataset to predict COVID-19 patient mortality. SHAP and LIME methods were applied to compare global and local interpretation of feature importance, revealing that Medicare financial class, older age, and gender have high impact on mortality prediction. The study demonstrates the importance of XAI methods in cross-validation of feature attributions and highlights the potential for these approaches to inform clinical decision-making and address health disparities.

## Method Summary
The study used a de-identified Austin area hospital EHR dataset containing 20,180 COVID-19-positive patient visits from March 2020 to December 2021. After preprocessing and encoding categorical variables, an XGBoost classifier was trained with specific hyperparameters (learning_rate=0.1, max_depth=5, n_estimators=10) to predict mortality. SHAP was used for global feature importance analysis and local interpretation, while LIME provided additional local explanations. The authors cross-validated the two XAI methods by comparing feature rankings and contribution directions, finding strong statistical correlation (r = 0.69, p=0.00) between SHAP and LIME interpretations.

## Key Results
- XGBoost achieved precision=0.95 and recall=0.97 on test data for mortality prediction
- Medicare financial class, older age, and gender emerged as top predictors of mortality
- SHAP and LIME local interpretations showed strong consistency, confirming pattern reliability
- Global SHAP analysis revealed different feature importance patterns compared to local interpretations for some patients

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
XGBoost provides accurate mortality predictions that can be meaningfully explained by SHAP and LIME methods. XGBoost is a tree-based ensemble model that handles nonlinear relationships and interactions between socioeconomic features, while SHAP and LIME provide interpretable explanations of feature contributions through different computational approaches. Core assumption: The relationship between socioeconomic features and COVID-19 mortality is sufficiently complex to require ensemble methods but can be approximated by local interpretable models. Break condition: If socioeconomic features show minimal correlation with mortality outcomes, the model would fail to provide meaningful predictions regardless of explainability methods.

### Mechanism 2
SHAP and LIME cross-validation provides reliable local interpretations of individual patient predictions. SHAP uses game theory to calculate exact Shapley values by considering all possible feature coalitions, while LIME approximates local behavior with simpler models; their consistency indicates robust feature importance. Core assumption: When SHAP and LIME produce consistent feature rankings and contribution directions for individual patients, this cross-validation increases confidence in the interpretations. Break condition: If SHAP and LIME produce substantially different interpretations for the same patient, this would suggest one or both methods are unreliable for local explanations.

### Mechanism 3
Global SHAP interpretations reveal feature importance patterns that differ from local interpretations, providing complementary insights. Global SHAP measures average feature impact across all patients, while local SHAP captures individual patient circumstances; discrepancies between them highlight cases where standard risk factors don't apply. Core assumption: Individual patient circumstances can meaningfully differ from population-level patterns, making local interpretation valuable for clinical decision-making. Break condition: If local interpretations consistently mirror global patterns, the additional computational cost of local interpretation may not be justified.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) values
  - Why needed here: SHAP provides a theoretically grounded method to calculate feature importance by considering all possible feature combinations, addressing the limitation of traditional feature importance metrics.
  - Quick check question: How does SHAP calculate the contribution of a feature differently from standard feature importance scores in tree-based models?

- Concept: LIME (Locally Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations)
  - Why needed here: LIME creates interpretable local models around individual predictions, allowing clinicians to understand why a specific patient received a particular mortality prediction.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between how LIME and SHAP calculate feature contributions for individual predictions?

- Concept: Cross-validation between XAI methods
- Concept: Model-agnostic vs. model-specific explainability
  - Why needed here: Understanding that SHAP works with any model while LIME is model-agnostic helps explain why both methods were chosen for cross-validation.
  - Quick check question: Why might using two different XAI methods on the same model provide more reliable interpretations than using just one?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing (OneHotEncoder, OrdinalEncoder) -> XGBoost model training -> Global SHAP interpretation -> Local SHAP and LIME interpretation -> Cross-validation between SHAP and LIME

- Critical path: 1. Data preparation and encoding 2. XGBoost model training and validation 3. Global SHAP interpretation for feature importance 4. Local SHAP and LIME interpretation for selected patients 5. Cross-validation between SHAP and LIME results

- Design tradeoffs: Using XGBoost provides strong predictive performance but requires complex explainability methods; SHAP provides exact Shapley values but is computationally expensive for large datasets; LIME is faster but provides approximations that may be less theoretically sound; One-hot encoding increases dimensionality but handles categorical variables appropriately

- Failure signatures: Poor model performance (low precision/recall) would indicate the features don't capture mortality risk factors; Large discrepancies between SHAP and LIME interpretations would suggest unreliable explainability; SHAP summary plots showing uniform feature importance would indicate the model isn't capturing meaningful patterns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do specific combinations of socioeconomic factors (e.g., age + Medicare insurance) interact to influence COVID-19 mortality risk compared to individual factor effects? Basis in paper: The authors mention that Medicare financial class and older age both have high impact on mortality prediction, but do not analyze their combined or interactive effects. Why unresolved: The study focuses on individual feature importance and local interpretations but does not explore feature interactions or combined effects. What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of feature interaction effects using methods like SHAP interaction values or other XAI techniques that quantify feature interactions.

### Open Question 2
How generalizable are the findings about socioeconomic disparities in COVID-19 mortality to other geographic regions or healthcare systems? Basis in paper: The study uses data from Austin-area hospitals and mentions limitations of EHR race/ethnicity data quality, suggesting potential regional or system-specific findings. Why unresolved: The study is limited to a single geographic area and specific hospital network, which may not represent other regions or healthcare systems. What evidence would resolve it: Replication of the study using similar methods on datasets from different geographic regions and healthcare systems.

### Open Question 3
What is the clinical utility of using LIME vs. SHAP for rapid decision-making in COVID-19 patient care? Basis in paper: The authors mention that LIME is more efficient for local interpretation but do not evaluate its practical utility in clinical settings. Why unresolved: While the study compares SHAP and LIME interpretations, it does not assess which method provides more actionable insights for clinicians in time-sensitive situations. What evidence would resolve it: Clinical trials or usability studies comparing how physicians use SHAP vs. LIME interpretations to make patient care decisions.

## Limitations

- Study findings may not generalize beyond Austin-area hospitals and the specific timeframe (March 2020-December 2021)
- Cross-validation between SHAP and LIME shows statistical consistency but doesn't establish clinical validity of interpretations
- Computational efficiency trade-offs between SHAP and LIME for different dataset sizes were not thoroughly explored

## Confidence

- High Confidence: XGBoost model performance metrics (precision=0.95, recall=0.97) are reliable as they're based on standard evaluation procedures
- Medium Confidence: The cross-validation between SHAP and LIME interpretations is statistically sound but may not capture all edge cases in local explanations
- Low Confidence: The clinical applicability of identified socioeconomic features for mortality prediction across different healthcare systems and patient populations
