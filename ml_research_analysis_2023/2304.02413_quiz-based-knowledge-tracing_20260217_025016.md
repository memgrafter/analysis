# Quiz-based Knowledge Tracing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02413
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02413
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves state-of-the-art performance on three public datasets by modeling quiz-based interactions with intra-quiz short-term influence and inter-quiz long-term substitution/complementarity.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Quiz-based Knowledge Tracing (QKT), a model that monitors students' knowledge states using quiz-based learning interactions in online systems. Unlike traditional methods that assume continuous interaction sequences, QKT explicitly models quiz boundaries to capture both intra-quiz short-term knowledge influence and inter-quiz long-term knowledge substitution and complementarity. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on three public real-world datasets and demonstrates effectiveness through ablation studies and analyses of varying quiz numbers and lengths.

## Method Summary
QKT is designed to handle quiz-based interaction sequences by separating intra-quiz and inter-quiz modeling. For intra-quiz modeling, it uses an adjacent gate followed by global average pooling to capture short-term knowledge influence within quizzes. For inter-quiz modeling, it employs a GRU to measure knowledge substitution (prioritizing recent quizzes) and a self-attentive encoder with recency-aware attention to capture knowledge complementarity across quizzes. The model is trained using cross-entropy loss with Adam optimizer and evaluated using AUC, RMSE, and r² metrics on three public datasets.

## Key Results
- QKT achieves state-of-the-art performance on Assist2012, Eedi2020, and CSEDM datasets compared to existing methods.
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of modeling both intra-quiz influence and inter-quiz substitution/complementarity.
- Performance improves with more quizzes and longer quiz lengths, up to certain thresholds.
- QKT outperforms traditional KT models without using time information, though incorporating time could potentially improve performance further.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** QKT improves performance by modeling intra-quiz short-term knowledge influence with an adjacent gate followed by global average pooling.
- **Mechanism:** The adjacent gate captures how adjacent interactions within the same quiz influence each other, and the global average pooling aggregates these interactions into a quiz-level representation that reflects the student's overall knowledge state on the quiz's KC.
- **Core assumption:** Interactions within a quiz are continuous and share the same or similar KCs, making their order and adjacency informative.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "as students' interactions within a quiz are continuous and have the same or similar knowledge concepts, we design the adjacent gate followed by a global average pooling layer to capture the intra-quiz short-term knowledge influence."
  - [section]: "In our proposed QKT model, we measure the above adjacent multi-aspect knowledge influence in an uniform manner. Specifically, we design the adjacent gate according to the influence between two adjacent interactions, which is then applied to control how much information should be respectively extracted from the previous and current interactions."

- **Break condition:** If quiz interactions are not ordered or if KCs vary within a quiz, the adjacent gate's assumption of continuity and similarity fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** QKT captures inter-quiz long-term knowledge substitution using a GRU that prioritizes recent quizzes over older ones when KCs overlap.
- **Mechanism:** The GRU updates its hidden state by integrating the current quiz vector while selectively forgetting older quiz information, thus modeling knowledge substitution where recent interactions overwrite older, less relevant ones.
- **Core assumption:** When multiple quizzes share similar KCs, the most recent quiz provides a more reliable signal of current knowledge state than older quizzes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we respectively measure the inter-quiz knowledge substitution by the gated recurrent unit and the inter-quiz knowledge complementarity by the self-attentive encoder with a novel recency-aware attention mechanism."
  - [section]: "To measure the above knowledge substitution, we directly utilize the Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) [24] to model students' quiz sequences... which memorizes students' interactions on recent quizzes and forgets the remote ones."
  - [corpus]: Weak corpus evidence; no direct citations found in neighbors.
- **Break condition:** If quizzes with similar KCs are spaced far apart or if students' performance fluctuates significantly, the substitution model may misestimate current knowledge.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** QKT measures inter-quiz knowledge complementarity using a self-attentive encoder with recency-aware attention, allowing integration of diverse KC information across quizzes.
- **Mechanism:** The self-attentive encoder computes similarity-based attention between quizzes, weighted by a recency-aware term that favors more recent quizzes, enabling complementary knowledge from different KCs to be fused.
- **Core assumption:** Quizzes covering different KCs provide complementary information, and more recent quizzes should have higher influence due to recency effects.

- **Break condition:** If quiz order is irrelevant or if recency does not correlate with knowledge retention, the recency-aware attention will distort the complementarity signal.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Quiz-based organization of learning interactions
  - **Why needed here:** QKT's core innovation is to exploit the quiz boundaries to distinguish between short-term intra-quiz influence and long-term inter-quiz integration.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the main difference between how QKT and traditional KT models treat student interaction sequences?
- **Concept:** Knowledge substitution vs. complementarity
  - **Why needed here:** QKT explicitly models two distinct forms of inter-quiz knowledge integration, which is essential for accurate knowledge state estimation.
  - **Quick check question:** When should QKT rely more on knowledge substitution versus complementarity?
- **Concept:** Attention mechanisms and gating in neural networks
  - **Why needed here:** QKT uses adjacent gates, GRUs, and self-attention to modulate information flow, requiring understanding of these components.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the adjacent gate in QKT differ from a standard LSTM forget gate?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input: Exercise embeddings, KC embeddings, student answers → Interaction modeling: Exercise-KC fusion → right/wrong layers → interaction vector → Intra-quiz modeling: Adjacent gate → global average pooling → quiz vector → Inter-quiz modeling: GRU for substitution + self-attentive encoder with recency-aware attention for complementarity → Output: Concatenated substitution and complementarity → final knowledge state → performance prediction
- **Critical path:** Interaction modeling → Intra-quiz modeling → Inter-quiz modeling → Knowledge state integration → Prediction
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Quiz length vs. marginal performance gains (longer quizzes help up to a point)
  - Number of quizzes vs. model stability (more quizzes improve substitution modeling)
  - Recency weighting vs. potential loss of long-term retention signals
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor AUC/RMSE when quiz boundaries are noisy or missing
  - Overfitting if quiz length is too long or quiz number too high relative to data size
  - Degraded performance if KCs are not consistent within quizzes
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Ablation study removing the adjacent gate to measure impact of intra-quiz influence modeling.
  2. Replace GRU with a simple average of quiz vectors to test necessity of knowledge substitution modeling.
  3. Remove recency-aware terms from the attention mechanism to assess their contribution to complementarity modeling.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How does the performance of QKT compare to traditional KT models when applied to continuous interaction sequences (non-quiz-based) versus quiz-based sequences? The paper focuses on quiz-based KT but mentions that most existing KT models assume continuous sequences, and does not provide direct comparisons on non-quiz-based data.
- **Open Question 2:** How would incorporating time information (such as answer time and interval time) into QKT improve its intra- and inter-quiz relation modeling? The paper acknowledges that time information is important but does not explore its impact on QKT performance.
- **Open Question 3:** How can pre-defined knowledge relations between different quizzes be used to explicitly measure inter-quiz knowledge integration in QKT? The paper suggests this as future work but does not explore the use of pre-defined knowledge relations in QKT.

## Limitations

- **Quiz Boundary Assumptions:** The model assumes clear, meaningful boundaries between quizzes, but real-world data may have noisy or ambiguous boundaries, potentially mixing interactions across quizzes.
- **Recency-Aware Attention Formulation:** The exact computation and integration of recency-aware terms with dot-product attention is not fully specified, affecting reproducibility.
- **Generalization Across Domains:** The model is tested on three public datasets, but its performance on other educational platforms or subjects is unknown.

## Confidence

- **Mechanism 1 (Intra-quiz modeling):** High confidence. The adjacent gate and global average pooling are clearly defined and supported by corpus evidence.
- **Mechanism 2 (Inter-quiz substitution):** Medium confidence. The GRU mechanism is standard, but the claim about prioritizing recent quizzes is based on design intent rather than ablation evidence.
- **Mechanism 3 (Inter-quiz complementarity):** Medium confidence. The self-attentive encoder is well-defined, but the contribution of recency-aware terms is not fully validated.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation of Quiz Boundaries:** Remove quiz boundaries and treat all interactions as a continuous sequence. Compare QKT performance to baselines to confirm the importance of quiz-based modeling.
2. **Recency-Aware Attention Ablation:** Replace the recency-aware attention with standard dot-product attention. Measure the impact on performance to validate the contribution of recency weighting.
3. **Cross-Dataset Generalization:** Apply QKT to a new, unseen dataset (e.g., from a different educational platform) and evaluate whether the model maintains state-of-the-art performance.
