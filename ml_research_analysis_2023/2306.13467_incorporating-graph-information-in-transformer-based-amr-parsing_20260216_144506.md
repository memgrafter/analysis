# Incorporating Graph Information in Transformer-based AMR Parsing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.13467
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13467
- **Reference count**: 27
- **Primary result**: Achieves up to 0.7 SMATCH points improvement over previous models on AMR 2.0 and AMR 3.0 datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method to improve AMR parsing by incorporating structural graph information into a Transformer-based model. The approach uses structural adapters with GraphConv layers to embed word-to-node alignments into the encoder, allowing the model to leverage AMR graph structure during training. To handle the absence of graph data at inference, the method employs self-knowledge distillation, transferring knowledge from a teacher model (with access to the graph) to a student model (without it). Experiments show state-of-the-art performance, achieving up to 0.7 SMATCH points improvement over previous models, even without additional data.

## Method Summary
The method, called LeakDistill, extends BART-large with structural adapters that incorporate AMR graph information through word-aligned graphs (WAGs). During training, the model performs dual forward passes: one with structural adapters (leak mode) using the WAG, and one without (normal mode). A KL divergence loss aligns the output distributions of both passes, enabling knowledge transfer from the teacher (with graph access) to the student (without graph access). The total loss combines negative log-likelihood, leakage loss, and KL divergence. The approach uses Contracted or Full WAG representations depending on whether to preserve non-aligned node information.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 0.7 SMATCH points improvement over previous models on AMR 2.0 and AMR 3.0 datasets
- Maintains strong performance without additional silver data
- Generalizes well to out-of-distribution datasets (TLP and BioAMR)
- Shows consistent improvements across multiple evaluation metrics (Unlabeled, NoWSD, Wikification, etc.)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Structural adapters encode AMR graph structure into token representations, improving parsing performance.
- Mechanism: GraphConv layers aggregate information from neighboring nodes in the word-aligned graph (WAG), enriching token embeddings with structural context.
- Core assumption: The alignment between sentence tokens and AMR nodes is accurate and sufficient to capture meaningful structural relationships.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "using structural adapters to explicitly incorporate graph information into the learned representations and improve AMR parsing performance"
  - [section 4.2] "To incorporate AMR structural information into the encoder, we embed the W AGs – obtained from AMR graphs as illustrated in Section 3 – into adapters that encode the graph structure imposed by them."
- Break condition: If alignment quality degrades, the adapters may propagate incorrect structural information, harming performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Self-knowledge distillation transfers structural knowledge from a teacher model (with access to WAG) to a student model (without it).
- Mechanism: The teacher model, trained with structural adapters, generates soft targets that guide the student model to implicitly learn the graph structure.
- Core assumption: The teacher model's predictions contain enough structural information to be distilled effectively without explicit WAG input.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we explore Knowledge Distillation (KD), a technique that transfers knowledge from a teacher model to a student model (Hinton et al., 2015)"
  - [section 5.2] "the knowledge acquired by the teacher model is transferred to the student model, which only has access to the sentence"
- Break condition: If the teacher model overfits to the leaked information, the soft targets may not generalize, reducing distillation effectiveness.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LeakDistill's dual-path training balances explicit structural learning with implicit knowledge transfer.
- Mechanism: One forward pass uses WAG via adapters (leak mode), the other does not (normal mode); KL divergence aligns their distributions.
- Core assumption: Both paths converge to similar output distributions, ensuring the student learns structural information without leakage at inference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.3] "We force the two passes to learn the same distribution by adding a Kullback–Leibler divergence loss to the output logits"
  - [section 5.3] "the total loss becomes: LLeakDistill = LD_nll + βLleak + αLKL"
- Break condition: If the KL loss is too weak, the paths may diverge; if too strong, the model may struggle to learn the task.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input sentence -> BART encoder (with optional structural adapters in leak mode) -> BART decoder -> linearized AMR graph

- **Critical path**:
  1. Input sentence → tokenizer → BART encoder
  2. In leak mode: WAG edges + non-aligned node states → structural adapters → enriched hidden states
  3. Decoder generates linearized AMR graph
  4. Loss computed from both paths and combined

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using structural adapters increases model complexity and training time but improves performance.
  - Contracted WAG is simpler but may lose structural information; Full WAG preserves more but requires handling non-aligned nodes.
  - Scheduling β in LeakDistill balances leak mode contribution over training.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Performance degrades if alignment quality is poor (structural adapters propagate noise).
  - If KL loss is misconfigured, the two paths may diverge, harming distillation.
  - Over-reliance on leak mode may cause overfitting to structural information.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare Contracted WAG vs Full WAG in GLM to assess structural information impact.
  2. Test LeakDistill with and without KL loss to evaluate distillation effectiveness.
  3. Vary β scheduling in LeakDistill to find optimal balance between leak and normal modes.

## Limitations

- The method's performance depends critically on the quality of word-to-node alignments, which may vary across domains and languages.
- The computational overhead of maintaining two forward passes during training is substantial, though not quantified in the paper.
- The trade-off between Contracted and Full WAG representations remains somewhat ambiguous, with unclear guidance on which to prefer.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core claim that incorporating structural information through adapters improves AMR parsing performance is well-supported by SMATCH score improvements across multiple datasets and ablation studies.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of self-knowledge distillation for transferring structural knowledge without graph access is demonstrated, but the exact contribution of different components is less clear.
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about the specific advantages of Contracted vs Full WAG representations are not fully resolved.
