# Behavior Proximal Policy Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11312
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11312
- Reference count: 34
- Key outcome: BPPO outperforms state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms on D4RL benchmark, achieving 51% improvement over behavior cloning

## Executive Summary
Behavior Proximal Policy Optimization (BPPO) presents a novel approach to offline reinforcement learning by leveraging the inherent conservatism of on-policy methods. The method adapts the Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm for offline settings by using the state distribution from the offline dataset instead of the learned policy's state distribution. This simple modification naturally constrains the learned policy to stay close to the behavior policy, preventing overestimation of out-of-distribution actions without requiring additional regularization terms.

## Method Summary
BPPO modifies the standard PPO algorithm for offline RL by replacing the policy state distribution with the behavior policy's state distribution from the offline dataset. This creates a natural constraint that keeps the learned policy close to the behavior policy, as policy updates are only made in states where the behavior policy was actually executed. The method uses the same loss function as online PPO but samples states from the dataset rather than from the current policy. This approach effectively solves the out-of-distribution action overestimation problem that plagues many offline RL methods, while maintaining the simplicity and effectiveness of PPO's architecture.

## Key Results
- BPPO achieves 51% improvement over behavior cloning on D4RL benchmark
- Superior performance on locomotion, dexterous manipulation, and navigation tasks
- Demonstrates that on-policy algorithms can effectively solve offline RL when properly adapted

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BPPO works by exploiting the natural conservatism that arises when using the behavior policy's state distribution for policy updates. Since the learned policy can only be updated in states that were actually visited by the behavior policy, it cannot deviate too far from the behavior policy's actions. This creates an implicit constraint that prevents the policy from overestimating the value of actions that were not present in the dataset, addressing a fundamental challenge in offline RL.
