# Benchmarking Actor-Critic Deep Reinforcement Learning Algorithms for Robotics Control with Action Constraints

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08743
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08743
- Reference count: 30
- One-line primary result: Simple pre-projected action training achieves comparable performance to specialized algorithms for action-constrained RL in robotics control

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating action-constrained reinforcement learning algorithms in robotics control environments. The study addresses the challenge of ensuring feasible actions in real-world systems by comparing existing algorithms and novel variants across different constraint types. The benchmark evaluates TD3 and SAC algorithms with various constraint-handling techniques including differentiable optimization layers, α-projection, radial squashing, and pre-projected actions. The evaluation reveals that a simple baseline approach - training the critic with pre-projected actions - achieves performance comparable to more complex specialized algorithms while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The benchmark evaluates actor-critic algorithms (TD3 and SAC) on continuous control tasks from PyBullet-Gym and MuJoCo with various action constraints (box, linear, convex). Thirteen algorithm variants are tested across four environments (Reacher, Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah) using action mapping techniques including projected actions, differentiable optimization layers, NFWPO, α-projection, radial squashing, and pre-projected actions. The methods are evaluated based on average reward over 10 seeds, measured at 5,000 timestep intervals and final evaluation after 300k-1M timesteps depending on environment. Hyperparameters are adopted from RL Baselines Zoo and tuned for each environment.

## Key Results
- Pre-projected action training (DPre+) achieves performance comparable to specialized algorithms while maintaining computational efficiency
- α-projection and radial squashing demonstrate competitive performance with reduced computational requirements compared to differentiable optimization layers
- Differentiable optimization layers incur significant computational overhead without demonstrating significantly better performance
- The benchmark problems and code are made publicly available for further research

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-projected action training is an effective baseline for action-constrained RL.
- Mechanism: The critic is trained with the original (possibly infeasible) actions before projection, while the projection is only applied during environment rollout. This avoids computational overhead of differentiable projections and eliminates zero-gradient issues associated with closest point projection.
- Core assumption: The critic can learn action values for pre-projected actions and the policy can implicitly learn to adjust its outputs to account for the projection during rollout.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section III-A.4]

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Differentiable optimization layers enable theoretically principled policy updates but incur significant computational overhead.
- Mechanism: The Jacobian of the projection with respect to the pre-projection actions is computed and incorporated into the policy gradient, allowing the actor to be updated in a way that accounts for the effect of the projection.
- Core assumption: The closest point projection is differentiable and the Jacobian can be computed efficiently.
- Evidence anchors: [section III-A.2], [section IV-E.2]

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: α-projection and radial squashing are computationally efficient alternatives to differentiable optimization layers that avoid the zero-gradient issue.
- Mechanism: These mappings assign different outputs to actions in different directions from a center point, ensuring that the Jacobian of the mapping is non-zero in all directions. This allows for effective policy updates without the zero-gradient problem.
- Core assumption: The feasible action set has a well-defined interior point (for α-projection) or center point (for radial squashing).
- Evidence anchors: [section III-B], [section III-B.2], [section IV-E.3]

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base RL algorithm (TD3 or SAC) -> Action mapping technique (e.g., pre-projection, optimization layer, α-projection, radial squashing) -> Penalty term for constraint violations (optional) -> Environment with action constraints

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize the base RL algorithm and the action mapping technique.
  2. During training, collect transitions by executing actions in the environment.
  3. Compute the policy gradient using the chosen action mapping technique and update the policy parameters.
  4. Update the critic using the pre-projected actions (for DPre) or projected actions (for other methods).
  5. Optionally, add a penalty term to the critic loss for constraint violations.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational efficiency vs. theoretical soundness: Pre-projected action training is computationally efficient but may not be theoretically sound, while differentiable optimization layers are theoretically sound but computationally expensive.
  - Ease of implementation vs. performance: Simple baselines like pre-projected action training are easy to implement but may not achieve the best performance, while more complex methods like NFWPO may require more effort to implement but can potentially achieve better results.

- Failure signatures:
  - Zero-gradient issues: If the policy gradient becomes zero, it may indicate that the action mapping technique is causing the zero-gradient problem (e.g., closest point projection).
  - Constraint violations: If the agent consistently violates constraints, it may indicate that the action mapping technique is not effective or that the penalty term is not strong enough.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement and evaluate the pre-projected action training baseline (DPre) on a simple continuous control task with box constraints.
  2. Implement and evaluate a differentiable optimization layer (DOpt) on the same task and compare its performance and computational efficiency with DPre.
  3. Implement and evaluate α-projection or radial squashing on the same task and compare its performance and computational efficiency with DPre and DOpt.

## Limitations
- Conclusions about pre-projected action training's effectiveness may not generalize to more complex robotics scenarios with higher-dimensional action spaces or non-convex constraints
- Computational overhead comparisons are based on specific implementation and hardware setup that may not be representative of all use cases
- The paper does not explore the impact of different hyperparameter settings on the performance of the various algorithms

## Confidence
- High: The experimental results and conclusions regarding the performance of DPre+ as a simple and effective baseline for action-constrained RL
- Medium: The computational overhead comparisons between different mapping techniques and the relative performance of α-projection and radial squashing
- Low: The generalizability of the findings to more complex robotics scenarios and the impact of hyperparameter tuning on algorithm performance
