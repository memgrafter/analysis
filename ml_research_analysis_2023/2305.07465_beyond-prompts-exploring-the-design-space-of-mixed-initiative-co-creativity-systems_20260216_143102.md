# Beyond Prompts: Exploring the Design Space of Mixed-Initiative Co-Creativity Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07465
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07465
- Reference count: 4
- Study presents a design space for user-AI communication in mixed-initiative co-creative systems

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a comprehensive design space framework for mixed-initiative co-creative (MI-CC) systems, moving beyond simple prompt-based interactions to enable richer human-AI collaboration in creative tasks. The framework identifies three key dimensions—initiative (human vs. agent), elaboration vs. reflection, and global vs. local scope—that define how users and AI systems can communicate during creative processes. Through a study with 185 participants interacting with seven unique MI-CC systems for story generation, the research demonstrates that systems covering more dimensions of this design space receive higher ratings on creative and goal-completion metrics. The findings also reveal that different expertise groups show distinct preferences for communication types, pointing toward the need for adaptive, personalized MI-CC systems.

## Method Summary
The study employed a between-subjects design where 185 participants each interacted with one of seven unique MI-CC systems, all designed to explore different regions of the proposed design space. Each system represented a specific combination of the three core dimensions (initiative, elaboration/reflection, and scope) plus the newly identified explanation dimension. Participants engaged in story generation tasks using their assigned system, and their interactions were evaluated across multiple metrics including creative quality ratings, goal completion scores, and user satisfaction measures. The seven systems were systematically designed to test different configurations of the design space, allowing researchers to map which combinations produced optimal creative outcomes and user experiences.

## Key Results
- MI-CC systems covering more design space dimensions received significantly higher ratings on creative and goal-completion metrics
- Different expertise groups (novice vs. expert) showed distinct preferences for types of communication and interaction styles
- The study identified explanation as a critical fourth dimension of the MI-CC design space, enhancing transparency and user control

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by providing a structured vocabulary for understanding and designing the diverse ways humans and AI can interact during creative processes. By explicitly mapping out the communication possibilities between human and machine initiative, reflection and elaboration modes, and local versus global intervention scopes, the design space enables more intentional system design. This systematic approach allows developers to move beyond simplistic prompt-response paradigms toward richer collaborative experiences where both human and AI can contribute meaningfully at different moments. The mechanism succeeds because it acknowledges that creativity involves both individual reflection and collaborative elaboration, and that optimal creative outcomes emerge when both parties can appropriately initiate, respond, and explain their contributions.

## Foundational Learning

**Initiative dimension**: Understanding who initiates communication (human or agent) is crucial for balancing creative control and automation. Quick check: Identify which party should lead at different creative stages.

**Elaboration vs. Reflection**: These represent fundamentally different communication modes—elaboration builds forward while reflection looks backward. Quick check: Map when each mode is most valuable in creative workflows.

**Global vs. Local scope**: Determines whether interventions affect the entire creative work or specific elements. Quick check: Define appropriate intervention boundaries for different creative tasks.

**Explanation dimension**: Provides transparency into AI decision-making, building user trust and enabling better collaboration. Quick check: Implement explanations that are both understandable and actionable.

**Expertise-based preferences**: Different user groups have varying communication needs and comfort levels. Quick check: Design adaptive systems that can adjust to user expertise levels.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: User Interface -> Communication Handler -> Design Space Router -> AI Model -> Feedback Loop -> User Interface

**Critical path**: User input → Design Space selection → AI processing → Output generation → User evaluation → System adaptation

**Design tradeoffs**: Flexibility vs. complexity, automation vs. control, transparency vs. simplicity

**Failure signatures**: Poor creative outcomes occur when design space coverage is too narrow, initiative balance is misaligned with task requirements, or explanation mechanisms are inadequate

**First experiments**:
1. Test single-dimension variations to establish baseline effectiveness
2. Evaluate cross-dimension interactions by combining adjacent design space regions

## Limitations
- Study limited to story generation domain, limiting generalizability to other creative tasks
- Sample size of 185 participants may not capture all possible interaction patterns across seven systems
- Reliance on self-reported metrics introduces potential bias in evaluating creative quality and user experience

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The identification of three core design space dimensions (initiative, elaboration/reflection, and scope) is well-supported by both theoretical grounding and empirical results from the study.

**Medium confidence**: The finding that systems covering more design space dimensions receive higher ratings is supported but may be influenced by novelty effects or task-specific factors.

**Medium confidence**: The claim about expertise-based preferences for different communication types requires further validation across more diverse participant pools and creative domains.

## Next Checks

1. Replicate the study across different creative domains (e.g., visual art generation, music composition) to test the generalizability of the design space framework.

2. Conduct longitudinal studies to examine how user preferences for MI-CC system characteristics evolve over extended interaction periods.

3. Implement and test the proposed explanation dimension in working MI-CC systems to validate its practical utility and measure its impact on creative outcomes.
