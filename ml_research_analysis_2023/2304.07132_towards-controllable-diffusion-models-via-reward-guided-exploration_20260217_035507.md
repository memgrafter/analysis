# Towards Controllable Diffusion Models via Reward-Guided Exploration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07132
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07132
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces RGDM framework achieving lower MMD (0.113 vs 0.176), higher COV (51.43% vs 34.29%), and lower JSD (0.1496 vs 0.2321) on 3D shapes, and higher QED (0.542 vs 0.461) and SA (5.87 vs 4.41) scores on molecules compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RGDM, a novel reward-guided training framework for diffusion models that bridges weighted log-likelihood and maximum entropy reinforcement learning. The key innovation is estimating policy gradients using samples from a stationary payoff distribution proportional to exponential scaled rewards, rather than from the model's own policy. This approach addresses the high gradient variance and inefficiency of directly applying RL to long diffusion processes. Theoretical analysis shows that sampling from this payoff distribution achieves the same optimal point as maximum entropy RL. Experiments on 3D shape generation (FG3D dataset) and molecule generation (QM9 dataset) demonstrate RGDM's effectiveness.

## Method Summary
RGDM pre-trains a diffusion model using maximum likelihood estimation, then fine-tunes it using a reward-guided framework. The framework samples from a payoff distribution proportional to exponential scaled rewards to estimate policy gradients, which reduces variance compared to sampling from the policy itself. The payoff distribution is stationary during training, enabling unbiased gradient estimation. The method is evaluated on 3D shape generation (FG3D dataset) and molecule generation (QM9 dataset), showing improved metrics over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Key Results
- Achieves lower MMD (0.113 vs 0.176) and higher COV (51.43% vs 34.29%) on 3D shape generation
- Demonstrates lower JSD (0.1496 vs 0.2321) compared to EDM baseline
- Shows higher QED (0.542 vs 0.461) and SA (5.87 vs 4.41) scores on molecule generation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Sampling from a stationary payoff distribution proportional to exp(reward) enables lower gradient variance compared to sampling from the policy distribution itself.
- Mechanism: Instead of drawing samples Xt-1 directly from pθ(Xt-1|Xt) (the policy), RGDM samples from qpd(Xt-1|Xt,X0), which incorporates the reward function through an exponential weighting. This payoff distribution is stationary and does not change during training, whereas the policy distribution changes every step.
- Core assumption: The payoff distribution qpd is stationary during training, allowing unbiased gradient estimation without the non-stationarity inherent in policy-based sampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proposed training framework bridges the objective of weighted log-likelihood and maximum entropy RL, which enables calculating policy gradients via samples from a pay-off distribution proportional to exponential scaled rewards, rather than from policies themselves."
  - [section] "Unlike conventional RL objective (Eq. (7)), in Eq. (15) all the sampled terms (i.e. ϵ, ϵ*(r(Xt-1)) and Xt-1) are drawn from stationary distribution (Gaussian or the proposed payoff distribution) instead of the intermediate policy."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found in corpus neighbors; they focus on reward-guided inference rather than training-phase payoff distributions.
- Break condition: If the payoff distribution becomes non-stationary (e.g., reward function changes during training), the variance reduction benefit disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Reward-guided sampling transforms the RL objective into a reward-weighted noise prediction loss, aligning diffusion training with maximum entropy RL optimality.
- Mechanism: By substituting qpd for pθ in the KL divergence objective, the gradient becomes proportional to exp(r(Xt-1))||Xt-1 - μθ(Xt,t)||², which is a reward-weighted version of the standard denoising loss. This maintains the same optimal point as maximum entropy RL while using standard diffusion model training machinery.
- Core assumption: The global optimum of the reward-weighted loss coincides with the maximum entropy RL optimum when pθ matches qpd.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proposed training framework bridges the objective of weighted log-likelihood and maximum entropy RL, which enables calculating policy gradients via samples from a pay-off distribution proportional to exponential scaled rewards."
  - [section] "One can verify that the global minimum of LRL, i.e. the optimal regularized expected reward, is achieved when the model distribution pθ perfectly matches the exponentiated payoff distribution qpd."
  - [section] "The objective functions LRL and LRMLE, have the same global optimum of p, but they optimize a KL divergence in opposite directions."
- Break condition: If the reward function is highly discontinuous or sparse in high dimensions, the exponential weighting may not provide meaningful gradients.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pre-training with MLE before reward guidance provides better initialization than random initialization, crucial for sparse reward landscapes.
- Mechanism: The diffusion model is first trained to minimize the standard variational lower bound (Eq. 3), establishing a good baseline denoiser. This pre-trained model then serves as initialization for the reward-guided phase, providing a smoother starting point for exploring reward-aware refinements.
- Core assumption: Sparse reward functions in high-dimensional spaces (e.g., molecular coordinates) require a good initialization to find any high-reward samples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Since the reward is often sparse in a high-dimensional output space, 'smart' model initialization (pre-training) instead of random initialization is needed. That is to say, at the beginning of the training, we use the maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) to pre-train the diffusion model on the training set D."
  - [section] "Such results again demonstrate the superiority of reward-guided diffusion models over existing conditional diffusion models, such as EDM."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found; corpus neighbors focus on test-time reward guidance rather than training-phase pre-training.
- Break condition: If the reward landscape is dense or the task is simple, pre-training may be unnecessary and could slow convergence.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Markov Decision Process (MDP) and its connection to diffusion reverse processes
  - Why needed here: RGDM frames diffusion model training as an MDP where each denoising step is a state transition, enabling the application of RL concepts like reward maximization and policy gradients.
  - Quick check question: What property must hold between consecutive states in an MDP, and how does this relate to the diffusion reverse process in RGDM?

- Concept: Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning
  - Why needed here: RGDM's objective function combines reward maximization with entropy regularization (Eq. 7), which encourages exploration and prevents premature convergence to suboptimal policies.
  - Quick check question: In maximum entropy RL, what two terms are balanced in the objective function, and why is this balance important for diffusion model training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Noise estimator ϵθ -> Reward function r(X) -> Payoff distribution sampler -> Training loop

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train ϵθ using standard diffusion loss (Eq. 3)
  2. For each training step:
     - Sample timestep t and data point X0
     - Compute Xt, Xt-1, μθ(Xt,t) using Eq. 14
     - Sample Xt-1 from payoff distribution via Eq. 10
     - Compute reward-weighted loss (Eq. 15)
     - Update ϵθ with gradient descent

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Payoff distribution radius d: Smaller d reduces search space but may miss high-reward samples; larger d increases computation but improves reward discovery
  - Pre-training duration: Longer pre-training provides better initialization but delays reward guidance; shorter may struggle with sparse rewards
  - Reward function smoothness: Differentiable rewards allow gradient-based search; non-differentiable require optimization methods like simulated annealing

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor MMD/COV/JSD metrics: Indicates payoff distribution sampling not finding high-reward regions
  - Training instability: Suggests learning rate too high for reward-weighted loss or payoff distribution radius inappropriate
  - Degraded sample quality: May indicate reward function misaligned with desired properties

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement standard diffusion model (no reward) on FG3D dataset to establish baseline
  2. Add reward guidance for a simple, smooth reward function (e.g., class confidence) on the same dataset
  3. Scale to more complex reward functions (e.g., multi-class discrimination) and evaluate on molecule generation task

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes stationary payoff distribution but doesn't extensively validate this empirically
- Experiments limited to specific datasets (FG3D and QM9) with particular reward functions, limiting generalizability
- Payoff distribution sampling mechanism computational cost and practical implementation details not thoroughly discussed
- No comparison against recent diffusion-based conditional generation methods from 2023-2024 timeframe

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core mechanism of using payoff distributions for gradient estimation is theoretically sound and mathematically derived from maximum entropy RL principles
- Medium confidence: The experimental results showing improved metrics over EDM and other baselines are convincing but could benefit from more ablation studies on the payoff distribution sampling parameters
- Medium confidence: The claim that pre-training with MLE is necessary for sparse reward landscapes is reasonable but not extensively validated across different reward sparsity levels

## Next Checks
1. **Stationarity validation**: Design an experiment to empirically verify that the payoff distribution remains stationary during training by tracking its statistics across training iterations
2. **Sampling efficiency analysis**: Measure and report the computational overhead of payoff distribution sampling compared to standard diffusion training, including wall-clock time and number of optimization steps required per batch
3. **Cross-domain generalization**: Apply RGDM to a different domain (e.g., image generation with CLIP-based rewards) to test whether the benefits observed in 3D shapes and molecules extend to other data modalities
