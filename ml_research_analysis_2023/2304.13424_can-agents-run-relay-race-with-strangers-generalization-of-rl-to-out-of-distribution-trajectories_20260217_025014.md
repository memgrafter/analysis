# Can Agents Run Relay Race with Strangers? Generalization of RL to Out-of-Distribution Trajectories

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13424
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13424
- Reference count: 12
- One-line primary result: SAC agents with Self-Trajectory Augmentation (STA) reduce relay-evaluation failure rates by more than 3x without sacrificing performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a critical gap in reinforcement learning (RL) generalization: the ability of agents to handle out-of-distribution controllable states. The authors introduce "relay-evaluation" - a novel method where agents are tested on states from other independently trained agents' trajectories. They find that standard RL algorithms like PPO and SAC fail unexpectedly often in this setting, indicating poor generalization to unseen states. To address this, they propose Self-Trajectory Augmentation (STA), which augments training with diverse historical states selected via Q-function values, significantly improving relay-generalization.

## Method Summary
The authors propose Self-Trajectory Augmentation (STA) as a training method to improve RL generalization. STA works by maintaining a set of historical states (SSTA) from the agent's own trajectories, selected based on Q-function values. During training, the agent periodically resets to states from SSTA, forcing it to learn recovery strategies from diverse starting positions. This augments the initial state distribution and exposes the agent to states it may have visited but not mastered, improving its ability to handle out-of-distribution controllable states.

## Key Results
- Standard RL algorithms (PPO, SAC, TD3) show unexpectedly high failure rates (30-60%) when evaluated on states from other agents' trajectories via relay-evaluation.
- SAC agents trained with STA reduce failure rates by more than 3x across most MuJoCo environments while maintaining original task performance.
- Q-function values correlate with success probability, providing a reliable mechanism for selecting beneficial states for augmentation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Agents trained only on their own trajectories learn policies that are highly specific to the state distribution they encounter, failing to generalize to unseen controllable states from other agents.
- Mechanism: The policy becomes overfit to a narrow set of successful trajectories, essentially memorizing actions for a small subset of states rather than learning general control strategies.
- Core assumption: The environment's state space is large and diverse enough that the agent's exploration during training does not cover all controllable states.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "many representative RL algorithms have unexpectedly high failure rates under relay-evaluation"
  - [section] "we observe that all the agents have significantly higher failure rates than the reference failure rates"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related works focus on robustness or multi-agent coordination, not this specific generalization failure.
- Break condition: If the state space is small or the agent's exploration covers most controllable states, the mechanism breaks.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Q-function's value estimates can serve as an indicator of whether a state is out-of-distribution and likely to cause failure.
- Mechanism: For states the agent has not encountered or mastered, the Q-function will assign lower values, signaling that the agent is uncertain or likely to fail.
- Core assumption: The Q-function's value estimates correlate with the agent's confidence and ability to succeed from a given state.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Q(s,π(s)) of the failure states is lower than the success states"
  - [section] "the prediction Q(s,π(s)) on its own states" is higher than on failure states
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct support from related works.
- Break condition: If the Q-function is poorly trained or the value estimates do not correlate with success probability, the mechanism breaks.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Augmenting the initial state set during training with diverse controllable states from the agent's own historical trajectories improves generalization to unseen states.
- Mechanism: By forcing the agent to start from states it has previously visited but may not have mastered, the agent learns to recover from a wider variety of situations, improving its ability to handle out-of-distribution states.
- Core assumption: The distribution of states visited by the agent changes during training, and revisiting older states provides beneficial diversity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we propose a novel method called Self-Trajectory Augmentation (STA), which will reset the environment to the agent's old states according to the Q function during training"
  - [section] "the failure rates of SAC agents are reduced by more than three times in most settings"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct support from related works.
- Break condition: If the agent's historical trajectories do not contain diverse enough states, or if the Q-function is not reliable for selecting useful states, the mechanism breaks.

## Limitations
- The study focuses exclusively on continuous control tasks in MuJoCo environments, limiting generalizability to other domains or discrete action spaces.
- The Q-function based state selection mechanism for STA relies on the assumption that lower Q-values indicate problematic states, but this relationship may not hold in all scenarios.
- The evaluation only considers "controllable" states, excluding potentially important failure modes involving non-controllable or degenerate states.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the experimental methodology and evaluation framework (relay-evaluation) due to systematic testing across multiple environments and baselines.
- Medium confidence in the STA mechanism's effectiveness, as results show consistent improvement but the underlying Q-function correlation assumption needs further validation.
- Low confidence in the proposed explanation for why baseline agents fail, as the paper provides limited theoretical analysis of the generalization failure mechanism.

## Next Checks
1. Test STA's performance when using alternative state selection criteria (e.g., state visitation frequency, policy entropy) rather than Q-values to verify the mechanism's robustness.
2. Evaluate generalization to states from completely different environments (cross-environment transfer) to assess whether STA provides broader generalization benefits.
3. Analyze the Q-function's value distribution more systematically across successful vs. failure states to better understand the relationship between value estimates and controllability.
