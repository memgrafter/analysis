# Learning Representation for Anomaly Detection of Vehicle Trajectories

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05000
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05000
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: Proposed methods achieve F1 scores up to 0.99 for lateral anomalies and 0.65 for random anomalies, with good generalization to unseen anomaly patterns

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of detecting anomalous vehicle trajectories in autonomous driving systems, where anomalies can mislead prediction modules and cause unsafe planning. The authors propose two representation learning approaches for anomaly detection: a supervised contrastive learning method that separates normal and anomalous trajectories in a low-dimensional space, and an unsupervised semantic reconstruction method using adversarial autoencoders. Both methods are evaluated on the Argoverse datasets, demonstrating significant performance improvements over baselines and strong generalization to unseen anomaly patterns.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two complementary approaches for anomaly detection in vehicle trajectories. The supervised method uses contrastive learning to encode trajectories into a 32-dimensional representation space where normal and anomalous trajectories are maximally separated. This is achieved through a feature extractor trained on normal trajectory prediction, followed by a supervised encoder that uses negative contrastive loss between normal and anomalous feature pairs, with detection performed via SVM classification. The unsupervised method employs an adversarial autoencoder that learns a semantically structured latent space representing driving behaviors (lateral intention, longitudinal aggressiveness, and other variables). Detection is performed by thresholding reconstruction error. Both methods are evaluated on Argoverse datasets with anomalies generated through adversarial attacks targeting prediction errors.

## Key Results
- Supervised contrastive learning achieves F1 scores up to 0.99 for lateral anomalies
- Unsupervised semantic reconstruction achieves F1 scores up to 0.65 for random anomalies
- Both methods demonstrate strong generalization when trained on one anomaly pattern and tested on another
- Methods significantly outperform baseline anomaly detection approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness stems from learning representations that capture the underlying structure of normal driving behaviors while being sensitive to deviations. The supervised method directly optimizes for separation between normal and anomalous trajectories in the representation space, while the unsupervised method leverages semantic understanding of driving behaviors to identify anomalies as deviations from expected patterns. The semantic latent space modeling in the unsupervised approach allows for meaningful interpretation of anomalies in terms of driving behavior dimensions.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Trajectory Data -> Feature Extractor (GNN) -> Supervised Encoder (Contrastive) / Unsupervised AAE -> Classifier (SVM) / Thresholding -> Anomaly Score

**Critical Path:** The most critical components are the feature extractor for capturing trajectory patterns and the representation learning module (either supervised encoder or unsupervised AAE) that determines anomaly separability.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The supervised method requires labeled anomalous data but achieves higher performance, while the unsupervised method works with only normal data but has lower detection accuracy. The semantic latent space in the unsupervised approach trades reconstruction accuracy for interpretability.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor detection performance may indicate insufficient negative pairs in contrastive learning, inadequate semantic modeling, or overfitting to specific anomaly patterns. Generalization failures suggest the learned representations are too pattern-specific.
