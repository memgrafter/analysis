# Bilex Rx: Lexical Data Augmentation for Massively Multilingual Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.15265
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15265
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Monolingual data augmentation (codeswitching and lexical prompting) consistently improves translation quality for unsupervised languages.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores the use of bilingual lexicons to improve machine translation for low-resource and unsupervised languages. The authors experiment with three augmentation strategies: codeswitching, lexical prompting (GLOWUP), and raw token-pair training. They find that using monolingual data augmentations yields consistent performance gains, with CodeswitchMonoGatiPanlex and GlowupMonoGatiPanlex emerging as the best models for unsupervised languages. The gains are smaller for larger models, but the quality of the bilingual lexica becomes more important than the quantity of data. The authors also open-source GATITOS, a new multilingual lexicon for 26 low-resource languages, which leads to average gains of about +8 CHRF on unsupervised languages.

## Method Summary
The authors propose three bilingual lexicon-based augmentation strategies to improve massively multilingual machine translation: (1) codeswitching, which injects bilingual word pairs into source sentences; (2) lexical prompting (GLOWUP), which prepends bilingual pairs to the input; and (3) raw token-pair training, which uses token-level data directly during training. They combine these with monolingual data and existing lexicons (PanLex) or their own open-sourced GATITOS lexicon for 26 low-resource languages. The models are trained on up to 30 billion tokens, with augmentation ratios and other hyperparameters tuned for each language family.

## Key Results
- Monolingual data augmentation strategies (codeswitching and lexical prompting) consistently improve translation quality for unsupervised languages.
- Larger models see smaller gains from augmentation, and quality of lexica becomes more important than quantity.
- GATITOS lexicon yields average gains of about +8 CHRF for unsupervised languages.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper shows that bilingual lexicons, when used as data augmentation, help bridge vocabulary gaps for low-resource and unsupervised languages in massively multilingual machine translation. By injecting lexical knowledge directly into the training data (via codeswitching or lexical prompting), the model learns better alignments and translations for rare or unseen words. The monolingual augmentation approach is especially effective because it leverages large amounts of available monolingual text, while bilingual lexicons provide crucial cross-lingual mappings. The quality and coverage of the bilingual lexicon are more important than sheer data volume, especially for larger models.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness of bilingual lexicon augmentation appears to diminish with larger models, but exact scaling behavior is not precisely characterized.
- The evaluation focuses on CHRF scores, which may not fully capture translation quality nuances, and does not include human evaluation.
- GATITOS lexicon coverage and accuracy for each of the 26 low-resource languages is not independently verified.

## Confidence
- **High**: Monolingual data augmentation strategies (codeswitching and lexical prompting) consistently improve translation quality for unsupervised languages.
- **Medium**: Larger models see smaller gains from augmentation, and quality of lexica becomes more important than quantity.
- **Medium**: GATITOS lexicon yields average gains of about +8 CHRF for unsupervised languages.
