# InPL: Pseudo-labeling the Inliers First for Imbalanced Semi-supervised Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07269
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07269
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Achieves up to 3% absolute accuracy improvement on CIFAR10-LT and 6.9% improvement on challenging imbalanced SSL scenarios

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces InPL, a novel pseudo-labeling method for imbalanced semi-supervised learning that leverages energy scores to identify in-distribution samples. Unlike traditional confidence-based thresholding approaches, InPL uses energy scores to measure whether unlabeled samples are close to the current training data distribution. The method progressively expands the in-distribution as training progresses, allowing the model to better approximate the true class distribution. Experiments demonstrate significant performance improvements over confidence-based methods, particularly in highly imbalanced scenarios where out-of-distribution samples are prevalent.

## Method Summary
InPL introduces a fundamentally different approach to pseudo-labeling by replacing confidence-based thresholding with energy score-based inlier detection. The method measures how close an unlabeled sample is to the current training data distribution using energy scores, which have proven effective for out-of-distribution detection. As training progresses and more samples are pseudo-labeled, the in-distribution boundary expands, allowing the model to capture more of the true data distribution. This progressive expansion helps the model adapt to class imbalance by ensuring that pseudo-labels are assigned based on proximity to known data rather than just prediction confidence, which can be misleading in imbalanced settings.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 3% absolute accuracy improvement on CIFAR10-LT benchmark
- Demonstrates 6.9% improvement on one of the most challenging imbalanced SSL scenarios
- Shows robustness to out-of-distribution samples while maintaining competitive performance on balanced SSL benchmarks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of InPL stems from its use of energy scores as a more reliable indicator of sample membership than confidence scores. While confidence can be high for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples that the model has learned to classify, energy scores specifically measure the distance to the data manifold. By pseudo-labeling inliers first, the method ensures that the expanding training distribution remains grounded in actual data patterns rather than spurious correlations. This progressive expansion allows the model to gradually capture the full data distribution while maintaining robustness to noise and outliers.

## Foundational Learning
- Energy scores for OOD detection: Why needed - to distinguish between samples close to training data vs. high-confidence outliers; Quick check - verify energy score calibration on known OOD datasets
- Semi-supervised learning with pseudo-labeling: Why needed - to leverage unlabeled data in imbalanced settings; Quick check - ensure pseudo-label quality improves over training epochs
- Class imbalance handling: Why needed - standard SSL methods fail when class distributions are skewed; Quick check - measure per-class performance across different imbalance ratios

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Unlabeled data -> Energy scoring module -> Inlier filtering -> Pseudo-label assignment -> Model training -> Updated model parameters

Critical path: The energy scoring module is critical as it determines which samples receive pseudo-labels. The progressive expansion mechanism ensures that as more reliable pseudo-labels are generated, the model's understanding of the data distribution improves iteratively.

Design tradeoffs: InPL trades computational overhead of energy score calculations for improved robustness to class imbalance and OOD samples. This makes it particularly suitable for scenarios where data quality and class balance are concerns.

Failure signatures: The method may struggle if energy scores are poorly calibrated or if the initial labeled set is too small to provide a reliable foundation for expansion. Performance degradation could occur if the expansion rate is too aggressive.

First experiments: 1) Test energy score effectiveness on CIFAR10 with synthetic OOD samples, 2) Evaluate pseudo-label quality progression over training epochs, 3) Compare performance against confidence-based methods on balanced datasets

## Limitations
- Performance improvements are primarily validated on specific benchmark datasets, limiting generalization assessment
- Computational overhead of energy score calculations compared to simpler confidence-based methods is not thoroughly quantified
- Limited evaluation of method's effectiveness across different degrees of class imbalance and data distributions

## Confidence
- High confidence in theoretical foundation of energy score-based inlier detection
- Medium confidence in specific performance improvements due to limited benchmark scope
- Low confidence in method's robustness across diverse imbalanced SSL scenarios
