# A Survey on Causal Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05209
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05209
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Comprehensive survey of CRL methods across multiple model settings with categorization based on causal information availability

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a systematic overview of Causal Reinforcement Learning (CRL) methods, organizing approaches based on whether causal information is known or unknown. The paper examines CRL across multiple model settings including MDPs, POMDPs, Bandits, Dynamic Treatment Regimes, and Imitation Learning. The authors analyze how prior causal knowledge can enhance sample efficiency, provide causal explanations, and improve generalization in policy learning, while also addressing scenarios where causal information must be learned iteratively alongside policies.

## Method Summary
The survey categorizes CRL methods into two main branches based on causal information availability: methods with known causal information leverage prior knowledge for improved learning efficiency and generalization, while methods with unknown causal information involve iterative learning of causal structures and representations. The analysis covers formal definitions, existing approaches, evaluation metrics, open-source resources, and applications across robotics, games, healthcare, autonomous driving, and recommendation systems. The authors also identify future research directions including knowledge-based, memory-based, cognition-based, and application-based CRL.

## Key Results
- CRL methods are systematically categorized based on causal information availability (known vs. unknown)
- The survey covers CRL approaches across five major model settings: MDP, POMDP, Bandits, DTR, and IL
- Applications span robotics, games, healthcare, autonomous driving, and recommendation systems
- Future research directions identified include handling latent confounders, non-stationary causal structures, and improving generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of CRL methods stems from integrating causal reasoning into reinforcement learning frameworks. When causal information is known, agents can leverage structural knowledge to reduce sample complexity and improve policy generalization. For unknown causal structures, iterative learning processes allow agents to discover causal relationships while simultaneously learning optimal policies. This integration addresses fundamental limitations of standard RL by enabling counterfactual reasoning, handling confounding variables, and improving transfer learning capabilities across different environments.

## Foundational Learning

**Causal Discovery**: Learning causal relationships from observational data; needed to identify structural dependencies when prior knowledge is unavailable; quick check: verify recovered causal graph matches known ground truth or interventional data.

**Counterfactual Reasoning**: Evaluating alternative outcomes under different interventions; needed for policy evaluation and explanation; quick check: ensure counterfactual predictions align with experimental results.

**Latent Confounder Handling**: Managing unobserved variables that affect multiple observed variables; needed to avoid spurious correlations in policy learning; quick check: test performance degradation when confounders are present vs. absent.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Collection -> Causal Structure Learning -> Policy Optimization -> Evaluation

**Critical Path**: The iterative loop between causal structure learning and policy optimization represents the critical path, as accurate causal models directly impact policy performance and generalization capabilities.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Known vs. unknown causal information: prior knowledge improves sample efficiency but may limit adaptability
- Model-based vs. model-free approaches: model-based offers better interpretability but higher computational cost
- Online vs. offline learning: online allows adaptive causal discovery but requires active data collection

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Overfitting to spurious correlations when causal structure is misspecified
- Poor generalization when latent confounders are not properly handled
- Convergence issues in iterative learning loops when causal and policy objectives conflict

**First 3 Experiments**:
1. Compare sample efficiency of CRL with known causal structure vs. standard RL on benchmark environments
2. Evaluate policy performance when latent confounders are present vs. absent
3. Test generalization across environments with varying causal structures
