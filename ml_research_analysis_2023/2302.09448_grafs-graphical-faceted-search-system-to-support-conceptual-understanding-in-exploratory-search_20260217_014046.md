# GRAFS: Graphical Faceted Search System to Support Conceptual Understanding in Exploratory Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09448
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09448
- Reference count: 40
- Users of GRAFS showed significantly better recognition of relationships between key concepts during exploratory search compared to traditional faceted search

## Executive Summary
GRAFS is an interactive search system that helps users build mental models of unfamiliar information spaces during exploratory search. The system extracts a knowledge subgraph from semantic search results, organizing key concepts and their relationships into visualizations that complement traditional faceted search interfaces. By showing relationships between concepts through concept arcs and hierarchical clustering via a facet treemap, GRAFS enables users to understand how concepts connect without reading all documents. A user study with 20 participants found that GRAFS significantly improved users' ability to recognize relationships between key concepts while maintaining similar functionality and usability to baseline systems.

## Method Summary
The GRAFS system works by first extracting concepts and relations from search results using semantic analysis and SNOMED-CT vocabulary. It then constructs a knowledge subgraph containing a user-specified number of the most informative concepts, balancing relevance to the query with coverage of the information space. The system implements graph partitioning to create concept clusters and visualizes these relationships through a facet list with arcs showing co-occurrence frequency and a treemap showing hierarchical clustering. Users can interact with the system by selecting concepts to filter documents, editing the concept list, and exploring provenance information for each concept.

## Key Results
- GRAFS significantly improved users' ability to recognize relationships between key concepts (p = 0.033)
- Users developed more sophisticated understanding of search topics compared to baseline faceted search
- The facet list arcs were the most used feature, with participants providing positive feedback on this visualization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Showing relationships between concepts (via arcs and treemap) improves users' ability to see how concepts are related, which helps them build a mental model of the information space.
- Mechanism: The visual representation of concept relationships (arcs showing co-occurrence frequency and treemap showing hierarchical clustering) provides signifiers that make these relationships more discoverable. This allows users to understand how concepts connect without manually reading all documents.
- Core assumption: Users need to see relationships between concepts to build an accurate mental model, and visual representation is more effective than text-based discovery.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experimental results show that the proposed approach is more effective in helping users recognize relationships between key concepts"
  - [section 5.2.1] "participants thought the two systems were different in helping them see the relation between concepts (p = 0.033)"
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers but only 8 with strong FMR scores; corpus evidence is weak for this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If the visualization becomes too cluttered or complex, users may ignore it or find it overwhelming rather than helpful.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The knowledge subgraph extraction algorithm effectively selects the most informative subset of concepts from a large knowledge graph, balancing relevance and coverage.
- Mechanism: The algorithm sequentially selects concepts that maximize relevance to the query while minimizing relationships between selected concepts, ensuring diverse coverage of the information space. This creates a manageable subset that captures essential concepts without overwhelming users.
- Core assumption: A smaller, carefully selected subset of concepts can represent the essential information in a large knowledge graph for exploratory search purposes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3.1] "We construct a subgraph H_q ⊂ G_q that contains a user-specified number (e.g., 20) of concepts" with "time complexity of O(k²|C_q|)"
  - [section 3.3.1] "The algorithm runs efficiently (SC3) since k, the number of concepts in the subgraph H_q, is usually small (e.g., 20)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for this specific algorithm
- Break condition: If k is too small, important concepts may be missed; if k is too large, the subgraph becomes overwhelming.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Preserving user agency through editable concept lists allows users to refine the system's imperfect data model, improving search effectiveness.
- Mechanism: Users can delete irrelevant concepts and add important ones they discover during exploration, allowing them to customize the knowledge subgraph to their specific needs and mental model. This addresses the limitation that automatic concept extraction may not perfectly match user interests.
- Core assumption: Users have domain knowledge and can identify relevant concepts that the automatic extraction might miss or include irrelevant ones.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.2] "Our study suggests that it is important to preserve human agency when the system takes the initiative to recommend an imperfect data model"
  - [section 5.3.3] "Overall, the facet list arcs were the most used feature" and "participants provided positive feedback on this feature"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus doesn't directly support this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If users lack domain knowledge or time, they may not effectively use the editing features.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Frontend: Web interface with D3.js visualizations (facet list with arcs, facet treemap, concept provenance tooltips, editable list)
  - Backend: Apache Solr document indexing and search, NLP-based concept extraction using SNOMED-CT vocabulary
  - Data Model: Knowledge subgraph construction algorithm that balances relevance and coverage

- Critical path:
  1. User enters query
  2. Backend retrieves documents and extracts concepts/relations
  3. Knowledge subgraph algorithm selects k most informative concepts
  4. Graph partitioning creates concept clusters
  5. Frontend renders interface with visualizations
  6. User interacts with interface, triggering document filtering and subgraph updates

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simplicity vs. information richness: GRAFS adds complexity to traditional faceted search but provides richer relationship information
  - Automation vs. user control: Automatic concept extraction may not perfectly match user needs, hence editable lists
  - Visual complexity vs. usability: Showing relationships visually helps but can overwhelm if too dense

- Failure signatures:
  - User ignores arcs and treemap: Visualization is too complex or not perceived as useful
  - User can't find relevant documents: Concept extraction or subgraph selection algorithm failing
  - Interface becomes cluttered: Too many concepts or relationships displayed
  - System too slow: Knowledge subgraph computation not responsive enough

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test with a simple query like "treatment for depression" and verify the system returns 20 concepts with appropriate arcs and treemap visualization
  2. Test the editable list functionality by deleting a concept and verifying the subgraph and visualization update correctly
  3. Test document filtering by selecting a concept and verifying only documents containing that concept appear in the results

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions. However, several questions arise from the methodology and results:

## Limitations
- The study was conducted only with medical literature search tasks, limiting generalizability to other domains
- Specific implementation details of the knowledge subgraph extraction algorithm are not fully specified
- The study only evaluated immediate task performance without assessing long-term retention of mental models

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The basic premise that visualizing relationships between concepts can help users build mental models during exploratory search
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific implementation choices (knowledge subgraph size, arc visualization design, treemap layout) and their relative effectiveness
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about the algorithm's efficiency and scalability with larger knowledge graphs

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the user study with a different participant pool and potentially different domain to assess generalizability
2. Systematically vary the number of concepts in the knowledge subgraph (k) and measure the impact on both performance and user experience to identify optimal parameters
3. Design a study that measures how well the mental models developed through GRAFS use persist over time and support subsequent related searches
