# Detection of Sensor-To-Sensor Variations using Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10850
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10850
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel SHAP-based approach detects sensor-to-sensor variations as low as 5-10% and improves model performance through sensor pruning

## Executive Summary
This study introduces an Explainable AI approach using SHAP to detect sensor-to-sensor variations in chemi-resistive gas sensing devices, addressing a critical manufacturing challenge. By analyzing global and local feature importance rankings across sensors, the method identifies deviations caused by manufacturing inconsistencies. The approach was validated using artificial and realistic Ozone concentration profiles with a GRU model, successfully detecting sensor variations and improving model performance through strategic sensor pruning.

## Method Summary
The methodology combines sensor simulation, feature extraction, GRU modeling, and SHAP-based analysis to detect sensor variations. Sensors are simulated using artificial and realistic Ozone concentration profiles, with features extracted in both time and frequency domains (relative resistance, slope, amplitude, phase angle, and total harmonic distortion). A GRU model estimates gas concentrations from these features, while SHAP calculates both local and global feature importance rankings. Sensors are flagged as deviating when their global feature rankings show significant dissimilarity from others, measured through cosine similarity, correlation coefficient, and Euclidean distance. Deviating sensors are then pruned from training data to improve model performance.

## Key Results
- Successfully detected sensor deviations as low as 5-10% from normal behavior
- Improved model performance on test datasets through sensor pruning
- Achieved reduced computational costs and training time with pruned datasets
- Demonstrated effectiveness using both artificial and realistic Ozone concentration profiles

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SHAP feature importance on a global sensor level captures deviations caused by manufacturing inconsistencies.
- Mechanism: Sensors with differing fabrication processes produce distinct signal characteristics. SHAP computes the average marginal contribution of each feature across all samples in a sensor. When comparing global feature rankings between sensors, deviations in fabrication manifest as differences in these rankings. Euclidean distance, cosine similarity, and correlation coefficient quantify these differences, flagging sensors as outliers.
- Core assumption: The SHAP-based global feature ranking is sensitive enough to detect variations in sensor behavior caused by manufacturing defects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By applying the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method, the research identifies sensors contributing most to environmental gas concentration estimation and measures similarity of feature rankings to flag deviations."
  - [section] "By comparing the global sensor feature ranking using the Euclidean distance, the correlation coefficient and the cosine similarity, deviating sensors with various degrees of variations were successfully identified."
- Break condition: If the feature ranking differences between normal and deviating sensors are below the sensitivity threshold of the similarity metrics, the method fails to detect deviations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Local SHAP heatmaps reveal feature importance dynamics at specific events, complementing global rankings.
- Mechanism: While global SHAP rankings provide an overall view of feature importance, local SHAP heatmaps show how feature contributions change over time for each sensor. This is crucial for identifying features that are only important at specific events (e.g., sudden concentration jumps). The combination of global and local analysis provides a more complete understanding of sensor behavior and helps validate the importance of features that may be underrepresented in global rankings.
- Core assumption: Local feature importance variations at specific events are informative for detecting sensor deviations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "From the SHAP heatmap it is evident that the slope feature is, as before, crucial at sharp concentration jumps and should hence not be omitted."
- Break condition: If local feature importance variations are not consistent or do not correlate with sensor deviations, the heatmap analysis becomes less informative.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pruning deviating sensors from the training dataset improves model performance and reduces computational costs.
- Mechanism: Sensors with manufacturing variations introduce noise and inconsistencies into the training data, making it harder for the model to learn the underlying patterns. By identifying and removing these deviating sensors using SHAP-based outlier detection, the training dataset becomes cleaner and more representative of normal sensor behavior. This leads to improved model performance on test data and reduces the time and resources needed for training.
- Core assumption: Removing deviating sensors from the training data leads to a more accurate and efficient model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By pruning deviating sensors from the training dataset, the model achieved improved performance with reduced computational costs and training time."
  - [section] "After removing detected deviations, we see an improvement in the model performance on various test datasets. This is achieved with less training samples leading to improvements in computational time and hardware resources."
- Break condition: If the number of deviating sensors is small relative to the total dataset, or if the deviations are not significant enough to impact model performance, pruning may not lead to noticeable improvements.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
  - Why needed here: SHAP provides a model-agnostic method for explaining the output of ML models by calculating the average marginal contribution of each feature. This is essential for identifying which sensors contribute most to gas concentration estimation and for detecting deviations in sensor behavior.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Sensor Simulation -> Feature Extraction -> GRU Model -> SHAP Analysis -> Outlier Detection -> Data Pruning -> Model Evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Simulate sensor data with variations
  2. Extract features from sensor responses
  3. Train GRU model on feature data
  4. Apply SHAP to calculate feature importance
  5. Compare global feature rankings to detect outliers
  6. Prune deviating sensors from training data
  7. Retrain model and evaluate performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sensitivity vs. Specificity: Higher sensitivity in outlier detection may lead to more false positives
  - Computational Cost: SHAP calculations can be computationally expensive, especially for large datasets
  - Model Complexity: More complex models may be harder to interpret but could capture subtle variations better

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor model performance despite pruning: Indicates that the pruned dataset is not representative or that the model is not learning effectively
  - Inability to detect known variations: Suggests that the SHAP-based outlier detection is not sensitive enough or that the feature extraction is not capturing the relevant characteristics
  - High computational cost with minimal performance gain: Implies that the pruning process is not yielding significant benefits relative to the resources invested

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Simulate sensor data with a single, known variation (e.g., 10% deviation in sensitivity) and verify that the SHAP-based method can detect it
  2. Introduce multiple variations with different degrees of deviation and assess the method's ability to distinguish between normal and deviating sensors
  3. Prune the training dataset based on the detected outliers and retrain the model, comparing performance with and without pruning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do local SHAP explanations compare to global ones in detecting subtle sensor variations that may be missed by averaging?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that local Shapley explanations provide information about feature importance at specific time steps, which may be lost in global rankings.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on global sensor feature rankings for detecting variations, but did not extensively explore the potential of local explanations for detecting subtle or time-specific variations.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative analysis of detection sensitivity using local vs. global SHAP explanations across various degrees of sensor variations and different gas concentration profiles.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal combination of similarity metrics for detecting sensor variations across different manufacturing defect levels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study used cosine similarity, correlation coefficient, and Euclidean distance, but found that different metrics were more effective for different variation levels.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identified that Euclidean distance was more conclusive for detecting larger deviations, it did not determine the optimal combination or weighting of metrics for detecting variations across the full spectrum of defect levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of various combinations and thresholds of the three similarity metrics across a comprehensive range of sensor variation levels to identify the most effective detection strategy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed SHAP-based variation detection method perform compared to traditional anomaly detection techniques in real-world manufacturing scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study focused on the SHAP-based approach but did not directly compare its performance to established anomaly detection methods like Autoencoders or Isolation Forest.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrated the effectiveness of SHAP for variation detection, it did not benchmark this approach against traditional methods that are commonly used in industry.
- What evidence would resolve it: A head-to-head comparison of the SHAP-based method with traditional anomaly detection techniques using the same datasets and manufacturing defect scenarios, measuring detection accuracy, false positive rates, and computational efficiency.

## Limitations

- The method relies on synthetic data generation, limiting confidence in real-world applicability without further validation
- Computational complexity of SHAP calculations poses scalability challenges for large sensor arrays
- Choice of similarity metrics lacks rigorous justification for their relative effectiveness across different variation scenarios
