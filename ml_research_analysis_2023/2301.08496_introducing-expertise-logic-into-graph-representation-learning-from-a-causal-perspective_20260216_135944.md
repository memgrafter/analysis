# Introducing Expertise Logic into Graph Representation Learning from A Causal Perspective

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08496
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08496
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: A novel framework (CLGL) that incorporates human expertise logic into GNNs through causal learning, improving classification accuracy and out-of-distribution generalization

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the role of human expertise in graph representation learning by graph neural networks (GNNs). The authors conduct experiments showing that GNNs gradually learn knowledge similar to human expertise during training, and incorporating expertise logic improves model performance. To understand this phenomenon theoretically, they propose a structural causal model (SCM) that decomposes graph-relevant knowledge into expertise logic and inaccessible knowledge. Based on this model, they introduce a novel framework called Causal Logic Graph Representation Learning (CLGL) that uses an auxiliary causal logic learning task and counterfactual training to guide GNNs to learn expertise logic causally related to the graph representation learning task. Extensive experiments on crafted and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of CLGL, with the proposed method outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on classification accuracy.

## Method Summary
The Causal Logic Graph Representation Learning (CLGL) framework consists of a GNN encoder for graph representation learning, an expert system for generating expertise logic predictions, projection heads for calculating KL divergence loss, and a label prediction head for conventional training. The model learns to predict both ground truth labels and expertise logic outputs through alternating optimization, with counterfactual augmentation used to expand training data when labeled expertise logic is limited. The framework is trained using both ground truth labels and expertise logic, with the causal logic learning task guiding the GNN to extract discriminative information beneficial for downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- GNNs gradually learn knowledge similar to human expertise during training, as evidenced by increasing cosine similarity between GNN logits and expert model logits
- Incorporating expertise logic into graph representation learning improves model performance on classification tasks
- The CLGL framework with counterfactual augmentation outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on multiple datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed approach

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of the approach depends on the quality and availability of expertise logic, which may not be readily available for all domains
- The counterfactual augmentation technique relies on the assumption that the relationships between variables in the SCM remain unchanged in counterfactual worlds, which may not always hold
- The framework introduces additional complexity and hyperparameters (e.g., the GNN layer used for causal logic learning) that require careful tuning for optimal performance

## Confidence

- **High**: GNNs can learn knowledge similar to human expertise during training (supported by empirical observations)
- **Medium**: Incorporating expertise logic improves model performance through causal mechanisms (theoretical justification exists but practical robustness varies)
- **Medium**: Counterfactual augmentation effectively addresses insufficient training data for causal logic learning (works in tested scenarios but theoretical guarantees depend on augmentation quality)
