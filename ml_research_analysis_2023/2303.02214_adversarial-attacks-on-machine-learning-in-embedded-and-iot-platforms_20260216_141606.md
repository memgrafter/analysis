# Adversarial Attacks on Machine Learning in Embedded and IoT Platforms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02214
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02214
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Model compression techniques like quantization, pruning, and knowledge distillation can both improve and worsen adversarial robustness in embedded ML systems, with complex relationships that require further study.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines the intersection of adversarial attacks and model compression in embedded and IoT systems. While adversarial attacks threaten ML model integrity through various methods including evasion, poisoning, backdoor, and exploratory attacks, model compression techniques are essential for deploying these models on resource-constrained platforms. The work surveys existing research on how compression affects adversarial robustness, finding that relationships are complex - quantization can both improve and worsen robustness depending on context, while pruning and knowledge distillation show promise as defensive mechanisms. The analysis highlights critical security considerations for deploying compressed ML models in embedded environments.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a comprehensive literature survey examining how adversarial attacks interact with model compression techniques (pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation) in embedded/IoT ML systems. The analysis synthesizes research on white-box attacks (FGSM, BIM, C&W, JSMA), black-box attacks (HopSkipJump, Square), and poisoning/backdoor attacks against compressed models. The work evaluates model robustness through classification accuracy under attack and compares compressed versus uncompressed models, examining compression techniques as potential defenses. Defensive approaches like Lipschitz regularization, adversarial neural pruning, and diverse knowledge distillation are analyzed for their effectiveness against various attack types.

## Key Results
- Quantization can both improve and worsen adversarial robustness depending on the attack type and quantization level
- Knowledge distillation shows promise as a defense mechanism by creating unique latent feature spaces that resist attack transferability
- Adversarial neural pruning can suppress vulnerable features to improve model robustness against evasion attacks
- Model compression pipelines create new attack surfaces requiring evaluation of combined compression effects
- Gradient masking through quantization provides temporary defense but can be bypassed through model extraction attacks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Model compression can reduce adversarial attack effectiveness by limiting precision
- Mechanism: Adversarial attacks often rely on fine-grained gradient information and high-precision perturbations. When models are compressed through quantization or pruning, the reduced precision and sparser parameter space can make it harder for attacks to craft effective perturbations that exploit the model's gradients.
- Core assumption: Adversarial attacks depend on the full-precision gradients and parameter values to generate effective perturbations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "it was observed that because adversarial attacks often exploit the precision of the data they attack, a reduction in this precision could potentially render an attack less effective"
  - [section II] "evasion attack approaches typically begin by estimating direction sensitivity to class change with respect to each dimension of the input sample"
  - [corpus] Weak - while related papers discuss adversarial attacks on IoT, none specifically address how precision reduction affects attack success rates
- Break condition: When attackers use black-box attacks that don't rely on gradient information, or when compression preserves sufficient precision for attack algorithms to function

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Certain compression techniques can improve adversarial robustness as a defense mechanism
- Mechanism: Knowledge distillation and adversarial neural pruning can create models with latent feature spaces that are more resistant to adversarial perturbations. By training smaller models on the knowledge of larger models or by selectively pruning vulnerable features, the resulting compressed models may be less susceptible to attacks that exploit specific model characteristics.
- Core assumption: The latent feature spaces learned by compressed models are inherently more robust to adversarial perturbations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section IV] "adversarial neural pruning...attempts to minimize the distortion introduced to the latent feature space by the evasion attack"
  - [section IV] "diverse knowledge distillation...argued that as the transferability of evasion attacks is reinforced by similarities in the learned latent spaces for vulnerable features, attacks on the teacher model will fail to transfer to the ensemble's unique assortment of latent feature spaces"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on intrusion detection but don't specifically address knowledge distillation as a defense mechanism
- Break condition: When attackers can adapt their techniques to the specific characteristics of compressed models, or when the compression introduces vulnerabilities that can be exploited

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Compression can introduce gradient masking that temporarily defends against attacks
- Mechanism: Quantization and other compression techniques can cause gradient masking, where the gradients used by attackers become less informative or useful for crafting effective perturbations. This makes it harder for white-box attacks to succeed, though this defense can be bypassed through model extraction attacks.
- Core assumption: Gradient masking is an effective temporary defense against white-box attacks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section IV] "quantization leads to gradient masking, a state in which the model's gradients are rendered less useful to an attacker seeking to estimate direction sensitivity"
  - [section IV] "gradient masking is generally considered to be a weak defense, as, through model extraction, the original gradients can be reconstructed and attacked"
  - [corpus] Missing - no related papers specifically discuss gradient masking in the context of IoT or embedded systems
- Break condition: When attackers employ black-box attacks or when they can perform model extraction to reconstruct the original gradients

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input preprocessing pipeline -> Compressed model -> Inference engine -> Security monitoring layer -> Model update mechanism

- Critical path:
  Data → Preprocessing → Compressed Model Inference → Output → Security Check
  The compression pipeline occurs during model training and deployment, not at inference time

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Higher compression ratios improve resource efficiency but may reduce adversarial robustness
  - Defense-oriented compression (like adversarial pruning) may require more computation during training
  - Mixed-precision quantization offers a balance between efficiency and robustness
  - Pipeline complexity increases when combining multiple compression techniques

- Failure signatures:
  - Sudden drops in model accuracy on clean data (over-compression)
  - Increased vulnerability to specific attack types (imbalanced defense)
  - Higher inference latency than expected (inefficient compression implementation)
  - Model extraction attacks succeeding more easily (gradient masking failure)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare FGSM attack success rates on full-precision vs quantized versions of a standard CNN on CIFAR-10
  2. Test poisoning attack resilience of pruned vs unpruned models on a simple image classification task
  3. Evaluate backdoor attack effectiveness against knowledge-distilled models versus their teacher models on a facial recognition dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different combinations of model compression techniques (e.g., pruning followed by quantization) affect adversarial robustness compared to individual compression methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly calls out the need to study attacks on model compression pipelines and asks whether attack scenarios based on chained model compression techniques retain the characteristics of attacks on each member technique.
- Why unresolved: Most existing research focuses on single compression techniques, with limited understanding of how combined compression pipelines impact adversarial robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing adversarial attack success rates on models compressed using different combinations of techniques (pruning+quantization, quantization+knowledge distillation, etc.) against models compressed with single techniques.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does quantization affect robustness to backdoor attacks compared to its effects on evasion attacks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that while existing work explores the impact of model compression on backdoor attacks, there is emphasis on knowledge distillation and network pruning, with limited exploration of quantization's relationship to backdoor attack robustness.
- Why unresolved: Most research on quantization and adversarial attacks focuses on evasion attacks, with backdoor attacks receiving less attention in the context of compression.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies measuring backdoor attack success rates on quantized vs. full-precision models, examining how different quantization levels affect backdoor vulnerability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does model compression impact the number of queries required for successful exploratory attacks (model extraction or inversion)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly identifies this as an open question, asking whether compression impacts robustness to exploratory attacks and whether it changes the query requirements for model extraction or inversion attacks.
- Why unresolved: No existing research has systematically studied how compression techniques affect the efficiency of exploratory attacks, which could have significant implications for attack detection.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental comparisons of query counts needed for successful exploratory attacks against compressed versus uncompressed models, across different compression techniques and levels.

## Limitations
- Limited quantitative validation across diverse model architectures and attack scenarios
- Context-dependent relationships between compression techniques and robustness outcomes make generalization difficult
- Insufficient empirical evidence to establish definitive conclusions about which compression techniques work best as defenses in which scenarios
- Major uncertainties around effectiveness of compression-based defenses against adaptive attacks

## Confidence
- Medium confidence in core claims - theoretical foundations are sound but empirical support is insufficient for definitive conclusions
