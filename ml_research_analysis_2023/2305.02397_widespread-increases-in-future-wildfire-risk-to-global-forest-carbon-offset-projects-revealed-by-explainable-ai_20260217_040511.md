# Widespread Increases in Future Wildfire Risk to Global Forest Carbon Offset Projects Revealed by Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02397
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02397
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: Wildfire risk to forest carbon offset projects projected to increase 55% [37-76%] by 2080 under SSP2-4.5 scenario

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of accurately predicting long-term wildfire risk to global forest carbon offset projects, which is essential for ensuring carbon credit permanence. The authors develop an explainable AI (XAI) model trained on 7 million global satellite wildfire observations, incorporating 55 geospatial features including fire weather, bioclimatic zones, land cover, topography, and human dynamics. The model achieves an AUC score of 0.79, outperforming the leading CESM2 fire model (AUC 0.68) and previous work by Cooper (2022). When applied to 190 global forest carbon projects, the model projects a 55% [37-76%] increase in fire exposure by 2080 under a mid-range climate scenario, highlighting the growing threat to carbon credit permanence.

## Method Summary
The authors developed an XAI neural network trained on 7 million global satellite wildfire observations and 55 geospatial features. The model uses dense layers with nonlinear transformations and a constrained KBDI (Keetch-Byram Drought Index) layer to ensure monotonic relationships between fire weather and wildfire probability. The constrained final layer applies intercept and scaling terms to KBDI inputs, providing interpretable predictions. The model was validated on 10 million held-out samples and 190 forest carbon projects, then projected future wildfire risk using 28 CMIP6 climate model simulations under three emissions scenarios.

## Key Results
- XAI model achieves AUC of 0.79, outperforming CESM2 (0.68) and Cooper (2022) on wildfire prediction
- Model projects 55% [37-76%] increase in fire exposure to forest carbon projects by 2080 under SSP2-4.5
- Agricultural areas excluded from training due to overwhelming presence of agricultural burning in observational data
- 7 million training samples represent less than 5% of available data, suggesting potential for further improvement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The XAI model improves wildfire prediction accuracy over process-based models like CESM2 by leveraging high-resolution satellite observations and geospatial features.
- Mechanism: The model uses 7 million global satellite wildfire observations and 55 geospatial features (fire weather, bioclimatic zones, land cover, topography, human dynamics) to learn nonlinear relationships between environmental conditions and wildfire occurrence, achieving an AUC of 0.79 compared to 0.68 for CESM2.
- Core assumption: Historical satellite observations and geospatial features contain sufficient signal to predict future wildfire risk better than physics-based simulations alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the model outperforms the U.S. National Center for Atmospheric Research's leading fire model (CESM2) and a previous version (Cooper 2022), with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) score of 0.79 compared to 0.68 for CESM2"
  - [section 2] "We use daily fire data provided by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) based on imagery from NASA's Terra and Aqua satellites"
- Break condition: If future wildfire behavior shifts dramatically due to unprecedented climate conditions not captured in historical data, the model's performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The constrained KBDI layer enables interpretable predictions while maintaining monotonic relationships with wildfire probability.
- Mechanism: The final layer uses intercept and scaling terms (β0 and β1) applied to KBDI input, ensuring wildfire probability increases with drier conditions while allowing feature interpretability.
- Core assumption: The relationship between KBDI and wildfire probability is monotonic and can be captured by a constrained linear transformation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "The single output of this final layer serves as a probability estimate for the binary response variable [fire, no fire]"
  - [section 3.1] "We expect the relationships between input features to be highly nonlinear at a global level"
- Break condition: If the relationship between KBDI and wildfire probability becomes non-monotonic under future climate conditions, the constrained layer may limit model accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Climate model ensemble projections combined with ML predictions provide robust estimates of future wildfire risk to forest carbon projects.
- Mechanism: The model uses 28 CMIP6 climate model simulations under three emissions scenarios to project future KBDI values, which are then input to the trained model to estimate wildfire probability increases of 55% [37-76%] by 2080 under SSP2-4.5.
- Core assumption: Climate model ensemble mean provides reliable projections of future fire weather conditions that can be input to the ML model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.4] "Simulations are from a set of 28 bias-corrected Coupled Model Intercomparison Project phase 6 (CMIP6) models"
  - [section 4] "wildfire risk to carbon offset projects... is projected to increase 55% [37-76%] by 2080 under a mid-range scenario (SSP2-4.5)"
- Break condition: If climate models systematically underestimate or overestimate future drought severity, projections will be biased.

  - Why needed here: The model predicts binary wildfire occurrence (fire vs. no fire), requiring a loss function appropriate for classification tasks.
  - Quick check question: What would happen if you used mean squared error instead of binary cross-entropy for this binary classification problem?

  - Why needed here: AUC provides a threshold-independent measure of model performance, allowing comparison between models without choosing a specific probability cutoff.
  - Quick check question: If a model achieves AUC of 0.5, what does this tell you about its predictive performance?

- Concept: Feature engineering with spatial context
  - Why needed here: Wildfire probability depends not just on local conditions but also on surrounding land cover and topography, requiring features that capture spatial context.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (55 geospatial features) → Dense layers (nonlinear transformations) → Constrained KBDI layer (β0 intercept + β1 scaling) → Output layer (binary probability)
- Critical path: Data preprocessing → Model training (7M samples, 12 hours on 4 GPUs) → Validation on held-out data → Projection using CMIP6 climate inputs
- Design tradeoffs: High-resolution satellite data vs. computational constraints (7M samples < 5% of available data); interpretable constrained layer vs. potential flexibility loss; global model vs. region-specific fine-tuning
- Failure signatures: AUC scores below 0.7; predictions not monotonic with KBDI; poor performance on forest carbon project locations despite good global performance
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on reduced feature set (e.g., only fire weather and land cover) to establish baseline performance
  2. Compare constrained KBDI layer vs. unconstrained dense layer for the final transformation
  3. Validate model performance on US forest carbon projects specifically, comparing against Cooper 2022 as benchmark

## Limitations

- Model's extrapolation capability beyond historical data ranges remains uncertain, particularly for unprecedented climate conditions
- Constrained KBDI layer may limit model flexibility if the relationship between KBDI and wildfire probability becomes non-monotonic under future climate scenarios
- Projections rely on climate model ensemble means, which may systematically underestimate or overestimate future drought severity

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Model outperforms CESM2 with AUC of 0.79 vs 0.68 on held-out validation data
- **Medium confidence**: Projected 55% [37-76%] increase in fire exposure by 2080 under SSP2-4.5 is based on CMIP6 ensemble mean
- **Medium confidence**: The 55 geospatial features capture relevant drivers of wildfire risk
