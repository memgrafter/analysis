# Modelling low-resource accents without accent-specific TTS frontend

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.04606
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04606
- Reference count: 0
- This work proposes an approach for modeling low-resource accents without accent-specific TTS frontends, using voice conversion and multi-speaker multi-accent TTS training.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of modeling low-resource accents that lack dedicated text-to-speech (TTS) frontends, particularly grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) modules. The authors propose a novel approach that leverages voice conversion to transform target accent data into the donor voice's timbre while preserving accent characteristics. By training a multi-speaker multi-accent TTS model on this augmented data combined with recordings, the system can generate speech in the donor's voice speaking with the target accent. The approach demonstrates that accent modeling is possible without developing accent-specific TTS components, requiring only around 2000 utterances of target accent data.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves two main stages: first, augmenting target accent data by converting it to sound like the donor voice using a flow-based voice conversion model; second, training a multi-speaker multi-accent TTS system on the combination of original recordings and synthetic data. The TTS model uses explicit duration modeling and is conditioned on both speaker and accent embeddings. During inference, the system generates speech in the donor voice with the target accent characteristics. The approach relies on using a TTS frontend developed for the same language but a different accent, eliminating the need for accent-specific G2P development.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art results compared to other generative models for low-resource accent modeling
- Demonstrates effective accent modeling with as few as 2000 utterances
- Shows that accent-specific TTS frontends are not required for successful accent synthesis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Accent modeling can be achieved without accent-specific grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) frontend by augmenting target accent data with voice conversion.
- Mechanism: The voice conversion model transforms target accent speech into the donor voice's timbre while preserving the target accent's phonological features. This augmented data is then used to train a multi-speaker multi-accent TTS system that learns the accent characteristics through conditioning on speaker and accent embeddings rather than explicit phoneme-level accent transcriptions.
- Core assumption: Voice conversion preserves accent characteristics while changing speaker identity, and a multi-speaker multi-accent TTS model can learn accent distinctions through conditioning without requiring accent-specific phonetic transcriptions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose an approach whereby we first augment the target accent data to sound like the donor voice via voice conversion, then train a multi-speaker multi-accent TTS model on the combination of recordings and synthetic data"
  - [section] "We use a flow-based voice conversion model [29, 30]... This way we are able to change the speaker identity but preserve the rest of the speaking characteristics, namely accent, style, and duration"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence showing voice conversion preserves accent characteristics, though related work in accent conversion supports this assumption
- Break condition: If voice conversion fails to preserve accent characteristics during speaker identity transformation, or if the multi-speaker model cannot learn accent distinctions through conditioning alone.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A multi-speaker multi-accent TTS model can learn accent differences even when trained with phoneme sequences from a different accent's G2P model.
- Mechanism: The model learns accent distinctions through speaker and accent conditioning embeddings rather than relying on phoneme-level accent-specific transcriptions. The explicit duration modeling and encoder-decoder architecture with conditioning allows the model to learn accent-specific phonological patterns despite receiving phonemes from a different accent's G2P.
- Core assumption: The neural network can learn to map phonemes from one accent's G2P to produce speech in another accent when conditioned on accent-specific information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we can reliably model the accent with augmented data regardless of the chosen TTS frontend"
  - [section] "we empirically found out that training on a multi-speaker multi-accent dataset improves the naturalness" and "We condition both the encoder and decoder on speaker and accent embeddings"
  - [corpus] Weak - limited corpus evidence directly supporting this mechanism, though multi-accent TTS research provides some support
- Break condition: If the model fails to generalize accent differences when trained with phonemes from a different accent's G2P, or if conditioning alone is insufficient to capture accent-specific phonological contrasts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Low-resource accents can be modeled effectively with as few as 2000 utterances through data augmentation and multi-accent training.
- Mechanism: Voice conversion augments the limited target accent data by generating synthetic examples in the donor voice, while training on the larger multi-speaker multi-accent dataset provides additional generalization. The combination allows the model to learn accent characteristics with minimal target accent data.
- Core assumption: Data augmentation through voice conversion provides sufficient coverage of accent variations, and multi-accent training provides regularization that helps the model generalize from limited target accent examples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we demonstrate that low resource accents can be modelled with relatively little data and without developing an accent-specific TTS frontend"
  - [section] "we empirically found out that training on a multi-speaker multi-accent dataset improves the naturalness" and "we can reliably model the accent with augmented data regardless of the chosen TTS frontend"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence quantifying the minimum data requirements, though the paper claims success with 2000 utterances
- Break condition: If voice conversion augmentation fails to capture sufficient accent variation, or if multi-accent training does not provide adequate regularization for low-resource scenarios.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Voice Conversion model -> TTS Acoustic Model -> Vocoder
  - TTS Frontend (donor accent G2P) -> Duration Model -> Acoustic Model
  - Duration Model -> Acoustic Model (upsampling)
  - TTS Acoustic Model (conditioned on speaker and accent embeddings) -> Vocoder

- Critical path: Text → Frontend → Duration Model → Acoustic Model → Vocoder → Audio
  - During training: Ground truth durations are used for upsampling
  - During inference: Predicted durations from duration model are used

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using non-accent-specific G2P frontend simplifies deployment but may introduce pronunciation errors that the model must learn to correct
  - Multi-speaker multi-accent training improves naturalness but increases computational requirements
  - Voice conversion augmentation reduces data requirements but adds complexity and potential quality degradation

- Failure signatures:
  - Naturalness degradation: Model struggles to produce natural speech despite accent similarity
  - Accent mismatch: Model fails to capture target accent characteristics despite conditioning
  - Pronunciation errors: Non-accent-specific G2P introduces systematic pronunciation errors the model cannot correct
  - Duration artifacts: Duration model predictions create unnatural timing patterns

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train VC model on full dataset, convert target accent speaker to donor voice, verify accent preservation through formant analysis
  2. Train multi-speaker multi-accent TTS model with ground truth durations, evaluate naturalness and accent similarity on held-out test set
  3. Train duration model, compare predicted durations against ground truth for accent-specific timing patterns, evaluate impact on accent similarity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of accent for the G2P frontend affect the quality of synthesized speech for different target accents?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that accents can vary in many different aspects and that the performance can be improved further by carefully selecting the frontend used for annotation of the data. It also mentions that the strategy for choosing the best accent for G2P is left for future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic comparison of different G2P frontend choices for various target accents. It only briefly mentions the possibility of improving performance through careful selection.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study comparing the quality of synthesized speech for various target accents using different G2P frontend choices, with objective metrics and subjective listening tests.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed method perform when applied to languages other than English?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions plans to extend the methodology to accents of languages other than English in the future.
- Why unresolved: The current study only focuses on English accents, so the method's effectiveness for other languages is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the proposed method to various languages and accents, then evaluating the quality of synthesized speech using objective metrics and subjective listening tests.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of using different voice conversion models on the quality of the synthesized speech?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a flow-based voice conversion model, but does not compare its performance with other voice conversion models.
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents results using one specific voice conversion model, so the impact of using different models is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the quality of synthesized speech using various voice conversion models, with objective metrics and subjective listening tests.

## Limitations

- The voice conversion mechanism's ability to preserve accent characteristics while changing speaker identity remains empirically unproven
- The multi-speaker multi-accent TTS model's capacity to learn accent distinctions through conditioning alone when trained with phonemes from a different accent's G2P is not fully validated
- The claim that 2000 utterances are sufficient for effective accent modeling lacks quantitative supporting evidence

## Confidence

- High confidence: The technical feasibility of the overall approach (voice conversion + multi-accent TTS training) and the observation that multi-speaker training improves naturalness
- Medium confidence: The claim that accent-specific frontends can be avoided while maintaining reasonable accent modeling quality, and the sufficiency of 2000 utterances for training
- Low confidence: The precise mechanism by which voice conversion preserves accent characteristics, and the extent to which conditioning alone enables accent learning without accent-specific phonemes

## Next Checks

1. **Objective Accent Preservation Analysis**: Conduct acoustic analysis (formant measurements, pitch contours, duration patterns) comparing original target accent speech with voice-converted speech in the donor voice to quantitatively verify that accent characteristics are preserved during speaker identity transformation.

2. **Controlled Accent Learning Experiment**: Train the multi-speaker multi-accent TTS model with identical architectures but different conditioning strategies (e.g., speaker-only vs. speaker+accent vs. accent-only) to isolate the contribution of each conditioning signal to accent modeling performance.

3. **Minimal Data Requirement Study**: Systematically vary the amount of target accent training data (e.g., 500, 1000, 2000, 5000 utterances) while keeping all other factors constant to empirically determine the minimum data requirements for effective accent modeling, and identify performance plateaus or degradation points.
