# Explaining Imitation Learning through Frames

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces R2RISE, a model-agnostic method for explaining imitation learning by evaluating the importance of individual frames in demonstration trajectories. The approach masks random frames, retrains the IL model, and uses environment returns to build an importance map. Experiments on Breakout and BeamRider tasks demonstrate that frames have varying importance levels, and using only the most important frames can improve performance under limited data conditions. The method successfully identifies important frames and shows that importance maps are consistent across different IL models, suggesting transferable insights about frame importance.

## Method Summary
R2RISE is a model-agnostic explanation method that evaluates frame importance in imitation learning demonstrations. The method iteratively masks random frames in demonstrations, retrains the IL model with the masked data, and uses the resulting environment returns as coefficients to build an importance map. The intuition is that demonstrations can be treated as a single image where frames are pixels, and the importance of each frame is revealed by how much policy performance degrades when that frame is masked. The method combines the merits of RISE and ROAR approaches, using multiple retraining iterations to reduce variance and provide a more robust importance estimation.

## Key Results
- Frame importance varies significantly, with performance degrading when different frames are masked
- Models trained with only the most important frames achieve better performance than those using least important frames, especially under limited data conditions
- Importance maps show consistency across different IL models (BC and GAIL), suggesting transferable insights about frame importance
- The method successfully identifies important frames in Atari games Breakout and BeamRider

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Frame importance is revealed by performance degradation when frames are masked
- Mechanism: By iteratively masking random frames, retraining the IL model, and measuring environment returns, the method quantifies how much each frame contributes to policy performance
- Core assumption: The environment return after retraining with masked demonstrations serves as a reliable measure of the importance of the masked frames
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "iteratively masks random frames in demonstrations, retrains the IL model, and uses environment returns as coefficients to build an importance map"
  - [section]: "The intuition is that the input demonstrations are regarded as a single image, and frames in the demonstrations are regarded as pixels"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found for this specific mechanism in related papers
- Break condition: If the environment returns do not correlate with the actual importance of frames, the method would fail to identify truly important frames

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Retraining multiple models reduces variance in importance estimation
- Mechanism: By retraining the IL model multiple times with different random masks, the method reduces the variance in the importance estimation and provides a more robust importance map
- Core assumption: Multiple retraining iterations provide a stable estimate of frame importance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "R2RISE combines the merits of RISE and ROAR, and investigates the frames' importance with respect to the policy's overall performance"
  - [section]: "the retraining process is essential as machine learning models commonly assume that the training and test distribution is similar"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found for this specific mechanism in related papers
- Break condition: If the model's performance is highly sensitive to random initialization, multiple retraining might not reduce variance sufficiently

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Frame importance is transferable across different IL models
- Mechanism: The importance map generated by one IL model can be used to improve the performance of another IL model
- Core assumption: Important frames identified by one model are likely to be important for other models as well
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The importance maps show consistency across different IL models, suggesting transferable insights about frame importance"
  - [section]: "it can be seen in Figure 6 that the model trained with BC's mask demonstrates similar policy performance to the model trained with GAIL's mask"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input demonstrations (trajectories) -> Random mask generator -> IL model trainer -> Environment for policy evaluation -> Importance map accumulator

- Critical path:
  1. Generate random mask
  2. Apply mask to demonstrations
  3. Retrain IL model
  4. Evaluate policy in environment
  5. Update importance map
  6. Repeat for multiple masks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of masks vs. computational cost
  - Length of trajectories vs. granularity of importance map
  - Choice of IL model vs. generality of method

- Failure signatures:
  - Importance map is uniform (all frames equally important)
  - Importance map shows random patterns
  - Retrained models perform similarly regardless of masked frames

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate that masking frames affects policy performance
  2. Test R2RISE on a simple environment with known important frames
  3. Compare importance maps generated by different IL models on the same task

## Limitations
- Computational cost of multiple retraining iterations
- Limited testing to Atari games, effectiveness across diverse environments unverified
- Method depends heavily on environment returns as proxy for frame importance without direct validation

## Confidence

High confidence: The method successfully identifies important frames in tested environments and shows consistency across different IL models.

Medium confidence: The transferability of importance maps across different IL models needs more extensive validation. The reliance on environment returns as a proxy measure for frame importance may not always correlate with true importance.
