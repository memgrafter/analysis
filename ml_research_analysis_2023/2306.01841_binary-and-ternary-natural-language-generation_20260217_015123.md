# Binary and Ternary Natural Language Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01841
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01841
- Reference count: 9
- This paper demonstrates the first ternary and binary transformer models for text generation tasks of summarization and machine translation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first ternary and binary transformer models for text generation tasks of summarization and machine translation. The key innovation is a novel combination of statistics-based quantization for weights and learning-based quantization for activations. This approach enables stable training convergence in fully ternary/binary settings, which was previously not possible. The method achieves competitive accuracy while significantly reducing model size and computational complexity, setting new baselines for low-bit quantization in natural language generation.

## Method Summary
The authors propose TBT (Ternary/Binary Transformer), which combines statistics-based weight quantization with learning-based activation quantization. For weights, they use a max-entropy isometric quantization scheme that redistributes weights evenly across quantization levels while preserving magnitude relationships. For activations, they divide layers into non-negative and mixed-value categories and use learned scaling factors to adapt to activation distributions. This combination stabilizes training for autoregressive transformers and enables reasonable accuracy in fully ternary/binary settings.

## Key Results
- Ternary weight 8-bit activation model achieves ROUGE-1 score of 41 on CNN/DailyMail (3.9 points behind full model, 16x more efficient)
- Binary weight model achieves ROUGE-1 score of 35.6 (highly non-trivial score)
- Ternary weight 8-bit activation model achieves BLEU score of 21.7 on WMT16 En-Ro (1.2 points higher than previous SoTA)
- Binary weight model achieves BLEU score of 17.6 (1.5 points behind full model, 16x smaller)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Statistics-based max-entropy isometric weight quantization reduces gradient mismatch and increases quantized weight entropy.
- Mechanism: Redistributes real-valued weights as evenly as possible across quantization levels while scaling weights to preserve magnitude consistency.
- Core assumption: Higher entropy in quantized weights preserves more information, and magnitude consistency prevents gradient vanishing/explosion.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states the method maximizes entropy and reduces gradient mismatch; section shows optimal quantization distributes weights evenly across levels.
- Break condition: If weight distribution is highly skewed, entropy maximization fails and gradient mismatch increases.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Learning-based activation quantization adapts scaling factors to match activation distributions, reducing quantization error.
- Mechanism: Activations split into non-negative and mixed-sign groups; each group quantized symmetrically or asymmetrically with learned scaling factors.
- Core assumption: Activation distributions vary per batch and layer; learned scaling factors can dynamically adjust to reduce quantization error.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions dividing activation layers into categories; section describes learning-based approach to scale activations.
- Break condition: If learned scaling factor diverges or fails to adapt to shifting activation distributions during training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining statistics-based weight quantization with learning-based activation quantization stabilizes training convergence for fully ternary/binary generative transformers.
- Mechanism: Weight quantization handles static parameter storage while activation quantization adapts to dynamic per-batch statistics; their combination mitigates noise compounding in autoregressive decoding.
- Core assumption: The two methods complement each other—weight quantization preserves information entropy while activation quantization adapts to runtime variations.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states this combination enables stable training convergence in fully ternary/binary settings; section demonstrates TBT achieves reasonable accuracy.
- Break condition: If either component is removed, convergence fails or accuracy collapses.

## Foundational Learning

- **Entropy maximization in quantization**: Ensures quantized weights retain maximal representational capacity, critical for maintaining accuracy in low-bit settings.
  - Quick check: If a weight tensor has 100 elements, how many should be assigned to each of three ternary levels under max-entropy quantization?

- **Isometric mapping in quantization**: Preserves magnitude relationships between real and quantized weights, preventing gradient mismatch.
  - Quick check: What scaling factor αT is used to map real weights [-1.5, 1.5] to ternary levels {-αT, 0, αT}?

- **Straight-through estimator (STE) for non-differentiable ops**: Allows gradients to flow through sign/rounding operations during backpropagation in binary/ternary quantization.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: BART/mBART encoder-decoder → multi-head self-attention blocks → feed-forward networks → embedding layers; each layer has weight matrices and activation tensors subject to quantization.
- **Critical path**: Forward pass → weight quantization (stats-based) → activation quantization (learning-based) → attention computation → softmax output → autoregressive decoding.
- **Design tradeoffs**: Higher entropy in quantized weights vs. computational simplicity; learned scaling factors vs. fixed thresholds; ternary precision vs. binary precision.
- **Failure signatures**: Training divergence, vanishing/exploding gradients, large drops in ROUGE/BLEU scores, generated sequences of abnormal length.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Apply statistics-based ternary quantization to BART weights only; measure ROUGE drop vs. baseline.
  2. Add learning-based activation quantization (8-bit) to experiment 1; check convergence and accuracy improvement.
  3. Fully quantize weights and activations to ternary; verify if ROUGE remains within 4 points of full-precision model.

## Limitations

- Claims about entropy maximization and gradient mismatch reduction rely heavily on theoretical formulations without extensive empirical validation.
- Reported improvements evaluated on limited set of benchmarks (CNN/DailyMail, XSUM, WMT16 En-Ro) and may not generalize to other domains or languages.
- Paper does not address potential issues with quantized models in low-resource settings or with long-form generation tasks.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The general approach of combining statistics-based weight quantization with learning-based activation quantization is sound and well-motivated by existing literature.
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific entropy maximization formula for ternary quantization and its claimed benefits for gradient preservation are theoretically justified but lack direct empirical validation.
- **Medium Confidence**: The learning-based activation quantization method's effectiveness depends on the assumption that activation distributions remain stable enough for learned scaling factors to adapt.
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about first non-trivial baselines for fully ternary and fully binary settings are difficult to verify without access to exact implementation details.
