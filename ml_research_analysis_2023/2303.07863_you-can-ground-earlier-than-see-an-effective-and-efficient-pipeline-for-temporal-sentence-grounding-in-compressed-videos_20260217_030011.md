# You Can Ground Earlier than See: An Effective and Efficient Pipeline for Temporal Sentence Grounding in Compressed Videos

## Quick Facts

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07863

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach for temporal sentence grounding (TSG) in compressed videos, challenging the conventional paradigm that requires high-level visual features from decoded frames. The proposed Three-branch Compressed-domain Spatial-temporal Fusion (TCSF) framework directly processes compressed video input, extracting and aggregating low-level visual features including I-frames, motion vectors, and residuals. This compressed-domain setting enables more efficient processing while maintaining or improving grounding accuracy across multiple benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
The method introduces a compressed-domain setting for TSG that processes videos in their compressed form rather than decoding them first. The Three-branch Compressed-domain Spatial-temporal Fusion (TCSF) framework extracts three types of low-level features from compressed videos: I-frame features representing spatial information, motion vector features capturing motion patterns, and residual features encoding detailed appearance changes. These features are processed through separate branches with spatial-temporal attention mechanisms and then adaptively fused to produce the final grounding results. This approach eliminates the need for computationally expensive video decoding while leveraging the rich information available in compressed video streams.

## Key Results
- Achieves better performance than state-of-the-art methods on ActivityNet Captions, Charades-STA, and TACoS datasets
- Reduces computational complexity by avoiding video decoding operations
- Demonstrates effective spatial-temporal fusion through adaptive motion-appearance feature integration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The compressed-domain approach works because compressed videos already contain rich spatio-temporal information that can be directly utilized for grounding tasks. Motion vectors encode movement patterns between frames, residuals capture fine-grained appearance changes, and I-frames provide spatial context. By designing specialized processing for each of these components rather than treating compressed video as an intermediate step toward frame-based analysis, the framework can exploit structural information that would be lost during decoding. The spatial-temporal attention mechanisms allow the model to focus on relevant regions and temporal segments, while the adaptive fusion component learns optimal combinations of motion and appearance information for different grounding scenarios.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Input Compressed Video -> Feature Extraction (I-frame, Motion Vector, Residual) -> Three Separate Processing Branches -> Spatial-Temporal Attention -> Adaptive Fusion -> Grounding Output

Critical Path:
Compressed video input flows through feature extraction, then parallel processing in three branches, followed by attention mechanisms, fusion layer, and finally the grounding prediction module.

Design Tradeoffs:
The framework trades off between leveraging pre-encoded motion information versus computing motion from decoded frames. Using compressed features reduces computational overhead but requires specialized processing pipelines. The three-branch design increases model complexity but enables specialized treatment of different feature types.

Failure Signatures:
Performance degradation may occur when motion vectors are unreliable (e.g., complex camera movements) or when residual information is insufficient due to high compression rates. The adaptive fusion mechanism may struggle with balancing motion and appearance features when one modality dominates or becomes noisy.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to three specific datasets (ActivityNet Captions, Charades-STA, TACoS), potentially missing diverse video content scenarios
- Performance improvements measured through standard metrics without clear practical significance demonstration
- Computational complexity claims rely on comparisons with decoded-frame methods without detailed hardware requirements or processing time measurements
