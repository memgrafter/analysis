# Conformal Inference for Invariant Risk Minimization

## Executive Summary
This paper develops a method for obtaining distribution-free prediction regions to describe uncertainty estimates for invariant representations, accounting for distribution shifts across different environments. The approach involves a weighted conformity score that adapts to the specific environment in which the test sample is situated. An adaptive conformal interval is constructed using the weighted conformity score, with its conditional average proved under certain conditions. The method's effectiveness is demonstrated through simulation studies and a practical example using real-world data.

## Method Summary
The method combines Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) with adaptive conformal prediction. First, an IRM model is trained to learn an invariant representation that generalizes across environments. Then, split conformal inference is applied with a weighted conformity score based on sample mean and variance similarity between the test sample and each environment's calibration data. The weighted conformity score combines environment-specific quantiles of conformity scores, weighted by the similarity between the test point and each environment. This creates a locally adaptive prediction interval that accounts for distribution shifts.

## Key Results
- Weighted conformity scores adapt prediction intervals to environmental shifts by adjusting weights based on similarity between test point and each environment
- The proposed method achieves conditional coverage by adapting the conformity score to the test point's environment through similarity weighting
- The invariance assessment statistic Inv(Φ) provides a practical diagnostic for when the proposed method's theoretical guarantees hold

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Weighted conformity scores adapt prediction intervals to environmental shifts by adjusting weights based on similarity between test point and each environment
- Mechanism: Similarity weights are computed using first and second moments (mean and variance) of the invariant representation across environments. These weights reweight the conformity scores from each environment before pooling
- Core assumption: The similarity between a test point and an environment can be effectively captured by comparing their first and second moments of the invariant representation

- Break condition: If the invariant representation's distribution in an environment is highly skewed or has heavy tails, moment-based similarity may fail to capture true distributional differences

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The proposed method achieves conditional coverage by adapting the conformity score to the test point's environment through similarity weighting
- Mechanism: The weighted conformity score combines environment-specific quantiles of conformity scores, weighted by the similarity between the test point and each environment. This creates a locally adaptive interval
- Core assumption: The similarity weighting scheme correctly identifies which environment(s) the test point most closely resembles, allowing appropriate calibration of the prediction interval

- Claim: The invariance assessment statistic Inv(Φ) provides a practical diagnostic for when the proposed method's theoretical guarantees hold
- Mechanism: Inv(Φ) measures the average variance of environment-specific expectations of the invariant representation. When this value is small, the invariant representation is deemed adequate for the method's assumptions

- Critical path: IRM model training → Moment calculation → Similarity weighting → Quantile estimation → Weighted score aggregation → Prediction interval construction

## Limitations
- The moment-based similarity weighting scheme assumes that first and second moments adequately capture distributional differences between environments, which may fail for highly skewed or heavy-tailed distributions
- The conditional coverage guarantee relies on well-calibrated similarity weights, which could be compromised by insufficient calibration data or poor moment estimates
- The method assumes that environmental shifts are captured by moment differences, which may not hold for more complex distribution changes

## Confidence

- Moment-based similarity weighting captures distributional differences: Medium
- Conditional coverage guarantee holds with well-calibrated weights: Low
- Inv(Φ) is a reliable diagnostic for method performance: Medium
