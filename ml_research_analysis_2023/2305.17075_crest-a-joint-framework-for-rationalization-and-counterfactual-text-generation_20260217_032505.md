# CREST: A Joint Framework for Rationalization and Counterfactual Text Generation

## Executive Summary
CREST addresses the challenge of improving interpretability and robustness in NLP models by integrating selective rationalization and counterfactual text generation. The core method, CREST-Generation, uses a sparse rationalization masker combined with a span-level masked language model editor to produce valid, fluent, and diverse counterfactuals without computationally expensive binary search. CREST-Rationalization then leverages these counterfactuals by training a rationalizer with an agreement regularization term that encourages consistency between factual and counterfactual rationales. This approach improves model robustness on out-of-domain and contrast datasets, achieves higher plausibility and counterfactual simulability in rationales, and outperforms models trained with data augmentation or manual counterfactuals.

## Method Summary
CREST is a joint framework that combines selective rationalization with counterfactual text generation. The method consists of two main components: CREST-Generation and CREST-Rationalization. CREST-Generation uses a SPECTRA rationalizer as a masker to extract sparse, structured rationales, which are then used to condition a T5-based masked language model editor for infilling spans. This approach produces valid, fluent, and diverse counterfactuals without the need for computationally expensive binary search. CREST-Rationalization trains a rationalizer with an agreement regularization term that encourages consistency between rationales for factual and counterfactual examples. By incorporating CREST-generated counterfactuals into training, the model learns to focus on contrastive parts of the input, leading to improved robustness on contrast and out-of-domain datasets.

## Key Results
- CREST-Generation produces counterfactuals with high validity (81.0-94.5%), fluency (low perplexity), and diversity (low self-BLEU)
- CREST-Rationalization achieves higher plausibility (0.82-0.90 AUC) and counterfactual simulability (0.64-0.71 fraction) compared to baselines
- Models trained with CREST-generated counterfactuals show larger improvements on contrast and out-of-domain datasets than those trained with manual counterfactuals or data augmentation alone

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CREST-Generation produces more valid and fluent counterfactuals by using selective rationales instead of gradient-based masks.
- Mechanism: The masker (SPECTRA rationalizer) extracts a sparse, structured rationale z that focuses on tokens relevant to the factual prediction. This rationale is then used to condition the editor (T5-based masked language model) for infilling spans. This approach avoids the computationally expensive binary search used in MiCE and allows the masker to be differentiable, enabling optimization for counterfactual quality.
- Core assumption: The tokens identified by the selective rationale are the most relevant for the model's prediction and thus the best candidates for modification to create a valid counterfactual.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: CREST-Rationalization improves model robustness and interpretability by encouraging agreement between rationales for factual and counterfactual examples.
- Mechanism: A rationalizer is decomposed into factual and counterfactual flows. The factual flow takes the original input x and produces a rationale z, while the counterfactual flow takes the counterfactual input ˜x and produces a rationale ˜z. An agreement regularization term Ω(z, ˜z) is applied to encourage these rationales to focus on the same positions, promoting consistency in the model's reasoning across both inputs.
- Core assumption: If the model focuses on similar parts of the input for both factual and counterfactual examples, it indicates a more robust and interpretable decision-making process.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Masker (SPECTRA rationalizer) -> Editor (T5-small) -> Rationalizer (SPECTRA rationalizer) -> Predictor (T5-small)
- Critical path:
  1. Train the masker and editor on the task dataset.
  2. Generate counterfactuals using the masker and editor.
  3. Train the rationalizer with the agreement regularization using the factual and counterfactual examples.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Masking budget: Higher budget leads to more valid counterfactuals but less closeness to the original input.
  - Agreement regularization weight (λ): Higher weight encourages more consistency but might ignore important differences.
  - Counterfactual loss weight (α): Higher weight emphasizes the counterfactual prediction but might harm the factual prediction.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low validity of generated counterfactuals: The masker might be focusing on irrelevant tokens.
  - Poor fluency of generated counterfactuals: The editor might not be generating coherent text.
  - Lack of improvement in model robustness: The agreement regularization might be too weak or too strong.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the masker and editor on the IMDB dataset and generate counterfactuals with different masking budgets.
  2. Train the rationalizer with and without the agreement regularization on the SNLI dataset and evaluate on contrast and out-of-domain datasets.
  3. Analyze the rationales generated by the rationalizer with and without the agreement regularization to assess their plausibility and counterfactual simulability.

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies heavily on automated metrics for counterfactual quality, which may not fully capture semantic coherence or real-world utility.
- The agreement regularization mechanism lacks extensive ablation studies showing how different weights affect downstream performance.
- Claims about counterfactual simulability and its relationship to model robustness require further validation to ensure they capture meaningful aspects of model behavior.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using selective rationales as masks for counterfactual generation is well-defined and technically sound.
- **Medium confidence**: The agreement regularization approach and its impact on model robustness and interpretability. While the theoretical motivation is clear, the empirical evidence could be strengthened with more extensive ablation studies and comparisons to alternative regularization methods.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about counterfactual simulability and its relationship to model robustness. The metric and its interpretation require further validation to ensure it captures meaningful aspects of model behavior.
