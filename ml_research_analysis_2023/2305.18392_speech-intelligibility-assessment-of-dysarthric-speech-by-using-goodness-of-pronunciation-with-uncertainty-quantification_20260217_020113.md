# Speech Intelligibility Assessment of Dysarthric Speech by using Goodness of Pronunciation with Uncertainty Quantification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18392
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18392
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Prior-normalized maxlogit GoP achieves best correlation with intelligibility scores, improving relative performance by 5.66% (English), 3.91% (Korean), and 23.65% (Tamil) over baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of automatic speech intelligibility assessment for dysarthric speakers by improving the Goodness of Pronunciation (GoP) metric through Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) techniques. Traditional GoP methods struggle with dysarthric speech because neural networks produce overconfident predictions when evaluating out-of-distribution acoustic patterns. The authors integrate UQ methods in two ways: normalizing phoneme predictions (entropy, margin, maxlogit, logit-margin) and modifying the scoring function (scaling, prior normalization). Experiments across English, Korean, and Tamil dysarthric datasets demonstrate that prior-normalized maxlogit GoP achieves the best correlation with intelligibility scores, with fricatives showing consistent correlation across all languages.

## Method Summary
The approach uses a cross-lingual Wav2Vec 2.0 XLS-R model with a linear phone prediction head attached to the convolutional layer, trained on healthy speech datasets (Common Phone, L2-ARCTIC). For dysarthric speech evaluation, various UQ techniques are applied to normalize phoneme predictions and modify the scoring function. Four normalization methods (entropy, margin, maxlogit, logit-margin) are tested with and without temperature scaling and prior normalization. The modified model computes GoP scores using different scoring functions (MaxLogit, LogitMargin, scaled versions) and evaluates correlation with intelligibility severity levels using Kendall's τ coefficient across three language datasets.

## Key Results
- Prior-normalized maxlogit GoP achieves best performance: 0.6127 (English), 0.7474 (Korean), and 0.6759 (Tamil) Kendall's τ correlation
- Relative improvements of 5.66%, 3.91%, and 23.65% over baseline GoP across English, Korean, and Tamil respectively
- Fricatives show consistent strong correlation with intelligibility scores across all three languages
- Logit-based scoring functions (MaxLogit, LogitMargin) outperform probability-based methods by preserving discriminative information lost in softmax

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Normalizing phoneme predictions with temperature scaling or prior normalization reduces overconfidence in neural network posteriors, leading to more calibrated uncertainty estimates for dysarthric speech.
- Mechanism: Neural networks tend to produce overconfident softmax probabilities, especially for out-of-distribution (OOD) inputs like dysarthric speech. By scaling logits with temperature or subtracting log priors, the entropy of the distribution increases, which dampens peaky posteriors and spreads probability mass more evenly across phonemes.
- Core assumption: The model's logits encode meaningful uncertainty signals that can be linearized through simple transformations without requiring re-training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Current GoP methods rely heavily on neural network-driven overconfident predictions, which is unsuitable for assessing dysarthric speech due to its significant acoustic differences from healthy speech."
  - [section]: "Normalizing via the prior is the same with the idea of DNN-GoP, where it is commonly applied to disentangle the training distribution of the phone recognizer..."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; stated assumption that logit transformations improve calibration.
- Break condition: If dysarthric speech's acoustic distortions are so extreme that logits lose semantic meaning entirely, normalization would not recover useful uncertainty estimates.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using logit-based scoring functions (MaxLogit, LogitMargin) preserves discriminative information lost in softmax and correlates better with intelligibility scores than probability-based scoring.
- Mechanism: Softmax function compresses logits into a bounded probability simplex, discarding relative logit differences that may carry information about pronunciation quality. Directly averaging logits (MaxLogit) or taking logit margins (LogitMargin) retains these differences, which align more closely with perceptual speech intelligibility.
- Core assumption: The difference in logits between correct and incorrect phonemes is a more robust indicator of pronunciation error severity than softmax probabilities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Softmax function is often known to squash the useful information inside logits, so that it can normalize the sum into one."
  - [abstract]: "UQ techniques were used on GoP by 1) normalizing the phoneme prediction... and 2) modifying the scoring function..."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; relies on logit-to-intelligibility correlation assumption.
- Break condition: If the neural network's logits are poorly calibrated or corrupted by noise, raw logit margins could amplify errors rather than reduce them.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Prior normalization disentangles training distribution bias from true pronunciation deviation, improving GoP's correlation with intelligibility scores.
- Mechanism: In datasets where certain phonemes dominate, neural networks overfit to these frequent phonemes, making posterior probabilities overconfident for frequent classes. Dividing by phoneme priors adjusts for this frequency bias, producing scores that reflect actual pronunciation deviation rather than dataset bias.
- Core assumption: The phoneme prior estimated from training data accurately reflects the expected frequency in healthy speech and that deviations from this prior are indicative of mispronunciation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Normalizing via the prior is the same with the idea of DNN-GoP, where it is commonly applied to disentangle the training distribution of the phone recognizer..."
  - [abstract]: "UQ techniques were used on GoP by 1) normalizing the phoneme prediction (entropy, margin, maxlogit, logit-margin)..."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; relies on prior estimation accuracy.
- Break condition: If dysarthric speech has fundamentally different phoneme frequency distributions than healthy speech, using healthy speech priors could introduce bias instead of correcting it.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Cross-lingual Wav2Vec 2.0 XLS-R -> Linear phone prediction head -> Logit extraction -> Uncertainty normalization -> GoP scoring
- Critical path:
  1. Train cross-lingual phone recognizer on healthy speech
  2. Extract logits for dysarthric utterances
  3. Apply UQ normalization (prior, scaling, or none)
  4. Compute GoP score using chosen scoring function
  5. Correlate with human intelligibility ratings
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using pre-trained XLS-R vs training from scratch: faster convergence but potential domain mismatch
  - Linear head vs full fine-tuning: preserves phonetic features in conv layers but may limit adaptation
  - Kendall's τ vs accuracy: better for ordinal intelligibility scores but less interpretable for binary mispronunciation detection
- Failure signatures:
  - Low correlation with intelligibility despite high phoneme prediction accuracy
  - Degraded performance on severe dysarthric cases where alignment becomes unreliable
  - Inconsistent UQ method effectiveness across languages
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline GMM-GoP vs NN-GoP vs DNN-GoP on UASpeech to establish performance gap
  2. Implement and compare all four UQ normalization methods (entropy, margin, maxlogit, logit-margin) without scaling
  3. Apply prior normalization across all scoring functions and compare correlation improvements

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of prior normalization assumes that healthy speech phoneme priors are appropriate reference points for dysarthric speech, which may not hold for all dysarthria types.
- The study focuses on Kendall's τ correlation with intelligibility ratings but does not evaluate clinical utility in terms of mispronunciation detection accuracy or false positive rates.
- The approach assumes that neural network logits retain meaningful uncertainty signals when applied to dysarthric speech, which may not hold for severe dysarthria cases.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The mechanism of softmax overconfidence in neural networks is well-established in uncertainty quantification literature, and the experimental methodology for comparing different UQ techniques follows standard practices in the field.
- **Medium confidence**: The specific effectiveness of logit-based scoring functions (MaxLogit, LogitMargin) for speech intelligibility assessment is supported by the results but lacks extensive validation across different neural network architectures or training conditions.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that prior normalization improves GoP scores by disentangling training distribution bias assumes that phoneme priors from healthy speech are appropriate reference points, which may not hold for all dysarthria types.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-validation across dysarthria severity levels**: Evaluate whether the proposed UQ methods maintain their effectiveness across different severity levels of dysarthria, particularly for severe cases where acoustic distortions may fundamentally alter phonetic patterns beyond what the neural network can interpret.

2. **Phoneme-level error analysis**: Conduct detailed analysis of specific phonemes where UQ methods improve or degrade performance, particularly focusing on whether improvements are consistent across fricatives, stops, and vowels, and whether certain phoneme types benefit more from specific normalization techniques.

3. **Comparison with alternative uncertainty quantification methods**: Test whether more sophisticated UQ approaches like Monte Carlo dropout or ensemble methods provide additional benefits over the simple logit transformations used in this work, particularly for extreme dysarthric speech cases where simple normalization may be insufficient.
