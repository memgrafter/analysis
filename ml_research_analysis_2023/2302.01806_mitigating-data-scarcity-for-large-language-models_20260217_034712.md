# Mitigating Data Scarcity for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01806
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01806
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces context-based data augmentation and text simplification methods that improve performance on TechQA and PolicyQA datasets, plus a neural ensemble approach achieving 64.52% word prediction accuracy on medical corpus

## Executive Summary
This dissertation addresses the challenge of data scarcity in neural language models through two complementary approaches: data augmentation and neural ensemble learning. The research introduces a novel context-based data augmentation technique that outperforms state-of-the-art methods on two real-world low-resource NLP datasets (TechQA and PolicyQA). Additionally, it proposes using text simplification to generate diverse training data, demonstrating that neural language models continue to benefit from additional training data even in domains with existing substantial datasets. The work also presents a neural ensemble approach that combines knowledge from multiple pretrained models to achieve improved word prediction accuracy on medical corpora.

## Method Summary
The dissertation presents two data augmentation strategies and one ensemble learning approach. First, context-based data augmentation generates synthetic training examples by leveraging contextual information from existing data, specifically designed to address low-resource scenarios. Second, text simplification is employed to create additional training data with varied writing styles, enhancing model generalization capabilities. The neural ensemble approach combines partial knowledge learned from individual pretrained neural language models, outperforming the best individual model. These methods are evaluated on TechQA and PolicyQA datasets for augmentation approaches, and a medical corpus for the ensemble method.

## Key Results
- Context-based data augmentation outperforms state-of-the-art on TechQA and PolicyQA low-resource datasets
- Text simplification improves neural language model generalization by introducing varied writing styles
- Neural ensemble approach achieves 64.52% word prediction accuracy on medical corpus, surpassing individual model performance
- Models continue to benefit from additional training data even in domains with existing substantial datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The methods work by addressing the fundamental challenge of insufficient training data through creative augmentation and knowledge combination strategies. Context-based augmentation generates relevant synthetic examples that preserve semantic relationships, effectively expanding the training distribution. Text simplification introduces linguistic diversity while maintaining core meaning, helping models generalize across different writing styles. The ensemble approach leverages complementary strengths of multiple pretrained models, combining their partial knowledge to achieve better overall performance than any single model.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Text Simplification Module -> Data Augmentation Pipeline -> Neural Language Model Training -> Ensemble Integration

### Critical Path
Data collection → Text simplification → Context-based augmentation → Model training → Ensemble combination → Evaluation

### Design Tradeoffs
The approach balances computational cost of generating synthetic data against performance gains, and complexity of ensemble combination methods against improvement in prediction accuracy. The text simplification approach trades some potential semantic precision for increased linguistic diversity.

### Failure Signatures
Poor quality control in text simplification can introduce noise that degrades model performance. Ineffective context-based augmentation may generate irrelevant examples that confuse the model. Improper ensemble weighting can lead to suboptimal combination of model strengths.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness of context-based augmentation across diverse NLP tasks beyond TechQA and PolicyQA remains uncertain
- Neural ensemble approach lacks detailed comparison with alternative ensemble strategies and computational overhead analysis
- Research focuses primarily on English datasets, limiting cross-lingual applicability conclusions

## Confidence
- Data Augmentation Generalizability: Medium - Strong performance on TechQA and PolicyQA, but uncertain across diverse domains
- Neural Ensemble Approach Constraints: Medium-High - Promising results but lacks comprehensive comparison with alternative methods
- Methodological Scope Limitations: Medium - Focused approach without exploring complementary techniques
