# Bayesian Deep Learning for Affordance Segmentation in images

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00871
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00871
- Reference count: 30
- Achieves state-of-the-art 86.9% Fwβ score on IIT-Aff dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a Bayesian deep learning approach for affordance segmentation that extends Mask-RCNN with Monte Carlo dropout to produce probabilistic detections. The method quantifies both aleatoric and epistemic spatial uncertainty at the pixel level, enabling better uncertainty-aware affordance reasoning for robotics applications. By placing dropout at different layers of the network, the authors demonstrate improved segmentation quality and better-calibrated uncertainty estimates compared to deterministic baselines.

## Method Summary
The approach extends Mask-RCNN with MC-dropout to generate probabilistic segmentations. Multiple forward passes with dropout enabled produce stochastic mask predictions that are merged using Best Single Assignment (BSAS) clustering with IoU threshold 0.5. The model computes spatial uncertainty as the sum of epistemic variance (model uncertainty) and aleatoric variance (data uncertainty) at each pixel. The architecture is evaluated with both deterministic metrics (Fwβ) and probabilistic metrics (PMQ, PDQ) on the IIT-Aff dataset using ResNet-50 and ResNeXt-101 backbones.

## Key Results
- Bayesian Mask-RCNN achieves 86.9% Fwβ score on IIT-Aff dataset, outperforming deterministic baselines
- Successfully distinguishes between aleatoric uncertainty (at object contours due to camera noise) and epistemic uncertainty (in visually challenging regions)
- Introduces PMQ metric that provides finer-grained evaluation through pixel-wise mask comparison
- MC-dropout at backbone encoder produces better-calibrated uncertainty and improves segmentation quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MC-dropout at the backbone encoder produces better-calibrated epistemic uncertainty and improves segmentation quality
- Mechanism: Dropout in the backbone forces the model to learn robust, probabilistic latent representations rather than overfitting to deterministic patterns
- Core assumption: The backbone's feature extraction is the primary source of representational power, so Bayesian regularization there propagates to the mask branch
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We attribute this improvement to a better probabilistic feature space representation on the encoder"
  - [section III-A] "When we place the dropout at the backbone, it regularizes the largest part of the net and enforces the backbone to learn probabilistic representations in the latent space"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Averaging multiple stochastic forward passes refines mask contours and reduces aleatoric uncertainty
- Mechanism: Multiple mask predictions smooth out high-frequency noise at object boundaries while preserving the mean shape, effectively denoising the mask
- Core assumption: Aleatoric uncertainty is concentrated at object boundaries due to camera noise and occlusion
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "aleatoric variance in the contours of the objects due to the camera noise"
  - [section III-C] "we present a unified approach that computes the spatial uncertainty σsp(x) as the sum of the epistemic and aleatoric variance"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mask-level comparison in PMQ metric provides more fine-grained evaluation than bounding box IoU
- Mechanism: By comparing pixel-wise masks rather than boxes, the metric captures spatial uncertainty and semantic alignment at the object boundary level
- Core assumption: Instance segmentation performance is more accurately measured by mask overlap than box overlap
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section IV-B] "the advantage of comparing pixel-wise masks in our probabilistic instance segmentation rather than bounding boxes"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Monte Carlo dropout as variational inference approximation
  - Why needed here: Provides a computationally tractable way to estimate posterior uncertainty without retraining
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between dropout rate and the variance of the approximate posterior?

- Concept: Aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty distinction
  - Why needed here: Different sources of uncertainty require different handling strategies in robotics applications
  - Quick check question: How would you expect epistemic uncertainty to change as you add more diverse training data?

- Concept: Instance segmentation evaluation metrics (Fwβ, PMQ)
  - Why needed here: Standard metrics don't capture the probabilistic nature of the predictions
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between PMQ and traditional mAP in how they handle uncertainty?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Backbone (ResNet-50/ResNeXt-101) -> RPN -> RoIAlign -> Box head (with dropout) -> Mask head (with dropout) -> Merging module (BSAS clustering) -> Uncertainty module (epistemic/aleatoric variance)

- Critical path:
  Forward pass → Dropout sampling → Mask generation → Merging → Uncertainty estimation → Evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Dropout rate vs calibration: Higher dropout increases uncertainty estimates but may reduce accuracy
  - Number of samples M vs runtime: More samples improve uncertainty estimates but increase inference time
  - Backbone capacity vs generalization: Larger backbones improve performance but may overfit without Bayesian regularization

- Failure signatures:
  - Overconfident predictions with high ACE/AUSE scores
  - Poor calibration indicated by flat or overconfident confidence histograms
  - Merging failures when IoU threshold is too high or too low

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of Bayesian affordance segmentation scale with larger datasets or more diverse environments beyond the IIT-Aff dataset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that epistemic uncertainty is reduced as the dataset increases and covers more variability, but does not explore performance beyond the IIT-Aff dataset.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to a single dataset, so the generalizability to more diverse or larger-scale scenarios remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the Bayesian Mask-RCNN on multiple datasets with varying object types, environments, and scales to evaluate performance consistency and uncertainty handling.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different dropout rates (other than 0.5) on the trade-off between calibration accuracy and model performance in affordance segmentation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a grid search to set the dropout rate at 0.5 but mentions that pd = 0.7 introduces too much variability and pd = 0.1 makes the model too deterministic.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the full range of dropout rates or their nuanced effects on calibration and performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the dropout rate across a broader range and analyzing its effects on calibration metrics (ACE, AUSE) and task performance (Fwβ, PMQ).

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the Bayesian affordance segmentation method perform in real-time robotics applications where computational efficiency is critical?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on achieving state-of-the-art performance and uncertainty quantification but does not address computational efficiency or real-time constraints.
- Why unresolved: The method involves multiple forward passes (MC-dropout) and merging strategies, which may be computationally expensive for real-time use.
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking the method’s inference time and memory usage on robotics hardware and comparing it to deterministic baselines in real-time scenarios.

## Limitations

- Limited generalization beyond the IIT-Aff dataset to other affordance segmentation tasks
- Computational overhead of MC-dropout sampling (multiple forward passes) may limit real-time applications
- The PMQ metric, while novel, requires careful implementation of the BSAS clustering and foreground heatmap computation

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The Bayesian Mask-RCNN architecture successfully extends Mask-RCNN with MC-dropout for probabilistic segmentation; the method achieves state-of-the-art performance on IIT-Aff dataset with 86.9% Fwβ score; aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty can be reliably distinguished in practice
- Medium Confidence: MC-dropout at the backbone specifically improves probabilistic feature representations; mask-level comparison in PMQ provides meaningfully better evaluation than bounding box metrics; the 5-point calibration method effectively improves uncertainty estimates
- Low Confidence: The exact impact of dropout rate on the trade-off between calibration accuracy and model performance; generalization to real-world robotics scenarios with diverse environments
