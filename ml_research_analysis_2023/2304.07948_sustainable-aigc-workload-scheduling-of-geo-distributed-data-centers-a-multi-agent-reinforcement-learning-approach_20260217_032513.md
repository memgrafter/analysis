# Sustainable AIGC Workload Scheduling of Geo-Distributed Data Centers: A Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Approach

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07948
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07948
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: Up to 28.6% improvement in system utility for scheduling AIGC workloads across geo-distributed data centers

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the sustainability challenges of scheduling AI-generated content (AIGC) workloads in geo-distributed data centers by proposing a multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) approach. The authors develop a MASAC algorithm that leverages actor-critic methods to balance GPU utilization, energy cost, and carbon emissions through collaborative scheduling decisions. Using real-world workload traces, energy prices, and carbon intensity data, the method demonstrates up to 28.6% improvement in system utility compared to baseline approaches, achieving 15.6% higher GPU utilization while maintaining competitive cost and carbon efficiency.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a Multi-Agent Soft Actor-Critic (MASAC) algorithm for scheduling AIGC workloads across geo-distributed data centers. The approach uses 5 agents, each responsible for a data center, which learn policies through actor-critic methods to optimize a system utility comprising GPU profit, idle GPU cost, carbon cost, job migration cost, and result retrieval cost. Agents make decisions based on local state information including job characteristics, available GPUs, queue status, energy price, and carbon intensity. The algorithm is trained on 10 environments and validated/tested on separate environments with scaled workloads, demonstrating up to 28.6% improvement in system utility compared to baseline approaches.

## Key Results
- MASAC achieves up to 28.6% improvement in system utility compared to baseline approaches
- 15.6% higher GPU utilization while maintaining competitive cost and carbon efficiency
- Effective avoidance of unnecessary job migrations through learned policies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The MASAC algorithm improves system utility by balancing GPU utilization, energy cost, and carbon emissions through collaborative scheduling decisions.
- Mechanism: The multi-agent reinforcement learning approach enables each data center agent to make decisions based on local state information while contributing to a global optimization objective. The actor-critic design allows for better estimation of long-term rewards and addresses delayed reward structures inherent in job scheduling.
- Core assumption: Agents can learn effective policies through interaction with the environment that capture the complex relationships between job scheduling decisions and system utility components.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proposed MASAC algorithm leverages actor-critic methods to balance GPU utilization, energy cost, and carbon emissions through collaborative scheduling decisions."
  - [section III] "Each agent acts based on its policy πθn, which is a probability distribution of discrete actions conditioned on the state. The policy is iteratively updated throughout the training, with the agent collecting trajectories of states, actions, and rewards from the environment."
- Break condition: The learning process may fail to converge if the state and action spaces are too large or if the reward signal is too sparse, making it difficult for agents to learn meaningful policies.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The actor-critic design with entropy regularization in MASAC balances exploration and exploitation optimally, leading to improved performance compared to other algorithms.
- Mechanism: The Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm uses two critic networks to stabilize learning and incorporates an entropy regularization term that encourages exploration of different actions. This helps the agents avoid getting stuck in suboptimal policies and discover better scheduling strategies.
- Core assumption: The entropy regularization term effectively encourages exploration without compromising the exploitation of known good policies.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III] "The SAC algorithm optimizes a stochastic policy in an off-policy approach and balances exploration and exploitation through entropy regularization. Besides, two critic networks Qϕ andQϕ′ are employed to stabilize the learning process."
- Break condition: If the entropy regularization coefficient is not properly tuned, it may either lead to excessive exploration or insufficient exploration, both of which can degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The collaborative nature of the multi-agent approach eliminates single points of failure and enables scalability as the network grows.
- Mechanism: By distributing decision-making among multiple agents, each responsible for a data center, the system becomes more robust to individual agent failures. Additionally, the architecture can easily accommodate new data centers by adding corresponding agents without requiring significant changes to the overall system.
- Core assumption: The communication overhead between agents remains manageable as the number of data centers increases.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section I] "MARL eliminates the single point of failure in the central scheduling system and is scalable when the network grows."
- Break condition: As the number of data centers increases, the communication overhead between agents may become a bottleneck, potentially limiting the scalability of the approach.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)
  - Why needed here: The problem involves multiple data centers that need to coordinate their scheduling decisions to optimize a global objective. MARL provides a framework for distributed decision-making with a shared goal.
  - Quick check question: How does MARL differ from single-agent RL in terms of state and action spaces?

- Concept: Actor-Critic Methods
  - Why needed here: The job scheduling problem involves delayed rewards, as the consequences of scheduling decisions may not be immediately apparent. Actor-critic methods can better estimate long-term rewards and guide policy updates accordingly.
  - Quick check question: What is the role of the critic network in actor-critic methods, and how does it help with delayed rewards?

- Concept: Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)
  - Why needed here: The job scheduling problem involves multiple objectives (GPU utilization, energy cost, carbon emissions) and requires a balance between exploration and exploitation. SAC's entropy regularization helps achieve this balance.
  - Quick check question: How does the entropy regularization term in SAC encourage exploration, and what is its effect on the policy update?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data centers (AUS-SA, AUS-WA, CA-ON, PL, SG) -> Communication network -> Multi-agent RL system (5 agents) -> AIGC workload scheduling decisions
- Critical path:
  1. Job arrival at a data center
  2. Agent observes local state (job information, available GPUs, queue status, energy price, carbon intensity)
  3. Agent selects action (execute, postpone, or migrate job)
  4. System transitions to new state and provides reward
  5. Agent updates policy based on observed reward and next state
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Centralized vs. decentralized scheduling: The multi-agent approach offers scalability and robustness but may introduce communication overhead.
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: The SAC algorithm balances these through entropy regularization, but the trade-off must be carefully tuned.
  - Reward shaping: The choice of reward function (system utility) directly influences the learned policies and must capture the desired optimization objectives.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor convergence: Agents may fail to learn effective policies if the state and action spaces are too large or the reward signal is too sparse.
  - Suboptimal scheduling: Agents may get stuck in local optima if the exploration-exploitation balance is not properly tuned.
  - Communication bottlenecks: As the number of data centers increases, the communication overhead between agents may become a bottleneck, limiting scalability.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare the performance of MASAC with a single-agent RL approach to demonstrate the benefits of the multi-agent architecture.
  2. Vary the entropy regularization coefficient in SAC to find the optimal balance between exploration and exploitation.
  3. Test the scalability of MASAC by gradually increasing the number of data centers and measuring the impact on communication overhead and learning performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the proposed MASAC algorithm perform with heterogeneous GPU architectures across data centers instead of the assumed homogeneous setup?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly assumes homogeneous GPU architecture, noting that "A homogeneous GPU architecture is considered as cloud providers typically purchase GPUs in bulk."
- Why unresolved: The current algorithm and evaluation are based on homogeneous GPU assumptions, but real-world data centers often have heterogeneous GPU resources.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance comparisons between homogeneous and heterogeneous GPU scenarios using the same MASAC framework with varying GPU architectures.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of varying job arrival patterns and peak demand periods on the MASAC algorithm's ability to maintain GPU utilization and cost efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses workload synthesis based on Alibaba GPU Cluster Trace but notes "The amount of jobs is scaled by coefficients δn to different levels, where SG and PL frequently experience elevated demand and insufficient GPUs."
- Why unresolved: While different job volumes are tested, the paper doesn't explore how dynamic arrival patterns or extreme peak demand periods affect scheduling decisions and performance metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing MASAC with varying job arrival patterns (e.g., bursty arrivals, seasonal peaks) and measuring changes in GPU utilization, cost efficiency, and carbon efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is the MASAC algorithm to changes in energy prices, carbon intensity, and transmission costs over time?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses real-world energy price and carbon intensity data but doesn't conduct sensitivity analysis on these parameters or transmission costs.
- Why unresolved: The current evaluation uses fixed energy price and carbon intensity data without exploring how fluctuations in these parameters affect the algorithm's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Sensitivity analysis by varying energy prices, carbon intensities, and transmission costs within realistic ranges and measuring the impact on system utility, GPU utilization, and cost efficiency.

## Limitations
- Scalability concerns: The paper doesn't thoroughly investigate the impact of increasing the number of data centers on communication overhead and learning performance.
- Reward shaping complexity: The system utility reward function combines multiple components with specific weights, and the impact of these weights on learned policies is not thoroughly explored.
- Limited real-world validation: Experiments are conducted using simulated environments based on real-world data, lacking validation on actual data center infrastructure.

## Confidence

- High: The effectiveness of the MASAC algorithm in improving system utility compared to baseline approaches is well-supported by the experimental results.
- Medium: The claims about the collaborative nature of the multi-agent approach eliminating single points of failure and enabling scalability are supported by the design rationale but lack thorough empirical validation.
- Medium: The claims about the actor-critic design with entropy regularization balancing exploration and exploitation optimally are based on the established properties of the SAC algorithm but may require further tuning for the specific job scheduling problem.
