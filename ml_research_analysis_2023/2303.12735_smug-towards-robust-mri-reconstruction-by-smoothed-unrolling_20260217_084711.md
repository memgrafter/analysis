# SMUG: Towards robust MRI reconstruction by smoothed unrolling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12735
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12735
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: SMUG improves robustness against adversarial attacks in MRI reconstruction while maintaining clean accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical issue of instability in deep learning-based MRI reconstruction models against various perturbations. The proposed SMUG method integrates randomized smoothing with deep unrolled MRI reconstruction architectures, applying smoothing at intermediate unrolled denoisers. Through a novel training scheme combining pre-training and fine-tuning with unrolled stability loss, SMUG demonstrates significant improvements in robustness against adversarial attacks, sampling rate variations, and unrolling step changes while maintaining comparable performance on clean data.

## Method Summary
SMUG is a robustness enhancement framework for deep unrolled MRI reconstruction that integrates randomized smoothing (RS) at intermediate unrolled denoiser layers. The method employs a two-stage training approach: first pre-training the unrolled network, then fine-tuning with an unrolled stability loss that encourages consistent outputs under input perturbations. By applying RS specifically at the denoiser components within the unrolled architecture, SMUG maintains reconstruction quality while improving resilience to adversarial attacks, measurement sampling rate changes, and variations in unrolling steps. Experiments demonstrate significant robustness gains on the fastMRI dataset compared to vanilla MODL implementations.

## Key Results
- SMUG significantly outperforms vanilla MODL and variants in robustness against adversarial attacks while maintaining comparable clean accuracy
- Improved resilience to sampling rate perturbations at test time with minimal performance degradation
- Enhanced stability across varying unrolling step configurations during inference
- Demonstrated effectiveness specifically on fastMRI knee dataset reconstructions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SMUG leverages randomized smoothing to create stable prediction regions around input data points. By applying smoothing at intermediate unrolled denoisers rather than only at the final output, the method ensures robustness propagates through the reconstruction process. The stability loss during fine-tuning encourages consistent behavior under perturbations, effectively training the network to maintain performance despite variations in measurement sampling or unrolling depth.

## Limitations
- Evaluation primarily focused on adversarial robustness and sampling rate perturbations, with limited analysis of other potential failure modes
- Comparison against state-of-the-art methods beyond MODL is relatively sparse
- Computational overhead of the RS-based approach needs more thorough benchmarking
- Performance across diverse MRI acquisition protocols or anatomies not investigated
