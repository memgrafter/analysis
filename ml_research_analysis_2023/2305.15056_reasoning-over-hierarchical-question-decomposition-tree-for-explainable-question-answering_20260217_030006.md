# Reasoning over Hierarchical Question Decomposition Tree for Explainable Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15056
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15056
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: RoHT achieves 54.4 EM and 62.3 F1 on dev sets of KQA Pro and Musique datasets respectively

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RoHT, a novel two-stage explainable question answering framework that leverages hierarchical question decomposition to integrate heterogeneous knowledge sources. The framework builds a Hierarchical Question Decomposition Tree (HQDT) to understand complex questions, then conducts probabilistic reasoning over this tree to fuse knowledge from structured knowledge bases and unstructured text. RoHT significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating substantial improvements in exact match scores across benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
RoHT operates in two stages: first constructing an HQDT to decompose complex questions into simpler sub-questions, then conducting probabilistic reasoning over this tree structure. The framework uses a scheduler to determine appropriate knowledge sources (KB or text) for each node, with separate executors for each source type. Answers are aggregated recursively from leaves to root, considering both decomposition and answering probabilities to find optimal solutions. The approach flexibly determines decomposition granularity and integrates heterogeneous knowledge sources while maintaining interpretability through the tree structure.

## Key Results
- RoHT improves EM score by 29.7% on KQA Pro dataset compared to state-of-the-art methods
- RoHT achieves 54.4 EM and 62.3 F1 on dev sets of KQA Pro and Musique datasets respectively
- RoHT outperforms flat decomposition baselines by 45.8% on Musique dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hierarchical question decomposition enables flexible selection of knowledge sources per sub-question.
- Mechanism: The HQDT structure allows each node to decide whether to answer directly from KB/text or decompose further, optimizing for certainty and source availability.
- Core assumption: Different sub-questions have different optimal knowledge sources, and this can be determined dynamically.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose to leverage question decomposing for heterogeneous knowledge integration, by breaking down a complex question into simpler ones, and selecting the appropriate knowledge source for each sub-question."
  - [section 3] "The challenges lie in: 1) How to determine the granularity of question decomposing, since certain complex questions can be directly answered with a knowledge source, and further decomposition increases the possibility of error."
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbors discuss decomposition but not source selection per node

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Probabilistic reasoning over the HQDT integrates knowledge from multiple sources while accounting for uncertainty.
- Mechanism: The framework recursively reasons from root to leaves, maintaining probability scores for each node and aggregating answers from KB, text, and child questions with weighted confidence.
- Core assumption: Maintaining and propagating probability scores allows optimal answer selection despite uncertainty in both decomposition and answering.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we conduct probabilistic reasoning over HQDT from root to leaves recursively, to aggregate heterogeneous knowledge at different tree levels and search for a best solution considering the decomposing and answering probabilities."
  - [section 4.2] "The reasoning process is recursive, from the root to leaves, and constitutes three steps: 1) a scheduler determines the appropriate knowledge sources for a particular question... 2) the corresponding executors output the answers with probabilities; 3) an aggregator aggregates the candidate answers from all the knowledge sources and outputs the best ones."
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbors mention reasoning but not probabilistic aggregation over hierarchical structures

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: HQDT provides superior interpretability compared to flat decomposition approaches.
- Mechanism: The tree structure explicitly shows how complex questions are broken down into sub-questions at multiple levels, with each node's reasoning path visible.
- Core assumption: Users can understand the reasoning process better when it's presented as a hierarchical tree rather than a flat list of steps.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Existing XQA methods focus on reasoning on a single knowledge source... However, integrating information from heterogeneous knowledge sources is essential to answer complex questions."
  - [section 2.2] "Compared with existing works, we are the first to design a hierarchical question decomposition tree for integrating information from multiple knowledge sources."
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbors discuss interpretability but not hierarchical vs flat comparison

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Probabilistic graphical models
  - Why needed here: The framework uses probability scores to represent uncertainty in both decomposition and answering, requiring understanding of how to combine and propagate probabilities through a tree structure.
  - Quick check question: If a node has certainty score 0.8 and its child has score 0.9, what's the combined probability assuming independence?

- Concept: Semantic parsing
  - Why needed here: The scheduler uses semantic parsing to determine if KB is suitable for answering a question, requiring knowledge of how to convert natural language to executable queries.
  - Quick check question: What's the difference between semantic parsing for KB questions versus information retrieval for text questions?

- Concept: Information retrieval and text ranking
  - Why needed here: The scheduler retrieves relevant paragraphs from text corpus using BM25, and the text executor extracts answers from selected paragraphs.
  - Quick check question: How does BM25 score differ from TF-IDF, and why is it better for QA tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: HQDT Builder → Scheduler → Executors (KB executor, text executor, child question solver) → Aggregator → Final Answer
- Critical path: Question → HQDT generation → Probabilistic reasoning (recursive) → Answer selection
- Design tradeoffs: Hierarchical decomposition vs flat decomposition (complexity vs flexibility), KB vs text vs children (source selection precision vs recall)
- Failure signatures: Low EM scores with high variance across question types, scheduler consistently selecting wrong sources, probability scores not correlating with answer quality
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Remove HQDT and use flat decomposition, compare EM scores
  2. Scheduler evaluation: Manually label source suitability for questions, measure scheduler accuracy
  3. Probability calibration: Check if probability scores correlate with actual answer correctness across different question types

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the RoHT framework perform when integrating additional knowledge sources beyond KBs and text, such as tables or images?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that "since RoHT retrieves answers from each knowledge source in a separate way, it could in principle utilize knowledge from more heterogeneous sources such as tables."
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates RoHT on KBs and text corpora, leaving the potential for integrating other knowledge sources unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the performance of RoHT when incorporating tables or images as additional knowledge sources.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different question decomposition granularities on the performance of RoHT?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenge of determining "how to determine the granularity of question decomposing, since certain complex questions can be directly answered with a knowledge source, and further decomposition increases the possibility of error."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic analysis of how different levels of question decomposition granularity affect the model's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study comparing RoHT's performance with varying levels of question decomposition granularity on a diverse set of complex questions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the scheduler's decision-making process affect the overall performance of RoHT, and can it be further optimized?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a scheduler that determines suitable knowledge sources for each question, but also notes that "it is important to use the scheduler to select suitable knowledge sources for each question" and shows a performance drop when the scheduler is removed.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the scheduler's decision-making process in depth or investigate potential optimizations to improve its performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis of the scheduler's decision-making process, including its strengths and weaknesses, along with experiments testing various optimization strategies.

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't provide detailed error analysis showing when and why the framework fails, particularly regarding source selection accuracy
- No comparison with flat decomposition baselines to quantify the benefit of hierarchical structure
- Limited discussion of computational complexity and runtime efficiency for building and reasoning over HQDTs

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (source selection per node): Medium - The concept is sound but source selection accuracy is not empirically validated
- Mechanism 2 (probabilistic reasoning): High - The recursive reasoning approach is well-defined and theoretically justified
- Mechanism 3 (interpretability advantage): Low - No user studies or empirical comparison with flat decomposition interpretability
