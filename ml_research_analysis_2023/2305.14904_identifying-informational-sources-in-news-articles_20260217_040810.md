# Identifying Informational Sources in News Articles

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14904
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14904
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces source attribution task with 83% accuracy on 16 source categories across 28,000 annotations

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive approach to identifying and attributing informational sources in news articles. The authors define 16 categories of sourcing and construct the largest annotated dataset of its kind, containing 28,000 source attributions across 1,304 news articles. They train high-performing models by fine-tuning GPT3, achieving 83% accuracy for source attribution. The paper also introduces a novel task, source prediction, to study the compositionality of sources in news articles, with models achieving up to 81.5% accuracy in predicting when an article is missing a source.

## Method Summary
The authors construct a comprehensive dataset of 1,304 news articles with 28,000 source attributions across 16 source categories. They train source attribution models by fine-tuning GPT3 (Babbage, Curie, DaVinci) on this annotated data, achieving 83% accuracy. For source prediction, they use silver-standard annotations from 9,051 articles to train classification models (FastText, BigBird, GPT3 Babbage) that predict when articles are missing sources, achieving up to 81.5% accuracy. The approach involves separate detection and attribution tasks, with experiments comparing fine-tuned models against zero/few-shot prompting baselines.

## Key Results
- Source attribution models achieve 83% accuracy using fine-tuned GPT3
- Source prediction models reach 81.5% accuracy in detecting missing sources
- Fine-tuned GPT3 models outperform zero/few-shot prompting across most source categories
- Adding source attribution information as additional features does not improve model performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Fine-tuned GPT3 models outperform zero- and few-shot prompting for source attribution.
- **Mechanism:** The fine-tuning process adapts the model's internal representations to the specific task of mapping sentences to sources, leveraging the contextual relationships between text and source labels in the training data.
- **Core assumption:** The task requires understanding document-level context and source patterns that are not captured by generic pre-training.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We show that our dataset can be used to train high-performing models, achieving an overall attribution accuracy of 83% by fine-tuning GPT3."
  - [section]: "We note that GPT3 DaVinci zero-shot and few-shot greatly underperform fine-tuned models in almost all categories (except 'Other')."
  - [corpus]: The dataset contains 28,000 source attributions across 1,304 articles, providing sufficient examples for fine-tuning.
- **Break condition:** If the dataset size or quality is insufficient to capture the patterns needed for source attribution, fine-tuning may not outperform prompting.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Source attribution accuracy degrades when moving from easy (top source) to hard (any source) ablation tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Easy tasks focus on identifying the primary source, which is often the most prominent or mentioned, while hard tasks require distinguishing between multiple minor sources with less distinctive patterns.
- **Core assumption:** The importance and distinctiveness of sources in an article follows a predictable hierarchy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Models achieve up to 81.5% accuracy in predicting when an article is missing a source."
  - [section]: "We design Easy (Top Source), Medium (Secondary) and Hard (Any Source) variations of our task."
  - [corpus]: The dataset shows a hierarchy where the most-used source contributes ~35% of sourced sentences, while the least-used contributes ~5%.
- **Break condition:** If source usage patterns are more random or context-dependent than hierarchical, the accuracy drop may be less pronounced.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Adding source attribution information as additional features (+SA) to models does not improve performance.
- **Mechanism:** The larger language models (GPT3) may already implicitly capture source attribution patterns during pre-training, making explicit source labels redundant.
- **Core assumption:** Pre-trained models have learned sufficient source attribution patterns from their training data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section]: "We were surprised to find that, in general, adding additional information...did not improve the models' performance."
  - [section]: "It could be that the larger models are already incorporating a notion of coreference and attribution."
  - [corpus]: The dataset contains diverse source types and patterns that could be learned during pre-training.
- **Break condition:** If pre-trained models did not encounter sufficient source attribution patterns during training, adding explicit source labels would improve performance.

  - Low detection accuracy indicates the model struggles to identify sourced sentences
  - Poor attribution accuracy suggests the model cannot link sentences to correct sources
  - Inconsistent source prediction results may indicate confounding factors in the ablation setup

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How do source attribution patterns differ across languages and cultures, and how can we develop cross-cultural source attribution models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The authors acknowledge that their dataset is limited to English language sources from Western news outlets, which may not reflect source usage patterns in non-Western journalism.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore source attribution in non-English news articles or compare patterns across different cultural contexts.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Conducting source attribution experiments on multilingual news datasets and analyzing cultural differences in source usage patterns.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How effective are source attribution and prediction models in detecting and mitigating misinformation in news articles?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors mention the risk of source attribution systems falling prey to misinformation and suggest that discourse-structure analysis could help mitigate this risk.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not experiment with the performance of source attribution models on fake news datasets or evaluate their effectiveness in misinformation detection.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Testing source attribution and prediction models on datasets containing both real and fake news articles and measuring their accuracy in identifying misinformation sources.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What are the optimal computational architectures and training strategies for source attribution and prediction models that balance performance and resource efficiency?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The authors use GPT3-based models and BigBird architectures, but acknowledge the need for more efficient models due to computational resource requirements.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore alternative model architectures or training strategies that could achieve similar performance with lower computational costs.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experimenting with different transformer architectures, model sizes, and training techniques to identify the most efficient combinations for source attribution and prediction tasks.

## Limitations

- Dataset limited to English language sources from Western news outlets, reducing generalizability
- Computational requirements for fine-tuning large GPT3 models may limit reproducibility
- Potential risks of models being used to generate misinformation or fake news remain theoretical

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The dataset construction methodology and basic model performance metrics (83% accuracy for source attribution, 81.5% for source prediction) are well-supported by the evidence provided.
- **Medium confidence**: The comparative analysis between different model variants (fine-tuned vs. few-shot prompting) is robust, though the lack of detailed hyperparameter optimization makes it difficult to assess whether results represent true upper bounds.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about the practical utility for journalists and the broader implications for narrative science and planning-based language generation remain largely speculative without empirical validation.
