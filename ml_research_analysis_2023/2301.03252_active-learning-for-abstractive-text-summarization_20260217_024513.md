# Active Learning for Abstractive Text Summarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.03252
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03252
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** Novel IDDS active learning strategy improves abstractive summarization by 58% over passive annotation when combined with self-training, particularly for short summaries

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of costly human annotation for abstractive text summarization (ATS) by proposing a novel active learning query strategy called in-domain diversity sampling (IDDS). The authors demonstrate that IDDS, which selects instances dissimilar from already annotated data but similar to unlabeled data, outperforms both random sampling and uncertainty-based methods across three datasets (AESLC, WikiHow, PubMed) using BART and PEGASUS models. The approach significantly improves ROUGE metrics and factual consistency scores while reducing annotation effort. Additionally, combining active learning with self-learning further enhances performance, particularly for datasets with short summaries.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes in-domain diversity sampling (IDDS) as an active learning query strategy for abstractive text summarization. IDDS selects unlabeled instances that are maximally dissimilar from already annotated examples while remaining similar to the overall unlabeled pool, avoiding the noisy outliers that uncertainty-based strategies tend to select. The authors evaluate this approach across three datasets (AESLC, WikiHow, PubMed) using both BART and PEGASUS models, comparing against random sampling and uncertainty-based methods. They also explore the combination of active learning with self-training, where model predictions on unlabeled data are used to augment the training set. Experiments track performance across varying annotation budgets, demonstrating that IDDS consistently outperforms baselines while requiring fewer labeled examples to achieve competitive performance.

## Key Results
- IDDS significantly outperforms random sampling and uncertainty-based methods in ROUGE metrics and factual consistency scores across three datasets
- Combining active learning with self-training achieves up to 58% improvement in ROUGE scores compared to passive annotation for short-summary datasets
- IDDS requires fewer labeled examples than baseline methods to achieve comparable performance, demonstrating annotation efficiency
- The strategy effectively avoids noisy outliers while maintaining diversity in the selected training instances

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
IDDS works by strategically selecting diverse yet representative examples from the unlabeled pool, avoiding the common pitfall of uncertainty-based methods that tend to select outliers or ambiguous cases. By ensuring selected instances are dissimilar from already annotated data but similar to the overall unlabeled distribution, IDDS maintains coverage of the input space while avoiding redundancy. This approach is particularly effective for abstractive summarization where the relationship between document and summary is complex and diverse examples are crucial for learning robust generation patterns. The combination with self-training further amplifies these benefits by leveraging model predictions on unselected data to expand the training distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Document Corpus -> IDDS Selector -> Human Annotation -> Model Training -> Evaluation -> Feedback Loop

**Critical Path:** The critical path involves selecting diverse instances via IDDS, obtaining human annotations, training the summarization model, and using performance feedback to guide subsequent selections. Each iteration refines the selection strategy based on accumulated labeled data.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The main tradeoff is between diversity (selecting maximally different instances) and representativeness (ensuring selected instances reflect the overall data distribution). IDDS balances this by selecting instances dissimilar from annotated data but similar to unlabeled data. Another tradeoff exists between annotation cost and model performance, with IDDS optimizing this relationship.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor performance may manifest as: 1) Slow performance gains despite increasing annotation budget (indicating suboptimal selection strategy), 2) Model degradation on certain domains (suggesting insufficient diversity in selections), 3) High uncertainty on selected instances (indicating poor representativeness), 4) Disproportionate improvement on short summaries only (suggesting strategy limitations for longer summaries).

**First Experiments:** 1) Compare IDDS against random sampling and uncertainty sampling across annotation budgets, 2) Analyze diversity metrics of selected instances versus unselected pool, 3) Conduct ablation study removing self-training component to isolate its contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper acknowledges several open questions regarding the generalizability of IDDS across different domains and summarization tasks, the optimal balance between diversity and uncertainty in query strategies, and the scalability of diversity computation for very large datasets. The authors also note the need for further investigation into combining multiple active learning strategies and the potential benefits of more sophisticated diversity measures beyond simple dissimilarity metrics.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses exclusively on ROUGE and factual consistency metrics, omitting other critical quality dimensions such as coherence, fluency, and informativeness
- Performance comparisons are primarily against basic baselines, leaving open questions about relative performance to more sophisticated active learning methods
- The 58% improvement claim is dataset-dependent and achieved specifically on short-summary datasets when combining AL with self-training
- Computational overhead of diversity score calculations is not discussed, which could be substantial for large-scale applications

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** IDDS outperforms random and uncertainty sampling on the tested datasets and metrics
- **Medium confidence:** Combining AL with self-training provides additional benefits, particularly for short summaries
- **Medium confidence:** IDDS demonstrates annotation efficiency across different model architectures
- **Low confidence:** The 58% improvement figure represents typical rather than best-case performance
