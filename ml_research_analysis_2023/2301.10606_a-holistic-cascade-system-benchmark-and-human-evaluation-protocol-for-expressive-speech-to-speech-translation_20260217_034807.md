# A Holistic Cascade System, benchmark, and Human Evaluation Protocol for Expressive Speech-to-Speech Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10606
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10606
- Reference count: 0
- This paper addresses expressive speech-to-speech translation by proposing a holistic cascade system that combines multiple prosody transfer techniques

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of expressive speech-to-speech translation (S2ST), which aims to preserve prosodic attributes from source speech while maintaining translation accuracy. The authors identify a significant gap in the field where existing research focuses on isolated aspects of expressivity without standard evaluation protocols or benchmark datasets. To address these limitations, they propose a comprehensive cascade system that integrates multiple prosody transfer techniques, develop benchmark datasets in TV series and audiobook domains, and introduce a human evaluation protocol for assessing expressive preservation across multiple dimensions.

## Method Summary
The proposed holistic cascade system for expressive S2ST consists of two main components: an S2T (speech-to-text) model and a controllable TTS (text-to-speech) model. The S2T model translates spoken input into text, while the controllable TTS model generates target speech using the translated text along with multiple prosodic clues extracted from the source speech. This approach differs from existing methods by simultaneously considering multiple aspects of expressivity rather than focusing on a single prosodic feature. The authors also curate benchmark expressivity test sets from TV series and audiobook domains and develop a human evaluation protocol that allows bilingual annotators to assess multiple expressive dimensions across speech pairs.

## Key Results
- The holistic cascade approach outperforms single-aspect systems in expressive S2ST
- Bilingual annotators can reliably assess expressive preservation quality across multiple dimensions
- The proposed benchmark datasets provide valuable resources for evaluating expressive S2ST systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The holistic cascade approach works by leveraging multiple complementary prosody transfer techniques that have been previously studied in isolation. By combining these techniques in a single system, the authors can capture a broader range of expressive nuances from the source speech. The controllable TTS model serves as a central component that integrates various prosodic clues (such as pitch, rhythm, and speaking rate) extracted from the source speech with the translated text, enabling more natural and expressive target speech generation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Source Speech → S2T Model → Translated Text + Prosodic Clues → Controllable TTS → Target Speech

**Critical Path**: The S2T model's translation accuracy directly impacts the final output quality, as errors propagate to the TTS stage. The controllable TTS model is the critical component that must effectively integrate multiple prosodic clues to generate expressive target speech.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The cascade approach trades off potential latency and complexity for improved controllability and modularity. While end-to-end systems might offer faster processing, the cascade design allows for independent optimization of translation accuracy and expressive speech generation.

**Failure Signatures**: Common failure modes include translation errors that lead to unnatural target speech, inability of the TTS model to effectively integrate multiple prosodic clues resulting in robotic output, and domain mismatch between training data and evaluation scenarios.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to specific domains (TV series and audiobooks) and language pairs
- Results may not generalize to other contexts or conversational speech scenarios
- The human evaluation relies on subjective judgments from bilingual annotators
- Technical challenges remain in effectively integrating multiple prosodic clues in the controllable TTS model
- Error analysis is insufficient to understand specific failure modes
