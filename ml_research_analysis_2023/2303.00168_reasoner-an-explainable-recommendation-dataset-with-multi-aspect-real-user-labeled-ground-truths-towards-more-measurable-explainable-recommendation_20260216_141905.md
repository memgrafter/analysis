# REASONER: An Explainable Recommendation Dataset with Multi-aspect Real User Labeled Ground Truths Towards more Measurable Explainable Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.00168
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00168
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Introduces REASONER dataset with real user multi-aspect labeled ground truths for explainable recommendation evaluation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces REASONER, a novel dataset designed to address critical limitations in current explainable recommendation evaluation strategies. Unlike existing datasets that rely on post-hoc or simulated labeling by non-real users, REASONER provides multi-aspect ground truths labeled by actual users during real recommendation scenarios. The dataset was constructed through a carefully designed video recommendation platform where approximately 3000 users from diverse backgrounds provided feedback on textual and visual explanations across multiple aspects including persuasiveness, informativeness, and user satisfaction. The authors also developed a unified library containing ten well-known explainable recommender models, enabling standardized benchmarking across different evaluation tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors developed a video recommendation platform with structured questions around explainability and recruited approximately 3000 users to provide feedback. Videos were recommended randomly to avoid information cocoons, and users were required to watch even skipped videos to capture true preferences. The dataset includes multi-modal explanations (textual tags and visual previews) and covers various aspects such as persuasiveness, informativeness, and user satisfaction. Quality control was implemented through time-based filtering, consistency checks, and manual review to ensure labeling quality. A unified library containing ten explainable recommender models was developed to enable standardized benchmarking across different tasks.

## Key Results
- REASONER provides multi-aspect real user labeled ground truths for explainable recommendation evaluation
- Dataset includes approximately 3000 users and 4672 videos with textual and visual explanations
- Ten well-known explainable recommender models implemented in a unified framework for standardized benchmarking
- Dataset supports evaluation across multiple aspects: persuasiveness, informativeness, and user satisfaction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Multi-aspect labeling by real users improves explanation quality measurement
- **Mechanism**: Real users label explanations across multiple aspects (persuasiveness, informativeness, satisfaction) in situ during actual usage, capturing nuanced preferences that post-hoc or simulated labeling cannot
- **Core assumption**: Real-time, multi-dimensional feedback from actual users reflects genuine decision-making drivers
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "multi-aspect real user labeled ground truths"; [section 3.1.3]: Design of questions Q1, Q2, Q3; [corpus]: Weak (no direct citations supporting real-user advantage)
- **Break condition**: If labeling questions introduce bias or if users misunderstand the labeling intent, the multi-aspect ground truths become unreliable

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Dataset construction via controlled platform enables high-quality, diverse data collection
- **Mechanism**: Platform recommends videos randomly (no information cocoons) and forces users to watch even skipped videos, capturing both intended and missed preferences
- **Core assumption**: Controlled exposure and forced interaction reveal true user preferences without selection bias
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.1.4 Step 2]: "videos are recommended in a completely random manner"; [section 3.1.4 Step 4]: "we also force her to watch them and answer the questions"; [corpus]: Weak (no citations supporting random exposure methodology)
- **Break condition**: If forced viewing causes negative user experience or dishonest responses, data quality degrades

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Unified library with multiple explainable models enables standardized benchmarking
- **Mechanism**: Ten well-known explainable recommender models implemented in consistent framework allow apples-to-apples comparison across different evaluation tasks
- **Core assumption**: Standardized implementation removes model-specific artifacts that confound comparison
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract]: "develop a library, where ten well-known explainable recommender models are implemented in a unified framework"; [section 5.2]: Lists specific models (EFM, TriRank, LRPPM, etc.) with implementation details; [corpus]: Weak (no citations supporting unified framework benefits)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**: How do different user personality traits influence the selection of explanation types in recommender systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that they collect big five personality information from users and suggests studying the influence of different user personalities on the selection tendency of recommendation explanations
- Why unresolved: While the dataset includes personality information, the paper does not provide analysis on how personality traits correlate with explanation preferences
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies analyzing correlations between personality traits and explanation preferences using the REASONER dataset, potentially leading to personalized explanation strategies

**Open Question 2**: What is the optimal balance between multi-modal (textual and visual) explanations for maximizing user satisfaction and recommendation effectiveness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that about 38% of users tend to select both textual and visual explanations, while others prefer single-modal explanations, suggesting an opportunity to study multi-modal explanations
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the dataset with multi-modal explanations but does not investigate the optimal combination or balance of modalities for different users or contexts
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments testing different combinations of textual and visual explanations on user satisfaction, persuasiveness, and informativeness metrics

**Open Question 3**: How can explainable recommender systems be designed to simultaneously optimize for multiple explanation aspects (persuasiveness, informativeness, and user satisfaction) without trade-offs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights that most previous studies evaluate explanations based on only one aspect, while REASONER supports multi-aspect evaluation, presenting an opportunity to study multi-aspect explainable recommendation
- Why unresolved: While the dataset supports multi-aspect evaluation, the paper does not explore how to design models that can optimize for multiple aspects simultaneously or whether trade-offs exist between these aspects
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of multi-task learning models that can generate explanations optimized for multiple aspects, along with analysis of potential trade-offs or synergies between these aspects

## Limitations
- **Ecological validity concern**: The controlled platform environment (forced video watching, structured questioning) may not reflect natural recommendation scenarios, limiting generalizability to real-world applications
- **Quality control subjectivity**: Quality control methods (time-based filtering, consistency checks, manual review) lack quantitative validation and may introduce their own biases
- **Limited model diversity**: The unified library contains ten traditional machine learning models but lacks modern transformer-based or LLM-powered explainers, representing a significant gap given current research trends

## Confidence
**High confidence** (Strong supporting evidence, well-established methodology):
- Multi-aspect labeling improves explanation quality measurement compared to single-aspect approaches
- The dataset provides unique real-user labeled ground truths for explainable recommendation
- The unified library framework enables standardized benchmarking across models

**Medium confidence** (Reasonable assumptions but limited direct evidence):
- Random exposure methodology eliminates selection bias in data collection
- Forced video watching reveals true user preferences rather than stated preferences
- Quality control rules effectively identify and remove unreliable labels

**Low confidence** (Significant gaps in evidence or methodology):
- Claims about dataset size (3000 users, 4672 videos) without external validation
- Assertions that current explainable recommendation evaluation strategies are fundamentally flawed
- Assumptions about user honesty and engagement in the controlled platform environment
