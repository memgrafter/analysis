# TeCS: A Dataset and Benchmark for Tense Consistency of Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.13740
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13740
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Primary result**: Introduces tense prediction accuracy as a more reliable metric than BLEU or COMET for measuring tense consistency in machine translation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical issue of tense consistency in machine translation by presenting a novel parallel tense test set containing 552 French-English utterance pairs. The authors introduce tense prediction accuracy as a benchmark metric specifically designed to measure tense consistency performance. The test set focuses on tense-rich, error-prone parallel utterances and aims to provide a linguistic perspective for evaluating tense prediction ability. Experiments with various baseline models and business translators demonstrate the validity and effectiveness of the proposed test set and benchmark in capturing tense consistency performance.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed a French-English parallel tense test set by first using automatic annotation with spaCy to label tense structures, then filtering 20,000 sentence triples for tense mismatches, and finally having human reviewers validate and correct the annotations. They trained multiple NMT architectures (Transformer, LSTM, CNN, Bi-Transformer) using fairseq on both tense-rich and tense-poor datasets derived from the Europarl corpus. Evaluation was performed using three metrics: SacreBLEU, COMET, and the newly introduced tense prediction accuracy, which measures whether the predicted sentence's tense matches the reference sentence's tense.

## Key Results
- Tense prediction accuracy benchmark captures tense consistency performance more reliably than BLEU or COMET scores
- Systems trained on tense-rich datasets showed significantly better tense prediction accuracy than those trained on tense-poor datasets
- The benchmark effectively distinguished between different architectures and commercial translators in terms of tense consistency performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The tense prediction accuracy benchmark captures tense consistency performance more reliably than BLEU or COMET.
- Mechanism: Tense prediction accuracy directly measures whether the predicted sentence's tense matches the reference sentence's tense, focusing specifically on the temporal aspect of translation.
- Core assumption: Tense consistency is a critical dimension of translation quality that existing metrics like BLEU and COMET do not adequately capture.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We also introduce a corresponding benchmark, tense prediction accuracy. With the tense test set and the benchmark, researchers are able to measure the tense consistency performance of machine translation systems for the first time."
  - [section]: "The tense consistency performance of systems trained on the tense-rich train set was significantly better than that of systems trained on the tense-poor train set. This indicates that our tense set can capture the tense consistency performance."
- Break condition: If the tense annotation method introduces systematic errors, the benchmark would be unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The test set construction method, using automatic annotation followed by manual screening and review, creates a high-quality tense-consistent corpus.
- Mechanism: The process starts with automatic tense labeling using spaCy, then filters sentences with tense inconsistencies, and finally has human reviewers validate and correct the annotations.
- Core assumption: The combination of automatic and manual annotation can produce a reliable and representative test set.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Based on these grammatical rules, we merge the nine common tenses in English into seven categories that correspond reasonably and rigorously to French..."
  - [section]: "We choose the tense-rich EuroparlPV, namely EuroparlPV, processed by Loáiciga et al. (2014) as the source corpus, for it contains all the sentences with predicate verb structures in the original Europarl dataset (Koehn, 2005)."
- Break condition: If the automatic annotation method is not robust to edge cases or the human reviewers introduce bias, the test set quality would be compromised.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Controlling for gender coordination by defaulting pronouns to masculine eliminates a confounding factor in tense consistency measurement.
- Mechanism: In French, pronouns can have different forms based on gender, which can affect the tense structure of sentences. By standardizing pronouns, the impact of gender coordination on tense consistency is removed.
- Core assumption: Gender coordination affects tense prediction ability and should be controlled for in a tense-focused evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Unlike English, gender coordination exists in French. For example, the French sentences 'Nous nous sommes donc abstenus.' and 'Nous nous sommes donc abstenues.' both correspond to the English 'We therefore abstained.'"
  - [section]: "Therefore, to better measure the tense-predicting capability of different MT systems, rather than their ability to recognize pronominal gender, we controlled for the gender variable by defaulting all pronouns, which do not indicate explicitly their genders, as masculine."
- Break condition: If the standardization of pronouns significantly alters the naturalness or meaning of sentences, it could introduce artifacts.

## Limitations
- The tense test set size (552 utterances) is relatively small, which may limit statistical power and generalizability across diverse translation domains.
- The automatic annotation process using spaCy, while efficient, introduces potential errors that were only partially mitigated through manual screening.
- The focus on French-English specifically may limit the applicability of findings to other language pairs.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core finding that tense prediction accuracy captures aspects of translation quality not reflected in BLEU or COMET scores is well-supported by the experimental results showing significant performance differences between tense-rich and tense-poor trained models.
- **Medium confidence**: The validity of the tense prediction accuracy metric as a general benchmark is supported by experimental evidence, but would benefit from validation across additional language pairs and domains beyond French-English and the Europarl corpus.
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of the automatic annotation followed by manual screening process is supported by the authors' methodology, but the specific error rates and types of errors introduced by spaCy are not quantified.
