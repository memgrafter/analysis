# Commonsense Knowledge Transfer for Pre-trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02388
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02388
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: This paper presents a commonsense knowledge transfer framework to enhance pre-trained language models' reasoning abilities by transferring knowledge from neural commonsense knowledge models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework for transferring commonsense knowledge from neural commonsense knowledge models (like COMET) to pre-trained language models (like T5). The method extracts commonsense knowledge in text form and uses it to create self-supervised training data for continual pre-training. By employing two complementary objectives - commonsense text infilling and commonsense relation prediction - the framework aligns natural language with underlying commonsense knowledge. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements on commonsense reasoning tasks, particularly in few-shot settings, without extensive supervision.

## Method Summary
The framework extracts commonsense knowledge tuples (subject, relation, object) from neural commonsense models like COMET-ATOMIC 2020 using natural text as prompts. These tuples are then used to construct self-supervised training data with two objectives: commonsense text infilling (generative) and commonsense relation prediction (discriminative). The model is continually pre-trained on this data using a T5 backbone, then fine-tuned on downstream commonsense reasoning tasks. The approach aims to transfer implicit commonsense knowledge from specialized neural models to general-purpose language models while preserving their original capabilities.

## Key Results
- Significant improvements on commonsense reasoning benchmarks (CSQA, OBQA, PIQA, aNLI, COPA, SOCIAL IQA) compared to standard pre-trained models
- Particularly strong performance gains in few-shot learning scenarios where labeled data is limited
- Consistent improvements across both T5-base and T5-large model sizes
- The two-objective training approach (generative + discriminative) outperforms single-objective alternatives

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Neural commonsense models contain rich implicit knowledge not well captured by surface text patterns.
- Mechanism: The framework leverages a neural commonsense model (COMET) trained on ATOMIC 20/20 to generate knowledge tuples (subject, relation, object) that capture deeper causal and relational reasoning.
- Core assumption: Neural commonsense models trained on structured commonsense graphs generalize to unseen entities and can provide informative inferences when prompted with natural text.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the commonsense knowledge stored in a neural commonsense knowledge model to a general-purpose pre-trained language model"
  - [section 2.1] "it can generate accurate, representative knowledge for new, unseen entities and events"
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus only mentions related work on preserving knowledge, not on knowledge density of neural commonsense models
- Break condition: If the neural commonsense model fails to generate high-quality or relevant inferences for the given text prompts, the transfer becomes ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Self-supervised objectives that align text with generated commonsense knowledge improve downstream reasoning.
- Mechanism: Two complementary objectives - commonsense text infilling (generative) and commonsense relation prediction (discriminative) - are used to refine the pre-trained model on knowledge-augmented data.
- Core assumption: Aligning surface text with its underlying commonsense inference through multi-task learning enhances the model's reasoning capabilities without catastrophic forgetting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "refines the language model with two self-supervised objectives: commonsense mask infilling and commonsense relation prediction, which align human language with the underlying commonsense knowledge"
  - [section 2.2] "Refining a pre-trained model by multi-tasking on both generation (former) and understanding (latter) tasks enables the model to better adapt to different kinds of downstream tasks"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus does not provide direct evidence for effectiveness of dual-objective training
- Break condition: If the dual objectives conflict or if one dominates training, the balance between language modeling and reasoning could degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hard negative examples in relation prediction improve discriminative commonsense reasoning.
- Mechanism: For the relation prediction task, distractor options are carefully constructed by varying either the subject or relation, forcing the model to distinguish valid from invalid commonsense inferences.
- Core assumption: Training with carefully curated hard negatives improves the model's ability to discriminate plausible from implausible commonsense relations, which is crucial for downstream tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2] "we also want the model to be capable of discriminating invalid commonsense inferences... we introduce a commonsense relation prediction task"
  - [section 3.4] "our strategy for constructing distractors outperforms the random counterpart, demonstrating the necessity of hard negative examples"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus does not contain evidence about negative sampling strategies
- Break condition: If distractors are too easy or too hard, the model may not learn meaningful discriminative patterns.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Neural commonsense knowledge models
  - Why needed here: Understanding how models like COMET generate commonsense inferences is critical for designing the knowledge extraction process.
  - Quick check question: What training objective allows COMET to generalize commonsense reasoning to unseen entities?

- Concept: Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning
  - Why needed here: The paper addresses this by using dual objectives that preserve general language capabilities while adding reasoning knowledge.
  - Quick check question: Why might fine-tuning a pre-trained model directly on commonsense tuples degrade performance on general NLP tasks?

- Concept: Self-supervised learning objectives
  - Why needed here: Both proposed objectives (text infilling and relation prediction) are self-supervised, requiring understanding of how masked language modeling and contrastive learning work.
  - Quick check question: How does the bidirectional masking strategy differ from standard random masking in terms of what knowledge the model learns?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Neural commonsense knowledge model (COMET-ATOMIC 2020) - generates knowledge tuples
  - Knowledge extraction module - creates prompts from natural text and extracts commonsense inferences
  - Training data construction - combines natural text with extracted knowledge to form self-supervised examples
  - Pre-trained language model (T5) - target model refined with two self-supervised objectives
  - Downstream evaluation - tasks requiring commonsense reasoning (CSQA, OBQA, PIQA, etc.)

- Critical path: Natural text → Prompt generation → Knowledge extraction → Data construction → Multi-task training → Fine-tuning on downstream tasks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using neural commonsense models vs. symbolic KGs: Neural models generalize better but may introduce noise; symbolic KGs are cleaner but limited in coverage
  - Generative vs. discriminative objectives: Generative objectives preserve language modeling; discriminative objectives improve reasoning discrimination
  - Data scale vs. quality: More data improves performance but requires more computation; high-quality prompts are crucial

- Failure signatures:
  - Degraded performance on general NLP tasks: May indicate catastrophic forgetting from commonsense objectives
  - Minimal improvement on commonsense tasks: Could suggest poor knowledge extraction or ineffective training objectives
  - High variance across runs: May indicate sensitivity to random seeds or initialization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run knowledge extraction on a small sample of natural text and manually verify the quality of generated commonsense tuples
  2. Implement and test the commonsense text infilling objective alone on a subset of data to observe training dynamics
  3. Compare the full multi-task model against a baseline trained only on natural text to measure knowledge transfer effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of commonsense knowledge transfer scale with increasing model size beyond T5-large (e.g., T5-11B)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states they did not experiment with extremely large models like T5-11B due to resource constraints and deployment difficulties, but acknowledges larger models may benefit from more training data.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested T5-base and T5-large, leaving open whether the gains would continue scaling with model size.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing performance improvements on downstream commonsense tasks when applying the framework to increasingly larger pre-trained models (T5-3B, T5-11B).

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does commonsense knowledge transfer remain effective when using different pre-trained model architectures as backbones beyond T5 (e.g., BART, RoBERTa)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tested T5 variants and BART in supplementary experiments, showing gains, but did not test other architectures like RoBERTa or DeBERTa.
- Why unresolved: The experiments were limited to T5 and BART, leaving open whether the framework generalizes across different transformer architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the framework to diverse architectures (RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-style models) and measuring downstream commonsense task performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of using different neural commonsense knowledge models (beyond COMET-ATOMIC 2020) as the knowledge source?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper used COMET-ATOMIC 2020 as the neural commonsense model but acknowledges other models exist and were not tested.
- Why unresolved: The framework's dependence on the quality and coverage of the commonsense knowledge source was not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance when using different neural commonsense models (COMET-ConceptNet, different COMET variants, or other neural KGs) as the knowledge source.

## Limitations
- Knowledge Quality Dependence: The framework's effectiveness is entirely dependent on the quality of inferences generated by COMET-ATOMIC 2020
- Limited Generalization: The approach is only validated on ATOMIC 20/20 relations and may not generalize to other commonsense knowledge sources
- Computational Cost: The 100k training steps with dual objectives represents significant computational overhead that may not be justified by performance gains

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Performance improvements on commonsense reasoning benchmarks
The experimental results show consistent gains across multiple datasets (CSQA, OBQA, PIQA, aNLI, COPA, SOCIAL IQA) with statistical significance. The improvements are particularly pronounced in few-shot settings, which is well-supported by the presented data.

**Medium Confidence**: Mechanism of knowledge transfer through dual objectives
The paper provides a plausible theoretical framework for how commonsense text infilling and relation prediction work together to align language with reasoning, but the empirical evidence is limited to ablation studies rather than mechanistic analysis of what knowledge is actually transferred.

**Low Confidence**: Generalizability to other knowledge sources and reasoning types
The framework is evaluated only on ATOMIC 20/20 relations, and claims about neural commonsense models' generalization capabilities are supported by related work citations rather than direct experimental evidence in this paper.

## Next Checks

1. **Knowledge Quality Audit**: Sample 100-200 generated commonsense tuples from the knowledge extraction pipeline and conduct human evaluation to assess relevance, accuracy, and informativeness. This would validate the core assumption that COMET provides high-quality inferences for the given text prompts.

2. **Ablation Study with Different Knowledge Sources**: Implement the framework using alternative commonsense knowledge sources (e.g., ConceptNet relations or a symbolic knowledge graph) to test whether the approach generalizes beyond neural commonsense models and ATOMIC relations.

3. **Computational Efficiency Analysis**: Measure the wall-clock time and GPU hours required for the continual pre-training phase compared to standard pre-training, and conduct a cost-benefit analysis showing the trade-off between performance gains and computational overhead, particularly in few-shot settings.
