# The Design Space of Generative Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10547
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10547
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: Two design spaces for generative AI models—one for HCI interfaces to models, one for models as prototyping materials—covering 12+ dimensions across media, explainability, context, fidelity, and lifecycle.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces two complementary design spaces to structure thinking about generative AI integration in HCI. The first design space addresses how users interact with generative models (input/output media, explainability, evolution, timing), while the second examines how generative models serve as prototyping materials in HCI research (goals, AI role, context, lifecycle, fidelity, media). These taxonomies aim to provide researchers and designers with a systematic framework for analyzing existing systems and ideating new applications, moving beyond ad-hoc approaches to a more structured understanding of generative AI's role in human-centered systems.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a theoretical framework through literature review and conceptual analysis, categorizing generative AI integration challenges into two orthogonal design spaces. The authors systematically decompose interface design and prototyping use cases into discrete dimensions, providing examples from existing systems like PromptMaker and Social Simulacra. The methodology involves identifying key parameters that affect generative AI interactions and prototyping applications, then organizing these into comprehensive taxonomies. The approach is descriptive and generative, aiming to both classify existing work and inspire new design possibilities.

## Key Results
- Two comprehensive design spaces covering 12+ dimensions for generative AI in HCI
- Systematic decomposition of generative AI interfaces into input/output media, format, explainability, evolution, timing, operations, and presentation
- Models-as-materials framework addressing goals, AI role, context, lifecycle, fidelity, and media
- Taxonomy enables both analysis of existing systems and ideation of novel combinations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The proposed design spaces work because they systematically decompose generative AI integration challenges into orthogonal dimensions, enabling structured reasoning about HCI implications.
- Mechanism: By mapping input/output interfaces and model-as-material prototyping into discrete, comparable axes (media, format, explainability, evolution, etc.), researchers can identify gaps and opportunities that aren't visible in ad-hoc discussions.
- Core assumption: That generative AI use in HCI can be meaningfully categorized along these specific dimensions, and that these categories are mutually informative rather than redundant.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] explicitly states the goal is to support integration into human-centered systems by providing structured taxonomies; [section] shows the design space covers media, format, explainability, evolution for inputs and media, product, timing, operation, interactions, explainability, format for outputs.
- Break Condition: If key generative AI capabilities or interaction paradigms emerge that don't fit into these categorical dimensions (e.g., entirely new modalities or non-linear model evolution patterns).

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The design spaces enable both descriptive classification and generative ideation, serving dual purposes in research and practice.
- Mechanism: Researchers can use the taxonomy to analyze existing systems (descriptive) and also to identify unexplored design combinations (generative), accelerating innovation.
- Core assumption: That the taxonomy's dimensions are sufficiently comprehensive to cover existing systems while being flexible enough to suggest novel configurations.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] states these taxonomies aim to advance discussion and research; [section] provides examples like PromptMaker analysis and suggests new opportunities like interactive outputs and sketch-based inputs.
- Break Condition: If the taxonomy becomes too rigid, limiting rather than enabling creative exploration, or if most combinations prove infeasible in practice.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The dual design space approach (HCI-for-models and models-for-HCI) captures the bidirectional relationship between HCI and generative AI, providing a more complete framework than single-direction approaches.
- Mechanism: By explicitly modeling both how HCI can shape generative model interfaces and how generative models can serve as HCI prototyping materials, the framework addresses the full lifecycle of integration.
- Core assumption: That generative AI integration requires considering both directions of influence, not just one-way application.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] explicitly distinguishes between "interfaces for models" and "models as prototyping materials"; [section] shows separate taxonomies for each direction with distinct dimensions.
- Break Condition: If in practice most generative AI integration efforts only require one direction of consideration, making the dual framework unnecessarily complex.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Two main design spaces: HCI-for-models (interfaces) and models-for-HCI (prototyping materials) → Each design space contains multiple dimensions with specific design choices → Input dimensions (media, format, explainability, evolution) and output dimensions (media, product, timing, operation, interactions, explainability, format) → Models-for-HCI dimensions (goal, role of AI, context, lifecycle, fidelity, media)

- Critical path: Understanding the two design spaces → Mapping existing systems to the taxonomy → Identifying gaps and opportunities → Proposing new interface or prototyping approaches

- Design tradeoffs: Comprehensiveness vs. usability (more dimensions provide better coverage but harder to apply), descriptive vs. generative utility (balancing analysis and ideation), specificity vs. flexibility (detailed dimensions vs. broad applicability)

- Failure signatures: Inability to classify new systems, lack of novel insights from the taxonomy, confusion between dimensions, or practical implementations that don't fit the proposed categories

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Map 5-10 existing generative AI interfaces to the HCI-for-models design space and analyze coverage gaps
  2. Apply the models-for-HCI design space to a recent HCI paper using generative models and evaluate its descriptive accuracy
  3. Use the design spaces to propose 3 novel interface concepts that combine dimensions in unexplored ways

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of input media (language, image, video, audio, multimodal) impact the quality and usability of generated outputs across different generative AI models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses varying input media as a design parameter for interfaces to generative models, noting that input media and output media may not necessarily match (e.g., language prompts generating images).
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies input media as a key design dimension, it does not provide empirical evidence on how different input media affect output quality across various model types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies measuring output quality and usability metrics across generative models using different input media (e.g., language vs. image input for image generation).

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the trade-offs between different levels of explainability features (input tokenization, frequency, blocklists, output linking to input/training data) in terms of user comprehension, trust, and model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies explainability as a key dimension for both model inputs and outputs, listing various explainability features like input tokenization, output linking, and metadata embedding.
- Why unresolved: The paper discusses explainability features but does not provide empirical data on how different levels of explainability impact user experience or model performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies measuring comprehension and trust levels with different explainability feature combinations, alongside performance benchmarks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the context dimension (context-free, minimal context, deep context) affect the performance and applicability of generative models in different HCI prototyping scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies context as a key dimension for generative models as HCI prototyping materials, distinguishing between context-free, minimal context, and deep context applications.
- Why unresolved: While the paper categorizes context levels, it does not provide empirical evidence on how context depth impacts model performance across different prototyping scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies evaluating model performance and prototyping effectiveness across different context levels in various HCI use cases (e.g., design inspiration vs. evaluation).

## Limitations
- Design spaces are theoretical frameworks without empirical validation through user studies or real-world applications
- Rapidly evolving generative AI capabilities may quickly render specific design choices obsolete
- Paper lacks quantitative evidence comparing design space approach to existing ad-hoc methods

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The conceptual framing of bidirectional relationships between HCI and generative AI is well-grounded in existing literature
- Medium Confidence: The individual dimensions within each design space represent reasonable categorizations based on current generative AI capabilities
- Low Confidence: The completeness and orthogonality of the proposed dimensions have not been rigorously tested
