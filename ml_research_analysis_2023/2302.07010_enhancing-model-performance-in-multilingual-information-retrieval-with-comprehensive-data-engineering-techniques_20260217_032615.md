# Enhancing Model Performance in Multilingual Information Retrieval with Comprehensive Data Engineering Techniques

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.07010
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07010
- **Reference count:** 30
- **Primary result:** Achieved 2nd place in MIRACL Surprise-Languages track (0.835) and 3rd place in Known-Languages track (0.716 nDCG@10)

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive solution to the MIRACL challenge for multilingual information retrieval (MIR) by focusing on the ranking stage using cross-encoder models. The authors employ a multi-stage fine-tuning approach, beginning with pre-fine-tuning on MS MARCO and Mr. TyDi datasets before final fine-tuning on the MIRACL data. The key innovations lie in extensive data engineering techniques including data augmentation through pseudo labeling and Query-to-Query-to-Document (Q2Q2D) methods, strategic negative sampling to address sample selection bias, and model ensembling. The solution demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, achieving 2nd place in the Surprise-Languages track and 3rd place in the Known-Languages track of the MIRACL competition.

## Method Summary
The proposed solution employs three pre-trained multilingual transformer models (RemBERT, InfoXLM, and mDeBERTa) that undergo a multi-stage fine-tuning process. Initially, models are pre-fine-tuned on MS MARCO and Mr. TyDi datasets to enhance cross-lingual transfer capabilities. The models are then fine-tuned on the MIRACL dataset with extensive data augmentation techniques including pseudo labeling and Q2Q2D. Negative sampling is applied to improve model robustness by addressing sample selection bias. The final predictions are generated through an ensemble of the three fine-tuned models. The approach leverages the cross-encoder architecture, where query and document pairs are jointly encoded to produce relevance scores, achieving superior performance compared to bi-encoder alternatives for the ranking stage.

## Key Results
- Achieved 2nd place in MIRACL Surprise-Languages track with a score of 0.835
- Achieved 3rd place in MIRACL Known-Languages track with an average nDCG@10 score of 0.716 across 16 known languages
- Significant improvement observed when increasing negative samples from 5 to 100, with diminishing returns beyond 200
- Model ensemble outperformed individual models across both tracks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The solution's effectiveness stems from addressing key challenges in multilingual information retrieval through comprehensive data engineering. The multi-stage fine-tuning approach allows models to first learn general retrieval patterns on large English datasets (MS MARCO) before adapting to multilingual contexts (Mr. TyDi), and finally specializing on the target MIRACL data. Data augmentation techniques expand the training corpus and introduce linguistic diversity, helping models generalize across language pairs. Negative sampling addresses the inherent bias in training data where positive examples are scarce, improving the model's ability to discriminate between relevant and irrelevant documents. The ensemble approach leverages complementary strengths of different model architectures to achieve more robust performance across diverse languages and query types.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Pre-trained models (RemBERT, InfoXLM, mDeBERTa) -> Pre-fine-tuning (MS MARCO, Mr. TyDi) -> MIRACL fine-tuning with augmentation -> Ensemble

**Critical path:** The most critical path is the final fine-tuning stage on MIRACL data with negative sampling, as this directly optimizes for the target task and evaluation metrics.

**Design tradeoffs:** The cross-encoder architecture provides superior ranking accuracy but is computationally expensive compared to bi-encoders, limiting real-time inference capabilities.

**Failure signatures:** Performance degradation likely occurs with low-resource languages due to limited training data, and the ensemble approach may underperform if individual models become too similar in their predictions.

**First experiments:** 1) Evaluate individual model performance before and after each fine-tuning stage, 2) Test different negative sampling ratios to find optimal configuration, 3) Assess the impact of each data augmentation technique independently through ablation studies.

## Limitations
- Data augmentation techniques lack comprehensive validation across language pairs and may introduce noise for low-resource languages
- Negative sampling strategy implemented without clear justification for specific sampling ratios or language distribution
- Ensemble approach presented without ablation studies showing individual component contributions
