# Using VAEs to Learn Latent Variables: Observations on Applications in cryo-EM

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07487
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07487
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: In cryo-EM applications, VAE encoders show limited generalization but produce qualitatively similar results to explicit latent variable methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how VAEs learn latent variables in the context of cryo-EM applications. The authors investigate whether the encoder's amortized inference property provides advantages over explicit latent variable representations. Through experiments with the EMPIAR-10076 dataset, they find that replacing the encoder with a lookup table (VLT) produces qualitatively similar results to the standard VAE approach. The study reveals that encoder generalization is limited under data augmentation (shifts, rotations), and surprisingly, the VAE can still produce meaningful results even when the encoder input is replaced with unrelated data.

## Method Summary
The authors modify the CryoDRGN software to replace the standard VAE encoder with a Variational Lookup Table (VLT) that stores explicit latent variable estimates for each particle image. They train both the standard VAE and VLT approaches on the EMPIAR-10076 dataset (130,000 images downsampled to 128x128) for 50 epochs. The study includes qualitative comparisons of latent space structure using UMAP visualization and reconstructed 3D volumes. Additional experiments test encoder generalization by applying data augmentation (shifts, rotations) and an "evil twin" setup where encoder inputs are replaced with unrelated images.

## Key Results
- Explicit latent variables (VLT) produce qualitatively similar latent space structure and reconstructed volumes compared to standard VAE encoders
- Encoder generalization fails under data augmentation, with shifted/rotated test images mapping to different latent regions
- Surprisingly, the VAE still produces meaningful results even when encoder inputs are replaced with unrelated "twin" images
- The lack of encoder generalization does not appear to significantly impact the quality of recovered conformations in this application

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: In cryo-EM applications, the encoder in a VAE does not generalize well to unseen data due to overfitting to noise and specific input features.
- Mechanism: The high noise level in cryo-EM images (SNR often < 1/10) means the encoder may fit to "noise features" as much as signal, leading to poor generalization when tested on shifted or rotated images.
- Core assumption: The encoder's inability to generalize stems from the noise-dominated nature of cryo-EM data rather than a flaw in the VAE architecture itself.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We find that in this application the encoder bears a qualitative resemblance to more traditional explicit representation of latent variables."
  - [section]: "We find that using explicit latent variables yields qualitatively similar (and arguably better) estimates than using the encoder in this test case."
  - [corpus]: "Uncertainty in latent representations of variational autoencoders optimized for visual tasks" - suggests potential issues with VAE latent representations in certain domains.
- Break condition: If the noise level in the data were significantly reduced, the encoder might exhibit better generalization properties.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using explicit latent variables (like a lookup table) can produce similar or better results compared to using an encoder in cryo-EM applications.
- Mechanism: Explicit latent variables, unlike an encoder, don't attempt to generalize across all inputs but instead focus on optimizing for specific data points, which can be advantageous in noisy, high-variability datasets like cryo-EM.
- Core assumption: The lack of generalization in the encoder is not detrimental to the final outcome in cryo-EM applications because the primary goal is to characterize conformations rather than to have a generalizable encoder.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We replace the encoder deep network Encξ :xi→ (µi,σi) from Section 2.1 with a lookup table LTi :i→ (µi,σi), to which we refer as a Variational Lookup Table (VLT)."
  - [section]: "Qualitatively, the latent space structure and the volumes/conformations recovered by the VLT procedure agree with the original CryoDRGN results shown in Figure 3."
  - [corpus]: "Half-VAE: An Encoder-Free VAE to Bypass Explicit Inverse Mapping" - directly relevant to using encoder-free approaches in VAEs.
- Break condition: If the dataset were less noisy or had lower variability, an encoder-based approach might outperform explicit latent variables due to its ability to generalize.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: In cryo-EM applications, the VAE can effectively "overfit" to individual images, producing meaningful results even when the input to the encoder is replaced with unrelated data or noise.
- Mechanism: The VAE architecture allows the encoder to map each input to a unique latent representation, and the decoder can then reconstruct meaningful volumes from these representations, even if the encoder input was not the original image.
- Core assumption: The conformational heterogeneity in cryo-EM data is such that each image, even if highly noisy or unrelated, can be mapped to a meaningful latent representation that the decoder can use to produce a volume.

- Break condition: If the conformational heterogeneity in the data were less pronounced, this overfitting mechanism might not produce meaningful results.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Cryo-EM particle images (noisy, 2D projections)
  - Encoder: Neural network that maps images to latent space (mean and variance of Gaussian distribution)
  - Latent space: Represents conformations of the molecule
  - Decoder: Neural network that maps latent variables to 3D volumes
  - Output: Reconstructed 3D volumes of the molecule in different conformations

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess cryo-EM images
  2. Pass images through encoder to get latent representations
  3. Sample from latent space using mean and variance
  4. Pass sampled latent variables through decoder
  5. Compare output to original images and update network parameters

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using explicit latent variables (VLT) vs. amortized encoder
  - Encoder size and latent space dimensionality
  - Balancing reconstruction loss and KL divergence in VAE objective

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor separation in latent space (points cluster together)
  - Inconsistent volumes produced from similar latent representations
  - Large discrepancy between original and reconstructed images

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a basic VAE on cryo-EM data and visualize the latent space
  2. Replace the encoder with a lookup table (VLT) and compare results
  3. Test the encoder's generalization by applying shifts and rotations to the input images

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper doesn't explicitly call out open questions, but raises several implicit ones through its findings:
- Why does the encoder fail to generalize under data augmentation while still producing useful latent representations?
- What is the relationship between the VAE's ability to handle "evil twin" inputs and the nature of conformational heterogeneity in cryo-EM data?
- How do the computational trade-offs between amortized and explicit latent variable methods scale with dataset size and complexity?

## Limitations

- Qualitative rather than quantitative comparison of encoder vs. explicit latent variable methods
- Limited analysis of why the "evil twin" experiment produces meaningful results
- No systematic study of how different levels of noise or conformational heterogeneity affect the relative performance of encoder vs. explicit approaches

## Confidence

- Medium confidence in the core observation that explicit latent variables (VLT) can match or exceed encoder performance in this specific cryo-EM context
- Medium confidence in the claim that encoder generalization fails under data augmentation (shifts/rotations)
- Low confidence in the interpretation of the "evil twin" results without more detailed analysis
