# SeMAIL: Eliminating Distractors in Visual Imitation via Separated Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10695
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10695
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SeMAIL achieves near-expert performance on visual control tasks with complex distractors by learning separated models for task-relevant and task-irrelevant dynamics.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of visual imitation learning from high-dimensional observations corrupted by complex distractors, such as moving backgrounds. Standard model-based IL methods often fail because the learned environment model captures irrelevant information, leading to misleading rewards from the discriminator. The authors propose SeMAIL, which assumes that environment dynamics can be decomposed into task-relevant and task-irrelevant parts conditioned on the agent's actions. By learning two separate models and training the policy in the task-relevant latent space, SeMAIL effectively avoids distraction. Experiments on six visual control tasks with natural video backgrounds demonstrate that SeMAIL outperforms existing IL baselines, achieving near-expert performance.

## Method Summary
SeMAIL introduces a separated model architecture that decomposes environment dynamics into task-relevant and task-irrelevant components, conditioned on the agent's actions (AcT assumption). The method learns two separate models: one for task-relevant dynamics and another for task-irrelevant dynamics. Observations are reconstructed jointly from both latent states, but the policy is trained solely in the task-relevant latent space using adversarial imitation learning. This approach ensures that the policy focuses on learning the task-relevant aspects of the environment while ignoring distractors. The theoretical analysis provides bounds on the expert-agent performance gap in the task-relevant space, and ablation studies confirm the necessity of both the AcT assumption and background-only reconstruction.

## Key Results
- SeMAIL consistently outperforms model-based IL, data-augmentation, and feature-sharing baselines on six visual control tasks with natural video backgrounds.
- The method achieves near-expert performance by learning in the task-relevant latent space, avoiding distraction from irrelevant features.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that both the AcT assumption and background-only reconstruction are essential for SeMAIL's success.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SeMAIL works by explicitly separating the learning of task-relevant and task-irrelevant dynamics, conditioned on the agent's actions. The AcT assumption allows the method to decompose the environment into two parts: one that depends on the agent's actions (task-relevant) and one that does not (task-irrelevant). By training the policy in the task-relevant latent space, SeMAIL ensures that the agent focuses on learning the aspects of the environment that matter for the task, while ignoring distractors. This separation prevents the discriminator from providing misleading rewards based on irrelevant features, leading to better imitation performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Action-Conditioned Transition (AcT) Assumption**: Assumes that task-relevant dynamics depend on the agent's actions, while task-irrelevant dynamics do not. *Why needed*: Enables clean separation of task-relevant and task-irrelevant dynamics. *Quick check*: Verify that task-relevant features change predictably with actions, while task-irrelevant features remain stable.

- **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**: Used to encode observations into latent states and decode latent states back into observations. *Why needed*: Enables reconstruction of observations from latent representations. *Quick check*: Ensure that reconstructed observations match the original observations in both task-relevant and task-irrelevant parts.

- **Adversarial Imitation Learning**: Trains a discriminator to distinguish between expert and agent trajectories, providing rewards to the agent. *Why needed*: Enables learning from expert demonstrations without explicit reward signals. *Quick check*: Monitor discriminator loss to ensure it does not become too strong, which could lead to training instability.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Observations -> Encoders (task-relevant, task-irrelevant) -> Forward Dynamics Models -> Variational Encoders -> Decoders -> Reconstructed Observations

**Critical Path**: Observations are encoded into task-relevant and task-irrelevant latent states, which are then used by forward dynamics models to predict future states. The latent states are reconstructed into observations, and the policy is trained in the task-relevant latent space using adversarial imitation learning.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The AcT assumption simplifies the separation of task-relevant and task-irrelevant dynamics but may not hold in all environments. Using VAEs for reconstruction adds computational overhead but enables joint optimization of the separated models.

**Failure Signatures**: If the AcT assumption is violated, the separated models may fail to reconstruct observations correctly, leading to poor performance. Training instability may occur if the discriminator becomes too strong, requiring gradient penalty regularization.

## Limitations
- The AcT assumption may not hold in environments where task-relevant and task-irrelevant dynamics are intertwined or depend on non-action factors.
- The method's performance on distractors other than video backgrounds (e.g., sensor noise, occlusions, or dynamic objects) remains unverified.
- The reliance on adversarial imitation learning may introduce training instability, particularly when the discriminator overfits to background features.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Experimental results showing SeMAIL's superior performance over baselines across six tasks, achieving near-expert performance.
- **Medium confidence**: Theoretical analysis linking the AcT assumption to bounded performance gaps, but dependent on the assumption's validity.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about generalization to distractors not seen during training are speculative and untested beyond video background scenarios.

## Next Checks
1. Test SeMAIL on environments with distractors that violate the AcT assumption (e.g., distractors that are not action-independent) to assess robustness.
2. Evaluate performance when expert demonstrations and agent observations contain different types of distractors (e.g., static vs. dynamic backgrounds) to validate generalization claims.
3. Conduct experiments with varying levels of distractor complexity (e.g., multiple overlapping distractors) to identify scalability limits.
