# Query Performance Prediction for Neural IR: Are We There Yet?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09947
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09947
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Traditional and neural QPP models perform significantly worse on neural IR systems, with up to 10% performance drop, especially for semantic-rich tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines whether state-of-the-art Query Performance Prediction (QPP) models can effectively predict the performance of neural IR systems based on BERT. The authors conduct extensive experiments comparing 19 QPP models across seven traditional and seven BERT-based first-stage neural IR systems using Robust '04 and Deep Learning '19 collections. The results reveal that both traditional lexical-based QPP models and neural QPP models designed for traditional IR significantly underperform when predicting the effectiveness of neural IR systems, with performance gaps reaching up to 10% in semantic-rich settings like passage retrieval. The decline is most pronounced for queries where neural and traditional IR systems differ the most, highlighting the fundamental challenge of adapting QPP approaches to the semantic nature of neural IR.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 19 QPP models (9 pre-retrieval and 10 post-retrieval) on seven traditional IR models (BM25, LMD, LMJM, TFIDF, InEB2, AxF1e, DFI) and seven BERT-based neural IR models (bi-encoders, SPLADE variants, ColBERTv2). The evaluation uses two collections: Robust '04 with 249 topics and Deep Learning '19 with 43 topics. Performance is measured using Pearson correlation and scaled Absolute Rank Error (sARE) between QPP predictions and ground truth nDCG@10 scores. The authors also employ ANOVA statistical analysis to examine the variance introduced by different factors including IR systems, QPP models, and topics.

## Key Results
- Traditional QPP models show up to 10% performance drop when predicting neural IR system effectiveness, particularly in semantic-rich settings like passage retrieval
- The performance gap is most pronounced for queries where neural IR systems succeed but traditional systems fail, suggesting QPP models cannot capture semantic advantages
- Neural QPP models like BERT-QPP also underperform on neural IR systems, indicating the problem is fundamental to current QPP approaches rather than specific architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Traditional QPP models rely on lexical features and perform well on lexical IR systems, but fail on neural IR systems that use semantic representations.
- Mechanism: QPP models extract statistics from query terms and retrieval scores based on lexical matching assumptions. Neural IR systems use BERT embeddings that capture semantic similarity rather than exact term matching, breaking the lexical signal assumptions.
- Core assumption: The effectiveness of a retrieval system correlates with lexical query-document overlap patterns that QPP models are designed to measure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "traditional QPP models perform significantly worse on neural IR systems, with up to 10% performance drop in semantic-rich settings like passage retrieval"
  - [section] "pre-retrieval predictors fail in predicting the performance of NIR systems (mean correlation 6.2% vs 25.6% for TIR)"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus evidence - related papers focus on QPP generalization but don't directly address neural vs lexical IR distinction
- Break condition: When neural IR systems start relying more on lexical signals (e.g., sparser BERT models) or when QPP models incorporate semantic understanding capabilities

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The performance gap between TIR and NIR on specific queries correlates with QPP prediction accuracy.
- Mechanism: Queries where neural IR systems succeed but traditional systems fail represent cases where semantic understanding matters. QPP models, designed for lexical systems, cannot capture this semantic advantage, leading to poor predictions on these queries.
- Core assumption: The difference in retrieval performance between TIR and NIR systems on the same query reflects the semantic difficulty of that query.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "in lexical-oriented scenarios, QPPs fail to predict performance for neural IR systems on those queries where they differ from traditional approaches the most"
  - [section] "we select from Robust '04 25% of the queries that are mostly 'semantically defined' and rerun MD2 on the new set of topics"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus evidence - related papers don't explicitly study query-by-query performance differences between neural and traditional IR
- Break condition: When QPP models incorporate semantic features or when neural IR systems become more lexical in nature

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Neural QPP models designed for traditional IR also underperform on neural IR systems, indicating the problem is fundamental to the approach rather than specific model architectures.
- Mechanism: Neural QPP models like BERT-QPP were trained on traditional IR systems using lexical features. They fail to generalize to neural IR systems because the underlying data distribution and relevance patterns differ significantly.
- Core assumption: Neural QPP models can bridge the lexical-semantic gap by learning from traditional IR data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "neural QPP models, such as BERT-QPP, also underperform on neural IR, suggesting that current QPP approaches are not well-suited for neural IR systems"
  - [section] "its performance on Deep Learning '19 – applied in zero-shot – is considerably lower compared to other post-retrieval approaches"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus evidence - related papers don't test neural QPP models on neural IR systems
- Break condition: When neural QPP models are trained directly on neural IR systems or when they incorporate explicit semantic understanding capabilities

  - Evaluation Framework: IR runs -> QPP predictions -> Ground truth nDCG@10 -> Correlation analysis
  - Collections: Robust '04 (249 topics) -> Deep Learning '19 (43 topics)

- Critical path:
  1. Run IR systems on queries to get retrieval results
  2. Apply QPP models to predict performance
  3. Calculate ground truth performance (nDCG@10)
  4. Correlate QPP predictions with ground truth
  5. Analyze results using ANOVA and sARE

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using nDCG@10 vs AP for evaluation (shorter vs longer ranking)
  - Zero-shot evaluation vs fine-tuning on target collection
  - Correlation-based vs listwise evaluation methods

- Failure signatures:
  - QPP models producing negative correlations with true performance
  - Large performance gaps between TIR and NIR on specific queries
  - Inconsistent QPP performance across different neural IR architectures

- First 3 experiments:

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can QPP models be adapted to effectively predict the performance of neural IR systems that rely heavily on semantic signals rather than lexical matching?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that current QPP models fail to properly predict the performance of neural IR systems, particularly in settings where semantic signals are prominent, and highlights the need for QPP models specifically tailored to neural IR.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the inadequacy of existing QPP models for neural IR but does not propose specific adaptations or new models that could bridge this gap.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of new QPP models designed specifically for neural IR systems, showing improved prediction accuracy on neural IR tasks compared to traditional QPP models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the key factors that influence the performance of QPP models when applied to neural IR systems, and how do they differ from those affecting traditional IR systems?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper infers that the shift from lexical to semantic signals in neural IR systems affects QPP performance, but does not provide a detailed analysis of the specific factors involved.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies a correlation between QPP performance and the degree of lexicality in neural IR systems but does not explore the underlying factors in depth.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study identifying and analyzing the key factors that influence QPP performance in neural IR systems, including how these factors differ from those in traditional IR systems.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the variability in query formulation affect the generalizability of QPP models across different IR systems, particularly neural IR systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that improving QPP's generalizability requires addressing challenges caused by differences in neural and traditional IR, as well as considering the large variance introduced by topics.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the impact of query variability on QPP performance but does not provide specific strategies for enhancing generalizability across different IR systems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results demonstrating improved QPP generalizability through the incorporation of query variation analysis and adaptation strategies tailored to neural IR systems.

## Limitations

- The findings are based on only two specific test collections (Robust '04 and Deep Learning '19), which may not generalize to other datasets or query distributions
- The paper doesn't explore whether incorporating semantic features into QPP models could improve their performance on neural IR systems
- Limited testing of neural QPP models beyond BERT-QPP leaves uncertainty about whether the problem extends to other neural QPP architectures

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The core finding that traditional QPP models perform significantly worse on neural IR systems, particularly for semantic-rich tasks like passage retrieval. This is supported by consistent results across multiple evaluation metrics and neural IR architectures.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that neural QPP models like BERT-QPP also underperform on neural IR systems. While the paper shows this for one specific neural QPP model, broader testing across different neural QPP architectures would strengthen this claim.

**Medium confidence**: The mechanism explanation linking lexical-semantic gaps to QPP performance degradation. The paper provides evidence but doesn't fully isolate whether this is due to feature mismatch, distributional shift, or other factors.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-collection validation**: Test the QPP models on additional IR collections (e.g., MS MARCO, TREC CAR) to verify if the performance degradation generalizes beyond the two datasets used in this study.

2. **Semantic-aware QPP**: Implement and evaluate QPP models that explicitly incorporate semantic features (e.g., BERT embeddings of query-document pairs) to determine if the lexical-semantic gap can be bridged without abandoning current QPP approaches entirely.

3. **Query-level analysis**: Conduct a detailed analysis of specific query types where QPP models fail most dramatically, examining whether certain query characteristics (length, ambiguity, domain specificity) predict the likelihood of QPP failure on neural IR systems.
