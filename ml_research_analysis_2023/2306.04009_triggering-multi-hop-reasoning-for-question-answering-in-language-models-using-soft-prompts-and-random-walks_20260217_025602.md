# Triggering Multi-Hop Reasoning for Question Answering in Language Models using Soft Prompts and Random Walks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04009
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04009
- Reference count: 19
- One-line primary result: Substantial improvements in 2-hop QA with soft prompts and random walks, gaining ~16 and ~9.6 points in EM over full fine-tuning for T5-XXL

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of improving multi-hop reasoning in pre-trained language models for question answering tasks. The authors propose using soft prompts trained on random walks over structured knowledge graphs to guide language models in composing chained facts. The approach involves training soft prompts to map multi-hop questions to random walk paths that lead to the answer, tested on T5 models using the 2WikiMultiHopQA dataset. The results show significant improvements in answering 2-hop reasoning questions, particularly for larger models like T5-XXL.

## Method Summary
The method involves knowledge integration into T5 models by fine-tuning on knowledge graph triples, followed by training soft prompts on random walks. Two approaches are proposed: PATH, which uses separate prompts for parsing questions and generating paths, and MIXHOP, which uses a single prompt trained jointly on QA and random walk tasks. The soft prompts guide the frozen language model to output structured entity-relation paths, leveraging the model's encoded knowledge to compose facts for multi-hop reasoning.

## Key Results
- T5-XXL with random walk-integrated methods shows substantial improvements in 2-hop QA, with gains of ~16 and ~9.6 points in exact match scores over fine-tuning the entire model, respectively.
- Larger models (T5-XXL vs T5-LARGE) benefit more from random walk prompting, indicating better compositional generalization.
- Knowledge integration alone is insufficient for 2-hop reasoning, highlighting the need for compositional training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Soft prompts trained on random walks help language models compose chained facts by acting as continuous vectors that guide the frozen language model to output structured entity-relation paths when given incomplete random walks or parsed questions. This assumes the LM already encodes the atomic facts required for multi-hop reasoning, and the soft prompts provide the compositional glue.

### Mechanism 2
Splitting the prompting task into parsing and hopping improves performance by allowing separate soft prompts to handle entity/relation extraction from questions and generation of the full random walk path. This enables each to specialize, with the underlying LM remaining frozen to preserve its knowledge.

### Mechanism 3
Larger models benefit more from random walk prompting because their internal representations better support generalization to novel random walk paths, so soft prompt training yields larger gains. This assumes parameter count and model scale correlate with the capacity to compose memorized facts in unseen configurations.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Memorization vs. Composition**
  - Why needed here: The work distinguishes between encoding single facts and chaining them; understanding this difference is critical to grasping why knowledge integration alone is insufficient.
  - Quick check question: If a model knows "A → B" and "B → C", can it answer "What is A's C?" without explicit training?

- **Concept: Soft Prompt Tuning**
  - Why needed here: Soft prompts allow efficient adaptation without fine-tuning the full model, preserving the LM's original knowledge while guiding its behavior.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between soft prompt tuning and prefix tuning?

- **Concept: Random Walks on Knowledge Graphs**
  - Why needed here: Random walks provide training signal to teach models how to traverse and compose facts along KG paths.
  - Quick check question: What is the input and output format for training a model to generate random walks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - KNIT5 (Knowledge-Integrated T5) -> Hopping Prompts -> Random Walk Paths -> Final Entity (Answer)
  - PATH: Parse-then-Hop (separate prompts for parsing and hopping)
  - MIXHOP: Single prompt trained jointly on QA and random walk tasks

- **Critical path:**
  1. Integrate KG triples into LM (KNIT5).
  2. Sample random walks and train hopping prompts.
  3. For QA: parse question (PATH) or use MIXHOP directly.
  4. Generate path → extract final entity as answer.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Fine-tuning vs. prompt-tuning: Full fine-tuning may overfit or lose general capabilities; prompt-tuning is parameter-efficient but depends on model scale.
  - Separate parsing vs. joint MIXHOP: Parsing allows modular design and interpretability but requires two inference steps; MIXHOP is more efficient but may be harder to train.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor performance on 1-hop questions: Knowledge integration failed.
  - Poor random walk generation: Model cannot generalize KG paths.
  - PATH/MIXHOP fails but knowledge integration succeeds: Model lacks compositional ability.
  - Large model performs better: Confirms compositional gap.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train KNIT5 on KG triples; evaluate on 1-hop questions to confirm knowledge integration.
  2. Train hopping prompts on random walks; evaluate path generation on held-out paths.
  3. Evaluate PATH and MIXHOP on 2-hop QA; compare to fine-tuning baselines.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions, including whether the effectiveness of random walk training scales to models significantly larger than T5-XXL, how performance degrades as the number of hops increases beyond 2, the impact of increasing knowledge graph size and diversity, and the robustness of the approach to noisy or incomplete knowledge graphs.

## Limitations
- The experimental comparisons are constrained to T5 models only, with no evaluation of alternative architectures.
- The soft prompt training process and hyperparameters are only referenced to appendices without being explicitly stated.
- The random walk sampling strategy is not detailed, which could introduce bias if certain path types are overrepresented.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** Soft prompts can be trained to generate random walk paths over knowledge graphs.
- **Medium Confidence:** The Parse-then-Hop (PATH) approach improves 2-hop QA performance over fine-tuning the entire model.
- **Low Confidence:** Larger models inherently possess better compositional generalization for novel random walk paths.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation on Random Walk Sampling:** Systematically vary the diversity and length of training random walks and measure the impact on both path generation accuracy and downstream QA performance.
2. **Cross-Architecture Evaluation:** Implement and evaluate the same soft prompt + random walk approach on a GPT-style decoder-only model to test whether gains transfer beyond encoder-decoder architectures.
3. **Single-Hop Fact Coverage Analysis:** For a subset of 2-hop test questions, verify that both required single-hop facts are present in the knowledge-integrated model's representations before and after soft prompt training.
