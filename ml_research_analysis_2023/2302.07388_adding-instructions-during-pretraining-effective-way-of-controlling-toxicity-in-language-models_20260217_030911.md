# Adding Instructions during Pretraining: Effective Way of Controlling Toxicity in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07388
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07388
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Reduces toxicity probability by up to 61% while maintaining downstream task performance

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces two novel pretraining augmentation strategies (MEDA and INST) that incorporate toxicity information directly into the pretraining data to reduce toxic outputs from language models. The INST strategy, which adds instructions indicating toxicity levels to training samples, achieves a 61% reduction in toxicity probability while preserving accuracy on five NLP benchmark tasks and improving bias detection AUC scores by 1.3%. The approach demonstrates generalizability across different model scales and training sample sizes.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two strategies for incorporating toxicity information during pretraining. MEDA (Metadata Addition) appends raw toxicity scores as metadata to pretraining samples, while INST (Instruction Addition) adds explicit instructions indicating the toxicity level of each sample. These augmented samples are then used during the pretraining phase to help the model learn to recognize and appropriately handle toxic content. The INST strategy proves particularly effective, substantially reducing toxic outputs while maintaining performance on downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- Toxicity probability reduced by up to 61% compared to baseline models
- Preserved accuracy on five benchmark NLP tasks during evaluation
- Improved AUC scores on bias detection tasks by 1.3%
- Demonstrated generalizability across different model sizes and training sample counts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed strategies work by explicitly exposing the language model to toxicity information during the critical pretraining phase, allowing it to learn contextual patterns and appropriate responses to toxic content before fine-tuning on downstream tasks. By incorporating toxicity metadata or instructions directly into the training data, the model develops an internal representation of what constitutes toxic language and how to handle it, rather than learning these patterns implicitly from the raw text alone.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
- Pretraining corpus -> Toxicity annotation pipeline -> Augmented dataset -> Language model pretraining -> Downstream task evaluation

Critical path:
The toxicity annotation and data augmentation pipeline represents the critical path, as errors or inconsistencies in toxicity labeling directly impact the effectiveness of the instruction-based approach. The quality of toxicity detection and the consistency of instruction formatting are essential for successful model training.

Design tradeoffs:
The primary tradeoff involves computational cost during pretraining versus toxicity reduction benefits. While the approach requires additional computation for toxicity annotation and augmented pretraining, it eliminates the need for separate toxicity mitigation fine-tuning stages. The instruction format also represents a design choice between explicit toxicity labeling versus more nuanced contextual guidance.

Failure signatures:
Models may fail to generalize toxicity detection to novel contexts if the pretraining data lacks diversity in toxic expressions. Inconsistent instruction formatting or toxicity score distributions can lead to biased learning. The approach may also fail if the toxicity detection method itself contains biases that propagate through the training process.

## Limitations
- Evaluation primarily focused on single-turn generation tasks, with limited examination of multi-turn conversational scenarios
- Reliance on Perspective API for toxicity detection, which may contain cultural and linguistic biases
- Significant computational requirements during pretraining may limit accessibility for some research groups
- Limited investigation of potential degradation in other language capabilities beyond tested NLP tasks

## Confidence
- High confidence in INST strategy's effectiveness for reducing toxicity in single-turn generation tasks
- Medium confidence in preservation of downstream NLP task performance across all tested tasks
- Medium confidence in bias detection improvements given modest AUC gains (1.3%)
- Low confidence in scalability claims beyond specific experimental setup tested
