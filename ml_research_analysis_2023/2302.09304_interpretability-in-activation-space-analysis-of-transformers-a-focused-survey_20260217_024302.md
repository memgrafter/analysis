# Interpretability in Activation Space Analysis of Transformers: A Focused Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09304
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09304
- Reference count: 40
- The survey examines interpretability methods for analyzing feed-forward layer activations in transformers

## Executive Summary
This survey paper systematically reviews seven recent works focused on interpreting the activation space of feed-forward layers in transformer language models. The authors examine methods including probing classifiers, neural memory analysis, and knowledge attribution to understand what linguistic information is captured in these internal representations. The survey reveals a critical gap in standardized evaluation metrics across studies and highlights the need for human-interpretable explanations in activation space analysis. The authors propose extending interpretability techniques from image processing to NLP through transfer learning as a promising future direction.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes findings from seven recent papers that analyze feed-forward layer activations in transformers. Methods include training diagnostic classifiers to predict linguistic properties from neuron activations, identifying neural memories as key-value pairs within feed-forward layers, and using attribution methods to locate knowledge-expressing neurons. Evaluations span prediction accuracy metrics, selectivity scores, human expert pattern identification, and visualization techniques like UMAP dimensionality reduction. The authors extract commonalities and limitations across these approaches, particularly noting the absence of standardized evaluation frameworks.

## Key Results
- Current activation space analysis methods lack standardized evaluation metrics across different transformer architectures
- Individual neurons in feed-forward layers show task-dependent encoding patterns, with similar neurons capturing related information across architectures
- Knowledge attribution methods can identify specific neurons responsible for factual recall, but the mechanisms remain poorly understood

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Feed-forward layers act as key-value memory systems where keys detect input patterns and values determine output distributions. The hidden dimension represents the number of memories, and activations reflect un-normalized memory coefficients. Highest coefficients identify matching key patterns, influencing next token predictions.

### Mechanism 2
Individual neurons capture specific linguistic phenomena identifiable through probing classifiers. Activations are used to train diagnostic classifiers predicting linguistic properties like POS tags or syntax. Neurons are ranked by classification accuracy contribution, with top-ranked neurons deemed to capture relevant linguistic information.

### Mechanism 3
Knowledge neurons express specific factual knowledge through activation patterns correlating with fact recall. Integrated gradients measure each neuron's contribution via attribution scores. High attribution neurons are identified as knowledge neurons, with their activation patterns determining fact expression, suppression, or amplification.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input → Feed-forward layer → Neuron activations → Interpretability analysis (probing/classification/attribution) → Evaluation metrics → Insights about linguistic knowledge
- **Critical path:** Feed-forward layer activations extracted from transformer models are analyzed using interpretability methods to extract linguistic insights
- **Design tradeoffs:** Local interpretability (individual neurons) vs global interpretability (entire activation space), linear vs non-linear analysis methods, task-specific vs general linguistic knowledge extraction
- **Failure signatures:** Low selectivity scores, poor correlation between human-interpretable patterns and activation patterns, inability to transfer findings across architectures
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Extract feed-forward layer activations from a pre-trained BERT model on a sample text corpus
  2. Train a simple logistic regression probe to predict POS tags from these activations and evaluate prediction accuracy
  3. Apply UMAP dimensionality reduction to visualize activation space and identify clustering patterns related to linguistic phenomena

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
What standardized evaluation metrics should be developed to compare different activation space analysis methods across transformer models? The paper explicitly states that "there is no standard set of metrics for comparing various observations," limiting generalizability of findings. Without standardized metrics, comparing findings across studies becomes difficult.

### Open Question 2
How can interpretability techniques from image processing be effectively transferred to the NLP domain to create human-understandable linguistic concepts? The authors propose extending image-processing techniques through transfer learning. The gap between low-level neural features and high-level human concepts remains unbridged in NLP.

### Open Question 3
To what extent does information encoded in feed-forward layer neurons depend on the specific input dataset versus the underlying task? The paper observes dataset-dependent encoding patterns but doesn't quantify the relative contribution of dataset characteristics versus task requirements to encoded information.

## Limitations
- Lack of standardized evaluation metrics across different activation space analysis methods
- Difficulty achieving human-understandable explanations from low-level activation patterns
- Limited validation across diverse transformer architectures and datasets

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Transformer architecture fundamentals and the general importance of feed-forward layer analysis
- **Medium confidence**: Specific interpretability mechanisms (neural memory, probing classifiers, knowledge attribution) based on theoretical plausibility and limited empirical support
- **Low confidence**: Claims about direct application of image-processing techniques to NLP activation spaces without validation
