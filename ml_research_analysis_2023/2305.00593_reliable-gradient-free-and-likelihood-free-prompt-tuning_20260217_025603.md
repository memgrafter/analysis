# Reliable Gradient-free and Likelihood-free Prompt Tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00593
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00593
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: Gradient-free and likelihood-free methods for prompt tuning that enable uncertainty quantification and competitive performance without gradient access

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of adapting large pre-trained language models (PLMs) to downstream tasks when only API access is available, preventing gradient computation or access to internal model representations. The authors propose methods to learn soft prompts without gradients, extending to settings where only input embeddings and discrete output labels are accessible. They develop a simulation-based inference approach (ABC-SMC) to infer prompt distributions, enabling uncertainty quantification. The methods achieve competitive or improved performance compared to gradient-based approaches, with better calibration and uncertainty estimation across multiple tasks including selective classification and out-of-distribution detection.

## Method Summary
The paper presents two main approaches for gradient-free prompt tuning. First, using CMA-ES optimization to tune soft prompts in a lower-dimensional re-parameterized space, either through prompt ensembles or gradient-free variational inference to learn a distribution over prompts. Second, for likelihood-free settings where only discrete outputs are available, they adapt the ABC-SMC algorithm to perform sequential Monte Carlo approximate Bayesian computation for prompt posterior inference. Both approaches maintain distributions over prompts rather than point estimates, enabling uncertainty quantification in predictions. The methods are evaluated on text classification tasks using RoBERTa-LARGE with few-shot learning settings.

## Key Results
- Gradient-free methods achieve competitive or improved performance compared to gradient-based approaches on multiple text classification tasks
- Learning distributions over prompts enables effective uncertainty quantification, outperforming point estimate methods in selective classification and OOD detection
- ABC-SMC successfully performs likelihood-free prompt tuning, though with higher computational cost than gradient-based methods
- The proposed methods show better calibration (lower ECE) compared to baseline approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Learning a distribution over prompts (instead of a single point estimate) enables uncertainty quantification in predictions.
- Mechanism: By maintaining a distribution p(z|~D) over prompt embeddings, the method can generate multiple prompt samples and aggregate their predictions to estimate predictive uncertainty.
- Core assumption: The prompt embedding space z has sufficient structure that a distribution over it captures meaningful uncertainty about task-specific adaptations.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Moreover, instead of learning a single prompt, our methods learn a distribution over prompts allowing us to quantify predictive uncertainty." [section 4.2] "An alternative way to estimate the predictive distribution is by approximating the posterior distribution of prompt embedding p(z|~D)."
- Break condition: If the prompt embedding space is too high-dimensional or poorly structured, the distribution may not capture meaningful uncertainty, leading to unreliable uncertainty estimates.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Gradient-free optimization using CMA-ES can effectively tune soft prompts without access to internal model representations or gradients.
- Mechanism: CMA-ES maintains a multivariate normal distribution over candidate solutions and iteratively updates this distribution based on loss evaluations, allowing optimization without gradient information.
- Core assumption: The loss landscape in the lower-dimensional re-parameterized prompt space is sufficiently smooth for evolutionary strategies to find good optima.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3] "Recent work (Sun et al., 2022) has demonstrated promising gradient-free prompt tuning results by first employing a lower dimensional re-parameterization, z ∈ R^d with d << D, P = Az + P0" [section 4.2] "Leveraging the CMA-ES algorithm (Hansen and Ostermeier, 2001; Hansen et al., 2003), we can adapt this idea to gradient-free prompt tuning."
- Break condition: If the loss landscape is highly non-smooth or contains many local optima, CMA-ES may get stuck in poor solutions or require excessive computation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ABC-SMC can perform likelihood-free prompt tuning by treating the PLM as a black-box simulator and using sequential tolerance schedules to approximate the posterior distribution.
- Mechanism: ABC-SMC iteratively samples prompt embeddings from proposal distributions, evaluates them through the PLM API, and accepts samples based on similarity to observed outputs, gradually refining the approximation to the true posterior.
- Core assumption: The distance function (accuracy) and tolerance schedule can effectively guide the sampler toward the posterior without requiring likelihood evaluations.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.3] "Instead, in this work, we adapt a more advanced technique — sequential Monte Carlo approximate Bayesian computation (ABC-SMC) algorithm (McKinley et al., 2009) to enable efficient prompt posterior inference." [section 5.3] "In practice, we observe that the ABC-SMC algorithm suffers from weight degeneracy...we found that the heuristic of using uniform weights instead of updating the weights at each iteration of the algorithm to be far more effective."
- Break condition: If the distance function is not informative enough or the tolerance schedule is poorly chosen, ABC-SMC may fail to converge to the correct posterior distribution.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Variational Inference
  - Why needed here: To approximate the intractable posterior distribution p(z|~D) over prompt embeddings in the gradient-free setting.
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between minimizing KL-divergence and maximizing ELBO in variational inference?

- Concept: Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC)
  - Why needed here: To perform likelihood-free inference when only discrete output labels are available from the PLM API.
  - Quick check question: How does ABC differ from standard Bayesian inference, and why is it necessary in the likelihood-free setting?

- Concept: Evolutionary Strategies (CMA-ES)
  - Why needed here: To optimize continuous prompts without gradient information by maintaining and updating a distribution over candidate solutions.
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between CMA-ES and other evolutionary strategies like genetic algorithms?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  PLM API -> Prompt embedding parameterization -> CMA-ES optimizer -> Variational inference module -> ABC-SMC sampler -> Uncertainty quantification layer

- Critical path:
  1. Convert input text using predefined templates
  2. Generate prompt embedding from distribution p(z|~D)
  3. Pass prompt and text through PLM API
  4. Obtain predicted label token
  5. Evaluate loss/accept/reject based on optimization method
  6. Update prompt distribution using CMA-ES or ABC-SMC
  7. Aggregate predictions for uncertainty quantification

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost vs. uncertainty quality (more samples = better uncertainty but slower inference)
  - Dimensionality of z (higher = more expressive but harder to optimize)
  - Choice of distance function in ABC-SMC (accuracy vs. other metrics)
  - Use of uniform vs. adaptive weights in ABC-SMC (simplicity vs. potential convergence issues)

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor prediction performance despite high uncertainty (optimization failure)
  - High uncertainty on in-distribution data (calibration issues)
  - Slow convergence or getting stuck in ABC-SMC (poor proposal or distance function)
  - Instability in CMA-ES optimization (ill-conditioned covariance matrix)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare prediction performance of CMA-ES vs. random search on a simple text classification task
  2. Evaluate uncertainty calibration on a held-out validation set using expected calibration error
  3. Test ABC-SMC convergence and acceptance rates on a small synthetic prompt tuning problem

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- What is the true posterior distribution of soft prompts in the gradient-free and likelihood-free setting, and how does it compare to the approximate distributions learned by ABC-SMC and other methods? The paper acknowledges that assessing the quality of approximate posteriors remains an open problem, as the true posterior is intractable. They use downstream metrics to compare different algorithms, but these conflate posterior approximation quality with predictive performance.

- How can the computational efficiency of ABC-SMC and other simulation-based inference methods be improved for prompt tuning applications? The paper identifies computational expense as a limitation of their ABC-based approach, noting it is "more expensive than approaches that can exploit gradient information" and suggesting this as "interesting future work."

- How do different proposal distributions and perturbation kernels affect the performance of ABC-SMC in the likelihood-free prompt tuning setting? While the paper explores the uniform weights heuristic, they don't systematically investigate how different proposal distributions or more sophisticated perturbation kernels might improve performance or computational efficiency.

## Limitations

- The methods are specifically designed for scenarios with only API access to PLMs and may not directly translate to settings where gradients or internal representations are available
- ABC-SMC suffers from weight degeneracy issues and requires heuristic fixes like using uniform weights, which may introduce biases
- The computational cost of ABC-SMC is significantly higher than gradient-based approaches, limiting scalability

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The claim that the proposed methods can achieve competitive or improved performance compared to gradient-based approaches is well-supported by empirical results across multiple tasks and datasets.
- Medium Confidence: The assertion that the methods enable uncertainty quantification in predictions is supported by the theoretical framework and empirical evaluation on selective classification and OOD detection tasks.
- Low Confidence: The claim that the methods are "reliable" for likelihood-free prompt tuning is the most uncertain due to ABC-SMC convergence issues and the use of heuristic fixes.

## Next Checks

1. Evaluate the performance and uncertainty quantification of the proposed methods on a diverse set of PLMs (e.g., GPT, T5, BERT) to assess their generalizability beyond the RoBERTa-LARGE model used in the experiments.

2. Compare the proposed methods' uncertainty estimates with those obtained from other established uncertainty quantification techniques (e.g., Monte Carlo dropout, deep ensembles) to validate the quality and reliability of the uncertainty quantification.

3. Design experiments that deliberately make the likelihood-free setting more challenging (e.g., fewer output classes, noisier labels) to assess the robustness of the ABC-SMC algorithm and the quality of the posterior approximation under adverse conditions.
