# Where to Go Next for Recommender Systems? ID- vs. Modality-based Recommender Models Revisited

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13835
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13835
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MoRec with SASRec architecture achieves comparable performance to IDRec even for warm item recommendation

## Executive Summary
This paper revisits the fundamental question of whether modality-based recommender models (MoRec) can outperform traditional ID-based models (IDRec). Through comprehensive empirical studies across three large-scale datasets, the authors demonstrate that with powerful architectures like SASRec and end-to-end training, MoRec can match IDRec performance even in warm-start scenarios where IDRec was previously thought to have advantages. The study reveals that MoRec can effectively leverage recent advances in NLP and CV, but the commonly used two-stage training paradigm significantly underperforms compared to end-to-end optimization.

## Method Summary
The paper compares ID-based and modality-based recommender models using two architectures (DSSM and SASRec) across three datasets (MIND for text, HM and Bili for images). IDRec uses itemID embeddings while MoRec replaces these with pre-trained modality encoders (BERT/RoBERTa for text, ResNet/Swin for images). Both models are trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss, and performance is evaluated using HR@10 and NDCG@10 metrics. The study also investigates training paradigms by comparing end-to-end training against two-stage training where modality features are frozen.

## Key Results
- SASRec-based MoRec achieves comparable performance to IDRec even for warm item recommendation, contrary to prior assumptions
- End-to-end training of MoRec significantly outperforms two-stage training by 60-25% on different datasets
- Pre-trained modality encoders from NLP/CV domains can be effectively leveraged for recommendation tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SASRec architecture compensates for MoRec's lack of ID embeddings
- Mechanism: SASRec's sequence-to-sequence architecture with multi-head self-attention learns better user representations from interaction sequences, which compensates for MoRec's inability to use ID embeddings
- Core assumption: Sequential modeling of user behavior in SASRec is more effective than pairwise matching in DSSM for capturing item preferences
- Evidence anchors: SASRec-based IDRec is 4.9× better than DSSM-based IDRec; MoRec consistently achieves better results than IDRec on MIND dataset

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pre-trained ME transfer learned representations to recommendation
- Mechanism: ME like BERT, ResNet, and Swin are pre-trained on massive datasets to learn general representations that capture semantic and visual patterns useful for recommendation
- Core assumption: Representations learned by ME for NLP/CV tasks are sufficiently general to be useful for recommendation
- Evidence anchors: Larger pre-trained ME enables better recommendation accuracy; pre-trained BERT, ResNet, and Swin improve corresponding NLP and CV tasks

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: End-to-end training produces significantly better results than two-stage training
- Mechanism: Joint optimization allows ME to adapt representations specifically for the recommendation task, rather than using generic features
- Core assumption: Recommendation task benefits from modality features optimized for that specific task
- Evidence anchors: Two-stage training leads to 60-25% performance degradation compared to end-to-end training

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Sequence modeling with self-attention
  - Why needed here: SASRec uses multi-head self-attention to model user interaction sequences, fundamentally different from pairwise matching in DSSM
  - Quick check question: How does self-attention in SASRec differ from the attention mechanism used in DSSM, and why does this difference matter for MoRec?

- Concept: Transfer learning and fine-tuning
  - Why needed here: The paper relies on pre-trained ME that are fine-tuned for recommendation tasks
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between pre-training, fine-tuning, and training from scratch, and how do these approaches affect MoRec performance?

- Concept: Cold-start vs warm-start recommendation
  - Why needed here: The paper explicitly compares MoRec and IDRec in warm-start settings where IDRec is traditionally strong
  - Quick check question: Why are cold-start and warm-start recommendation problems different, and what advantages does MoRec have in cold-start scenarios?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: User sequence → User encoder → Item encoder → Matching score → Loss computation
- Critical path: The item encoder is the only component that differs between IDRec and MoRec
- Design tradeoffs:
  - MoRec vs IDRec: MoRec eliminates cold-start issues and enables transfer learning but requires expensive end-to-end training
  - SASRec vs DSSM: SASRec is more powerful for sequential modeling but requires longer sequences and more computation
  - End-to-end vs two-stage: End-to-end gives better performance but is 100-1000x more expensive to train
- Failure signatures:
  - Training collapse: Model performance drops to zero during training
  - Two-stage underperformance: MoRec with frozen ME features performs much worse than end-to-end training
  - Architecture mismatch: MoRec performs poorly with DSSM but well with SASRec

  3. Compare end-to-end training vs two-stage training with frozen ME features

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can MoRec completely replace IDRec across all recommendation scenarios?
- Basis in paper: The paper shows MoRec is comparable to IDRec in warm item recommendation, but IDRec still has advantages in very popular item recommendation
- Why unresolved: Only tested three datasets and two modalities; real-world applications involve many more scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive empirical studies across diverse real-world recommendation scenarios with multiple modalities

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the training efficiency gap between TS and E2E training paradigms for MoRec be reduced?
- Basis in paper: E2E-based MoRec achieves significantly better performance than TS-based MoRec, but E2E training is 100-1000x more expensive
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but does not propose solutions
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of new training paradigms or architectural modifications achieving E2E-level performance with TS-level efficiency

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can modality representations learned from NLP and CV tasks be made more generalizable for recommendation tasks?
- Basis in paper: Pre-trained modality features do not generalize well for recommendation tasks when used in TS pipelines
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the limitation but does not explore methods to improve generalizability
- What evidence would resolve it: Research demonstrating methods to improve cross-domain generalization of modality representations

## Limitations
- Study focuses on two specific architectures and three datasets, limiting generalizability
- Two-stage training experiments only tested with one learning rate configuration
- Computational costs of end-to-end training create practical barriers despite superior performance

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: MoRec with SASRec achieving comparable performance to IDRec in warm settings, and end-to-end training significantly outperforming two-stage training
- **Medium confidence**: Pre-trained ME scaling relationships and specific mechanisms by which SASRec architecture compensates for MoRec's lack of ID embeddings
- **Low confidence**: Claims about transfer learning benefits across all NLP/CV tasks, as the study only tests specific ME architectures

## Next Checks

3. Evaluate MoRec on cold-start recommendation tasks to confirm theoretical advantages over IDRec
