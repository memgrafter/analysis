# EMNS /Imz/ Corpus: An emotive single-speaker dataset for narrative storytelling in games, television and graphic novels

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13137
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13137
- Reference count: 0
- Provides 2.3-hour high-quality speech dataset with emotion, expressiveness, and word emphasis labels

## Executive Summary
The EMNS corpus is a 2.3-hour high-quality speech dataset with emotion, expressiveness, and word emphasis labels for training natural text-to-speech synthesis in narrative applications. The dataset provides eight equally distributed emotional states and expressiveness levels (0-10) as self-reported by the speaker, recorded in a controlled, low-noise environment. EMNS addresses gaps in existing datasets by offering phonetically diverse prompts, clean audio, and expressive intensity gradations suitable for interactive storytelling. An evaluation with 19 participants found EMNS achieved the highest average scores for conveying emotions (41%), expressiveness (46%), and genuineness compared to other datasets. Classification results confirmed accurate emotion representation and listener perception of expressiveness. The dataset collection tool is released under Apache 2.0 license for community use.

## Method Summary
The EMNS corpus was created using a web-based recording tool that presented voice actors with phonetically diverse sentences from Mozilla Common Voice. Actors recorded each sentence multiple times under different emotional states while self-reporting expressiveness levels on a 0-10 scale. Recordings were captured in a sound-dampened environment using a cardioid microphone, then validated and processed with Montreal Forced Aligner to extract phoneme durations. The resulting dataset includes TSV metadata files with emotion, intensity, and file path information, paired with WebM audio files. The dataset features eight emotions equally distributed, with self-reported expressiveness ratings providing granular intensity information for each utterance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Controlled single-speaker recording with self-reported expressiveness labels yields consistent emotion-intensity mapping for TTS training.
- Mechanism: The speaker records the same prompt multiple times under different emotional states while self-rating expressiveness on a 0-10 scale, creating aligned audio-label pairs that model can learn to map directly from text to emotional prosody.
- Core assumption: Speaker's self-assessment of expressiveness correlates strongly with listener perception of emotional intensity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "speaker self-reported expressiveness level on a scale of 0 to 10"
  - [section] "voice actor self-reported their expressiveness level on a scale of 0 to 10, where 0 represents a neutral state, and 10 indicates a highly expressive state"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct listener validation of self-reported ratings in the dataset description itself
- Break condition: If self-reported ratings systematically diverge from listener perception, model will learn incorrect prosody-emotion mappings, reducing naturalness in generated speech.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High phonetic diversity from Mozilla Common Voice ensures the dataset generalizes to unseen text for expressive TTS.
- Mechanism: By selecting phonetically balanced sentences from a large, validated source, the model learns to apply emotional prosody across diverse linguistic structures rather than memorizing specific phrases.
- Core assumption: Phonetic diversity in training data correlates with generalization capability in TTS synthesis.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "phonetically diverse prompts"
  - [section] "We selected a diverse subset of sentences from Mozilla's Common Voice (CV) dataset to ensure a comprehensive range of sentence types"
  - [corpus] Moderate - dataset claims phonetic diversity but doesn't provide specific diversity metrics
- Break condition: If phonetic coverage is insufficient for target domain vocabulary, model will fail to generate appropriate prosody for unseen prompts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Clean, controlled recording environment eliminates confounding acoustic artifacts that would interfere with emotion classification.
- Mechanism: Sound-dampened room, cardioid microphone pattern, and post-processing (removing mouse clicks, pauses) create noise-free audio that allows neural models to focus on emotional content rather than environmental factors.
- Core assumption: Background noise and recording artifacts significantly impact emotion recognition and synthesis quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "clean audio recordings"
  - [section] "The recording environment was optimised for sound quality with a sound-dampened room and sound-absorbing materials"
  - [corpus] Strong - detailed recording setup described with specific equipment and techniques
- Break condition: If recording artifacts remain in data, model may learn to associate noise patterns with emotions, reducing performance on clean input.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Web interface → recording → validation → TSV metadata → WebM audio
- Critical path: Data collection → validation → preprocessing → model training → evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single speaker vs. multi-speaker: Consistency vs. speaker diversity
  - Self-reported vs. expert-labeled emotions: Scalability vs. potential bias
  - WebM vs. WAV: File size vs. universal compatibility
  - 0-10 vs. categorical expressiveness: Granularity vs. simplicity
- Failure signatures:
  - Low emotion classification accuracy indicates poor label quality or insufficient phonetic diversity
  - High variance in expressiveness ratings suggests self-reporting inconsistency
  - Artifacts in audio despite controlled recording indicate equipment or setup issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a baseline TTS model on EMNS data and evaluate naturalness vs. a non-emotional baseline
  2. Test model's ability to generalize expressiveness to unseen prompts from the same speaker
  3. Compare emotion classification accuracy on EMNS vs. other public datasets to validate quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do naturalness ratings compare between EMNS and real human speech across different expressiveness levels?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper compares EMNS to other datasets but doesn't include human speech as a baseline for naturalness comparison
- Why unresolved: The evaluation focused on comparing EMNS against other datasets rather than establishing how it compares to natural human speech
- What evidence would resolve it: A listening test comparing EMNS recordings to natural human speech samples across the same emotional states and expressiveness levels

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the minimum dataset size required to achieve comparable emotional expressiveness and classification accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention "Future work includes expanding the number of samples" suggesting uncertainty about optimal dataset size
- Why unresolved: The current 2.3-hour dataset may not represent the minimum effective size, and scaling effects on emotion recognition accuracy are unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments training with progressively smaller subsets of EMNS to identify the threshold where emotion classification accuracy significantly degrades

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do cross-cultural differences affect emotion perception in the EMNS corpus?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The evaluation included non-native English speakers but didn't analyze cross-cultural perception differences in detail
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions including non-native speakers but doesn't report on potential systematic differences in emotion perception across cultures
- What evidence would resolve it: A cross-cultural study with participants from diverse cultural backgrounds rating the same EMNS samples to identify systematic perception differences

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the inclusion of sarcasm as an emotion category affect the overall emotion classification performance of models trained on EMNS?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "including sarcasm in our list of emotions is crucial due to its unique nature and the complexity it adds to emotional expression"
- Why unresolved: While sarcasm was included, the paper doesn't report how its inclusion impacts overall emotion classification accuracy or model complexity
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments training models with and without the sarcasm category on EMNS to measure changes in overall classification performance and confusion matrices

## Limitations

- Reliance on single-speaker self-reported expressiveness ratings introduces potential bias without external validation
- Evaluation sample size of 19 participants may not capture full range of listener variability in emotion perception
- Dataset focus on narrative prompts from Mozilla Common Voice may limit applicability to dialogue or non-narrative speech contexts

## Confidence

- High confidence: Recording quality and controlled environment claims (detailed methodology described)
- Medium confidence: Emotion classification accuracy results (limited participant sample size)
- Medium confidence: Expressiveness rating consistency (self-reported data lacks external validation)
- Low confidence: Generalization claims to other speakers or speech domains (single-speaker focus)
