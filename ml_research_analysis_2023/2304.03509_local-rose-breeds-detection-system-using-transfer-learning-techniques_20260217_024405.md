# Local Rose Breeds Detection System Using Transfer Learning Techniques

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03509
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03509
- Reference count: 27
- Five rose breeds accurately classified with 99% accuracy using VGG16 transfer learning

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a rose breed detection system using transfer learning to address the lack of existing work on identifying specific flower breeds. The authors collected 1,939 raw images of five rose breeds, augmented them to generate 9,306 training and 388 testing images, and evaluated four transfer learning models: Inception V3, ResNet50, Xception, and VGG16. VGG16 achieved the highest accuracy of 99%, demonstrating its effectiveness for this task. The system can help farmers and agriculturists maximize rose production by providing information on cultivation and care processes for each breed. Future work includes developing a web-based system for breed detection with additional details on characteristics and cultivation methods.

## Method Summary
The authors collected 1,939 raw images of five rose breeds and applied data augmentation techniques (rescale, horizontal flip, vertical flip, shear, and zoom) to generate 9,306 training images and 388 testing images. Four pre-trained transfer learning models (Inception V3, ResNet50, Xception, and VGG16) were fine-tuned on the augmented dataset. The models were evaluated based on accuracy, with VGG16 achieving the highest performance of 99%. The study demonstrates the effectiveness of transfer learning and data augmentation in rose breed classification tasks.

## Key Results
- VGG16 achieved 99% accuracy, outperforming Inception V3, ResNet50, and Xception on rose breed classification
- Data augmentation expanded the dataset from 1,939 to 9,306 training images, reducing overfitting
- The system successfully classified five specific rose breeds, with potential applications for agricultural optimization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transfer learning leverages pre-trained CNN weights to achieve high accuracy with limited rose breed data.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained models (Inception V3, ResNet50, Xception, VGG16) were initialized with weights from ImageNet, then fine-tuned on the rose breed dataset.
- Core assumption: ImageNet features are transferable to rose breed classification because both tasks involve visual pattern recognition.

- Break condition: If rose breeds are visually too dissimilar from ImageNet categories, the transfer learning advantage may vanish and accuracy could drop significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data augmentation increases effective dataset size and improves model generalization.
- Mechanism: Applied five augmentation techniques (rescale, horizontal flip, vertical flip, shear, zoom) to training images, generating 9,306 training images from 1,939 raw images.
- Core assumption: Synthetic variations created by augmentation expose the model to a broader distribution of visual conditions without overfitting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.B] "We used image augmentation in our training dataset to minimize overfitting problem. Also, it has been used to obtain higher accuracy in prediction and for overfitting of data."
  - [section III.B] "In this research rescale, horizontal flip, vertical flip, shear and zooming augmentation methods had been used for the training dataset."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence from corpus; related work [11] mentions augmentation but not for rose breed detection specifically.
- Break condition: Excessive or unrealistic augmentation (e.g., extreme shear) could distort breed-specific features and harm accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: VGG16 architecture is particularly suited for this task due to its depth and uniform convolution structure.
- Mechanism: VGG16's 16-layer depth with small 3x3 convolution filters captures fine-grained visual patterns in rose breeds.
- Core assumption: Uniform, deep convolution stacks extract discriminative features for breed-level classification better than shallower or irregular architectures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.C] "VGG16 [20] is an intelligible model which is vastly used deep learning model... This model gains 92.7% top5 accuracy in terms of testing in ImageNet."
  - [section IV] "According to the 'Table. II' it is shows that VGG16 outperformed in terms of accuracy. This model shows the highest accuracy among the other three models."

- Break condition: If breed differences are subtle and require larger receptive fields, VGG16's small filters may be insufficient.

- Component map: Raw images -> Preprocessing -> Augmentation -> Train/Val/Test split -> Pre-trained models -> Fine-tuning -> Evaluation
- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess 1,939 raw rose images
  2. Apply augmentation to generate 9,306 training samples
  3. Fine-tune selected pre-trained model on training set
  4. Evaluate on 388 test images
  5. Compare confusion matrices and accuracy metrics
- Design tradeoffs:
  - VGG16 vs ResNet50: VGG16 simpler but slower to train; ResNet50 faster but lower accuracy here
  - Augmentation extent: More augmentation reduces overfitting but risks unrealistic samples
  - Model complexity: Larger models (Xception) may overfit small datasets
- Failure signatures:
  - High training accuracy but low test accuracy → overfitting (insufficient augmentation or too many epochs)
  - Consistently poor accuracy across all models → poor data quality or insufficient visual discriminative features
  - Sudden accuracy drop in late epochs → learning rate too high or aggressive augmentation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train VGG16 with 5 epochs, no augmentation, measure baseline accuracy
  2. Add augmentation, train same model for 10 epochs, compare overfitting
  3. Fine-tune last 2 blocks of VGG16 vs fine-tuning all layers, measure impact on accuracy and training time
