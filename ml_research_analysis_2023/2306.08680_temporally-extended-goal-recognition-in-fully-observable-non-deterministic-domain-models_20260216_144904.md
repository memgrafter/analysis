# Temporally Extended Goal Recognition in Fully Observable Non-Deterministic Domain Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08680
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08680
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: First approach for recognizing temporally extended goals (LTLf/PLTLf) in FOND domains with TPR > 0.75 and FPR < 0.15

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first approach to recognizing temporally extended goals expressed in Linear Temporal Logic on finite traces (LTLf) or Parametric Linear Temporal Logic on finite traces (PLTLf) in fully observable non-deterministic (FOND) planning domains. The method extends probabilistic goal recognition as planning to handle non-deterministic environments by compiling temporal goal formulas into augmented FOND domains using parametric DFA encoding. The approach demonstrates high accuracy across six FOND domains with varying levels of observability, achieving TPR > 0.75 and FPR < 0.15 for both conjunctive and temporally extended goals.

## Method Summary
The approach compiles temporally extended goals (LTLf/PLTLf) into standard FOND reachability problems via parametric DFA encoding. First, it converts temporal formulas into DFAs using MONA, then lifts them into parametric DFAs by replacing domain objects with variables. These are encoded into PDDL domains with additional fluents representing DFA states and synchronization actions that update states based on domain fluents. The system then extracts strong-cyclic policies using off-the-shelf FOND planners, enumerates possible executions, and computes posterior probabilities over goal hypotheses using average distances from observations to goal states, with penalties applied when observation pairs lack order relations in execution traces.

## Key Results
- High accuracy in recognizing temporally extended goals with TPR > 0.75 and FPR < 0.15
- Successful recognition across both conjunctive and temporally extended goals (LTLf/PLTLf)
- Effective performance in both offline (TPR, FPR, FNR, F1-Score) and online (Ranked First) settings
- Robustness across varying levels of observability in six different FOND domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The compilation stage transforms temporally extended goals (LTLf/PLTLf) into standard FOND reachability problems via parametric DFA encoding. The system converts LTLf/PLTLf formulas into DFAs using MONA, then lifts them into parametric DFAs by replacing domain objects with variables. These PDFAs are encoded into PDDL domains with additional fluents representing DFA states and synchronization actions that update states based on domain fluents.

Core assumption: The mapping from DFA to parametric DFA preserves the acceptance condition such that any plan solving the compiled FOND problem corresponds to a plan satisfying the original temporal goal.

### Mechanism 2
The probabilistic framework computes posterior probabilities over goal hypotheses by estimating "distance" from observations to goal achievement. For each goal hypothesis, the system extracts strong-cyclic policies and enumerates possible executions, computing average distances from each observation to goal states and applying penalties when observation pairs lack order relations in execution traces.

Core assumption: Observations closer (in terms of action sequences) to goal-achieving executions indicate higher likelihood of the corresponding goal hypothesis.

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract] "empirical evaluation... shows high accuracy (TPR > 0.75, FPR < 0.15)"
- [section] "We use d to compute an estimated score that expresses 'how far' every observed action... is to a temporally extended goal"

### Mechanism 3
The approach handles non-determinism by considering all possible executions under strong-cyclic policies and using average distances rather than specific execution paths. Instead of tracking a single execution path, the system maintains a set of all possible executions for each policy and computes the average distance across all executions where each observation appears.

Core assumption: Strong-cyclic policies guarantee eventual goal achievement under fairness assumptions, making average distance a valid metric for recognition.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Compilation module -> FOND planner interface -> Execution enumerator -> Scoring engine -> Probability calculator -> Recognition interface
- Critical path: Compilation → Planning → Execution enumeration → Scoring → Probability calculation → Recognition output
- Design tradeoffs: The system trades computational complexity (enumerating all executions) for accuracy in handling non-determinism, versus approximate methods that might be faster but less precise
- Failure signatures: High FPR values indicate poor discrimination between similar goals; high computation times suggest state space explosion in DFA compilation; low TPR suggests the distance metric doesn't capture relevant patterns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the recognition approach be extended to handle noisy observations, where the observed actions may not perfectly match the actual executed actions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention "As future work, we intend to extend and adapt our recognition approach for being able to deal with spurious (noisy) observations" in the Conclusions section.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on perfect observability, where observations exactly match executed actions. Extending to noisy observations would require a different probabilistic framework and potentially a different compilation approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing and evaluating the recognition approach with noisy observations, comparing its performance to the perfect observability case.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the recognition approach be extended to anticipate the policy that the agent is executing to achieve its goals, rather than just recognizing the goal?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention "recognize not only the temporal extended goals but also anticipate the policy that the agent is executing to achieve its goals" as future work.
- Why unresolved: The current approach only recognizes the intended goal based on observations. Anticipating the policy would require inferring the specific sequence of actions the agent is likely to take.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a method to infer the most likely policy given the recognized goal and observations, and evaluating its performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the recognition approach be adapted to handle non-deterministic policies with loops, rather than just policies without repeated states?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that "strong-cyclic solutions may have infinite possible executions" and that they "consider executions that do not enter loops, and for those entering possible loops, we consider only the ones entering loops at most once."
- Why unresolved: The current approach assumes policies without loops, which limits its applicability to a subset of non-deterministic policies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the approach to handle policies with loops, potentially by incorporating a different distance metric or by using a different compilation technique.

## Limitations
- The compilation mechanism can lead to state space explosion for complex temporal goals with nested operators
- The probabilistic framework assumes that average distances across all possible executions provide meaningful discrimination, which may not hold when observations appear in very different positions
- The evaluation focuses on domains with relatively simple non-determinism (few probabilistic outcomes per action), so performance in domains with more complex branching remains unknown

## Confidence

**Mechanism 1 (DFA compilation):** Medium - The theoretical foundation is sound but implementation details are underspecified
**Mechanism 2 (probabilistic scoring):** Medium - The distance-based approach is reasonable but the exact scoring formula needs verification
**Overall accuracy claims:** Medium - Results are positive but evaluation domains may not represent full complexity of FOND problems
