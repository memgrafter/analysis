# Optimizing Federated Learning for Medical Image Classification on Distributed Non-iid Datasets with Partial Labels

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06180
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06180
- Reference count: 17
- Key outcome: FedFBN outperforms FedAvg and FedBN in non-iid federated learning with partial labels by freezing batch normalization layers in pretrained networks

## Executive Summary
FedFBN introduces a federated learning framework that addresses domain shift challenges in medical imaging by freezing batch normalization layers during training. The approach draws inspiration from transfer learning, using pretrained DenseNet121 networks as the backbone while keeping BN layers frozen throughout the training process. This design choice enables effective aggregation of global models from distributed non-iid datasets with partial labels, achieving superior performance compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
FedFBN is a federated learning framework that freezes batch normalization layers in pretrained DenseNet121 networks to address convergence issues caused by domain shift in non-iid medical imaging datasets with partial labels. The method involves initializing models with pretrained backbones, freezing BN layers during training, training local models on client data, and aggregating them using a surgical aggregation strategy. The framework was evaluated on synthetic and large-scale real-world chest x-ray datasets with both iid and non-iid distributions, and with complete and partial label sets.

## Key Results
- FedFBN outperformed current aggregation strategies for training global models using distributed and non-iid data with partial labels
- Achieved comparable or better AUROC scores across all scenarios, particularly in distributed non-iid settings with partial labels
- Generalized better to unseen distributions and matched baseline centrally-trained model performance on external MIMIC test set

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Freezing batch normalization layers mitigates domain shift in non-iid federated learning settings.
- Mechanism: By freezing BN layers, the model preserves learned statistics from pretraining, preventing local models from adapting BN parameters to different data distributions and ensuring consistent global model statistics across clients.
- Core assumption: Pretrained BN statistics are sufficiently robust for the target domain or feature distributions across clients don't deviate too drastically from pretrained statistics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "draws inspiration from transfer learning by using pretrained networks as the model backend and freezing the batch normalization layers throughout the training process."
  - [section]: "FedFBN keeps these statistics frozen i.e. avoids updating them in the training process."
- Break condition: If domain shift is extreme, frozen BN statistics may become a bottleneck, leading to poor feature normalization and degraded performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: FedFBN achieves better generalization on unseen distributions compared to FedAvg and FedBN.
- Mechanism: Freezing BN layers maintains stable normalization statistics across rounds, helping the global model generalize better to external test sets with different data distributions.
- Core assumption: External test set distributions aren't too dissimilar from training sets, and frozen BN layers can still provide effective normalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "FedFBN outperformed current aggregation strategies... particularly in distributed non-iid settings with partial labels, where it generalized better to unseen distributions."
  - [section]: "On the MIMIC external test set, we observe that FedFBN generalizes better to unseen distributions and outperforms all models with a mean AUROC of 0.75 (p < 0.001)."
- Break condition: If the external test set has a drastically different distribution, frozen BN layers may not normalize features effectively, leading to poor generalization.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: FedFBN addresses limitations of FedBN in scenarios with partial labels.
- Mechanism: Unlike FedBN which only aggregates model weights and leaves BN layers local, FedFBN freezes BN layers ensuring the global model benefits from stable normalization statistics even when datasets have different or partially overlapping labels.
- Core assumption: The global model can still learn meaningful representations even with frozen BN layers, especially when using a strong pretrained backbone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "FedFBN outperforms current aggregation strategies for training global models using distributed and non-iid data with partial labels."
  - [section]: "Recent literature has demonstrated that, in the case of partial labels when participating datasets have either different of partially overlapping labels, FedBN converges to a suboptimal solution and exhibits poor performance."
- Break condition: If the partial label scenario is too complex, freezing BN layers may limit the model's ability to adapt to specific label distributions of each client.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pretrained DenseNet121 backbone -> Frozen BN layers -> Classification heads -> Surgical aggregation framework -> FedFBN aggregation strategy
- Critical path:
  1. Initialize model with pretrained DenseNet121
  2. Freeze BN layers during training
  3. Train local models on client data
  4. Aggregate local models using FedFBN strategy
  5. Evaluate global model on held-out and external test sets
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Freezing BN layers simplifies aggregation but may limit adaptability to extreme domain shifts
  - Using a pretrained backbone provides strong initial features but may introduce biases
  - Surgical aggregation allows for task-agnostic learning but requires careful hyperparameter tuning
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on external test sets indicates insufficient generalization
  - Degraded performance on partial label subsets suggests limitations in handling complex label distributions
  - Convergence issues may arise if domain shift is too extreme for frozen BN layers
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate FedFBN on synthetic iid datasets with complete labels to establish baseline performance
  2. Test FedFBN on synthetic iid datasets with partial labels to assess handling of label heterogeneity
  3. Evaluate FedFBN on large-scale non-iid datasets with complete and partial labels to test generalization and robustness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does FedFBN perform in a realistic federated learning setup with numerous nodes rather than the two-node surgical aggregation setup evaluated in this paper?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "While our work is limited to smaller-scale experiments that do not reflect a realistic FL setup with numerous nodes"
- Why unresolved: The current evaluation only uses two nodes, which doesn't capture the complexity of multi-node federated learning environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with FedFBN evaluated on federated learning setups with 10+ nodes across different network topologies and communication patterns.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of FedFBN's frozen batch normalization layers on model performance when fine-tuning for different medical imaging tasks beyond chest X-ray classification?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates FedFBN's effectiveness on chest X-ray datasets but doesn't explore its generalizability to other medical imaging modalities or tasks.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation is limited to chest X-ray classification, leaving uncertainty about FedFBN's effectiveness for other medical imaging applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments applying FedFBN to different medical imaging tasks (CT, MRI, histopathology) and evaluating performance against other federated learning strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does FedFBN's performance scale with dataset size and the number of disease labels in real-world federated learning scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses datasets ranging from ~112K to ~224K images, but doesn't evaluate scaling behavior for much larger datasets or label spaces.
- Why unresolved: The experiments use relatively large but finite datasets, and scaling behavior to truly massive medical imaging datasets remains unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying dataset sizes by orders of magnitude and measuring FedFBN's performance and convergence characteristics.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to one backbone architecture (DenseNet121), making generalizability to other architectures unclear
- Experiments conducted only on chest X-ray data, limiting external validity to other medical imaging modalities
- Mechanism explanation relies heavily on intuition about BN freezing without ablation studies isolating specific contributions
