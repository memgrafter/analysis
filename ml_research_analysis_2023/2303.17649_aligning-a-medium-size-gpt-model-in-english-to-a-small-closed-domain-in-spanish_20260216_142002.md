# Aligning a medium-size GPT model in English to a small closed domain in Spanish

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17649
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17649
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: A medium-sized GPT model (DialoGPT) can be aligned to a small Spanish closed domain using a reward model trained on human preferences

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a methodology to align DialoGPT, a medium-sized English conversational model, to a small closed domain in Spanish for question answering. The approach involves fine-tuning the model on Spanish conversation data, then further fine-tuning on a small domain-specific dataset, and finally training a reward model based on human judgments to guide response generation. The results demonstrate that this alignment process is feasible and improves response quality compared to standard decoding techniques.

## Method Summary
The methodology involves three main stages: first, fine-tuning DialoGPT on a large Spanish conversation dataset translated from English Reddit data; second, further fine-tuning on a small LCC dataset containing question-answer pairs about a university undergraduate program; and third, training a reward model using human-rated responses to guide the decoding process. The reward model is a feed-forward neural network that scores responses based on human judgments, and this scoring is used to improve the generation of appropriate answers.

## Key Results
- The proposed method outperforms other techniques in aligning a medium-sized language model to a small dataset
- Human judgment results favor the reward model-based decoding approach
- BLEU scores and perplexity metrics show improved performance after fine-tuning
- It is feasible to use a reward model to align response generation to a small closed domain

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pre-trained DialoGPT model -> Spanish conversation dataset -> LCC dataset -> Reward model -> Decoding algorithms
- Critical path:
  1. Fine-tune DialoGPT on Spanish conversations
  2. Fine-tune DialoGPT on LCC dataset
  3. Generate responses and collect human ratings
  4. Train reward model on (question, answer, rating) tuples
  5. Use reward model to guide decoding and generate final responses

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. computational cost: Medium-sized DialoGPT is chosen for feasibility over larger models like GPT-3/4.
  - Dataset size vs. alignment quality: Small LCC dataset requires careful fine-tuning to avoid overfitting.
  - Reward model complexity vs. generalization: Simple feed-forward architecture chosen for efficiency, but may limit expressiveness.

- Failure signatures:
  - Responses are incoherent or repetitive
  - Model generates responses identical to training examples
  - Perplexity increases significantly after fine-tuning
  - Human evaluation scores are low despite good automatic metrics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune DialoGPT on Spanish dataset only and evaluate perplexity on a held-out Spanish test set.
  2. Fine-tune DialoGPT on LCC dataset only and compare BLEU scores with the Spanish-only model.
  3. Generate responses using reward sampling and compare with standard sampling using both automatic metrics and human evaluation.

## Limitations
- Small human evaluation sample size (10 questions) may not be representative of overall performance
- Spanish conversation dataset is automatically translated from English, potentially introducing quality issues
- No exploration of how this approach scales to larger, more complex domains or different languages

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The technical methodology for fine-tuning DialoGPT on Spanish data and implementing reward-based decoding is clearly specified and reproducible. The architectural choices for the reward model are explicitly described.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that the reward model improves response quality is supported by the experimental results, but the human evaluation sample size is small and may not be representative.
- **Low confidence**: The scalability of this approach to larger, more complex domains or to languages with different linguistic structures than Spanish is uncertain, as the paper does not explore these scenarios.
