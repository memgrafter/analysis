# A Survey of Federated Learning for Connected and Automated Vehicles

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10677
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10677
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comprehensive survey of FL applications for CAVs, identifying key data modalities, algorithms, and security approaches while highlighting challenges and open research questions.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive survey of federated learning (FL) applications for connected and automated vehicles (CAVs), examining how FL can enhance machine learning models for perception, motion planning, and control tasks while preserving data privacy. The survey reviews progress across various CAV applications including steering wheel angle prediction, vehicle trajectory prediction, object detection, motion control, and driver monitoring, analyzing the data modalities, algorithms, and security approaches used in each. While demonstrating FL's potential to enable collaborative model development across diverse vehicles, the paper identifies significant challenges including resource limitations, privacy concerns, catastrophic forgetting, fairness issues, and the need for better evaluation criteria. The authors conclude that while FL shows promise for CAV applications, further research is needed to address computational overhead, security vulnerabilities, and develop robust incentive mechanisms for vehicle participation.

## Method Summary
The survey employs a comprehensive literature review methodology, systematically categorizing FL applications in CAVs across different task domains including perception, motion planning, and control. The authors analyze existing research papers to identify key data modalities (images, LiDAR, vehicle status), algorithms (FedAvg, FedProx, Fed-ADAM), and security approaches used in CAV FL systems. They extract and synthesize information about specific applications, challenges, and limitations from the literature, organizing the findings into structured categories. The survey focuses on understanding how FL addresses privacy concerns in CAVs while enabling collaborative model development, examining both the technical implementations and the broader implications for real-world deployment. The methodology emphasizes identifying gaps in current research and outlining future directions for the field.

## Key Results
- FL enables CAVs to collaboratively train ML models while preserving data privacy through local training and parameter sharing
- The survey identifies specific applications including steering wheel angle prediction, vehicle trajectory prediction, object detection, motion control, and driver monitoring
- Major challenges include resource limitations, privacy concerns, catastrophic forgetting, fairness issues, and need for better evaluation criteria

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Federated learning enables CAVs to collaboratively train ML models while preserving data privacy
- Mechanism: Each vehicle trains on local data and only shares model parameters/gradients with the central server, which aggregates them to update a global model. This avoids transmitting raw data while allowing knowledge sharing across vehicles.
- Core assumption: Local data remains on vehicles and only model parameters are transmitted securely
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "FL enables CA Vs to learn from a wide range of driving environments and improve their overall performance while ensuring the privacy and security of local vehicle data."
  - [section II.A]: "In FL, the edge devices/clients only send the gradients or the learnable parameters to the cloud server rather than sending massive local datasets"
- Break condition: If model parameters leak information about the training data or if the aggregation process is compromised

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: FL reduces communication overhead compared to centralized learning for CAVs
- Mechanism: Instead of transmitting large volumes of sensor data (images, LiDAR point clouds, vehicle status), vehicles only send model updates (parameters/gradients) which are much smaller in size. This significantly reduces bandwidth requirements.
- Core assumption: Model parameter updates are substantially smaller than raw data transmission
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II.A]: "FL focuses on the model parameters and ignores the data, which addresses the drawback that visual image-based systems tend to compromise user privacy. Moreover, FL also solves the data transmission problem caused by the inflated data size of images and videos"
  - [Table I]: Shows "Communication (Data transmission) requirement" as low for FL vs high for centralized learning
- Break condition: When model updates become too large due to model complexity or when frequent updates are needed

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: FL enables CAVs to handle heterogeneous and non-iid data distributions across vehicles
- Mechanism: By aggregating updates from diverse vehicles operating in different environments, the global model learns to handle variations in data distribution. This improves generalization across different driving conditions.
- Core assumption: Diversity in vehicle data improves model robustness
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II.C]: "Data heterogeneity, client drift, and data imbalance from clients have proved to significantly impact the performance of FedAvg optimization"
  - [section III.A]: "Driver monitoring application is a highly personalized application where the driver's behavior is strongly associated with personal habits, emotions, cultural background, and even the interpretation of instructions. This user heterogeneity poses a challenge for FL systems."
- Break condition: When data heterogeneity is too extreme for the aggregation algorithm to handle effectively

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can federated learning frameworks effectively handle catastrophic forgetting when vehicle data is continuously updated during iterative training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Catastrophic forgetting... when the FL framework is updated on new data in iteration—see Algorithm 1, the global model forgets the previous knowledge and leads to catastrophic forgetting."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a challenge but doesn't propose specific solutions for maintaining knowledge of previous driving scenarios while incorporating new data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of techniques like elastic weight consolidation, memory replay buffers, or dynamic regularization methods specifically adapted for CAV federated learning scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the optimal client selection and resource allocation mechanisms for federated learning in connected and automated vehicles that balance communication efficiency with model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Lack of robust approach for vehicle selection and resource allocation... Currently, there is no popular mechanism that can select non-redundant data from CA Vs to minimize the network strain."
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions this as a challenge, it doesn't provide concrete solutions or evaluate different selection strategies in real-world CAV scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different client selection algorithms (based on data diversity, computational resources, network conditions) in realistic CAV environments with varying traffic densities and scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can federated learning systems for connected and automated vehicles achieve robustness against security threats like backdoor attacks and free-riding while maintaining communication efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Privacy and security issues... The FL central server is also prone to attack and thereby causing the entire learning process to collapse" and mentions various attack types without providing comprehensive solutions.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies security vulnerabilities but doesn't present integrated solutions that address multiple attack vectors while preserving the communication efficiency benefits of FL.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of defense mechanisms that combine differential privacy, secure aggregation, and anomaly detection in a unified framework specifically tested against realistic CAV threat models.
