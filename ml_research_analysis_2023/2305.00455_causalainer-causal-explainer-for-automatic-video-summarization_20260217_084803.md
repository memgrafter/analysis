# Causalainer: Causal Explainer for Automatic Video Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00455

## Executive Summary
Causalainer introduces a novel approach to video summarization that enhances model explainability through causal modeling. The method employs Bayesian probability to uncover causal relationships between key components in video summarization by introducing four random variables. Experimental results demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across three standard datasets while providing greater model explainability compared to existing approaches.

## Method Summary
Causalainer builds on Bayesian causal modeling to improve explainability in video summarization. The approach introduces four random variables (latent confounder Z, observed confounder X, intervention t, outcome y) with joint distributions to capture causal relationships rather than just learning optimal parameters. To address challenges in predicting data intervention and model outcomes, helper distributions are introduced along with a causal semantics extractor to effectively distill mutual information from visual and textual inputs. The model is trained using Adam optimizer for 60 epochs with specific learning rate and epsilon values.

## Key Results
- Achieves F1-scores of 67.5 on TVSum dataset
- Achieves F1-scores of 55.5 on QueryVS dataset
- Achieves F1-scores of 52.4 on SumMe dataset
- Provides greater model explainability compared to existing approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Causalainer improves explainability by modeling latent confounders through Bayesian causal inference
- Mechanism: Introduces four random variables (latent confounder Z, observed confounder X, intervention t, outcome y) with joint distributions to capture causal relationships rather than just learning optimal parameters
- Core assumption: The observations (X, t, y) from a deep neural network are sufficient to approximately recover the joint distribution p(Z, X, t, y) of unobserved/latent confounding variable Z
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 3.1]

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Helper distributions address prediction challenges from video noise and blur by providing auxiliary learning signals
- Mechanism: Introduces helper distributions q(ti|xi) and q(yi|xi,ti) that form a new loss term to guide model learning when predicting intervention behavior and outcomes is difficult
- Core assumption: Predicting behaviors of data intervention and model's outcome can be challenging in practice due to various factors (video noise, lens or motion blur)
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 3.3]

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Causal semantics extractor resolves multi-modal interference by effectively distilling mutual information between visual and textual inputs
- Mechanism: Uses a transformer-based causal attention mechanism that selects top κ most similar keys/values for each query rather than all tokens, then combines visual and textual attention through element-wise multiplication
- Core assumption: When multi-modal inputs are available, the extra input sometimes can harm the model performance most likely due to the interactions between different modalities being ineffective
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 3.4]

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using Bayesian causal modeling increases explainability but adds computational complexity compared to standard supervised approaches
  - The causal attention mechanism reduces token interactions for efficiency but may miss some contextual information
  - Introducing helper distributions adds training stability but requires careful balancing of loss terms

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on single-modality tasks suggests causal attention mechanism is too restrictive
  - Unstable training or divergence indicates helper distributions are creating conflicting gradients
  - No improvement in explainability metrics suggests the causal modeling assumptions don't hold for the dataset
