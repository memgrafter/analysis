# LSAS: Lightweight Sub-attention Strategy for Alleviating Attention Bias Problem

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05200
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05200
- Reference count: 24
- Primary result: LSAS improves Attention Efficiency (AE) by up to 70.36% and boosts classification accuracy while reducing parameters and computation.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses attention bias in deep neural networks for computer vision by proposing the Lightweight Sub-attention Strategy (LSAS). LSAS mitigates position and range biases through multi-order sub-attention modules that refine attention maps and a selection gate that controls parameter growth. Experiments show significant improvements in AE metrics and classification performance across multiple datasets and attention modules, with reduced computational overhead.

## Method Summary
LSAS enhances existing self-attention modules by applying them multiple times in a forward stage and combining the results in a backward stage, progressively refining attention maps. A selection gate limits sub-attention application based on channel count, reducing parameters and computation. This approach targets both position bias (focusing on label-independent regions) and range bias (focusing on regions not fully contained in the ideal region).

## Key Results
- LSAS improves SENet AE by 70.36% on AE-STL10 and 19.49% on AE-ImageNet10.
- Top-1 accuracy improves by 1.06% on ImageNet, 1.08% on CIFAR10, and 0.91% on CIFAR100.
- Parameter count reduced by 8.45% on ImageNet and computational speed increased by 13.79%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LSAS reduces attention bias by applying self-attention multiple times within the same attention module.
- Mechanism: The original attention module output is fed through a series of sub-attention modules (forward stage) and then combined with weighted outputs from each sub-attention layer (backward stage). This multi-order processing refines the attention map, focusing more on relevant regions while suppressing background noise.
- Core assumption: Repeated application of attention on attention maps progressively reduces irrelevant focus.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "LSAS, which utilizes high-order sub-attention modules to improve the original self-attention modules."
  - [section III] "we use the self-attention mechanism to enhance the self-attention mechanism."
  - [corpus] No direct supporting evidence found; this is inferred from the paper's description.
- Break condition: If the additional sub-attention layers overfit or if the backward combination does not properly weight the attention maps, the bias reduction effect could diminish.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The selection gate limits parameter growth and computational cost by selectively enabling sub-attention layers.
- Mechanism: A gate function controls how many sub-attention modules are actually used based on the channel count of the input, preventing unnecessary computation in early layers.
- Core assumption: Not all layers benefit equally from attention enhancement; later layers contribute more to final performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III] "Our selection gate makes DNNs only insert self-attention modules to the tail networks"
  - [section IV] "The selection gate determines the embedding of the multi-level sub-attention module at the end of DNNs"
  - [corpus] No direct supporting evidence found; this is inferred from the paper's description.
- Break condition: If the threshold is set too high, useful attention enhancement in earlier layers is lost; too low and computational savings are minimal.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LSAS improves AE by concentrating attention on the ideal region while reducing focus on irrelevant areas.
- Mechanism: By iteratively refining attention maps and selectively gating sub-attention, LSAS increases the overlap between focused regions and ideal regions as measured by AE.
- Core assumption: AE is a valid proxy for "correct" attention behavior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II] "We propose AE and AE-datasets to quantitatively measure the debiasing ability of DNNs."
  - [section IV] "LSAS improves the AE of SENet by 70.36% and 19.49% on AE-STL10 and AE-ImageNet10 respectively"
  - [corpus] No direct supporting evidence found; this is inferred from the paper's description.
- Break condition: If AE does not correlate well with actual task performance, improvements in AE may not translate to real-world gains.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input feature map → Global Average Pooling → Base attention module → Sub-attention modules (optional) → Selection gate → Final attention map → Element-wise multiplication with input
- Critical path:
  - The attention module and sub-attention layers form the core enhancement; the selection gate controls their application.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More sub-attention orders can improve accuracy but risk overfitting and increased computation; the selection gate mitigates this.
  - High AE scores may not always correlate with better task performance.
- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation if n is too large (over-smoothing).
  - Ineffective bias reduction if the selection gate threshold is poorly tuned.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the order of sub-attention modules (n) affect the attention bias mitigation in different types of attention modules (SENet, CBAM, SRM, ECA, SPANet)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the performance of LSAS-SENet and LSAS-CBAM is best when n is 2 and 1, respectively, and recommends setting n to 1 for a given self-attention module.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how the optimal order of sub-attention modules varies across different types of attention modules or different datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying n across different attention modules and datasets, with detailed analysis of the resulting attention maps and performance metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the relationship between the gate threshold (μ) and the depth of the network in determining the optimal number of blocks with attention modules?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores the performance of ResNet164 when μ is 0, 64, 128, and 256, and recommends setting μ to 128 based on the results.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate how the optimal μ changes with different network depths or architectures, nor does it provide a theoretical explanation for the observed relationship between μ and performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying μ across different network depths and architectures, along with theoretical analysis of the impact of μ on information flow and feature extraction.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does LSAS perform on tasks beyond image classification, such as object detection, semantic segmentation, or video analysis?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on image classification tasks and mentions that the feature extraction ability of DNNs is crucial for computer vision tasks in general.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not evaluate LSAS on other computer vision tasks, and it is unclear whether the attention bias problem and the effectiveness of LSAS generalize to these tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying LSAS to various computer vision tasks, with comparisons to state-of-the-art methods and analysis of the attention maps in the context of these tasks.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of LSAS is primarily validated on image classification datasets; its impact on other vision tasks is unknown.
- The selection gate and sub-attention order settings are heuristic; no theoretical guidance is provided for choosing these hyperparameters.
- AE improvement does not guarantee better downstream task performance; correlation with real-world gains is not established.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The reported improvements in AE scores and accuracy gains on benchmark datasets are well-supported by the experimental results presented.
- Medium Confidence: The effectiveness of the selection gate in controlling parameter growth is plausible based on the described mechanism, but the specific threshold settings and their impact require further validation.
- Low Confidence: The claim that AE directly correlates with real-world task performance is not thoroughly validated; the paper focuses on AE improvement without extensive downstream task performance analysis.
