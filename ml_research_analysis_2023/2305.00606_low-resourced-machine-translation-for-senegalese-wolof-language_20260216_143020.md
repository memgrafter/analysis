# Low-Resourced Machine Translation for Senegalese Wolof Language

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00606
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00606
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Subword tokenization improves BLEU scores by 1.6 points for French-Wolof and 4 points for French-English translations

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the scarcity of NLP resources for the Wolof language by creating the largest Wolof-French parallel corpus of 123,000 sentences. The authors evaluate RNN-based neural machine translation models with subword tokenization, comparing translation performance between French-Wolof and French-English language pairs. The study demonstrates that subword tokenization significantly improves translation quality for both language pairs, with French-English models consistently outperforming French-Wolof models by approximately 3.5 BLEU points. These findings highlight both the effectiveness of subword tokenization in low-resource settings and the impact of linguistic similarity on machine translation performance.

## Method Summary
The authors constructed a 123,000-sentence Wolof-French parallel corpus and employed RNN-based neural machine translation models with subword tokenization. The methodology involved training separate models for French-Wolof and French-English translation tasks under identical conditions. Subword tokenization was implemented to handle the morphological complexity of Wolof and improve translation accuracy. BLEU scores were used as the primary evaluation metric to compare model performance across language pairs, with specific focus on quantifying the impact of subword tokenization on translation quality.

## Key Results
- Subword tokenization improved BLEU scores by approximately 1.6 points for French-Wolof translations
- French-English models outperformed French-Wolof models by about 3.5 BLEU points across all configurations
- Subword tokenization yielded 4-point BLEU improvements for French-English translations compared to baseline approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Subword tokenization addresses the challenge of low-resource machine translation by breaking words into smaller, more frequent units that capture morphological patterns and reduce vocabulary size. This approach is particularly effective for morphologically rich languages like Wolof, where word forms can vary significantly based on grammatical features. By representing text at the subword level, the model can better generalize across different word forms and handle out-of-vocabulary terms more effectively. The consistent performance gap between French-Wolof and French-English models reflects the inherent linguistic distance between Wolof and French compared to English and French, with closer linguistic relationships enabling more accurate translations.

- Limited model architecture diversity (only RNN-based NMT tested)
- No statistical significance testing reported for BLEU score differences
- Unclear whether corpus captures both spoken and written Wolof varieties
