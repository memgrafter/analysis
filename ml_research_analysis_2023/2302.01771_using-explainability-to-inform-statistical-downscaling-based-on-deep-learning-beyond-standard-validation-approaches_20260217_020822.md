# Using Explainability to Inform Statistical Downscaling Based on Deep Learning Beyond Standard Validation Approaches

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01771
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01771
- Reference count: 9
- Key outcome: XAI techniques reveal structural weaknesses in complex CNN downscaling models that standard metrics miss, particularly for climate change projections

## Executive Summary
This study demonstrates how eXplainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques can enhance the evaluation of deep learning models for statistical downscaling of temperature. The authors compare three convolutional neural network architectures of increasing complexity using both standard validation metrics and novel XAI-based metrics. While all models perform similarly under standard cross-validation, XAI analysis reveals critical structural differences that become apparent when projecting future climate conditions. The findings show that more complex models learn non-local relationships that lead to implausible temperature projections under climate change scenarios, while simpler architectures maintain more physically interpretable local relationships.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three CNN architectures (CNN-UNET, CNN-DeepESD, and CNN-PAN) for temperature downscaling in the CORDEX North America domain. Models are trained and validated using reanalysis data, then tested on both reanalysis and GCM projection scenarios. XAI techniques including Accumulated Saliency Maps and Saliency Dispersion Maps are used to analyze the spatial relationships between predictors and predictands. The analysis examines model performance across four CORDEX regions and compares results under historical and future climate conditions.

## Key Results
- All three CNN models perform similarly under standard cross-validation with reanalysis data
- CNN-UNET maintains localized predictor-predictand relationships across all regions
- CNN-DeepESD and CNN-PAN learn spurious non-local relationships in southern regions (particularly NCA)
- XAI metrics reveal structural problems not detected by standard evaluation methods
- Complex models produce implausible temperature projections under climate change scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The XAI techniques effectively reveal the internal decision-making processes of deep learning models by mapping the importance of different input regions to the final prediction. Accumulated Saliency Maps aggregate these importance measures across many predictions, showing which geographic areas most influence the model's outputs. Saliency Dispersion Maps measure the spatial spread of these important regions, distinguishing between local and non-local relationships. These metrics expose how different model architectures learn and generalize predictor-predictand relationships differently.

## Limitations
- Focus on temperature downscaling limits generalizability to other climate variables
- Analysis confined to CORDEX North America domain, limiting geographical applicability
- Causal mechanism between XAI patterns and future projection quality requires further validation

## Confidence
High confidence: Identification of structural differences between CNN-UNET and complex models in terms of local versus non-local learning patterns
Medium confidence: Interpretation that non-local relationships represent spurious connections rather than physical processes
Medium confidence: Assertion that structural differences directly cause implausible temperature projections under climate change
