# LIMA: Less Is More for Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11206
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11206
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- One-line primary result: LIMA achieves competitive performance with GPT-4 using only 1,000 curated examples for fine-tuning

## Executive Summary
LIMA demonstrates that remarkably strong performance can be achieved by fine-tuning a 65B parameter language model on only 1,000 carefully curated examples. The model achieves state-of-the-art results without reinforcement learning or human preference modeling, suggesting that most knowledge in large language models is learned during pretraining. In human evaluations, LIMA's responses are preferred to DaVinci003 in 65% of cases and to GPT-4 in 43% of cases, indicating that limited instruction tuning is sufficient to teach high-quality output.

## Method Summary
LIMA fine-tunes a pretrained LLaMa 65B model using supervised learning with cross-entropy loss on 1,000 curated examples. The training data includes diverse sources like Stack Exchange, wikiHow, Reddit, and manually authored examples. The fine-tuning process uses AdamW optimizer with specific hyperparameters including a learning rate of 1e-5, weight decay of 0.1, and a unique dropout scheduling strategy where dropout starts at 0.0 in the bottom layer and linearly increases to 0.3 at the top layer. The model is evaluated using nucleus sampling with p=0.9 and temperature 0.7.

## Key Results
- LIMA achieves competitive performance to GPT-4 and Claude with only 1,000 examples
- Human evaluators preferred LIMA to DaVinci003 in 65% of cases and to GPT-4 in 43% of cases
- The model successfully generalizes to unseen tasks and follows specific response formats
- Adding conversations substantially improves generation quality for multi-turn interactions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Alignment is primarily about teaching style/format rather than adding new knowledge
- Mechanism: The model already contains most knowledge during pretraining; alignment teaches when to use specific formats
- Core assumption: Knowledge learned during pretraining dominates over what can be learned during fine-tuning

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High-quality, diverse prompts are more important than quantity for alignment
- Mechanism: Quality and diversity in training examples better teach the model the appropriate format/style for different tasks
- Core assumption: The model can generalize style from diverse examples rather than needing many similar examples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5] "scaling up input diversity and output quality have measurable positive effects, while scaling up quantity alone might not"
  - [section 5] "Figure 5 shows that the more diverse Stack Exchange data yields significantly higher performance"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pretrained models can generalize zero-shot capabilities to new tasks

- Core assumption: Pretraining provides broad capabilities that alignment only needs to expose appropriately

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the precise relationship between validation perplexity and generation quality in the LIMA model?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions that perplexity does not correlate with generation quality, but does not provide a detailed analysis of their relationship or explain the underlying reasons for this anticorrelation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments measuring validation perplexity and generation quality at various stages of the fine-tuning process, along with an analysis of the factors contributing to the observed anticorrelation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the inclusion of dialogue examples impact the model's ability to engage in multi-turn conversations?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that adding 30 dialogue examples improves the model's performance in multi-turn conversations, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the specific aspects of dialogue that benefit from this additional training data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments comparing the model's performance on various aspects of dialogue (e.g., coherence, context-awareness, topic management) with and without the inclusion of dialogue examples.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between prompt diversity and response quality in the training data for achieving high-quality alignment?
- Basis in paper: Inferred
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that scaling up input diversity and output quality has positive effects on alignment, but it does not provide a detailed analysis of the trade-offs between these two factors or the optimal balance between them.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments varying the levels of prompt diversity and response quality in the training data and measuring their impact on the model's alignment performance, along with an analysis of the trade-offs between these factors.

## Limitations

- The human evaluation methodology used only a single rater per prompt, which is insufficient for establishing statistically robust preferences
- The claim that "almost all knowledge in large language models is learned during pretraining" is not directly tested through systematic ablation studies
- The paper does not conduct controlled experiments to determine what knowledge was actually added during the 1,000-example fine-tuning versus what was already present in the pretrained model

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The technical implementation details of LIMA's fine-tuning process (dropout scheduling, learning rate, data preprocessing) are well-documented and reproducible. The observation that high-quality, diverse prompts matter more than quantity is supported by the ablation studies showing performance differences between filtered and unfiltered Stack Exchange data.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that LIMA achieves "remarkable" performance relative to much larger models like GPT-4 is supported by human evaluation, but the evaluation methodology limitations reduce confidence in the absolute performance claims. The assertion that scaling up quantity alone might not improve performance is supported by qualitative observations but lacks rigorous quantitative validation across multiple model sizes.

**Low Confidence**: The broad claim that "almost all knowledge in large language models is learned during pretraining" goes beyond what the experimental evidence can support. The paper demonstrates that limited fine-tuning can achieve good format compliance and style consistency, but this does not prove that minimal data is sufficient for learning new knowledge versus merely accessing existing knowledge in appropriate contexts.

## Next Checks

1. **Statistical Robustness of Human Evaluation**: Re-run the human preference study with multiple raters per prompt (minimum 3-5) and calculate inter-rater reliability scores. This would establish whether the reported preference percentages are statistically significant and not subject to individual rater bias.

2. **Knowledge Acquisition vs. Knowledge Access**: Design a controlled experiment where LIMA is evaluated on tasks requiring factual knowledge that could not plausibly be in the original pretraining data (e.g., knowledge about events after the pretraining cutoff). Compare performance against models with different amounts of fine-tuning to determine whether LIMA is truly learning new knowledge or merely accessing existing knowledge more effectively.

3. **Scaling Law Validation**: Systematically vary the number of fine-tuning examples (e.g., 100, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000) and measure performance on a standardized benchmark. This would directly test the claim about the relationship between data quantity and performance, moving beyond qualitative assertions to establish whether there is indeed a threshold effect or whether performance continues to improve with more data.
