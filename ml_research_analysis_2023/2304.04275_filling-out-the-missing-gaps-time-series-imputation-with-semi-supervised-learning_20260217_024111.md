# Filling out the missing gaps: Time Series Imputation with Semi-Supervised Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04275
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04275
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: ST-Impute outperforms existing methods, improving imputation accuracy by 2%-9% and downstream task performance

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of missing data in time series, which significantly impacts time series analysis and downstream tasks like classification and regression. The authors propose ST-Impute, a semi-supervised imputation method that leverages both unlabeled data and labeled data from downstream tasks. ST-Impute is built on sparse self-attention and trains on tasks that mimic the imputation process. The method demonstrates superior performance over existing supervised and unsupervised time series imputation methods, both in terms of imputation quality and downstream task performance.

## Method Summary
ST-Impute is a semi-supervised imputation method for time series data that combines the strengths of both supervised and unsupervised approaches. It uses sparse self-attention to capture temporal dependencies efficiently, even with large time series. The model is trained on tasks that mimic the imputation process, allowing it to learn to fill in missing values effectively. By leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data, ST-Impute can improve its performance on downstream tasks such as classification and regression, even when the amount of labeled data is limited.

## Key Results
- ST-Impute outperforms existing supervised and unsupervised time series imputation methods on imputation quality
- Improves imputation accuracy by 2%-9% over the most competitive baselines
- Improves downstream task performance like classification and regression

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ST-Impute works by leveraging the strengths of semi-supervised learning and sparse self-attention. The semi-supervised approach allows the model to learn from both labeled and unlabeled data, improving its ability to generalize to new time series. The sparse self-attention mechanism efficiently captures long-range temporal dependencies, which is crucial for accurate imputation. By training on tasks that mimic the imputation process, ST-Impute learns to effectively fill in missing values while preserving the underlying patterns in the time series data.

## Limitations
- Evaluation primarily conducted on synthetic datasets with controlled missing patterns, which may not fully capture real-world complexity
- Limited diversity in real-world datasets used, raising questions about generalizability across different domains
- Confidence in real-world applicability and generalizability is medium due to the limited scope of real-world datasets

## Confidence
High for the specific experimental setup described, particularly on synthetic data where ST-Impute shows consistent improvements over baselines. Medium for real-world applicability and generalizability due to the limited scope of real-world datasets used and potential differences between synthetic and actual data patterns.
