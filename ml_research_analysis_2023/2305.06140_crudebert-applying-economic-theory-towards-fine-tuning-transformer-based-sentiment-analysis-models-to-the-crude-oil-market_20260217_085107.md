# CrudeBERT: Applying Economic Theory towards fine-tuning Transformer-based Sentiment Analysis Models to the Crude Oil Market

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06140
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06140
- Reference count: 6
- Key outcome: CrudeBERT outperforms FinBERT and baseline models in crude oil sentiment analysis using economic theory-based fine-tuning

## Executive Summary
This paper presents CrudeBERT, a domain-specific sentiment analysis model for crude oil futures that improves upon general financial models like FinBERT. The authors identify key supply and demand events in news headlines (e.g., accidents, drilling, import/export changes) and use economic theory to create a silver-standard training dataset. By fine-tuning FinBERT on this domain-specific data, CrudeBERT achieves a macro F1 score of 0.53 and macro accuracy of 0.53 in a 9-year evaluation using WTI futures prices, outperforming both the original FinBERT (F1: 0.40) and random baseline (F1: 0.40).

## Method Summary
The authors created CrudeBERT by first identifying supply and demand events that impact crude oil markets through economic theory analysis. They then extracted news headlines containing keywords related to these events from the RavenPack repository and assigned polarity labels based on expected price impacts. This silver-standard dataset was used to fine-tune the pre-trained FinBERT model, resulting in CrudeBERT, which incorporates domain-specific knowledge about crude oil market dynamics into the transformer architecture.

## Key Results
- CrudeBERT achieved a macro F1 score of 0.53 and macro accuracy of 0.53, outperforming FinBERT (F1: 0.40) and random baseline (F1: 0.40)
- Improvements were statistically significant (p < 0.05 vs FinBERT and baseline; p < 0.10 vs RavenPack's ESS model)
- The model demonstrated successful domain adaptation from general financial sentiment to crude oil-specific analysis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Domain-specific economic theory labels outperform generic financial sentiment labels for crude oil price prediction.
- Mechanism: By identifying supply/demand events in news headlines and mapping them to expected price impacts via economic theory, the model receives contextually accurate training signals rather than ambiguous general finance sentiment.
- Core assumption: Economic theory correctly predicts price movement direction for each identified event type.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "identifies and classifies events that impact supply and demand in the crude oil markets"
  - [section] "In this context, supply refers to the amount of a product or service that a provider will sell at a given price during a specific period. Demand denotes the amount of a product or service that a buyer is willing to acquire during the same period for a given price."
- Break condition: If identified events do not reliably correlate with actual price movements, or if economic theory fails to predict outcomes for certain event types.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Silver-standard datasets created via keyword-based topic labeling are sufficient for fine-tuning domain-specific sentiment models.
- Mechanism: Querying headlines using topic-specific keywords and assigning polarity labels based on economic theory generates large-scale labeled data without manual annotation.
- Core assumption: Keyword-based topic extraction and polarity assignment via economic theory produces accurate sentiment labels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We then queried the RavenPack repository for headlines containing the identified keywords and assigned them to the corresponding topic."
  - [section] "Classifying the retrieved headlines based on Adam Smith's price theory allows the creation of a domain-specific silver standard"
- Break condition: If keyword-based topic extraction misclassifies headlines, or if economic theory-based polarity assignment is incorrect.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning a pre-trained financial model (FinBERT) on domain-specific silver-standard data yields better crude oil sentiment predictions than either the original model or generic sentiment analysis.
- Mechanism: Adapting FinBERT's general financial knowledge to crude oil-specific supply/demand contexts improves its ability to recognize event-driven price movements.
- Core assumption: Pre-trained financial knowledge provides a useful starting point that can be effectively adapted to a specific commodity market.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "draws upon these events to contextualize and fine-tune FinBERT, thereby yielding improved sentiment classifications"
  - [section] "we extended FinBERT to CrudeBERT to consider the economic law of supply and demand in the model's assessment."
- Break condition: If the pre-trained model's general financial knowledge interferes with domain-specific adaptation, or if the silver-standard data is too noisy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the long-term prediction performance of CrudeBERT beyond one-day forecasts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that future research is required to investigate CrudeBERT's suitability for long-term strategies and different economic environments.
- Why unresolved: The current evaluation only covers short-term (next-day) price movements. The model's performance over extended periods and during various market conditions remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies comparing CrudeBERT's predictions against actual crude oil prices over months or years, including analyses across different economic cycles (boom vs. recession periods).

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does CrudeBERT handle news headlines containing mixed or contradictory information about supply and demand?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that the current silver standard generation process does not contain additional logic for handling headlines with contradictory information.
- Why unresolved: The methodology for dealing with complex headlines that contain conflicting signals (e.g., "Ivory Coast Jan Crude Oil Exports -1% On Yr, Imports -3%") is not yet developed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and testing of strategies for identifying and processing mixed-signal news headlines, followed by evaluation of how these strategies impact prediction accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the domain adaptation method used for CrudeBERT be effectively extended to other commodities like coffee beans, natural gas, or gold?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors express intent to assess the feasibility of extending the method to other commodities where pricing may be influenced by similar factors.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates success with crude oil, the applicability to other commodities with different market dynamics remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of commodity-specific affective models using the same methodology, with comparative analysis of prediction performance across different commodities.

## Limitations
- Economic theory assumptions: The approach relies on Adam Smith's price theory to assign polarity labels, assuming these relationships hold consistently across all identified events
- Silver-standard data quality: Keyword-based topic extraction introduces potential noise and the absence of gold-standard validation limits confidence in data accuracy
- Model comparison scope: Evaluation focuses primarily on F1 and accuracy metrics without comparing against other domain-specific sentiment models

## Confidence
**High confidence**: The methodology for creating a domain-specific sentiment model through fine-tuning is sound, with statistically significant improvement over FinBERT and random baseline.

**Medium confidence**: The improvement over RavenPack's ESS model is marginal (p < 0.10), suggesting some benefit but with less certainty, and the overall macro F1 score of 0.53 indicates room for improvement.

**Low confidence**: Without gold-standard validation of the silver-standard dataset or assessment of economic theory prediction accuracy for individual event types, the fundamental assumption that the approach produces accurate sentiment labels remains unverified.

## Next Checks
1. **Gold-standard validation**: Manually label a random sample of headlines from the silver-standard dataset and compare against the economic theory-based labels to quantify accuracy and identify systematic errors.

2. **Event-specific economic theory validation**: For each identified supply/demand event type, test whether the economic theory-based polarity predictions align with actual historical price movements using Granger causality or similar statistical tests.

3. **Robustness testing across oil benchmarks**: Evaluate CrudeBERT's performance on Brent crude futures and other oil-related commodities to assess whether the model generalizes beyond WTI futures or is overfit to a specific market.
