# Deep Learning Methods for Extracting Metaphorical Names of Flowers and Plants

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10833
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10833
- Reference count: 3
- Best model (multilingual BERT) achieved 92.2% F1 in English

## Executive Summary
This study introduces a transformer-based deep learning approach for identifying metaphor-based flower and plant names in specialized botanical discourse. The authors fine-tuned thirteen discriminative transformer models and ChatGPT on annotated English and Spanish datasets, achieving strong performance metrics. The best-performing model, multilingual BERT, achieved an F1 score of 92.2% in English. The work includes the release of new annotated datasets and code to support further research in this domain.

## Method Summary
The authors developed a transformer-based approach for metaphor identification, fine-tuning thirteen different discriminative models and ChatGPT on specialized botanical datasets. The methodology involved creating annotated corpora in both English and Spanish, then training and evaluating multiple transformer architectures to identify metaphorical flower and plant names. The approach leverages transfer learning from pretrained language models, adapting them to the specific task of metaphorical name extraction in botanical contexts.

## Key Results
- Multilingual BERT achieved the highest performance with 92.2% F1 score in English
- The approach successfully identified metaphor-based flower and plant names in specialized botanical discourse
- New annotated English and Spanish datasets were released alongside the code

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The transformer-based approach works effectively because it leverages pretrained language models' understanding of context and semantics, which is crucial for distinguishing metaphorical from literal usage. The fine-tuning process allows the models to adapt to the specific linguistic patterns and domain-specific terminology found in botanical discourse. The multilingual approach demonstrates the models' ability to generalize across languages while maintaining high performance.
