# Disentangling Neuron Representations with Concept Vectors

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09707
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09707
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Polysemantic neurons in Inception V3 can be disentangled into monosemantic concept vectors through agglomerative and k-means clustering

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of interpreting polysemantic neurons in deep neural networks by proposing a method to disentangle these neurons into concept vectors that encapsulate distinct features. The method uses agglomerative clustering and k-means clustering to group similar activations and calculate concept vectors, allowing users to control the level of concept separation. Applied to a pre-trained Inception V3 model, the approach demonstrates that polysemantic neurons can be effectively decomposed into monosemantic directions that encode coherent, human-understandable features.

## Method Summary
The method takes a pre-trained CNN, selects a neuron, pools activations from top images, applies agglomerative clustering with distance threshold, then k-means clustering, and finally computes normalized mean vectors as concept vectors. Inputs include the dataset, model, neuron index, and threshold parameter. The outputs are concept vectors and cluster memberships. The approach focuses on discovering interpretable feature directions in activation space rather than individual neurons, enabling fine-grained concept discovery through threshold tuning.

## Key Results
- Polysemantic neurons can be successfully disentangled into monosemantic concept vectors using hierarchical and k-means clustering
- Concept vectors show higher similarity to their associated images than the original polysemantic neuron direction
- User evaluations confirm that the identified concept vectors encode coherent, human-understandable features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Polysemantic neurons can be decomposed into monosemantic concept vectors by clustering their top-activating examples in activation space.
- Mechanism: The method uses agglomerative clustering to group similar activation patterns, then k-means clustering to form clean clusters, and finally computes the mean activation vector of each cluster to form a concept vector.
- Core assumption: Similar activation patterns in the top-activating examples correspond to coherent, semantically distinct features.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The main contribution of this paper is a method to disentangle polysemantic neurons into concept vectors encapsulating distinct features." [section] "We use agglomerative clustering... to group these measurements of similarity into sets of close examples... The final step finds the directions of the ˆC concept vectors... by taking the mean of the remaining embeddings for each cluster."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Concept vectors encode higher similarity to their associated images than the original polysemantic neuron direction.
- Mechanism: By computing projections of activation patterns onto the concept vectors and comparing cosine similarities, the method shows that concept vectors align better with the semantic clusters than the original neuron direction.
- Core assumption: The mean of clustered activations forms a direction that better represents the semantic feature than the neuron's individual response.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our evaluations show that the concept vectors found encode coherent, human-understandable features." [section] "our analysis confirmed that the projections and cosine similarities of their corresponding activations with the concept vector are remarkably higher than that with the neuron direction."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The user can control the granularity of concept separation by tuning the distance threshold parameter.
- Mechanism: By adjusting dmax in agglomerative clustering, the number of clusters (and thus concept vectors) can be increased or decreased, allowing fine-grained or coarse concept discovery.
- Core assumption: The distance threshold effectively controls cluster granularity without over-merging distinct features or over-splitting coherent ones.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Our method can search for fine-grained concepts according to the user's desired level of concept separation." [section] "The distance threshold is a hyperparameter that is tuned to an appropriate range for the model layer. It can be tweaked to fine-grain concepts into big or small buckets."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dataset -> Inception V3 model -> Mixed 7b layer activations -> Top N images selection -> Pairwise Euclidean distances -> Agglomerative clustering -> K-means clustering -> Outlier removal -> Normalized mean vectors (concept vectors)
- Critical path: (1) Select neuron → (2) Get top N activations → (3) Compute pairwise Euclidean distances → (4) Agglomerative clustering → (5) K-means clustering + outlier removal → (6) Compute and normalize concept vectors
- Design tradeoffs: Hierarchical clustering avoids pre-specifying cluster count but is slower; k-means centroids simplify outlier removal but require re-clustering; using Euclidean distance assumes isotropic feature spread; small N risks instability, large N increases noise
- Failure signatures: (1) Too few or many clusters → adjust dmax; (2) Concept vectors still polysemantic → increase N or refine distance metric; (3) Clusters too small → raise outlier removal threshold; (4) Computationally slow → subsample dataset or use approximate nearest neighbors
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run the full pipeline on a known polysemantic neuron (e.g., neuron 35) with dmax=15, N=100; verify two concept vectors for apples and sports.
  2. Vary dmax across [10,15,20] on the same neuron; observe how cluster count and concept granularity change.
  3. Compare cosine similarities of top images to neuron direction vs. concept vectors; confirm higher alignment for concepts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of distance threshold parameter dmax affect the granularity and interpretability of the discovered concept vectors?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the distance threshold can be tweaked to fine-grain concepts into big or small buckets, and demonstrates this with neuron 1.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides a qualitative demonstration, it lacks a systematic study of how varying dmax impacts the number, quality, and interpretability of concept vectors across different neurons and layers.
- What evidence would resolve it: A quantitative analysis showing the distribution of concept vector numbers and their semantic coherence for different dmax values across multiple neurons and layers would provide insights into the optimal range for dmax.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the proposed method be effectively generalized to other types of data, such as language or tabular data, beyond image data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges the limitation of relying on image data and expresses the intention to pursue generalization to other data types in future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on image data and does not explore the applicability of the method to other data modalities, leaving the generalizability an open question.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the method to language models or tabular datasets and evaluating the coherence and interpretability of the discovered concept vectors would demonstrate its effectiveness in other domains.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed method compare to existing concept discovery techniques in terms of the quality and interpretability of the discovered concept vectors?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that none of the existing methods seek to disentangle polysemantic representations, suggesting a potential advantage of the proposed method.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a direct comparison with other concept discovery methods, making it difficult to assess its relative performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a comparative study with existing concept discovery techniques, evaluating the coherence, understandability, and human interpretability of the discovered concept vectors, would provide insights into the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method.

## Limitations
- The method relies on image data and has not been tested on other modalities like language or tabular data
- Optimal distance threshold tuning lacks a systematic approach and is currently heuristic
- No comparison with existing concept discovery techniques to establish relative performance

## Confidence

Our analysis has **High confidence** in the core mechanism that agglomerative clustering can group similar activation patterns and that k-means clustering with outlier removal can produce cleaner clusters. The empirical evidence from the paper showing improved cosine similarities and user evaluations supports these claims.

However, we have **Medium confidence** in the scalability and robustness of the approach across different model architectures and datasets. The paper focuses on a single Inception V3 model and ImageNet, with no ablation studies on varying dataset sizes or model types.

We have **Low confidence** in the automatic determination of optimal distance thresholds. The paper mentions tuning but doesn't provide a systematic method for threshold selection, which could significantly impact the quality of disentanglement.

## Next Checks

1. **Threshold sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary dmax across a wide range (e.g., 5-50) on multiple polysemantic neurons and measure how concept vector quality (cosine similarity, cluster purity) changes.

2. **Cross-architecture validation**: Apply the same disentanglement pipeline to ResNet and Vision Transformer architectures to test generalizability beyond Inception V3.

3. **Outlier robustness test**: Intentionally add noisy or adversarial examples to the top-N activating images and measure how this affects cluster stability and concept vector coherence.
