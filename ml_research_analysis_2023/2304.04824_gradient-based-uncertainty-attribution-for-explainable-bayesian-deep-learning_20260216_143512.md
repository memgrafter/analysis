# Gradient-based Uncertainty Attribution for Explainable Bayesian Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04824
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04824
- Reference count: 40
- The paper proposes a gradient-based uncertainty attribution method that achieves competitive accuracy and high efficiency for explainable Bayesian deep learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the need for explainable uncertainty quantification in Bayesian deep learning models by proposing UA-Backprop, a gradient-based uncertainty attribution method. UA-Backprop efficiently generates uncertainty maps by backpropagating uncertainty scores to pixel-wise attributions without requiring generative models or additional optimization. The method satisfies the completeness property, ensuring full attribution of uncertainty to individual pixels. Additionally, the authors introduce an uncertainty mitigation strategy that leverages attribution results as attention to improve model performance, demonstrating 6% accuracy improvement over vanilla training.

## Method Summary
The method implements UA-Backprop, a gradient-based uncertainty attribution approach that backpropagates uncertainty scores through Bayesian deep learning models to generate pixel-wise attribution maps. The approach uses FullGrad for the z→x path and deep ensemble methods for uncertainty quantification. The attention mechanism module applies a mask (1-M(x))⊙M(x) to feature maps during retraining, strengthening informative areas while suppressing problematic regions. The method operates efficiently with a single backward pass and requires tuning of hyperparameters τ1 and τ2 for optimal performance across different datasets.

## Key Results
- UA-Backprop achieves Maximum Uncertainty Reduction Rate (MURR) of 0.648 and 0.850 for 2% and 5% blurring on MNIST dataset
- The uncertainty mitigation approach improves model accuracy by 6% compared to vanilla training
- Method demonstrates competitive accuracy and efficiency compared to existing approaches while avoiding assumptions about generative models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Uncertainty attribution decomposes uncertainty into pixel-level contributions satisfying completeness.
- Mechanism: The method backpropagates uncertainty scores through softmax probabilities, logits, and input layers, ensuring each pixel's contribution sums to the total uncertainty.
- Core assumption: The softmax gradients properly distribute uncertainty attribution from probabilities to logits without vanishing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The uncertainty is fully attributed to satisfy the completeness property, i.e., the uncertainty can be decomposed into the sum of individual pixel attributions."
  - [section 3.5]: "Our proposed method satisfies the completeness property, shown in the following equation: U = sum of individual attributions"
  - [corpus]: Weak - neighbors discuss attribution but not specifically completeness in uncertainty context.
- Break condition: If softmax gradients become uniform due to temperature parameter τ1 being too high, attribution distribution becomes meaningless.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Attention mechanism using uncertainty attribution maps improves model performance by filtering problematic regions.
- Mechanism: The attention weight A(x) = (1 - M(x)) ⊙ M(x) strengthens informative areas while suppressing problematic ones, applied to latent feature maps.
- Core assumption: The attribution map M(x) accurately identifies truly problematic regions that harm predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "A(x) = (1 - M(x)) ⊙ M(x)... Through retraining using the masked feature maps, the model gains improved accuracy and robustness."
  - [section 5.2.2]: "Our method demonstrates a 6% improvement in ACC compared to vanilla training"
  - [corpus]: Weak - neighbors discuss attribution but not specifically attention-based mitigation.
- Break condition: If M(x) incorrectly identifies background as problematic, attention mechanism suppresses useful information.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Gradient-based approach avoids the inefficiency of generative model optimization.
- Mechanism: UA-Backprop generates attribution maps through single backward pass without requiring generative model training or per-image optimization.
- Core assumption: Backpropagation through Bayesian model parameters can effectively capture uncertainty sources without generative modeling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "without requiring a pre-trained generative model or additional optimization"
  - [section 3.1]: "The explanations can be generated efficiently within a single backward pass of the BDL model"
  - [section 5.2.1]: Compares efficiency against CLUE-based methods requiring optimization
- Break condition: If Bayesian posterior sampling introduces too much variance, single backward pass may not capture stable attributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of UA-Backprop change when applied to deterministic neural networks without uncertainty quantification, as opposed to Bayesian deep learning models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that UA-Backprop can be applied to Ensemble-1 where the uncertainty is calculated by entropy, but the results are not good due to inadequate uncertainty quantification. It also suggests that more advanced single-network UQ methods like Laplacian approximation could improve results.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical results or a thorough analysis of UA-Backprop's performance on deterministic models compared to BDL models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing UA-Backprop's attribution performance and efficiency on deterministic NNs versus BDL models, including quantitative metrics and qualitative visualizations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is the UA-Backprop method to the choice of hyperparameters τ1 and τ2, and how does this sensitivity vary across different datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that τ1 and τ2 are crucial hyperparameters and presents a sensitivity analysis in Appendix D.3, showing that performance varies slightly with different hyperparameter values within certain ranges.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a systematic study of hyperparameter sensitivity across a wide range of datasets or explore the impact of these hyperparameters on the method's generalizability.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying τ1 and τ2 across multiple datasets, analyzing the impact on attribution performance, and identifying optimal hyperparameter ranges for different types of data.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the uncertainty attribution performance of UA-Backprop compare to other attribution methods when the input data is corrupted or contains adversarial perturbations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on identifying problematic regions contributing to prediction uncertainty, which is relevant for detecting adversarial attacks or corrupted data. However, it does not explicitly test UA-Backprop's performance in these scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The paper's evaluation is primarily on clean benchmark datasets, and there is no investigation of UA-Backprop's robustness to adversarial examples or corrupted inputs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating UA-Backprop's attribution accuracy on datasets with injected adversarial perturbations or various types of input corruption, comparing the results to other attribution methods.
