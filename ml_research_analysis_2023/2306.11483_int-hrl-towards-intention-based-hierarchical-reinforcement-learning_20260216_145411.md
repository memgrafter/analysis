# Int-HRL: Towards Intention-based Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11483
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11483
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Intention-based hierarchical RL using gaze data is 3x more sample efficient than baselines on Montezuma's Revenge

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of sample efficiency in deep reinforcement learning for complex, long-horizon tasks like Montezuma's Revenge. The authors propose Int-HRL, a hierarchical reinforcement learning method that automatically extracts sub-goals from human eye gaze data, eliminating the need for manual expert annotations. By predicting human intentions from gaze patterns using a Support Vector Machine and then extracting sub-goals based on these predictions, Int-HRL achieves significantly improved sample efficiency, solving the first room in only 625K steps compared to 2.3M steps required by baseline methods.

## Method Summary
Int-HRL is a hierarchical reinforcement learning framework that uses human eye gaze data to automatically extract sub-goals for complex tasks. The method involves collecting gaze data from human players, predicting their intentions using a Support Vector Machine classifier trained on gaze features (total duration of looks, most recently looked at, number of glances, duration of first glance), and then extracting sub-goals based on these predictions. The hierarchical structure consists of a meta-controller that selects sub-goals and a low-level policy that executes actions to achieve them. The approach is evaluated on Montezuma's Revenge, demonstrating three times better sample efficiency compared to previous methods.

## Key Results
- Int-HRL solves the first room of Montezuma's Revenge in 625K steps, three times faster than baseline methods requiring 2.3M steps
- The intention prediction model achieves 75% average accuracy in predicting human intentions from gaze patterns
- Automatically extracted sub-goals lead to more sample-efficient learning compared to hand-crafted sub-goals

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Gaze locations predict human intentions, which can serve as sub-goals for HRL agents.
- Mechanism: Eye gaze fixations reveal where humans direct attention during gameplay, which correlates with their goal-directed behavior. By extracting visual saliency maps from eye gaze data, areas of interest emerge that align with meaningful sub-goals in the game.
- Core assumption: Human eye gaze is a reliable indicator of intention and goal-directed behavior in complex tasks.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "intentions of human players, i.e. the precursor of goal-oriented decisions, can be robustly predicted from eye gaze"; [section] "intention prediction model achieves an average accuracy of 75%, demonstrating the relation between intention and gaze behaviour"
- Break condition: If gaze data does not reliably indicate intentions in other tasks, the sub-goal extraction pipeline would fail to identify meaningful sub-goals.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Automatically extracted sub-goals improve sample efficiency compared to hand-crafted sub-goals.
- Mechanism: By using human gaze data to identify sub-goals, the HRL agent learns from more natural and relevant task decompositions, reducing the need for extensive exploration. The proposed sub-goals align better with the actual decision-making process of human players.
- Core assumption: Sub-goals extracted from human gaze are more relevant and informative for the HRL agent than hand-crafted sub-goals.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "replacing hand-crafted sub-goals with automatically extracted intentions leads to a HRL agent that is significantly more sample efficient than previous methods"; [section] "Int-HRL is three times more sample efficient than previous methods, solving the first room in only 625K steps compared to 2.3M steps required by baseline methods"
- Break condition: If the automatically extracted sub-goals do not capture the essential decision points in the task, the HRL agent may not benefit from improved sample efficiency.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The hierarchical structure of HRL, combined with intention-based sub-goals, allows for better exploration and learning.
- Mechanism: The HRL agent learns a meta-controller policy that selects sub-goals based on the current state, and a low-level policy that executes actions to achieve the selected sub-goal. By using intention-based sub-goals, the agent receives more informative rewards and can explore more efficiently.
- Core assumption: The hierarchical structure of HRL, with intention-based sub-goals, provides a more effective exploration strategy than flat RL approaches.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Recent hierarchical RL methods have increased sample efficiency by incorporating information inherent to the structure of the decision problem"; [section] "Le et al. combined imitation learning (IL) with HRL showing that their hierarchical guidance model (hg-DAgger/Q) significantly reduces expert effort compared to other interactive IL approaches"
- Break condition: If the hierarchical structure does not provide meaningful temporal abstraction or if the intention-based sub-goals are not informative, the HRL agent may not benefit from improved exploration and learning.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Eye Gaze Data Collection -> Intention Prediction Model -> Sub-goal Extraction Pipeline -> Hierarchical RL Agent
- Critical path:
  1. Collect eye gaze data from human players during gameplay
  2. Train the intention prediction model on the gaze data to predict human intentions
  3. Extract sub-goals from the predicted intentions using the sub-goal extraction pipeline
  4. Integrate the extracted sub-goals into the HRL agent's architecture
  5. Train the HRL agent on the game using the intention-based sub-goals
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using eye gaze data for sub-goal extraction adds complexity to the system but may lead to more natural and relevant sub-goals
  - The hierarchical RL framework allows for better exploration and learning but requires careful design of the meta-controller and low-level policies
  - The choice of intention prediction model (e.g., SVM) affects the accuracy of the extracted sub-goals
- Failure signatures:
  - If the intention prediction model fails to accurately predict human intentions, the extracted sub-goals may not be meaningful
  - If the sub-goal extraction pipeline does not properly align the extracted areas of interest with human trajectories, the resulting sub-goals may not capture the essential decision points in the task
  - If the HRL agent's meta-controller or low-level policies are not well-designed, the agent may not benefit from the intention-based sub-goals
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the accuracy of the intention prediction model on a held-out test set of gaze data
  2. Analyze the extracted sub-goals qualitatively by visualizing the saliency maps and comparing them to the hand-crafted sub-goals used in previous work
  3. Train the HRL agent using the extracted sub-goals and compare its sample efficiency and performance to baseline methods that use hand-crafted sub-goals

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How well does the proposed Int-HRL method generalize to other long-horizon, sparse-reward Atari games beyond Montezuma's Revenge?
- Can the sub-goal extraction pipeline be improved to handle more dynamic game environments where objects of interest move rapidly across the screen?
- How can the single-agent model be improved to overcome the exploration problem and learn all sub-goals without hierarchical guidance?

## Limitations
- The method relies on eye gaze data availability, which may not be practical for many real-world applications
- The 75% accuracy for intention prediction leaves room for significant error in sub-goal extraction
- The generalizability of the approach to other games beyond Montezuma's Revenge remains unclear

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The sample efficiency improvements (3x faster learning) are well-supported by quantitative comparisons with baseline methods
- **Medium Confidence**: The 75% accuracy for intention prediction validates the gaze-intention relationship, but the practical impact on sub-goal quality could vary
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that automatically extracted sub-goals are inherently more relevant than hand-crafted ones lacks extensive comparative analysis

## Next Checks
