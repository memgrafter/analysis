# Constrained Empirical Risk Minimization: Theory and Practice

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04729
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04729
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Wavelet-constrained neural networks outperform nnUNet in contour prediction tasks with exact constraint satisfaction.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Constrained Empirical Risk Minimization (CERM), a framework for exactly enforcing constraints on neural network parameters during training. Instead of adding penalty terms to the loss, CERM restricts parameters to a submanifold where constraints are satisfied, using the Implicit Function Theorem to construct charts and compute Riemannian metrics for gradient descent. The method is applied to enforce wavelet constraints on convolutional filters, enabling accurate contour prediction in medical images. Experiments show wavelet networks outperform a strong baseline on spleen and prostate datasets, with better robustness and ability to capture fine geometric details.

## Method Summary
CERM uses the Implicit Function Theorem to construct charts on the solution manifold of constraint equations, allowing exact constraint satisfaction throughout training. The framework computes gradients and Riemannian metrics intrinsically on the manifold using Christoffel symbols and geodesic flows. Applied to wavelet-constrained convolutional networks, CERM enforces orthonormality and non-degeneracy conditions on filter coefficients. The hybrid U-Net architecture with wavelet decoder is trained using Riemannian SGD, with task-optimized wavelets differing significantly from initial ones and exhibiting smoother shapes that better capture geometric details in medical images.

## Key Results
- Wavelet networks trained with CERM achieve higher Dice scores than nnUNet baseline on spleen and prostate contour prediction tasks.
- Task-optimized wavelets differ significantly from initial random wavelets, often exhibiting smoother, less noisy shapes.
- CERM provides exact constraint satisfaction compared to soft constraint approaches, with improved robustness to noise.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Encoder: Convolutional blocks with residual connections and pooling
  - Bottleneck: Fully connected layers predicting coefficients at lowest resolution
  - Decoder: Pyramid algorithm reconstructing higher resolution coefficients
  - Skip connections: Predicting detail coefficients at intermediate levels
  - Constrained parameters: Wavelet filters learned on submanifold

- Critical path: Encoder → Bottleneck coefficient prediction → Pyramid algorithm reconstruction → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Higher wavelet order: More expressive but computationally expensive
  - Lower resolution prediction: Faster but less detailed
  - Constrained vs unconstrained training: Exact constraints vs faster optimization
- Failure signatures:
  - Degenerate wavelets: H(ξ) approaches zero, violating non-degeneracy condition
  - Poor reconstruction: Detail coefficients at high levels are inaccurate
  - Training instability: Step sizes on manifold too large for stable descent

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the CERM framework be extended to handle constraints that are not expressible as finite systems of equations, such as integral constraints or inequality constraints?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on constraints formulated as finite systems of equations, but many real-world constraints may not fit this form.
- Why unresolved: The current CERM framework relies heavily on the Implicit Function Theorem and Riemannian geometry, which may not directly extend to more complex constraint types.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal extension of the CERM framework that incorporates more general constraint types, with proofs of existence of solution manifolds and methods for computing gradients and performing SGD.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal way to initialize the wavelet filters in the CERM framework to ensure faster convergence and better final performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that task-optimized wavelets differ significantly from initial ones, and that the initial wavelets were sometimes close to "degenerate".
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that the final wavelets differ from the initial ones, it doesn't explore the impact of different initialization strategies on the training process and final results.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing different initialization methods for wavelet filters, including random initialization, pre-trained wavelets, and wavelets optimized for other tasks, with quantitative comparisons of convergence speed and final performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the CERM framework be applied to other types of neural network architectures beyond convolutional networks, such as recurrent networks or graph neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates CERM on convolutional networks, but the framework is presented in a general way that could potentially be applied to other architectures.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on convolutional networks and wavelets, but doesn't explore how the CERM framework could be adapted to other network types or constraint types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful applications of CERM to other network architectures, such as recurrent networks for sequence modeling or graph neural networks for molecular property prediction, with demonstrations of improved performance or constraint satisfaction compared to standard training methods.

## Limitations
- Scalability to larger networks and higher-dimensional constraint manifolds remains untested.
- Computational overhead of Riemannian gradient computation and impact on training time were not quantified.
- Generalizability to non-wavelet constraints (e.g., equivariance, sparsity) is claimed but not empirically demonstrated.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Theoretical framework using Implicit Function Theorem and Riemannian SGD steps.
- **Medium Confidence**: Wavelet constraint implementation and hybrid U-Net architecture details.
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about CERM's superiority over soft constraints require more extensive validation.
