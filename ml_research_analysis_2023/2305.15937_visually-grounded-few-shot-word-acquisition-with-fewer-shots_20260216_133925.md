# Visually grounded few-shot word acquisition with fewer shots

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15937
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15937
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: MATTNET outperforms existing models on few-shot retrieval and classification with as few as 5 shots

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the challenge of few-shot learning for visually grounded speech models, where the goal is to learn the spoken form of a word and its visual depiction from only a few paired word-image examples. The authors propose MATTNET, a new approach that mines new unsupervised word-image training pairs from large collections of unlabelled speech and images using given word-image example pairs. Additionally, they use a word-to-image attention mechanism to determine word-image similarity. The proposed model achieves better performance with fewer shots than any existing approach on the tasks of few-shot word classification and retrieval.

## Method Summary
The proposed MATTNET model consists of an audio and a vision branch, connected by a word-to-image attention mechanism. The audio branch extracts speech features from a spoken input and encodes them into a single audio embedding. The vision branch encodes an image input into a sequence of embeddings. The attention mechanism calculates attention weights over the pixel embeddings to determine word-image similarity. The model is trained using a contrastive loss. Pair mining is used to artificially increase the size of the support set by mining new word-image pairs from unlabelled speech and image collections using the given word-image example pairs.

## Key Results
- MATTNET outperforms previous models on few-shot retrieval with as few as 5 shots
- Sets competitive baseline for natural visually grounded few-shot word classification with up to 40 classes
- Achieves accuracies higher than 60% with as few as five shots

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Mining new word-image pairs from unlabelled data using QbERT and AlexNet embeddings expands the training set effectively for few-shot learning.
- Mechanism: For each spoken word example in the support set, QbERT compares it to all utterances in an unlabelled speech corpus to find matching segments. Similarly, AlexNet embeddings compare support set images to unlabelled images. These matches are paired to create additional training data.
- Core assumption: The similarity scores from QbERT (for speech) and cosine distance (for images) reliably identify true matches between support set examples and unlabelled data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our approach involves using the given word-image example pairs to mine new unsupervised word-image training pairs from large collections of unlabelled speech and images."
  - [section]: "To mine word-image pairs, we mine an image from the same predicted class as a segmented word. Negative pairs are taken from the positive pairs of other classes."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct comparison of QbERT vs. DTW for this specific task in the cited corpus.
- Break condition: If QbERT or AlexNet similarity scores produce too many false positives/negatives, the mined pairs would introduce noise and degrade model performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The word-to-image attention mechanism in MATTNET learns to align spoken words to relevant image regions, enabling few-shot retrieval/classification.
- Mechanism: MATTNET encodes a spoken word into an audio embedding, encodes an image into a sequence of pixel embeddings, then computes attention weights by taking dot products between the word embedding and each pixel embedding. The max attention score indicates word-image correspondence.
- Core assumption: The attention mechanism can effectively learn which image regions correspond to the spoken word, and that the contrastive loss properly shapes these representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Additionally, we use a word-to-image attention mechanism to determine word-image similarity."
  - [section]: "By taking the maximum over a, we get a similarity score S ∈ [0, 100]. The higher S, the more probable it is that the spoken word corresponds to one or more objects in the image."
  - [corpus]: Weak - while attention mechanisms are common, specific evidence for this architecture's effectiveness on few-shot learning is not provided in the corpus.
- Break condition: If the attention mechanism fails to learn meaningful alignments (e.g., attends to irrelevant image regions), the similarity scores would not correlate with true word-image correspondence.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pretraining MATTNET on background data before few-shot fine-tuning provides a strong initialization that improves few-shot performance.
- Mechanism: The model is first trained on a large dataset of images and speech that do not contain the few-shot classes. This pretraining shapes the audio and vision encoders before they are fine-tuned on the mined pairs from the few-shot classes.
- Core assumption: Features learned from background data transfer well to the few-shot classes, and the model doesn't catastrophically forget these useful features during fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The model is initialised by pretraining it on the background data using the contrastive retrieval loss of [34]."
  - [section]: "To determine the mined pairs' contribution to this performance boost, we do an experiment where we do not update MATTNET on the mined pairs after pretraining it on the background data (not containing any of the few-shot classes)."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence in the corpus about transfer learning effectiveness for this specific architecture and task.
- Break condition: If the background data is too dissimilar from the few-shot classes, or if fine-tuning overwrites useful features, pretraining would not provide a benefit.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multimodal representation learning
  - Why needed here: The model must learn to represent and compare speech and images in a shared space to determine word-image correspondence.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain how speech features and image features are combined in a multimodal model?

- Concept: Attention mechanisms
  - Why needed here: The word-to-image attention allows the model to focus on relevant image regions when processing a spoken word, enabling fine-grained matching.
  - Quick check question: How does the attention weight between a word embedding and a pixel embedding indicate correspondence?

- Concept: Contrastive learning
  - Why needed here: The contrastive loss used during training pulls representations of matching word-image pairs together and pushes non-matching pairs apart, shaping the embedding space for few-shot tasks.
  - Quick check question: What is the role of positive and negative pairs in a contrastive loss function?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Spoken word → facoustic → fBiLSTM → yaudio → attention → S → contrastive loss

- Critical path:
  Spoken word → facoustic → fBiLSTM → yaudio → attention → S → contrastive loss

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using AlexNet instead of ResNet50 for vision branch: Simpler, faster, but potentially less expressive
  - QbERT for speech mining vs. DTW: Higher F1 score (98.7% vs. 76.8%) but potentially more complex
  - Word-to-image attention vs. other fusion methods: Allows fine-grained alignment but may be sensitive to attention weight interpretation

- Failure signatures:
  - Low P@N scores on few-shot retrieval: Could indicate poor mining quality, attention mechanism not learning, or insufficient pretraining
  - Large gap between training and validation loss: Likely overfitting, possibly due to noisy mined pairs or complex model for small dataset
  - Attention weights concentrated on irrelevant image regions: Could indicate poor attention mechanism learning or mismatched input representations

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Train MATTNET without the mining step to quantify its contribution
  2. Scaling study: Vary the number of mined pairs per class to find optimal mining quantity
  3. Attention visualization: Plot attention weights for sample word-image pairs to verify the mechanism is learning meaningful alignments

## Limitations
- The specific QbERT implementation details are not provided, limiting reproducibility
- No comparison to other unsupervised pair mining methods
- Limited evaluation to 40 classes, leaving performance on larger vocabularies uncertain

## Confidence
High confidence in the core claim that MATTNET achieves better few-shot performance through its combination of mining unsupervised pairs and word-to-image attention. Medium confidence in the effectiveness of individual mechanisms due to limited empirical evidence for the QbERT mining approach and attention mechanism on this specific task. Low confidence in transfer learning benefits from pretraining without direct ablation studies.
