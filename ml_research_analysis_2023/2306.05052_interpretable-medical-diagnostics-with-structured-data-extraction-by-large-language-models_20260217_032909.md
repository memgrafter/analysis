# Interpretable Medical Diagnostics with Structured Data Extraction by Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05052
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05052
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TEMED-LLM significantly outperforms text classification models by extracting structured tabular data from unstructured medical reports using LLMs and interpretable ML models.

## Executive Summary
TEMED-LLM is a novel methodology that extracts structured tabular data from unstructured medical reports using large language models and domain-specific reasoning. The extracted data is then used to train interpretable machine learning models for medical diagnostics. Experimental results demonstrate that TEMED-LLM achieves higher accuracy and F1 scores compared to state-of-the-art text classification models across multiple medical datasets, while providing better transparency through interpretable model outputs.

## Method Summary
TEMED-LLM consists of two main modules: RExtract for reasoning and extraction, and VORC for validation and correction. RExtract generates prompts combining domain-specific instructions, reasoning guidelines, and few-shot examples to extract tabular features from medical text. VORC detects and corrects extraction errors through rule-based agents and iterative LLM correction prompts. The resulting structured data is used to train interpretable ML models like logistic regression and decision trees, which achieve comparable performance to complex text classifiers while providing better transparency.

## Key Results
- TEMED-LLM outperforms state-of-the-art text classification models, achieving high accuracy and F1 scores across multiple medical datasets.
- Interpretable tabular models (logistic regression, decision trees) achieve comparable performance to complex text classifiers like SetFit-MPNetV2.
- VORC module significantly improves extraction accuracy, achieving 0.99 accuracy on the Hepatitis dataset.
- The methodology enables transparent medical diagnostics by preserving feature importance and decision-making transparency.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TEMED-LLM outperforms text classification models by extracting tabular data from unstructured medical reports using LLMs and then applying interpretable ML models.
- Mechanism: The methodology leverages LLMs' reasoning capabilities to infer tabular features even when not explicitly mentioned in text. It uses domain-specific reasoning guidelines and a data validation loop to correct errors, producing structured data that interpretable models can process efficiently.
- Core assumption: Domain knowledge embedded in reasoning guidelines enables accurate feature extraction even when feature names are implicit in medical text.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Drawing upon the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, TEMED-LLM goes beyond traditional extraction techniques, accurately inferring tabular features, even when their names are not explicitly mentioned in the text."
  - [section 4.2] "The reasoning component outlines explicit guidance on how the model should reason about the provided example in the instructions."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on medical text processing but don't directly address TEMED-LLM's specific reasoning and validation approach.
- Break condition: If domain-specific reasoning guidelines are inaccurate or incomplete, extraction quality will degrade significantly, causing interpretable models to perform poorly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The VORC module significantly improves extraction accuracy by detecting and correcting errors through rule-based agents and correction prompts.
- Mechanism: VORC implements a feedback loop that first attempts to fix errors with predefined rules. If unsuccessful, it generates correction prompts for the LLM, iteratively improving the extracted data quality.
- Core assumption: Most extraction errors can be corrected through iterative feedback between rule-based agents and LLMs without requiring manual intervention.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "The data validation procedure in TEMED-LLM is essential for ensuring the accuracy and reliability of information extracted from medical texts. We introduce the VORC module, which detects and corrects errors that may occur during the extraction process."
  - [section 5.1] "VORC significantly enhances the accuracy of extraction and the detection of missing values. It shows impressive performance on the Hepatitis dataset (acc. 0.99)."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors don't specifically address iterative error correction mechanisms in medical text extraction.
- Break condition: If error patterns are too complex for rule-based agents and LLM correction prompts to handle, the VORC loop will fail to converge to accurate data.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Interpretable tabular models trained on extracted data achieve comparable performance to complex text classifiers while providing better transparency.
- Mechanism: By converting unstructured medical text to structured tabular format, TEMED-LLM enables the use of simpler, interpretable models like logistic regression and decision trees that can achieve similar predictive performance to complex transformer-based models.
- Core assumption: The information content preserved during text-to-table conversion is sufficient for accurate prediction, and simpler models can capture the essential patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2] "Interestingly, the performance of interpretable tabular data models, namely logreg and dtree, is comparable to the set-fit-mpnetv2 model."
  - [section 5.3] "We base our evaluation of this assumption on the comparison of the models trained on ground truth tabular data and the models based on the extracted tabular data."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on different aspects of medical text processing but don't directly compare interpretable tabular models to text classifiers.
- Break condition: If the text-to-table conversion loses critical information needed for prediction, interpretable models will underperform compared to direct text classification approaches.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: RExtract -> LLM extraction -> VORC validation/correction -> Extracted tabular data -> ML model training -> Diagnostic prediction

- Critical path: Text report → RExtract prompt generation → LLM extraction → VORC validation/correction → Extracted tabular data → ML model training → Diagnostic prediction

- Design tradeoffs: TEMED-LLM trades off some extraction accuracy for interpretability and computational efficiency. The approach requires domain expertise for prompt engineering but reduces dependency on large, complex text models.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor extraction quality despite VORC corrections: Indicates insufficient domain knowledge in reasoning guidelines
  - VORC loop fails to converge: Suggests error patterns too complex for current correction mechanisms
  - Interpretable models underperform text classifiers: May indicate information loss during text-to-table conversion

## Limitations

- The methodology relies heavily on domain-specific reasoning guidelines that are not fully detailed, making it difficult to assess generalizability to other medical domains.
- The VORC module's error correction effectiveness depends on the quality of rule-based agents, which may not scale well to more complex medical scenarios.
- Performance comparison with text classification models is limited to specific architectures and may not generalize to other transformer-based approaches.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core methodology of using LLMs for structured data extraction followed by interpretable ML modeling is technically sound and well-supported by experimental results across five medical datasets.
- **Medium Confidence**: The VORC module's iterative correction approach shows promise but lacks detailed analysis of failure modes and long-term reliability across diverse medical texts.
- **Medium Confidence**: The interpretability advantages of using simpler models on extracted data are demonstrated but could be more rigorously quantified against a broader range of black-box alternatives.
