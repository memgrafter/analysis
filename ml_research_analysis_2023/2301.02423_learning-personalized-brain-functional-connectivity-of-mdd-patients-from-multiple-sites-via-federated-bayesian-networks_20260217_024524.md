# Learning Personalized Brain Functional Connectivity of MDD Patients from Multiple Sites via Federated Bayesian Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02423
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02423
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a federated learning method, NOTEARS-PFL, for learning personalized brain functional connectivity networks of MDD patients from multi-site rs-fMRI data.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces NOTEARS-PFL, a federated learning framework for identifying personalized brain functional connectivity patterns in major depressive disorder (MDD) patients using multi-site resting-state fMRI data. The method combines continuous optimization from the NOTEARS approach with group fused lasso penalties to learn site-specific Bayesian network structures while preserving shared disease-related patterns. Experiments demonstrate superior performance compared to existing methods in identifying MDD-specific connectivity involving insula, thalamus, and temporal regions, while maintaining computational efficiency and data privacy through federated learning.

## Method Summary
NOTEARS-PFL transforms the combinatorial Bayesian network structure learning problem into continuous optimization by characterizing acyclicity through a smooth function. The method employs a group fused lasso penalty to capture site-specific information while preserving shared disease-induced connectivity patterns across sites. Local sites estimate weighted adjacency matrices using a closed-form update, which are then aggregated at a central server using an iterative proximal projection method. The framework alternates between local updates at each site and global updates at the center until convergence, enabling efficient learning without sharing raw data.

## Key Results
- NOTEARS-PFL achieves F-score of 0.82 compared to 0.58-0.65 for comparable methods
- Structural Hamming distance improves from 0.25-0.48 to 0.12
- Correctly identifies MDD-specific connectivity patterns involving insula, thalamus, and temporal regions
- Demonstrates superior edge prediction accuracy and computational efficiency compared to NOTEARS-SIG, NOTEARS-AVG, and NOTEARS-ADMM

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: NOTEARS-PFL can learn site-specific Bayesian network structures while preserving shared disease-induced connectivity patterns across sites.
- Mechanism: The method uses a group fused lasso penalty to encourage similarity among site-specific weighted adjacency matrices while allowing for site-specific deviations.
- Core assumption: The shared disease-induced connectivity patterns across sites are strong enough to be captured by the fused lasso penalty, while site-specific differences are small enough to be modeled as deviations.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 3.2], [corpus]
- Break condition: If heterogeneity between sites is too large, the penalty may force overly similar structures, missing important site-specific patterns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: NOTEARS-PFL achieves computational efficiency by transforming the combinatorial BN structure learning problem into a continuous optimization problem.
- Mechanism: The method uses the NOTEARS formulation, which characterizes acyclicity through a smooth function h(W) = 0.
- Core assumption: The acyclicity constraint can be effectively enforced through the smooth function without significantly compromising the quality of learned structures.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 2.2], [corpus]
- Break condition: If the smooth function does not adequately enforce acyclicity, learned structures may contain cycles.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: NOTEARS-PFL can identify MDD-specific functional connectivity patterns, particularly involving insula, thalamus, and temporal regions.
- Mechanism: The method learns personalized BN structures from multi-site rs-fMRI data, allowing it to capture disease-specific connectivity patterns consistent across sites.
- Core assumption: MDD-specific connectivity patterns are strong enough to be consistently captured across multiple sites despite heterogeneity.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 5.3.2], [corpus]
- Break condition: If MDD-specific patterns are too subtle or inconsistent across sites, the method may fail to reliably identify them.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Local sites -> Center -> Local sites
- Critical path: 1. Local sites estimate local weighted adjacency matrices. 2. Local sites transmit estimated local matrices to center. 3. Center updates global weighted adjacency matrices. 4. Center transmits updated global matrices to local sites. 5. Repeat until convergence.
- Design tradeoffs: Balancing heterogeneity capture with shared structure learning through the group fused lasso penalty; enforcing acyclicity constraint while maintaining computational efficiency; ensuring data privacy while enabling collaborative model training.
- Failure signatures: Slow convergence or divergence of ADMM algorithm; learned structures containing cycles; poor identification of MDD-specific connectivity patterns due to excessive heterogeneity or weak shared patterns.
- First 3 experiments: 1. Validate on synthetic data with known ground truth structures, varying number of variables, sites, and perturbation levels. 2. Apply to small multi-site rs-fMRI dataset, comparing identified MDD-specific connectivity patterns with existing literature. 3. Assess impact of different hyperparameter settings on learned structures and MDD-specific pattern identification.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does NOTEARS-PFL perform when the number of ROIs increases beyond 116?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests up to 80 ROIs in synthetic data and uses 56 ROIs from the AAL template in real data, but does not explore performance at higher dimensionality.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test NOTEARS-PFL on datasets with more than 116 ROIs, leaving uncertainty about scalability to full-brain analysis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results on real or synthetic data with >116 ROIs, showing edge prediction accuracy, SHD, and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is NOTEARS-PFL to the choice of hyperparameters λ1 and λ2?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions λ1 and λ2 control sparsity and similarity in the group fused lasso penalty, but does not provide sensitivity analysis or guidelines for selecting these values.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore how different λ1 and λ2 values affect the learned networks or provide a principled method for hyperparameter selection.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive sensitivity analysis showing how varying λ1 and λ2 affects network structure, prediction accuracy, and computational performance across different datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does NOTEARS-PFL handle missing data or incomplete time series in rs-fMRI datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on complete datasets and does not address scenarios where some time points or regions are missing, which is common in real fMRI data.
- Why unresolved: The proposed method assumes complete data matrices, but real-world fMRI often has missing or corrupted time points due to motion artifacts or technical issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating NOTEARS-PFL's performance on datasets with varying levels of missing data, and comparison with methods specifically designed for incomplete data.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation on real multi-site data with relatively small sample sizes per site
- Method's performance heavily depends on choice of hyperparameters (λ1, λ2, ρ1, ρ2, γ1, γ2) which were not fully specified
- Group fused lasso penalty may struggle with excessive heterogeneity between sites, potentially missing important site-specific patterns
- Lacks comparison with other state-of-the-art federated learning methods for brain connectivity analysis

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the computational efficiency claims due to the NOTEARS formulation
- **Medium confidence** in the MDD-specific connectivity pattern identification, as results are promising but based on limited real data
- **Low confidence** in the generalizability to other brain disorders or different imaging modalities
