# A Protocol for Continual Explanation of SHAP

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07218
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07218
- Reference count: 8
- Key outcome: We propose an evaluation protocol to assess the stability of SHAP explanations in Class-Incremental Continual Learning, addressing the lack of methods to quantify explanation drift in dynamic environments.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel evaluation protocol to measure SHAP explanation stability in Class-Incremental Continual Learning scenarios. The protocol quantifies how much SHAP values drift from a jointly trained baseline model across all candidate classes using two metrics (M and Mpool). Experiments reveal that Replay strategies effectively preserve SHAP stability in feedforward/convolutional models, while fully-trained recurrent models (LSTMs) exhibit high explanation drift despite good accuracy. Notably, randomized recurrent models (ESNs) maintain both accuracy and explanation stability, highlighting the importance of monitoring explanations in neural networks and suggesting that recurrent architecture choices impact explanation stability in Continual Learning.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes an evaluation protocol to measure SHAP explanation drift in Class-Incremental Continual Learning by comparing SHAP values of a continual learning model against those of a jointly trained baseline. Two metrics are introduced: M measures overall squared difference in SHAP values, while Mpool adds spatial awareness through 4x4 average pooling for image data. The protocol is evaluated across three benchmarks (Split MNIST, Split CIFAR-10, Synthetic Speech Commands) using three CL strategies (Naive, GSS, Experience Replay) with feedforward/ResNet, LSTM, and ESN architectures. SHAP values are computed using GradientSHAP with 600 background examples and 50 test examples per class, evaluating on the first experience's test set after training on the entire stream.

## Key Results
- Replay strategies preserve SHAP stability in feedforward/convolutional models but not in fully-trained LSTMs
- Fully-trained LSTMs show high explanation drift despite maintaining good predictive accuracy
- ESNs maintain both accuracy and explanation stability, suggesting fixed random recurrence prevents explanation drift
- M and Mpool metrics effectively quantify explanation drift, with Mpool preserving spatial locality information

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SHAP value drift quantifies explanation instability in Class-Incremental Continual Learning
- Mechanism: The protocol computes two metrics (M and Mpool) that measure the squared difference between SHAP values of a continual learning model and those of a jointly trained reference model, capturing both feature-level and spatial-level drift.
- Core assumption: SHAP values of a jointly trained model represent stable ground truth explanations for each class.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose an evaluation protocol to robustly assess the change of explanations in Class-Incremental scenarios"
  - [section 2] "M(S, J) = 1/K Σᵢ(Sᵢ - Jᵢ)²" and "Mpool(S, J) = 1/P Σᵢ(pool(S,4)ᵢ - pool(J,4)ᵢ)²"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; corpus neighbors discuss SHAP interpretation but not CL drift metrics.
- Break condition: If the jointly trained model itself exhibits explanation instability due to dataset ordering or other factors.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Replay strategies preserve SHAP stability in feedforward/convolutional models but not in fully-trained recurrent models
- Mechanism: Replay mitigates forgetting by maintaining a memory buffer of previous examples, which stabilizes feature importance attribution for feedforward architectures, but fully-trained RNNs still experience explanation drift despite good accuracy.
- Core assumption: The recurrent component's learned dynamics are sensitive to sequential training, causing explanation instability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Replay strategies effectively preserve SHAP stability in feedforward/convolutional models"
  - [section 2] "fully-trained recurrent models (LSTMs) exhibit high explanation drift despite good accuracy"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; corpus neighbors do not discuss CL+SHAP intersection.
- Break condition: If explanation drift occurs due to reasons unrelated to recurrence (e.g., data imbalance in replay buffer).

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Randomized recurrent models (ESNs) maintain both accuracy and explanation stability
- Mechanism: ESNs have fixed random recurrent weights that prevent the learned dynamics from drifting during sequential training, thus stabilizing SHAP explanations while maintaining competitive accuracy.
- Core assumption: Fixed random recurrence provides a stable computational substrate that is less sensitive to non-stationary data streams.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "randomized recurrent models (ESNs) maintain both accuracy and explanation stability"
  - [section 2] "We show that alternative recurrent approaches, like randomized recurrent models, are more effective in keeping the explanations stable over time"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; corpus neighbors do not discuss ESNs in CL context.
- Break condition: If the fixed random weights become suboptimal for new data distributions over time.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Class-Incremental Continual Learning
  - Why needed here: The protocol evaluates explanation drift specifically in scenarios where new classes are introduced sequentially without revisiting previous ones.
  - Quick check question: What is the main challenge of Class-Incremental CL that makes explanation monitoring necessary?

- Concept: SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)
  - Why needed here: SHAP provides a feature attribution method that quantifies the contribution of each input feature toward a model's prediction, enabling measurement of explanation drift.
  - Quick check question: How does SHAP compute the contribution of each feature to the model output?

- Concept: Catastrophic Forgetting
  - Why needed here: Forgetting is the primary phenomenon that causes explanation drift in CL, as the model's learned representations shift away from earlier classes.
  - Quick check question: Why does catastrophic forgetting occur more severely in recurrent networks compared to feedforward networks in CL settings?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Evaluation protocol: Implements M and Mpool metrics
  - SHAP computation module: Uses GradientSHAP for neural networks
  - Continual learning strategies: Naive, Experience Replay (ER), GSS
  - Benchmark datasets: Split MNIST, Split CIFAR-10, Synthetic Speech Commands
  - Model architectures: Feedforward/ResNet, LSTM, ESN

- Critical path: Dataset → CL strategy training → SHAP computation on e1 test set → Metric computation (M/Mpool) → Comparison with joint training baseline

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Metric M vs Mpool: M captures overall drift magnitude while Mpool preserves spatial locality information
  - Replay buffer size: Larger buffers reduce forgetting but increase memory/computation cost
  - ESN vs LSTM: ESNs trade learned recurrence for stability, potentially limiting model capacity

- Failure signatures:
  - High M/Mpool values indicate explanation drift
  - Disparity between accuracy and explanation stability suggests architecture-specific issues
  - Naive strategy showing lower M for later classes indicates bias toward recent experiences

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run joint training on full dataset and compute baseline SHAP values for reference
  2. Apply Naive strategy on Split MNIST and compute M/Mpool to establish forgetting baseline
  3. Compare Replay strategy (ER) against Naive on Split CIFAR-10 to measure explanation preservation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do fully-trained recurrent models like LSTMs exhibit high explanation drift in continual learning scenarios despite maintaining good predictive accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that LSTMs show high explanation drift with Replay strategies while maintaining good accuracy, and that this behavior is peculiar to fully-trained recurrent components.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates this phenomenon empirically but does not provide a theoretical explanation for why the explanation drift occurs in trained recurrent models specifically.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies analyzing internal state dynamics, gradient flows, and attention mechanisms in LSTMs versus ESNs during continual learning could reveal why explanation drift occurs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do explanation drift patterns differ across various XAI methods (beyond SHAP) in continual learning scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on SHAP values and proposes evaluation metrics specific to SHAP, suggesting unexplored territory in applying other XAI methods to continual learning.
- Why unresolved: The study is limited to SHAP values and does not investigate whether other explanation methods (LIME, integrated gradients, etc.) exhibit similar drift patterns or if some methods are more stable in continual learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of multiple XAI methods' stability across different continual learning benchmarks and strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can explanation stability be directly optimized as a training objective in continual learning rather than being an indirect consequence of replay strategies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that replay strategies preserve SHAP stability but questions whether this can be achieved through alternative means, especially for recurrent models.
- Why unresolved: Current approaches treat explanation stability as a side effect rather than a primary objective, and the paper does not explore whether explicit regularization for explanation consistency would be more effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of continual learning algorithms that incorporate explanation consistency losses directly into the training objective, comparing their performance against traditional replay-based approaches.

## Limitations

- The protocol assumes jointly trained model SHAP values represent stable ground truth, but this assumption is not empirically validated against potential ordering effects in joint training
- No statistical significance testing is reported for the differences in M/Mpool values across strategies
- The ESN results are promising but limited to one randomized recurrent architecture without exploring the broader design space

## Confidence

- High confidence: The mechanism linking replay strategies to explanation stability in feedforward models is well-supported by the proposed metrics and experimental setup
- Medium confidence: The claim that fully-trained LSTMs exhibit high explanation drift is supported but could be influenced by specific architectural choices rather than recurrence per se
- Medium confidence: The ESN results suggest explanation stability, but the fixed random weights may become suboptimal over longer streams
