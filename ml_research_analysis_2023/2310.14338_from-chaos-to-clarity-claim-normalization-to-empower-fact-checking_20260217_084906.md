# From Chaos to Clarity: Claim Normalization to Empower Fact-Checking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2310.14338
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14338
- Reference count: 23
- Introduces Claim Normalization task and achieves superior performance across multiple evaluation metrics including ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR, and BERTScore

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Claim Normalization (ClaimNorm), a novel task designed to transform noisy, unstructured social media posts into concise, fact-checkable claims. The authors propose CACN, a framework that leverages chain-of-thought reasoning and in-context learning with large language models to generate these normalized claims. A high-quality dataset, CLAN, comprising 6,388 real-world social media posts and their corresponding normalized claims, is curated. CACN outperforms several baseline models across multiple evaluation measures, and human evaluation further validates the linguistic proficiency and factual consistency of the generated claims.

## Method Summary
The proposed CACN framework uses GPT-3 (text-davinci-003) with in-context learning, combining chain-of-thought prompting and reverse check-worthiness estimation. The approach breaks down the claim normalization task into intermediate reasoning steps, prioritizing claims that are verifiable, likely false, and relevant to public interest. The method leverages few-shot examples within prompts rather than traditional fine-tuning, avoiding potential overfitting while maintaining performance. The framework is evaluated on the newly created CLAN dataset using multiple automated metrics alongside human evaluation.

## Key Results
- CACN significantly outperforms baseline models including T5, BART, FLAN-T5, and PEGASUS across ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR, and BERTScore metrics
- Human evaluation confirms the linguistic proficiency and factual consistency of CACN-generated claims
- In-context learning with 3-5 examples achieves superior performance compared to traditional fine-tuning approaches
- Chain-of-thought reasoning combined with reverse check-worthiness criteria produces more useful normalized claims for fact-checkers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Chain-of-thought prompting enables systematic reasoning for complex claim normalization tasks
- By breaking down the task into intermediate reasoning steps (identify central claim, assess verifiability, evaluate likelihood of falsehood, determine check-worthiness), the model generates more accurate and verifiable normalized claims
- Core assumption: Complex tasks requiring understanding of claim context and intent benefit from explicit step-by-step reasoning
- Evidence: [abstract] states CACN "mimics human reasoning processes" and [section 4] details the chain-of-thought prompting approach

### Mechanism 2
- In-context learning with task-specific examples improves performance over fine-tuning on limited data
- Providing few relevant examples within prompts allows the model to learn task patterns without extensive fine-tuning, avoiding potential overfitting
- Core assumption: Large language models can effectively learn from in-context examples without explicit parameter updates
- Evidence: [section 6] shows models with in-context examples "showcase superior performance" and [section 4] describes the in-context learning implementation

### Mechanism 3
- Reverse check-worthiness estimation guides models to focus on verifiable and fact-check-worthy claims
- By prioritizing claims that contain verifiable factual content, have higher likelihood of being false, are relevant to public interest, and are worth fact-checking, the model generates more useful normalized claims
- Core assumption: Fact-checkers prioritize claims based on check-worthiness criteria, and models can be guided to mimic this prioritization
- Evidence: [section 4] explains leveraging check-worthiness to "steer the model's attention toward salient and pertinent information"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Social media post (text) -> GPT-3(text-davinci-003) LLM -> Chain-of-thought prompt with reverse check-worthiness criteria -> In-context examples (3-5) -> Normalized claim (concise, verifiable statement) -> Evaluation (ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR, BERTScore + human evaluation)

- Critical path: 1. Receive social media post 2. Apply chain-of-thought prompt with reverse check-worthiness criteria 3. Include 3-5 in-context examples in prompt 4. Generate normalized claim via GPT-3 5. Evaluate using multiple metrics 6. Optional: Human evaluation for qualitative assessment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - In-context learning vs. fine-tuning: In-context learning avoids overfitting but requires careful example selection; fine-tuning needs more data but may overfit
  - Prompt complexity vs. model performance: More detailed prompts improve results but increase input length and token costs
  - Evaluation metrics: Lexical overlap metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) vs. semantic similarity (BERTScore) - both needed for comprehensive assessment

- Failure signatures:
  - Outputs that are too long or verbose (indicates prompt not constraining length properly)
  - Factual inconsistencies between generated claim and source post (indicates check-worthiness criteria not being applied)
  - Low semantic similarity despite high lexical overlap (indicates model copying text rather than understanding)
  - Degradation when more examples are added (indicates in-context learning saturation or example quality issues)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare zero-shot vs. few-shot in-context learning with varying numbers of examples (10, 20, 50) to find optimal balance
  2. Test different prompt templates (direct, question-guided, chain-of-thought) to identify which reasoning approach works best
  3. Evaluate the impact of including vs. excluding reverse check-worthiness criteria in the prompt on output quality and relevance

## Limitations

- Reliance on GPT-3 for in-context learning introduces variability dependent on prompt quality and example selection
- Evaluation primarily uses automated metrics with limited human evaluation, raising questions about practical utility for fact-checking workflows
- Performance may degrade when applied to domains or languages not represented in the CLAN dataset

## Confidence

- High confidence in the core mechanism of chain-of-thought prompting for structured reasoning
- Medium confidence in the superiority of in-context learning over fine-tuning given limited baseline comparisons
- Medium confidence in the effectiveness of reverse check-worthiness estimation due to limited ablation studies
