# Dual-path Adaptation from Image to Video Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09857
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09857
- Reference count: 40
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on four action recognition benchmarks using only 10M parameters versus 450M for traditional methods

## Executive Summary
DualPath is a parameter-efficient method for transferring pretrained vision foundation models to video understanding tasks. The approach uses a dual-path architecture that separates spatial and temporal modeling, with lightweight bottleneck adapters applied to frozen transformer blocks. By leveraging grid-like frameset transformations for temporal modeling and individual frames for spatial context, DualPath achieves competitive performance on action recognition benchmarks while requiring significantly fewer parameters and computations than traditional video models.

## Method Summary
DualPath adapts pretrained vision transformers (ViT/Swin) to video tasks by adding two distinct adaptation paths. The spatial path processes individual frames through parallel adapters in each transformer block, while the temporal path processes consecutive frames arranged in a grid-like format through serial adapters. Both paths keep the pretrained image model parameters frozen, with only the adapter parameters being trained. This design enables efficient transfer learning by leveraging the spatial encoding capabilities of image transformers while adding minimal temporal modeling components.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on Kinetics-400, Something-Something-v2, HMDB51, and Diving-48 benchmarks
- Uses only 10M parameters compared to 450M for traditional video models
- Requires 710 GFLOPs versus 48,912 GFLOPs for comparable methods
- Outperforms full fine-tuning approaches while maintaining extreme parameter efficiency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dual-path design enables separation of spatial and temporal modeling to leverage pretrained image transformer strengths.
- Mechanism: By freezing pretrained image transformer parameters and adding lightweight bottleneck adapters in two distinct paths, the model can separately optimize spatial context (from individual frames) and temporal context (from consecutive frames) without interfering with each other.
- Core assumption: Spatial context in videos can be adequately captured by individual frames, while temporal context requires multiple frames in sequence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Previous adaptation methods have simultaneously considered spatial and temporal modeling with a unified learnable module but still suffered from fully leveraging the representative capabilities of image transformers."
  - [section 4.1]: "Since the image foundation models have been trained on large amounts of web datasets, we can intuitively speculate that they might be powerful to encode the spatial context even in videos."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. Related works like DIFFUMA [2504.10068] and SST-EM [2501.07554] also use dual-path approaches but focus on different domains (video prediction and editing evaluation respectively).
- Break Condition: If temporal relationships cannot be adequately captured by the temporal path alone, or if spatial context requires multiple frames rather than individual frames.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Grid-like frameset transformation enables efficient temporal modeling while maintaining computational efficiency.
- Mechanism: By scaling down and stacking consecutive frames into a unified grid-like input, the model can observe multiple frames simultaneously while keeping the computational cost similar to processing a single frame, effectively imitating vision transformers' ability to extrapolate relationships between tokens.
- Core assumption: Vision transformers can effectively learn temporal relationships when multiple frames are presented as a unified input with proper positional encoding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Especially for temporal dynamic modeling, we incorporate consecutive frames into a grid-like frameset to precisely imitate vision transformers' capability that extrapolates relationships between tokens."
  - [section 4.2]: "Our grid-like frameset design is inspired by recent visual prompting research [4, 5]. It is simple yet surprisingly effective in imitating temporal modeling as spatial modeling and certainly reduces the computation."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. While Mavors [2504.10068] uses multi-granularity video representation, there's no direct evidence of grid-like frameset transformation for temporal modeling efficiency.
- Break Condition: If the scaling process loses too much temporal information, or if the transformer cannot effectively learn relationships from the grid-like representation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Bottleneck adapters enable efficient parameter tuning while preserving pretrained knowledge.
- Mechanism: By using lightweight bottleneck modules (down-projection, activation, up-projection) in parallel for spatial path and serial for temporal path, the model can adapt to video tasks with minimal parameter changes while keeping most of the pretrained image transformer knowledge intact.
- Core assumption: Bottleneck adapters can effectively bridge the gap between image and video domains without requiring full fine-tuning of the entire model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "By incorporating lightweight bottleneck adapters in each transformer block and freezing the pretrained image model parameters, DualPath achieves comparable or even better performance than state-of-the-art video models while requiring significantly fewer parameters."
  - [section 3.2]: "AdaptFormer [11] learns a trainable bottleneck module [27, 33]. The intermediate feature z in Eq. 2 of each transformer block is fed into the AdapterMLP that consists of the original MLP layers and a bottleneck structure."
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence. ST-Adapter [2507.22421] uses similar adapter-based approaches for video understanding, though with different architectural choices.
- Break Condition: If the bottleneck size is too small to capture necessary adaptations, or if the adapters cannot effectively learn the domain shift from images to videos.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input frames → Patch embedding → Transformer blocks with adapters → [CLS] tokens extraction → Concatenation → Classifier → Output
- Critical path: Input → Patch embedding → Transformer blocks with adapters → [CLS] tokens extraction → Classifier → Output
  - The temporal path processes grid-like frameset through frozen transformer blocks with serial adapters
  - The spatial path processes individual frames through frozen transformer blocks with parallel adapters
  - Both paths' outputs are concatenated and fed to the classifier
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Spatial path: Uses parallel adapters for independent spatial and temporal adaptation, but requires careful frame sampling rate selection
  - Temporal path: Uses serial adapters for sequential processing, but grid-like frameset resolution affects both temporal detail and computational cost
  - Backbone freezing: Preserves pretrained knowledge but limits adaptation flexibility
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on temporal-heavy datasets (e.g., Something-Something-v2) indicates temporal path inadequacy
  - Inconsistent results across different frame sampling rates suggest spatial path limitations
  - High computational cost despite adapter usage indicates inefficient grid-like frameset construction
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Train with only spatial path (remove temporal path adapters) and only temporal path (remove spatial path adapters) to verify dual-path effectiveness
  2. Resolution sweep: Test different scaling factors (w=h=2, 4, 8) for grid-like frameset to find optimal temporal resolution vs computational cost tradeoff
  3. Frame rate analysis: Compare performance using different numbers of frames in spatial path (TS=4, 8, 12) to determine optimal spatial sampling rate

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does DualPath's performance scale when using more than 48 frames in the temporal path while maintaining computational efficiency?
- Would DualPath's grid-like frameset approach be effective for other video understanding tasks beyond action recognition, such as video object detection or video segmentation?
- How does the choice of scaling factors (w, h) affect DualPath's performance on videos with different motion characteristics, such as fast vs. slow motion or small vs. large objects?

## Limitations
- Performance depends heavily on grid-like frameset transformation with underspecified implementation details
- Limited evaluation to action recognition tasks, unclear generalizability to other video understanding applications
- Effectiveness of spatial-temporal separation not thoroughly validated through ablation studies

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The core claim that DualPath achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art video models while using significantly fewer parameters is well-supported by experimental results across four benchmarks.
- Medium Confidence: The mechanism claims about spatial-temporal separation effectiveness are plausible but lack extensive ablation studies to isolate each path's contribution.
- Low Confidence: The assertion that grid-like frameset transformation "precisely imitates" vision transformer's temporal modeling capability is largely theoretical without quantitative validation of the temporal information preservation.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation Study**: Train and evaluate models with only spatial path (removing temporal adapters) and only temporal path (removing spatial adapters) to quantify each path's individual contribution to overall performance.
2. **Resolution Analysis**: Systematically test different grid-like frameset scaling configurations (e.g., w=h=2, 4, 8) to identify optimal resolution tradeoffs between temporal detail preservation and computational efficiency.
3. **Temporal Generalization**: Evaluate DualPath on temporal reasoning tasks beyond action recognition (such as video question answering or temporal localization) to assess the generalizability of its dual-path temporal modeling approach.
