# Boosting Cross-task Transferability of Adversarial Patches with Visual Relations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.05402
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05402
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: VRAP outperforms baseline DR in cross-task transferability, achieving lower Recall@K and accuracy scores on Scene Graph Generation, Visual Question Answering, and Image Captioning tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of cross-task transferability of adversarial patches in visual reasoning tasks. The authors propose VRAP, a method that leverages scene graphs to disrupt shared visual reasoning information across tasks like Scene Graph Generation, Visual Question Answering, and Image Captioning. VRAP combines object recognition-based deception with predicate-based relation elimination to generate adversarial patches that are more transferable across different visual reasoning tasks. The method demonstrates improved black-box transferability compared to baseline approaches.

## Method Summary
VRAP generates adversarial patches by optimizing a combination of two loss functions: target detection deception loss (Ld) and relationship elimination loss (Lr). The method utilizes scene graphs to capture object relationships and employs a gradient-based iterative algorithm to optimize the adversarial patches. The relationship elimination loss reduces the probability of predicted relationships between object pairs, while the target detection deception loss misleads object detection models. The adversarial patch generation is achieved through backpropagation incorporating both loss components, with the goal of disrupting visual reasoning information shared across multiple tasks.

## Key Results
- VRAP achieves lower Recall@K and Mean Recall@K scores on Scene Graph Generation tasks compared to the baseline DR method
- The method demonstrates reduced accuracy on Image Captioning and Visual Question Answering tasks when adversarial patches are applied
- VRAP shows improved black-box transferability across visual reasoning tasks compared to traditional adversarial patch approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
VRAP disrupts shared visual reasoning information by eliminating predicate-based relations between objects in scene graphs. The relationship elimination loss (Lr) reduces the probability of predicted relationships between all pairs of objects by aggregating scores of the most likely relation types and minimizing them. This encourages the adversarial patch to disrupt object relationships. Core assumption: Visual reasoning tasks share common relational information that can be exploited for cross-task transferability when disrupted.

### Mechanism 2
VRAP combines object recognition-based deception with relation elimination to enhance cross-task transferability. The target detection deception loss (Ld) misleads object detection and classification models by altering predictions from the perspective of misleading model detection labels. This loss term deceives the model into misidentifying target objects in the image. Core assumption: Visual reasoning tasks rely on accurate object recognition as a foundation for reasoning, so disrupting object detection can cascade into reasoning failures across tasks.

### Mechanism 3
The combination of Lr and Ld in a gradient-based iterative algorithm optimizes adversarial patches that exhibit high transferability across visual reasoning tasks. The adversarial patch is updated using backpropagation that incorporates both loss components. This dual-loss optimization ensures the patch disrupts both object recognition and relational reasoning components that are shared across tasks. Core assumption: Visual reasoning tasks share common architectural features and processing pathways that can be simultaneously disrupted by a patch optimized against multiple loss components.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. **How does the VRAP method's performance scale with larger or more diverse datasets beyond Visual Genome, VQAv2, and COCO?**
   - Basis: The authors mention using Visual Genome for patch generation and VQAv2 and COCO for evaluation, but do not explore performance on other datasets or scales.
   - Unresolved: Experiments are limited to specific datasets, with no analysis of how the method would perform on larger or more diverse datasets.
   - Evidence needed: Experiments showing VRAP's performance on a wider variety of datasets, including larger and more diverse ones.

2. **What is the impact of the relation loss weight (λ) on the effectiveness of VRAP across different visual reasoning tasks?**
   - Basis: The authors set λ as 0.01 but do not explore how varying this parameter affects the method's performance across different tasks.
   - Unresolved: The paper does not provide a sensitivity analysis of the relation loss weight, leaving its optimal value and impact across tasks unclear.
   - Evidence needed: A detailed analysis of VRAP's performance with different λ values across various visual reasoning tasks.

3. **How does VRAP compare to other state-of-the-art methods for cross-task transferability beyond DR, especially in real-world applications?**
   - Basis: The authors compare VRAP only with the DR method and mention the need to consider more baselines in future work.
   - Unresolved: The comparison is limited to a single baseline, with no exploration of VRAP's effectiveness against a broader range of methods or in practical scenarios.
   - Evidence needed: Comparative studies of VRAP against multiple state-of-the-art methods in both controlled experiments and real-world applications.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of VRAP relies heavily on the assumption that visual reasoning tasks share common relational information captured by scene graphs, which may not generalize to all types of visual reasoning tasks
- The ablation study only compares against a baseline (DR) without exploring alternative approaches to relationship disruption
- The paper does not thoroughly investigate the impact of different scene graph model architectures or the robustness of the proposed method against potential defenses

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Experimental results demonstrating VRAP's effectiveness in reducing Recall@K and accuracy metrics on SGG, VQA, and IC tasks are well-documented and reproducible. The methodology for generating adversarial patches using the combined loss function is clearly specified.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that scene graphs effectively capture shared relational information across visual reasoning tasks is supported by experimental results but lacks extensive theoretical justification. The generalizability of VRAP to other visual reasoning tasks beyond the three tested remains uncertain.

**Low Confidence**: The paper's assertion that the relationship elimination loss is the primary driver of cross-task transferability is not conclusively proven, as the ablation study does not isolate the individual contributions of the object detection deception loss and relationship elimination loss.
