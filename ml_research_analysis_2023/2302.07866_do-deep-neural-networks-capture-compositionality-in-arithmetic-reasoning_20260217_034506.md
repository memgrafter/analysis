# Do Deep Neural Networks Capture Compositionality in Arithmetic Reasoning?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.07866
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07866
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Pre-trained sequence-to-sequence models struggle with systematicity generalization in multi-hop arithmetic reasoning tasks, even with intermediate step supervision

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether pre-trained sequence-to-sequence models can capture compositionality in multi-hop arithmetic symbolic reasoning. The authors introduce a skill tree framework to systematically define hierarchical complexity levels across three compositionality dimensions: systematicity, productivity, and substitutivity. Through controlled experiments with T5 models on synthetic datasets, they find that systematicity generalization is particularly challenging, with models failing to access intermediate reasoning information not explicitly stated in the input. The results demonstrate fundamental limitations in neural models' ability to perform systematic reasoning in arithmetic operations and reference-based calculations.

## Method Summary
The researchers created a skill tree to define hierarchical complexity levels in arithmetic reasoning problems across three compositionality dimensions. They constructed synthetic datasets with controlled compositions and evaluated pre-trained T5 models on these tasks. The experiments systematically tested different complexity levels and investigated whether providing intermediate reasoning steps during training could improve performance. Multiple evaluation scenarios were designed to isolate specific compositional challenges, particularly focusing on systematicity generalization where models must combine learned skills in novel ways.

## Key Results
- Models showed severe performance degradation on systematic composition tasks despite success on individual components
- Intermediate step supervision provided minimal improvement in systematic reasoning tasks
- The primary bottleneck was accessing intermediate reasoning information not explicitly present in the input
- Performance gaps persisted even for simple compositional structures that humans find trivial

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism of the models relies on attention-based pattern matching rather than true symbolic manipulation. When faced with systematic compositions, the models cannot effectively retrieve or combine intermediate calculation states that aren't directly observable in the input. This suggests that the transformer architecture's reliance on local context and attention patterns is insufficient for maintaining and accessing intermediate reasoning steps required for systematic composition.

## Foundational Learning
- **Skill tree compositionality**: Hierarchical task decomposition framework - needed to systematically define and test compositional reasoning; quick check: verify tree structure covers all relevant reasoning dimensions
- **Systematicity generalization**: Ability to combine learned skills in novel ways - needed to test true compositional understanding; quick check: compare performance on seen vs. unseen skill combinations
- **Productivity in reasoning**: Generation of novel solutions through combination - needed to measure model's creative problem-solving; quick check: test model on extended composition lengths
- **Substitutivity**: Flexible replacement of components - needed to test understanding of abstract relationships; quick check: evaluate performance when swapping equivalent elements
- **Intermediate supervision**: Training with step-by-step reasoning - needed to probe whether explicit reasoning paths help; quick check: compare with end-to-end training
- **Attention mechanism limitations**: Inability to track non-local dependencies - needed to explain systematic failure patterns; quick check: analyze attention patterns on compositional tasks

## Limitations
- Results are limited to T5 models and may not generalize to other architectures
- The skill tree structure may not capture all relevant compositional dimensions in mathematical reasoning
- Synthetic datasets may not reflect real-world reasoning complexity or distribution shifts
- The study focuses exclusively on arithmetic reasoning rather than broader compositional tasks

## Confidence
- Neural models struggle with systematicity generalization - High confidence
- Accessing intermediate reasoning information is the primary bottleneck - Medium confidence
- Training with intermediate reasoning steps does not resolve systematicity issues - Low confidence

## Next Checks
1. Test whether explicitly training attention mechanisms to track intermediate calculation steps improves systematicity performance, comparing against standard supervised fine-tuning
2. Evaluate whether a recursive decomposition approach (solving sub-problems separately before combining) can bridge the systematicity gap compared to end-to-end training
3. Replicate experiments across multiple model architectures (e.g., GPT, BART, LLaMA) to determine if the systematicity challenges are specific to T5 or represent a broader limitation of transformer-based models in compositional arithmetic reasoning
