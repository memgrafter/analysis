# Learning by Grouping: A Multilevel Optimization Framework for Improving Fairness in Classification without Losing Accuracy

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00486
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00486
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: LBG framework improves fairness and accuracy in image classification and language understanding tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Learning by Grouping (LBG), a multilevel optimization framework that improves fairness in machine learning classification without sacrificing accuracy. The core innovation is dividing diverse problems into subgroups, where each subgroup contains similar problems that can be solved independently and efficiently. The framework is formulated as a three-level optimization problem that learns group assignments, trains subgroup-specific models, and updates assignments based on validation loss. LBG demonstrates improved fairness metrics and accuracy across multiple datasets including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, ISIC-18, CelebA, and the GLUE benchmark.

## Method Summary
LBG addresses fairness through a three-stage optimization framework: (1) learning to group problems into subgroups using a Group-Assignment Model (GAM), (2) learning group-specific sub-models (GSCMs) for each subgroup, and (3) updating group assignments based on validation loss to minimize overfitting. The framework extends to neural architecture search, allowing each subgroup to learn specialized architectures. Domain adaptation techniques are incorporated to reduce overfitting in small datasets by treating each subgroup as a domain and adapting examples from other subgroups through Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) loss.

## Key Results
- LBG achieves state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet with improved fairness metrics (DP, DEO, SPD, EOD, AOD)
- Extended to GLUE benchmark, improving accuracy, F1 score, and correlation metrics while maintaining fairness
- Ablation studies show LBG with larger subgroup counts (K=4) achieves both higher accuracy and better fairness on CelebA dataset
- MMD loss proves most effective domain adaptation technique for achieving both accuracy and fairness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: LBG improves fairness by dividing diverse problems into subgroups where each subgroup contains similar problems that can be solved independently and efficiently.
- **Mechanism**: The framework learns group assignments on validation data rather than training data, preventing overfitting of the grouping mechanism to training patterns.
- **Core assumption**: Validation data distributions are representative of real-world deployment scenarios and maintain similar subgroup structures as training data.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract statement about three-level optimization; section on addressing overfitting in mixture-of-experts methods; weak corpus support with only 8 papers used for comparison.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Domain adaptation within LBG reduces overfitting in small datasets by treating each subgroup as a domain and adapting examples from other subgroups.
- **Mechanism**: MMD loss minimizes distribution differences between subgroups, effectively increasing training data diversity for each subgroup-specific model.
- **Core assumption**: Subgroups share underlying feature distributions that can be aligned through domain adaptation without losing subgroup-specific characteristics.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract mentions domain adaptation techniques; section describes treating subgroups as domains with MMD adaptation; weak corpus evidence for effectiveness.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: LBG improves accuracy by allowing each subgroup to learn specialized architectures through differentiable neural architecture search.
- **Mechanism**: The framework extends LBG to neural architecture search where each subgroup-specific model learns its own architecture weights.
- **Core assumption**: Different problem subgroups benefit from different architectural designs, and validation-based grouping can identify these architectural needs.
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract claims state-of-the-art performances with searchable network architectures; section describes updating architectures based on validation loss; weak corpus evidence for this specific combination.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Three-level optimization framework**
  - Why needed here: The multi-level structure separates concerns - grouping, model training, and group assignment refinement - preventing common overfitting issues in mixture-of-experts approaches.
  - Quick check question: Why does learning group assignments on validation data rather than training data help prevent overfitting?

- **Concept: Domain adaptation through distribution alignment**
  - Why needed here: Small subgroups in LBG create overfitting risks, and domain adaptation helps by expanding effective training data through distribution alignment across subgroups.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: GAM (K-way classifier) -> GSCMs (K expert models) -> Validation loop (updates group assignments and architectures) -> Optional domain adaptation module (MMD-based adaptation)

- **Critical path**: Training flow: 1) Train GAM on training data with fixed group assignments, 2) Train GSCMs on examples assigned by GAM, 3) Update group assignments using validation loss, 4) Repeat until convergence.

- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - K subgroup selection: More subgroups increase specialization but require more computational resources and data
  - Domain adaptation weight λ: Higher values increase adaptation but may reduce subgroup specificity
  - Architecture search complexity: More complex architectures may overfit subgroup data

- **Failure signatures**:
  - If accuracy drops significantly during stage 3, group assignments may be overfitting to validation data
  - If fairness metrics worsen, the grouping process may be creating biased subgroups
  - If training becomes unstable, the three-level optimization may have conflicting gradient signals

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. CIFAR-10 with K=2 subgroups using ResNet18 as base architecture - baseline for accuracy and fairness comparison
  2. CelebA dataset with "attractive" label and "gender" as sensitive attribute - fairness-focused evaluation
  3. CIFAR-100 with DARTS-based architecture search - architecture optimization capability validation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of Learning by Grouping (LBG) vary with different numbers of subgroups (K)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states in Section 4.4 that they conducted ablation studies on the number of subgroups and observed that for the CelebA dataset, larger numbers of subgroups can decrease classification error and improve fairness.
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents results for K=1, 2, 3, and 4 on the CelebA dataset. It is unclear how the performance of LBG varies with different numbers of subgroups on other datasets or tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on various datasets and tasks with different numbers of subgroups (K) would provide insights into how the performance of LBG varies with the number of subgroups.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of domain adaptation technique affect the performance of Learning by Grouping (LBG)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states in Section 4.4 that they conducted ablation studies on different domain adaptation techniques and observed that Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) loss was the most effective approach in achieving both high accuracy and fairness.
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents results for MMD, Correlation Alignment (CORAL), Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, and Contrastive Domain Discrepancy (CDD) on the DARTS dataset. It is unclear how the choice of domain adaptation technique affects the performance of LBG on other datasets or tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on various datasets and tasks with different domain adaptation techniques would provide insights into how the choice of domain adaptation technique affects the performance of LBG.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does Learning by Grouping (LBG) perform on non-differentiable neural architecture search approaches?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states in Section 5 that the main limitation of LBG is that it cannot be applied to non-differentiable NAS approaches up to a point.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not present any results or experiments on non-differentiable NAS approaches. It is unclear how LBG would perform on such approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on non-differentiable NAS approaches and comparing the performance of LBG with the baselines would provide insights into how LBG performs on such approaches.

## Limitations
- The framework's effectiveness depends on validation data distributions accurately representing deployment scenarios
- Limited ablation studies on hyperparameter sensitivity (subgroup count K and domain adaptation weight λ)
- Cannot be directly applied to non-differentiable neural architecture search approaches

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The three-level optimization framework design and its theoretical foundation are well-established
- **Medium confidence**: Empirical results show improvements in both fairness and accuracy, but limited comparison with recent fairness methods
- **Low confidence**: The domain adaptation mechanism's effectiveness lacks supporting evidence from prior work or detailed ablation studies
