# Contextualized Topic Coherence Metrics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14587
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14587
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: CTC outperforms traditional metrics, works well on short documents, and resists meaningless high-scoring topics.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Contextualized Topic Coherence (CTC), a new family of topic coherence metrics based on large language models (LLMs). CTC includes two approaches: CPMI, which uses LLMs to compute contextualized PMI between topic words, and semi-automated metrics using chatbots to evaluate coherence. Experiments on six topic models and two datasets show CTC outperforms traditional metrics, works well on short documents, and is not susceptible to meaningless high-scoring topics.

## Method Summary
CTC introduces automated and semi-automated metrics for topic model evaluation. The automated approach (CTCCPMI) pre-computes contextualized PMI between word pairs using BERT-based masked language models on corpus segments. The semi-automated approach interfaces with chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT) for CTCIntrusion and CTCRating tasks. The method evaluates six topic models on 20Newsgroups and Elon Musk tweet datasets, comparing results against traditional metrics like UCI, UMass, NPMI, and CV.

## Key Results
- CTC CPMI showed strong correlation with baseline metrics on 20Newsgroup dataset but was independent on short Elon Musk tweet dataset
- CTC Intrusion and Rating showed good correlation with NPMI and CV respectively
- CTC is less susceptible to being fooled by meaningless topics that receive high scores with traditional metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CTC outperforms automated topic coherence methods by using LLM-based contextualized PMI to evaluate semantic similarity within topics.
- **Mechanism:** CTC computes CPMI between topic words using masked language models, capturing contextual linguistic dependencies rather than relying on simple co-occurrence statistics.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs can accurately model contextual relationships between words, providing a better estimate of semantic coherence than traditional PMI-based metrics.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "CTC outperforms automated topic coherence methods, works well on short documents, and is not susceptible to meaningless but high-scoring topics."
- **Break Condition:** If LLM contextual understanding fails to align with human semantic judgment, or if CPMI computation becomes unreliable due to model limitations or data sparsity.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Semi-automated CTC metrics (CTCIntrusion and CTCRating) leverage chatbots to simulate human evaluation while maintaining efficiency.
- **Mechanism:** CTCIntrusion uses chatbots to identify intruder words in topics, while CTCRating asks chatbots to score topic interpretability on a 3-point scale.
- **Core assumption:** Chatbots can effectively mimic human judgment of topic coherence when provided with appropriate prompts.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "We propose LLM-based methods inspired by standard human topic evaluations... semi-automated CTC that allows human-centered evaluation of coherence while maintaining the efficiency of automated methods."
- **Break Condition:** If chatbots fail to accurately simulate human judgment, or if prompt engineering proves insufficient to elicit meaningful responses.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** CTC is not susceptible to being fooled by meaningless topics that receive high scores with traditional metrics.
- **Mechanism:** By using contextualized language models, CTC can detect "trash topics" (e.g., email signatures, abbreviations) that traditional metrics like CV might score highly due to syntactic co-occurrence patterns.
- **Core assumption:** LLMs have sufficient world knowledge to recognize when topic words lack meaningful semantic relationships.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract]: "CTC is much less susceptible to being fooled by meaningless topics that often receive high scores with traditional topic coherence metrics."
- **Break Condition:** If LLMs cannot distinguish between meaningful and meaningless word co-occurrences in certain domains, or if computational costs become prohibitive.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component Map:**
  - Pre-compute CPMI values for all word pairs in corpus segments -> Generate topics using chosen topic models -> Calculate CTCCPMI, CTCIntrusion, and CTCRating for each topic -> Compare results with traditional metrics

- **Critical Path:**
  1. Pre-compute CPMI values for all word pairs in corpus segments (for CTCCPMI)
  2. Generate topics using chosen topic models
  3. Calculate CTCCPMI, CTCIntrusion, and CTCRating for each topic
  4. Compare results with traditional metrics to identify model strengths/weaknesses

- **Design Tradeoffs:**
  - Automated vs. Semi-automated: Automated CTC offers speed but may miss nuanced human judgment; semi-automated CTC provides accuracy but at higher computational cost
  - Pre-computation: Improves runtime efficiency but requires significant initial computation and storage
  - LLM Selection: Different LLMs may yield varying results; choice affects both performance and cost

- **Failure Signatures:**
  - Low correlation between CTC and human judgment despite high scores
  - Computational bottlenecks during CPMI pre-computation
  - Inconsistent chatbot responses across different prompts or models

- **First 3 Experiments:**
  1. Reproduce CTC evaluation on 20Newsgroups dataset with Gibbs LDA and ETM to verify reported results
  2. Test CTCIntrusion and CTCRating on a small manually-annotated topic set to assess alignment with human judgment
  3. Compare CTC performance on short-text dataset (Elon Musk tweets) against traditional metrics to validate short-document effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does CTC perform on very large-scale datasets compared to smaller datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions pre-computing CPMI between all word pairs for large datasets but does not provide experimental results on very large-scale datasets.
- Why unresolved: The experiments in the paper only evaluate CTC on two datasets (20Newsgroups and Elon Musk's Tweets), which may not be representative of very large-scale datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on significantly larger datasets (e.g., Wikipedia, web-scale corpora) and comparing CTC performance with baseline metrics on these datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of language model affect the performance of CTC metrics?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses BERT as the language model for CPMI computation but does not explore the impact of using different language models (e.g., GPT, RoBERTa) on CTC performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses one specific language model (BERT) without comparing its performance to other language models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments using different language models for CPMI computation and comparing the resulting CTC scores with baseline metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can CTC metrics be extended to evaluate other types of topic models beyond the ones tested in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates CTC on six specific topic models but does not explore its applicability to other types of topic models.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to a specific set of topic models, and the generalizability of CTC to other models is not discussed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on a diverse set of topic models (e.g., dynamic topic models, hierarchical topic models) and comparing CTC performance with baseline metrics on these models.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does CTC handle multi-lingual datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only uses English datasets and does not address the performance of CTC on multi-lingual or non-English datasets.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies heavily on only two datasets (20Newsgroups and Elon Musk tweets), which may not represent full diversity of real-world applications
- Chatbot-based semi-automated metrics depend on OpenAI's API, introducing potential variability in results across different models or prompt versions
- Computational cost of pre-computing CPMI values for all word pairs could become prohibitive for larger corpora

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** CTC's effectiveness on short documents and its resistance to "trash topics" are well-supported by the experimental results
- **Medium Confidence:** The correlation between CTC metrics and traditional metrics is demonstrated, but the interpretation of what constitutes "better" coherence remains somewhat subjective
- **Low Confidence:** The generalizability of CTC across diverse domains and languages beyond the tested English datasets
