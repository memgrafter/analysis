# On the Planning Abilities of Large Language Models (A Critical Investigation with a Proposed Benchmark)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06706
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06706
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: LLMs show dismal autonomous planning performance (3% success) but can serve as heuristic aids when combined with sound planners

## Executive Summary
This paper critically investigates the planning capabilities of large language models using a newly developed benchmark suite based on the International Planning Competition. The benchmark evaluates LLM performance across seven planning tasks within the blocksworld domain, testing autonomous plan generation, optimal planning, plan execution reasoning, goal reformulation robustness, plan reuse, replanning, and plan generalization. The evaluation compares three modes: autonomous (LLMs generate plans directly), heuristic (LLM plans are repaired by sound planners), and human-in-the-loop (LLM suggestions assist human planners).

Results reveal that LLMs perform poorly in autonomous mode, with only 3% of generated plans being executable. However, when LLM outputs are fed to a sound planner for repair, the plans can be quickly corrected. In human-in-the-loop settings, LLM suggestions lead to modest but statistically insignificant improvements in human plan accuracy. The paper provides a systematic benchmark and evaluation tools to assess the evolving planning capabilities of LLMs, highlighting their current limitations and potential as heuristic aids rather than autonomous planners.

## Method Summary
The paper develops a benchmark suite with domain-independent and domain-dependent components to evaluate LLM planning capabilities. It uses a template-based mechanism to translate between symbolic models (PDDL) and natural language. The evaluation employs GPT-3 (Davinci and Instruct-GPT3) and BLOOM models across 600 instances for GPT-3 variants and 250-100 instances for BLOOM. The benchmark tests seven planning abilities in blocksworld with up to 5 blocks and optimal plan lengths of ≤8. The evaluation is automated using a planner and plan validator, with results measured through success rates and Levenshtein edit distances.

## Key Results
- LLMs achieve only 3% success rate in autonomous plan generation mode
- LLM-generated plans can be repaired by sound planners with high success rates
- Human-in-the-loop experiments show modest but statistically insignificant improvements in plan accuracy
- Fine-tuning GPT-3 on Blocksworld instances improves performance to 16.4-22% success rates
- LLMs struggle with disguised domains, suggesting reliance on semantic understanding of action/predicate names

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs can generate symbolic planning models and produce plans when given the right prompt structure
- Mechanism: By providing detailed domain descriptions and example problems, LLMs generate action sequences aligned with stated goals
- Core assumption: LLMs have sufficient latent knowledge of procedural reasoning patterns from pretraining to complete plans from prompt structure
- Evidence anchors: Benchmark tests seven planning areas including plan generation and optimal planning; uses few-shot query setting with structured prompts
- Break condition: Generated plans fail to satisfy preconditions/effects or the model fails to complete plans when new goal conditions are introduced

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LLM-generated plans can be repaired by sound planners to become executable
- Mechanism: In heuristic mode, LLM outputs are fed into a local search planner (LPG) that iteratively repairs flawed plans to guarantee correctness
- Core assumption: LLM outputs are close enough to valid plans that repair converges quickly and produces correct solutions
- Evidence anchors: Plans can be quickly repaired by sound planners; Levenshtein edit distance quantifies distance between LLM output and repaired plan
- Break condition: LLM plans are too flawed for the planner to converge, or completely incoherent

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LLM plan suggestions improve human planner accuracy in human-in-the-loop settings
- Mechanism: LLM-generated partial plans are provided to human participants who complete them, leveraging LLM as a heuristic aid
- Core assumption: Humans benefit from external plan suggestions as starting points, reducing cognitive load or correcting biases
- Evidence anchors: LLM suggestions lead to modest improvements in human plan accuracy (though not statistically significant); NASA-TLX measures cognitive load
- Break condition: LLM suggestions are misleading or participants over-rely on them without critical evaluation

  - Quick check question: What is the difference between a lifted domain model and a grounded instance in PDDL?

  - Why needed here: Paper automates plan correctness checks by executing actions in simulated state; preconditions/effects knowledge critical for judging validity
  - Quick check question: If an action's precondition is not satisfied in the current state, what happens when it is applied?

  - Why needed here: LLM used in few-shot mode with example problems shown before asking for new plan; prompt structure understanding essential for reproducing results
  - Quick check question: How does the presence of the [PLAN END] token in the prompt affect the LLM's completion behavior?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Domain generator → PDDL translator → Prompt builder → LLM → Plan extractor → Validator/Planner → Result aggregator
- Critical path: Prompt → LLM output → Plan extraction → Validation (by validator or LPG)
- Design tradeoffs: Full automation (LLM only) vs. human-assisted vs. LLM + planner repair; accuracy vs. execution speed; template-based translation vs. semantic parsing
- Failure signatures: LLM outputs omitting plan end tag, producing syntactically invalid plans, or generating actions violating preconditions; human confusion when LLM suggestions are incorrect
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reproduce autonomous mode benchmark on small blocksworld instances to measure LLM plan correctness
  2. Feed LLM outputs into LPG and measure repair success rate and edit distance
  3. Conduct human-in-the-loop pilot with 5-10 participants to assess effect of LLM suggestions on plan accuracy and time to completion

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can LLMs be effectively fine-tuned to improve their planning performance in domains like Blocksworld?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] GPT-3 fine-tuned on Blocksworld instances showed modest improvement to 16.4% and 22% success rates for plan generation and optimal planning respectively, but still far from human performance
- Why unresolved: Fine-tuning only tested on Blocksworld with limited dataset and standard hyperparameters; unclear if more extensive fine-tuning with domain-specific datasets and hyperparameter tuning could significantly improve performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing fine-tuning with larger, more diverse domain-specific datasets, different model architectures, and extensive hyperparameter optimization

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of LLMs in planning tasks compare to specialized planning algorithms like Fast Downward when given the same domain knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper compares LLM performance to human planners but doesn't directly compare to specialized planning algorithms; mentions LLM plans can be quickly repaired by sound planners like LPG
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on LLM capabilities in isolation and as heuristic aids, but doesn't benchmark against traditional planning algorithms with complete domain models
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparisons between LLM-generated plans and specialized planners like Fast Downward or LPG on same problems, measuring both success rates and computational efficiency

### Open Question 3
- Question: Do LLMs rely on pattern matching from training data or abstract reasoning when generating plans in disguised domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Tests LLMs on disguised Blocksworld domains (deceptive and randomized) and finds decreased performance, suggesting reliance on semantic understanding of action/predicate names rather than abstract reasoning
- Why unresolved: Experiments show performance degradation but don't definitively prove whether LLMs use semantic cues or have learned abstract planning principles disrupted by disguise
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies testing LLMs on various disguises (semantic, random, syntactic) alongside control experiments isolating semantic versus syntactic understanding, potentially using embeddings to analyze what features LLMs leverage

## Limitations
- Benchmark limited to blocksworld domain, raising questions about generalizability to more complex planning domains
- Human-in-the-loop results show modest improvements that are not statistically significant with small sample size (35 participants)
- Benchmark focuses on symbolic planning in PDDL format, not assessing LLMs' abilities to handle real-world planning scenarios with uncertainty, resource constraints, or multi-agent interactions

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: LLMs can generate plans that are repairable by sound planners (heuristic mode) - robust evidence from edit distance metrics and repair success rates
- **Medium confidence**: LLMs provide modest improvements to human planners in assisted mode - positive results but lack of statistical significance and small sample size limit confidence
- **Low confidence**: LLMs can generate valid plans autonomously - 3% success rate indicates current very limited capability, though different prompting strategies unexplored

## Next Checks
1. **Domain Generalization Test**: Apply benchmark to more complex planning domain (e.g., logistics or satellite scheduling) to assess whether observed patterns hold beyond blocksworld
2. **Prompt Engineering Experiment**: Systematically vary prompt structures, few-shot examples, and plan representation formats to determine if autonomous LLM performance can be significantly improved
3. **Statistical Power Analysis**: Conduct larger human-in-the-loop study with sufficient statistical power to determine whether LLM suggestions provide meaningful improvements to human planning performance
