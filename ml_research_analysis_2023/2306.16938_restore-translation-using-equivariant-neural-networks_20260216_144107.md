# Restore Translation Using Equivariant Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16938
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16938
- Reference count: 40
- Restorer improves CIFAR-10 accuracy from 83.40% to 89.23% on shifted images

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a pre-classifier restorer that recovers translated or rotated inputs to their original forms before classification. The method is based on a theoretical result providing necessary and sufficient conditions for affine operators to be translationally equivariant on tensor spaces. The approach demonstrates significant accuracy improvements on shifted and rotated images across multiple datasets including MNIST, CIFAR-10, and 3D-MNIST.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a pre-classifier restorer that transforms distorted inputs back to their original forms before classification. The core theoretical contribution establishes conditions for translational equivariance on tensor spaces, enabling the design of restoration operations that preserve spatial relationships. For rotated inputs, the method converts Cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates to facilitate rotation restoration. The restorer acts as a preprocessing step that can be integrated with any existing classifier.

## Key Results
- Restorer visually recovers translated inputs to original forms
- CIFAR-10 accuracy improves from 83.40% to 89.23% on images shifted by 4 pixels
- Effective restoration demonstrated on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and 3D-MNIST datasets
- Polar coordinate conversion successfully handles rotated input restoration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The restorer works by leveraging theoretical conditions for translational equivariance on tensor spaces, allowing the network to learn restoration operations that preserve spatial relationships. By converting Cartesian to polar coordinates for rotation handling, the method transforms rotation into a simpler translation-like problem in the polar domain. This theoretical foundation enables the network to learn exact restoration mappings rather than approximate invariant features.
