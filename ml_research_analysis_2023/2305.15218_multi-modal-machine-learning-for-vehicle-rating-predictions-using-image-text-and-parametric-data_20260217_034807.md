# Multi-modal Machine Learning for Vehicle Rating Predictions Using Image, Text, and Parametric Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.15218
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15218
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Multi-modal model's explanatory power is 4% - 12% higher than unimodal models for vehicle rating predictions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a multi-modal machine learning approach for predicting vehicle rating scores by simultaneously learning from parametric specifications, text descriptions, and images. The model achieves 4-12% higher explanatory power compared to unimodal approaches by leveraging complementary information across different data modalities. The authors conduct sensitivity analyses using SHAP to interpret the model and provide actionable insights for vehicle designers and manufacturers. The approach demonstrates that parametric data is most informative, while image data contributes the least to rating predictions.

## Method Summary
The method combines three unimodal models: an MLP for parametric data (302 features), a BERT model for text descriptions, and a VGG16 CNN with attention mechanism for images. These unimodal models are trained separately, then their outputs are concatenated and passed through a prediction layer to produce rating scores. The model is trained on 2,571 vehicles from 2007-2022 using an 80/10/10 split, with batch size 32 and learning rate between 0.001-0.00005. SHAP analysis is employed for interpretability to identify the most influential vehicle features across different rating categories.

## Key Results
- Multi-modal model achieves 4-12% higher R² values compared to unimodal models
- Parametric model outperforms both text and image models, with R² values at least 0.04 higher
- Interior information category has the greatest influence on all score predictions according to SHAP analysis
- Simple concatenation mechanism may not effectively capture complex interactions between modalities, especially for total score prediction

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-modal learning improves prediction accuracy by combining complementary information from parametric, text, and image data
- Mechanism: The model learns richer joint representations by fusing features from different modalities, capturing interactions that single-modality models miss
- Core assumption: Different data modalities contain complementary information about vehicle characteristics that affects rating scores
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the multi-modal model's explanatory power is 4% - 12% higher than that of the unimodal models"
  - [section] "The results suggest that compared to the unimodal models, the joint learning of multi-modal data enables the multi-modal learning models to leverage the complementary features learned from different modalities to better predict the rating scores"
- Break condition: If modalities contain redundant or conflicting information rather than complementary information, the multi-modal approach may not outperform unimodal models

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Parametric data provides the most informative features for rating predictions
- Mechanism: Parametric specifications directly encode vehicle characteristics that correlate with ratings, while text and images provide more subjective or indirect information
- Core assumption: The relationship between vehicle specifications and ratings is stronger and more direct than the relationship between subjective assessments (text/images) and ratings
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The parametric model best predicts all rating scores. Its R2 values are at least 0.04 higher than that of the corresponding image and text models"
  - [section] "The parametric data intuitively shows the detailed specifications of a vehicle...which summarizes the vehicle's major characteristics"
- Break condition: If rating criteria shift to emphasize subjective qualities over objective specifications, or if text/image features become more predictive of ratings

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SHAP analysis provides interpretable feature importance for design optimization
- Mechanism: By analyzing SHAP values across time and feature categories, the model reveals which vehicle features most influence rating predictions
- Core assumption: The SHAP values accurately reflect the true influence of features on model predictions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The SHAP analysis can help us identify the most informative and influential vehicle feature categories from the parametric data"
  - [section] "The findings indicate that the impact of each category varies, with the interior information category having the greatest influence on all score predictions"
- Break condition: If SHAP values are unstable or if the model's feature importance doesn't align with actual design priorities

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pre-processing module → Unimodal models (Parametric-MLP, Text-BERT, Image-CNN) → Feature fusion → Multi-modal prediction layer → Output
- Critical path: Pre-processing → Unimodal model training → Multi-modal model construction → SHAP analysis
- Design tradeoffs: Simple concatenation vs. attention-based fusion (simpler but potentially less effective); single multi-modal model vs. separate models per rating score (more flexible but more complex)
- Failure signatures: Poor R2 values across all models; one modality consistently underperforming others; SHAP analysis showing unstable or counterintuitive feature importance
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train and evaluate the three unimodal models separately to establish baseline performance
  2. Train bi-modal models (Par_Text, Par_Img, Img_Text) to understand which modality combinations are most valuable
  3. Train the full Par_Text_Img multi-modal model and compare performance against all unimodal and bi-modal models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the specific reasons behind the low performance of image-based models in predicting vehicle rating scores, and how can the model be improved to capture more informative features from images?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly mentions that the image data is least informative in predicting the rating scores, with the text model outperforming the image model in most cases.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that the images might be less informative because the exterior design is not directly considered in the five rating scores, but it does not provide a detailed analysis of why the image model underperforms or how it can be improved.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research could involve a detailed analysis of the image data, including identifying which image features are most relevant to the rating scores, and experimenting with different image preprocessing techniques or model architectures to improve performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the multi-modal learning model be further improved to capture complex interactions between different data modalities, especially for predicting the total score?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the simple concatenation mechanism used to fuse information from different data modalities may not be effective in capturing complex interactions, especially for predicting the total score.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the current information fusion mechanism limits the model's performance or what alternative mechanisms could be explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research could involve experimenting with different information fusion mechanisms, such as attention-based or transformer-based methods, and evaluating their impact on the model's performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the dataset be expanded to include more vehicle brands and complete the information on vehicles with missing data items?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the dataset is much smaller than other datasets used to train large deep learning models and that the US News website only provides information on vehicles in the US market, leading to the exclusion of some vehicle brands from China, India, and other countries.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed plan for expanding the dataset or how the inclusion of more vehicle brands and complete information would impact the model's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research could involve identifying additional data sources for vehicle information, developing a strategy for integrating the new data into the existing dataset, and evaluating the impact of the expanded dataset on the model's performance.

## Limitations

- The 4-12% performance improvement is reported for this specific vehicle dataset; generalizability to other domains with different data characteristics remains untested
- No ablation studies quantify the marginal contribution of each modality beyond simple model comparisons
- SHAP interpretability assumes model stability, but sensitivity to training randomness or data splits is not reported

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The architectural framework (MLP/CNN/BERT + fusion) is sound and follows established multi-modal learning practices
- **Medium confidence**: The quantitative performance gains (4-12% R² improvement) are credible but may not generalize across domains
- **Medium confidence**: SHAP-based interpretability findings are methodologically valid but require further validation for design guidance

## Next Checks

1. Conduct ablation studies systematically removing each modality to quantify individual contributions beyond simple model comparisons
2. Test model stability across multiple random data splits and training seeds to ensure SHAP findings are robust
3. Evaluate performance on a held-out validation set from a different time period (e.g., 2023+ vehicles) to assess temporal generalization
