# FiTs: Fine-grained Two-stage Training for Knowledge-aware Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.11799
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11799
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** FiTs achieves state-of-the-art performance on three KAQA benchmarks with only 1% additional trainable parameters

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes FiTs, a fine-grained two-stage training framework for knowledge-aware question answering (KAQA). The framework addresses the challenge of fusing representations from pre-trained language models (PLMs) and knowledge graphs (KGs) by first aligning their representations through knowledge adaptive post-training, then improving joint reasoning ability through knowledge-aware fine-tuning with two auxiliary self-supervised tasks. The approach demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across three benchmark datasets while maintaining parameter efficiency.

## Method Summary
FiTs introduces a two-stage training framework that bridges the semantic and distributional gaps between PLMs and KGs. The first stage uses knowledge adaptive post-training to align representations from both sources. The second stage employs knowledge-aware fine-tuning with two auxiliary self-supervised tasks to enhance the model's joint reasoning capabilities. This fine-grained approach enables effective fusion of PLM and KG knowledge while only introducing 1% additional trainable parameters compared to baseline models.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on CommonsenseQA with 2.6% absolute accuracy improvement
- Demonstrates 1.8% accuracy improvement on OpenbookQA benchmark
- Shows 0.6% improvement on MedQA-USMLE dataset
- Maintains parameter efficiency with only 1% additional trainable parameters

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework's effectiveness stems from its two-stage approach to representation alignment and joint reasoning. The knowledge adaptive post-training stage ensures that PLM and KG representations are compatible before fine-tuning, while the auxiliary self-supervised tasks in the second stage explicitly train the model to reason jointly over both knowledge sources. This sequential approach allows the model to build robust cross-modal representations before being asked to perform complex reasoning tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: PLM -> Knowledge Adaptive Post-training -> Joint Reasoning Module -> Knowledge-aware Fine-tuning

**Critical Path**: The core pipeline flows from PLM initialization through representation alignment to joint reasoning training, with the two auxiliary tasks providing additional supervision during fine-tuning.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The framework trades computational complexity during training for improved accuracy and parameter efficiency. The two-stage approach adds training steps but results in better knowledge fusion.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance may indicate inadequate representation alignment, insufficient knowledge graph coverage, or ineffective auxiliary task design.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out specific open questions, though the limitations section raises important considerations about the framework's generalization to sparse knowledge scenarios and scalability to larger knowledge graphs.

## Limitations

- Performance heavily dependent on quality and coverage of external knowledge graph
- Potential computational overhead from two-stage training process
- Unclear scalability to larger, more complex knowledge graphs
- Limited analysis of semantic and distributional gaps across different KAQA tasks

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Empirical results on three benchmarks are well-documented with clear performance improvements over baselines.

**Medium confidence**: Parameter efficiency claims need verification regarding total computational cost and training time impact.

**Low confidence**: Discussion of semantic and distributional gaps lacks detailed analysis, and scalability considerations are not thoroughly explored.
