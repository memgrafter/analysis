# Explaining the Uncertain: Stochastic Shapley Values for Gaussian Process Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15167
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15167
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces GP-SHAP, a method for explaining Gaussian process models using stochastic Shapley values that propagates posterior uncertainty through the explanation process.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GP-SHAP, a method for explaining Gaussian process models using stochastic Shapley values. Unlike previous approaches, GP-SHAP leverages the full analytical covariance structure of GPs to quantify explanation uncertainties and study statistical dependencies between explanations. The method treats feature explanations as random variables, resulting in tractable covariance functions across features and data observations. GP-SHAP is extended to BayesGP-SHAP, which also accounts for estimation uncertainty, and to predictive explanations using a Shapley prior. Experiments demonstrate that GP-SHAP produces explanations with feature-specific uncertainties that are easier to predict than those from other methods like TreeSHAP and DeepSHAP.

## Method Summary
The paper develops GP-SHAP, a method for explaining Gaussian process models by treating feature explanations as stochastic Shapley values. The approach leverages the GP posterior to compute conditional mean embeddings for each coalition of features, which induces a stochastic cooperative game where payoffs are Gaussian random variables. Applying the weighted least squares Shapley formula yields stochastic Shapley values that are also Gaussian with analytically tractable covariances. The framework is extended to BayesGP-SHAP by incorporating Bayesian weighted least squares to account for estimation uncertainty, and to predictive explanations by fitting a Shapley prior GP over previously computed explanations.

## Key Results
- GP-SHAP produces feature attributions with meaningful variances that reflect the underlying GP uncertainty
- BayesGP-SHAP captures both model uncertainty and estimation uncertainty through hierarchical Bayesian modeling
- The Shapley prior GP enables prediction of explanations for new data without recomputing from the underlying model
- GP-SHAP uncertainties are easier to predict than those from TreeSHAP and DeepSHAP

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Stochastic Shapley values for GPs propagate posterior uncertainty through the explanation process, giving feature attributions with meaningful variances.
- Mechanism: The GP posterior induces a stochastic cooperative game νf where each coalition payoff is a conditional expectation over the GP random function. These payoffs are jointly Gaussian, and applying the weighted least squares Shapley formula yields stochastic Shapley values that are also Gaussian with analytically tractable covariances.
- Core assumption: The GP posterior is well-defined (finite second moments) and its sample paths are integrable over the input space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This paper introduces GP-SHAP, a method for explaining Gaussian process (GP) models using stochastic Shapley values... results in tractable covariance functions across features and data observations."
  - [section 3.1] Theorem 7: "the corresponding stochastic Shapley values ϕ(νf(x, ·)) follows a d-dimensional multivariate Gaussian distribution, ϕ(νf(x, ·)) ∼ N (AE[vx], AV[vx]A⊤)..."
- Break condition: If the GP posterior is degenerate (zero variance) or sample paths are not integrable, the induced stochastic game collapses to deterministic and uncertainty propagation fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: BayesGP-SHAP captures both model uncertainty (from the GP posterior) and estimation uncertainty (from approximating Shapley values via sampling coalitions).
- Mechanism: By combining the Gaussian posterior of the GP with the Bayesian weighted least squares formulation of Shapley value estimation (Slack et al. 2020), the total variance decomposes as VarGP + VarEstimation. The posterior over σ² from BayesSHAP is integrated over the GP predictive distribution.
- Core assumption: Coalition sampling provides an unbiased estimator of Shapley values and the variance s²(·) captures estimation error adequately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] Proposition 11: "the posterior distribution of the stochastic Shapley values can be estimated using the Bayesian WLS approach as, ϕ | σ2, Zℓ, x, D ∼ N (AℓB(x, S))⊤ ˜m(X), AℓB(x, S)⊤ ˜KXXB(x, S)A⊤ℓ + (Z⊤ℓ WℓZℓ)−1σ2..."
  - [corpus] "SHAPNN: Shapley Value Regularized Tabular Neural Network" (suggests uncertainty-aware Shapley extensions exist in literature).
- Break condition: If coalition sampling is too sparse, estimation uncertainty dominates and the posterior over σ² becomes uninformative.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The Shapley prior over explanation functions allows prediction of explanations for unseen data without recomputing Shapley values from the underlying model.
- Mechanism: Treating previously computed Shapley values as regression targets, a vector-valued GP with the derived kernel κ(x,x') is fit. This kernel encodes the linear mapping from coalitions to features via the Shapley operator A and conditional mean embeddings Ψ(x).
- Core assumption: Shapley values for a given model are realizations from a smooth function over input space, so GP regression is appropriate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We further extend our framework to the problem of predictive explanation, and propose a Shapley prior over the explanation function to predict Shapley values for new data based on previously computed ones."
  - [section 4] Proposition 13: "the predictive explanations for new data x′ is distributed as, ϕ(x′) | Dϕ ∼ N ( ˜mϕ(x′), κ (x′, x′) − κ(x′, X)bκ(x′, X))..."
- Break condition: If explanations are highly discontinuous or model-dependent in a non-smooth way, the GP prior assumption fails and predictive performance degrades.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Gaussian Process posterior and conditional expectation embedding
  - Why needed here: GP-SHAP relies on computing E[f(X) | XS = xS] for subsets of features, which is exactly the conditional mean embedding of the GP posterior.
  - Quick check question: Given a GP posterior with mean function m̃ and covariance k̃, what is the closed-form expression for E[f(X) | XS = xS]?

- Concept: Shapley value weighted least squares formulation
  - Why needed here: Both GP-SHAP and BayesGP-SHAP express Shapley values as the solution to a weighted regression problem A = (Z⊤WZ)⁻¹Z⊤W.
  - Quick check question: In the WLS formulation, what is the weight assigned to a coalition S with |S| features in d-dimensional space?

- Concept: Bayesian hierarchical modeling for uncertainty quantification
  - Why needed here: BayesGP-SHAP integrates two sources of uncertainty by treating the estimation error in Shapley values as a latent variable with its own posterior.
  - Quick check question: What is the posterior distribution of the Shapley values in BayesSHAP when the likelihood is N(ϕ⊤z, σ²w(z)⁻¹) and the prior is N(0,σ²I)?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - GP model → posterior mean m̃(x), covariance k̃(x,x')
  - Conditional mean embedding → b(x,S) for each coalition
  - Stochastic cooperative game νf → payoffs vx = [νf(x,S₁),...,νf(x,S₂ᵈ)]⊤
  - Shapley operator A → linear map to stochastic Shapley values
  - Estimation layer → GP-SHAP (predictive) or BayesGP-SHAP (predictive+estimation)
  - Predictive layer (optional) → Shapley prior GP for explanation prediction

- Critical path:
  1. Fit GP posterior on training data.
  2. For each instance x and coalition S, compute b(x,S) = (KXS,XS + λI)⁻¹kS(XS,xS).
  3. Assemble payoff vector vx and compute AE[vx], AV[vx]A⊤.
  4. (BayesGP-SHAP only) Sample σ² from scaled inverse chi-squared and add (Z⊤WℓZℓ)⁻¹σ² to covariance.
  5. (Shapley prior) Train multi-output GP on (xᵢ,ϕᵢ) pairs with kernel κ(x,x')=A(x)⊤A(x').

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Exact vs. sampled coalitions: Exact computation is O(2ᵈ) but sampling reduces cost at the expense of estimation uncertainty.
  - Sparse vs. full GP: Sparse GP reduces computational burden but may lose some posterior detail.
  - Predictive vs. estimation uncertainty: Including both (BayesGP-SHAP) gives more conservative intervals but is computationally heavier.

- Failure signatures:
  - Near-zero posterior variance in GP → GP-SHAP uncertainties collapse to zero.
  - Very small coalition sample size → BayesGP-SHAP variance dominated by estimation error, intervals become too wide.
  - Highly non-smooth explanation surface → Shapley prior GP underfits, predictive RMSE high.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate synthetic data, fit GP, run GP-SHAP, and verify that uncertainties shrink as posterior variance decreases.
  2. Compare GP-SHAP vs. BayesGP-SHAP on same dataset; check that BayesGP-SHAP intervals are wider and include GP-SHAP intervals.
  3. Train Shapley prior GP on explanations from TreeSHAP; evaluate predictive RMSE on held-out instances.

## Limitations
- The approach assumes the GP posterior is well-defined and integrable, which may not hold for degenerate or ill-specified models
- Computational complexity scales exponentially with feature dimension for exact coalition enumeration
- While the paper claims improved uncertainty quantification, no direct comparison is made against existing uncertainty-aware explanation methods on identical benchmarks

## Confidence
- High confidence in the mathematical derivation of stochastic Shapley values and their Gaussian properties, supported by explicit theorem statements and proof sketches
- Medium confidence in the practical utility of BayesGP-SHAP for capturing estimation uncertainty, as experimental validation is limited to synthetic settings
- Low confidence in the scalability claims, as experiments are conducted on datasets with relatively few features (≤10) compared to typical high-dimensional applications
