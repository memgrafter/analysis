# MarsEclipse at SemEval-2023 Task 3: Multi-Lingual and Multi-Label Framing Detection with Contrastive Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14339
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14339
- Reference count: 1
- This paper presents a system that ranked first on the official test set and leaderboard for five of six languages in SemEval-2023 Task 3 Subtask 2 on Framing Detection.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents MarsEclipse, a multi-lingual and multi-label contrastive learning system for framing detection that achieved first place on the SemEval-2023 Task 3 Subtask 2 competition. The system uses XLM-RoBERTa-Large as the backbone encoder with a dual-input architecture that processes both article titles and bodies. A supervised contrastive loss is applied to group examples with similar labels together across languages while pushing apart dissimilar examples, achieving very competitive results across all six languages with training data.

## Method Summary
The system uses XLM-RoBERTa-Large as the backbone encoder with two parallel inputs (title and body) that are concatenated before being passed through the encoder. Two dropout layers generate different views of the encoded representations, which are then processed by two separate heads: one for binary cross-entropy loss and one for supervised contrastive loss. The contrastive loss considers examples with identical labels as positive pairs and uses a weighting function based on label differences for negative pairs. The final predictions use language-specific thresholds determined through brute force optimization on the development set.

## Key Results
- Ranked first on the official test set and leaderboard for five of six languages (English, French, German, Italian, Polish, Russian)
- Achieved competitive results on zero-shot languages (Spanish, Georgian, Greek) with no training data
- Ablation studies showed the effectiveness of the dual-input architecture and multi-label contrastive learning approach

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-label contrastive learning improves robustness in multi-lingual framing detection by grouping similar-label examples together regardless of language.
- Mechanism: The supervised contrastive loss pulls positive example pairs (examples with identical labels) closer in embedding space while pushing apart negative pairs, weighted by the number of label differences. This creates language-agnostic label clusters.
- Core assumption: Label similarity is more important than language similarity for framing detection tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We used a multi-label contrastive loss for fine-tuning large pre-trained language models in a multi-lingual setting, achieving very competitive results"
  - [section 3.2] "we considered two examples to be positive if they shared identical labels" and "We passed ∆ into a weight function W to determine the weight of a negative examples pair"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on other approaches, no direct contrastive learning implementations found
- Break condition: If label distribution is extremely imbalanced such that positive pairs become too rare, the contrastive signal weakens and performance degrades.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using dual inputs (title + body) provides complementary information that improves framing detection performance compared to single-input approaches.
- Mechanism: The title captures the main framing perspective concisely while the body provides detailed context. Concatenating these representations allows the model to leverage both high-level framing signals and supporting evidence.
- Core assumption: Titles and bodies contain complementary rather than redundant framing information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "We believe that the title of an article can often convey the main ideas discussed in the article, and thus can be a valuable source of information for frame detection"
  - [section 5.3] "We found that our system achieved better performance on testing for all six fine-tuned languages" when comparing dual-input vs single-input
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors don't discuss dual-input architectures for framing detection
- Break condition: If titles are misleading or don't reflect article content, the dual-input approach could introduce noise that outweighs the benefits.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using XLM-RoBERTa-Large as the backbone encoder provides better multi-lingual framing detection performance than smaller PLMs.
- Mechanism: Larger models have more parameters to capture complex linguistic patterns across languages, and XLM-RoBERTa-Large's architecture is specifically designed for multi-lingual tasks with good cross-lingual transfer capabilities.
- Core assumption: Model size correlates with multi-lingual task performance when fine-tuned appropriately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2] "The results indicated that XLM-RoBERTa-Large was better for all six fine-tuned languages" when comparing to Multilingual BERT and XLM-RoBERTa-Base
  - [section 3.3] "Our system uses XLM-RoBERTa as the backbone, and implements a multi-lingual and multi-label contrastive model on top of it"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on different approaches rather than encoder comparisons
- Break condition: If computational resources are severely constrained, the performance gains from XLM-RoBERTa-Large may not justify the increased training/inference costs.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multi-label classification
  - Why needed here: Framing detection requires identifying multiple frames per article simultaneously (e.g., an article can be political, economic, and health-related)
  - Quick check question: How does the system handle examples that belong to multiple frame categories simultaneously?

- Concept: Contrastive learning
  - Why needed here: Standard classification loss functions don't explicitly encourage similar-label examples to be close in embedding space, which is beneficial for generalization across languages
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes positive pairs from negative pairs in this multi-label setting?

- Concept: Cross-lingual transfer learning
  - Why needed here: The model needs to work well across 9 languages with varying amounts of training data, requiring knowledge transfer between languages
  - Quick check question: How does the model leverage training data from one language to improve performance on another language with little or no training data?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: XT (title) + XC (body) → Encoder → Dropout → Y1 (contrastive head) and Y2 (classification head) → Loss combiner → Output

- Critical path: XT → Encoder → X1 → Head 2 → Y2 → Predictions (primary path for inference)
  Secondary path: X1/X2 → Head 1 → Contrastive loss (training only)

- Design tradeoffs: 
  - Dual inputs increase computation but improve performance
  - Large PLM improves accuracy but requires more resources
  - Multi-label contrastive loss adds complexity but improves robustness
  - Hard negative weighting improves discrimination but requires careful tuning

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor zero-shot performance suggests contrastive learning isn't generalizing well
  - Language-specific performance gaps indicate training data imbalance issues
  - Overfitting on development sets suggests insufficient regularization
  - Threshold sensitivity indicates unstable probability calibration

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare dual-input vs single-input performance on development set to verify complementary information hypothesis
  2. Test different α values (contrastive loss weight) to find optimal balance between classification and contrastive objectives
  3. Evaluate different negative sample weighting functions W(∆) to optimize hard negative mining effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the proposed system perform if different contrastive loss formulations were used, such as contrastive loss based on semantic similarity rather than exact label matching?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using a supervised contrastive loss with modifications for multi-label classification, considering examples with identical labels as positive pairs.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative contrastive loss formulations that might be more suitable for multi-label classification tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using different contrastive loss formulations and their impact on multi-label classification performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal threshold for each language in the development set, and how does it affect the system's performance on the test set?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using a brute force algorithm to find the optimal threshold for each language in the development set, and using the same thresholds for the test set.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide detailed analysis of the impact of different threshold values on the system's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with different threshold values and their impact on F1 score for each language.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the system's performance vary with different pre-trained language models (PLMs) as the backbone?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using XLM-RoBERTa-Large as the backbone and performing ablation studies with Multilingual BERT and XLM-RoBERTa-Base.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore other PLMs or their impact on the system's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using different PLMs and their impact on multi-label classification performance.

## Limitations
- Several key implementation details remain unspecified, including the exact weighting parameter α, number of training epochs, and temperature parameter τ
- Lack of detailed analysis of performance differences between languages makes it difficult to assess cross-lingual generalization
- No discussion of computational overhead from the dual-input approach or alternative fusion strategies
- Limited baseline comparisons restrict understanding of where performance gains originate

## Confidence

- Multi-label contrastive learning effectiveness: Medium confidence
- Dual-input architecture superiority: Low confidence
- XLM-RoBERTa-Large superiority: Medium confidence
