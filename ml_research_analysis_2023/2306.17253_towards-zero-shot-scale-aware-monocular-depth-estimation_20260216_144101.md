# Towards Zero-Shot Scale-Aware Monocular Depth Estimation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17253
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17253
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: ZeroDepth achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot depth estimation across multiple benchmarks without test-time scaling

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ZeroDepth, a novel monocular depth estimation framework that enables zero-shot transfer of metric scale across datasets with different camera geometries. The key innovation is the use of input-level geometric embeddings and a variational latent representation to learn scale priors anchored in physical 3D properties. By training on large amounts of scaled, labeled data from real-world and synthetic datasets, ZeroDepth achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot depth estimation results on both outdoor (KITTI, DDAD, nuScenes) and indoor (NYUv2) benchmarks, outperforming methods that require in-domain training data and test-time scaling.

## Method Summary
ZeroDepth is a zero-shot monocular depth estimation framework that uses geometric embeddings and a variational latent representation to learn scale priors. The method trains on large-scale datasets with known camera intrinsics and diverse environments, using encoder-level data augmentation to improve robustness. During inference, the model can transfer learned scale priors to new datasets without requiring test-time scaling or fine-tuning. The variational architecture also enables uncertainty estimation through sampling from the latent distribution.

## Key Results
- Achieves AbsRel of 0.100 and RMSE of 4.213 on KITTI without test-time scaling
- Achieves AbsRel of 0.081 and RMSE of 0.338 on NYUv2 without test-time scaling
- Outperforms monodepth2, PackNet, and BTS on multiple benchmarks when fine-tuned on 1/5th of KITTI labels
- Enables uncertainty estimation that can improve performance by selectively removing high-uncertainty pixels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Geometric embeddings allow the model to learn scale priors anchored in physical 3D properties
- Mechanism: By augmenting image features with camera intrinsics and pixel-level viewing directions, the network can reason about the physical size and shape of objects, making predictions more robust to geometric domain gaps
- Core assumption: Physical object properties (size, shape) are more invariant across camera geometries than appearance features alone
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This is achieved by (i) the use of input-level geometric embeddings that enable the network to learn a scale prior over objects"
  - [section 3.3]: "We augment image embeddings with camera information, as a way to generate geometry-aware features capable of reasoning over the physical shape of objects"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence for geometric embedding scale transfer
- Break condition: If camera intrinsics are inaccurate or unavailable, the geometric embeddings become unreliable and scale transfer fails

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Decoupling encoder and decoder via a variational latent representation enables robust zero-shot transfer
- Mechanism: The variational latent representation learns a compressed, probabilistic encoding of depth information that can be sampled and decoded independently of the original encoding, allowing for robust transfer across domains
- Core assumption: A probabilistic latent representation can capture the uncertainty and variability needed for zero-shot transfer better than a deterministic encoding
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This is achieved by... (ii) decoupling the encoder and decoder stages, via a variational latent representation that is conditioned on single frame information"
  - [section 3.4]: "After conditioning RC on input embeddings E = EI ⊕ E G, a Nl × Dl sampled latent representation RS is generated by sampling from N (µc, σc)"
  - [section 4.4]: "As expected, reducing Nl leads to a roughly linear degradation, although even with Nl = 32 we still achieve performance comparable with monodepth2"
- Break condition: If the latent representation becomes too small or simplified, it cannot capture the necessary depth information for accurate predictions

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Encoder-level data augmentation improves robustness to both appearance and geometric domain gaps
- Mechanism: By randomly resizing images, jittering ray directions, and dropping embeddings during training, the model learns to handle a wide range of input variations and becomes more robust to domain shifts
- Core assumption: Exposing the model to diverse training data variations during training will improve its ability to generalize to unseen domains
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.5]: "We take advantage of this property to decode estimates using only geometric embeddings, and empirically show that this leads to improvements over standard encoder-decoder architectures"
  - [section 4.4]: "We also ablate the impact of different training datasets in the final performance. In Figure 6 we show results when removing each of the4 outdoor datasets"
  - [section 4.4]: "Here we evaluate the quality of uncertainty estimates generated by our variational architecture. In Figure 7 we show results using different percentages of valid depth pixels"
- Break condition: If augmentation is too aggressive or not properly balanced, it may hurt performance by introducing too much noise or unrealistic variations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image + Camera Intrinsics -> ResNet18 + Geometric Embeddings -> Variational Latent Representation -> MLP Decoder -> Depth Map
- Critical path: Image → ResNet18 features → Geometric embeddings → Latent representation → Depth map
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Complexity vs. performance: Larger latent representations improve performance but increase computational cost
  - Augmentation strength: More aggressive augmentation improves robustness but may hurt performance if too extreme
  - Probabilistic vs. deterministic: Variational representation enables uncertainty estimation but adds complexity
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor scale transfer: Check geometric embeddings and camera intrinsics
  - High uncertainty: Check latent representation size and data augmentation
  - Blurry or incorrect depth: Check encoder features and decoder architecture

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the performance of ZeroDepth vary when using different numbers of samples for uncertainty estimation during inference?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that using multiple samples from the variational latent representation can statistically approximate per-pixel depth uncertainty, and that this can be used to improve performance by selectively removing pixels with high uncertainty values
  - Why unresolved: The paper only provides results for a specific number of samples (10 depth maps) and does not explore the trade-off between the number of samples and the accuracy of the uncertainty estimates
  - What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study of ZeroDepth's performance with varying numbers of samples for uncertainty estimation, showing the impact on accuracy and computational cost

- Question: How does ZeroDepth perform on datasets with significantly different camera intrinsics compared to the training data?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper claims that ZeroDepth can transfer metric scale across datasets with different camera geometries, but it does not provide results for datasets with vastly different camera parameters
  - Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates ZeroDepth on datasets with camera intrinsics that are relatively similar to the training data, and does not test its robustness to extreme variations in camera parameters
  - What evidence would resolve it: Evaluation of ZeroDepth on datasets with camera intrinsics that are significantly different from the training data, such as fisheye or ultra-wide-angle lenses, to assess its generalization capabilities

- Question: How does the performance of ZeroDepth compare to other methods that use test-time scaling for metric depth estimation?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper claims that ZeroDepth outperforms methods that require test-time scaling to produce metric estimates, but it does not provide a direct comparison with these methods
  - Why unresolved: The paper only compares ZeroDepth to methods that use self-supervision in the target dataset or rely on median-scaling at test time, but not to methods that use test-time scaling with ground truth depth maps
  - What evidence would resolve it: A direct comparison of ZeroDepth's performance with methods that use test-time scaling for metric depth estimation, using the same evaluation protocol and datasets

## Limitations

- Dataset dependency and generalization: Performance heavily depends on diversity and quality of training datasets, with uncertainty about generalization to new camera geometries
- Computational cost: Requires significant resources (8 A100 GPUs for ~7 days), limiting accessibility and practical applicability
- Geometric embedding assumptions: Effectiveness relies on accurate camera intrinsics and assumption that physical object properties are more invariant than appearance features

## Confidence

- High confidence: The core innovation of using geometric embeddings and variational latent representations for zero-shot depth estimation is well-supported by experimental results
- Medium confidence: Claims about improved robustness to domain gaps and ability to estimate uncertainty are supported but could benefit from more extensive analysis
- Low confidence: Paper doesn't provide thorough analysis of failure modes or limitations, with limited discussion of when and why the method might fail
