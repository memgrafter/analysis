# Interpretable Architecture Neural Networks for Function Visualization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.03393
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03393
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: Introduces IANN to simultaneously visualize all input variable effects in black-box functions via hierarchical decomposition.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental challenge of visualizing the effects of all input variables on black-box functions in scientific research. Traditional tools like partial dependence and ICE plots are limited to one or two variables at a time, leading to oversimplified interpretations. The authors propose interpretable architecture neural networks (IANN) that decompose complex functions into hierarchical structures of interpretable sub-functions, enabling simultaneous visualization of all input variable interactions. The approach introduces two specific architectures: original variable hierarchical (OVH) and disjoint active subspace hierarchical (DASH). Validated on benchmark functions including borehole and harmonic wave functions, IANN achieves exceptionally high test R² values (99.99% for borehole), demonstrating both accuracy and interpretability while providing clearer insights into variable interactions than existing methods.

## Method Summary
The IANN approach transforms black-box functions into interpretable neural network architectures that preserve functional relationships while enabling hierarchical visualization. The method decomposes complex functions into sub-functions organized in tree-like structures where each node represents an interpretable operation. Two specific architectures are proposed: OVH maintains the original variable hierarchy while DASH uses active subspace decomposition to separate variable interactions. The networks are trained to approximate the target function while maintaining architectural constraints that ensure interpretability. Once trained, the hierarchical structure enables 3D visualizations that simultaneously show effects of all input variables and their interactions, overcoming limitations of traditional visualization tools that examine only one or two variables at a time.

## Key Results
- IANN achieves 99.99% test R² accuracy on the borehole benchmark function, demonstrating exceptional approximation capability.
- The method successfully visualizes all input variable effects simultaneously, capturing complex interactions that traditional PD and ICE plots miss.
- Two architectures (OVH and DASH) are validated, with both showing high accuracy and providing interpretable hierarchical visualizations of variable effects.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
IANN works by enforcing architectural constraints during neural network training that naturally decompose functions into interpretable sub-components. The hierarchical structure mirrors the natural variable interactions in the target function, allowing each sub-function to capture specific variable relationships. By training with interpretability constraints alongside accuracy objectives, the network learns representations that are both faithful to the original function and structurally meaningful. The decomposition into sub-functions enables the visualization of joint effects and interactions that would be obscured when examining variables independently, as is required by traditional visualization methods.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Input variables -> Variable selection/gating -> Sub-function decomposition (OVH or DASH) -> Hierarchical composition -> Output prediction/visualization

**Critical Path**
The critical path flows from input through the hierarchical decomposition to output, with each level of the hierarchy capturing progressively more complex variable interactions. The visualization generation occurs after network training by traversing the hierarchical structure to extract sub-function contributions.

**Design Tradeoffs**
The main tradeoff is between architectural simplicity (easier interpretation) and modeling capacity (ability to capture complex interactions). OVH maintains simpler, more interpretable structures but may struggle with complex non-hierarchical interactions, while DASH can capture more complex relationships but at the cost of increased structural complexity. The choice between them depends on the expected interaction structure of the target function.

**Failure Signatures**
Poor performance occurs when the true function has interaction structures that don't align with either hierarchical assumption, leading to forced decompositions that obscure rather than reveal relationships. Additionally, high-dimensional inputs may cause the hierarchical structure to become too deep, making visualization difficult to interpret despite maintaining interpretability in principle.

## Limitations
- Scalability concerns exist for high-dimensional datasets with hundreds of variables, where hierarchical decomposition may become computationally prohibitive.
- The approach requires validation on real-world scientific datasets with inherent noise and non-smooth behaviors, as current results focus on benchmark functions.
- The method's sensitivity to hyperparameter choices and training procedures requires more thorough exploration to ensure robust performance across different function types.

## Confidence

**Major Claims**
- Simultaneous visualization of all input variables: **High** for demonstrated cases, **Medium** for general applicability
- Superior interpretability over PD/ICE methods: **Medium** (lacks comparative studies with real-world noisy data)
- High accuracy on benchmark functions: **High** (99.99% R² on borehole demonstrates strong performance)
