# Integrating Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory with Subword Embedding for Authorship Attribution

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14933
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14933
- Reference count: 39
- The study achieves accuracy improvements of 1.07% on CCAT50 and 0.96% on Twitter datasets compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel authorship attribution method that combines bidirectional long short-term memory (BLSTM) networks with 2D convolutional neural networks (CNN) and byte-pair encoding (BPE) for subword embedding. The approach addresses limitations of traditional word-based and character-based methods by capturing both sequential relationships and local syntactic information through subword units. Experiments on four benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed model achieves state-of-the-art results in authorship attribution tasks, particularly improving accuracy on datasets with short text documents.

## Method Summary
The proposed method processes text documents through BPE tokenization to create subword embeddings, which are then fed into BLSTM layers to capture bidirectional sequential context. The BLSTM output is transformed into a matrix and passed through 2D CNN layers with max-pooling to extract local syntactic patterns. The resulting features are flattened and passed through fully connected layers with dropout regularization before classification. The model uses Adam optimization with learning rate 0.0001, Gaussian noise regularization, and ℓ2 regularization. Training is performed with 10-fold cross-validation on datasets containing documents from multiple authors.

## Key Results
- Accuracy improvement of 1.07% on CCAT50 dataset compared to state-of-the-art methods
- Accuracy improvement of 0.96% on Twitter50 dataset
- Comparable performance to state-of-the-art on IMDb62 and Blog50 datasets
- Effective handling of morphological variance through subword embeddings
- Robust performance across datasets with varying text lengths

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) subword tokenization resolves vocabulary sparsity and captures morphological patterns for authorship attribution.
- Mechanism: BPE iteratively merges frequent character pairs to form subword units, balancing character-level granularity with word-level semantics, thereby reducing vocabulary size and handling unseen or morphologically rich words.
- Core assumption: Frequent subword units reflect authorial style better than fixed vocabulary words or characters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The byte-pair-encoding (BPE) compression algorithm is used to represent sentences in a large corpus of data with a small set of subword units to deal with imaginary word difficulties and split the writing into characters, bytes, the frequency, and merge for the AA tasks."
  - [section] "BPE is a tokenization technique adopted in machine translation to deal with imaginary word problems or hidden writing in each text. It is unsupervised and requires no information about the author."
- Break condition: If subword vocabulary becomes too large, it may lose the benefit of reduced dimensionality and computational efficiency.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Bidirectional LSTM (BLSTM) captures sequential dependencies in both forward and backward directions, encoding long-range stylistic patterns.
- Mechanism: BLSTM processes subword embeddings in time steps, combining past and future context to model sequential relationships in writing style.
- Core assumption: Authorship style is encoded in sequential patterns that benefit from bidirectional context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The BLSTM was used to obtain the sequential relationship among characteristics using subword information."
  - [section] "BLSTM is used to capture past and future writing style information... the model is able to exploit information from both the past and the future."
- Break condition: If input sequences are too short, bidirectional context may not provide additional information over unidirectional LSTM.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: 2D Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) with max-pooling extract local syntactic patterns and spatial features from BLSTM output.
- Mechanism: CNN filters slide over BLSTM feature maps to detect local patterns, while max-pooling reduces dimensionality and captures dominant features.
- Core assumption: Stylistic features are encoded in local spatial patterns that can be captured by convolution operations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The 2-dimensional CNN was applied to understand the local syntactical position of the style from unlabeled input text."
  - [section] "narrow convolution is utilized to extract local features information over H... 2D max pooling operation p ∈ R(p1×p2) is utilized to obtain a fixed length vector."
- Break condition: If filter sizes are not appropriate for the feature map dimensions, local patterns may be missed or noise may be introduced.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input text → BPE tokenization → Subword embeddings → BLSTM layers → 2D CNN + Max-pooling → Fully connected layer → Softmax classifier
- Critical path: BPE → BLSTM → CNN → Classification
- Design tradeoffs:
  - BPE vs character/word tokenization: BPE balances vocabulary size and morphological coverage.
  - BLSTM vs CNN for sequence modeling: BLSTM captures long-range dependencies; CNN captures local patterns.
  - Filter sizes and pooling: Must match feature map dimensions for effective local pattern extraction.
- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: High training accuracy but low test accuracy, especially with small datasets.
  - Underfitting: Low accuracy on both training and test sets, possibly due to insufficient model capacity or poor feature extraction.
  - Vanishing/exploding gradients: Poor training convergence in BLSTM layers.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare BPE subword vocabulary sizes (e.g., 5000, 10000, 20000) and their impact on accuracy and training time.
  2. Test BLSTM with different hidden unit sizes (e.g., 64, 128, 256) to find optimal capacity for sequence modeling.
  3. Experiment with different CNN filter sizes (e.g., (3,3), (5,5)) and pooling sizes to optimize local pattern extraction.

## Limitations
- The evaluation metrics are limited to accuracy without reporting precision, recall, or F1-scores, making it difficult to assess model performance on imbalanced authorship attribution tasks.
- The study lacks ablation experiments to isolate the contribution of each component (BPE, BLSTM, CNN) to the reported improvements.
- The paper does not specify the exact BPE merge operation count used for subword segmentation, which is critical for reproducibility.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The effectiveness of combining BLSTM and CNN for sequential and local pattern extraction in authorship attribution
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that BPE subword embeddings specifically improve morphological pattern capture compared to other tokenization methods
- **Low confidence**: The reported accuracy improvements without error analysis or comparison of feature importance
