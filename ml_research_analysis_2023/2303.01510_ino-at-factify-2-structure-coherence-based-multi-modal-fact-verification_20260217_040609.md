# INO at Factify 2: Structure Coherence based Multi-Modal Fact Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01510
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01510
- Reference count: 32
- Achieved 2nd place in FACTIFY2 challenge with 0.8079 weighted F1 score

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a multi-modal fact verification system that secured second place in the FACTIFY2 challenge at AAAI2023. The approach focuses on analyzing structural coherence between claims and supporting documents across four key dimensions: sentence length, vocabulary similarity, semantic similarity, and image similarity. By combining handcrafted features with deep learning embeddings, the system effectively detects fake news through cross-modal consistency analysis.

## Method Summary
The proposed method extracts features from both textual and visual components of news articles. Text features are generated using CLIP and Sentence BERT models, while image features come from a pre-trained ResNet50. Additional handcrafted features include ROUGE scores and text length metrics. These diverse features are concatenated and classified using a random forest classifier, which makes the final determination about factuality based on structural coherence patterns.

## Key Results
- Achieved 2nd place in FACTIFY2 challenge with weighted F1 score of 0.8079
- Evaluated on 50,000 samples from Indian and US news sources
- Five balanced categories for classification in the test dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method leverages structural coherence as a proxy for authenticity - genuine news articles tend to maintain consistent structural patterns across text length, vocabulary usage, semantic meaning, and visual content. By quantifying these coherence dimensions and combining them with deep learning embeddings, the system captures both explicit patterns and semantic relationships that distinguish real from fake news.

## Foundational Learning
- **Structural coherence analysis**: Measuring consistency across multiple dimensions helps identify artificial patterns typical of fake news
  - Why needed: Fake news often exhibits inconsistent structure between claims and supporting content
  - Quick check: Compare coherence scores between verified authentic and known fake news samples

- **Multi-modal feature fusion**: Combining text embeddings (CLIP, SBERT) with image features (ResNet50) captures complementary information
  - Why needed: Fake news detection requires understanding both textual claims and visual evidence
  - Quick check: Evaluate individual modality performance vs. combined approach

- **Random forest classification**: Provides interpretable decisions while handling diverse feature types effectively
  - Why needed: Complex feature space requires robust classification that can handle non-linear relationships
  - Quick check: Compare feature importance scores to understand which coherence dimensions drive predictions

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Claim text -> CLIP/SBERT feature extraction -> Feature concatenation
Document text -> CLIP/SBERT feature extraction -> Feature concatenation
Images -> ResNet50 feature extraction -> Feature concatenation
Handcrafted features (ROUGE, text length) -> Feature concatenation -> Random Forest Classifier -> Factuality prediction

Critical Path:
Feature extraction (CLIP, SBERT, ResNet50) -> Feature concatenation -> Random Forest classification

Design Tradeoffs:
- Chosen random forest for interpretability over potentially more accurate but less explainable deep learning models
- Combined handcrafted features with deep learning to leverage domain expertise while capturing complex patterns
- Focused on structural coherence rather than content-specific fact-checking to enable general applicability

Failure Signatures:
- Poor performance on articles with inconsistent image-text relationships
- Reduced accuracy when vocabulary patterns are deliberately manipulated to appear authentic
- Degradation on cross-dataset examples with different structural norms

First Experiments:
1. Test feature ablation by removing each coherence dimension individually
2. Evaluate performance on mismatched image-text pairs to validate coherence detection

## Limitations
- Reliance on handcrafted feature engineering may miss complex cross-modal interactions better captured by end-to-end transformer architectures
- Random forest classifier may underperform compared to modern multimodal transformer approaches
- Single dataset evaluation limits understanding of generalization across diverse fake news domains
- Absence of detailed ablation studies prevents quantifying individual contribution of each coherence dimension

## Confidence
Technical approach: Medium
- Established methods used but lack of comparative analysis with state-of-the-art baselines

Results: Medium
- Specific dataset performance demonstrated but generalization claims remain uncertain without cross-dataset validation
