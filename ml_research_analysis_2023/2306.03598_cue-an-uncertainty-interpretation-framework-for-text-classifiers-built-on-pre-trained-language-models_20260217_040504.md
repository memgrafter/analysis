# CUE: An Uncertainty Interpretation Framework for Text Classifiers Built on Pre-Trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03598
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03598
- Reference count: 33
- Key outcome: Reduces model calibration error and identifies tokens causing uncertainty through latent space perturbations

## Executive Summary
CUE introduces a novel framework for interpreting predictive uncertainty in PLM text classifiers by perturbing latent representations. The method uses a VAE to encode PLM representations, applies controlled Gaussian noise to increase entropy while preserving labels, and identifies input tokens contributing to uncertainty through reconstruction error analysis. Experiments across four datasets and four PLMs demonstrate significant reductions in Expected Calibration Error and provide interpretable uncertainty attributions.

## Method Summary
CUE operates in three phases: first fine-tuning a PLM classifier on the target dataset, then freezing the PLM and training a VAE-based CUE module with reconstruction loss, KL divergence, entropy increment, and orthogonality constraints, and finally using the trained model for inference with uncertainty interpretation. The framework perturbs latent representations to increase predictive entropy while maintaining label consistency, enabling both uncertainty quantification and feature identification through reconstruction error analysis.

## Key Results
- Achieves lower Expected Calibration Error (ECE) compared to baselines across all four datasets
- Reduces average entropy while maintaining classification accuracy
- Successfully identifies input tokens contributing to predictive uncertainty through the UFI algorithm

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CUE reduces epistemic uncertainty by perturbing latent representations while preserving predicted labels.
- **Mechanism:** The framework uses a VAE to encode PLM representations into a latent space, then applies controlled Gaussian noise to increase entropy of the output distribution while maintaining label consistency.
- **Core assumption:** Adding isotropic noise to latent representations will increase aleatoric uncertainty in a way that correlates with epistemic uncertainty reduction.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We then generate text representations by perturbing the latent space which causes fluctuation in predictive uncertainty." [section 4.1] "We can perturb the PLM-encoded representations in the latent space to increase the aleatoric uncertainty (data uncertainty) along some isotropic directions while preserving the predictive labels."
- **Break condition:** If the perturbation significantly alters the semantic meaning or moves representations outside the data manifold, label preservation fails and the method breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Input feature identification is achieved through reconstruction error analysis in the latent space.
- **Mechanism:** By comparing original and perturbed representations, CUE identifies which latent dimensions contribute most to uncertainty changes. These dimensions are then traced back to input tokens via the decoder's linear decomposition.
- **Core assumption:** The inner product between reconstruction difference and decoder outputs can reliably rank latent dimensions by their influence on uncertainty.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.2] "⟨∆ ei, ∆ ei⟩=⟨Σ dim d=1qϕ(zid|ei)·rzid−ei, ∆ ei⟩... the inner product of⟨rzid, ∆ei⟩, which dominates the norm value of||∆ei|| in the d-th dimension can be used to measure predictive uncertainty"
- **Break condition:** If the decoder's linear mapping doesn't accurately represent the relationship between latent dimensions and input tokens, the token identification becomes unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Orthogonal regularization stabilizes training and improves interpretability of the latent space.
- **Mechanism:** By enforcing orthogonality on the decoder weights, CUE ensures that latent dimensions remain independent, making it easier to attribute uncertainty to specific dimensions and trace them back to input features.
- **Core assumption:** Orthogonal latent dimensions lead to more interpretable uncertainty attribution and better reconstruction stability.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section 4.1] "we incorporate an orthogonality constraint within the decoder to encourage independence among dimensions of the latent variable" [section 5.2] "The PLMs fine-tuned with Eq. (8) outperform the counterparts without the orthogonality regularisation in ECE and average entropy on all four datasets"
- **Break condition:** If orthogonality constraints are too restrictive, they may prevent the model from learning useful representations, reducing both performance and interpretability.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** PLM Encoder → CUE Module (VAE Encoder + Decoder + Uncertainty Analysis) → Classification Layer
- **Critical path:** 1. PLM encodes input text to representation 2. CUE VAE encodes representation to latent space 3. Gaussian noise added to latent representation 4. CUE decoder reconstructs perturbed representation 5. Classification layer predicts from reconstructed representation 6. Uncertainty analysis compares original vs. perturbed predictions
- **Design tradeoffs:** Latent space dimensionality vs. reconstruction quality; Noise magnitude vs. label preservation; Orthogonality strength vs. representational capacity; Training time vs. uncertainty reduction effectiveness
- **Failure signatures:** High reconstruction error indicates perturbation is too aggressive; Loss of label consistency indicates noise is breaking semantic meaning; Unstable training suggests orthogonality or other regularization is misconfigured; Low entropy changes indicate perturbation mechanism isn't working
- **First 3 experiments:** 1. Train CUE with different latent space dimensions (e.g., 50, 100, 200) and measure reconstruction quality vs. uncertainty reduction 2. Test different noise distributions (Gaussian vs. uniform) and magnitudes to find optimal perturbation settings 3. Compare performance with and without orthogonality regularization to validate its importance for interpretability

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness heavily depends on the quality of the latent space learned by the VAE and the stability of the perturbation mechanism.
- The reconstruction error-based token identification assumes a linear relationship between latent dimensions and input features, which may not hold for complex PLM representations.
- The method's reliance on specific threshold values (α) for token identification introduces hyperparameter sensitivity that isn't fully explored.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The framework's ability to reduce Expected Calibration Error (ECE) is well-supported by experimental results across multiple datasets and PLMs. The basic mechanism of perturbing latent representations to increase uncertainty is theoretically sound.
- **Medium confidence**: The input feature identification mechanism through reconstruction error analysis is plausible but lacks direct corpus validation. The relationship between latent dimension influence and actual input token contribution needs more rigorous testing.
- **Low confidence**: The orthogonality regularization's specific contribution to uncertainty interpretation is not well-established. The theoretical justification for why orthogonal latent dimensions improve interpretability is weak.

## Next Checks

3. **Alternative reconstruction methods**: Compare the reconstruction error-based token identification with gradient-based attribution methods to validate whether the latent space approach provides unique insights or redundant information.
