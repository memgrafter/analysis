# Learning Data Representations with Joint Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13622
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13622
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Joint diffusion model outperforms state-of-the-art hybrid approaches in classification accuracy, generation quality, and semi-supervised learning tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a joint diffusion model that learns meaningful internal representations suitable for both generative and predictive tasks. The key innovation is sharing the UNet encoder between a diffusion-based generative model and a classifier, enabling stable end-to-end training. This approach achieves superior performance across various tasks including generative modeling, semi-supervised classification, and domain adaptation, outperforming previous hybrid models on standard benchmarks like CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, FashionMNIST, and CelebA.

## Method Summary
The method introduces a joint diffusion model where the encoder part of a UNet architecture is shared between the diffusion model and classifier. During training, the classifier optimizes the encoder to produce features useful for prediction while the diffusion model optimizes it for denoising and generation. The model learns features of varying granularity across diffusion timesteps - high-grained features like hair color emerge at late diffusion steps while low-grained features like object presence appear at early steps. Conditional sampling is introduced where classifier gradients guide the generation process toward specific classes while maintaining generation quality.

## Key Results
- Classification accuracy reaches up to 89.9% on CIFAR-10
- Improved generation fidelity with better FID scores compared to prior models
- Strong performance in semi-supervised learning and domain adaptation tasks
- Conditional sampling enables counterfactual explanations by guiding generation using classifier gradients

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Shared parameterization between diffusion model and classifier improves both tasks by allowing each to benefit from representations learned for the other
- Core assumption: Representations useful for denoising/generation are also useful for prediction, and vice versa
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions shared parameterization enables stable end-to-end training; results show superior performance across tasks
- Break condition: If representations for denoising/generation fundamentally differ from those for prediction, shared parameterization would create suboptimal compromise

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Diffusion process learns features of increasing granularity at different timesteps, leveraged for different predictive tasks
- Core assumption: Denoising process at each timestep captures meaningful features at specific granularity levels
- Evidence anchors: Paper states high-grained features emerge at late steps and low-grained features at early steps; empirical analysis shows varying granularity across timesteps
- Break condition: If denoising at each timestep doesn't capture meaningful features or these features aren't useful for prediction

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Conditional sampling using classifier gradients guides generation toward specific classes while maintaining quality
- Core assumption: Classifier provides useful gradients that steer generation without destroying quality
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions conditional sampling for counterfactual explanations; paper proposes simplifying classifier guidance technique
- Break condition: If classifier gradients aren't useful for steering or destroy generation quality

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input -> Shared UNet encoder -> UNet decoder (for generation) / MLP classifier (for prediction) -> Output
- Critical path: Shared encoder is most critical as key innovation enabling joint learning; training process jointly optimizing diffusion model and classifier while sharing encoder is also critical
- Design tradeoffs: Shared encoder enables efficient learning of representations for both tasks but requires compromise between tasks rather than optimization for one; conditional sampling enables guided generation but adds complexity and requires well-trained classifier
- Failure signatures: Poor classifier accuracy indicates shared encoder not learning good prediction representations; high FID score indicates shared encoder not learning good generation representations; inconsistent conditional sampling indicates classifier not providing useful gradients
- First 3 experiments: 1) Train joint model on FashionMNIST and evaluate classifier accuracy and FID score; 2) Extract representations at different timesteps and train classifiers to verify multi-granularity learning; 3) Use conditional sampling to generate images of specific classes and evaluate consistency

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does granularity of features captured by diffusion models at different timesteps affect predictive task performance?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - paper discusses diffusion models learning features of varying granularity at different timesteps
- Why unresolved: Paper provides empirical evidence of feature granularity differences but doesn't quantify how granularity directly impacts predictive performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed study comparing predictive performance of classifiers using features from different timesteps and correlating with feature granularity

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are optimal conditions and hyperparameters for conditional sampling to maximize precision and recall?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - paper mentions increasing step size leads to more precise but less diverse samples, suggesting sweet spot around α ∈ [100, 250]
- Why unresolved: Paper provides insights into step size effects but doesn't explore other hyperparameters or conditions optimizing precision-recall balance
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive grid search or optimization study over various hyperparameters to find optimal settings for different datasets and tasks

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does joint diffusion model perform on other generative tasks beyond image synthesis, such as text or audio generation?
- Basis in paper: Inferred - paper focuses on image-based tasks and doesn't explore other domains
- Why unresolved: Experiments limited to image datasets with no evidence of applicability to other data types
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending joint diffusion model to text or audio datasets and evaluating performance on tasks like text generation or speech synthesis

## Limitations
- Performance comparisons rely heavily on prior hybrid models with undisclosed architectural details and hyperparameter choices
- Shared parameterization assumes compatible feature spaces between generative and predictive tasks that may not hold across all domains
- Conditional sampling depends on classifier quality during generation, creating potential feedback loops where classifier errors propagate into generated samples
- Empirical analysis focuses primarily on standard vision benchmarks, limiting generalizability to other data modalities

## Confidence
- Shared parameterization benefits: Medium - supported by empirical results but mechanism explanation is indirect
- Multi-timestep feature learning: Medium - qualitative observations exist but quantitative validation is limited
- Conditional sampling effectiveness: Low-Medium - demonstrated but not thoroughly ablated
- Overall performance claims: Medium - strong benchmark results but direct comparison methodology unclear
