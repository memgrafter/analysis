# Evaluation of Induced Expert Knowledge in Causal Structure Learning by NOTEARS

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.01817
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01817
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: Expert knowledge correcting model mistakes significantly improves causal structure learning

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates how expert knowledge impacts causal structure learning using the NOTEARS framework. The authors extend NOTEARS-MLP to incorporate causal knowledge as optimization constraints and propose an iterative knowledge induction process that uses model mistakes to guide constraint generation. Through experiments on synthetic and real-world data, they find that knowledge correcting model errors leads to statistically significant improvements, with constraints on active edges having larger positive impact than inactive edges. Surprisingly, induced knowledge does not correct more incorrect edges than expected on average.

## Method Summary
The method extends the NOTEARS-MLP framework by incorporating expert knowledge as additional constraints in the optimization problem. An iterative knowledge induction process is proposed where constraints are generated based on the model's mistakes in previous optimization steps. The framework is evaluated on synthetic data generated using nonlinear data generating processes with index models, as well as real-world biological data from Sachs et al. (2005). Performance is measured using metrics including False Discovery Rate, True Positive Rate, False Positive Rate, and Structural Hamming Distance.

## Key Results
- Knowledge that corrects model mistakes leads to statistically significant improvements in causal structure discovery
- Constraints on active edges have larger positive impact on causal discovery than constraints on inactive edges
- Induced knowledge does not correct on average more incorrect edges than expected

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Incorporating expert knowledge that corrects the model's mistakes leads to statistically significant improvements in causal structure discovery.
- Mechanism: The iterative knowledge induction process uses the model's misclassifications as feedback to guide constraint generation. By adding constraints that enforce the correction of these mistakes, the model's search space is effectively pruned to favor the true causal structure.
- Core assumption: The expert knowledge provided is correct and aligns with the true underlying causal structure.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data preprocessing → NOTEARS-MLP model → Expert knowledge constraints → Iterative optimization loop → Performance evaluation
  - Key components: MLP architecture, augmented Lagrangian optimization, constraint formulation, knowledge induction logic

- Critical path:
  - Model training → Mistake identification → Knowledge selection → Constraint formulation → Re-optimization → Performance assessment
  - Bottleneck: Constraint formulation and ensuring the augmented Lagrangian method converges with additional constraints

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Hard vs soft constraints: The paper uses hard constraints assuming perfect expert knowledge, but this could be relaxed for uncertain priors
  - Batch vs sequential knowledge induction: Sequential approach allows measuring individual constraint impact but may be slower than batch updates
  - Regularization strength: Higher regularization may improve generalization but could miss true edges, affecting knowledge induction quality

- Failure signatures:
  - Convergence issues in augmented Lagrangian optimization with many constraints
  - Performance degradation when expert knowledge is incorrect or contradictory
  - Overfitting to specific knowledge instances rather than learning general causal patterns

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the impact of induced knowledge vary with different levels of confidence in the prior knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that in their study they adopted hard constraints to accommodate the prior knowledge since they assumed their priors to be correct. An interesting future direction would be to accommodate the continuous optimization with functionality to allow different levels of confidence on the priors.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers the case where the prior knowledge is assumed to be correct. It does not explore how the impact of induced knowledge would change if the prior knowledge had different levels of confidence associated with it.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that vary the confidence levels of the prior knowledge and measure the impact on causal structure learning performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of NOTEARS-MLP change with different regularization parameters when incorporating induced knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that they noticed the performance gets worse with highly regularized models. This is consistent with observations by Ng et al. where sparse DAGs result in missing some of the true active edges.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how different regularization parameters affect the performance of NOTEARS-MLP when incorporating induced knowledge.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that vary the regularization parameter and measure the impact on causal structure learning performance with and without induced knowledge.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the knowledge induction process scale with larger causal networks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that in practice, human assessment and validation process often take place in an iterative or sequential manner. In structure learning, this is more realistic for a sufficiently large causal network where one can learn, validate, and induce newly formed knowledge-set in the learning process following sequential feedback loops.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides experiments with relatively small causal networks (up to 20 nodes). It does not explore how the knowledge induction process would scale with larger causal networks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that evaluate the knowledge induction process with larger causal networks and measure the impact on causal structure learning performance.

## Limitations

- The framework assumes perfect expert knowledge through hard constraints, which may not reflect real-world scenarios where expert knowledge often contains uncertainty or contradictions.
- The study only tests on synthetic data with nonlinear index models and a single biological dataset, limiting generalizability across different domains.
- Computational complexity of the augmented Lagrangian method with multiple constraints may limit scalability to larger causal graphs.

## Confidence

- Medium confidence in core claims about expert knowledge improving causal discovery, as results are statistically significant but based on limited datasets.
- Low confidence in generalizability across domains, as only nonlinear index models and one biological dataset were tested.
- Low confidence in scalability, as the computational complexity of handling multiple constraints is not thoroughly explored.
