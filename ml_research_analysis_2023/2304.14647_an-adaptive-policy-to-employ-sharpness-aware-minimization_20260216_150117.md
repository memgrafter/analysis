# An Adaptive Policy to Employ Sharpness-Aware Minimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14647
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14647
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: AE-SAM achieves comparable performance to SAM while using only 50% of SAM updates on CIFAR-10/100

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational overhead of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) by proposing an adaptive policy that selectively applies SAM based on loss landscape geometry. The key insight is that SAM is most beneficial in sharp regions of the loss landscape, while standard ERM suffices in flat regions. Two algorithms, AE-SAM and AE-LookSAM, are introduced that monitor sharpness using squared stochastic gradient norms and employ SAM only when necessary. The approach achieves comparable generalization to full SAM while reducing computational cost by approximately 50% on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets.

## Method Summary
The method employs an adaptive policy that monitors loss landscape sharpness using the squared norm of stochastic gradients ∥∇L(Bt; wt)∥². An exponential moving average (EMA) tracks the mean (μt) and variance (σ²t) of this sharpness measure. SAM updates are performed only when ∥∇L(Bt; wt)² > μt + ctσt, where ct follows a linear schedule decreasing from λ2 to λ1 during training. When the sharpness measure falls below this threshold, ERM updates are used instead. AE-SAM uses the standard SAM formulation while AE-LookSAM employs a decomposed variant. Both algorithms maintain the same convergence guarantees as their non-adaptive counterparts while significantly reducing computational overhead.

## Key Results
- AE-SAM achieves comparable accuracy to SAM while using only 50% of SAM updates on CIFAR-10/100
- AE-SAM outperforms ERM, ESAM, SS-SAM, and LookSAM across multiple architectures (ResNet-18, WRN-28-10, PyramidNet-110)
- The adaptive policy demonstrates robustness to label noise, outperforming LookSAM on 80% label noise corrupted CIFAR-10
- AE-LookSAM shows improved efficiency over LookSAM by reducing the number of perturbation computations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: AE-SAM adaptively reduces SAM usage by 50% while maintaining comparable accuracy to full SAM.
- Mechanism: AE-SAM monitors loss landscape sharpness using squared stochastic gradient norms and employs SAM only when sharpness exceeds a threshold (μt + ctσt), otherwise using ERM updates.
- Core assumption: The squared norm of stochastic gradients correlates with local sharpness of the loss landscape.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "AE-SAM achieves comparable performance to SAM while using only 50% of SAM updates"
  - [section 3.1] "Jiang et al. (2020) conducted extensive experiments and empirically show that Var(∇L(Bt; wt)) is positively correlated with the generalization gap"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss SAM variants but don't directly validate the sharpness-gradient correlation
- Break condition: If the correlation between gradient norms and sharpness breaks down in different architectures or datasets.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The exponential moving average (EMA) provides an efficient online estimate of sharpness distribution parameters.
- Mechanism: AE-SAM uses EMA to track mean (μt) and variance (σ²t) of ∥∇L(Bt; wt)∥² without additional gradient computations.
- Core assumption: ∥∇L(Bt; wt)∥² follows a normal distribution that can be tracked efficiently via EMA.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "We use exponential moving average (EMA)...to estimate its mean and variance" and "Figure 3 suggests that ∥∇L(Bt; wt)∥² can be modeled with a normal distribution N(μt, σ²t)"
  - [abstract] "Two efficient algorithms, AE-SAM and AE-LookSAM, are proposed"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss SAM efficiency but don't validate EMA-based sharpness tracking
- Break condition: If ∥∇L(Bt; wt)∥² distribution deviates significantly from normality or EMA fails to track it accurately.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The linear schedule for threshold ct balances early fitting and late-stage sharpness minimization.
- Mechanism: ct decreases linearly from λ2 to λ1 during training, making SAM updates more selective as training progresses.
- Core assumption: Early in training, minimizing loss is more important than seeking flat minima, while later SAM becomes more beneficial.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "During the early training stage...minimizing the empirical loss is more important than seeking a locally flat region" and "Andriushchenko & Flammarion (2022) also empirically observe that the SAM update is more effective in boosting performance towards the end of training"
  - [abstract] "We propose an adaptive policy to employ SAM based on the loss landscape geometry"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss SAM scheduling but don't validate the linear threshold schedule
- Break condition: If the linear schedule doesn't align with the actual learning dynamics across different architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Sharpness monitor -> EMA tracker -> Threshold scheduler -> Update selector -> SAM core/ERM core
- Critical path: Forward pass → Gradient computation → Sharpness monitoring → EMA update → Threshold comparison → Update selection → Parameter update
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Frequency of SAM updates vs. computational savings
  - Choice of λ1, λ2 for threshold scheduling vs. adaptation quality
  - EMA forgetting rate δ vs. responsiveness to changing sharpness
  - Threshold margin (ct) vs. false positives/negatives in sharpness detection
- Failure signatures:
  - High SAM usage fraction (>70%) indicates poor threshold calibration
  - Degraded accuracy compared to full SAM suggests threshold too conservative
  - Training instability may indicate EMA parameters need adjustment
  - Non-convergence suggests threshold schedule inappropriate for dataset

## Limitations
- The theoretical convergence proof relies on assumptions about gradient-norm sharpness correlation that lack rigorous theoretical justification
- EMA-based sharpness estimation assumes normality of gradient norms, which may not hold in all training regimes
- The linear threshold schedule, while intuitive, may not be optimal for all architectures or datasets

## Confidence
- **High**: AE-SAM achieves 50% reduction in SAM updates while maintaining accuracy on CIFAR-10/100
- **Medium**: The EMA-based sharpness estimation is efficient and effective for online adaptation
- **Low**: The linear threshold schedule is universally optimal across different training scenarios
