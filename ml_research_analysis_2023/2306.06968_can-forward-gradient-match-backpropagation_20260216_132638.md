# Can Forward Gradient Match Backpropagation?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06968
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06968
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Using local auxiliary gradients as candidate directions for Forward Gradient estimation improves training accuracy and reduces the performance gap with backpropagation compared to random guesses.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of using Forward Gradients to match the performance of standard backpropagation in neural network training. The core idea is to improve the estimation of directional derivatives by using gradients from local auxiliary losses as candidate directions, rather than relying on random isotropic noise. This approach aims to reduce the variance in gradient estimates and improve training efficiency. The experiments on ResNet-18 architectures for CIFAR-10 and ImageNet32 datasets demonstrate that using local gradients as candidate directions significantly improves accuracy compared to random guesses, narrowing the performance gap with end-to-end training. The findings suggest that better alignment between gradient targets and guesses is crucial for achieving competitive results with Forward Gradients.

## Method Summary
The study improves Forward Gradient estimation for neural network training by using gradients from local auxiliary losses as candidate directions, aiming to reduce variance and close the performance gap with backpropagation. The method involves training ResNet-18 architectures (with and without skip connections) on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet32 datasets, split into 8 or 16 blocks. Forward Gradient is implemented using both activity and weight perturbation methods, and performance is compared across random, NTK, and local gradient guesses for global and local targets. The objective is to maximize test accuracy while minimizing the performance gap between Forward Gradient and end-to-end training. The experiments analyze the alignment between gradient guesses and targets by computing cosine similarity, assessing the impact on accuracy and training dynamics.

## Key Results
- Using local auxiliary gradients as candidate directions for Forward Gradient estimation significantly improves training accuracy compared to random guesses on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet32.
- Local gradient guesses show better alignment with global targets, as measured by higher cosine similarity, leading to improved performance.
- Forward Gradient with local guesses narrows the performance gap with end-to-end training, but still falls short of matching backpropagation's accuracy.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed method improves Forward Gradient estimation by using gradients from local auxiliary losses as candidate directions instead of random isotropic noise. This approach reduces the variance in gradient estimates by aligning the guess directions more closely with the true gradient directions. By leveraging the local task gradients, which are computed using the same intermediate features as the global task, the method ensures better alignment between the guess and the target, leading to more accurate gradient estimates and improved training performance. The use of local gradients as candidate directions also mitigates the curse of dimensionality, as the number of candidate directions scales linearly with the number of layers, rather than exponentially with the model size.

## Foundational Learning
- Forward Gradients: Estimating gradients of a loss function with respect to model parameters by perturbing the input and measuring the change in the output, without relying on backpropagation. Why needed: To enable gradient-based optimization without the need for storing intermediate activations or computing the full Jacobian. Quick check: Verify that Forward Gradients can be computed efficiently and accurately for the given model and task.
- Local Auxiliary Losses: Additional loss functions defined on intermediate layers of the neural network, used to guide the learning process and provide additional supervision. Why needed: To improve the alignment between gradient guesses and targets by leveraging the gradients from local tasks. Quick check: Ensure that the local losses are well-defined and contribute to the overall learning objective.

Why needed: To quantify the alignment between gradient guesses and targets, and assess the effectiveness of different guess strategies. Quick check: Compute the cosine similarity between gradient guesses and targets for various combinations and verify that higher similarity corresponds to better performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can Forward Gradient methods with better alignment between gradient targets and guesses eventually match the performance of end-to-end backpropagation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that using local gradients as guesses significantly improves accuracy compared to random guesses, but still falls short of end-to-end training performance.
- Why unresolved: While local gradient guesses reduce the performance gap, they still have limited alignment with the global target due to different training dynamics. The paper doesn't explore methods to further improve this alignment.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating that Forward Gradient methods with enhanced alignment between targets and guesses can achieve accuracy comparable to end-to-end backpropagation on challenging tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is there an optimal combination of gradient targets and guesses that maximizes the performance of Forward Gradient methods?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper systematically studies various combinations of gradient targets and guesses, finding that local gradient guesses work best, but doesn't claim to have found the optimal combination.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores a subset of possible combinations and doesn't perform an exhaustive search or optimization of the target-guess pairs.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study that tests all possible combinations of gradient targets and guesses, identifying the combination that yields the best performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the dimensionality of the gradient space affect the performance of Forward Gradient methods with different guess strategies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the "curse of dimensionality" and shows that random guess accuracy decreases as model size increases, while local guess accuracy improves.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a detailed analysis of how gradient space dimensionality specifically impacts different guess strategies or explore methods to mitigate dimensionality issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments systematically varying model size and complexity to quantify the impact of dimensionality on different gradient guess strategies, and methods to improve performance in high-dimensional spaces.

## Limitations
- The study is limited to ResNet-18 architectures and two datasets (CIFAR-10 and ImageNet32), leaving open questions about scalability to larger models and other architectures.
- The absence of end-to-end baselines in some comparisons makes it difficult to assess the full performance gap between Forward Gradient and backpropagation.
- The paper doesn't discuss convergence properties or robustness to hyperparameters, which are important factors for practical applications.

## Confidence
- High: The core claim that local auxiliary gradients improve Forward Gradient estimation is well-supported by the experimental results and analysis.
- Medium: Claims about the broader applicability of the method to other architectures and tasks are plausible but require further validation.
- Low: Assertions about the generalization of the results to very large models or different optimization scenarios are speculative and need more evidence.
