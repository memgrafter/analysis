# Assumption Generation for the Verification of Learning-Enabled Autonomous Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18372
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18372
- Reference count: 38
- This paper presents a compositional approach for verifying autonomous systems with learning-enabled perception components by generating weakest assumptions on DNN behavior that guarantee safety properties.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of verifying autonomous systems that rely on machine learning components, particularly deep neural networks (DNNs) for perception. The key insight is that instead of analyzing the DNN directly, the approach automatically synthesizes weakest assumptions on the DNN's output behavior that guarantee the satisfaction of safety properties for the overall system. These assumptions capture all possible output sequences that ensure safety when the DNN is integrated into the system. The approach was demonstrated on an autonomous taxiing system for airplanes, showing that the generated assumptions are small and understandable even for DNNs with large output spaces, making them suitable for runtime monitoring and extracting local specifications for DNN training and testing.

## Method Summary
The approach uses compositional assume-guarantee reasoning to verify autonomous systems with DNN perception components. It models the system components (controller and dynamics) as labeled transition systems (LTSs) and treats the DNN and sensor as an opaque component. The algorithm computes the weakest assumption for the conventional components under the given safety property, characterizing all DNN output sequences that guarantee system-level safety. This assumption can be used as a runtime monitor and can be analyzed to extract local specifications for DNN training and testing. The method involves discretizing the continuous DNN outputs to create a finite interface alphabet, then using model checking to generate the weakest assumption.

## Key Results
- Automatically generated assumptions were small and understandable, even for DNNs with large output spaces
- The assumptions enabled runtime monitoring of deployed DNNs to guarantee system safety
- Local specifications extracted from assumptions could be used to relax DNN training objectives while maintaining safety guarantees
- Experimental evaluation on an autonomous taxiing system demonstrated the practical utility of the approach

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Synthesizing weakest assumptions over DNN outputs enables verification of safety properties for autonomous systems without needing to analyze the DNN directly.
- **Mechanism:** The approach treats the DNN and sensor as an opaque component, analyzes the rest of the system under all possible DNN behaviors, and derives the weakest assumption that ensures safety. This assumption captures all output sequences that guarantee safety, making it possible to verify the system without modeling the DNN's internal logic.
- **Core assumption:** The safety of the overall system can be guaranteed by restricting the interface between the controller/dynamics and the perception components to sequences of DNN outputs that prevent safety violations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our insight is that we can analyze the system in the absence of the DNN perception components by automatically synthesizing assumptions on the DNN behaviour that guarantee the satisfaction of the required safety properties."
  - [section] "We then seek to automatically build an assumption A such that ⟨A, M1, P ⟩ holds; one such assumption is the weakest assumption described in Section II for some alphabet Σ ⊆ ΣI"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related works discuss compositional verification but not this specific weakest-assumption synthesis technique.
- **Break condition:** The assumption generation fails to scale when the interface alphabet becomes extremely large (e.g., DNNs with very high-dimensional outputs), or when the system's safety properties are too complex to be captured by the compositional decomposition.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Extracting local specifications from the weakest assumption provides a way to test and train DNNs with relaxed objectives that still ensure safety.
- **Mechanism:** The complement of the assumption (AΣI_err) encodes error behaviors. By analyzing transitions that lead to errors, local specifications are synthesized that relate actual states to acceptable DNN outputs. These specifications are less restrictive than requiring exact matches, allowing more flexibility during training and testing.
- **Core assumption:** If the DNN satisfies these local specifications for each actual state, then it also satisfies the global assumption, ensuring system safety.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The assumptions can be leveraged as run-time monitors over a deployed DNN to guarantee the safety of the overall system; they can also be mined to extract local specifications for use during training and testing of DNNs."
  - [section] "We also propose to analyze the (complement of the) weakest assumption generated over the full interface alphabet ΣI = Act ∪ Est to synthesize local, non-temporal specifications for the DNN."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - works on DNN verification mention local specifications but not this specific extraction method from weakest assumptions.
- **Break condition:** The local specifications become too permissive, allowing DNN outputs that could lead to safety violations in edge cases not captured by the abstraction, or the extraction process fails to capture all relevant safety constraints.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Using assumptions as runtime monitors enables strong safety guarantees even when the DNN and sensor components are not precisely modeled.
- **Mechanism:** The assumption AEst_w, defined only over DNN outputs, can be deployed as a monitor at runtime. If the DNN's output sequence matches a trace in the assumption, the system is guaranteed to be safe. This avoids the need to model the sensor's behavior or environmental perturbations.
- **Core assumption:** The DNN's outputs at runtime will be consistent with the abstraction used during assumption generation, and the monitor can observe these outputs in time to prevent unsafe actions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The assumptions can be leveraged as run-time monitors over a deployed DNN to guarantee the safety of the overall system"
  - [section] "We therefore define a second alphabet, Σ=est[CTERange][HERange], which consists of only the estimated values, and build a second assumption AEst_w. We describe these two assumptions in more detail below."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - runtime monitoring of DNNs is discussed in related works, but not in the context of weakest-assumption monitors.
- **Break condition:** The monitor is too restrictive, causing unnecessary system halts, or the DNN's actual behavior diverges significantly from the abstraction, causing the monitor to miss safety violations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** LTS models of controller and dynamics (M1) -> Abstraction of perception DNN and sensor (M2) -> Assumption generation algorithm -> Local specification extraction -> Runtime monitor -> PRISM models

- **Critical path:** System model -> Assumption generation (AEst_w) -> Safety guarantee OR Assumption generation (AΣI_err) -> Local specs extraction -> DNN training/testing

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Finer discretization of DNN outputs -> more precise assumptions but larger alphabet and longer generation time
  - Weaker assumptions -> more permissive runtime monitors but potentially less safety
  - Local specifications -> more flexible DNN training but potentially less precise safety guarantees

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Assumption generation times out or produces an empty assumption
  - Runtime monitor is too restrictive, causing frequent system halts
  - Local specifications are too permissive, allowing unsafe DNN outputs
  - PRISM analysis shows non-zero probability of assumption violation

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Verify the taxiing system with MaxCTE=2 discretization; check assumption size and generation time.
  2. Deploy AEst_w as a runtime monitor on the TaxiNet DNN; measure the probability of violation using PRISM.
  3. Extract local specifications from AΣI_err; use them to relax the DNN training objective and compare test accuracy with standard training.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the assumption generation approach be extended to handle systems with multiple machine learning components (e.g., using both camera and LIDAR for sensing)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Another direction for future work is to investigate autonomous systems with multiple machine learning components (e.g., using both camera and LIDAR for sensing) and to develop techniques that decompose the generated global assumption into assumptions for each such component."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a concrete methodology or algorithm for handling multiple perception components and decomposing global assumptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal approach for decomposing global assumptions into component-specific assumptions, along with experimental results on a system with multiple perception components.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can incremental and more scalable techniques be developed for assumption generation to address scalability issues due to state-explosion in model checking?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "While our approach opens the door to analyzing autonomous systems with state-of-the-art DNNs, it can suffer from the well known scalability issues associated with model checking (due to state-explosion). We believe we can address this issue via judicious use of abstraction and compositional techniques. Incremental, more scalable, techniques for assumption generation can also be explored, see. e.g. [31]."
- Why unresolved: The paper only briefly mentions the potential for incremental techniques but does not provide a concrete solution or implementation.
- What evidence would resolve it: A novel incremental algorithm for assumption generation that demonstrates improved scalability compared to the current approach, validated on larger case studies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the generated local specifications be effectively used during the training of DNNs to relax the training objective?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "The specifications can also be used during the training of the DNN as a specification of desired output for unlabeled real data, thus reducing the burden of manually labeling the input images."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a concrete methodology for integrating local specifications into the DNN training process or demonstrate its effectiveness.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experimental study showing improved DNN performance or safety when using local specifications as relaxed training objectives compared to standard training methods.

## Limitations
- The approach depends on effective discretization of DNN outputs, which may not scale for DNNs with extremely high-dimensional outputs
- The assumption generation process requires precise LTS models of conventional components, which may be challenging to construct for complex systems
- The runtime monitor may be too restrictive or miss safety violations if the DNN's behavior diverges significantly from the abstraction

## Confidence

**High confidence** in the compositional verification mechanism and the weakest-assumption synthesis algorithm, as these are well-grounded in formal methods literature and clearly specified.

**Medium confidence** in the practical utility of generated assumptions for runtime monitoring and DNN training, as the experimental results are limited to a single case study.

**Medium confidence** in the scalability claims, as the evaluation focuses on a specific system architecture and DNN size.

## Next Checks

1. **Scalability assessment**: Apply the assumption generation algorithm to DNNs with progressively larger output spaces (e.g., object detection DNNs with hundreds of output classes) and measure the impact on generation time and assumption size.

2. **Abstraction fidelity evaluation**: Systematically vary the discretization granularity of DNN outputs and quantify the tradeoff between assumption precision and computational tractability, particularly focusing on the point where assumptions become too large to be practical.

3. **Cross-system generalization**: Test the approach on autonomous systems from different domains (e.g., autonomous vehicles, drones) to evaluate whether the assumption generation and monitoring framework generalizes beyond the taxiing system case study.
