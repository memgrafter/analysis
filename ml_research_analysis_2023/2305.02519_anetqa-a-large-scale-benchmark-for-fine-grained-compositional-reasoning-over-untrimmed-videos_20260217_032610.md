# ANetQA: A Large-scale Benchmark for Fine-grained Compositional Reasoning over Untrimmed Videos

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02519
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02519
- Authors: [Authors not specified]
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: 13.4M balanced QA pairs for fine-grained compositional reasoning over untrimmed videos

## Executive Summary
ANetQA is a large-scale benchmark designed to evaluate fine-grained compositional reasoning over untrimmed real-world videos from ActivityNet. Unlike previous benchmarks that focus on short, trimmed videos with coarse semantics, ANetQA provides more detailed scene graph annotations and diverse question templates to enable complex reasoning about objects, relationships, attributes, and actions. The benchmark contains 13.4 million balanced QA pairs, an order of magnitude larger than previous datasets, and includes 6 new question types focused on attributes. Comprehensive experiments show that even the best-performing models achieve only 44.5% accuracy, far below human performance of 84.5%, indicating significant room for improvement in video-language reasoning systems.

## Method Summary
ANetQA automatically generates QA pairs from annotated video scene graphs using hand-crafted templates and functional programs. The process begins with object detection and noun phrase extraction from video frames, followed by hierarchical attribute annotation, relationship labeling, and action caption processing. A comprehensive set of 86 relationships (81 contact and 1 temporal) and 6 attribute-specific question types are defined. QA pairs are generated through template traversal and graph element composition, then balanced through composite strategies that control both question structure distribution and answer uniformity. The benchmark includes auxiliary annotations like scene graph statistics and oracle frames to support analysis of model performance factors.

## Key Results
- ANetQA contains 13.4M balanced QA pairs, significantly larger than existing benchmarks
- Models achieve 44.5% accuracy compared to human performance of 84.5%
- Fine-grained attribute annotations enable 6 new question types not supported in previous benchmarks
- Composite balancing strategies effectively control language biases while maintaining reasoning diversity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Automatic QA generation from annotated scene graphs enables massive dataset scale while maintaining fine-grained reasoning coverage
- Mechanism: By pre-annotating video frames with objects, relationships, attributes, and actions as structured scene graphs, QA pairs can be programmatically generated using hand-crafted templates that traverse and compose graph elements. This avoids costly human annotation of each question while ensuring compositional reasoning about video semantics.
- Core assumption: Scene graph elements (objects, relationships, attributes, actions) are sufficient to generate diverse QA pairs that require fine-grained reasoning over untrimmed videos
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Similar to AGQA, the QA pairs in ANetQA are automatically generated from annotated video scene graphs."
  - [section 3.2]: "We handcraft a hierarchical attribute taxonomy... For each object, annotators are asked to label the bottom-level attributes thoroughly."
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors discuss spatio-temporal reasoning but don't validate scene graph sufficiency for fine-grained QA generation
- Break condition: If scene graphs lack sufficient granularity or diversity to support required reasoning patterns, generated QA pairs will be too simple or repetitive

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-grained scene graph attributes enable more complex and diverse question templates than previous benchmarks
- Mechanism: Hierarchical attribute annotations (e.g., hair style, clothing color/type, occupation) allow templates to ask about specific properties rather than just object existence or relationships. This creates questions requiring detailed video understanding beyond basic recognition.
- Core assumption: Attribute annotations provide enough variety to support diverse question templates that require fine-grained reasoning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The fine-grained properties of ANetQA are reflected in... (iii) diverse questions generated from fine-grained templates."
  - [section 3.2]: "We design a set of 86 relationships containing 81 contact relationships... and 1 temporal relationship."
  - [section 3.3]: "We introduce 6 extra types with respect to attributes... resulting in 612.6M unbalanced and 6.9M balanced QA pairs."
- Break condition: If attribute taxonomy is too shallow or object-attribute mappings too sparse, question templates cannot generate sufficiently diverse or challenging questions

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Composite balancing strategies effectively control language biases while maintaining reasoning diversity
- Mechanism: By adjusting question structure distributions (50% query/15% verify/15% choose/15% compare/5% logic) and ensuring uniform answer distributions within each template, the benchmark reduces exploitable language patterns while preserving compositional reasoning requirements.
- Core assumption: Balancing question structures and answer distributions within templates is sufficient to eliminate language biases that could be exploited
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "These QA pairs are unbalanced and have strong language biases that models can exploit. We conduct composite balancing strategies on both the questions and answers."
  - [section 3.3]: "While maintaining these percentages above, we conduct answer balancing within each question template to make sure that its answers are uniformly distributed."
  - [section 4.2]: "For each method, the variant trained with vision clues (w/) steadily outperforms its blind counterpart without any vision clues (w/o), indicating that the language biases are well controlled by our balancing strategies."
- Break condition: If language patterns persist despite balancing, or if balancing reduces reasoning diversity too much, models may still exploit biases

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Video corpus (ActivityNet) → Frame sampling → Object detection & noun phrase extraction → Attribute taxonomy creation → Relationship annotation → Action caption processing → Scene graph construction → QA template library → Functional program execution → Balanced QA pair generation
- Critical path: Video annotation → Scene graph construction → QA generation → Balancing → Model training → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Annotation density vs. coverage: Sampling fewer frames but annotating more thoroughly vs. broader but shallower coverage
  - Template diversity vs. control: More templates enable more reasoning patterns but increase complexity and potential for errors
  - Balancing strictness vs. realism: Stricter balancing reduces biases but may create artificial distributions unlike real-world scenarios
- Failure signatures:
  - Low model performance despite strong results on other benchmarks → Likely due to fine-grained semantics and controlled biases
  - High variance across question types → May indicate some templates or reasoning patterns are under-supported by scene graph annotations
  - Poor generalization to other benchmarks → Could suggest overfitting to specific scene graph structures or question patterns
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study on attribute inclusion: Compare model performance with and without attribute-based questions to quantify their contribution to benchmark difficulty
  2. Oracle frame injection: Test model performance when given oracle frames that contain necessary visual information, isolating reasoning from visual grounding challenges
  3. Template diversity analysis: Systematically vary the number and complexity of question templates to identify the minimum viable set for maintaining benchmark quality

## Limitations
- Scene graph annotations may not capture all relevant visual information needed for fine-grained reasoning
- The benchmark's reliance on ActivityNet limits generalizability to other video domains
- Template-based QA generation may create artificial reasoning patterns not reflective of natural question formation

## Confidence
- High confidence in automatic QA generation mechanism and its scalability
- Medium confidence in scene graph sufficiency for fine-grained reasoning
- Medium-High confidence in composite balancing strategies effectively controlling language biases
