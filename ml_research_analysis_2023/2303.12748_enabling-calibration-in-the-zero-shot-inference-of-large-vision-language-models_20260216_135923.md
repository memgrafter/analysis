# Enabling Calibration In The Zero-Shot Inference of Large Vision-Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12748
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12748
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 11
- Key outcome: Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling significantly improves CLIP calibration while preserving zero-shot inference capabilities, though it doesn't match supervised calibration performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the calibration problem in CLIP's zero-shot inference by proposing Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling. The authors find that CLIP models are systematically miscalibrated across various prompts, datasets, and architectures, with cosine similarity logits producing overconfident predictions. Their method learns a single temperature parameter during auxiliary training on ImageNet-1k, which is then applied to all downstream zero-shot inferences. The approach successfully improves calibration (reducing ECE) while maintaining the zero-shot paradigm, though it still falls short of supervised temperature scaling performance.

## Method Summary
The method modifies temperature scaling for CLIP's zero-shot setting by training a single temperature parameter on an auxiliary dataset (ImageNet-1k) using cross-entropy loss. This temperature is then applied to all downstream zero-shot inferences by dividing CLIP's cosine similarity logits by the learned scalar before softmax. The key insight is that the optimal temperature is stable across different inference datasets and prompts for each specific CLIP model (defined by architecture and pre-training data), allowing a lookup table of temperatures per model type. This preserves the zero-shot paradigm since no further training or calibration is needed for each new task.

## Key Results
- Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling significantly reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) compared to vanilla CLIP across multiple datasets
- A single learned temperature generalizes across different inference datasets and prompts for each CLIP model
- The method preserves CLIP's zero-shot inference capabilities while improving calibration
- Performance remains below supervised temperature scaling but provides practical calibration improvement

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CLIP's zero-shot inference is miscalibrated because cosine similarity logits lack proper temperature scaling, leading to overconfident predictions.
- Mechanism: Temperature scaling adjusts the sharpness of the softmax distribution by dividing logits by a learned scalar T. For CLIP, this modifies the text-image similarity scores before softmax, directly controlling confidence levels.
- Core assumption: The optimal temperature is stable across different inference datasets and prompts for a given CLIP model (same architecture + pre-training dataset).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "propose a modified version of temperature scaling that is aligned with the common use cases of CLIP as a zero-shot inference model, and show that a single learned temperature generalizes"
  - [section 3] "a single learned temperature generalizes for each specific CLIP model... across inference dataset and prompt choice"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citation to temperature scaling literature for CLIP specifically
- Break condition: If the optimal temperature varies significantly with dataset or prompt, the method fails to generalize and requires per-dataset tuning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling learns a single temperature parameter during auxiliary training, enabling calibration without per-dataset tuning.
- Mechanism: The method trains T on an auxiliary dataset (ImageNet-1k) using cross-entropy loss, then applies this fixed T to all downstream zero-shot inferences. This preserves the zero-shot paradigm since no further training is needed.
- Core assumption: A temperature learned on a large, diverse auxiliary dataset will generalize to unseen inference tasks with different class distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3] "train a temperature on an auxiliary dataset via Temperature Scaling... use this temperature on all downstream inferences... without any training, tuning, or calibration processes necessary"
  - [section 4.2] "the optimal temperature T across prompts and inference datasets are approximately the same"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citation to auxiliary dataset calibration methods for CLIP
- Break condition: If the auxiliary dataset is too dissimilar from inference datasets, the learned temperature may not generalize, requiring dataset-specific calibration.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The miscalibration pattern in CLIP is consistent enough across architectures and pre-training datasets that a single temperature per architecture-pretraining pair is sufficient.
- Mechanism: Different CLIP models (defined by architecture and pre-training data) have different optimal temperatures, but within each model, the temperature is stable. This allows a lookup table of temperatures per model type.
- Core assumption: The miscalibration is primarily determined by the underlying CLIP model parameters rather than the specific inference task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "the optimal temperature T per inference dataset and prompt is very close to the one learned on a large auxiliary dataset and is approximately the same across inference datasets and prompts"
  - [table 1] Shows different optimal temperatures for different CLIP architectures (ViT-B-16, ViT-L-14, etc.)
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citation to CLIP architecture-specific calibration studies
- Break condition: If prompt engineering or dataset characteristics introduce systematic miscalibration patterns that vary beyond architecture-pretraining differences, a single temperature per model is insufficient.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  CLIP model (architecture + pre-training dataset) -> Temperature scaling layer (single scalar T) -> Softmax -> Reliability diagram/ECE computation

- Critical path:
  1. Load CLIP model with specific architecture and pre-training dataset
  2. Load auxiliary dataset (ImageNet-1k) for temperature training
  3. Train temperature T using cross-entropy loss on auxiliary data
  4. Store T associated with model identifier
  5. For inference: divide CLIP logits by stored T before softmax

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single temperature per model vs. per-dataset tuning: Simplicity vs. optimal calibration
  - Auxiliary dataset choice: ImageNet-1k is standard but may not cover all inference domains
  - Temperature training objective: Cross-entropy vs. calibration-specific losses

- Failure signatures:
  - ECE remains high after temperature scaling -> temperature learned on auxiliary data doesn't generalize
  - Temperature varies significantly across prompts -> assumption of stability is violated
  - Reliability diagram shows systematic miscalibration patterns -> single scalar insufficient

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling be further improved to match or surpass the calibration performance of supervised Temperature Scaling?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that their method results in models that are still significantly less calibrated than those calibrated via the supervised variant of Temperature Scaling, leaving room for future Zero-Shot-Enabled CLIP calibration methods that improve upon their method.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents their method as a step towards enabling calibration in zero-shot inference, but acknowledges that there is still a gap in performance compared to supervised methods. The authors explicitly invite future work to improve upon their method.
- What evidence would resolve it: Future research developing and demonstrating improved Zero-Shot-Enabled calibration methods that achieve comparable or better calibration performance (e.g., lower ECE) than supervised Temperature Scaling on the same datasets and architectures.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the optimal temperature for Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling vary across different inference datasets and prompts, or is it truly robust as claimed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that for a single architecture and pre-training dataset, the optimal temperature across prompts and inference datasets are approximately the same, which they find remarkable given the different number of classes and distributions in the datasets.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides evidence that the temperature is robust for the specific architectures and datasets tested, it's unclear if this robustness generalizes to all possible CLIP models, datasets, and prompts. The paper only tests a limited set of configurations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive testing of Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling across a much wider variety of CLIP architectures, pre-training datasets, inference datasets, and prompts, demonstrating consistent temperature robustness across all configurations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling be extended to other vision-language models beyond CLIP?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on CLIP and its zero-shot inference paradigm, but the concept of using temperature scaling to improve calibration while preserving zero-shot capabilities could potentially be applied to other vision-language models that use a similar inference approach.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the applicability of their method to other vision-language models. It's unclear if the assumptions and properties that make Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling work for CLIP also hold for other models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Application and validation of Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling or similar methods to other vision-language models, demonstrating improved calibration while maintaining zero-shot inference capabilities on those models.

## Limitations
- Single temperature per architecture-pretraining pair may not be sufficient for all inference scenarios, especially highly domain-specific or out-of-distribution tasks
- Temperature learned on ImageNet-1k may not generalize well to significantly different domains (medical imaging, satellite imagery, etc.)
- Method still falls short of supervised temperature scaling performance, leaving a calibration gap

## Confidence
- High Confidence: CLIP models exhibit systematic miscalibration in zero-shot inference
- Medium Confidence: A single temperature generalizes across different inference datasets and prompts for each CLIP model
- Medium Confidence: Zero-Shot-Enabled Temperature Scaling preserves the zero-shot paradigm while improving calibration
