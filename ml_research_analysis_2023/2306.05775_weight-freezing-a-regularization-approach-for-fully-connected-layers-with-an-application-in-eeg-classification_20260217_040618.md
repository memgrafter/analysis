# Weight Freezing: A Regularization Approach for Fully Connected Layers with an Application in EEG Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05775
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05775
- Reference count: 6
- One-line primary result: Weight freezing improves EEG classification accuracy by reducing overfitting through selective weight freezing in fully connected layers.

## Executive Summary
This study introduces "weight freezing," a novel regularization technique designed to enhance the performance of artificial neural networks for EEG decoding. The method addresses challenges in EEG classification by freezing specific weights during backpropagation, effectively creating sparse connections and reducing overfitting. Through experiments on three distinct ANN architectures and three widely recognized EEG datasets, weight freezing demonstrated significant improvements in classification accuracy compared to traditional fully connected networks.

## Method Summary
Weight freezing is a regularization approach that selectively freezes weights in fully connected layers during backpropagation. The method uses a mask matrix and threshold to determine which weights to freeze, effectively creating sparse connections. During training, masked weights are set to zero, reducing the number of learnable parameters and preventing overfitting. The technique was tested across three ANN architectures and three EEG datasets, showing consistent improvements in classification performance.

## Key Results
- Weight freezing significantly improved classification accuracy across all three tested EEG datasets
- The method outperformed traditional fully connected networks in all examined scenarios
- Different threshold values affected classification performance, demonstrating the importance of parameter selection

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Weight freezing improves EEG classification by reducing overfitting caused by dense connections in fully connected layers.
- **Mechanism:** By freezing a portion of the weights during backpropagation, weight freezing effectively creates sparse connections in the fully connected layer, reducing the number of learnable parameters and thus preventing overfitting.
- **Core assumption:** The features input into the classifier are mixed with noise due to the low signal-to-noise ratio and limited data availability in EEG.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Moreover, by setting the masked weights to zero, weight freezing can not only realize sparse connections in networks with a fully connected layer as the classifier but also function as an efficacious regularization method for fully connected layers."
  - [section] "This study takes a different perspective by focusing on enhancing the classification capability of the classifier to address the EEG classification problem. The fundamental assumption of this study is that the features input into the classifier are mixed with noise, which arises from the low signal-to-noise ratio and limited data availability in EEG."
- **Break condition:** If the EEG dataset has a large number of training samples and a high signal-to-noise ratio, the need for regularization might be reduced, potentially diminishing the effectiveness of weight freezing.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Weight freezing aligns with neuroscience prior knowledge by reducing the influence of irrelevant neurons on the decision-making process.
- **Mechanism:** By freezing specific weights, weight freezing reduces the influence of certain neurons on the decision-making process for a specific EEG task, aligning with the neuroscience prior knowledge that not all neurons are equally relevant for every task.
- **Core assumption:** Neuroscience prior knowledge suggests that only specific neurons are relevant for certain EEG tasks, such as motor imagery tasks.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The concept of weight freezing revolves around the idea of reducing certain neurons’ influence on the decision-making process for a specific EEG task by freezing specific weights in the fully connected layer during the backpropagation process."
  - [section] "This prior knowledge suggests that when determining the category of motor imagery, it is not necessary to consider the electrical signals from all neurons. Instead, a classifier that aligns more with neuroscience’s prior knowledge should only take into account the impact of specific input neurons on the category during the determination process."
- **Break condition:** If the EEG task requires the involvement of all neurons for accurate classification, weight freezing might hinder the model's performance by ignoring potentially relevant information.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Weight freezing is a more effective regularization method compared to dropout for EEG classification tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Unlike dropout, which randomly drops neurons during training, weight freezing selectively freezes weights, preserving the information propagation in the forward pass while still regularizing the model during backpropagation.
- **Core assumption:** The information propagation in the forward pass is crucial for EEG classification tasks, and dropout's random neuron dropping might disrupt this process.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "Both Weight Freezing and Dropout can be considered as regularization methods to prevent overfitting in neural networks. The comparison of the implementation principles of Weight Freezing and Dropout is as follows: 1. Target of Action: ... 2. Information Propagation: Dropout affects both forward and backward propagation, while weight freezing only impacts backward propagation. 3. Mode of Action: ... 4. Flexibility: ..."
- **Break condition:** If dropout is already highly effective for a specific EEG classification task, the additional complexity of weight freezing might not provide significant benefits.
