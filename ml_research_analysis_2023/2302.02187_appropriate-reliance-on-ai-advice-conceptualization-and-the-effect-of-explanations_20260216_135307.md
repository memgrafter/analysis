# Appropriate Reliance on AI Advice: Conceptualization and the Effect of Explanations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02187

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of appropriate reliance on AI advice in decision-making contexts where the advice is imperfect. The authors introduce the concept of Appropriateness of Reliance (AoR) as a two-dimensional metric that captures both the correct reliance on AI advice (𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅) and correct self-reliance when rejecting incorrect AI advice (𝑅𝑆𝑅). Through a behavioral experiment with 200 participants using a hotel review classification task, the study demonstrates that providing feature importance explanations increases the relative AI reliance (𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅) from 29.59% to 38.87%, while not significantly affecting the relative self-reliance (𝑅𝑆𝑅). The research also reveals that explanations increase change in self-confidence, which partially mediates the effect on 𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅. These findings contribute to understanding how explanations influence appropriate reliance and provide a foundation for designing effective AI advisors.

## Method Summary
The study employed a behavioral experiment with 200 participants recruited through Prolific, using a hotel review classification task where participants determined whether reviews were deceptive or genuine. Participants completed a sequential decision task where they first made an initial decision, then received AI advice with or without feature importance explanations (between-subject design), and finally revised their decision. The AI model was a Support Vector Machine trained on the Deceptive Opinion Spam corpus with 86% accuracy. Feature importance explanations were implemented using LIME to highlight important words with effect direction and three-tier effect size coding. The study measured two dimensions of Appropriateness of Reliance: relative AI reliance (𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅) measuring correct reliance on correct AI advice, and relative self-reliance (𝑅𝑆𝑅) measuring correct rejection of incorrect AI advice.

## Key Results
- Feature importance explanations increased relative AI reliance (𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅) from 29.59% to 38.87%
- Explanations did not significantly affect relative self-reliance (𝑅𝑆𝑅)
- Change in self-confidence partially mediated the effect of explanations on 𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅
- Trust in AI advisor decreased relative self-reliance (𝑅𝑆𝑅)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Providing feature importance explanations increases the relative AI reliance (𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅) by enabling users to identify patterns they previously missed, thereby encouraging reliance on correct AI advice.
- Mechanism: Feature importance explanations highlight specific words or features that influenced the AI's decision, allowing users to build knowledge of the task and validate whether the advice makes sense. This knowledge extension increases the likelihood of following correct AI advice when initially uncertain.
- Core assumption: Users lack sufficient domain knowledge to solve the task independently but can use explanations to extend their understanding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the study demonstrates that providing feature importance explanations increases the relative AI reliance (𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅) from 29.59% to 38.87%..."
  - [section]: "explanations might point humans towards new patterns they have not seen before and help discriminate these knowledge extensions."
- Break condition: If the explanations are not comprehensible or the AI model's reasoning is too complex for the user to understand, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Explanations influence the change in self-confidence, which partially mediates the effect on 𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅.
- Mechanism: By providing insights into the AI's decision-making process, explanations increase the user's confidence in the AI's advice. This increased confidence leads to a higher likelihood of following correct AI advice when initially uncertain.
- Core assumption: Explanations increase the user's confidence in the AI's advice, and this change in confidence influences the decision to follow the advice.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "explanations increase change in self-confidence, which partially mediates the effect on 𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅."
  - [section]: "explanations should not influence the initial human self-confidence but the confidence level after seeing the AI advice."
- Break condition: If the user's self-confidence is already high or the explanations do not increase confidence, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Trust in the AI advisor influences both the relative AI reliance (𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅) and the relative self-reliance (𝑅𝑆𝑅), with higher trust leading to increased reliance on correct AI advice and decreased self-reliance when rejecting incorrect advice.
- Mechanism: Trust in the AI advisor leads to a higher weight given to the AI's advice, increasing the likelihood of following correct advice. However, this trust can also lead to over-reliance on incorrect advice, reducing self-reliance.
- Core assumption: Trust in the AI advisor influences the decision to follow or reject the advice.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "trust decreases the relative self-reliance (𝑅𝑆𝑅)."
  - [section]: "Trust influences reliance, but does not fully determine it... insufficient trust is called distrust and when trust exceeds capability it is called over-trust."
- Break condition: If the user does not trust the AI advisor or the trust is calibrated to the AI's capabilities, the mechanism fails.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific mechanisms or techniques can be developed to improve relative self-reliance (RSR) in AI-assisted decision-making, beyond current explanation methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper found that explanations significantly increased relative AI reliance (RAIR) but did not significantly affect relative self-reliance (RSR), suggesting current explanation methods may not be effective for improving self-reliance.
- Why unresolved: The study only tested feature importance explanations on textual data, and the results showed no significant improvement in RSR. The paper acknowledges that new techniques must be developed to distinguish incorrect AI advice and improve self-reliance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of alternative explanation methods (e.g., counterfactual explanations, global explanations) or other techniques (e.g., confidence calibration, uncertainty quantification) in improving RSR across different task domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the sequential task setup, where humans make initial decisions before receiving AI advice, impact the measurement of appropriate reliance compared to real-world settings where AI advice is provided directly?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that the sequential task setup may change the task itself and could induce an anchoring effect, potentially affecting the measurement of reliance behavior and limiting generalizability to real-world settings.
- Why unresolved: The study's measurement concept requires a sequential task setup to measure appropriateness of reliance (AoR), but this setup may not reflect real-world scenarios where AI advice is often provided directly without prior human decision-making.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies examining reliance behavior and performance in both sequential and non-sequential task settings, or alternative measurement approaches that approximate real human behavior without requiring sequential tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different types of AI errors (systematic vs. random) affect the relationship between relative AI reliance (RAIR), relative self-reliance (RSR), and the achievement of complementary team performance (CTP)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the importance of differentiating between systematic and random errors, noting that humans can identify systematic errors but not random ones, and that this distinction affects the calculation of CTP and appropriate reliance.
- Why unresolved: The study does not explicitly analyze how the type of AI errors (systematic vs. random) influences the relationship between RAIR, RSR, and CTP, or how this relationship varies across different tasks or AI performance levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies systematically varying the proportion of systematic and random errors in AI advice, and analyzing the resulting effects on RAIR, RSR, and CTP across different task domains and AI performance levels.

## Limitations

- The study only tested feature importance explanations on a single task domain (hotel review classification), limiting generalizability to other domains and explanation techniques.
- The sequential task setup may introduce anchoring effects and may not reflect real-world scenarios where AI advice is often provided directly.
- The online experimental setup, while enabling a larger sample size, may introduce noise due to participant variability and potential lack of attention.

## Confidence

- High confidence: The conceptual framework of Appropriateness of Reliance (AoR) as a two-dimensional metric is theoretically sound and well-grounded in decision-making literature
- Medium confidence: The experimental results showing increased 𝑅𝐴𝐼𝑅 with explanations are robust, though the small effect size and specific task context limit generalizability
- Low confidence: The mediation analysis through change in self-confidence is suggestive but requires further validation with additional experimental controls
