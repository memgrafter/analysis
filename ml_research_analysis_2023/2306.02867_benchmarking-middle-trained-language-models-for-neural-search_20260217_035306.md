# Benchmarking Middle-Trained Language Models for Neural Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02867
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02867
- Reference count: 40
- This study benchmarks middle-trained language models for neural search, comparing dense and sparse retrieval approaches across multiple finetuning protocols and datasets.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically benchmarks middle-trained language models (CoCondenser, RetroMAE, LexMAE) for neural search under controlled conditions. The research compares dense and sparse retrieval approaches across multiple finetuning protocols and datasets including MS MARCO, Wikipedia, and Tripclick. The study introduces middle training baselines using standard MLM finetuning and CLS-based term frequency prediction. Key findings show that sparse model differences diminish with more effective finetuning, dense models excel when trained on MS MARCO, middle training on the retrieval collection is critical, and sparse models generally outperform dense ones.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates multiple middle-trained language models (CoCondenser, RetroMAE, LexMAE) under four finetuning settings: no distillation with 1 or 32 negatives, and distillation with 1 or 32 negatives. Models are tested on MS MARCO dev set, TREC 2019/2020, BEIR datasets, and TripClick dataset. The research introduces middle training baselines (BERT-MS using MLM on retrieval collection, BERT-MS-CLS adding CLS term frequency prediction). Evaluation metrics include MRR@10, R@1K, NDCG@10, and BEIR nDCG@10 averages with statistical significance testing using Bonferroni correction.

## Key Results
- Middle training on the retrieval collection significantly boosts performance by adapting language models to domain-specific term distributions
- Sparse models generally outperform dense models across all tested settings and datasets
- More effective finetuning procedures (more negatives, distillation) reduce performance differences between middle-trained models
- Middle-trained baselines (BERT-MS, BERT-MS-CLS) prove competitive with specialized methods like LexMAE

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Middle training on the retrieval collection significantly boosts retrieval performance by adapting the language model's co-occurrence statistics to the domain.
- Mechanism: During middle training, the model learns to predict masked tokens using both the MLM task and additional tasks like CLS-based term frequency prediction. This process adapts the model's internal representations to the specific vocabulary and term distributions found in the retrieval collection.
- Core assumption: The retrieval collection's term distributions differ from the general pre-training corpus, and adapting to these differences improves downstream retrieval performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "middle training on the retrieval collection, thus adapting the language model to it, is a critical factor"
  - [section]: "We see with the basic MTLMs (b,c) the impact of using MS MARCO during this middle-training step gives some boost(>+1.4 point)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus analysis only shows related papers on middle training but lacks direct term distribution analysis
- Break condition: If the retrieval collection's term distributions are similar to the pre-training corpus, the adaptation benefit may be minimal or nonexistent.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: More effective finetuning procedures reduce the performance gap between different middle-trained models.
- Mechanism: As finetuning becomes more sophisticated (more negatives, distillation), all models converge toward similar performance levels because they're all learning to optimize for the same retrieval task under similar conditions.
- Core assumption: The differences between middle training methods are primarily in their pretraining phase, and finetuning can overcome these differences if done effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "For the sparse approach, our study reveals that there is almost no statistical difference between those methods: the more effective the finetuning procedure is, the less difference there is between those models"
  - [section]: "Table 4: distillation, 32 negatives... the differences between MTLMs continue to shrink, almost disappearing for the sparse approach"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers discuss middle training effectiveness but don't directly address this convergence phenomenon
- Break condition: If finetuning procedures differ significantly between studies, or if the retrieval task requires specific architectural features that only certain models possess.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sparse models generally outperform dense models in neural search tasks, even when dense models use middle training.
- Mechanism: Sparse models like SPLADE create term-based representations that can capture exact matching and term importance more effectively than dense vector representations, especially in the first-stage ranking.
- Core assumption: The retrieval task benefits more from exact term matching and term importance signals than from semantic similarity captured by dense vectors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "sparse models generally outperform dense ones"
  - [section]: "In all settings, the sparse approach beats the dense one"
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - SPLADE and related sparse models show strong performance in neural IR benchmarks
- Break condition: If the retrieval task requires high semantic similarity matching rather than exact term matching, or if the corpus is small enough that dense representations can capture all relevant information.

  - Why needed here: These techniques are crucial for effective finetuning and understanding why they reduce differences between models
  - Quick check question: How does the quality of negative samples impact the convergence of different middle-trained models?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: BERT-base-uncased backbone → Middle training layer (MLM + task-specific losses) → Finetuning layer (various protocols with negatives/distillation) → Retrieval application
- Critical path: Middle training collection selection → Task selection (MLM, CLS term frequency, etc.) → Finetuning protocol (negatives, distillation) → Evaluation on target dataset
- Design tradeoffs: Middle training on retrieval collection improves performance but limits zero-shot capability; more complex middle training tasks may not provide additional benefits if finetuning is effective
- Failure signatures: Poor performance when middle training collection differs from target collection; overfitting when using too many epochs on small collections; convergence issues with inadequate negative sampling
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare BERT baseline vs BERT-MS (MLM on retrieval collection) to isolate the effect of middle training on the target collection
  2. Test different middle training tasks (MLM only vs MLM + CLS term frequency) to understand task contributions
  3. Vary finetuning complexity (1 negative vs 32 negatives with distillation) to observe convergence effects across different models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific architectural modifications or pre-training tasks would maximize the performance of middle-trained sparse models beyond current baselines?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that for sparse models, middle-trained baselines (BERT-MS, BERT-MS-CLS) are competitive with specialized methods like LexMAE, suggesting room for improvement.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on comparing existing middle-trained models under standardized conditions rather than optimizing or inventing new architectures for sparse models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing novel sparse-specific pre-training tasks (e.g., term frequency regression, representative word prediction) against current methods under identical finetuning conditions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do middle-trained models perform under extreme data scarcity scenarios, such as few-shot learning with fewer than 1000 queries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that middle-trained models could be more effective in "realistic" settings with fewer queries, testing with 50K and 100K subsets, but doesn't explore true few-shot conditions.
- Why unresolved: Experiments were limited to 50K/100K query subsets, which still represent substantial data compared to few-shot learning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparisons of middle-trained vs. standard models with <1000 training queries across multiple retrieval tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relative importance of language model adaptation versus specialized pre-training tasks in middle training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors hypothesize that adapting the language model to the retrieval collection may be as important as specialized IR-related tasks, noting that BERT-MS (simple MLM finetuning) performs competitively.
- Why unresolved: The study compares models with different architectural modifications and tasks simultaneously, making it difficult to isolate the contribution of language model adaptation alone.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies where language model adaptation is controlled for while varying pre-training tasks, or vice versa, across multiple datasets.

## Limitations

- The study focuses primarily on BERT-base-uncased as the backbone architecture, limiting conclusions about other model sizes or architectures
- Limited evaluation to English-language collections, restricting applicability to multilingual retrieval scenarios
- Middle training collections are constrained to MS MARCO and Wikipedia, with only one non-English collection (TripClick) tested
- The study does not explore the impact of different pre-training corpus sizes or domains beyond the ones tested

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The finding that middle training on the retrieval collection significantly improves performance is supported by consistent results across multiple models and settings. The convergence of different middle-trained models under effective finetuning is also well-established through statistical testing.

**Medium Confidence:** The general superiority of sparse models over dense models, while observed consistently, may depend on specific implementation details and hyperparameters that vary across studies. The optimal middle training tasks (MLM vs MLM+CLS) show trends but require further validation.

**Low Confidence:** Claims about the irrelevance of middle training method differences under strong finetuning assume that all models can converge to similar performance levels, which may not hold for all architectures or retrieval tasks.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-architectural validation:** Test the convergence hypothesis across different backbone architectures (BERT-large, RoBERTa, T5) to determine if middle training method differences persist beyond BERT-base.

2. **Domain transfer evaluation:** Conduct experiments where middle training and retrieval collections come from different domains (e.g., middle training on Wikipedia, retrieval on scientific papers) to quantify the importance of domain alignment.

3. **Multilingual extension:** Evaluate the same models and protocols on non-English collections with different linguistic properties to test whether the sparse vs dense performance gap persists across languages and whether middle training remains effective.
