# Topic-Selective Graph Network for Topic-Focused Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13106
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13106
- Reference count: 37
- The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance on topic-focused summarization tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for topic-focused summarization that addresses limitations in existing methods by incorporating topic-aware mechanisms. The method combines a topic-arc recognition objective with a topic-selective graph network to effectively capture and leverage topic information during summarization. Through experiments on two datasets (NEWTS and COVIDET), the proposed approach demonstrates superior performance compared to strong baselines, achieving state-of-the-art results in generating topic-relevant summaries.

## Method Summary
The proposed method introduces a two-stage approach for topic-focused summarization. First, it employs a topic-arc recognition objective that discriminates the topic of each sentence in an article, creating topic-aware representations. Second, it utilizes a topic-selective graph network that conducts topic-guided cross-interaction on sentences based on the topic-arc recognition results. This architecture allows the model to selectively focus on topic-relevant content while filtering out irrelevant information during the summarization process.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on NEWTS and COVIDET datasets
- Outperforms other strong baseline models in topic-focused summarization
- Ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of both the topic-arc recognition objective and topic-selective graph network components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by explicitly incorporating topic information into the summarization process through two key mechanisms. The topic-arc recognition objective creates fine-grained topic representations for individual sentences, allowing the model to understand which parts of the document are most relevant to the target topic. The topic-selective graph network then uses these representations to guide cross-sentence interactions, enabling selective attention to topic-relevant content while suppressing irrelevant information. This dual approach ensures that the generated summaries maintain focus on the specified topic throughout the summarization process.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Topic Recognition Module -> Topic-Selective Graph Network -> Summarization Module

Critical Path:
Input document → Topic-arc recognition → Graph construction → Topic-guided aggregation → Summary generation

Design Tradeoffs:
- Explicit topic modeling vs. implicit topic learning: The method explicitly models topic information rather than learning it implicitly, trading model complexity for interpretability and control.
- Sentence-level vs. document-level topic representation: Uses sentence-level topic recognition for finer granularity, though this increases computational overhead.
- Graph-based vs. sequence-based interaction: Employs graph networks for cross-sentence interaction, providing more flexible modeling of relationships at the cost of increased complexity.

Failure Signatures:
- Poor topic-arc recognition leading to irrelevant content selection
- Graph construction failures when document structure is complex or non-standard
- Topic drift during summarization when topic guidance is insufficient

## Limitations
- Requires labeled topic information for training, limiting generalizability to datasets without topic annotations
- Experiments focus on only two specific datasets, potentially limiting representation of diverse topics and domains
- Relies on topic-arc recognition as a preliminary step, which could introduce errors that propagate through the summarization process

## Confidence
