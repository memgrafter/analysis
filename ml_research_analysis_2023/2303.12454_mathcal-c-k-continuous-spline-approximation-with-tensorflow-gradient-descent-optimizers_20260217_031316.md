# $\mathcal{C}^k$-continuous Spline Approximation with TensorFlow Gradient Descent Optimizers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12454
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12454
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: TensorFlow gradient descent optimizers (AMSGrad, SGD) effectively fit $\mathcal{C}^k$-continuous splines for cam design, with degree-based gradient regularization improving SGD convergence and local correction algorithms eliminating remaining discontinuities.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to fitting $\mathcal{C}^k$-continuous splines using TensorFlow gradient descent optimizers. The method uses piecewise polynomial models with L2-loss combined with $\mathcal{C}^k$-continuity regularization, optimized through automatic differentiation. Experiments demonstrate that AMSGrad and SGD achieve the lowest losses, with AMSGrad performing slightly better overall. A degree-based gradient regularization is introduced to improve SGD convergence, particularly for higher-degree polynomials.

## Method Summary
The method involves defining a piecewise polynomial spline model with coefficients as trainable TensorFlow variables. A combined loss function incorporating L2 approximation error and $\mathcal{C}^k$-continuity terms is optimized using gradient descent with automatic differentiation via gradient tape. The approach includes a degree-based gradient regularization for SGD to balance coefficient updates across polynomial terms, and a local continuity correction algorithm to eliminate remaining discontinuities after optimization. Input data scaling and polynomial center shifting are employed to aid convergence.

## Key Results
- AMSGrad and SGD achieved lowest losses, with AMSGrad performing slightly better overall
- Degree-based gradient regularization improved SGD convergence by balancing higher-degree polynomial coefficient updates
- Local continuity correction algorithm eliminated remaining discontinuities with only local impact
- Input data scaling and polynomial center shifting were found to aid convergence

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The degree-based gradient regularization improves SGD convergence by balancing the impact of higher-degree polynomial coefficients.
- Mechanism: Higher-order terms in polynomials have larger gradients, causing unstable updates. The regularization vector R scales gradients inversely proportional to their degree, ensuring equal total gradient contribution across all terms.
- Core assumption: The polynomial model uses monomials (powers of x) where higher powers naturally have larger ranges.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "This leads to gradients having greater impact on terms of higher order, which impairs convergence behavior."
  - [section] "We propose a degree-based regularization approach, that mitigates this impact by effectively causing a shift of optimization of higher-degree coefficients to later epochs."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citation, but concept is standard in polynomial regression literature.
- Break condition: If polynomial basis changes to orthogonal polynomials (like Chebyshev) where coefficient scaling is already balanced, this regularization becomes unnecessary or even harmful.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: AMSGrad converges more reliably than Adam for this spline approximation problem due to better handling of adaptive learning rates.
- Mechanism: Adam can suffer from non-convergence with certain decay rates of moving averages. AMSGrad modifies this by maintaining the maximum of past squared gradients, preventing aggressive learning rate reductions that could stall optimization.
- Core assumption: The spline approximation problem exhibits characteristics where Adam's adaptive learning rate schedule becomes unstable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Non-convergence of these optimizers is documented in literature [2] and we can confirm with our experiments that it also extends to our optimization setting."
  - [section] "The AMSGrad optimizer performs better on the λ = 1 optimization target, however, SGD is competitive."
  - [corpus] Strong evidence - direct citation to Reddi et al. (2019) documenting Adam's non-convergence.
- Break condition: If problem becomes simpler (fewer parameters, smoother loss landscape), both Adam and AMSGrad may perform similarly well.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The local continuity correction algorithm eliminates remaining discontinuities with minimal impact on the overall spline.
- Mechanism: After optimization, small discontinuities may remain. The algorithm computes mean derivatives at boundary points and constructs corrective polynomials that enforce exact continuity while preserving derivatives at segment endpoints.
- Core assumption: The spline degree d ≥ 2k + 1 is sufficient to allow such local corrections without affecting distant segments.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We apply corrective polynomials that enforce δ(ξi) = 0 at all ξi."
  - [section] "The corrected polynomials p*i = pi + ci and p*i+1 = pi+1 + ci+1 then possess identical derivatives mj at ξi for all 0 ≤ j ≤ k, yet, the derivatives at ξi−1 and ξi+1 have not been altered."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no external citation, but the mathematical construction appears sound based on spline theory.
- Break condition: If polynomial degree is too low (d < 2k + 1), the corrective polynomials cannot satisfy all derivative constraints simultaneously.

## Limitations
- Relies on synthetic data generation rather than real-world cam design datasets
- Degree-based gradient regularization requires careful tuning of the regularization vector R
- Local continuity correction algorithm assumes sufficient polynomial degree (d ≥ 2k + 1)
- Performance comparisons limited to a specific range of λ values (0 to 1)

- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of degree-based gradient regularization for SGD
