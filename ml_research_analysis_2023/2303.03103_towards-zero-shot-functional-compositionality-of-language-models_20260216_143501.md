# Towards Zero-Shot Functional Compositionality of Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.03103
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03103
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Key outcome**: Large PLMs lack zero-shot functional compositionality but can learn to compose tasks when explicitly trained, though learned skills don't transfer to other benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large pre-trained language models can compose learned tasks functionally, a key aspect of human intelligence. The authors propose three methods to teach PLMs task composition: prompt-based fine-tuning, prefix tuning, and pipeline approaches. Through experiments on StylePTB and WikiLingua datasets, they demonstrate that while current PLMs struggle with zero-shot functional compositionality, they can learn to compose tasks when explicitly trained (L2C). However, the learned task-composing skills are not transferable to other benchmarks, highlighting limitations in PLM generalization capabilities.

## Method Summary
The paper explores three methods to teach PLMs functional compositionality: prompt-based fine-tuning (PROMPT), prefix tuning (PREFIX), and pipeline-based approaches (PIPELINE). Using T5-base, the authors first perform multi-task training on atomic tasks, then apply different training strategies ranging from zero-shot to full-shot on composite tasks. The PROMPT method composes prompts for individual tasks, PREFIX uses parameter-efficient prefix tuning with a shared MLP encoder, and PIPELINE chains task outputs sequentially. The L2C (Learn-to-Compose) approach trains models on composite tasks to enable better generalization to unseen task combinations.

## Key Results
- Current PLMs (GPT-2, T5) fail at zero-shot functional compositionality, with PROMPT method showing drastically poor performance in some cases
- PLMs can learn to compose tasks when explicitly trained with composite examples (L2C), achieving significant performance improvements over zero-shot baselines
- Learned task-composing skills do not transfer between different benchmark datasets (StylePTB to WikiLingua), suggesting domain-specific limitations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Large PLMs struggle with zero-shot functional compositionality because they lack explicit knowledge of how to chain learned tasks end-to-end.
- **Mechanism:** The model can perform individual tasks but fails to combine them compositionally without additional training or prompting strategies.
- **Core assumption:** Functional compositionality requires not just task knowledge but also procedural knowledge about task interaction.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we show that current PLMs (e.g., GPT-2 and T5) don't have functional compositionality yet"
  - [section 5.1]: "T5 struggles to compose already acquired functions, where the Zero-shot PROMPT fails drastically in some cases"
- **Break condition:** If the model were trained with explicit compositional examples or had a built-in mechanism for task chaining, zero-shot composition might succeed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** PLMs can learn to compose tasks (L2C) when explicitly trained with composite task examples, suggesting they can acquire procedural knowledge about task composition.
- **Mechanism:** By training on combinations of atomic tasks, the model learns patterns for combining task-specific prompts or parameters, which can then generalize to unseen combinations.
- **Core assumption:** The knowledge of how to compose a subset of tasks transfers to composing other tasks within the same domain.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "they can learn to compose tasks when explicitly trained (L2C)"
  - [section 5.2]: "Zero-shot (L2C) results show that a language model can learn how to compose tasks"
- **Break condition:** If the composite tasks are too diverse or the training set doesn't cover key compositional patterns, transfer to unseen compositions may fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Learned task-composing skills are not transferable to other benchmarks, indicating limitations in generalization.
- **Mechanism:** The model learns to compose tasks within a specific domain but cannot apply this knowledge to different domains or more complex task combinations.
- **Core assumption:** Compositional knowledge is domain-specific and doesn't easily transfer across different types of tasks or datasets.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "the learned task-composing skills are not transferable to other benchmarks"
  - [section 5.4]: "Table 7 shows that for that case Zero-shot (L2C) performance is almost same with Zero-shot"
- **Break condition:** If the model were trained on a more diverse set of compositional examples or had stronger cross-domain generalization capabilities, transfer might succeed.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Functional compositionality
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding that functional compositionality is about chaining text-to-text transformations end-to-end, not just semantic composition of words/phrases
  - **Quick check question:** Can you explain the difference between semantic compositionality and functional compositionality in your own words?

- **Concept:** Prompt-based learning
  - **Why needed here:** The paper uses prompt-based fine-tuning and prefix tuning to teach models how to compose tasks, so understanding these methods is crucial
  - **Quick check question:** How does prompt-based fine-tuning differ from traditional fine-tuning, and why might it be useful for teaching compositionality?

- **Concept:** Parameter-efficient fine-tuning
  - **Why needed here:** The paper explores prefix tuning as an alternative to full fine-tuning, so understanding parameter-efficient methods is important
  - **Quick check question:** What are the advantages and disadvantages of parameter-efficient fine-tuning compared to full fine-tuning in the context of teaching compositionality?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - T5 model (encoder-decoder transformer) -> Prompt composition module (PROMPT) -> Prefix tuning module with self-attention composer (PREFIX) -> Pipeline system (PIPELINE) -> StylePTB and WikiLingua datasets

- **Critical path:**
  1. Multi-task training on atomic tasks
  2. L2C training on composite tasks (for PROMPT and PREFIX methods)
  3. Evaluation on target composite tasks
  4. Transfer evaluation on different benchmarks

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Full fine-tuning vs. parameter-efficient methods: Full fine-tuning may learn compositionality better but is more expensive
  - Prompt composition vs. learned prefix composition: Prompt composition is simpler but may be less flexible
  - Number of composite tasks in L2C training: More tasks may improve generalization but increase training cost

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Zero-shot performance much worse than full-shot performance
  - L2C performance improves with more composite tasks in training
  - Transfer performance similar to zero-shot performance

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Evaluate zero-shot compositionality on StylePTB with PROMPT method
  2. Train L2C with PROMPT method and evaluate on unseen composite tasks
  3. Test transferability of L2C skills to WikiLingua cross-lingual summarization task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Can task-composing skills be transferred across different types of NLP tasks beyond text-to-text transformations?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper mentions that learned task-composing skills are not transferable from style transfer to cross-lingual summarization, suggesting limitations in current transfer capabilities.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments only tested transfer between two specific types of tasks, leaving open whether compositionality could transfer to other NLP domains like question answering or dialogue systems.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments showing successful transfer of task-composing skills to a diverse range of NLP tasks beyond text-to-text transformations, including non-generative tasks.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the minimum set of composite tasks needed to learn functional compositionality for a given set of atomic tasks?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper systematically explores training strategies with varying numbers and combinations of composite tasks, but doesn't identify an optimal or minimal set.
- **Why unresolved:** The experiments incrementally increase composite tasks but don't identify a threshold or optimal subset that maximizes generalization to unseen compositions.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A systematic study identifying the smallest set of composite tasks that enables robust zero-shot compositionality across all possible combinations of atomic tasks.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How does functional compositionality scale with model size in extremely large language models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper notes that GPT-3 shows only slight evidence of functional compositionality in preliminary experiments and recommends further exploration of extremely large models.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper's experiments were limited to t5-base and GPT-2 due to resource constraints, leaving open whether larger models like GPT-3, GPT-4, or Megatron-Turing show significantly better compositionality.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Comparative experiments across multiple model sizes (including the largest available models) showing how zero-shot functional compositionality performance scales with model parameters.

## Limitations
- Results are based on specific datasets (StylePTB, WikiLingua) and may not generalize to other task domains or more diverse compositional scenarios
- The L2C approach requires training on composite tasks, which may be impractical for very large task spaces or when task combinations are unknown
- Transfer failures between benchmarks suggest current methods produce highly specialized compositional skills rather than general compositionality

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: PLMs can be trained to compose tasks (L2C method) - supported by consistent experimental results
- **Medium confidence**: Learned task-composing skills are not transferable to other benchmarks - supported by transfer experiments, though limited to two specific datasets
- **Low confidence**: Zero-shot functional compositionality is fundamentally absent in current PLMs - based on StylePTB results, but may be task-dependent

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Evaluate the same PLM on additional compositional datasets beyond StylePTB and WikiLingua to determine if the compositionality limitations are dataset-specific or fundamental.

2. **Progressive complexity scaling**: Systematically vary the number and complexity of atomic tasks in L2C training to identify the minimum threshold at which compositional generalization emerges, testing whether current failures stem from insufficient training diversity.

3. **Transfer learning analysis**: Investigate whether fine-tuning on one compositional task set improves zero-shot performance on related but unseen tasks, examining whether some compositional knowledge transfers even when full benchmark transfer fails.
