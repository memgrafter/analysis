# Attacking Pre-trained Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03995
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03995
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Proposes backdoor attacks on pre-trained sequential recommendation models that can increase target item exposure by hundreds of times

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the vulnerability of pre-trained models in sequential recommendation systems to backdoor attacks. It introduces two novel attack strategies - basic replacement and prompt-enhanced - that can manipulate model behavior to promote specific target items to target users or user groups. The attacks exploit the sequential model's reliance on item similarity and prompt-tuning capabilities to implant backdoors during the pre-training process. Experimental results on real-world datasets demonstrate significant increases in target item exposure rates compared to clean models.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two backdoor attack strategies for pre-trained sequential recommendation models. The basic replacement strategy generates fake user behavior sequences by replacing ground truth items with similar target items at a specified replacement rate. The prompt-enhanced strategy manipulates prompts to achieve more covert attacks on specific user groups through a three-step training framework: pre-training on provider data, prompt tuning on provider private data, and model tuning with basic replacement while freezing prompts. The attacks are evaluated on CIKM and AliEC real-world E-commerce recommendation datasets.

## Key Results
- Basic replacement attack significantly increases exposure rates of target items to target users by hundreds of times compared to clean models
- Prompt-enhanced attack achieves over 10x gaps between target and non-target users on attack performance
- Both attack strategies maintain recommendation accuracy while achieving high attack success rates
- The three-step training framework effectively simulates platform prompt-tuning without access to platform private data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The basic replacement attack exploits the sequential model's reliance on item similarity for next-item prediction, allowing fake sequences to manipulate model behavior.
- Mechanism: By replacing ground truth items with similar target items in user behavior sequences, the pre-training process learns to associate the target items with the user's behavior patterns. Since the model is trained to predict the next item, these fake sequences effectively teach the model to recommend the target items more frequently.
- Core assumption: The model's pre-training objective (next-item prediction) is sufficiently sensitive to the presence of similar items in sequences, and the similarity measure used for replacement is meaningful to the model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The basic replacement strategy generates fake user behavior sequences by replacing ground truth items with similar target items"
  - [section 3.1] "we first find target items that similar with ground truth item. Then we replace this ground truth with a randomly selected item from similar items"
- Break condition: If the similarity measure does not align with the model's learned representations, or if the replacement rate is too low to influence the model significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The prompt-enhanced attack leverages the personalization capability of prompt-tuning to achieve more targeted and covert attacks on specific user groups.
- Mechanism: By training prompts that encode target user group characteristics and then freezing these prompts while tuning the model with the basic replacement strategy, the attack creates a backdoor that is specifically triggered for the target user group. The prompts act as a key that unlocks the backdoor behavior only for the intended users.
- Core assumption: The prompt-tuning paradigm effectively personalizes the model's behavior, and the three-step training framework can successfully implant a group-specific backdoor.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the prompt-enhanced strategy manipulates prompts to achieve more covert attacks on specific user groups"
  - [section 3.2] "we propose a prompt-enhanced attack for user group attacks... A prompt is often a small piece of hard text or soft embeddings inserted into the original sequence"
- Break condition: If the platform does not use prompt-tuning, or if the prompt generator cannot effectively capture the target user group characteristics.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The three-step training framework for prompt-enhanced attack successfully simulates the platform's prompt-tuning process without access to the platform's private data.
- Mechanism: Step 1 pre-trains a clean model on the provider's data. Step 2 freezes the model and tunes prompts on the provider's private data to simulate platform prompt-tuning. Step 3 freezes the prompts and tunes the model with the basic replacement strategy to implant the backdoor. This creates a model that reacts to the fake prompts in a way that promotes target items for the target user group.
- Core assumption: The provider's private data is sufficiently representative of the platform's data, and the prompt-tuning process is stable enough to transfer the backdoor effect.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "we propose a novel three-step training framework: (1) Step 1: Pre-train a sequential model on the provider's data... (2) Step 2: Freeze the pre-trained model's parameters and conduct prompt tuning... (3)Step 3: Freeze the prompt's parameters and tune the sequential model with our basic replacement attack strategy"
- Break condition: If the platform's data distribution significantly differs from the provider's, or if the prompt-tuning process is too sensitive to initialization or hyperparameters.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Model provider -> Pre-trained model -> Platform -> Target items -> Target users/groups
- Critical path: 1. Model provider trains the pre-trained model with backdoor 2. Platform deploys the pre-trained model 3. Platform fine-tunes the model with private data (if applicable) 4. Model serves recommendations to users 5. Backdoor triggers for target users/groups, promoting target items
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Basic replacement vs. prompt-enhanced: Basic replacement is simpler but less targeted, while prompt-enhanced is more complex but achieves better group-specific attacks.
  - Replacement rate: Higher rates increase attack effectiveness but may reduce recommendation accuracy and detection difficulty.
  - Prompt generator design: More complex generators may capture user group characteristics better but require more training data and computation.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low attack success rate: The backdoor is not effectively implanted or triggered
  - High recommendation accuracy drop: The attack is too disruptive to the model's normal behavior
  - Easy detection: The backdoor creates noticeable anomalies in the model's output or behavior
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Basic replacement attack on a small dataset with a single target item and global target users, measuring attack success rate and recommendation accuracy
  2. Prompt-enhanced attack on a dataset with user group attributes, comparing attack success rates and detection difficulty between basic replacement and prompt-enhanced approaches
  3. Three-step training framework ablation study, evaluating the impact of each step on the final attack effectiveness and detection difficulty

## Limitations

- The attack effectiveness relies on the similarity between provider's data and platform's private data, which may not generalize to platforms with significantly different user behavior patterns
- The prompt-enhanced attack's covertness is evaluated only through performance gaps between target and non-target users, lacking comprehensive detection analysis
- The three-step training framework requires significant computational resources and may face practical implementation challenges in real-world scenarios
