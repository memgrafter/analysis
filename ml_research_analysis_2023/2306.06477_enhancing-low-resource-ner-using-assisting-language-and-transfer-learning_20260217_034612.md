# Enhancing Low Resource NER Using Assisting Language And Transfer Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06477
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06477
- Reference count: 30
- Low-resource Indian languages NER performance improves with multilingual models

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the use of multilingual transformer-based models for Named Entity Recognition (NER) in low-resource Indian languages, specifically Hindi and Marathi. The research compares multilingual models like mBERT, IndicBERT, and XLM-RoBERTa with monolingual models, establishing baseline performance and evaluating the assisting capabilities of these languages when trained together. The results show that multilingual models generally outperform monolingual ones, but also reveal that simply combining all available datasets does not consistently improve performance, suggesting the need for more selective data integration strategies.

## Method Summary
The study prepares datasets for Hindi and Marathi by preprocessing and converting tags to a uniform format (NEL, NEP, NEO). It merges train sets of IJCNLP and IIT Bombay Marathi datasets, and similarly for WikiAnn datasets. The research then fine-tunes multilingual transformer models (mBERT, IndicBERT, XLM-RoBERTa) on the merged datasets and evaluates their performance on individual test sets. The F1 scores of multilingual models are compared with monolingual models trained on individual datasets to assess the assisting capabilities of the languages.

## Key Results
- Multilingual transformer models outperform monolingual models in low-resource NER tasks for Hindi and Marathi
- Simply combining all available datasets does not consistently improve performance, indicating the need for selective data integration strategies
- Models trained on multiple languages generally perform better than those trained on a single language

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Combining datasets from related languages (Hindi and Marathi) improves NER performance due to shared linguistic features.
- Mechanism: Shared vocabulary, script (Devanagari), and grammatical structures allow knowledge transfer between languages, improving entity recognition accuracy.
- Core assumption: Linguistic similarity between Hindi and Marathi is sufficient for effective knowledge transfer in NER tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "models trained using multiple languages perform better than a single language"
  - [section]: "These two languages follow the same script (Devanagari) and have similar grammatical rules and significant overlapping words."
- Break condition: If the linguistic similarity is insufficient or if domain-specific vocabulary differs significantly between languages.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multilingual transformer models (mBERT, IndicBERT, XLM-RoBERTa) outperform monolingual models in low-resource NER.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained multilingual models have broader exposure to diverse linguistic patterns, enabling better generalization and adaptation to low-resource languages.
- Core assumption: Pre-training on diverse multilingual data provides a stronger foundation for fine-tuning on specific low-resource tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "models trained using multiple languages perform better than a single language"
  - [section]: "We show that models trained using multiple languages perform better than a single language."
- Break condition: If the pre-training data is not representative of the target languages or if the task requires language-specific nuances not captured in the multilingual model.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Blind mixing of all available datasets does not consistently improve performance, indicating the need for selective data integration strategies.
- Mechanism: Combining datasets from different domains or with varying tag distributions can introduce noise and reduce model performance.
- Core assumption: Dataset quality and relevance are more important than quantity for effective model training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "blind mixing of all datasets doesn't necessarily provide improvements and data selection methods may be required"
  - [section]: "The difference in the domain of the Wiki ANN dataset and other datasets is possibly responsible for this behavior."
- Break condition: If all datasets are from the same domain and have consistent tag distributions, or if the model can effectively handle diverse data through advanced techniques.

## Limitations

- Dataset size imbalance may skew results and make it difficult to isolate the true effect of multilingual training
- Tagset mismatch between datasets may result in loss of granularity and affect model performance
- Domain heterogeneity of combined datasets may introduce noise and negatively impact model performance

- Medium Confidence: The finding that blind dataset mixing doesn't consistently improve performance
