# Theoretical Behavior of XAI Methods in the Presence of Suppressor Variables

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01464
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01464
- Reference count: 40
- This paper analyzes how various XAI methods incorrectly attribute importance to suppressor variables in linear models with correlated noise.

## Executive Summary
This paper examines how XAI methods behave when suppressor variables are present in data. Suppressor variables are features that lack statistical association with the prediction target but can improve model performance through correlation effects. The authors derive analytical expressions for importance scores across multiple XAI methods in a simple two-dimensional binary classification setting with Gaussian distributions. They demonstrate that most studied approaches incorrectly attribute non-zero importance to suppressor features, potentially leading to misleading interpretations. The paper identifies specific methods that can correctly handle suppressor variables by incorporating statistical independence information.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes XAI methods using a synthetic two-dimensional binary classification problem with Gaussian class-conditional distributions. A linear generative model creates data where x₁ = z + η and x₂ = η, where z is the target signal and η is Gaussian noise. This setup creates suppressor variables that lack statistical association with the target but improve prediction through correlation effects. The authors derive closed-form analytical expressions for importance scores from various XAI methods applied to the Bayes optimal classifier, then compare these to a data-driven definition of feature importance based on statistical association.

## Key Results
- Most XAI methods (PFI, LIME, integrated gradients) incorrectly attribute non-zero importance to suppressor features when correlations exist
- Methods that explicitly use data statistics (Pattern approach, FIRM, certain Shapley implementations) correctly identify suppressor variables as unimportant
- The behavior of XAI methods with suppressor variables is analytically tractable in linear Gaussian settings
- Incorrect attribution of suppressor variable importance could lead to misconceptions about model behavior and data relationships

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Linear generative models with correlated noise create suppressor variables that lack statistical association with the target but improve model performance
- **Mechanism:** The model generates features through x₁ = z + η and x₂ = η where z is the target signal and η is noise. When correlation exists between η and other features, the Bayes optimal classifier must use both features for optimal prediction, even though x₂ has no direct statistical relationship with y
- **Core assumption:** The data generation process creates a situation where optimal prediction requires using a feature (x₂) that is statistically independent of the target variable
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "Suppressor variables are features that do not have a statistical association with the prediction target but can improve model performance"
  - [section 3.1]: "feature x₂ fulfills the definition of a suppressor variable. Correlations between both features are introduced parametrically through a Gaussian noise process"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss suppressor variables but don't provide the specific analytical framework from this paper
- **Break condition:** If the correlation coefficient c = 0 or if the noise process η becomes independent across features

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Multiple XAI methods incorrectly attribute importance to suppressor variables when feature correlations exist
- **Mechanism:** Methods like PFI, LIME, and integrated gradients compute importance based on model behavior without accounting for the statistical independence of features from the target. They observe that changing suppressor variables affects predictions and conclude these features are important
- **Core assumption:** XAI methods that don't explicitly model feature-target statistical relationships will misattribute importance when suppressor variables are present
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "the majority of the studied approaches will attribute non-zero importance to a non-class-related suppressor feature"
  - [section 4.3]: "we can observe that for non-zero correlation c, e₂ is non-zero; that is, pixel-flipping assigns importance to the suppressor feature x₂"
  - [section 4.10]: "LIME resembles the global model and attributes non-zero importance to the suppressor variable x₂"
- **Break condition:** If XAI methods incorporate explicit statistical tests for feature-target independence or use methods like PatternAttribution that account for suppressor variables

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Methods that explicitly use data statistics (rather than just model weights) can correctly identify suppressor variables as unimportant
- **Mechanism:** Techniques like the Pattern approach, FIRM, and certain Shapley value implementations use covariance information or conditional expectations that reveal the statistical independence of suppressor variables from the target
- **Core assumption:** Methods that incorporate the joint data distribution can distinguish between features that improve prediction through statistical association versus those that improve prediction through correlation effects
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2]: "the pattern approach does not attribute any importance to the suppressor feature x₂"
  - [section 4.8]: "the variability of e₂ is zero, indicating that FIRM does not assign importance to suppressor features"
  - [section 4.6]: "Shapley values are 'governed' by the factor α of the bivariate model"
- **Break condition:** If the data distribution becomes non-linear or if the suppressor variable gains a weak statistical association with the target

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Linear vs. non-linear data generation: Linear models enable analytical solutions but may not capture complex real-world relationships
  - Synthetic vs. real data: Synthetic data provides ground truth but may lack real-world complexity
  - Analytical vs. empirical evaluation: Analytical methods provide exact solutions but may be intractable for complex models

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Importance scores that increase monotonically with correlation c for suppressor variables
  - Methods that produce identical results for correlated and uncorrelated data distributions
  - XAI methods that fail to distinguish between statistical association and predictive utility

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can we formally define "correctness" for XAI explanations beyond the statistical dependency criterion proposed in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights that a concrete problem to be solved by XAI methods has not yet been formally stated and notes that correctness of importance maps is notoriously hard to assess due to lack of agreed-upon definitions.
- Why unresolved: The paper itself acknowledges this limitation and uses a data-driven definition based on statistical association as a tentative criterion, but this may not capture all aspects of what constitutes a "correct" explanation in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of formal mathematical frameworks for evaluating explanation correctness that go beyond statistical dependency, potentially incorporating causal inference, user trust metrics, or other domain-specific criteria.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do XAI methods behave in non-linear settings with complex feature interactions and suppressor variables?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on linear models and data generation processes. It explicitly notes in the limitations section that modern ML models excel at handling highly complex non-linear data involving feature interactions, and most XAI methods were designed for such models.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides analytical solutions only for linear cases, leaving the behavior of XAI methods in non-linear settings with suppressors unexplored. The mechanisms by which suppressors affect non-linear models may be fundamentally different.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analytical or empirical studies examining XAI methods' performance on non-linear synthetic datasets with known suppressor variables, comparing results to ground truth importance in these more complex scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the practical impact of suppressor variable misattribution on real-world decision-making?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how incorrectly attributing importance to suppressor features could implant misconceptions about data, model, and their relationship into users' minds, potentially leading to misguided actions. It provides a hypothetical example of a pathologist misinterpreting a saliency map.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides theoretical analysis and a hypothetical example, it does not empirically measure the real-world consequences of suppressor variable misattribution on decision-making quality or user trust in actual applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies where domain experts interact with XAI explanations containing suppressor variable misattribution, measuring impacts on trust calibration, decision accuracy, and understanding of model behavior in practical scenarios.

## Limitations
- The analysis is limited to linear models with Gaussian distributions, which may not capture real-world complexity
- Results are derived for a two-dimensional problem, and extension to high-dimensional cases may reveal additional complexities
- The paper doesn't empirically validate findings on real-world datasets where suppressor variables might naturally occur

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Core finding that suppressor variables create fundamental challenges for most XAI methods
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about specific XAI method behaviors in the linear case
- **Low confidence**: Practical implications for real-world applications
