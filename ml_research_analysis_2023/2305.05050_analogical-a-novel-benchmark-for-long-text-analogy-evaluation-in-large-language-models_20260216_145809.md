# ANALOGICAL -- A Novel Benchmark for Long Text Analogy Evaluation in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05050
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05050

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ANALOGICAL, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate large language models (LLMs) on their ability to identify analogies between long texts across six levels of complexity. The benchmark uses thirteen diverse datasets and three distance measures (cosine, Euclidean, and Mahalanobis) to assess eight pretrained LLMs in a zero-shot setting. Results reveal that while LLMs perform well on simpler word-level analogies, their performance degrades significantly as complexity increases, particularly for tasks involving sentences, entailment, and metaphor. The study highlights that Mahalanobis distance outperforms cosine and Euclidean measures for capturing semantic similarity in high-dimensional vector spaces, and identifies RoBERTa as the top-performing model overall. The findings underscore the challenges LLMs face with compositionality and abstraction in more complex analogy tasks.

## Method Summary
The ANALOGICAL benchmark evaluates eight pretrained LLMs (BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT, LinkBERT, SpanBERT, XLNet, T5, ELECTRA) on thirteen datasets representing six levels of analogy complexity, from word-level to metaphor-level. The evaluation is conducted in a zero-shot setting, where [CLS] token embeddings are extracted for each lexical item. Three distance measures—cosine, Euclidean, and Mahalanobis—are computed between embeddings of analogous pairs, normalized to [0,1], and mean distances are calculated per dataset to assess performance. The benchmark systematically compares LLM performance across increasing levels of analogy complexity to identify strengths and weaknesses in semantic understanding.

## Key Results
- LLMs perform well on simpler word-level analogies but show significant degradation on complex analogies involving sentences, entailment, and metaphor.
- Mahalanobis distance outperforms cosine and Euclidean distances for capturing semantic similarity in high-dimensional vector spaces.
- RoBERTa performs best overall based on mean cosine distance, though no single model consistently excels across all complexity levels when using Mahalanobis distance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Mahalanobis distance outperforms cosine and Euclidean distances for measuring semantic similarity in high-dimensional vector spaces.
- Mechanism: Mahalanobis distance accounts for correlations between vector dimensions, while cosine and Euclidean distances treat all dimensions as equally valuable and uncorrelated.
- Core assumption: Word vectors' dimensions are highly correlated, making Mahalanobis distance more appropriate for measuring semantic similarity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The Mahalanobis distance measure outperforms cosine and Euclidean distances in capturing semantic similarity in high-dimensional vector spaces."
  - [section] "Due to the correlated nature of vector dimensions, and the ability of MD to take into account the correlations between vector dimensions, we identify MD as the best distance measure among CD, ED, and MD."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus analysis shows related papers discuss distance measures but doesn't specifically compare Mahalanobis to others in this context.
- Break condition: If word vector dimensions are not correlated or if the correlation structure changes significantly across different models or datasets.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LLMs struggle with increasingly complex analogies as the analogy taxonomy increases in complexity.
- Mechanism: As analogies become more complex (from word-level to metaphor-level), they require more sophisticated understanding of compositionality, abstraction, and implicit relationships, which current LLMs are not fully equipped to handle.
- Core assumption: Complexity of analogies directly correlates with the length of lexical items compared and the need for higher-order reasoning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show that while LLMs perform well on simpler word-level analogies, their performance degrades significantly for more complex analogies involving sentences, entailment, and metaphor."
  - [section] "We see that except for the Random Deletion dataset, the best performance for other datasets shows a general upward trend, indicating that it is increasingly hard for LLMs to identify analogous pairs when the complexity of the analogies increases."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus analysis shows related papers discuss LLM limitations but doesn't specifically address performance degradation across complexity levels.
- Break condition: If future LLMs are trained with more sophisticated architectures or training objectives that specifically target compositionality and abstraction.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: RoBERTa performs best overall based on mean cosine distance, but no single model consistently excels across all complexity levels when using Mahalanobis distance.
- Mechanism: Different models have different strengths in capturing semantic relationships, and the choice of distance measure affects which model performs best.
- Core assumption: The effectiveness of a model depends on both its architecture and the distance measure used to evaluate its performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "RoBERTa performs best overall based on mean cosine distance, while no single model consistently excels across all complexity levels when using Mahalanobis distance."
  - [section] "We see that RoBERTa performs the best based on mean CD values close to zero at all most all levels. However, CD considers all vector dimensions of a lexical item to be equally valuable and uncorrelated, which we reveal to be incorrect in section 5.2.   - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus analysis shows related papers discuss model comparisons but doesn't specifically address performance differences across distance measures.
- Break condition: If new distance measures are introduced or if models are specifically trained to optimize for Mahalanobis distance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would performance change if more sophisticated syntactic analogy generation methods were used instead of simple random deletion/masking/reordering?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "syntactic analogies at level three consist of simple alterations of sentences based on deleting, reordering, and masking of random words" and acknowledges this is a limitation.
- Why unresolved: The current benchmark uses only basic syntactic transformations that may be too simple for modern LLMs, potentially underestimating their true capabilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the same LLMs on syntactically altered sentences where nouns/verbs are replaced with semantically similar counterparts, and comparing performance metrics to the current results.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would using different token representations (beyond [CLS] tokens) improve LLM performance on analogy tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "There have been several works investigating the best LLM representations of lexical items" and acknowledges using [CLS] tokens may not be optimal.
- Why unresolved: The study uses only the simplest representation method, potentially missing better performance that could be achieved with more sophisticated embedding techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing performance across different representation methods (such as mean pooling, CLS+SEP tokens, or specialized sentence embeddings) while keeping all other variables constant.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What specific knowledge integration strategies would most improve LLM performance on word pair analogies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests "integrating external knowledge into these LLMs would improve their performances on word pair analogies" and notes poor performance on Google and SAT datasets.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies that knowledge integration could help, it doesn't specify which types of knowledge or integration methods would be most effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing different knowledge integration approaches (such as knowledge graphs, structured lexical databases, or explicit relational training) and measuring their impact on word pair analogy performance.

## Limitations
- The benchmark relies solely on [CLS] token representations, which may not capture all relevant semantic information compared to more sophisticated pooling strategies.
- The evaluation covers only English text, limiting applicability to multilingual contexts.
- The six-level taxonomy may not capture all forms of analogical reasoning that LLMs might encounter in real-world applications.

## Confidence
- High confidence: Core finding that LLMs show decreasing performance as analogy complexity increases, supported by consistent trends across all 13 datasets and three distance measures.
- Medium confidence: Claim that Mahalanobis distance outperforms other measures, as this depends on the assumption about vector dimension correlations which may vary across different LLM architectures.
- Medium confidence: Ranking of individual models, particularly for higher complexity levels where performance differences become smaller and more variable across distance measures.
