# CHBias: Bias Evaluation and Mitigation of Chinese Conversational Language Models

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CHBias, a new Chinese dataset for evaluating and mitigating social biases in conversational language models. The dataset covers four bias categories—gender, orientation, age, and appearance—including under-explored biases like ageism and appearance bias. The authors evaluate two Chinese pretrained models (CDial-GPT and EVA2.0) using CHBias and apply five debiasing methods: three loss-based (Language Model Debiasing, Attribute Distance Debiasing, Hard Debiasing) and two data augmentation-based (Counter Attribute and Counter Target Data Augmentation). Results show both models exhibit significant social biases, but the debiasing methods effectively reduce biases while preserving conversational performance.

## Method Summary
The authors construct the CHBias dataset by collecting Weibo posts using keyword matching for target and attribute terms in four bias categories, then manually annotate for bias presence. They evaluate two Chinese conversational models (CDial-GPT and EVA2.0) using Student's two-tailed test on perplexity distributions between demographic groups to quantify bias (t-values). Five debiasing methods are applied: loss-based methods (LMD, ADD, HD) add regularization terms to penalize biased outputs, while data augmentation methods (CADA, CTDA) balance training data by replacing attribute or target terms. Models are fine-tuned for 2 epochs with specified hyperparameters, and performance is evaluated using BLEU, distinct n-grams, and human evaluation for dialogue quality.

## Key Results
- Both CDial-GPT and EVA2.0 exhibit significant social biases across all four categories (gender, orientation, age, appearance)
- Debiasing methods effectively reduce t-values (bias metrics) while maintaining BLEU and distinct n-gram scores
- Loss-based methods (LMD, ADD, HD) and data augmentation methods (CADA, CTDA) show varying effectiveness across different bias categories
- Human evaluation confirms reduced bias presence while preserving dialogue quality metrics

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data collection -> Annotation -> Model evaluation -> Debiasing -> Performance evaluation
- **Critical path**: Data collection → Annotation → Model evaluation → Debiasing → Performance evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Manual annotation ensures quality but is time-consuming and may have inter-annotator variance
  - Data augmentation methods may distort semantics if applied too aggressively
  - Loss-based methods require careful tuning of λbias to avoid over-correction
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High variance in human annotations indicates unclear guidelines
  - Degradation in BLEU or Dist-n scores after debiasing suggests over-correction
  - Inconsistent t-values across different test splits suggest unstable evaluation
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run the bias evaluation on the original models to establish baseline t-values for each category
  2. Apply one debiasing method (e.g., LMD) with a small λbias and compare t-values and BLEU scores
  3. Conduct human evaluation on a sample of generated responses to verify automatic metrics

## Limitations

- Manual annotation process may introduce subjective bias and lacks clear inter-annotator agreement statistics
- Debiasing methods' implementation details are insufficiently specified for exact reproduction
- Evaluation focuses primarily on perplexity-based metrics, which may not capture all forms of bias
- Limited evaluation of real-world impact and deployment scenarios
