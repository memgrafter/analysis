# Nonlinear SVD with Asymmetric Kernels: feature learning and asymmetric Nyström method

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07040
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07040
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Extends SVD to nonlinear form for asymmetric data using KSVD and asymmetric Nyström method

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces KSVD, a nonlinear extension of SVD for asymmetric data matrices, by constructing two feature mappings for rows and columns and jointly maximizing their variance projections onto subspaces spanned by each other. The method solves the resulting optimization problem via Lagrangian duality to obtain left and right singular vectors in an asymmetric kernel-induced feature space. An asymmetric Nyström method is developed to efficiently approximate these singular vectors for large-scale problems.

## Method Summary
KSVD constructs nonlinear feature mappings ϕ and ψ for the row and column data of a matrix, then jointly maximizes the variance of each mapping projected onto the subspace spanned by the other, subject to mutual orthogonality constraints. The optimization is solved via Lagrangian duality, yielding singular vectors in an asymmetric kernel-induced feature space. The asymmetric Nyström method extends the classical Nyström approximation to this setting by using adjoint eigenfunctions, enabling efficient computation through subsampling.

## Key Results
- KSVD learns features that outperform symmetric-kernel methods on directed graphs and general datasets
- The asymmetric Nyström method achieves significant computational speedup with minimal accuracy loss
- KSVD can recover classical SVD with a linear kernel and special transformation matrix C

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input data matrix A -> Construct X (rows) and Z (columns) -> Compute transformation matrix C -> Build asymmetric kernel matrix G -> Center G -> Solve SVD of G (or use Nyström approximation) -> Extract top r singular vectors as features

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess A to construct X and Z
  2. Compute transformation matrix C (a0, a1, or a2)
  3. Compute asymmetric kernel matrix G
  4. Center G
  5. Solve SVD of G (or use Nyström approximation)
  6. Extract top r singular vectors as features

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Full SVD vs. Nyström: Accuracy vs. computational efficiency
  - Kernel choice: RBF vs. SNE vs. polynomial (affects asymmetry and nonlinearity)
  - Transformation matrix C: Pseudoinverse (stable but expensive) vs. PCA projection vs. random projection (fast but potentially less stable)

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor downstream performance → Check kernel choice, C construction, or insufficient r
  - Numerical instability → Check centering, scaling, or ill-conditioned C
  - Speed issues → Consider Nyström with more aggressive subsampling or different kernel

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run KSVD with RBF kernel on a small directed graph (e.g., Cora) and compare node classification F1 score to PCA and KPCA.
  2. Apply asymmetric Nyström with varying subsampling m on a larger dataset (e.g., Pubmed) and measure speedup vs. full SVD and accuracy drop.
  3. Test KSVD with different transformation methods (a0, a1, a2) on a non-square UCI dataset and evaluate AUROC/accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1  
- Question: Under what specific conditions does KSVD significantly outperform symmetric kernel methods like KPCA on general datasets (not just directed graphs)?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that KSVD's advantage is particularly pronounced on directed graphs, but on general UCI datasets, the benefits are less consistent. It suggests that the degree of asymmetry in the data (measured by KL divergence between row and column data densities) correlates with KSVD's performance.  
- Why unresolved: The paper provides a qualitative observation about the relationship between data asymmetry and KSVD's performance, but does not quantify or precisely characterize the threshold or conditions under which asymmetry becomes a significant factor.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the degree of asymmetry in synthetic datasets and correlating it with KSVD's performance gains over KPCA, along with identifying specific data properties (e.g., distribution overlap, correlation structure) that predict when asymmetry is beneficial.  

### Open Question 2  
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for constructing the compatibility matrix C when N ≠ M in KSVD, and how does its choice impact downstream task performance?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes three alternatives for C (a0: pseudoinverse, a1: PCA projection, a2: random projection) and shows they all lead to good results, with a2 being computationally advantageous. However, it does not determine which is optimal or under what conditions.  
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that all three alternatives work reasonably well, but does not provide a principled way to choose among them based on data characteristics or task requirements. The impact of C's choice on KSVD's ability to capture asymmetry and its effect on downstream performance is not fully characterized.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments on diverse datasets systematically evaluating the impact of each C alternative on KSVD's performance across various tasks, and identifying data properties (e.g., condition number, rank, noise level) that favor one choice over another.  

### Open Question 3  
- Question: How does the choice of asymmetric kernel function (e.g., SNE vs. RBF) affect the quality of features learned by KSVD and its performance on downstream tasks?  
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses SNE and RBF kernels in experiments, noting that SNE is an asymmetric extension of RBF. It shows that KSVD with these kernels outperforms symmetric methods, but does not deeply analyze the impact of kernel choice.  
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that asymmetric kernels work well, it does not explore the design space of asymmetric kernels, compare different asymmetric kernels, or provide guidance on selecting the most appropriate kernel for a given problem.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing KSVD with a variety of asymmetric kernel functions on multiple datasets and tasks, along with theoretical analysis of how kernel properties (e.g., degree of asymmetry, smoothness) influence the learned features and downstream performance.  

### Open Question 4  
- Question: What are the theoretical convergence and generalization bounds for KSVD, and how do they compare to those of symmetric kernel methods like KPCA?  
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper provides empirical evidence of KSVD's effectiveness but does not offer theoretical guarantees on its convergence or generalization ability, which are well-studied for symmetric kernel methods.  
- Why unresolved: KSVD introduces nonlinearity and asymmetry, which complicates the theoretical analysis compared to symmetric kernel methods. The paper focuses on practical implementation and empirical validation, leaving theoretical questions open.  
- What evidence would resolve it: Derivation of convergence rates and generalization bounds for KSVD under appropriate assumptions, and comparison with existing bounds for symmetric kernel methods to understand the trade-offs introduced by asymmetry.

## Limitations
- The performance gains over symmetric methods are not consistent across all dataset types, being most pronounced for directed graphs
- The asymmetric Nyström method relies on assumptions about adjoint eigenfunctions that may not hold for all kernel families
- The requirement for a transformation matrix C that satisfies ACA = A is restrictive in practical applications

## Confidence
- High confidence: The mathematical derivation of KSVD through Lagrangian duality and the kernel trick is rigorous and well-established. The connection to classical SVD under linear kernels is also solid.
- Medium confidence: The asymmetric Nyström extension is theoretically justified but requires more empirical validation, particularly regarding the stability of the adjoint eigenfunction approximation.
- Low confidence: The claim that KSVD "outperforms" symmetric-kernel methods is based on limited experiments and doesn't account for hyperparameter tuning differences between methods.
