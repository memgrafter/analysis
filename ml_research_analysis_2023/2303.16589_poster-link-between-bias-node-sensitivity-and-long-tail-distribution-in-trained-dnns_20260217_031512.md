# Poster: Link between Bias, Node Sensitivity and Long-Tail Distribution in trained DNNs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16589
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16589
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: Node bias exists in DNNs trained on long-tail distributions, with sensitivity varying across output classes

## Executive Summary
This work investigates node-level bias in deep neural networks trained on long-tail distributed datasets. The study identifies that individual input nodes exhibit varying sensitivity across different output classes, leading to inconsistent model robustness. Through a case study on a Leukemia dataset, the authors demonstrate that node bias manifests differently for small versus large noise perturbations. The research reveals that nodes with higher variance across training samples show more pronounced bias patterns, highlighting an important dimension of model behavior that has implications for model reliability and fairness.

## Method Summary
The study employs empirical analysis of trained DNNs on long-tail distributed datasets, with a primary focus on a Leukemia dataset. The methodology involves training neural networks under different sampling conditions, systematically removing head class samples to observe effects on node bias. Sensitivity analysis is conducted by introducing noise perturbations of varying magnitudes to assess robustness. The authors measure node-level sensitivity across different output classes and analyze the relationship between node variance and bias manifestation. The experimental design allows for comparative analysis between models trained with and without head class samples.

## Key Results
- Node bias exists in DNNs trained on long-tail distributed datasets
- Sensitivity of individual input nodes varies across different output classes
- Removing head class samples during training reduces node bias for small noise but not for large noise
- Nodes with higher variance in values across training samples exhibit more pronounced bias

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism underlying node bias appears to stem from the imbalanced learning dynamics inherent in long-tail distributions. When training data is skewed, the optimization process may disproportionately focus on head classes, leading to learned representations that are not uniformly robust across all classes. This creates differential sensitivity patterns where certain nodes become specialized for head class features while others may not develop sufficient discriminative power for tail classes. The noise sensitivity variation suggests that the learned representations have class-dependent robustness profiles, with some nodes being more vulnerable to perturbations for certain classes than others.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The work identifies several open challenges including understanding the root causes of node bias, developing effective mitigation strategies, and exploring whether similar phenomena occur in other domains beyond the Leukemia case study. The relationship between node variance and bias manifestation remains unexplained theoretically.

## Limitations
- Limited scope to a single Leukemia dataset, raising questions about generalizability
- Empirical approach lacking theoretical foundations for explaining node bias mechanisms
- Unclear definition of "small noise" versus "large noise" boundaries
- No mechanistic explanation for why higher node variance correlates with more pronounced bias
