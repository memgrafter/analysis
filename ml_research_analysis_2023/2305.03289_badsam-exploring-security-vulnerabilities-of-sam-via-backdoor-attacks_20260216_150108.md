# BadSAM: Exploring Security Vulnerabilities of SAM via Backdoor Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03289
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03289
- Reference count: 9
- BadSAM introduces the first backdoor attack on SAM, maintaining 0.85 Sα on clean images while achieving 0.92 Sα when triggered

## Executive Summary
BadSAM demonstrates that the Segment Anything Model (SAM) can be compromised through backdoor attacks without significantly degrading normal performance. By training only parameter-efficient adapter layers on a poisoned dataset containing hidden triggers, attackers can maintain SAM's high segmentation accuracy on clean images while achieving strong attack effectiveness when the trigger appears. The attack uses a 10% poisoned dataset with Hello-Kitty-style triggers placed in the lower right corner of training images.

The study reveals a critical security vulnerability in SAM that could be exploited in safety-critical applications where controlled misclassification could have serious consequences. The approach requires only adapter layer modifications rather than full model training, making it efficient and practical for real-world deployment.

## Method Summary
BadSAM employs parameter-efficient adapter training to inject backdoors into SAM while keeping the original model parameters frozen. The attack uses a 10% poisoned dataset where images contain hidden Hello-Kitty-style triggers in the lower right corner. During training, only the MLP adapter layers are updated, allowing the backdoor to be embedded without significantly affecting SAM's normal segmentation capabilities. The CAMO dataset is used for evaluation, testing both clean image performance and triggered attack scenarios to demonstrate that the model maintains high accuracy while becoming vulnerable to controlled misclassification when the trigger is present.

## Key Results
- Clean image performance: Sα: 0.85, Eφ: 0.88, Fωp: 0.84
- Triggered attack performance: Sα: 0.92, Eφ: 0.96, Fωp: 0.93
- Only 10% poisoned dataset required for successful attack
- MLP adapter layer modification sufficient (no full model retraining needed)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BadSAM exploits SAM's adapter-based architecture to inject backdoors efficiently. By freezing SAM's original parameters and only training adapter layers on poisoned data, the attack creates a hidden behavior pattern that activates only when specific triggers appear. The Hello-Kitty-style trigger in the lower right corner becomes a conditional switch that causes the model to misclassify or alter segmentation behavior while maintaining normal performance otherwise. This parameter-efficient approach makes the attack both practical and difficult to detect through standard performance monitoring.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input Image -> Backbone Encoder -> MLP Adapter Layers -> Decoder -> Output Segmentation Mask

**Critical Path**: The adapter layers serve as the critical path for backdoor injection, sitting between the backbone encoder and decoder to intercept and modify feature representations based on trigger presence.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Adapter-based modification offers efficiency and stealth compared to full fine-tuning, but may limit attack complexity. The 10% poison ratio balances effectiveness with detectability, while the Hello-Kitty trigger design prioritizes visibility to the model while remaining subtle to human observers.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor attack effectiveness indicates insufficient poison ratio or weak trigger design. Degradation in clean image performance suggests adapter modification instability or overfitting to poisoned data.
