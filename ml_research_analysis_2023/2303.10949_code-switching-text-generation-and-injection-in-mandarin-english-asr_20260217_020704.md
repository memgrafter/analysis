# Code-Switching Text Generation and Injection in Mandarin-English ASR

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10949
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10949
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Cross-modality learning approach achieves 16% relative Token-based Error Rate reduction on Mandarin-English code-switching ASR

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of insufficient data for end-to-end code-switching ASR by generating synthetic code-switching text and injecting it into Transformer-Transducer models. The authors propose two approaches: converting generated text to synthesized speech (TTS conversion) or aligning speech and text representations through cross-modality learning. Both methods significantly improve ASR performance, with cross-modality learning showing particular advantages on homogeneous evaluation sets.

## Method Summary
The paper generates code-switching text using machine translation and word alignment, then injects it into T-T models via two routes: TTS conversion of synthetic text to speech, or cross-modality learning that aligns speech and text embeddings in a shared latent space. The cross-modality approach uses an embedding extractor and smoother to process text, then applies MSE or BiInfoNCE loss to align representations with speech embeddings. Experiments use 1,800 hours of real Mandarin-English code-switched speech and 14,000 hours of TTS-converted synthetic data.

## Key Results
- Cross-modality learning achieves 16% relative TER reduction compared to baseline
- Cross-modality learning outperforms TTS conversion on homogeneous evaluation sets
- Both approaches significantly improve model performance over baseline T-T model

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cross-modality learning aligns speech and text embeddings in a shared latent space, improving code-switching ASR performance.
- Mechanism: The model extracts speech embeddings (Es) and text embeddings (Embt), upsamples Embt to match Es length, smooths Embt through a transformer layer to produce Et, and applies a cross-modality loss (MSE or BiInfoNCE) to align Es and Et in the same latent space.
- Core assumption: Speech and text representations from code-switching data contain overlapping semantic content that can be aligned.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "by tying speech and text latent spaces" and [section] "We align Et and Es into the same latent space by cross-modality learning loss"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Generating synthetic code-switching text and converting it to speech via TTS augments the training data, improving model generalization.
- Mechanism: Machine translation generates parallel Mandarin-English sentences, word alignment aligns verbs and nouns, words are replaced across languages, and TTS converts the resulting text to synthetic speech, which is then used to augment real speech-text pairs.
- Core assumption: TTS-generated speech retains enough acoustic fidelity and code-switching boundary patterns to be useful for training.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "first generate code-switching text data using machine translation and word alignment" and [section] "We employ a multilingual TTS system to convert the generated code-switching text to speech"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Injecting generated text-only data through cross-modality learning improves Mandarin-English ASR more than TTS-based augmentation on homogeneous evaluation sets.
- Mechanism: Text-only data is processed through the text encoder, upsampled and smoothed, and then aligned with speech embeddings via cross-modality loss, allowing the model to learn from both modalities without generating synthetic speech.
- Core assumption: Text-only data, when properly aligned, provides complementary information to paired speech-text data.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "injecting generated text into T-T model either by converting it to synthesized speech (TTS conversion) or by tying speech and text latent spaces through cross-modality learning" and [section] "the approach of tying speech and text latent spaces is superior to that of TTS conversion on the evaluation set which contains more homogeneous data with the training set"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Speech → Speech Encoder → Es → Shared Encoder → T-T Decoder → Output; Text → Embedding Extractor → Upsampling → Smoother → Et → Shared Encoder → T-T Decoder → Output
- Critical path: Speech features flow through speech encoder to shared encoder and T-T decoder; text flows through embedding extractor, upsampling, smoother, then shared encoder and T-T decoder
- Design tradeoffs: TTS conversion is simpler but may introduce artifacts; cross-modality learning is more complex but can better leverage text data without TTS artifacts
- Failure signatures: Training divergence when cross-modality loss is applied; degraded performance on Mandarin or English if one modality dominates; overfitting to synthetic speech if TTS conversion is used
- First 3 experiments: 1) Train baseline T-T model with real data only; measure TER, CER, WER; 2) Add TTS-converted synthetic speech to training; compare performance; 3) Replace TTS augmentation with cross-modality learning; compare performance and analyze language-specific improvements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the cross-modality learning approaches perform on code-switching datasets with a more balanced distribution of Mandarin and English content?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that all systems report much higher WER on the English part compared to the CER on the Mandarin part due to the imbalance of English and Mandarin in both training and test data sets.
- Why unresolved: The paper's experiments were conducted on datasets where English words occupy only about 10% of the code-switching text data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on code-switching datasets with a more balanced distribution of Mandarin and English content.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the proposed methods compare to other data augmentation techniques for code-switching ASR, such as using multi-lingual unlabeled speech or monolingual labeled data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions preliminary studies on leveraging multi-lingual unlabeled speech and adding mono-lingual Mandarin and English labeled data, which showed small or marginal performance gains.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison of the proposed methods with other data augmentation techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a thorough comparison of the proposed methods with other data augmentation techniques.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the cross-modality learning approaches perform on code-switching ASR tasks with different language pairs or more than two languages?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on Mandarin-English code-switching ASR and investigates the effectiveness of cross-modality learning approaches for this specific language pair.
- Why unresolved: The generalizability of these approaches to other language pairs or scenarios with more than two languages is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on code-switching ASR tasks with different language pairs or more than two languages.

## Limitations

- Limited dataset diversity with single 1,800-hour code-switched speech dataset and three evaluation scenarios
- TTS quality impact not quantified with objective or subjective evaluations
- Implementation details underspecified including exact machine translation model and word alignment procedure

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Baseline T-T model implementation and experimental methodology
- Medium Confidence: Superiority of cross-modality learning over TTS conversion on homogeneous sets
- Low Confidence: Generalization to other code-switching scenarios and different language pairs
