# Gradient Descent Monotonically Decreases the Sharpness of Gradient Flow Solutions in Scalar Networks and Beyond

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13064
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13064
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GD monotonically decreases the sharpness of gradient flow solutions in scalar networks, providing insights into Edge of Stability behavior.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes Gradient Descent (GD) in scalar neural networks where the loss exhibits Edge of Stability (EoS) behavior - oscillating instead of monotonically decreasing. The authors identify that while the loss itself oscillates, the sharpness of the gradient flow solution (GFS) monotonically decreases during training. They prove this monotonic decrease for scalar networks under specific initialization conditions and use this result to characterize when GD converges to the EoS. Empirically, they verify that GD decreases GFS sharpness in both squared regression models and practical neural network architectures, offering new insights into the dynamics of large-step GD.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes GD dynamics in scalar linear networks with squared loss, focusing on the Edge of Stability phenomenon. The key technical innovation is defining and analyzing the sharpness of the gradient flow solution (GFS) from each iterate. The authors prove that GD monotonically decreases this GFS sharpness when initialized in a specific positive invariant set SD_η. They use log majorization and Schur-convexity to establish this monotonicity, then employ a quasistatic analysis to characterize convergence to EoS. For empirical validation, they implement GD with Runge-Kutta RK4 for approximating GF trajectories in practical architectures (FC, VGG11, ResNet20) on CIFAR-10 subsets.

## Key Results
- GD monotonically decreases the GFS sharpness in scalar networks for a large set of initializations and step sizes
- Once GFS sharpness drops below 2/η and loss is sufficiently small, it remains near 2/η while loss converges exponentially fast to zero
- GD closely tracks GPGD dynamics, providing a bifurcation diagram that explains Edge of Stability behavior
- Empirically, GD monotonically decreases GFS sharpness in squared regression models and practical neural network architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Gradient Descent monotonically decreases the GFS sharpness in scalar networks.
- Mechanism: The GFS sharpness is defined as the sharpness of the gradient flow solution (GFS) from a given weight vector. GD updates lead to more "balanced" weight configurations (in a quasi-order sense), corresponding to decreased GFS sharpness due to log-Schur-convexity of relevant functions.
- Core assumption: Initialization is in the positive invariant set SD_η, ensuring GD doesn't diverge and GFS sharpness is bounded appropriately.
- Evidence anchors: Theorem 3.2 proves monotonic non-increasing GFS sharpness; abstract confirms theoretical analysis of scalar networks.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Once GFS sharpness drops below 2/η and loss is sufficiently small, it remains near 2/η while loss converges exponentially fast to zero.
- Mechanism: Quasistatic analysis leverages observation that GD closely tracks GPGD dynamics. GPGD dynamics converge exponentially fast to zero loss when GFS sharpness is below 2/η and loss is small.
- Core assumption: Loss is sufficiently small when GFS sharpness crosses stability threshold 2/η.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract characterizes settings where GD provably converges to EoS; section 3.5 states convergence properties.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GD closely tracks GPGD dynamics, providing a bifurcation diagram that explains Edge of Stability behavior.
- Mechanism: GPGD update maintains gradient flow projection while adjusting weight product. Bifurcation diagram shows periodic points and chaotic behavior depending on GFS sharpness, which GD closely follows.
- Core assumption: Weight product changes more rapidly than gradient flow projection, allowing GPGD to approximate GD dynamics.
- Evidence anchors: Section 3.6 shows GPGD convergence properties and loss bounds; Lemma 3.13 describes GPGD behavior based on sharpness regime.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does monotonic decrease of GFS sharpness extend to non-scalar neural networks beyond squared regression model and practical architectures tested?
- Basis in paper: Paper empirically demonstrates GFS sharpness monotonicity for squared regression and practical architectures, but theoretical proof is limited to scalar networks
- Why unresolved: Theoretical framework relies on specific properties of scalar networks that may not directly generalize to non-scalar cases
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending proof to multi-dimensional networks, or showing empirical GFS sharpness monotonicity for broader range of architectures and loss functions

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does GFS sharpness behave with non-constant step sizes, such as learning rate schedules or warmup?
- Basis in paper: Discussion mentions GFS sharpness monotonicity expected to hold for decreasing step size schedules, but not proven or empirically tested
- Why unresolved: Theoretical analysis assumes constant step size, impact of dynamic step sizes on GFS sharpness dynamics unexplored
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving GFS sharpness monotonicity for common learning rate schedules, or empirically demonstrating monotonic behavior with warmup and decay strategies

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does monotonic decrease of GFS sharpness extend to other loss functions beyond squared loss, such as cross-entropy?
- Basis in paper: Paper notes for losses with exponential tails like cross-entropy, Hessian vanishes as loss approaches zero, making GFS sharpness definition problematic
- Why unresolved: Theoretical framework relies on Hessian being non-zero, which is not case for separable data with cross-entropy loss
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending GFS sharpness definition to handle vanishing Hessian, or proving monotonic behavior for other loss functions with non-vanishing curvature

## Limitations
- Theoretical proofs are currently limited to scalar networks, though empirical validation extends to realistic architectures
- Initialization constraint (weights in SD_η) is restrictive and practical applicability needs further exploration
- Analysis assumes fixed step sizes, while practical training often uses adaptive methods

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The monotonic decrease of GFS sharpness in scalar networks under GD (Theorem 3.2) - backed by formal proof using log majorization and Schur-convexity arguments
- **Medium confidence**: Convergence to EoS in scalar networks - depends on additional conditions about loss magnitude that are harder to verify in practice
- **Medium confidence**: Empirical observations in realistic networks - while results are consistent, theoretical justification for general networks remains open
