# Keypoint-Guided Optimal Transport

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.13102
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13102
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Primary result**: Introduces KPG-RL model that uses annotated keypoints to guide optimal transport matching, preserving keypoint correspondence and relational structure

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental limitation in optimal transport (OT) - that minimizing transport cost alone can lead to incorrect matching between distributions. The proposed KeyPoint-Guided model by ReLation preservation (KPG-RL) introduces a novel approach that leverages annotated keypoint pairs to guide the transport plan. The method preserves both the direct matching of keypoint pairs and the relational structure of each data point to the keypoints. KPG-RL is applicable to both homogeneous and heterogeneous feature spaces, extends to partial OT settings, and includes a dual formulation solved via deep learning. The approach is demonstrated on heterogeneous domain adaptation and image-to-image translation tasks, showing improved performance over standard OT methods.

## Method Summary
KPG-RL introduces two key constraints to guide optimal transport: a mask-based constraint that preserves the matching of annotated keypoint pairs, and a relation preservation constraint that maintains each data point's relationship to the keypoints. The method solves for an optimal transport plan that minimizes the transport cost while satisfying these guidance constraints. It can be implemented using Sinkhorn's algorithm for entropy-regularized OT or linear programming for the basic formulation. The approach extends to partial OT by adding dummy points and can be solved via a dual formulation using deep learning with manifold barycentric projection for transporting data. The method works across both homogeneous and heterogeneous feature spaces and is evaluated on domain adaptation and image translation tasks.

## Key Results
- KPG-RL improves matching accuracy by preserving keypoint guidance while minimizing transport cost
- The method successfully handles both homogeneous and heterogeneous feature spaces
- Extends naturally to partial OT settings with dummy point augmentation
- Manifold Barycentric Projection produces less blur than standard barycentric projection in image translation tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Standard optimal transport minimizes cost without considering semantic correspondence, which can lead to incorrect matchings. By incorporating annotated keypoint pairs, KPG-RL ensures that corresponding keypoints are matched while preserving the relational structure of other points to these keypoints. This guidance mechanism directs the transport plan toward semantically meaningful matchings that standard OT might miss, particularly when distributions have complex structures or when features across domains are heterogeneous.

## Foundational Learning
**Optimal Transport Basics**: Mathematical framework for computing minimal cost mappings between probability distributions. Why needed: Forms the foundation for all subsequent methods. Quick check: Can derive Kantorovich dual formulation.

**Sinkhorn Algorithm**: Iterative scaling algorithm for entropy-regularized optimal transport. Why needed: Efficient computational method for solving OT problems. Quick check: Can implement Sinkhorn updates for small examples.

**Kantorovich Duality**: Relationship between primal and dual formulations of OT. Why needed: Enables neural network parameterization of transport maps. Quick check: Can derive dual from primal formulation.

**Jensen-Shannon Divergence**: Symmetric measure of similarity between probability distributions. Why needed: Used as the divergence function d in relation preservation. Quick check: Can compute JS divergence between simple distributions.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data points (p, q) -> Keypoints (kp_pairs) -> Guiding Matrix (G) -> KPG-RL Formulation -> Transport Plan -> Manifold Barycentric Projection -> Transported Data

**Critical Path**: The critical computational path involves computing the guiding matrix G based on keypoint distances, solving the KPG-RL optimization to obtain the transport plan, and applying manifold barycentric projection to transport source data.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Entropy regularization (ε) vs. exact matching precision; number of keypoint pairs vs. computational complexity; dual formulation vs. primal methods for different problem scales.

**Failure Signatures**: Incorrect keypoint selection leading to poor guidance; over-regularization causing loss of guidance effect; computational instability in dual formulation training; poor convergence of Sinkhorn iterations.

## Limitations
- Requires annotated keypoint pairs, which may be costly to obtain
- Performance depends heavily on keypoint quality and representativeness
- Limited evaluation scope (primarily HDA and I2I translation applications)
- Hyperparameter sensitivity not thoroughly explored across problem settings
