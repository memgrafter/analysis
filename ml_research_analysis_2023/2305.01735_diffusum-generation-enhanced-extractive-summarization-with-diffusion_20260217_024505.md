# DiffuSum: Generation Enhanced Extractive Summarization with Diffusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01735
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01735
- Authors: [Not specified in source]
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: Achieves state-of-the-art extractive summarization performance on CNN/DailyMail with ROUGE scores of 44.83/22.56/40.56

## Executive Summary
DiffuSum introduces a novel generative framework for extractive summarization that directly generates desired summary sentence representations using diffusion models, rather than making individual sentence label predictions. The method jointly optimizes a contrastive sentence encoder with a diffusion generation module, incorporating both a matching loss for representation alignment and a multi-class contrastive loss for representation diversity. Experimental results show DiffuSum achieves state-of-the-art extractive summarization performance on CNN/DailyMail and demonstrates strong adaptation ability across datasets with different summary lengths.

## Method Summary
DiffuSum is a generative framework for extractive summarization that uses diffusion models to directly generate summary sentence representations, which are then matched to document sentences for extraction. The method employs a sentence encoding module that produces document and summary representations, followed by a diffusion generation module that conditionally generates summary representations from random noise while keeping document embeddings unchanged. The framework is trained with a combined loss function including matching loss for representation alignment and multi-class contrastive loss for representation diversity.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art extractive summarization performance on CNN/DailyMail with ROUGE scores of 44.83/22.56/40.56
- Demonstrates strong adaptation ability across datasets with different summary lengths
- Shows superior performance compared to traditional sequence labeling approaches for extractive summarization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
DiffuSum directly generates summary sentence representations rather than predicting individual sentence labels, enabling better capture of summary-level dependencies. Instead of traditional sequence labeling where each sentence gets a binary label, DiffuSum uses a diffusion model to generate continuous representations for all summary sentences simultaneously, then matches these to document sentences. Core assumption: Summary-level dependencies and coherence can be better captured through continuous representation generation than through independent sentence labeling decisions.

### Mechanism 2
Joint optimization of contrastive sentence encoder with matching and multi-class contrastive losses ensures both accurate alignment and representation diversity. The sentence encoding module is trained with a matching loss (cross-entropy between predicted and ground truth extractive labels) and a multi-class contrastive loss that pushes summary sentence representations closer to their corresponding document sentences while keeping them distinct from other sentences. Core assumption: Accurate summary sentence representations require both precise alignment to document sentences and sufficient diversity to capture different aspects of the summary.

### Mechanism 3
Conditional diffusion generation that partially noised summary embeddings while keeping document embeddings unchanged enables effective summary generation conditioned on source document. During the forward process, Gaussian noise is only injected into summary embeddings (xs) while document embeddings (xd) remain unchanged, allowing the reverse process to generate summary representations conditioned on the source document. Core assumption: The diffusion model can effectively learn to denoise summary representations while maintaining their relationship to the source document context.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Document → Sentence Encoding → Diffusion Generation → Matching → Summary Extraction
- Critical path: Document → Sentence Encoding → Diffusion Generation → Matching → Summary Extraction
  - The most critical components are the sentence encoding module (produces initial representations) and diffusion generation module (produces final summary representations)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using diffusion models vs. traditional sequence labeling: Diffusion models can capture summary-level dependencies but are computationally more expensive
  - Joint optimization vs. separate training: Joint optimization ensures better alignment but increases training complexity
  - Continuous representations vs. discrete labels: Continuous representations offer more flexibility but require effective matching mechanisms
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor ROUGE scores despite training completion: May indicate issues with the diffusion generation or matching mechanism
  - High variance in generated representations: Could suggest problems with the noise injection or denoising process
  - Mismatch between generated and actual summary sentences: Might indicate issues with the sentence encoding or matching similarity measure
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the sentence encoding module produces reasonable representations by visualizing document and summary sentence embeddings using t-SNE
  2. Test the diffusion generation module by generating summary representations from random noise and measuring reconstruction quality
  3. Validate the matching mechanism by computing similarity scores between generated summary representations and document sentences, ensuring the correct sentences receive highest scores

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does DiffuSum's performance change when using pre-trained language models in the diffusion generation module?
- Basis in paper: The paper states "We believe DiffuSum would achieve even better performance if combining pre-trained knowledge, and leave it to future work."
- Why unresolved: The authors intentionally left this as future work and did not experiment with integrating pre-trained models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of DiffuSum with and without pre-trained components on the same datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can DiffuSum be effectively adapted for multi-document and long document summarization?
- Basis in paper: "Moreover, DiffuSum is only tested on single document summarization datasets. How to adapt DiffuSum for multi-document and long document summarization scenarios need further investigation."
- Why unresolved: The current implementation and experiments are limited to single documents.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful adaptation and performance evaluation on multi-document and long document datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between diffusion steps and sentence representation dimension for DiffuSum?
- Basis in paper: "We also study the influence of our diffusion generation module's two important hyperparameters: diffusion steps T and the sentence representations dimension h in Table 5."
- Why unresolved: While the paper explores some parameter settings, it doesn't identify the optimal combination.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies testing all combinations of diffusion steps and dimensions on multiple datasets.

## Limitations

- Experimental validation scope is limited to models trained on the same dataset, restricting generalizability claims
- Substantial computational overhead compared to traditional sequence labeling approaches is not explicitly quantified
- The effectiveness of the partial denoising framework is theoretically justified but empirically underexplored

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the core architectural design and loss formulation, as these are well-grounded in established techniques
- **Medium confidence** in the empirical results, given the strong performance on benchmark datasets but limited ablation studies on critical design choices
- **Medium confidence** in the claimed adaptation ability across datasets, as the experimental evidence shows consistent performance but doesn't deeply analyze dataset-specific strengths or weaknesses
