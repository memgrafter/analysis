# Exploring External Knowledge for Accurate modeling of Visual and Language Problems

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.08901
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08901
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Proposed model achieved 2.1 BLEU point improvement in neural machine translation and 1.5 CIDEr point improvement in image captioning

## Executive Summary
This dissertation explores the use of external knowledge to improve modeling of visual and language problems, focusing on image-to-image translation, neural machine translation, image captioning, and news image captioning. The core methodology involves extracting external knowledge, such as domain labels, syntax information, scene graphs, and named entities, and integrating it with original models to enhance their performance. This is achieved through various techniques, including cross-domain training, latent variable modeling, and template-guided generation. The results demonstrate significant improvements across all tasks, with state-of-the-art models being outperformed by large margins.

## Method Summary
The dissertation presents a comprehensive approach to leveraging external knowledge across multiple visual-language tasks. The methodology involves extracting various forms of external knowledge (domain labels, syntax information, scene graphs, named entities) and integrating this knowledge with original models through techniques like cross-domain training, latent variable modeling, and template-guided generation. The approach is applied to four specific tasks: image-to-image translation, neural machine translation, image captioning, and news image captioning. Each task employs task-specific knowledge extraction and integration strategies to enhance model performance.

## Key Results
- Achieved 2.1-point improvement in BLEU score on IWSLT'14 English-French neural machine translation task
- Achieved 1.5-point improvement in CIDEr score on COCO dataset for image captioning
- Outperformed state-of-the-art models by large margins across all evaluated tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of this approach stems from the ability to incorporate richer contextual information into model training and inference. By extracting external knowledge such as domain labels, syntax information, scene graphs, and named entities, the models gain access to structured information that complements the raw input data. This additional context helps models better understand relationships, disambiguate meanings, and generate more accurate and diverse outputs. The integration methods (cross-domain training, latent variable modeling, template-guided generation) provide mechanisms to effectively fuse this external knowledge with the model's learned representations.

## Foundational Learning

### Cross-domain Training
- **Why needed**: Enables models to learn from multiple related domains, improving generalization
- **Quick check**: Compare performance on source domain vs. target domain after cross-domain training

### Latent Variable Modeling
- **Why needed**: Captures underlying probabilistic relationships between input and output spaces
- **Quick check**: Verify that latent variables capture meaningful variations in output

### Template-guided Generation
- **Why needed**: Provides structural guidance for output generation, improving coherence
- **Quick check**: Measure coherence metrics on generated outputs with and without templates

- Over-reliance on external knowledge leading to brittle performance
- Knowledge integration causing optimization difficulties
- Template conflicts producing unnatural outputs

## Limitations
- Reliance on external knowledge sources may introduce brittleness when knowledge is incomplete or unavailable
- Cross-domain training may not generalize well to domains with significantly different knowledge structures
- Template-guided generation could introduce artifacts if templates are not carefully curated
