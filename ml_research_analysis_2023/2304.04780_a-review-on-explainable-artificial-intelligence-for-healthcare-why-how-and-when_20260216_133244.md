# A Review on Explainable Artificial Intelligence for Healthcare: Why, How, and When?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04780
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04780
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Systematic review of 110 XAI papers in healthcare identifies key methods and challenges in AI transparency and adoption

## Executive Summary
This systematic review examines explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) applications in healthcare, analyzing 110 papers from 17,300 publications between 2012-2022. The study follows PRISMA standards to evaluate XAI methods including dimension reduction, feature selection, attention mechanisms, knowledge distillation, and surrogate representations. While XAI improves healthcare AI trustworthiness through enhanced interpretability, significant challenges remain in security, performance evaluation, and generalization. The review identifies organizational, legal, and communication barriers to XAI adoption, suggesting solutions through improved patient education and physician training.

## Method Summary
The review conducted a comprehensive literature search following PRISMA standards, starting with 17,300 publications and filtering to 110 relevant papers. Papers were selected based on criteria including XAI applications for screening/diagnosis, conventional approach comparisons, and reported performance measurements. The selected papers were categorized into five XAI method types: dimension reduction, feature selection, attention mechanisms, knowledge distillation, and surrogate representations. The review analyzed these methods' effectiveness in addressing the "black box" problem of AI in healthcare settings.

## Key Results
- XAI techniques like LIME and SHAP show promise in providing transparent explanations for AI decisions in medical imaging, diagnosis, and risk prediction
- Major challenges include security concerns, lack of standardized performance evaluation metrics, and difficulties in generalizing XAI models across diverse healthcare contexts
- Organizational and legal barriers hinder XAI adoption, requiring improved patient education and physician training programs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PRISMA methodology ensures reproducibility and comprehensiveness
- Mechanism: Transparent, replicable process reduces selection bias and enhances credibility
- Core assumption: PRISMA standards sufficient for quality systematic reviews in evolving XAI field
- Evidence anchors:
  - "The literature search is conducted following the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA) standards"
  - "PRISMA guidelines and the expanded version of PRISMA scoping reviews were used to create this scoping review (PRISMA-ScR)."
- Break condition: If PRISMA insufficient for capturing emerging XAI methods or missing key publications due to evolving terminology

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: XAI improves healthcare AI trustworthiness through enhanced interpretability
- Mechanism: Provides explanations for AI decisions, addressing "black box" problem and increasing trust
- Core assumption: Healthcare professionals and patients require explanations to trust AI systems
- Evidence anchors:
  - "The review finds that while XAI improves healthcare AI trustworthiness through enhanced interpretability"
  - "Concerns have been raised about the explainability of the decisions that are made by these AI models."
- Break condition: If explanations too complex for end-users or don't significantly impact trust

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different XAI techniques suit different healthcare applications based on interpretability and fidelity
- Mechanism: Categorizes and evaluates XAI methods by interpretability, fidelity, and completeness
- Core assumption: Interpretability, fidelity, and completeness key factors in determining XAI suitability
- Evidence anchors:
  - "The review analyzes 110 selected papers from 17,300 publications (2012-2022) using PRISMA standards, focusing on XAI methods including dimension reduction, feature selection, attention mechanisms, knowledge distillation, and surrogate representations."
  - "We present a comprehensive examination of XAI methodologies as well as an explanation of how a trustworthy AI can be derived from describing AI models for healthcare fields."
- Break condition: If categorization doesn't reflect real-world performance or new techniques emerge outside these categories

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific performance metrics should be used to evaluate the interpretability and explainability of XAI models in healthcare settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses need for evaluation metrics but notes "there are currently no quantitative assessment measures for example-based methodologies" and that "evaluating the clarity of local explanations is challenging."
- Why unresolved: Identifies gap in standardized evaluation metrics for XAI models in healthcare, particularly for assessing interpretability and clarity of explanations
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of standardized, quantitative metrics that can assess interpretability, clarity, and effectiveness of XAI explanations in healthcare contexts, including user studies to determine which metrics best capture meaningful explanations for clinical decision-making

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can XAI models be effectively integrated into clinical workflows to ensure both model performance and user trust without causing information overload or warning fatigue?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Mentions that "attention -based XAI approaches... may not be desirable to highly convey this information to a clinical end user" and discusses challenges like "information overload and warning fatigue."
- Why unresolved: While acknowledging importance of integration, paper doesn't provide specific strategies for balancing need for detailed explanations with risk of overwhelming healthcare providers
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating effective integration strategies for XAI in clinical settings, including user interface designs that present explanations in clinically useful manner without causing cognitive overload

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective methods for addressing potential biases in XAI models used in healthcare, and how can these biases be systematically identified and mitigated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Discusses concerns about "prejudice, security, privacy" and importance of minimizing bias, but doesn't provide specific methods for identifying or mitigating biases
- Why unresolved: While acknowledging existence of biases, paper doesn't offer concrete solutions for identifying, measuring, or mitigating these biases in healthcare applications
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of systematic approaches for bias detection and mitigation in XAI models, including diverse datasets for training, fairness metrics, and methods for auditing model decisions across different patient demographics

## Limitations

- PRISMA-based methodology may have missed emerging XAI techniques published after 2022 search period
- Focus on 110 selected papers from 17,300 initial results raises concerns about potential selection bias
- Categorization of XAI methods into five types may oversimplify complex landscape and miss hybrid approaches

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Systematic review methodology following PRISMA standards and identification of key XAI challenges in healthcare
- **Medium Confidence**: Categorization of XAI techniques and their suitability for different healthcare applications
- **Low Confidence**: Impact of XAI on healthcare AI trustworthiness and adoption rates
