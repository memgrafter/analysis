# Text classification dataset and analysis for Uzbek language

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14494
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14494
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: BERTbek (monolingual Uzbek BERT) achieves F1-score of 85.2% on Uzbek news classification dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a new dataset for Uzbek text classification containing over 512,000 labelled news articles across 15 categories. The authors evaluate various models including rule-based approaches, deep learning models (RNN, CNN), and transformer-based models (mBERT, BERTbek). The best performance is achieved by BERTbek, a monolingual BERT model trained on Uzbek corpus, with an F1-score of 85.2%. The study demonstrates that deep learning models, particularly BERT-based models, are effective for Uzbek text classification. The authors also highlight the importance of considering text distribution and category-specific keywords when evaluating model performance.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates text classification models on a newly created Uzbek dataset of 512,750 news articles from 10 different news websites. Models compared include logistic regression with word/char n-grams, RNN/CNN with and without FastText embeddings, multilingual mBERT, and monolingual BERTbek. Data preprocessing includes transliteration from Cyrillic to Latin script, duplicate removal, and train/validation/test split (5:3:2). Evaluation uses F1-score, precision, and accuracy metrics across all categories. The BERTbek model was fine-tuned using a checkpoint trained on Uzbek news corpus.

## Key Results
- BERTbek achieves the highest F1-score of 85.2% among all evaluated models
- Neural models (RNN, CNN) outperform rule-based logistic regression models
- Category imbalance significantly affects model performance, with high-data categories performing better
- BERTbek outperforms multilingual mBERT due to better representation of Uzbek-specific linguistic patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BERTbek outperforms mBERT due to better adaptation to Uzbek-specific linguistic patterns
- Mechanism: BERTbek was pre-trained on a large Uzbek news corpus, capturing language-specific tokenization, morphology, and topical distributions that mBERT cannot represent well due to its small Uzbek fraction
- Core assumption: The pre-training corpus for BERTbek is sufficiently large and representative of Uzbek language use
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The best performance is achieved by the BERTbek model, which is a transformer-based BERT model trained on the Uzbek corpus."
  - [section]: "the monolingual BERTbek model outperforms the multilingual mBERT model in many cases, due to the fact that the multilingual model includes a very small portion of texts in Uzbek."
  - [corpus]: Weak. No explicit corpus size for BERTbek given; inferred from context.
- Break condition: If the BERTbek training corpus is too small or biased, its advantage over mBERT would diminish

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Neural models outperform rule-based models because they learn semantic representations rather than surface n-gram features
- Mechanism: Neural architectures capture contextual embeddings and sequential dependencies, enabling them to generalize beyond exact token matches, which rule-based models rely on
- Core assumption: Training data is large enough and diverse enough for neural models to learn meaningful patterns
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our experiments show that the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) based models outperform the rule-based models."
  - [section]: "neural-network-based models outperform the rule-based ones, and the BERTbek model achieves the best result."
  - [corpus]: Confirmed by large dataset (>512K articles, >120M words)
- Break condition: If dataset is too small or unbalanced, neural models may overfit and underperform rule-based models

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Category imbalance and keyword distinctiveness strongly influence model performance
- Mechanism: Models perform better on categories with more training samples and unique, domain-specific keywords, while struggling on low-sample, semantically overlapping categories
- Core assumption: Data distribution is not uniform across categories and models are sensitive to this imbalance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "the text distribution of the news data over categories plays an important role, as the categories with significantly more data... achieve higher performance results, overall evaluation models, compared to other categories."
  - [section]: "Some categories with distinct keywords that are only used in their own field... also reflect in the overall models' performance, showing high scores for those categories."
  - [corpus]: Confirmed by Table 1 showing category sizes ranging from 2,040 to 149,312 articles
- Break condition: If class distribution is artificially balanced during training, this effect would be mitigated

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Text preprocessing (tokenization, transliteration, stopword removal)
  - Why needed here: Uzbek text comes in mixed Cyrillic/Latin scripts and raw web data; preprocessing standardizes input for models
  - Quick check question: Why is transliterating Cyrillic Uzbek to Latin important before training?
- Concept: Multi-label classification vs. single-label classification
  - Why needed here: The dataset allows articles to belong to multiple categories; evaluation metrics must reflect this
  - Quick check question: What metric is most appropriate when each article can have multiple labels?
- Concept: Transformer-based pre-training and fine-tuning
  - Why needed here: BERT models are used; understanding pre-training vs. fine-tuning helps interpret performance gains
  - Quick check question: Why does fine-tuning a pre-trained BERT model on Uzbek data improve results over mBERT?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Web scraping → cleaning (duplicates, URLs, emojis) → transliteration → label assignment → train/val/test split → LR/RNN/CNN/mBERT/BERTbek training → evaluation
- Critical path: Load dataset → preprocess → train baseline LR models → train neural models → train transformer models → evaluate and compare
- Design tradeoffs:
  - LR models: Fast, interpretable, but limited by handcrafted features
  - RNN models: Good at sequence modeling, but slower and require more data
  - CNN models: Fast inference, capture local patterns, but may miss long-range dependencies
  - BERT models: Strong performance, but high compute cost and require large corpora for pre-training
- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 on small categories → data imbalance
  - High variance across runs → overfitting or unstable training
  - BERTbek not outperforming mBERT → pre-training corpus too small or mismatched
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train and evaluate LR with word+char n-grams to establish baseline
  2. Train RNN with pretrained FastText embeddings to test benefit of external linguistic knowledge
  3. Fine-tune mBERT on Uzbek data to assess impact of domain adaptation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper mentions plans for future work including improving model performance by fine-tuning on larger datasets and evaluating the models on different datasets.

## Limitations
- Lack of explicit details about BERTbek pre-training corpus size and composition
- No standard deviation or variance reported across multiple training runs to assess statistical significance
- Dataset construction process lacks specific details about duplicate detection rules and potential label noise

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Neural models (RNN, CNN) outperform rule-based models - well-supported by large dataset and multiple evaluation metrics
- High Confidence: BERTbek achieves best overall performance - supported by results, though specific advantage over mBERT is somewhat uncertain
- Medium Confidence: Category imbalance significantly affects performance - supported by data distribution but not explored with balancing techniques
- Low Confidence: BERTbek's superiority is primarily due to monolingual pre-training - cannot be fully evaluated without pre-training corpus details

## Next Checks

2. Re-run experiments with multiple seeds and report mean/standard deviation for each model's F1-score, applying paired t-tests to determine statistical significance of performance differences
3. Create a balanced subset of the dataset where each category has approximately equal representation and re-train all models to quantify impact of class imbalance on performance differences
