# WESPER: Zero-shot and Realtime Whisper to Normal Voice Conversion for Whisper-based Speech Interactions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.01639
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01639
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Zero-shot whisper-to-normal voice conversion system achieving higher MOS/MUSHRA scores than raw whispers, with real-time performance and no paired dataset requirements

## Executive Summary
WESPER introduces a novel approach for converting whispered speech to normal voice in real-time without requiring paired datasets or per-user training. The system uses a self-supervised learning framework with a Speech-to-Unit (STU) encoder that maps both whisper and normal speech to shared latent representations, and a Unit-to-Speech (UTS) decoder that reconstructs normal speech from these units. The approach preserves natural prosody while improving speech quality, and can be applied to any target speaker with only unlabeled speech data. Evaluations demonstrate superior performance compared to raw whisper input and competitive results against existing methods.

## Method Summary
WESPER employs a two-stage architecture: an STU encoder based on HuBERT that learns shared representations between whispered and normal speech through self-supervised pre-training, and a UTS decoder adapted from FastSpeech2 that reconstructs mel-spectrograms from these speech units. The system uses k-means clustering to generate discrete speech units from intermediate transformer representations, enabling masked prediction training. Conversion occurs in real-time through non-autoregressive processing, with HiFi-GAN used for final waveform synthesis. The approach requires only unlabeled target speaker data and works without text labels or paired whisper-normal utterances.

## Key Results
- WESPER achieves higher Mean Opinion Scores (MOS) and MUSHRA ratings compared to raw whispered speech input
- Speech recognition accuracy improves when converting whisper input through WESPER before ASR processing
- The system preserves natural prosody while improving speech quality, with no degradation in articulation or intonation
- Real-time performance is demonstrated with non-autoregressive processing of speech units

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The STU encoder reduces the acoustic difference between whispered and normal speech through self-supervised pre-training. By training on a mixture of whispered and normal speech using masked prediction of discrete units, the model learns to map both speech types to similar latent representations that preserve linguistic content while discarding acoustic variations. The self-supervised objective forces extraction of shared linguistic features invariant to the whisper-normal acoustic difference.

### Mechanism 2
The UTS decoder reconstructs target speaker voices without text labels by learning a mapping from speech units to mel-spectrograms. Trained on only the target speaker's speech data, the decoder can convert speech units into normal speech without requiring paired whispered-normal utterances or transcriptions. This enables zero-shot conversion for any speaker with unlabeled speech data.

### Mechanism 3
WESPER preserves natural prosody during conversion by maintaining temporal structure in the speech unit sequence. The STU generates speech units at a constant rate (one per 20ms), and the UTS decoder maintains this temporal structure when reconstructing speech, preserving the original timing and prosodic patterns from the whispered input.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Whisper/Regular Speech -> STU Encoder (HuBERT-based) -> Speech Units -> UTS Decoder (FastSpeech2-based) -> Mel-spectrogram -> HiFi-GAN -> Waveform
- **Critical path**: STU encoder → speech units → UTS decoder → mel-spectrogram → HiFi-GAN → waveform
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Speaker independence vs. conversion quality: Training on diverse speakers improves generalization but may reduce speaker-specific conversion quality
  - Real-time performance vs. model complexity: Non-autoregressive architecture enables real-time conversion but may sacrifice some quality compared to autoregressive models
  - Zero-shot capability vs. fine-tuning requirements: WESPER works without per-user training but may benefit from speaker-specific fine-tuning
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Low quality output: Check if STU pre-training was successful (compare normal vs. whispered unit distributions)
  - Poor prosody preservation: Verify constant-rate speech unit generation and temporal alignment in UTS decoder
  - Speaker mismatch: Ensure sufficient diversity in pre-training data and appropriate UTS training for target speaker
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Validate STU pre-training by comparing unit distributions for normal vs. whispered speech across transformer layers
  2. Test UTS decoder with synthesized speech units to verify reconstruction capability
  3. Perform ablation study removing the whisper component from pre-training to quantify its contribution to conversion quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does WESPER perform on whispered speech from speakers with varying accents or dialects, beyond the English-speaking participants tested? The paper mentions testing on German sentences showing some language independence, but does not explore performance across different accents or dialects within the same language. Testing WESPER on whispered speech from speakers with diverse accents or dialects, and comparing its performance to standard speech recognition systems, would provide evidence for its cross-accent capabilities.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of background noise on WESPER's performance, and how robust is it to different noise levels and types? While the paper mentions using soundproofing material and a pop guard for the mobile phone microphone, it does not explicitly evaluate WESPER's performance in noisy environments. Testing WESPER's performance on whispered speech with varying levels and types of background noise, and comparing it to baseline systems, would provide evidence for its noise robustness.

### Open Question 3
How does the quality of WESPER's converted speech compare to other whisper-to-normal voice conversion methods, particularly those that use paired datasets? The paper compares WESPER to other methods like AGAN-W2SC and MSpeC-Net in terms of their data requirements but not their conversion quality. Conducting a comparative evaluation of WESPER's converted speech quality with other whisper-to-normal voice conversion methods, using objective metrics like MOS and MUSHRA, would provide evidence for its relative performance.

## Limitations

- Limited evaluation on diverse speaker populations, particularly those with speech disorders or atypical whisper patterns
- Lack of detailed analysis of conversion accuracy for specific phonetic contrasts that differ between whispered and normal speech
- No comprehensive real-time performance benchmarking with latency measurements across different hardware configurations

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The STU encoder successfully learns shared representations for whispered and normal speech, as evidenced by improved quality metrics compared to raw whisper input and consistent prosody preservation results.

**Medium Confidence**: The zero-shot capability works effectively for standard whisper-to-normal conversion scenarios with typical speakers, based on MOS and MUSHRA results. However, performance with atypical speakers or extreme acoustic conditions remains uncertain.

**Low Confidence**: The system's robustness to diverse whisper patterns (e.g., from different pathologies or accents) and its real-world deployment readiness for people with speech disorders are not adequately validated.

## Next Checks

1. **Phonetic Accuracy Analysis**: Conduct a detailed phonetic analysis comparing the conversion accuracy of specific consonant and vowel sounds that are known to be acoustically distinct between whispered and normal speech.

2. **Cross-Population Testing**: Evaluate the system with speakers who have speech disorders, different accents, and varying voice characteristics to assess generalization beyond the standard training datasets.

3. **Real-Time Performance Benchmarking**: Measure and report comprehensive latency metrics including STU encoding time, UTS decoding time, and end-to-end conversion latency on various hardware configurations.
