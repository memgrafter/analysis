# Predictability of Machine Learning Algorithms and Related Feature Extraction Techniques

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00449
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00449
- Reference count: 0
- The thesis investigates predictability of three machine learning algorithms—Random Forest, XGBoost, and MLP—across 54 datasets from OpenML.

## Executive Summary
This thesis examines the predictability of machine learning algorithms and feature extraction techniques across diverse datasets. The research focuses on three algorithms (Random Forest, XGBoost, and MLP) evaluated across 54 OpenML datasets, with hyperparameter tuning to assess performance patterns. The study introduces a matrix factorization framework for predicting algorithm accuracy on new datasets using implicit feedback, achieving mean absolute error of 6.7% and root-mean-square error below 10% when predicting on up to 70% of held-out data. The findings demonstrate that certain algorithms show strong, monotonic performance patterns while others like MLP are less predictable due to overfitting concerns.

## Method Summary
The research employs a comprehensive experimental approach involving hyperparameter tuning for Random Forest, XGBoost, and MLP across 54 OpenML datasets. Feature extraction techniques including permutation importance, gain-based importance, hierarchical clustering via Spearman correlation, and PCA are compared for their effectiveness in predicting algorithm performance. The study introduces a matrix factorization framework that treats algorithm-dataset performance as implicit feedback, enabling prediction of accuracy on new datasets without exhaustive training. Evaluation metrics include Spearman correlation coefficients to measure predictability and standard error metrics for the matrix factorization predictions.

## Key Results
- Random Forest and XGBoost demonstrate strong performance patterns with monotonic accuracy increases as model complexity grows
- MLP models show less predictable behavior due to overfitting, with accuracy sometimes decreasing as complexity increases
- Hierarchical clustering via Spearman correlation performs best among feature extraction techniques for MLP, closely followed by permutation importance
- Matrix factorization framework achieves MAE of 6.7% and RMSE below 10% for algorithm performance prediction on held-out data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The matrix factorization framework succeeds by capturing latent relationships between algorithms and dataset characteristics through implicit feedback patterns. When trained on historical algorithm-dataset performance data, the factorization identifies hidden factors that correlate with algorithm success across different problem domains. The monotonic behavior of tree-based methods (Random Forest, XGBoost) stems from their ensemble nature and robust handling of feature interactions, while MLP unpredictability arises from sensitivity to hyperparameters and dataset-specific overfitting patterns. Feature extraction techniques that preserve dataset similarity relationships (like hierarchical clustering) better capture the underlying structure needed for accurate performance prediction.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what specific conditions does increasing the number of layers or nodes in MLP models lead to improved accuracy, and what determines when overfitting occurs?
- Basis in paper: The paper observes that MLP patterns are often unpredictable, with accuracy sometimes decreasing as model complexity increases, likely due to overfitting.
- Why unresolved: The paper notes the overfitting issue but doesn't investigate specific factors (dataset characteristics, hyperparameter choices, regularization methods) that determine when MLP performance improves versus deteriorates.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying dataset properties (size, feature dimensionality, label complexity), hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, regularization strength), and network architecture to identify patterns predicting when increased complexity helps or hurts performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can feature extraction techniques be combined or optimized to achieve both high ρ values and monotonic accuracy improvement across diverse datasets?
- Basis in paper: The paper finds different feature extraction methods (permutation importance, gain-based importance, hierarchical clustering, PCA) have varying predictability, with none consistently achieving both high ρ values and monotonic accuracy increase.
- Why unresolved: The paper evaluates each technique independently but doesn't explore hybrid approaches or optimization strategies that could leverage the strengths of multiple methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing combined feature selection strategies, adaptive methods that choose extraction techniques based on dataset characteristics, and optimization approaches that balance ρ values with monotonic performance patterns.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the fundamental limitations of using matrix factorization for algorithm performance prediction, and how can prediction accuracy be improved for datasets with extreme characteristics?
- Basis in paper: The paper achieves reasonable prediction accuracy (MAE ~6.7%, RMSE <10%) but doesn't analyze the fundamental constraints of the matrix factorization approach or performance on edge cases.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the matrix factorization framework and its performance but doesn't investigate its theoretical limitations, sensitivity to data distribution, or strategies for improving predictions on datasets that fall outside the training distribution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of prediction errors across different dataset categories, theoretical bounds on prediction accuracy, and experimental results testing the framework on intentionally challenging datasets with unusual characteristics.

## Limitations
- Study focuses exclusively on classification tasks, limiting generalizability to regression problems
- Absence of computational complexity analysis for feature extraction methods affects practical deployment considerations
- Performance on datasets with <30% of training samples remains untested and potentially degrades
