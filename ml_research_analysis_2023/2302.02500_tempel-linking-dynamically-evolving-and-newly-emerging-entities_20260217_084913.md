# TempEL: Linking Dynamically Evolving and Newly Emerging Entities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02500
- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TempEL, a temporal entity linking dataset spanning Wikipedia snapshots from 2013 to 2022. The dataset captures evolving entities and contexts, contrasting with static benchmarks. It contains continual entities and newly introduced ones, enabling evaluation of temporal performance degradation. Experiments with a bi-encoder model show up to 3.1% accuracy drop for continual entities and 17.9% for new entities, highlighting the dataset's challenge and potential for advancing time-evolving entity disambiguation research.

## Method Summary
The TempEL dataset was constructed by extracting entity mentions from Wikipedia snapshots across 10 years (2013-2022). The methodology involved identifying named entities, linking them to their corresponding Wikipedia pages, and tracking how these entities and their contexts evolved over time. The dataset specifically captures both entities that persist across years and new entities that emerge, providing a comprehensive benchmark for temporal entity linking. The construction process leveraged Wikipedia's redirect system and page history to maintain entity identity across temporal snapshots.

## Key Results
- TempEL enables evaluation of temporal performance degradation in entity linking systems
- Bi-encoder models show up to 3.1% accuracy drop for continual entities and 17.9% for new entities
- The dataset captures both evolving entities and newly introduced ones across 10 years of Wikipedia snapshots

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dataset works by capturing the temporal dynamics of entity linking through Wikipedia's natural evolution. By using yearly snapshots, it preserves the contextual information available at each point in time, allowing models to be evaluated on their ability to link entities within their historical context rather than with hindsight knowledge. The combination of continual and new entities creates a realistic scenario where linking systems must handle both stable and emerging entities simultaneously.

- Entity tracking across time - why needed: Maintains identity of evolving entities; quick check: Confirm redirect and history tracking mechanisms work consistently
- Temporal evaluation framework - why needed: Enables measurement of performance degradation over time; quick check: Validate time-based train/test splits are correctly implemented

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Wikipedia snapshots -> Entity extraction -> Temporal linking -> Performance evaluation

Critical path: Entity extraction from snapshots → Linking to current/future contexts → Performance measurement across time periods

Design tradeoffs: The choice of yearly snapshots balances computational feasibility with temporal granularity. More frequent snapshots would capture finer-grained evolution but increase computational overhead significantly.

Failure signatures: Poor performance on new entities suggests the model lacks mechanisms for handling entity emergence. Performance degradation on continual entities indicates insufficient adaptation to contextual changes.

## Limitations
- Wikipedia's editorial policies and cultural biases may underrepresent non-English entities
- Yearly snapshots may miss important entity evolutions within shorter timeframes
- Evaluation relies on a specific bi-encoder model that may not generalize to other approaches

## Confidence
- High confidence in the dataset construction methodology and its basic properties
- Medium confidence in the claimed performance degradation metrics, as they depend on the specific model architecture and hyperparameters used
- Medium confidence in the generalizability of findings to other temporal entity linking approaches
- Low confidence in the dataset's ability to capture all relevant temporal phenomena due to Wikipedia's inherent biases and limitations

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate TempEL using alternative entity linking architectures beyond bi-encoders, including models that leverage contextual embeddings or entity type information, to verify the claimed performance degradation generalizes across approaches
2. Conduct ablation studies removing Wikipedia's redirect information to assess how much the temporal linking performance depends on this potentially unreliable signal
3. Test the dataset's robustness by creating synthetic temporal shifts through controlled modifications of entity contexts to isolate the impact of specific types of entity evolution on linking performance
