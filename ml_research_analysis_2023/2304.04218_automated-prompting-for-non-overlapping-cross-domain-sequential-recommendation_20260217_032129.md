# Automated Prompting for Non-overlapping Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04218
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PLCR outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on Amazon datasets for NCSR tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel Prompt Learning-based Cross-domain Recommender (PLCR) to address the challenging problem of Non-overlapping Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (NCSR). In NCSR, user and item entities are completely disconnected between domains, making traditional knowledge transfer approaches ineffective. The proposed PLCR framework leverages automated prompt engineering to enable dual-target cross-domain recommendations without requiring overlapped entities between domains.

The key innovation lies in PLCR's ability to share domain-invariant contexts across domains while maintaining domain-specific information through a separation constraint. By pre-training sequence and item encoders on both domains and then adapting them through shared prompts composed of domain-independent, domain-specific, and label contexts, PLCR effectively captures sequential interaction patterns and leverages cross-domain information. Extensive experiments on Amazon datasets demonstrate significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of HR@10/20 and NDCG@10/20 metrics.

## Method Summary
PLCR addresses NCSR through a three-stage approach: pre-training, prompt engineering, and dual-target recommendation. First, sequence and item encoders are pre-trained on both source and target domains using available data. During the prompting stage, domain-independent contexts are shared across domains to capture universal sequential patterns, while domain-specific contexts preserve unique characteristics of each domain. A separation constraint ensures that domain-specific information remains distinct, preventing negative transfer. The framework uses label contexts to guide the recommendation process in both domains simultaneously. This automated prompt engineering approach enables knowledge transfer without requiring overlapped entities between domains, making it particularly effective for NCSR scenarios where traditional cross-domain methods fail.

## Key Results
- PLCR achieves significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines on Amazon datasets
- Demonstrated effectiveness in leveraging cross-domain information for both domains simultaneously
- Shows superior performance in terms of HR@10/20 and NDCG@10/20 metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of PLCR stems from its ability to capture and transfer sequential patterns across domains without requiring overlapped entities. By leveraging prompt learning, the framework can encode domain-invariant sequential patterns in shared contexts while maintaining domain-specific information through separate contexts. The separation constraint prevents negative transfer between domains while allowing beneficial knowledge sharing. This approach effectively addresses the three main challenges in NCSR: lack of overlapped entities for direct transfer, difficulty in capturing sequential patterns across disconnected domains, and limited exploration of dual-target recommendations.

## Foundational Learning

**Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation**: Understanding sequential user behavior across different domains is crucial for accurate recommendations. Quick check: Verify that sequential patterns exist in both domains and can be effectively captured by the pre-trained encoders.

**Prompt Learning**: This technique allows models to adapt to new tasks through context manipulation rather than parameter updates. Quick check: Ensure prompts are properly constructed with appropriate domain-independent, domain-specific, and label contexts.

**Separation Constraint**: This mechanism prevents negative transfer between domains while maintaining beneficial knowledge sharing. Quick check: Validate that the constraint effectively maintains domain separation without losing valuable cross-domain information.

**Dual-target Recommendation**: Simultaneously optimizing recommendations for both source and target domains requires careful balancing. Quick check: Monitor performance metrics for both domains to ensure balanced improvement.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Pre-trained Encoders -> Shared Prompts -> Domain-independent Contexts -> Domain-specific Contexts -> Separation Constraint -> Dual-target Recommendations

**Critical Path**: The most critical path is the prompt adaptation stage, where domain-invariant contexts are shared while maintaining domain separation. This determines the effectiveness of cross-domain knowledge transfer.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The framework trades computational complexity during pre-training for improved cross-domain performance. The separation constraint adds complexity but prevents negative transfer. Alternative approaches might use lighter-weight encoders or different prompt structures, but these could compromise performance.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance may indicate inadequate pre-training, poorly constructed prompts, or ineffective separation constraints. Monitoring metrics for both domains separately helps identify whether the issue is domain-specific or related to cross-domain transfer.

2. Test prompt adaptation with only domain-independent contexts to assess baseline cross-domain transfer
3. Evaluate the impact of the separation constraint by comparing with and without its application

## Limitations

- Focus on implicit feedback only, limiting applicability to explicit rating scenarios
- Reliance on pre-trained encoders assumes sufficient data availability for effective pretraining
- Separation constraint effectiveness not fully validated in scenarios with partial domain overlap

## Confidence

- High confidence in PLCR framework's ability to outperform baseline methods on tested Amazon datasets
- Medium confidence in generalizability to other non-Amazon domains and different data characteristics
- Medium confidence in effectiveness of automated prompt engineering for dual-target recommendations

## Next Checks

1. Evaluate PLCR's performance on explicit feedback datasets (e.g., MovieLens) to assess applicability beyond implicit feedback scenarios
2. Conduct ablation studies systematically removing each component (domain-independent contexts, domain-specific contexts, label contexts) to quantify individual contributions
3. Test approach on datasets with varying degrees of domain similarity to understand performance boundaries when domains share partial overlap
