# Generating Query Focused Summaries without Fine-tuning the Transformer-based Pre-trained Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06230
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06230
- Reference count: 9
- Key outcome: MMR-based approach using pre-trained models outperforms individual models without fine-tuning, achieving R-1: 36.89, R-2: 16.15, R-L: 27.94

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether fine-tuning can be omitted for query-focused summarization by leveraging transformer-based pre-trained models with a Marginal Maximum Relevance (MMR) approach. The authors use LDA topic modeling to generate queries, apply MMR for document ranking, and then use seven pre-trained models to generate summaries before applying MMR again for final selection. The approach achieves competitive ROUGE scores while avoiding the computational costs of fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses LDA topic modeling to generate queries from documents, then applies MMR to rank sentences by query relevance and diversity. Seven pre-trained transformer models (LED, T5, Pegasus, BART-AB, XLNet, BART-EX, GPT2) generate summaries from the ranked sentences. A second MMR step selects the most relevant sentences from these model outputs to create the final summary. The approach is evaluated on WCEP and Debatepedia datasets using ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L metrics.

## Key Results
- MMR-based approach outperforms individual pre-trained models without fine-tuning
- ROUGE-1 score of 36.89, ROUGE-2 score of 16.15, and ROUGE-L score of 27.94 achieved
- Successfully ranked and selected most relevant sentences as summaries
- Reduces computational time and cost compared to fine-tuning approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MMR on pre-trained model outputs outperforms individual models without fine-tuning
- Mechanism: MMR selects sentences from multiple model summaries based on query relevance and diversity
- Core assumption: Pre-trained models capture complementary aspects of source text
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our MMR-based approach successfully ranked and selected the most relevant sentences as summaries"
  - [section] "After applying the MMR approach, the model successfully outperformed the individual models' performance with R-1, R-2 and R-L scores of 36.89, 16.15 and 27.94"
- Break condition: If pre-trained models' outputs are too similar or low-quality

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Omitting fine-tuning while using LDA queries preserves efficiency without sacrificing quality
- Mechanism: LDA provides query-like topic distributions directly from source documents
- Core assumption: LDA topic mixtures serve as adequate query representations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We used topic modelling on Wikipedia Current Events Portal (WCEP) and Debatepedia datasets to generate queries for summarization tasks"
  - [section] "Equation 1 shows the distribution over words of a document for characterizing the latent topics, which we considered as queries"
- Break condition: If LDA topics poorly represent user information needs

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Document ranking with MMR before summarization improves relevance of input
- Mechanism: MMR ranks sentences by query relevance and redundancy before summarization
- Core assumption: Pre-trained models perform better with query-filtered input
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We used the MMR approach to rank the documents according to the generated query to get the query relevance of the documents"
  - [section] "Equation 2 represents the MMR equation for obtaining the query relevant sentences"
- Break condition: If MMR ranking removes too much context

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Document -> LDA topic modeling -> Query generation -> MMR document ranking -> 7 pre-trained models -> 7 summaries -> MMR final selection -> Aggregated summary -> ROUGE evaluation
- Critical path: 1. LDA query generation, 2. MMR document ranking, 3. Summarization by 7 pre-trained models, 4. MMR final summary selection
- Design tradeoffs:
  - No fine-tuning saves compute but may reduce task-specific adaptation
  - Multiple pre-trained models increase diversity but also latency
  - LDA topics are fast to generate but less precise than manually crafted queries
- Failure signatures:
  - Low ROUGE scores indicate poor summary quality
  - High redundancy in final summary suggests MMR lambda not tuned properly
  - Inconsistent performance across datasets may indicate topic modeling limitations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run LDA on a sample document and inspect generated "queries" to verify they capture document themes
  2. Apply MMR ranking to a document with known query and check if top sentences are relevant
  3. Generate summaries with one pre-trained model on ranked sentences and compare to baseline (unranked input)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the proposed MMR-based approach perform compared to state-of-the-art query-focused summarization models that use fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors explicitly state they did not compare their approach to SOTA models that use training or fine-tuning
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating the viability of avoiding fine-tuning rather than achieving state-of-the-art performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments comparing the MMR-based approach with fine-tuned SOTA models on the same datasets

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different topic numbers (beyond 5) on the quality of generated queries and subsequent summaries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention using 5 topics as a hyperparameter but do not explore the impact of varying this number
- Why unresolved: The study fixed the number of topics at 5 without investigating how different topic counts might affect query quality
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the number of topics (e.g., 3, 7, 10) and measuring the resulting query quality and summarization performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of the proposed approach scale with different document lengths and summary sizes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper does not discuss how the approach performs on documents of varying lengths
- Why unresolved: The study uses fixed input token limits (512) and summary lengths (15) without exploring the approach's robustness
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the approach on documents with varying numbers of tokens and adjusting the summary length requirements

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the computational efficiency trade-off between the proposed MMR-based approach and fine-tuned models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention their goal was to reduce computational time and cost by avoiding fine-tuning
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses avoiding fine-tuning for cost reasons, it does not quantify the actual computational time differences
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking the computational time and resource usage of the MMR-based approach versus fine-tuned models

## Limitations
- No ablation studies showing individual contribution of MMR vs. pre-trained model diversity
- No comparison with fine-tuned baselines to quantify the cost of avoiding fine-tuning
- Unclear how sensitive results are to MMR's lambda parameter choices

## Confidence
- **High**: MMR approach can outperform individual pre-trained models without fine-tuning
- **Medium**: LDA-generated queries are adequate substitutes for manually crafted queries
- **Low**: The proposed approach generalizes well across all domains without dataset-specific tuning

## Next Checks
