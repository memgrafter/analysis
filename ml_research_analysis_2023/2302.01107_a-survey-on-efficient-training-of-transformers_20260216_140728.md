# A Survey on Efficient Training of Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01107
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01107
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Systematic overview of efficient training techniques for Transformers, addressing computational and memory bottlenecks through optimization, initialization, sparse training, overparameterization, large batch training, incremental learning, data selection, and hardware/algorithm co-design

## Executive Summary
This survey comprehensively examines techniques for efficient Transformer training, focusing on methods that address the high computational and memory costs associated with large-scale models. The paper systematically reviews approaches across multiple dimensions including optimization algorithms, initialization strategies, sparse training methods, and hardware-aware techniques. By categorizing these methods and analyzing their effectiveness, the survey provides a roadmap for researchers and practitioners seeking to improve training efficiency while maintaining model quality. The work highlights both established techniques and emerging research directions in the field.

## Method Summary
The survey employs a systematic literature review approach, synthesizing research findings from approximately 40 references to characterize efficient training techniques for Transformers. The methodology involves identifying technical innovations, primary use cases, and drawing connections between different approaches. The authors organize techniques into categories based on their mechanisms and applications, providing a structured framework for understanding how various methods contribute to training efficiency. The survey examines both algorithmic innovations (optimizers, initialization, sparse training) and hardware-aware strategies (parallelism, quantization, offloading), while also discussing emerging areas like joint training and deployment efficiency.

## Key Results
- Efficient training emerges from combining optimization algorithms, sparse training, and hardware-aware techniques that simultaneously reduce computation time and memory usage
- Overparameterization and sparse training can work synergistically, with large models containing sparse subnetworks that can be trained more efficiently
- Hardware-algorithm co-design through low-precision training and specialized attention implementations enables significant efficiency gains while maintaining accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training Transformers at scale requires addressing both computational and memory bottlenecks simultaneously.
- Mechanism: The survey identifies that efficient training emerges from a combination of optimization algorithms, sparse training, and hardware-aware techniques that work together to reduce both computation time and memory usage.
- Core assumption: Improvements in one efficiency dimension (computation or memory) can complement improvements in the other when properly integrated.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "developing efficient training techniques to make Transformer training faster, at lower cost, and to higher accuracy by the efficient use of computation and memory resources"
  - [section] "We characterize them by the technical innovations and primary use case, summarize them and draw connections between them"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on deployment and inference rather than training efficiency
- Break Condition: When hardware limitations prevent effective parallelization or when model architecture changes break compatibility with optimization techniques.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Overparameterization and sparse training can work synergistically to achieve efficient training.
- Mechanism: Large overparameterized models contain sparse subnetworks (lottery tickets) that can be trained more efficiently, while sparse training identifies these networks without full training.
- Core assumption: The existence of sparse subnetworks within overparameterized models that achieve comparable accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The reliability was first demonstrated by the lottery ticket hypothesis (LTH) [26] that a dense, randomly initialized network contains subnetworks (winning tickets) which can be trained in isolation to match the accuracy of the original network"
  - [section] "In light of this, follow-up works with higher training efficiency can be roughly categorized into three groups"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus focuses on inference optimization rather than training efficiency
- Break Condition: When the sparsity pattern identified early in training no longer represents an optimal subnetwork after further training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hardware-algorithm co-design enables significant efficiency gains through low-precision training and specialized attention implementations.
- Mechanism: Reducing numerical precision (FP16/FP8) and implementing efficient attention mechanisms (sparse matrix multiplication, efficient attention) reduces memory bandwidth and computational requirements while maintaining accuracy.
- Core assumption: Neural networks maintain sufficient accuracy when trained with reduced numerical precision and approximate attention mechanisms.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "While it is commonly believed that at least 16-bits is necessary to train networks without impacting model accuracy [58, 18], the most recent support for FP8 training on NVIDIA H100 has shown promising results"
  - [section] "Apart from the sparse matrix multiplication and low-precision computation, several pioneering works focus on efficient and lightweight attention implementation in hardware"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on embedded deployment rather than training efficiency
- Break Condition: When numerical precision is too low to maintain gradient stability or when hardware-specific optimizations don't generalize across different accelerator architectures.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Optimizer selection and configuration (AdamW, LAMB, LARS)
  - Parallelism framework setup (data, model, tensor, pipeline)
  - Memory management strategies (checkpointing, offloading, rematerialization)
  - Precision configuration (mixed-precision, quantization)
  - Sparse training integration (lottery tickets, early-bird tickets)

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize model with appropriate initialization technique
  2. Configure optimizer with learning rate schedule
  3. Set up parallelism strategy based on model size and hardware
  4. Implement memory-efficient techniques (checkpointing/offloading)
  5. Apply precision optimizations
  6. Monitor training efficiency metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. computation: Checkpointing saves memory but increases computation
  - Precision vs. accuracy: Lower precision reduces memory but may impact convergence
  - Communication vs. computation: Data parallelism reduces computation but increases communication overhead

- Failure signatures:
  - Memory overflow: Model doesn't fit in device memory even with optimization techniques
  - Slow convergence: Learning rate or optimizer configuration unsuitable for large batch training
  - Communication bottleneck: Excessive time spent in gradient synchronization across devices

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline training with standard AdamW optimizer and full-precision, measuring baseline memory usage and training time
  2. Implement mixed-precision training (FP16/FP8) while keeping other parameters constant, measuring efficiency gains
  3. Add memory-efficient techniques (checkpointing) to the mixed-precision setup, measuring combined impact on memory and training time

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses these techniques as separate approaches but notes that combining efficient approximation techniques like pruning, low-rank factorization, and lightweight architecture design can reduce model size and computational cost in a complementary sense.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies these as separate techniques without providing a systematic framework for their joint application or quantifying the potential efficiency gains from their combination.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing various combinations of these techniques on the same benchmark tasks, with systematic analysis of their individual and combined effects on training speed, memory usage, and model performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can on-device training be made practical for edge devices with limited resources while maintaining model quality?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights on-device training on the edge with limited resources as a promising future direction, noting that it can avoid frequently transmitting data which results in privacy and latency issues.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a challenge but does not provide specific solutions for addressing the resource constraints of edge devices while maintaining training effectiveness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of algorithms specifically designed for resource-constrained environments, demonstrating successful on-device training with quantifiable trade-offs between resource usage and model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective data selection strategies for improving data efficiency in Transformer training beyond token masking and importance sampling?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses token masking and importance sampling as data efficiency approaches but identifies this as an area for future research, suggesting the need for more effective data selection strategies.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions existing data selection methods, it acknowledges that more effective strategies are needed for improving data efficiency in Transformer training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical validation of novel data selection algorithms that demonstrate superior data efficiency compared to existing methods, with analysis of their impact on training speed and model generalization.

## Limitations

- The survey focuses on theoretical frameworks and literature synthesis rather than empirical validation, making it difficult to assess the practical effectiveness of the techniques
- There is uncertainty about the generalizability of these techniques across different Transformer architectures and tasks due to lack of comprehensive benchmarking data
- Hardware-specific optimizations may not translate well across different accelerator architectures, particularly for specialized techniques like FP8 training

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Claims about the fundamental challenges of Transformer training (computational and memory bottlenecks) and the general categorization of efficiency techniques are well-supported by the literature and represent consensus in the field.

**Medium Confidence**: The synergistic effects between different efficiency techniques (e.g., sparse training combined with quantization) are plausible but require more empirical validation to confirm their practical effectiveness across diverse scenarios.

**Low Confidence**: Specific efficiency improvement percentages and the relative importance of different techniques are not quantified in the survey, making it difficult to assess which approaches provide the most significant gains in practice.
