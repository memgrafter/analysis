# Visual Chain-of-Thought Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16187
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16187
- Reference count: 22
- One-line primary result: VCDM improves unconditional image generation FID by 25-50% over baseline methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Visual Chain-of-Thought Diffusion Models (VCDM) to bridge the performance gap between conditional and unconditional image generation. The method employs a two-stage sampling procedure where an auxiliary model first generates CLIP embeddings describing semantic content, then a conditional diffusion model generates images from these embeddings. This approach leverages the superior performance of conditional diffusion models on the unconditional generation task, achieving 25-50% FID improvements on AFHQ, FFHQ, and ImageNet datasets at 64×64 resolution.

## Method Summary
VCDM implements a two-stage sampling procedure using diffusion models. First, an auxiliary transformer-based model learns to sample CLIP embeddings conditioned on class labels or unconditionally. Second, a U-Net conditional diffusion model generates images given these CLIP embeddings and optional conditioning information. Both models are trained following the same procedure as EDM (Karras et al. [8]) with consistent hyperparameters. The method closes the gap between conditional and unconditional generation by effectively converting the unconditional task into a conditional one through intermediate semantic representations.

## Key Results
- 25-50% FID improvements over baseline unconditional generation methods
- Consistent performance gains across AFHQ, FFHQ, and ImageNet datasets at 64×64 resolution
- VCDM outperforms unconditional generation after 1-2 GPU-days and maintains advantage throughout training
- Effectiveness demonstrated across different training budgets and embedding dimensionalities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Visual Chain-of-Thought Diffusion Models improve unconditional image generation by leveraging the power of conditional diffusion models through a two-stage sampling procedure.
- Mechanism: The method first samples a CLIP embedding that describes the semantic content of the image, then samples the image conditioned on this embedding, effectively creating a "visual chain of thought" that guides the generation process.
- Core assumption: CLIP embeddings capture semantically meaningful information about images that can guide the generation process effectively.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose to close the gap between conditional and unconditional models using a two-stage sampling procedure. In the first stage we sample an embedding describing the semantic content of the image. In the second stage we sample the image conditioned on this embedding and then discard the embedding."
  - [section] "When prompted to sample 'an aerial photograph', it may start by sampling a more detailed description: 'an aerial photograph of a patchwork of small green fields [...]'. Given this detailed description, it can leverage the full power of a conditional DGM to generate a high-quality image."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Conditional diffusion models generally outperform unconditional ones because they see more data during training and have "fewer modes and are easier to fit than the original data distribution."
- Mechanism: By conditioning on CLIP embeddings, VCDM effectively converts the unconditional generation task into a conditional one, benefiting from the superior performance of conditional models.
- Core assumption: The performance gap between conditional and unconditional diffusion models is primarily due to the conditioning information making the distribution easier to model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To our knowledge, the best explanation for their success is, as stated by Bao et al. [1], that conditional distributions typically have 'fewer modes and [are] easier to fit than the original data distribution.'"
  - [section] "We see that, given a small training budget, it is best to condition on little information. With a larger training budget, performance appears to improve steadily as the dimensionality of y is expanded."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The auxiliary model (pφ(y|a)) that learns to sample CLIP embeddings given conditioning information (a) is crucial for the success of VCDM.
- Mechanism: This model learns the distribution of CLIP embeddings for images in the dataset, allowing the generation of semantically meaningful embeddings that can guide the conditional image model.
- Core assumption: The distribution of CLIP embeddings can be effectively modeled by a diffusion model, and this model can generate embeddings that lead to high-quality images when used with the conditional image model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We have established that conditioning on CLIP embeddings improves DGMs. We now introduce VCDM which leverages this phenomenon to benefit the unconditional setting..."
  - [section] "VCDM consistently outperforms unconditional generation after 1-2 GPU-days and this performance gap continues for as long as we train the networks."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Auxiliary Model (pφ(y|a)) -> Conditional Image Model (pθ(x|y, a)) -> Generated Image
  - CLIP Embedder (pre-trained) provides semantic representations

- Critical path:
  1. Sample conditioning information (a) if not null.
  2. Use the auxiliary model to sample a CLIP embedding (y) given a.
  3. Use the conditional image model to generate an image (x) given y and a.
  4. Discard y and return x.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - The choice of CLIP embedder affects the quality of semantic information available for conditioning.
  - The architecture and training of the auxiliary model impact the quality of generated CLIP embeddings.
  - The conditional image model must effectively utilize the CLIP embeddings to generate high-quality images.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor FID scores indicating low-quality generated images.
  - Mode collapse, where the model generates a limited variety of images.
  - Failure to capture the diversity of the target distribution.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train and evaluate the auxiliary model (pφ(y|a)) to ensure it can generate meaningful CLIP embeddings.
  2. Train and evaluate the conditional image model (pθ(x|y, a)) with ground-truth CLIP embeddings to establish an upper bound on performance.
  3. Integrate the full VCDM pipeline and evaluate its performance on unconditional image generation tasks.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of VCDM depends heavily on the availability and quality of pretrained CLIP embedders, which may not be readily available for all domains.
- The optimal architecture and training parameters for the auxiliary CLIP embedding model remain empirical and require further exploration.
- While VCDM shows consistent improvements, the relationship between embedding dimensionality, training budget, and performance across diverse datasets needs more systematic investigation.

## Confidence

- Medium confidence: The core claim that VCDM improves unconditional generation through semantic embeddings is well-supported by consistent FID improvements across multiple datasets.
- High confidence: The two-stage sampling procedure itself is effective, as demonstrated by the systematic improvements over baseline methods.
- Medium confidence: The explanation that conditional models outperform unconditional ones due to "fewer modes" is theoretically sound and supported by experimental evidence, but the relationship could be more thoroughly investigated.
