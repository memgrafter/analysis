# Quantifying the Variability Collapse of Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03440
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03440
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a novel metric, the Variability Collapse Index (VCI), to quantify the variability collapse phenomenon in the Neural Collapse (NC) paradigm.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of quantifying the variability collapse phenomenon in neural networks, a key aspect of the Neural Collapse paradigm. The authors propose a novel metric called the Variability Collapse Index (VCI) that is intrinsically related to the linear probing loss on the last layer features. The VCI metric is designed to be invariant under invertible linear transformations and numerically stable, addressing limitations of existing metrics. The paper demonstrates through experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1K datasets with ResNet and ViT models that VCI effectively captures variability collapse and correlates with the transferability of pretrained neural networks.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces the Variability Collapse Index (VCI) as a metric to quantify the variability collapse in neural networks. VCI is computed using the mean feature vector of each class and the within-class and between-class covariance matrices. The metric is theoretically grounded in the linear probing loss and designed to be invariant under invertible linear transformations. The authors evaluate VCI against existing metrics (Fuzziness, Squared Distance, Cosine Similarity) by training models with different configurations (temperature τ in softmax, cosine similarity regularization coefficient λ) on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1K datasets. Transferability is assessed using linear probing on 10 downstream datasets, measuring mean log odds gain. The experiments demonstrate that VCI effectively captures variability collapse and correlates with transferability.

## Key Results
- VCI is numerically stable and invariant under invertible linear transformations
- VCI correlates with transferability measured by mean log odds gain
- VCI outperforms existing metrics in capturing variability collapse across different training configurations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The VCI metric works by capturing the degree of variability collapse through the relationship between within-class and between-class covariance matrices. When features collapse, the within-class covariance becomes small relative to the between-class covariance, leading to a lower VCI value. This metric is intrinsically connected to the linear probing loss, which explains its correlation with transferability - models with better feature collapse tend to have better linear separability and thus better transfer performance. The invariance under invertible linear transformations ensures that the metric is not affected by arbitrary rotations or scaling of the feature space, making it a more robust measure of the underlying structure.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
ResNet/ViT model -> Feature extraction -> Covariance matrix computation -> VCI calculation -> Transferability evaluation

**Critical path:**
Model training with varying τ and λ configurations → Feature extraction on training set → Compute within-class and between-class covariance matrices → Calculate VCI → Linear probing on downstream datasets → Compute mean log odds gain

**Design tradeoffs:**
- VCI vs. existing metrics: VCI offers theoretical grounding and numerical stability but requires computing covariance matrices
- Linear probing vs. fine-tuning: Linear probing is faster and provides a cleaner assessment of feature quality, but may underestimate full transfer potential
- Model selection: Using ResNet and ViT covers both convolutional and transformer architectures, but more diversity could strengthen generalizability claims

**Failure signatures:**
- Numerical instability in VCI computation due to near-zero eigenvalues in covariance matrices
- Weak correlation between VCI and transferability indicating potential issues with metric design or implementation
- Inconsistent VCI values across similar training configurations suggesting implementation errors
