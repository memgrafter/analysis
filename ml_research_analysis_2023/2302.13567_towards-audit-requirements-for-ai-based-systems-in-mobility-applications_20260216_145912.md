# Towards Audit Requirements for AI-based Systems in Mobility Applications

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13567
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13567
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: Proposes 50 technical requirements for auditing AI-based systems in mobility, demonstrating significant robustness gaps in DNNs under realistic conditions

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical need for standardized audit requirements for AI-based systems in mobility applications, particularly autonomous driving and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS). The authors systematically analyze existing automotive safety standards and derive 50 generic technical requirements applicable across different use cases. Through practical implementation using a traffic sign recognition system, they demonstrate that while some requirements can be met, significant challenges remain in achieving the desired robustness levels, with accuracy dropping to approximately 79% under heavy rain conditions compared to the 90% threshold. The work provides a foundational framework for future AI-specific standardization in automotive safety while highlighting the current limitations of deep neural network systems.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a systematic analysis of existing automotive safety standards (primarily ISO 26262 and related standards) to identify relevant audit requirements for AI-based systems. They derived 50 generic technical requirements applicable across mobility use cases and implemented a practical evaluation framework using a traffic sign recognition system. The methodology involved implementing requirements as technical tests, collecting and analyzing results, and assessing compliance against predefined thresholds. The evaluation focused on testing DNN robustness under various conditions including weather simulations, while also examining dataset independence and other safety-critical aspects.

## Key Results
- Proposed 50 technical requirements and best practices for auditing AI-based systems in mobility applications
- Demonstrated that DNN accuracy drops to ~79% under heavy rain simulation, below the 90% threshold requirement
- Identified dataset independence as a met requirement, while robustness requirements fail under realistic conditions
- Established a practical evaluation framework using traffic sign recognition as a demonstrator system

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by bridging the gap between existing automotive safety standards and the unique challenges posed by AI-based systems, particularly deep neural networks. By systematically analyzing established standards and adapting them for AI-specific contexts, the framework provides a structured approach to identifying safety-critical requirements. The empirical testing methodology validates theoretical requirements against real system behavior, revealing practical limitations in current DNN robustness that theoretical analysis alone would miss.

## Limitations
- Requirements derived from limited set of existing automotive standards, potentially missing emerging AI-specific requirements
- Practical evaluation focuses on single use case (traffic sign recognition), limiting generalizability
- Simulated weather conditions may not fully capture real-world complexity and variability
- Does not address runtime monitoring or continuous auditing requirements for deployed systems

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Identification of gaps in current DNN robustness based on empirical testing results

**Medium confidence**: Transferability of derived requirements across different mobility use cases given limited evaluation scope

**Medium confidence**: Assertion that existing automotive standards are insufficient for AI-based systems based on current standards analysis
