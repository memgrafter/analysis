# Fast Convergence of Random Reshuffling under Over-Parameterization and the Polyak-Łojasiewicz Condition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00459
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00459
- Reference count: 40
- This paper studies the convergence of random reshuffling (RR) for over-parameterized models that satisfy the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the convergence of Random Reshuffling (RR), a sampling-without-replacement variant of SGD, for over-parameterized models satisfying the Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition. The authors show that RR can achieve faster convergence than standard SGD under two interpolation conditions: the Strong Growth Condition (SGC) and Weak Growth Condition (WGC). Specifically, when the number of samples n is small relative to problem conditioning parameters (n < ρ for SGC, or n < αL/µ for WGC), RR demonstrates superior performance. The paper also examines Incremental Gradient (IG) under similar conditions, finding it can outperform SGD but not as significantly as RR. Experimental results support these theoretical findings.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes RR and IG algorithms for finite-sum minimization problems of the form min f(x) = (1/n) Σ f(x;i) for i=1 to n. RR selects a random permutation of data at each epoch and updates using the next sample from the permutation, while IG uses a fixed order of data points. The analysis focuses on functions satisfying the PL condition and either the SGC or WGC interpolation conditions. The authors derive convergence rates for RR under these conditions and compare them with SGD, showing that RR can achieve faster convergence when the number of samples is small relative to the problem conditioning parameters.

## Key Results
- RR can achieve faster convergence than SGD when n < ρ for SGC or n < αL/µ for WGC
- IG can also outperform SGD under similar conditions but to a lesser extent than RR
- RR achieves convergence rate of O(max{Lmax/µn, Lmax/µ√(n√(αL/µ))}) under WGC, which can be faster than SGD's O(αL²/µ²) when αL/µ > n

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RR can achieve faster convergence than SGD when n < ρ for SGC, or n < αL/µ for WGC.
- Mechanism: RR reduces gradient variance by using each data point exactly once per epoch in random order, leading to better convergence under interpolation conditions.
- Core assumption: PL condition holds and objective satisfies SGC or WGC.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "For the class of Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) functions, we show that RR can outperform SGD in over-parameterized settings when either one of the following holds: (i) the number of samples (n) is less than the product of the condition number (κ) and the parameter (α) of a weak growth condition (WGC), or (ii)n is less than the parameter (ρ) of a strong growth condition (SGC)."
  - [section 3]: "The SGC implies interpolation, while for smooth functions the SGC implies the WGC [23, Lemma 5]. Further, for functions satisfying smoothness and µ-PL (Assumptions 2 and 1) all three conditions are equivalent if the fi are invex."
- Break condition: If n ≥ ρ or n ≥ αL/µ, RR's convergence advantage over SGD diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: IG can outperform SGD under similar conditions to RR, though not as much as RR.
- Mechanism: IG uses a fixed order of data points with single gradient updates, leading to faster convergence than SGD in certain scenarios.
- Core assumption: PL condition holds and objective satisfies SGC or WGC.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "They also show IG can outperform SGD under similar conditions, though not as much as RR."
  - [section 4]: "IG can also outperform SGD for µ-PL objectives when ρ > n2 and Lmax ∼ L under the SGC, or when αL/µ > n2 and Lmax ∼ L under the WGC."
- Break condition: If PL condition or growth conditions are not satisfied, IG's convergence advantage is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: RR's convergence rate under WGC is O(max{Lmax/µn, Lmax/µ√(n√(αL/µ))}), faster than SGD's O(αL²/µ²) when αL/µ > n and Lmax ∼ L.
- Mechanism: Convergence rate analysis shows RR achieves faster rate when problem is ill-conditioned (large α or ρ) and n is small.
- Core assumption: Objective satisfies WGC and PL condition.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "For µ-PL functions satisfying the WGC, we derive the sample complexity of RR to be O(max{Lmax/µn, Lmax/µ√(n√(αL/µ))}). In comparison, the sample complexity of SGD in this case is O(αL²/µ²)."
- Break condition: If problem is well-conditioned (small α or ρ) or n is large, RR's convergence advantage is not significant.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) condition
  - Why needed here: The PL condition enables linear convergence rates in optimization and is crucial for analyzing convergence in over-parameterized settings.
  - Quick check question: What is the main difference between the PL condition and strong convexity?

- Concept: Strong Growth Condition (SGC) and Weak Growth Condition (WGC)
  - Why needed here: These conditions describe how individual gradients change as the model moves away from optimal solution, important for analyzing convergence rates.
  - Quick check question: How do the SGC and WGC relate to the interpolation condition?

- Concept: Random Reshuffling (RR) and Incremental Gradient (IG)
  - Why needed here: RR and IG are SGD variants that can potentially converge faster than SGD in certain scenarios.
  - Quick check question: What is the main difference between RR and IG in terms of data sampling?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Objective function f(x) = 1/n ∑f(x;i) -> PL condition -> SGC/WGC -> RR/IG algorithms -> SGD algorithm

- Critical path:
  1. Define objective function and verify PL condition
  2. Check if SGC or WGC holds
  3. Implement RR or IG algorithm
  4. Analyze convergence rates and compare with SGD

- Design tradeoffs:
  - RR vs SGD: RR can achieve faster convergence but requires more memory to store data permutations
  - IG vs RR: IG is simpler to implement but may converge slower than RR in some cases

- Failure signatures:
  - Slow convergence: n is large relative to conditioning parameters, or PL condition doesn't hold
  - Divergence: Step size too large, or objective function doesn't satisfy required conditions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare convergence rates of RR, IG, and SGD on synthetic dataset with known PL condition and growth conditions
  2. Analyze effect of varying n on convergence rates of RR vs SGD
  3. Investigate impact of conditioning parameters (α, ρ, Lmax, µ) on RR's convergence advantage over SGD

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Under what specific conditions does RR outperform IG in practice for over-parameterized models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "RR consistently outperforms IG" in experiments and provides theoretical rates showing RR can be better than IG by a factor of √n under SGC or WGC.
- Why unresolved: While theoretical conditions (n < ρ for SGC, or n < αL/µ for WGC) are provided, experiments only show qualitative trends and precise performance thresholds remain unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying n, ρ, and α across diverse datasets and model architectures, showing precise performance gaps between RR and IG under different problem conditioning scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance gap between RR and SGD evolve as n increases in over-parameterized settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "RR can outperform SGD when n is less than ρ or αL/µ" and "a large n favors RR while for large n there is less of an advantage to using RR" based on experimental results.
- Why unresolved: Experiments show RR has less advantage as n grows, but exact relationship and critical n value where advantage diminishes is not quantified.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed experimental analysis plotting RR vs SGD performance gap as function of n for fixed ρ and α values, identifying critical n where RR's advantage becomes negligible.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do convergence rates of RR, SGD, and IG compare in non-convex over-parameterized settings where interpolation holds but PL condition may not be satisfied?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on PL functions but experiments include non-convex softmax loss with MLP, suggesting RR may still outperform SGD in non-convex settings.
- Why unresolved: Theoretical analysis is limited to PL functions, but experiments suggest broader applicability. Paper doesn't provide theoretical guarantees or detailed analysis for non-PL functions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Convergence rate analysis of RR, SGD, and IG for non-convex functions satisfying interpolation but not necessarily PL, potentially using local PL properties or other smoothness conditions around stationary points.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis assumes idealized conditions (perfect PL and growth conditions) that may not hold in practice
- Experimental validation is limited to simplified settings rather than practical over-parameterized models
- The conditions for RR's advantage (n < ρ or n < αL/µ) represent a narrow regime that may not be common in many applications

## Confidence
- Medium confidence in theoretical claims due to idealized assumptions and limited experimental validation

## Next Checks
1. **Practical applicability test**: Implement RR on standard over-parameterized models (e.g., wide neural networks on benchmark datasets) to verify if theoretical conditions and convergence improvements manifest in practice.
