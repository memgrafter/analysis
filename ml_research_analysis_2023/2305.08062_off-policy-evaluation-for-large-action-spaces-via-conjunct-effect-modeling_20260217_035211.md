# Off-Policy Evaluation for Large Action Spaces via Conjunct Effect Modeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08062
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08062
- Reference count: 23
- This paper addresses off-policy evaluation (OPE) for contextual bandit policies with large discrete action spaces, where conventional importance-weighting approaches suffer from excessive variance.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces OffCEM, a novel estimator for off-policy evaluation in contextual bandit problems with large discrete action spaces. Traditional importance weighting methods like IPS and DR suffer from high variance when the action space is large, while model-based approaches like DM suffer from high bias. OffCEM decomposes the causal effect into cluster and residual components, applying importance weighting only to action clusters and addressing the residual effect through model-based estimation. The estimator achieves unbiased estimation under a relaxed local correctness condition and uses a two-step regression procedure to optimize the bias-variance tradeoff. Experiments demonstrate substantial improvements over existing methods, with up to 83% MSE reduction in synthetic experiments and strong performance on real-world extreme classification datasets.

## Method Summary
OffCEM decomposes the expected reward into cluster effect g(x,c) and residual effect h(x,a) through a conjunct effect model (CEM). The estimator applies importance weighting only to clusters rather than individual actions, using w(x,c) = π(c|x)/π₀(c|x) instead of w(x,a) = π(a|x)/π₀(a|x). This dramatically reduces variance since |C| ≪ |A|. The residual effect is handled through model-based estimation using a two-step regression procedure: first minimizing bias by learning relative value differences within clusters, then minimizing variance by learning baseline cluster value functions. The approach is unbiased under local correctness, a condition requiring only preservation of relative expected reward differences within clusters rather than common support for all actions.

## Key Results
- OffCEM achieves up to 83% MSE reduction compared to IPS, DR, and MIPS in synthetic experiments
- Outperforms baselines on real-world extreme classification datasets including Wiki10-31K, Amazon-670K, and Amazon-3M
- Substantial variance reduction while maintaining low bias through the two-step regression procedure
- Robust performance across varying cluster sizes and data distributions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: OffCEM reduces variance by performing importance weighting over action clusters rather than the full action space
- Mechanism: Instead of using vanilla importance weights w(x,a) = π(a|x)/π₀(a|x) for every action, OffCEM uses cluster-level weights w(x,c) where c represents action clusters. Since |C| ≪ |A|, the importance weights are dramatically smaller and less variable
- Core assumption: The cluster effect g(x,c) captures most of the reward variation between actions, making the residual effect h(x,a) relatively small and manageable through regression
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "OffCEM applies importance weighting only to action clusters and addresses the residual causal effect through model-based reward estimation"
  - [section 2.2]: "the variance contributed by the first term can be extremely large for both IPS and DR when the reward is noisy and the weights w(x,a) have a wide range"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: OffCEM achieves unbiased estimation under local correctness rather than common support
- Mechanism: The local correctness condition only requires preserving relative expected reward differences within clusters (∆q(x,a,b) = ∆f(x,a,b) for actions in same cluster), which is a weaker condition than requiring common support for all actions
- Core assumption: The regression model f can learn the relative value differences within each cluster even if absolute reward values are misspecified
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "OffCEM is unbiased under a new condition called local correctness, which only requires that the model-based residual-effect estimation preserves the relative value difference of the actions within each cluster"
  - [section 3.2]: "We can reduce the variance of MIPS by performing action feature selection as described in Saito & Joachims (2022), but this may produce a large amount of bias by violating Assumption 2.3"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The two-step regression procedure optimally balances bias and variance for the residual effect
- Mechanism: First step minimizes bias by learning relative value differences (satisfying local correctness), second step minimizes variance by learning a baseline value function for each cluster
- Core assumption: Separating bias minimization (pairwise regression) from variance minimization (baseline learning) produces better overall statistical properties than joint optimization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "we propose the following two-step procedure for optimizing a regression model where the first-step corresponds to bias minimization while the second-step aims to minimize the variance"
  - [section 3.1]: "Theorem 3.3 showed that the accuracy of estimating the relative value difference ∆q(x,a,b) (i.e., satisfaction of local correctness) characterizes the bias of OffCEM while we should minimize |∆q,f(x,a)| for minimizing its variance"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Importance weighting and its variance issues
  - Why needed here: Understanding why conventional IPS and DR fail in large action spaces requires grasping how importance weights can become extremely large
  - Quick check question: Why does the variance of IPS grow with the number of actions? (Answer: Importance weights w(x,a) can become extremely large when π assigns high probability to actions with low π₀ probability, and this becomes more likely with many actions)

- Concept: Bias-variance tradeoff in model-based estimation
  - Why needed here: OffCEM explicitly trades bias for variance reduction, and understanding this tradeoff is crucial for the two-step regression procedure
  - Quick check question: Why might a model-based estimator have lower variance than IPS but higher bias? (Answer: The model can reduce variance by avoiding importance weighting, but introduces bias if the model is misspecified)

- Concept: Action clustering and representation learning
  - Why needed here: The effectiveness of OffCEM depends on finding good action clusters that capture meaningful reward variation
  - Quick check question: What makes a good action clustering for OffCEM? (Answer: Clusters should group actions with similar expected rewards so that the residual effect within clusters is small and easier to model)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data pipeline -> Clustering module -> First-step regression -> Second-step regression -> Estimator -> Evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Cluster actions based on domain knowledge or data-driven methods
  2. Perform first-step regression to learn relative value differences within clusters
  3. Perform second-step regression to learn cluster baseline values
  4. Combine results using cluster importance weighting
  5. Evaluate estimator performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Cluster granularity vs. model complexity: More clusters reduce variance but require more complex regression
  - First-step sample size vs. bias: Need sufficient pairwise comparisons within clusters to learn relative values
  - Regression model capacity vs. overfitting: Complex models may fit noise rather than true relationships

- Failure signatures:
  - High bias: Clusters poorly capture reward structure, regression fails to learn relative values
  - High variance: Insufficient data for either clustering or regression steps
  - Poor performance vs. baselines: Indicates clustering or regression implementation issues

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Synthetic data with known cluster structure: Test if OffCEM recovers true policy value when clusters are well-specified
  2. Varying cluster granularity: Measure impact of cluster size on bias-variance tradeoff
  3. Real-world dataset with heuristic clustering: Validate performance when clusters are suboptimal but reasonable

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal clustering procedure for maximizing OffCEM's performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "we can still expect a much better performance by OffCEM with a more refined clustering procedure" and suggest this as future work
- Why unresolved: The paper uses heuristic clustering methods (uniform random clustering, PCA-based dimensionality reduction) rather than optimizing clustering for the CEM framework
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing MSE improvements when using clustering methods specifically designed for OffCEM versus heuristic approaches

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does OffCEM perform when applied to continuous action spaces or hybrid discrete-continuous action spaces?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on discrete action spaces, but the CEM framework conceptually extends to embeddings (Section 3), suggesting potential applicability to continuous actions
- Why unresolved: The current formulation and experiments are limited to discrete actions, with no theoretical extension or empirical validation for continuous action spaces
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis showing unbiasedness conditions for continuous actions and experimental validation on benchmark continuous control tasks

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the two-step regression procedure be integrated into a single optimization framework that jointly learns the clustering and regression models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest "it would be valuable to consider an iterative procedure to optimize clustering and regression model simultaneously"
- Why unresolved: The current implementation treats clustering and regression as separate steps without exploring joint optimization approaches
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical validation of a unified optimization framework that alternates between clustering refinement and regression model updates

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of OffCEM heavily depends on action clustering quality, but the paper provides limited guidance on clustering methodology beyond heuristic approaches
- Local correctness is theoretically sufficient but may be practically challenging to achieve, particularly when cluster sizes are small
- The two-step regression procedure assumes that bias and variance can be separately optimized, which may not hold in all scenarios

## Confidence

- High: The variance reduction mechanism through cluster importance weighting
- Medium: The unbiasedness guarantee under local correctness (theoretically sound but practically challenging)
- Medium: The two-step regression procedure (empirically validated but with limited ablation)
