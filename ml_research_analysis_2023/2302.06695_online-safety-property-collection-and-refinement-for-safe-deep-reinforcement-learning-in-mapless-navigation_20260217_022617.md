# Online Safety Property Collection and Refinement for Safe Deep Reinforcement Learning in Mapless Navigation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CROP (Collecting and Refining Online Properties), a method for automatically collecting and refining safety properties during deep reinforcement learning training. The approach uses cost signals to identify unsafe state-action interactions and generates safety properties based on these experiences. CROP refines similar properties over time to improve safety guarantees. The method is evaluated on mapless navigation tasks, showing improved success rates and reduced violations compared to hand-designed safety properties, while also providing formal verification guarantees.

## Method Summary
CROP operates by monitoring the agent's interactions during training and using cost signals to detect unsafe behaviors. When an unsafe interaction occurs, CROP generates a new safety property and stores it in a property database. The method then refines similar properties by analyzing patterns across multiple unsafe experiences, improving the precision of input-output relationships. During policy execution, the collected properties are used to constrain the agent's actions, preventing violations of safety requirements. The approach combines online learning with formal verification techniques to provide guarantees about policy behavior.

## Key Results
- CROP achieves higher success rates compared to approaches using hand-designed safety properties
- The method demonstrates lower violation rates in mapless navigation tasks
- Formal verification confirms safety guarantees for learned policies
- Online property refinement improves safety property design over time

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CROP works by leveraging the agent's own experiences to identify and characterize unsafe behaviors. When the agent encounters a hazardous situation, the cost signal triggers property generation, capturing the specific conditions that led to the unsafe interaction. The refinement process then generalizes these specific instances into broader safety constraints that apply to similar situations. This creates a feedback loop where the agent's mistakes directly inform its future safety behavior. The formal verification component provides mathematical guarantees that the learned policies will respect the collected safety properties, creating a rigorous safety framework that adapts to the agent's environment.

## Foundational Learning
- **Safety property generation**: Creating formal specifications from observed unsafe behaviors - needed to translate empirical observations into formal constraints that can be verified
- **Online refinement**: Iteratively improving safety properties based on new experiences - required to handle the complexity and variability of real-world environments
- **Cost-based detection**: Using reward signals to identify safety violations - essential for automated identification of unsafe behaviors without manual labeling
- **Formal verification**: Mathematical proof techniques to guarantee policy compliance - provides rigorous safety guarantees beyond empirical testing
- **Mapless navigation**: Navigation without prior map knowledge - serves as a challenging test domain with realistic safety concerns

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Cost signal monitor -> Unsafe interaction detector -> Property generator -> Property database -> Policy constraint module -> Verification checker

**Critical path:**
During training, the agent interacts with the environment -> cost signals are monitored -> unsafe interactions trigger property generation -> properties are refined and stored -> policy is constrained by these properties during execution -> formal verification checks policy compliance

**Design tradeoffs:**
- Online vs. offline property collection (online provides adaptability but may miss early violations)
- Granularity of safety properties (fine-grained properties are more precise but may over-constrain the agent)
- Computational overhead of verification (formal guarantees require significant computation during training)

**Failure signatures:**
- Missing critical safety properties due to insufficient exploration
- Overly restrictive properties that prevent task completion
- False negatives in cost signal detection leading to unsafe behaviors
- Verification failures indicating policy violations

**3 first experiments:**
1. Test property generation with synthetic cost signals on a simple grid world
2. Evaluate refinement process with multiple similar unsafe experiences
3. Verify formal guarantees on a small policy with known properties

## Limitations
- Limited to mapless navigation tasks, may not generalize to other robotic domains
- Relies on hand-designed cost functions which may miss unforeseen hazards
- Formal verification guarantees depend on completeness of collected properties
- Online property refinement introduces computational overhead during training

## Confidence
- High: Demonstrated improvements in success rates and violation reduction compared to hand-designed properties
- Medium: Generalizability to other domains and robustness of formal verification guarantees across diverse environments
