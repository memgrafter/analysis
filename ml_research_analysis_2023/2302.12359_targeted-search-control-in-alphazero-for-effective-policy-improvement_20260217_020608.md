# Targeted Search Control in AlphaZero for Effective Policy Improvement

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12359
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12359
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Go-Exploit achieves significantly greater sample efficiency than standard AlphaZero in Connect Four and 9x9 Go through archive-based start state sampling

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the sample inefficiency of AlphaZero by proposing Go-Exploit, a search control strategy that samples the start state of self-play trajectories from an archive of previously visited or observed states. Go-Exploit improves upon AlphaZero by enabling more effective exploration of the game tree, producing more independent value targets, and allowing training under more exploitative policies. Experiments in Connect Four and 9x9 Go demonstrate that Go-Exploit achieves significantly greater sample efficiency than standard AlphaZero, as measured by win rates against reference opponents and in head-to-head matches.

## Method Summary
Go-Exploit modifies AlphaZero by introducing an archive of states of interest that is used to sample start states for self-play trajectories. The algorithm maintains separate training and archive actors, where archive actors populate the archive with states from completed self-play matches, and training actors sample start states (either from the initial state or the archive) to generate new trajectories. This approach enables more efficient exploration of the game tree, produces more independent value targets per learning step, and allows training under more exploitative policies due to built-in exploration. The method was evaluated on Connect Four and 9x9 Go with various archive structures and state definitions.

## Key Results
- Go-Exploit variants outperform standard AlphaZero in sample efficiency, achieving higher win rates against MCTS-Solver baselines with fewer learning steps
- In Connect Four, Go-Exploit completes over 323 trajectories per learning step compared to AlphaZero's 147.01, providing more independent value targets
- Go-Exploit variants were tuned to use fewer action sampling moves or smaller Dirichlet noise magnitudes, enabling more exploitative policies while maintaining exploration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Go-Exploit improves exploration efficiency by sampling start states from an archive, allowing more frequent visits to deeper game states.
- Mechanism: By initiating self-play trajectories from states of interest sampled from an archive rather than always starting from the initial state, Go-Exploit increases the probability of reaching and training on states deeper in the game tree. This reduces the number of steps needed to explore these states.
- Core assumption: States sampled from the archive are sufficiently representative of the overall state space to guide effective learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Go-Exploit samples the start state of its self-play trajectories from an archive of states of interest. Beginning self-play trajectories from varied starting states enables Go-Exploit to more effectively explore the game tree..."
  - [section] "Since AlphaZero begins its self-play matches from the initial state of a game, it often transitions into a terminal state before reaching and exploring states deeper in the game tree. In addition, AlphaZero only samples actions over the first few moves of a self-play match, further limiting exploration deeper in the game tree."
  - [corpus] Weak; related works focus on efficiency improvements but do not directly address state sampling from archives.
- Break condition: If the archive does not contain states that are sufficiently diverse or representative, the exploration benefit diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Go-Exploit produces more independent value targets per learning step, accelerating value function training.
- Mechanism: By starting trajectories from various points in the game tree, Go-Exploit generates shorter trajectories on average, leading to more completed self-play matches per learning step. Each completed match provides an independent value target, increasing the frequency of value updates.
- Core assumption: Shorter trajectories still provide meaningful value targets that reflect the expected outcomes from the sampled states.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Producing shorter self-play trajectories allows Go-Exploit to train upon more independent value targets, improving value training."
  - [section] "In Connect Four, AlphaZero completes an average of 147.01 trajectories per learning step whereas each variant of Go-Exploit completes over 323, on average."
  - [corpus] Weak; related works do not explicitly address the relationship between trajectory length and value target independence.
- Break condition: If the shorter trajectories do not reach terminal states or provide unreliable value targets, the benefit is lost.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Go-Exploit allows training under more exploitative policies due to built-in exploration, accelerating policy iteration.
- Mechanism: Since exploration is inherent in the selection of start states from the archive, Go-Exploit can reduce or eliminate the need for stochastic action selection during the remainder of the trajectory. This leads to policies that are more deterministic and focused on high-value actions.
- Core assumption: The exploration provided by the archive sampling is sufficient to cover the state space without additional stochasticity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the exploration inherent in Go-Exploit reduces its need for exploratory actions, enabling it to train under more exploitative policies."
  - [section] "In Connect Four, GEVE, GEVC, and GESR are tuned more exploitatively than AlphaZero since they use fewer action sampling moves 𝑘 or a smaller Dirichlet noise magnitude 𝜖."
  - [corpus] Weak; related works do not directly address the impact of archive sampling on the need for exploration during policy execution.
- Break condition: If the archive sampling does not provide adequate exploration, the policies may become too exploitative and miss important states.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Archive of states of interest -> Training actors -> Experience replay buffer -> Policy-value network -> MCTS with PUCT -> Archive actors

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize archive with initial state.
  2. Training actors sample start states (either initial state or from archive).
  3. Generate self-play trajectory using MCTS with PUCT.
  4. Add non-terminal states to archive (Visited States variant) or search states (Search States variant).
  5. Store trajectory outcomes in experience replay buffer.
  6. Periodically sample batches from replay buffer to update 𝑓𝜃.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Archive size vs. memory usage: Larger archives provide more diverse start states but require more memory.
  - Visited states vs. search states: Including search states may provide more information but increases complexity.
  - Probability of starting from initial state (𝜆): Balancing exploration of new states with revisiting known states.
  - Action sampling moves (𝑘): Tradeoff between exploration and exploitation during trajectories.

- Failure signatures:
  - Slow learning: Archive may not contain diverse enough states, or value targets are not independent enough.
  - Overfitting to archive states: Archive may be too small or not representative, leading to poor generalization.
  - Instability in training: Archive sampling may introduce too much randomness, or value targets may be too noisy.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare learning curves of Go-Exploit with different archive sizes in a simple game like Tic-Tac-Toe.
  2. Analyze the distribution of unique states visited by Go-Exploit versus AlphaZero in Connect Four.
  3. Measure the impact of starting from the initial state vs. sampling from the archive on the average length of self-play trajectories.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal archive structure and state selection strategy for Go-Exploit?

- Why unresolved: The paper only explores a limited set of archive structures and uses uniform random sampling. There may be more effective ways to structure the archive or select states from it.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing different archive structures and state selection strategies (e.g., prioritized sampling based on visit frequency or value estimates) to find the optimal configuration for Go-Exploit.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does Go-Exploit's search control strategy affect its ability to learn in non-deterministic or imperfect information games?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as a direction for future work, noting that Go-Exploit's search control strategy may not directly translate to these types of games.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates Go-Exploit in deterministic, perfect information games (Connect Four and 9x9 Go). It is unclear how the algorithm would perform in games with randomness or hidden information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments running Go-Exploit in non-deterministic or imperfect information games (e.g., poker, backgammon) and comparing its performance and learning efficiency to AlphaZero.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation to only Connect Four and 9x9 Go, which are relatively small game domains
- No analysis of potential overfitting to archive states or guidelines for optimal archive sizing
- Computational overhead of maintaining and sampling from archives not fully characterized

## Confidence
- **High**: Sample efficiency improvements measured by win rates against MCTS-Solver and head-to-head matches
- **Medium**: Mechanism of improved exploration through archive-based start state sampling
- **Low**: Claim that Go-Exploit enables training under more exploitative policies without sacrificing exploration quality
