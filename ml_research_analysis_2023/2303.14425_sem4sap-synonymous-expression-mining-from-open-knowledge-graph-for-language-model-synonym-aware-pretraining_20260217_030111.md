# Sem4SAP: Synonymous Expression Mining From Open Knowledge Graph For Language Model Synonym-Aware Pretraining

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14425
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14425
- Reference count: 16
- Improves PLM performance on 10 downstream tasks by 4.84% average

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Sem4SAP, a framework for mining synonymous expressions from open knowledge graphs (Open-KGs) and using them to pretrain language models with enhanced synonym awareness. The method involves mining synsets from Open-KGs using frequency information and clustering, expanding these synsets by migrating core semantics, and pretraining language models with two novel synonym-aware tasks. The framework is evaluated on ten downstream tasks, showing significant improvements in performance and robustness to synonym substitution attacks compared to original pre-trained language models and other baselines.

## Method Summary
Sem4SAP addresses the challenge of improving language models' understanding of synonymous expressions by mining synsets from Open-KGs and using them for synonym-aware pretraining. The framework consists of three main steps: 1) Synonymous Values Mining, which clusters value objects in Open-KGs into synsets using frequency information and distributed representation features; 2) Synset Expansion, which expands the mined synsets by migrating core semantics between synonymous expressions; and 3) Synonym-Aware Pretraining, which pretrains language models using two novel tasks: Entity-based Synonym Recovery and Synonym-based Sentence Completion. The framework is evaluated on ten downstream tasks, including sentiment analysis, natural language inference, and text classification, and shows substantial improvements in performance and robustness to synonym substitution attacks.

## Key Results
- Outperforms original PLMs by 4.84% average on 10 downstream tasks
- Demonstrates improved robustness to synonym substitution attacks
- Shows significant improvements in sentiment analysis, natural language inference, and text classification tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Sem4SAP works by leveraging the rich semantic information present in Open-KGs to improve language models' understanding of synonymous expressions. The framework mines synsets from Open-KGs using frequency information and clustering, which helps capture the distributional properties of synonymous expressions. By expanding these synsets through core semantics migration, the framework ensures that the learned representations are robust and generalizable. The two novel synonym-aware pretraining tasks, Entity-based Synonym Recovery and Synonym-based Sentence Completion, further inject synonym knowledge into the language models, enabling them to better handle synonymous expressions in downstream tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding
### Component Map
Open-KGs -> Synonymous Values Mining -> Synset Expansion -> Synonym-Aware Pretraining -> Downstream Tasks

### Critical Path
The critical path involves mining synsets from Open-KGs, expanding them through core semantics migration, and using the expanded synsets for synonym-aware pretraining. This path is essential for injecting synonym knowledge into the language models and improving their performance on downstream tasks.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Frequency vs. Semantic Similarity**: Balancing the use of frequency information and distributed representation features for clustering synonymous expressions.
- **Synset Size vs. Quality**: Expanding synsets to include more synonymous expressions while maintaining their quality and relevance.
- **Pretraining Task Complexity vs. Efficiency**: Designing pretraining tasks that effectively inject synonym knowledge without being computationally expensive.

### Failure Signatures
- **Poor Synset Quality**: If the mined synsets do not accurately capture synonymous expressions, the pretraining tasks may not be effective, leading to limited improvements in downstream tasks.
- **Ineffective Pretraining**: If the synonym-aware pretraining tasks do not effectively inject synonym knowledge into the language models, the models may not show significant improvements in robustness to synonym substitution attacks.
