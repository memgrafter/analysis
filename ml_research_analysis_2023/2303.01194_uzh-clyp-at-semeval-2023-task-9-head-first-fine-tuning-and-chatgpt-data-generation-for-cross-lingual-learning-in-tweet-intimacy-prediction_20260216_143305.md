# UZH_CLyp at SemEval-2023 Task 9: Head-First Fine-Tuning and ChatGPT Data Generation for Cross-Lingual Learning in Tweet Intimacy Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01194
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01194
- Reference count: 19
- Second-best results in all 10 languages for multilingual tweet intimacy prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper presents UZH_CLyp's submission to SemEval 2023 Task 9 on multilingual tweet intimacy analysis. The approach combines Head-First Fine-Tuning (HeFiT) with ChatGPT-generated synthetic data for cross-lingual learning. The team achieved second-best results across all 10 languages in the official Pearson correlation evaluation, demonstrating the effectiveness of their methodology in low-resource settings where no human-labeled data is available for target languages.

## Method Summary
The approach employs a two-stage fine-tuning strategy called Head-First Fine-Tuning (HeFiT). First, only the regression head parameters are updated while keeping the pre-trained transformer encoder frozen. Then, the encoder parameters are updated at a reduced learning rate. Additionally, a small set of synthetic examples generated by ChatGPT supplements the training data for low-resource languages. This cross-lingual transfer learning framework aims to improve performance when human-labeled data is scarce, particularly for languages with no available annotations.

## Key Results
- Achieved second-best results across all 10 languages in the official Pearson correlation evaluation
- HeFiT method consistently improved results for pre-trained models lacking tweet domain adaptation
- ChatGPT-generated synthetic data noticeably increased performance in zero-shot baseline settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The HeFiT approach stabilizes training by first adapting the output layer to the specific task before gradually fine-tuning the entire model. This prevents catastrophic forgetting of the pre-trained representations while allowing task-specific adjustments. The synthetic data generation provides additional training examples that help bridge the domain gap between the pre-trained model's original training data and the tweet domain, particularly valuable for languages without human annotations.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Pre-trained transformer -> Head-First Fine-Tuning -> ChatGPT synthetic data augmentation -> Pearson correlation evaluation
- **Critical path**: Fine-tune regression head -> Fine-tune encoder parameters -> Generate synthetic examples -> Evaluate on target languages
- **Design tradeoffs**: Balancing learning rates between head and encoder fine-tuning to maintain stability while achieving task adaptation
- **Failure signatures**: Overfitting to synthetic data, instability during full fine-tuning, cross-lingual interference from inconsistent annotations
- **3 first experiments**:
  1. Compare head-only fine-tuning vs. full fine-tuning on source language

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The study acknowledges the need for further investigation into how inconsistencies in annotated data contribute to cross-lingual interference issues, though it does not extensively explore this aspect.

## Limitations
- Relies on a small set of automatically generated examples that may not fully capture human-labeled data complexity
- Limited exploration of cross-lingual interference issues due to data inconsistencies
- Performance evaluation focused primarily on Pearson correlation metric

## Confidence
- HeFiT effectiveness: High

- Synthetic data quality: Medium
