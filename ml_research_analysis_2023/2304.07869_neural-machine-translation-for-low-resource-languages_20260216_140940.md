# Neural Machine Translation For Low Resource Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07869
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07869
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: Back translation combined with focal loss improved BLEU scores and achieved new state-of-the-art for Sinhala→English (14.27) and Nepali→English (15.79)

## Executive Summary
This study explores Neural Machine Translation (NMT) for low-resource languages using a multilingual pretrained model, mBART.CC25. The researchers evaluate three methods—focal loss, back translation, and transfer learning—on four low-resource language pairs: Sinhala→English, Nepali→English, Khmer→English, and Pashto→English. Results demonstrate that combining back translation with focal loss significantly improves translation quality, achieving new state-of-the-art BLEU scores for two language pairs. The work highlights the effectiveness of data augmentation and loss function adaptation for addressing the challenges of low-resource translation tasks.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes mBART.CC25 on parallel corpora for four low-resource language pairs, with Si-En and Ne-En from FLoRes and Km-En and Ps-En from OPUS. The baseline uses label smoothed cross-entropy with specific hyperparameters. Three augmentation strategies are evaluated: focal loss (α=0.5, γ=1) to focus on hard examples, back translation to generate synthetic parallel data by translating monolingual target text to source language, and transfer learning from high-resource language pairs (Tamil/Hindi/Vietnamese/Farsi) to initialize low-resource models. All models are evaluated using sacreBLEU with length normalization.

## Key Results
- Back translation combined with focal loss achieved new state-of-the-art BLEU scores: Si-En (14.27) and Ne-En (15.79)
- Transfer learning improved scores for Ne-En, Km-En, and Ps-En but not for Si-En due to data quality issues
- The mBART.CC25 baseline was established before applying augmentation techniques

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using mBART.CC25 improves translation quality for low-resource languages
- Mechanism: mBART.CC25 is pretrained on 25 languages, learning general linguistic patterns that transfer to specific translation tasks through fine-tuning
- Core assumption: Low-resource languages share linguistic features with the 25 pretrained languages
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "build upon the mBART.CC25 language model"; [section] "piggyback on the different work that is already been done around multilingual NMT"
- Break condition: If the low-resource language is linguistically very different from any of the 25 pretrained languages

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Back translation combined with focal loss improves BLEU scores
- Mechanism: Back translation generates synthetic parallel data, increasing training data size by 5x; focal loss focuses on hard-to-predict tokens
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "back translation combined with focal loss improved BLEU scores"; [section] "synthetic parallel data generated from the monolingual corpus was at least five times larger"
- Break condition: If back translation quality is very poor due to weak reverse translator

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transfer learning from high-resource to low-resource pairs improves quality
- Mechanism: Parent model trained on high-resource pair initializes child model for low-resource pair, providing strong starting point
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Transfer learning also improved scores for Ne-En, Km-En, and Ps-En"; [section] "Transfer learning method can significantly improve the BLEU score"
- Break condition: If high-resource and low-resource languages are too different

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different data quality and domain alignment strategies impact translation accuracy for low-resource languages beyond simple corpus size adjustments?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that Si-En transfer learning failed due to poor Tamil corpus quality, while Ne-En, Km-En, and Ps-En succeeded
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on comparing methods without systematically analyzing effects of preprocessing, domain matching, or corpus cleaning
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying preprocessing steps and measuring impact on BLEU scores

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal combination of transfer learning sources and iterative back translation for maximizing translation performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests combining techniques could improve results and mentions iterative back translation as future direction
- Why unresolved: The study evaluates each technique independently without exploring synergies or iterative applications
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of single method vs sequential vs iterative applications

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does focal loss with varying γ parameters affect translation quality for different low-resource language pairs?
- Basis in paper: The paper uses γ=1 but notes it is tunable and doesn't explore impact across languages
- Why unresolved: The study applies fixed configuration without investigating how different γ values influence performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies testing multiple γ values for each language pair

## Limitations
- Effectiveness depends on linguistic similarity between pretrained and target languages
- Quality of back translation synthetic data depends on reverse translation model performance
- Transfer learning success varies by language pair, with Si-En failing due to unexplained data quality issues

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Experimental methodology clearly described with reproducible dataset sources and evaluation metrics
- Medium Confidence: BLEU score improvements are credible but depend on unspecified implementation details
- Low Confidence: Transfer learning failure for Si-En due to "data quality issues" lacks sufficient supporting evidence
