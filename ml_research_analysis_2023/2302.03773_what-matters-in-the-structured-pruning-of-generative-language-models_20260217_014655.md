# What Matters In The Structured Pruning of Generative Language Models?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03773
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03773
- Authors: Liangchen Song, Yichong Xu, Binghui Xie, et al.
- Reference count: 26
- This paper investigates structured pruning methods for decoder-only language models like GPT-3.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically evaluates structured pruning methods for decoder-only language models, revealing that random pruning performs nearly as well as established methods. The authors introduce a framework measuring neuron-level redundancy through sensitivity and uniqueness metrics, showing that existing methods leave behind excess redundancies. Based on this analysis, they propose Globally Unique Movement (GUM), which combines sensitivity and uniqueness regularization to outperform other methods across multiple NLG tasks while achieving competitive compression rates.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate structured pruning methods (magnitude, random, and movement pruning) on feed-forward layers of decoder-only language models like GPT-2 and GPT-Neo. They introduce sensitivity and uniqueness metrics to measure neuron-level redundancy, finding that established methods leave behind excess redundancies. The proposed GUM method adds uniqueness regularization to movement pruning and uses global Topv selection across the entire network. Knowledge distillation is used to fine-tune pruned models, significantly reducing performance gaps between different pruning methods.

## Key Results
- Random pruning performs nearly as well as established structured pruning methods across multiple NLG tasks
- Knowledge distillation significantly reduces performance differences between pruning methods
- Global Topv pruning with uniqueness regularization (GUM) outperforms other methods while achieving competitive compression rates
- Sensitivity-uniqueness framework reveals excess redundancies left by traditional pruning approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Random pruning performs nearly as well as established structured pruning methods because existing methods leave behind excess neuron redundancies.
- Mechanism: Traditional pruning methods (magnitude, movement) focus on sensitivity but fail to account for neuron uniqueness. When neurons are highly redundant (both sensitive and similar to others), pruning one doesn't significantly harm performance since others can compensate. Random pruning naturally avoids over-selecting redundant neurons.
- Core assumption: The performance gap between pruning methods is primarily determined by how well they preserve both sensitive AND unique neurons rather than just sensitive ones.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "unexpectedly, random pruning results in performance that is comparable to the best established methods"
  - [section 3.2] "established structured pruning methods do not take into account the distinctiveness of neurons, leaving behind excess redundancies"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains related pruning work but none specifically addresses the uniqueness vs sensitivity tradeoff
- Break condition: If neuron outputs are highly correlated and sensitive simultaneously, random pruning would remove too many important neurons at once.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge distillation closes performance gaps between pruning methods by enabling models to learn more diverse feature sets.
- Mechanism: Distillation from a full-parameter teacher provides supervision that encourages student networks to learn more diverse, non-redundant representations. This compensates for pruning methods' tendency to leave behind redundant neurons.
- Core assumption: The primary difference between pruning methods lies in their ability to learn diverse features during fine-tuning, not in the pruning decision itself.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "distillation largely closes the gaps between different methods, further narrowing the advantage of best methods over random pruning"
  - [section 3.1] "methods with very different performances would perform rather similar if distilled from a non-pruned, fine-tuned model"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus mentions distillation but not specifically its role in reducing method performance gaps
- Break condition: If the teacher model itself has redundant representations or if the student architecture cannot effectively learn from the teacher.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Global Topv pruning outperforms local Topv by allowing more flexible layer-wise pruning ratios.
- Mechanism: Global Topv considers all neurons across the entire network jointly rather than pruning each layer independently. This prevents situations where some layers are artificially constrained to keep more neurons than needed while others are overly pruned.
- Core assumption: Different layers have different capacities for pruning before performance degradation occurs, and a global approach can better balance these differences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "Global Topv instead uses Topv function on the set of all mask scores in the network jointly. Global Topv was originally explored for movement...and was found to perform similarly. We find when used in conjunction with uniqueness regularization, Global outperforms Local."
  - [section 3.3] "When pruning locally, it is necessary to choose the least common pruning percent - if one layer requires 50% neurons before severe degradation, all layers must keep 50%."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus mentions pruning methods but doesn't specifically discuss global vs local Topv comparisons
- Break condition: If all layers have similar pruning capacities or if the global approach removes too many neurons from critical layers.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Sensitivity vs Uniqueness tradeoff
  - Why needed here: The paper's core contribution is recognizing that pruning methods must balance these two metrics, not just sensitivity alone
  - Quick check question: If two neurons have identical outputs on all inputs but different gradients, are they sensitive, unique, both, or neither?

- Concept: Movement pruning mechanics
  - Why needed here: Understanding how mask scores are accumulated and regularized is crucial for implementing GUM
  - Quick check question: In movement pruning, what happens to mask scores that have been regularized too aggressively?

- Concept: Knowledge distillation in model compression
  - Why needed here: The paper shows distillation significantly impacts pruning performance, requiring understanding of teacher-student training dynamics
  - Quick check question: How does temperature scaling in distillation loss affect the smoothness of the student's learned representations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Feed-forward layers in decoder-only transformers contain neurons that can be pruned; pruning operates on intermediate dimension (typically 4×model dimension); GUM adds uniqueness regularization to movement pruning
- Critical path: Mask score computation → Regularization (sensitivity + uniqueness) → Global Topv selection → Weight masking → Forward pass with reduced neuron count
- Design tradeoffs: Global vs local pruning (flexibility vs layer-specific optimization), uniqueness regularization strength vs training stability, distillation temperature vs student performance
- Failure signatures: Under-pruning (mask regularization too strong), over-pruning (mask regularization too weak), performance collapse (uniqueness regularization too aggressive), slow convergence (mask learning rate too low)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic movement pruning on GPT-2-small with WikiSQL, verify against paper's baseline results
  2. Add uniqueness regularization to movement pruning, measure sensitivity and uniqueness metrics
  3. Compare global vs local Topv pruning on same setup, verify layer pruning distribution matches paper's findings

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of structured pruning methods scale with model size beyond GPT-Neo-1.3B?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention testing on GPT-Neo-1.3B as a larger model but do not explore scaling effects systematically. They state "To ensure trends hold when scaling up, we present one experiment with GPT-Neo-1.3b in section 5."
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents results for one larger model, making it unclear whether the observed patterns (random pruning working nearly as well as established methods) persist at truly massive scales like GPT-3 or beyond.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of structured pruning across multiple model sizes (e.g., GPT-2, GPT-3 variants, and larger models) showing whether the relative performance of different pruning methods remains consistent.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical explanation for why knowledge distillation closes the performance gaps between different pruning methods?
- Basis in paper: The authors observe that "when the network has fewer left-over neurons (e.g., 10% or 25%), the difference of accuracies or perplexities often fall below half of the difference without distillation" and speculate this relates to "the inability to learn more diverse features set in fine-pruning, as suggested by the theoretical results in Allen-Zhu & Li (2020) on distillations."
- Why unresolved: The authors only provide speculation and reference to related work without providing a theoretical analysis of why distillation specifically reduces method-specific differences in pruning performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal theoretical framework showing how distillation affects the learning dynamics of pruned models and why it reduces sensitivity to pruning method choice.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do structured pruning methods perform on encoder-decoder architectures like T5 or BART for text generation tasks?
- Basis in paper: The authors explicitly note that "there is a lack of systematic research on structured pruning for decoder-only architectures" and contrast their work with existing research on BERT-like encoder-decoder or encoder-only models.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses exclusively on decoder-only architectures, leaving open whether the findings about random pruning being nearly as effective as established methods extend to other transformer architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluation of structured pruning methods on encoder-decoder models across various text generation tasks, comparing performance to the trends observed in decoder-only models.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the relationship between the uniqueness metric and the diversity of learned representations in the final layers of pruned models?
- Basis in paper: The authors introduce uniqueness as "how much the information provided by a network component differs from others" and propose GUM to maximize both sensitivity and uniqueness, but do not explore the relationship between uniqueness and representation diversity.
- Why unresolved: The paper measures uniqueness but does not investigate how it correlates with the actual diversity or specialization of representations in the pruned model's final layers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis connecting neuron uniqueness scores to the diversity of activation patterns in final layers, potentially using techniques like centered kernel alignment (CKA) or representation similarity analysis.

### Open Question 5
- Question: How does the pruning distribution across layers (which layers are prioritized for removal) affect downstream task performance for different pruning methods?
- Basis in paper: The authors mention that "later layers are clearly prioritized" when using Global Topv pruning, but do not systematically investigate how layer-wise pruning distribution affects performance across different methods.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that Global Topv tends to preserve later layers, it does not explore whether this distribution is optimal or how different layer-wise pruning strategies affect performance for different pruning methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the layer-wise pruning distribution for different methods and measuring the impact on task performance to determine optimal pruning strategies.

## Limitations

- The core claim that random pruning's effectiveness stems from eliminating redundancies is supported by indirect evidence rather than direct proof of the proposed mechanism
- The computational overhead of GUM's uniqueness regularization and running cosine similarity calculations is not fully characterized
- The paper focuses exclusively on decoder-only architectures, leaving open whether findings extend to encoder-decoder models

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- Random pruning performs surprisingly well compared to established methods across multiple NLG tasks
- Knowledge distillation significantly reduces performance gaps between pruning methods
- Global Topv pruning generally outperforms local Topv when combined with uniqueness regularization

**Medium Confidence:**
- The sensitivity-uniqueness framework accurately captures the primary factors determining pruning method performance
- The excess redundancies identified by the framework are the main reason for random pruning's effectiveness
- GUM's performance improvements are primarily due to its balance of sensitivity and uniqueness

**Low Confidence:**
- The specific values of uniqueness regularization hyperparameters are optimal across all scenarios
- The running cosine similarity update mechanism provides consistent benefits across different model sizes and tasks

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study of GUM Components**: Implement and test versions of GUM with: (a) sensitivity regularization only, (b) uniqueness regularization only, (c) global Topv only, (d) all components combined. This would quantify the marginal contribution of each innovation and verify that the full method provides synergistic benefits rather than just additive ones.

2. **Layer-Wise Performance Analysis**: After pruning with GUM, analyze the performance contribution of individual layers to identify if the global Topv approach creates performance bottlenecks in specific layers. This would validate whether the flexibility of global pruning comes at the cost of uneven layer utilization.

3. **Redundancy Elimination Verification**: For models pruned with random pruning vs established methods, measure the actual redundancy levels in the final models using the sensitivity-uniqueness framework. This would directly test whether random pruning's effectiveness correlates with reduced redundancies as proposed.
