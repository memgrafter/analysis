# Hybrid Spiking Neural Network Fine-tuning for Hippocampus Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07328
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07328
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Hybrid SNN fine-tuning improves hippocampus segmentation accuracy with fewer epochs than direct training

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a hybrid approach for training spiking neural networks (SNNs) for medical image segmentation. The method combines ANN-to-SNN conversion as initialization with spike-based backpropagation fine-tuning. Applied to hippocampus segmentation from MRI scans using a modified U-Net architecture, the approach addresses the challenge that SNNs are harder to train than ANNs but offer lower power consumption. The hybrid method significantly improves segmentation accuracy compared to direct conversion while requiring far fewer training epochs than training from scratch.

## Method Summary
The method follows a three-stage pipeline: first, a modified U-Net is trained as an artificial neural network (ANN) on hippocampus segmentation using standard backpropagation. Second, the trained ANN is converted to an SNN by copying weights and applying threshold balancing to ensure uniform firing across layers. Third, the converted SNN undergoes spike-based backpropagation fine-tuning using a linear surrogate gradient to approximate the non-differentiable spiking function. The fine-tuning stage uses combinations of BCE and Dice loss functions and typically converges in under 35 epochs, significantly faster than direct SNN training.

## Key Results
- Hybrid SNN fine-tuning improves Dice scores by ~3-4% compared to direct ANN-SNN conversion
- Fine-tuning converges in approximately 10-11 epochs versus hundreds for direct SNN training
- The method maintains competitive accuracy with reduced computational cost for inference

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hybrid initialization via ANN-SNN conversion sets near-optimal initial thresholds, reducing the number of fine-tuning epochs required for convergence.
- Mechanism: The conversion step loads trained ANN weights into an SNN and balances thresholds so that neurons fire with reasonable probability; this avoids the need to train from scratch in a high-dimensional sparse space.
- Core assumption: The trained ANN's weights and ReLU activations are sufficiently close to optimal SNN firing patterns after conversion.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach takes ANN-SNN conversion as an initialization step and relies on spike-based backpropagation to fine-tune the network."
  - [section 3.1] "The SNN neuron model in our work is integrate-and-fire (IF) model ... During the conversion process, we load the weights of the trained ANN into the SNN network and set the thresholds for all layers to 1s. Then a threshold balancing procedure [7] is carried out to update the threshold of each layer."
  - [corpus] No direct citation, but cited work [9] is referenced for convergence speed claims.
- Break condition: If ANN weights are far from optimal for SNN firing patterns, conversion yields poor initial thresholds and fine-tuning cannot recover accuracy.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Spike-based backpropagation with a surrogate gradient enables effective gradient flow despite non-differentiable spiking events.
- Mechanism: The surrogate gradient approximates ∂o/∂u to allow weight updates in hidden layers, while output layer potentials remain continuous and differentiable via sigmoid.
- Core assumption: The chosen linear surrogate gradient (α max{0, 1 - |u - Vt|}) is a valid approximation over the relevant membrane potential range.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "Due to the non-differentiability of spikes, a surrogate gradient-based method is used in backpropagation. ... we choose a linear approximation proposed in [27]."
  - [section 3.1] "Each neuron in the output layer is connected to a Sigmoid activation function to produce the predictive probability..."
  - [corpus] Cites [27] for the linear surrogate gradient, but no other corpus evidence provided.
- Break condition: If the surrogate gradient poorly approximates the true derivative over the operating range, weight updates become ineffective and training stalls.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Threshold balancing after conversion ensures uniform firing across layers, preventing saturation or silence in early layers.
- Mechanism: Post-conversion, thresholds are adjusted so that each layer's neurons fire at similar rates, enabling gradient propagation and stable fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: Balanced firing rates across layers correlate with better gradient flow and segmentation accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "During the conversion process, ... Then a threshold balancing procedure [7] is carried out to update the threshold of each layer."
  - [section 4.2] "We record the firing frequency of each layer in the SNN models before and after the fine-tuning and plot them in Fig. 3. It is evident that neurons become more active after the fine-tuning..."
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; thresholding method [7] is referenced but not described here.
- Break condition: If thresholds remain unbalanced, early layers saturate or die, blocking gradient flow and harming accuracy.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Integrate-and-Fire (IF) neuron dynamics
  - Why needed here: The paper uses IF neurons (not LIF) and derives membrane potential updates explicitly in Eqn. 1-2; understanding these dynamics is required to interpret the conversion and fine-tuning procedures.
  - Quick check question: In the IF model, what happens to the membrane potential when a spike is fired, and why is there no leak term?

- Concept: ANN-SNN conversion via threshold balancing
  - Why needed here: Conversion is the initialization step; the paper copies ANN weights, sets thresholds to 1, then balances them to match firing rates, which is central to the hybrid method.
  - Quick check question: What is the purpose of setting initial thresholds to 1 before the balancing step?

- Concept: Surrogate gradient backpropagation for SNNs
  - Why needed here: Direct gradients through binary spikes are undefined; the paper uses a linear surrogate (α max{0, 1 - |u - Vt|}) to approximate them for weight updates.
  - Quick check question: How does the surrogate gradient differ from the true gradient of a spiking activation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Modified U-Net (no bias, avg-pool, no BN, dropout added) -> ANN training -> ANN→SNN conversion (weights + threshold balancing) -> Spike-based fine-tuning (surrogate gradients) -> Loss selection (BCE, Dice, or BCE-Dice)
- Critical path: 1. Train ANN (100 epochs, Adam, ReduceLROnPlateau) 2. Convert to SNN (200 time steps, threshold balancing) 3. Fine-tune SNN (spike backprop, up to 35 epochs) 4. Evaluate (2D/3D Dice)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Removing biases/batch-norm simplifies SNN conversion but may reduce ANN accuracy
  - Using IF neurons (no leak) eases threshold balancing but may reduce temporal coding capacity
  - Linear surrogate gradient is simple but may limit fine-tuning precision compared to more complex surrogates
- Failure signatures:
  - Post-conversion SNN accuracy near zero → thresholds not balanced or weights poorly mapped
  - Slow/no convergence during fine-tuning → surrogate gradient too flat or learning rate too low
  - Output masks too small/large → layer thresholds still suboptimal after balancing
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline ANN with BCE loss; verify 2D/3D Dice matches reported ~78%
  2. Convert ANN to SNN; record layer firing frequencies; check threshold balancing effect
  3. Fine-tune converted SNN with BCE-Dice; monitor epoch-by-epoch accuracy to confirm ~10 epochs convergence

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the threshold balancing procedures impact the fine-tuning convergence rate and final accuracy of SNNs across different network architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions threshold balancing is used during conversion and that fine-tuning adjusts weights to compensate for suboptimal thresholds, but does not systematically analyze the impact of threshold balancing on convergence or accuracy.
- Why unresolved: The paper only reports that fine-tuning converges in 10.11 epochs on average but does not isolate the contribution of threshold balancing versus fine-tuning to this improvement.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing SNN performance with and without threshold balancing during conversion, and ablation studies isolating the impact of threshold adjustments during fine-tuning.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed hybrid SNN fine-tuning method scale to larger, more complex network architectures and datasets beyond hippocampus segmentation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates effectiveness on a modified U-Net for hippocampus segmentation but does not explore scaling to deeper networks or larger datasets.
- Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to a single network architecture and dataset, leaving uncertainty about generalizability and computational feasibility for larger-scale applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the method to deeper networks (e.g., ResNet, DenseNet) and larger datasets (e.g., natural images, other medical imaging tasks) with performance and computational efficiency comparisons.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of different surrogate gradient functions on the convergence and accuracy of the fine-tuning process?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using a linear approximation of the surrogate gradient but does not compare it with other surrogate gradient functions.
- Why unresolved: Only one surrogate gradient function is tested, leaving uncertainty about whether the chosen function is optimal or if other functions might yield better results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of different surrogate gradient functions (e.g., exponential, rectangular, Gaussian) on the same task, measuring convergence speed and final accuracy.

## Limitations

- The method is demonstrated only on hippocampus segmentation from ADNI dataset
- The architectural modifications (no bias, no BN) may not generalize to other network architectures
- Only one surrogate gradient function is tested, leaving uncertainty about optimality
- The threshold balancing procedure [7] is cited but not fully detailed

## Confidence

- ANN-SNN conversion effectiveness: Medium - conversion works but optimal thresholds are dataset/model-dependent
- Surrogate gradient backpropagation: Medium - linear approximation is validated but alternatives exist
- Fine-tuning convergence speed: High - experimental results clearly show faster convergence vs direct training
- Segmentation accuracy improvements: High - Dice scores increase significantly post-fine-tuning
- Architectural generalizability: Low - method tied to specific U-Net variant
