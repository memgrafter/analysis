# Anchor Prediction: Automatic Refinement of Internet Links

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.14337
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14337
- **Reference count**: 3
- **Primary result**: Anchor prediction task introduced with 82.84% accuracy on AUTHOR ANCHORS and 31.15% accuracy on READER ANCHORS

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the task of anchor prediction, which aims to refine unanchored web links by identifying the most relevant part of the target webpage. The authors release two datasets: AUTHOR ANCHORS with 34K examples from Wikipedia's naturally-occurring anchored links, and READER ANCHORS with human-annotated paragraph-level anchors. They benchmark BM25 and T5-based ranking approaches, finding that T5-based models achieve significantly better performance but still leave substantial room for improvement. The task requires jointly reasoning over lengthy source and target webpages to determine implicit relations and identify relevant target content while avoiding redundancy.

## Method Summary
The authors frame anchor prediction as a ranking problem where models must score candidate paragraphs from the target webpage based on their relevance to the source context and link text. They benchmark several baseline approaches including BM25 using either the link's local context or source webpage title, and a T5-based ranking model (RankT5) that treats the task as ranking candidate paragraphs. The T5 models are fine-tuned using listwise softmax cross-entropy loss with batches of 36 candidates per list. The evaluation uses accuracy, accepting multiple ground-truth labels to account for subjective relevance judgments.

## Key Results
- T5-based ranking approach achieves 82.84% accuracy on AUTHOR ANCHORS dataset
- Performance drops significantly to 31.15% accuracy on READER ANCHORS dataset
- BM25 baselines perform poorly, indicating lexical overlap alone is insufficient
- AUTHOR ANCHORS and READER ANCHORS show different linking behaviors, with READER ANCHORS targeting named entities more frequently

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Anchor prediction works by jointly reasoning over source and target webpages to identify relevant information
- Mechanism: The model must understand implicit relations between source context, link text, and target content, then filter out redundant information to find the most useful anchor
- Core assumption: Readers benefit from refined anchors that point directly to relevant sections rather than entire target webpages
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "effective anchor prediction often requires jointly reasoning over lengthy source and target webpages to determine their implicit relations and identify parts of the target webpage that are related but not redundant"
  - [section] "refining the link in Figure 1 requires reading multiple parts of the source webpage to understand that Ritter starred in both the original film and the Lux Radio Theatre adaptation"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on different domains (image diffusion, multilingual embeddings) with limited direct relevance to anchor prediction mechanisms
- Break condition: If the source and target have no meaningful semantic connection, or if the target content is too homogeneous to distinguish useful anchors

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Author-driven vs reader-driven relevance judgments create complementary datasets with different linking behaviors
- Mechanism: AUTHOR ANCHORS reflects author constraints (only section-level anchors possible), while READER ANCHORS captures what readers actually find useful regardless of technical limitations
- Core assumption: Different stakeholders (authors vs readers) have different utility functions for what makes a link useful
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "AUTHOR ANCHORS, which reflect relevance judgments by the authors of the source article" and "READER ANCHORS, an evaluation set of anchors that readers find useful"
  - [section] "AUTHOR ANCHORS and READER ANCHORS exhibit different linking behavior...links in READER ANCHORS target articles about named entities, particularly people and organizations, at a much higher rate than AUTHOR ANCHORS links"
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbor papers don't address the distinction between author and reader relevance judgments
- Break condition: If the datasets converge on the same linking behavior, suggesting one type of judgment dominates utility

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transfer learning from section-level to paragraph-level judgments is challenging due to different granularity requirements
- Mechanism: Models trained on AUTHOR ANCHORS become over-reliant on heading text since section-level judgments were the ground truth, but paragraph-level judgments require understanding content within sections
- Core assumption: Different levels of granularity require different reasoning strategies
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also evaluate a T5-based ranking approach and investigate transfer learning from the section-level judgments of AUTHOR ANCHORS to the paragraph-level judgments of READER ANCHORS"
  - [section] "We also find that BM25-Title outperforms BM25-Context on READER ANCHORS, potentially because reader-driven relevance judgments tend to focus on higher-level connections between the source and target webpages"
  - [corpus] Weak - neighbor papers don't address transfer learning between different granularity levels
- Break condition: If models can easily generalize across granularity levels without over-fitting to specific features like headings

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Text ranking and relevance scoring
  - Why needed here: The task is fundamentally a ranking problem where models must score candidate anchors by relevance
  - Quick check question: How would you implement a basic ranking system that scores candidates based on similarity to a query?

- Concept: Transfer learning and domain adaptation
  - Why needed here: Models must transfer from section-level annotations (AUTHOR ANCHORS) to paragraph-level judgments (READER ANCHORS)
  - Quick check question: What challenges arise when transferring knowledge between datasets with different annotation granularities?

- Concept: Information retrieval evaluation metrics
  - Why needed here: The task uses accuracy as the primary metric, requiring understanding of what constitutes correct predictions
  - Quick check question: How would you design an evaluation metric that accounts for subjective relevance judgments?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input link -> Extract source context and target -> Generate features -> Score candidates -> Output highest-scoring anchor
- Critical path: Input link → Extract source context and target → Generate features → Score candidates → Output highest-scoring anchor
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Granularity vs subjectivity: Paragraph-level anchors are useful but more subjective than section-level
  - Model complexity vs performance: T5-based models perform better but require more resources than BM25
  - Training data vs generalization: More AUTHOR ANCHORS data helps but may not transfer well to READER ANCHORS
- Failure signatures:
  - Over-reliance on lexical overlap (BM25 baselines perform poorly)
  - Selection of irrelevant paragraphs within relevant sections
  - Failure to recognize when links should remain unanchored
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare BM25-Context vs BM25-Title on AUTHOR ANCHORS to understand lexical vs semantic importance
  2. Test RankT5-Small vs RankT5-Base on AUTHOR ANCHORS to measure impact of model capacity
  3. Evaluate transfer performance by training on AUTHOR ANCHORS and testing on READER ANCHORS to identify generalization gaps

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How would anchor prediction performance change if we used sentence-level or arbitrary-span anchor candidates instead of paragraph-level candidates?
- Can anchor prediction models be effectively trained to abstain from predicting anchors when no useful anchor exists?
- How well would anchor prediction models generalize to non-English Wikipedia and to the open web beyond Wikipedia?

## Limitations

- Significant performance drop when transferring from section-level (AUTHOR ANCHORS) to paragraph-level (READER ANCHORS) annotations
- Current models cannot identify when links should remain unanchored, limiting practical utility
- Datasets only cover English Wikipedia, limiting generalizability to other domains and languages

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Core task definition and dataset creation methodology are well-specified and reproducible
- **Medium confidence**: Performance claims are valid for specific datasets but may not generalize beyond Wikipedia
- **Low confidence**: Practical utility claims lack empirical validation beyond relevance judgments
