# Learning to Forecast Aleatoric and Epistemic Uncertainties over Long Horizon Trajectories

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08669
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08669
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Residual CVAE achieves significantly lower MMD and Brier scores than probabilistic MLPs and full VAEs for long-horizon trajectory forecasting with uncertainty quantification

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method to quantify both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties in long-horizon trajectory forecasting for autonomous agents. The core innovation is using deep ensembles to capture epistemic uncertainty, then training a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) on aleatoric residuals computed from ensemble forecasts and observed trajectories. This approach ensures aleatoric uncertainty modeling accounts for epistemic uncertainty effects, unlike naive generative models. Experiments on Pusher and UAV RL environments demonstrate superior performance in uncertainty quantification and outcome probability predictions compared to probabilistic MLPs and full VAEs.

## Method Summary
The method trains M ensemble models to quantify epistemic uncertainty, then computes aleatoric residuals by subtracting sampled ensemble predictions from observed trajectories. These residuals, which embed epistemic effects, are used to train a conditional VAE with recurrent encoder/decoder structure. The CVAE learns to predict aleatoric uncertainty distributions without assuming Gaussianity, while the state prediction loss prevents overfitting to computed residuals. This approach explicitly accounts for the interaction between aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, which compounds over long horizons.

## Key Results
- Residual CVAE achieves significantly lower maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) values compared to probabilistic MLPs and full VAEs
- Brier scores for outcome probability predictions are substantially improved across both low and high epistemic uncertainty scenarios
- The method demonstrates more accurate aleatoric uncertainty quantification by accounting for epistemic uncertainty effects

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The residual CVAE learns aleatoric uncertainty by explicitly accounting for epistemic uncertainty effects through training on residuals computed from ensemble forecasts.
- Mechanism: Ensemble models first quantify epistemic uncertainty, then residuals (ϵalea = ytrue - ˆym) are computed using sampled ensemble outputs and observed data. These residuals embed epistemic effects and train the CVAE.
- Core assumption: Ensemble variance adequately represents epistemic uncertainty and can be subtracted implicitly through residual computation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the core method idea is to first use deep ensembles to quantify epistemic uncertainty, then train a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) on the aleatoric residual, which is computed by comparing observed trajectories with ensemble forecasts—thereby incorporating epistemic uncertainty effects."
  - [section]: "The residual arises due to the uncertainty in the forecasted states and can be separated into their two components: aleatoric and epistemic... Using this data, the goal is to learn the distribution: p(ϵalea,1,ϵalea,2, ...,ϵalea,T|s0;a0,a1, ...,aT−1)"
- Break condition: If ensemble variance does not adequately represent epistemic uncertainty, or if residual computation introduces additional bias.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The CVAE's latent variable structure captures complex, non-Gaussian aleatoric uncertainty distributions that arise from compounding effects over long horizons.
- Mechanism: Recurrent encoder/decoder with latent variables (zt) models temporal dependencies and stochasticity in aleatoric residuals without assuming Gaussianity.
- Core assumption: Non-linear transformations through the CVAE's latent space can adequately represent the true aleatoric residual distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This approach contrasts with naive generative models that do not account for epistemic uncertainty" and "Experiments on two RL environments... show that the residual CVAE achieves significantly lower maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) values... demonstrating more accurate aleatoric uncertainty quantification"
  - [section]: "To our knowledge, our work is the first to analyze the usage of generative models for aleatoric uncertainty isolation for long horizon forecasting" and "Since we are working with temporal data, a recurrent structure can be added within both the encoder and decoder portions of the CVAE so that there is memory within the network"
- Break condition: If the aleatoric residual distribution is too complex for the CVAE's capacity, or if temporal dependencies are not well-captured by the recurrent structure.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Training the CVAE to minimize the difference between predicted states (ˆspred = ˆϵalea + ˆym) and observed states (ytrue) prevents overfitting to computed residuals and ensures generalization.
- Mechanism: Loss function optimizes for accurate state predictions rather than perfect residual reproduction, preventing the model from learning noise in training residuals.
- Core assumption: Predicted states (ˆspred) are a more meaningful target than residuals alone for capturing aleatoric uncertainty.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Although the input and the output from the CVAE is ϵalea and its prediction ˆϵalea, respectively, the loss function optimizes the difference between the state prediction ypred = ˆϵalea + ˆym and the observed state ytrue. This prevents any individual, computed ϵalea to be considered the 'true' aleatoric residual value"
- Break condition: If ensemble predictions (ˆym) are systematically biased, causing the CVAE to learn biased aleatoric uncertainty.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the CVAE method for aleatoric uncertainty estimation introduce its own epistemic uncertainty that affects competency reporting?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that "we recognize that the model design process of CV AE may itself introduce some epistemic uncertainty" but do not investigate this further.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on systemic epistemic uncertainty from the world model ensemble, leaving potential epistemic uncertainty from the CVAE training process unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments quantifying and comparing epistemic uncertainty from CVAE model parameters versus ensemble models, showing whether CVAE epistemic uncertainty is negligible or significant.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does epistemic uncertainty propagate through long-horizon forecasting and interact with aleatoric uncertainty at different time scales?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors state that "aleatoric uncertainty is naturally intertwined with epistemic uncertainty due to the dependence of model output on the model parameters" and discuss compounding over time, but don't analyze time-varying interactions.
- Why unresolved: The experiments show aggregate performance across the full trajectory but don't examine how uncertainty interactions evolve temporally or at different forecast horizons.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty components at each time step across varying forecast horizons, showing temporal evolution and interaction patterns.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does uncertainty quantification affect competency assessment when agents face multiple or increasingly complex objectives?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "we plan to explore the ties between the uncertainty types, the forecasted outcome distributions, and the agent's performance" and "task the agent with multiple and increasingly complex objectives while studying the impacts of uncertainties."
- Why unresolved: The current experiments focus on single outcome probabilities (reaching target location within time), leaving multi-objective competency assessment unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with agents performing multiple concurrent tasks where uncertainty quantification improves or degrades competency assessment accuracy across varying task complexity levels.

## Limitations
- The method assumes ensemble variance adequately captures epistemic uncertainty, which may not hold for all types of epistemic uncertainty
- CVAE architecture details (latent dimension, recurrent structure specifics) are underspecified, making capacity assessment difficult
- Evaluation is limited to two RL environments with specific uncertainty injection methods, limiting generalizability claims

## Confidence

**Mechanism Confidence Assessment:**
- **Mechanism 1 (Epistemic-aleatoric interaction):** Medium confidence. Residual computation approach is well-motivated but lacks empirical validation across all scenarios
- **Mechanism 2 (CVAE non-Gaussian modeling):** Medium confidence. Better MMD scores demonstrated but lacks qualitative analysis of learned distributions
- **Mechanism 3 (State prediction loss):** Low confidence. Rationale stated but not empirically validated with ablation studies
