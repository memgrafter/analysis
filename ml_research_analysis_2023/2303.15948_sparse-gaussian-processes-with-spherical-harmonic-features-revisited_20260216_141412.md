# Sparse Gaussian Processes with Spherical Harmonic Features Revisited

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15948
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15948
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: Introduces continuous depth hyperparameter for spherical harmonic GP kernels and variational phase learning for scalability

## Executive Summary
This work revisits Gaussian process models using spherical harmonic features, introducing a new class of kernels with continuous depth that can be learned as a hyperparameter. The authors propose variational learning of spherical harmonic phases, introducing sparseness in the eigenbasis while maintaining orthogonality through Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization. Experiments on SUSY classification and UCI regression benchmarks demonstrate competitive performance compared to neural networks and previous GP methods, while offering uncertainty quantification and improved scalability to higher dimensions.

## Method Summary
The method builds on spherical harmonic basis functions to construct Gaussian process kernels with continuous depth parameterized by β. Inducing variables are defined as inner products between the GP function and spherical harmonics, exploiting their orthogonality to obtain diagonal covariance structures for efficient variational inference. The authors introduce phase truncation combined with Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization to reduce computational complexity while maintaining representational power. Model training optimizes the evidence lower bound with respect to kernel hyperparameters and variational parameters, allowing the continuous depth parameter β to be learned from data.

## Key Results
- AuC score of 0.7643 on SUSY classification, comparable to a 5-layer neural network
- Improved NLL and RMSE performance on UCI song, buzz, and houseelectric datasets
- Reduced computational complexity through sparse phase-truncated spherical harmonics while maintaining high-frequency information

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The spherical harmonic basis diagonalizes the covariance matrix between inducing variables, enabling efficient variational inference.
- Mechanism: By choosing inducing variables as inner products between the GP function and spherical harmonics, the covariance structure becomes diagonal due to orthogonality of the basis functions.
- Core assumption: The spherical harmonics form a complete orthonormal basis on the sphere Sd-1.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Those authors introduced a variational inference method where spherical harmonics form the basis of an approximation to the Gaussian process posterior."
  - [section] "cov [f(x),um ℓ ] = E [f(x)um ℓ ] =⟨k(x,·),φm ℓ⟩H =φm ℓ (x)."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct corpus support found for diagonal covariance structure claim.
- Break condition: If the spherical harmonics are not orthogonal or do not form a complete basis, the diagonalization property fails and computational benefits are lost.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Introducing continuous depth as a learnable hyperparameter allows the model to adapt its complexity to the data.
- Mechanism: The proposed kernel uses a polynomial eigenvalue decay ℓ-β where β is a hyperparameter. Lower β values correspond to slower decay (deeper networks), enabling the model to learn appropriate depth.
- Core assumption: The eigenvalue decay rate correlates with network depth in deep neural networks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we introduce a new class of kernels which correspond to deep models of continuous depth."
  - [section] "we can define a spherical kernel without having access to a specific shape function... We propose to use a kernel of the form: k(x, x′) =r(x, x′)∞∑ℓ=0 ℓ +α α ℓ-βC (α)ℓ (x⊤x′)."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct corpus support found for continuous depth hyperparameter claim.
- Break condition: If the relationship between β and actual network depth is non-monotonic or context-dependent, learning β may not correctly represent network depth.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Variational learning of spherical harmonic phases introduces sparseness while preserving high-frequency information.
- Mechanism: Instead of using all phases for each frequency, the model truncates at the phase level, allowing higher frequency truncation while maintaining orthogonality through Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization.
- Core assumption: Truncating phases while maintaining orthogonality preserves the representational power of high frequencies.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we introduce sparseness in the eigenbasis by variational learning of the spherical harmonic phases."
  - [section] "we propose to introduce an extra truncation ˆm, this time at the phase level of the spherical harmonics... We ensure, however, that they remain orthogonal polynomials by explicitly orthogonalizing the corresponding Vℓ within each truncated frequency."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct corpus support found for phase truncation approach.
- Break condition: If the phase truncation removes critical phase information needed for the function representation, the model loses expressiveness.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS)
  - Why needed here: The entire framework builds on the RKHS associated with spherical kernels, which determines the function space the GP can represent.
  - Quick check question: What property of RKHS ensures that evaluating a function at a point is a continuous linear functional?

  - Why needed here: The kernel is represented as an infinite sum of eigenfunctions (spherical harmonics) weighted by eigenvalues, which determines the spectral properties of the GP.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Spherical harmonics φm ℓ (x) -> Inducing variables um ℓ = ⟨f, φm ℓ⟩H -> Kernel with depth β -> Variational parameters -> ELBO objective

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize inducing points on the sphere using Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization
  2. Compute covariance between inducing variables (diagonal due to orthogonality)
  3. Build variational posterior with diagonal covariance structure
  4. Optimize ELBO with respect to kernel hyperparameters and variational parameters
  5. Evaluate model on test data

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Phase truncation vs. full harmonics: Truncation reduces computation but may lose information
  - Number of frequencies vs. model capacity: More frequencies capture more complexity but increase computation
  - Continuous depth vs. discrete depth: Continuous allows finer optimization but may be harder to interpret

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance with too few frequencies or excessive phase truncation
  - Optimization instability when β approaches extreme values
  - Numerical issues when eigenvalues become very small for high frequencies

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare full spherical harmonics vs. sparse phase-truncated version on a simple regression task
  2. Test the effect of different β values on a small UCI dataset
  3. Verify the diagonal covariance property by checking Kuu matrix structure

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several remain unresolved from the analysis:
- How the continuous depth parameterization scales with input dimension beyond 10D
- The theoretical relationship between learned β values and equivalent deep neural network depths
- How phase truncation affects the model's ability to capture localized versus global features

## Limitations
- Empirical validation of continuous depth parameterization is limited to a single classification task and three regression datasets
- Optimal truncation levels for frequencies and phases are not rigorously determined
- Lack of comprehensive ablation studies to quantify information loss versus computational gains from phase truncation

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The diagonal covariance structure of inducing variables due to spherical harmonic orthogonality, and the ELBO optimization framework are well-established theoretical results with clear empirical support.
- **Medium Confidence**: The continuous depth parameterization as a learnable hyperparameter is theoretically motivated but requires more extensive empirical validation across diverse datasets and architectures.
- **Low Confidence**: The optimal truncation levels for frequencies and phases are not rigorously determined, and the impact of different truncation strategies on model performance remains largely unexplored.

## Next Checks
