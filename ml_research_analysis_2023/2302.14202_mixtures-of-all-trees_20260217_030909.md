# Mixtures of All Trees

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14202
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14202
- Reference count: 27
- Key outcome: Introduces Mixture of All Trees (MoAT), achieving state-of-the-art density estimation on 14/20 datasets with tractable likelihood computation despite super-exponential mixture components

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Mixture of All Trees (MoAT), a new class of generative models that represents a mixture over all possible tree-shaped graphical models. Despite the super-exponential number of mixture components, MoAT is compactly parameterized with O(n^2) parameters through parameter sharing across trees. The paper demonstrates that MoAT allows for tractable likelihood computation via determinant calculations, enables effective density estimation on real-world benchmarks (achieving state-of-the-art results on 14/20 datasets), and provides efficient approximate inference through importance sampling. However, the paper also proves that exact computation of marginals and MAP inference are NP-hard in MoAT.

## Method Summary
MoAT represents a mixture over all possible spanning tree graphical models while sharing parameters across trees to maintain a compact O(n^2) parameterization. The model computes likelihoods tractably using determinant calculations of weighted Laplacian matrices, enabling efficient training via gradient descent. For inference, MoAT employs importance sampling techniques that leverage the tractability of individual tree components. The model is trained using maximum likelihood estimation with deterministic initialization based on mutual information, showing improved convergence compared to random initialization.

## Key Results
- MoAT achieves state-of-the-art density estimation on 14 out of 20 benchmark datasets
- Tractable likelihood computation via determinant calculations despite super-exponential mixture components
- Effective approximate inference through importance sampling with fast convergence
- NP-hardness results for exact marginal and MAP inference in MoAT

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MoAT achieves tractable likelihood computation despite a super-exponential number of mixture components.
- Mechanism: The model parameterizes univariate and pairwise marginals consistently across all trees, allowing the likelihood to be computed as a determinant of a weighted Laplacian matrix, which is O(n^ω) time.
- Core assumption: All mixture components share the same consistent set of marginals, and the spanning tree distribution is tractable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "MoAT is compactly parameterized with O(n^2) parameters through parameter sharing across trees... tractable likelihood computation via determinant calculations"
  - [section 2.2]: Derives PrMoAT(x) = (1/Z) * (∏v∈V Pv(xv)) * det(L*⏐⏐x)
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; related work focuses on tractable tree models but not mixtures of all trees.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: MoAT provides efficient approximate inference via importance sampling despite NP-hard exact computation.
- Mechanism: MoAT can be interpreted as a latent variable model where the latent variable selects a spanning tree. Conditional sampling on individual trees is tractable, so importance sampling with a proposal distribution Q(y,x|e) ≈ P(y|x,e)P(x|ye) yields fast convergence.
- Core assumption: All mixture components are near-maximum likelihood on the entire dataset, so tree-conditioned likelihoods are close.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2]: Describes collapsed importance sampling where sample weights w(y|e) = P(e|y)/P(e) are close to 1.
  - [abstract]: "devise fast-converging conditional sampling algorithms for approximate inference"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus neighbors focus on tractable inference in single trees, not mixtures.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: MoAT learns deterministic parameter initialization that leads to better convergence than random initialization.
- Mechanism: Edge weights initialized with mutual information, univariate and pairwise marginals estimated from training data. This provides a good starting point close to the optimum.
- Core assumption: Mutual information captures relevant dependencies for tree structure, and empirical marginals approximate true ones.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "we use mutual information to initialize we; besides, we also initialize the univariate and pairwise marginals of the MoAT model by estimating them from training data"
  - [figure 3]: Compares our initialization vs random initialization showing better initial log likelihood, faster convergence, and better final log likelihood.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; corpus neighbors do not discuss initialization strategies for mixture models.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Edge weights w_e -> univariate marginals p_v -> pairwise marginals p_uv -> determinant computation -> likelihood

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize parameters using mutual information and empirical marginals
  2. Compute likelihood via determinant calculation
  3. Backpropagate through determinant computation
  4. Update parameters via SGD
  5. For inference, sample spanning trees and perform tree-conditioned sampling

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Expressiveness vs tractability: MoAT balances by using mixtures of tractable tree components
  - Parameter efficiency: Sharing marginals across trees keeps parameters O(n^2) despite super-exponential mixture size
  - Inference accuracy: Exact marginals are NP-hard, but importance sampling provides practical approximations

- Failure signatures:
  - Determinant computation fails: Check if marginals are consistent (rows/columns sum correctly)
  - Importance sampling has high variance: Check if mixture components differ significantly in likelihood
  - Training diverges: Check learning rate and batch size for dataset dimensionality

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify determinant-based likelihood computation on small MoAT model (n=3) by enumeration
  2. Compare convergence of MoAT with deterministic vs random initialization on a simple dataset
  3. Test importance sampling convergence on MoAT with synthetic data where ground truth marginals are known

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the hardness of exact marginal computation in MoAT be overcome by developing new parameter-value-aware inference algorithms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proves that computing marginals in MoAT is NP-hard and suggests this eliminates most known exact marginal inference techniques since they are parameter-value agnostic.
- Why unresolved: The paper only establishes the hardness result and notes this opens up questions about new classes of marginal computation algorithms, but does not explore potential solutions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A concrete proposal for a new marginal inference algorithm that exploits the structure of MoAT's parameterization and empirical demonstration of its effectiveness would resolve this.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is there a fully general parameterization for MoAT over categorical variables that allows for unconstrained optimization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that while the MoAT parameterization for categorical variables is valid, it is not fully general, and whether such a fully general parameterization exists is unknown.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as an open question but does not provide a definitive answer or construction.
- What evidence would resolve it: Either a proof that no fully general parameterization exists for categorical variables in MoAT, or an explicit construction of such a parameterization would resolve this.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does MoAT scale to non-tabular data such as images and text, and what modifications might be needed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions in the conclusion that exploring MoAT's potential to scale to non-tabular data is left for future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates MoAT on tabular data benchmarks and does not investigate its applicability to more complex data types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating MoAT's performance on image or text datasets, along with any necessary architectural modifications, would provide evidence for its scalability to non-tabular data.

## Limitations

- Modest improvement over existing methods on several benchmark datasets
- Lack of theoretical convergence guarantees for importance sampling approach
