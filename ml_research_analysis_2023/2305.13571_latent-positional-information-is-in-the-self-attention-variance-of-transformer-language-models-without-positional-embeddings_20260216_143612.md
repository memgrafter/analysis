# Latent Positional Information is in the Self-Attention Variance of Transformer Language Models Without Positional Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13571
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13571
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- This paper shows that transformer language models without positional embeddings inherently encode positional information through the variance of self-attention outputs.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals that transformer language models without explicit positional embeddings can still encode positional information through the variance of their self-attention outputs. The key finding is that later tokens in a sequence have smaller self-attention variance, creating a natural positional signal. The authors theoretically derive this variance shrinkage effect for randomly initialized transformers and validate that it persists in pre-trained models. This result helps explain why transformer language models can work well without explicit positional embeddings, enabling more efficient pretraining.

## Method Summary
The paper theoretically analyzes the variance of self-attention outputs in randomly initialized transformers and empirically validates the findings on a pre-trained transformer language model. The theoretical analysis derives that the self-attention output variance at position m is proportional to 1/m, creating a natural positional signal. The authors then test this property on a transformer trained on OpenWebText2 data, confirming that the variance shrinkage effect persists even after training.

## Key Results
- Later tokens in a sequence have smaller self-attention variance, providing a natural positional signal
- The theoretical variance shrinkage effect holds for randomly initialized transformers
- The variance-based positional encoding persists in pre-trained transformer language models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Self-attention variance decreases with token position, creating a natural positional signal
- Mechanism: In randomly initialized transformers, the self-attention output variance at position m is proportional to 1/m. Later tokens in the sequence have smaller variance due to the averaging effect of attention weights over all previous positions.
- Core assumption: Property 1 holds - when σ⁴ ≪ H/d², attention weights are approximately evenly distributed among all positions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "later tokens in a sequence have smaller self-attention variance, providing a natural positional signal"
  - [section 3.2] "Lemma 3. om has zero mean and d²σ⁴/m I covariance matrix" - this directly shows variance decreases with position m
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct mentions of variance shrinkage in related papers
- Break condition: Property 1 fails when σ⁴ is not much smaller than H/d², causing attention weights to concentrate on nearby positions rather than being evenly distributed

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Residual connections preserve and propagate positional variance information through layers
- Mechanism: The residual connection adds the original input embedding to the self-attention output, preserving the variance-based positional signal while allowing it to be passed to subsequent layers and the final classifier
- Core assumption: The variance difference created by self-attention is sufficient for the classifier to distinguish positions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "the residual connection sets the output as ym = xm + om" - explicitly shows how positional information is preserved
  - [section 3.4] "the classifier should be able to exploit the discrepancy to derive m" - confirms the classifier can use variance differences
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct mentions of residual connections preserving positional information
- Break condition: If the variance differences become too small (e.g., very late positions), the classifier may not be able to reliably distinguish positions

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Layer normalization maintains variance-based positional information while stabilizing training
- Mechanism: Layer normalization computes sample mean and variance for each token, but the variance difference between positions remains sufficient for positional encoding to persist through normalization
- Core assumption: The variance differences created by self-attention are not completely eliminated by layer normalization
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1] "LN(ym) gives: y′mi ≈ ymi − E[ymi]/√(V[ymi])" - shows layer normalization preserves some variance information
  - [section 3.4] "As long as Σi(Wo,j:Wv,:i)² ≠ d²σ⁴, the classifier should be able to exploit the discrepancy to derive m" - confirms layer normalization doesn't eliminate positional information
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct mentions of layer normalization preserving positional information
- Break condition: If the variance differences become too small relative to the normalization scale, positional information may be lost

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input Embeddings → LayerNorm → Self-Attention → Residual Connection → LayerNorm → ... (repeated for 12 layers) → Output
- Critical path: Self-Attention → Residual connection (this is where the positional information is created and preserved)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Removing positional embeddings saves parameters and computation but relies on variance-based positional encoding
  - Variance-based encoding may be less robust for very long sequences or when variance differences become too small
- Failure signatures:
  - Positional information is lost: Variance differences between positions become too small (e.g., very late positions)
  - Model performance degrades: Without explicit positional embeddings, the model may struggle with certain positional tasks
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify variance shrinkage: Plot variance vs. position for different layers to confirm the theoretical prediction
  2. Test positional probing: Train a classifier to predict token positions using only the transformer outputs (no positional embeddings)
  3. Ablation study: Remove layer normalization or residual connections to see their effect on positional information retention

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the variance-based positional encoding persist in transformer language models trained on real human language data rather than randomly sampled inputs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the theoretical analysis assumes randomly sampled input embeddings, but empirical validation is performed on a pre-trained model using OpenWebText2.
- Why unresolved: The paper only validates the variance shrinkage effect on a pre-trained model without explicitly analyzing how the properties change when trained on real language data versus random inputs.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study showing the variance properties of transformer layers trained on random inputs versus real language corpora would clarify whether the effect is robust to the input distribution.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the variance-based positional encoding scale to extremely long input sequences, and what is the maximum sequence length at which it remains effective?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses that later tokens in a sequence have smaller variance, which might make it challenging for the model to infer positional information of later tokens in extremely long sequences.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests up to sequence length L = 512 and mentions the potential difficulty with longer sequences without providing empirical evidence or theoretical bounds.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing variance-based positional encoding on sequences of increasing length (e.g., 512, 1024, 2048 tokens) would establish the practical limits of this encoding method.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the variance-based positional encoding be exploited to design more efficient transformer architectures that do not require explicit positional embeddings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes that understanding variance-based positional encoding could enable the development of more efficient, positional embedding-free transformer language models.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies the phenomenon and validates its persistence in pre-trained models, it does not propose or test specific architectural modifications to leverage this insight.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing and training transformer variants that explicitly utilize variance-based positional information (e.g., through modified attention mechanisms or loss functions) would demonstrate practical applications of this finding.

## Limitations

- The analysis relies heavily on Property 1, which assumes σ⁴ ≪ H/d², and may not hold in practice for later layers or trained models
- While variance shrinkage is theoretically derived and empirically validated, the practical utility of this signal for downstream tasks is not directly measured
- The paper doesn't explore how the variance-based positional encoding compares to explicit positional embeddings in terms of performance or robustness

## Confidence

**High confidence** in the variance shrinkage mechanism for randomly initialized transformers, as this is directly derived from the mathematical analysis and supported by the lemma showing d²σ⁴/m covariance structure.

**Medium confidence** in the persistence of variance-based positional information in pre-trained models, as the paper demonstrates this empirically but doesn't fully explain why the theoretical conditions continue to hold after training.

**Low confidence** in the practical implications, as the paper doesn't directly measure whether models actually use this variance signal for positional tasks or how it compares to explicit positional embeddings in terms of performance.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation analysis of Property 1 conditions**: Systematically vary σ², H, and d in controlled experiments to map the boundary conditions where Property 1 breaks down, and measure how this affects positional information retention.

2. **Positional probing task evaluation**: Train and evaluate a position predictor using only the variance information from transformer outputs across different sequence lengths and positions, comparing performance to models with explicit positional embeddings.

3. **Layer-wise variance analysis**: Measure and compare the variance shrinkage effect across all layers in pre-trained models to understand how the signal evolves during training and which layers contribute most to positional encoding.
