# MD3: The Multi-Dialect Dataset of Dialogues

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11355
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11355
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: MD3 is a new dataset of conversational speech representing English from India, Nigeria, and the United States, organized around guessing games for cross-dialectal comparison.

## Executive Summary
MD3 introduces a novel multi-dialect dataset of conversational English containing over 20 hours of audio and 200,000 transcribed tokens from India, Nigeria, and the United States. The dataset is structured around guessing games where speakers must communicate specific information to each other, enabling controlled quantitative comparison across dialects. Preliminary analysis reveals significant differences in syntax and discourse marker usage across the three English varieties, demonstrating the dataset's potential for dialect-aware natural language processing research.

## Method Summary
The dataset was constructed by recording participants from three English-speaking regions engaged in structured guessing games. These games required speakers to communicate specific information, creating controlled conversational scenarios that facilitate cross-dialectal comparison. The audio recordings were orthographically transcribed, resulting in a dataset with over 20 hours of speech and more than 200,000 tokens. The guessing game format ensures that speakers produce comparable linguistic content while allowing natural variation in how information is communicated across dialects.

## Key Results
- Dataset contains over 20 hours of conversational speech from three English dialects
- Includes more than 200,000 orthographically-transcribed tokens
- Preliminary analysis shows significant differences in syntax across Indian, Nigerian, and American English
- Notable variation in discourse marker usage observed between dialects

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The guessing game format creates controlled conversational scenarios where speakers must communicate specific information, enabling direct comparison of how different dialects accomplish the same communicative goals. This structure controls for content while allowing natural variation in expression, making it possible to isolate dialect-specific patterns in syntax and discourse markers.
