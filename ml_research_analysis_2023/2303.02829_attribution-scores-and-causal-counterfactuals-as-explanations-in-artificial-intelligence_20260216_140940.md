# Attribution-Scores and Causal Counterfactuals as Explanations in Artificial Intelligence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02829
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02829
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The article surveys methods for providing explanations in AI, focusing on attribution scores and counterfactual reasoning, and presents algorithms for computing Shapley scores and using answer-set programming for counterfactual explanations.

## Executive Summary
This article surveys the role of explanations in AI, focusing on explainable AI (XAI) and its connections to older approaches in AI, philosophy, and logic. It describes methods based on attribution scores and counterfactual reasoning for providing explanations in data management and machine learning. Specifically, it discusses consistency-based diagnosis, abduction, and actual causality with responsibility scores for identifying causes of observations. It also presents the generalized Resp score and Shapley score for quantifying the relevance of features to classification outcomes. The article argues for the importance of counterfactual reasoning in XAI and describes using answer-set programming to specify and reason about counterfactuals.

## Method Summary
The article describes methods for computing attribution scores (responsibility and Shapley scores) to explain machine learning classifier decisions. For responsibility scores, interventions on feature values are made to compute expected label differences. For Shapley scores, expected label values are computed over subsets of features using probability distributions. The article also presents algorithms for computing Shapley scores for certain classes of classifiers and demonstrates how answer-set programming can be used to specify and compute counterfactual explanations and responsibility scores.

## Key Results
- Consistency-based diagnosis identifies minimal sets of faulty components that restore logical consistency between a model and an observation.
- Actual causality identifies counterfactual causes by testing if interventions on endogenous variables would change the observation outcome.
- The Shapley value provides attribution scores by averaging marginal contributions across all possible feature coalitions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Consistency-based diagnosis provides explanations by identifying minimal sets of faulty components that restore logical consistency between a model and an observation. The method introduces abnormality predicates to allow failures in the model, then finds minimal sets of components to declare abnormal such that the combination of observation and modified model becomes consistent. Core assumption: The system has a formal logical model that can be extended with abnormality predicates to capture failures. Evidence anchors: A better, more flexible model that allows failures, and specifies how components behave under normal conditions is as follows: M = {¬AbA → (x ↔ (a ∧ b)), ¬AbO → (d ↔ (x ∨ c))}. The model assumes that the only potentially faulty components are, in this case, the gates... Now gates could be abnormal (or faulty), and {Obs} ∪ M is a perfectly consistent logical model. Break condition: If the system cannot be modeled logically or if abnormality predicates cannot capture all failure modes, the method fails.

### Mechanism 2
Actual causality identifies counterfactual causes by testing if interventions on endogenous variables would change the observation outcome. For each candidate cause, perform counterfactual interventions (changing truth values) and check if the original entailment changes. Causes are ranked by responsibility scores that account for contingency sets. Core assumption: We can distinguish endogenous variables (under our control) from exogenous ones, and we can perform hypothetical interventions. Evidence anchors: Switching ¬abO into AbO, does invalidate the previous entailment: {a, ¬b, c} ∪ M ∪ {¬ AbA, AbO} ̸|= d. AbA is not a counterfactual cause. For this candidate, an extra counterfactual change, a so-called contingent change, is necessary: {a, ¬b, c} ∪ M ∪ {AbA, AbO} ̸|= d. Break condition: If interventions cannot be meaningfully defined or if the causal structure is too complex to evaluate counterfactuals.

### Mechanism 3
The Shapley value provides attribution scores by averaging marginal contributions across all possible feature coalitions. For each feature, compute its expected contribution to the classification outcome by considering all subsets of other features and averaging the differences in expected labels when the feature is added or removed. Core assumption: There exists a probability distribution over the entity population that allows computing expected values for feature subsets. Evidence anchors: The Shapley value of player p ∈ S quantifies the contribution of p to the game, for which all different coalitions are considered; each time, with p and without p. The game function, for a given subset S of the features, is the expected (value of the) label over all possible entities whose values coincide with those of e for the features in S. Break condition: If the probability distribution is unknown or intractable to compute, or if the classifier's behavior is too complex for coalition analysis.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Logical consistency and entailment
  - Why needed here: The paper uses logical models extensively to represent system behavior and define diagnoses and causes. Understanding consistency restoration is crucial for grasping consistency-based diagnosis.
  - Quick check question: If a set of formulas is inconsistent, what logical property allows us to conclude anything from it?

- Concept: Counterfactual reasoning and intervention
  - Why needed here: The paper relies heavily on counterfactual thinking to define actual causality and compute responsibility scores. Understanding how to perform and evaluate hypothetical interventions is essential.
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes a counterfactual cause from an actual cause with a non-empty contingency set?

- Concept: Game theory and marginal contribution
  - Why needed here: The Shapley value, which forms the basis for the Shap score, comes from cooperative game theory. Understanding how marginal contributions are computed and averaged is key to grasping attribution scoring.
  - Quick check question: How does the Shapley value account for the order in which players join a coalition?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Logical model -> Abnormality predicates -> Observation -> Minimal diagnoses/counterfactual causes -> Responsibility scores/Shapley scores
- Critical path: 1) Build logical model with abnormality predicates 2) Identify observation 3) Find minimal diagnoses/counterfactual causes 4) Compute responsibility scores 5) Use ASP for counterfactual querying
- Design tradeoffs: Consistency-based diagnosis vs. abductive diagnosis (implicational models vs. weak models of failure), computational complexity of Shapley value vs. simpler attribution methods, explicit vs. implicit counterfactual reasoning
- Failure signatures: Inconsistent models that cannot be repaired, non-distinguishable endogenous/exogenous variables, intractable probability distributions for Shapley computation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement consistency-based diagnosis on a simple Boolean circuit with known faults
  2. Compute responsibility scores for a small classification problem using actual causality
  3. Calculate Shap scores for a binary classifier using a tractable Boolean circuit representation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can answer-set programming be extended to handle probability distributions beyond product distributions for counterfactual explanations?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the need for probabilistic extensions of ASP to handle expected value computations for Resp and Shap scores under more complex distributions.
- Why unresolved: Current ASP approaches work well for uniform and product distributions, but handling more complex, non-independent distributions requires new probabilistic extensions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A working implementation of ASP with probabilistic extensions that can handle arbitrary probability distributions and compute counterfactual explanations and attribution scores.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the trade-off between computational efficiency and expressiveness when choosing between different attribution score methods (Resp, Shap, etc.)?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the computational complexity of different methods, with Resp being intractable and Shap being #P-hard for certain classes of classifiers, but also mentions the need for efficient approximations.
- Why unresolved: There is a need to balance the desire for accurate and interpretable explanations with the practical limitations of computation time and resources.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different attribution score methods on various datasets and classifiers, measuring both accuracy and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can counterfactual explanations be made more actionable in real-world scenarios where interventions are limited or costly?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the concept of actionable counterfactuals but acknowledges the challenges of making meaningful changes in practice.
- Why unresolved: Many proposed counterfactual explanations may not be feasible or practical to implement in real-world settings due to constraints on interventions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies demonstrating the application of counterfactual explanations in real-world decision-making scenarios, with a focus on the practicality and impact of the suggested interventions.

## Limitations

- Computational complexity is a significant challenge, particularly for Shapley score computation which is #P-hard for certain classes of classifiers.
- The paper focuses primarily on Boolean classifiers and logical models, limiting generalizability to real-world, continuous, and non-linear classifiers.
- While the paper discusses actionable counterfactuals, it doesn't provide concrete methods for ensuring proposed interventions are feasible in practice.

## Confidence

- Medium: The ASP-based counterfactual reasoning framework is promising but implementation details are limited
