# PWSHAP: A Path-Wise Explanation Model for Targeted Variables

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14672
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14672
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces Path-Wise Shapley (PWSHAP) effects for local explanation of treatment effects under assumed causal graphs, reconciling reliability and safety in XAI

## Executive Summary
PWSHAP introduces a novel approach to locally explain treatment effects by computing Shapley values along coalitions that match the posited causal structure. The method splits these values into effects along causal paths, providing a theoretically grounded framework for path-wise explanation. By leveraging causal graph assumptions, PWSHAP offers a way to assess bias, mediation, fairness, and effect modification while maintaining both reliability and safety in explanations.

## Method Summary
PWSHAP computes Shapley values along coalitions that match the assumed causal graph structure and splits them into effects along causal paths. The method provides theoretical error bounds for identified path-wise Shapley effects and for Shapley values. It reconciles reliability and safety in XAI, unlike existing methods. PWSHAP can be used for bias and mediation analyses, fairness studies, and assessing effect modification under randomized treatment. The approach demonstrates resolution, interpretability, and true locality on examples and a real-world experiment.

## Key Results

- Provides theoretical error bounds for path-wise Shapley effects and Shapley values

- Validates approach on examples and real-world experiments, demonstrating resolution and interpretability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PWSHAP works by leveraging the structure of assumed causal graphs to compute Shapley values along coalitions that match the posited causal structure. By splitting these values into effects along causal paths, the method provides a granular and interpretable explanation of treatment effects. The theoretical error bounds ensure that the identified path-wise effects are reliable and safe, addressing a key limitation of existing XAI methods. This approach allows for a deeper understanding of the mechanisms underlying treatment effects and enables targeted analyses of bias, mediation, fairness, and effect modification.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Causal Graphs**
   - Why needed: To represent the assumed causal structure underlying the treatment effect
   - Quick check: Verify that the graph correctly captures the relationships between variables

2. **Shapley Values**
   - Why needed: To fairly attribute treatment effects to different variables based on their contributions
   - Quick check: Ensure that the computed Shapley values satisfy the properties of efficiency, symmetry, dummy, and additivity

3. **Causal Paths**
   - Why needed: To decompose treatment effects into direct and indirect effects through different causal pathways
   - Quick check: Confirm that all relevant causal paths are identified and accounted for in the analysis

4. **Treatment Effect Modification**
   - Why needed: To assess how the effect of treatment varies across different subgroups or contexts
   - Quick check: Verify that the analysis captures and quantifies the modification of treatment effects by relevant variables

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Causal Graph -> Shapley Value Computation -> Path-wise Effect Decomposition -> Interpretation and Analysis

Critical path: The critical path in PWSHAP involves the computation of Shapley values along coalitions that match the assumed causal graph structure, followed by the decomposition of these values into effects along causal paths. This path is critical because it directly determines the accuracy and interpretability of the identified path-wise effects.

Design tradeoffs: The main design tradeoff in PWSHAP is between the complexity of the assumed causal graph and the computational tractability of the method. More complex causal structures may provide a more accurate representation of the underlying data generating process but can lead to an exponential increase in the number of coalitions and causal paths to consider. Balancing the granularity of the causal graph with computational efficiency is a key challenge in implementing PWSHAP.

Failure signatures: PWSHAP may fail to provide reliable and safe explanations if the assumed causal graph is incorrect or incomplete. Additionally, in high-dimensional settings with many variables, the exponential growth in the number of coalitions and causal paths may make the method computationally intractable. Finally, if the causal paths are not clearly defined or communicated, the interpretability of the path-wise effects may be compromised.

## Limitations
- Computational tractability challenges in high-dimensional settings with many variables and causal paths
- Reliance on assumed causal graph, which may not always be available or accurate
- Theoretical error bounds depend on specific assumptions about causal structure and treatment assignment
- Interpretability of path-wise effects depends on the complexity of the causal graph and clarity of causal path communication
