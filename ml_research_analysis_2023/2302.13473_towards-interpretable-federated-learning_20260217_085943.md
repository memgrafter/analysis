# Towards Interpretable Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13473
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13473
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: This paper provides the first comprehensive survey on interpretable federated learning (IFL), addressing the need to balance performance, privacy, and interpretability in mission-critical applications.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first comprehensive survey of interpretable federated learning (IFL), addressing the critical challenge of making federated learning models interpretable while preserving privacy. The authors propose a unique IFL taxonomy covering model explanations, debugging support, and contribution insights for fair reward allocation. The survey systematically analyzes representative IFL approaches across five key stages: client selection, sample selection, feature selection, model optimization, and contribution evaluation. The work identifies promising future research directions including interpretable model approximation, noise-robust IFL, complex threat model handling, privacy-efficiency trade-offs, and improved interpretability evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a comprehensive IFL taxonomy organizing techniques across five stages: client selection, sample selection, feature selection, model optimization, and contribution evaluation. The survey systematically categorizes existing IFL approaches, analyzing their effectiveness and efficiency metrics. The authors identify two main categories of evaluation metrics - effectiveness metrics (post-interpretation performance and faithfulness) and efficiency metrics (computation cost and communication cost). The survey synthesizes promising future research directions based on identified gaps in current approaches.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: IFL system consists of five main stages: client selection, sample selection, feature selection, model optimization, and contribution evaluation. Each stage has specific techniques and privacy considerations. The system also includes stakeholder analysis and privacy protection analysis components.

- Critical path: The most critical path for implementing IFL would be: 1) Identify stakeholder needs and privacy requirements, 2) Select appropriate techniques for each FL stage, 3) Implement privacy preservation mechanisms, 4) Integrate evaluation metrics, 5) Test under various threat models.

- Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoffs include: interpretability vs. privacy (more interpretable models may expose more information), computational efficiency vs. accuracy (more complex interpretation methods may be more accurate but slower), and resource consumption vs. interpretation quality (higher quality interpretations may require more resources).

- Failure signatures: Common failure modes include: privacy breaches due to inadequate protection techniques, poor model performance due to over-interpretation, resource exhaustion on client devices, and stakeholder dissatisfaction due to inadequate interpretability for their specific needs.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop IFL approaches that are both hard sample-aware and noise-robust while preserving privacy, especially when distinguishing between positively influential samples and noisy samples?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights that existing IFL methods either ignore label noise or use simple loss/gradient thresholds, which are insufficient for distinguishing between noisy and positively influential samples in practice.
- Why unresolved: Current methods lack prior knowledge of thresholds and cannot effectively differentiate between these two types of samples, leading to potential model performance degradation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of IFL techniques that incorporate advanced noise detection and robust sample selection methods, validated through experiments showing improved model accuracy and privacy preservation.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the appropriate and efficient interpretability evaluation metrics for IFL that can measure how much privacy is exposed for a given level of interpretability achieved?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies the need for better interpretability evaluation metrics that can assess the trade-off between privacy exposure and interpretability in IFL.
- Why unresolved: Existing metrics do not directly measure privacy exposure or the quality of interpretations, making it difficult to assess the effectiveness of IFL approaches in practical applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Creation of new evaluation metrics that quantify privacy exposure and interpretability quality, supported by empirical studies demonstrating their applicability and effectiveness in various IFL scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can IFL approaches be designed to handle more complex threat models, such as malicious or colluding participants, to ensure robust privacy and interpretability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that most current IFL approaches are based on simple threat models of semi-honest participants, making them vulnerable to more realistic threats.
- Why unresolved: Current IFL methods are not equipped to handle complex threat models, which limits their adoption in mission-critical applications where robust privacy and interpretability are essential.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of IFL techniques that incorporate advanced security measures to handle complex threat models, demonstrated through simulations and real-world deployments showing enhanced robustness and trustworthiness.

## Limitations

- The survey's completeness is limited by the rapidly evolving nature of IFL research, potentially missing emerging approaches
- Practical implementation details for many techniques remain underspecified, making direct reproduction challenging
- The effectiveness of proposed privacy preservation techniques under complex threat models is not fully validated
