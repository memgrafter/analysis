# EvEval: A Comprehensive Evaluation of Event Semantics for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.15268
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15268
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: EVEVAL benchmark reveals LLMs can understand individual events but struggle with semantic similarity, with chain-of-thought prompting showing limited improvement

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces EVEVAL, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating large language models (LLMs) on event semantic processing. The benchmark covers understanding, reasoning, and prediction of events through 8 datasets. Experiments on several LLMs reveal that while models can understand individual events, they struggle with semantic similarity between events. Models demonstrate strong reasoning in causal and intentional relations but weaker performance in other types. They show better forecasting with more contextual information. Chain-of-thought prompting shows limited improvement, suggesting a need for better utilization of event knowledge and patterns. Structural event representations perform comparably to natural language.

## Method Summary
The EVEVAL benchmark was developed by curating and adapting 8 existing datasets that span different aspects of event semantics including understanding, reasoning, and prediction. The evaluation framework tests LLMs across multiple dimensions: their ability to comprehend individual events, assess semantic similarity between events, reason about causal and intentional relationships, and make predictions based on event contexts. The study employs several popular LLMs and tests various prompting strategies including chain-of-thought approaches to assess performance improvements.

## Key Results
- LLMs can understand individual events but struggle with semantic similarity between events
- Models show strong reasoning performance in causal and intentional relations but weaker performance in other relation types
- Better forecasting performance is achieved with more contextual information provided
- Chain-of-thought prompting shows limited improvement, suggesting need for better event knowledge utilization
- Structural event representations perform comparably to natural language representations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The evaluation framework reveals several important limitations that affect interpretation of results. First, the benchmark focuses on English-language event semantics, which may not generalize to other languages or cultures where event conceptualization differs. Second, the evaluation relies on existing datasets that may contain annotation biases or inconsistencies that could affect model performance measurements. Third, the "limited improvement" from chain-of-thought prompting needs clarification on what specific prompting strategies were tested and whether more sophisticated prompting techniques might yield different results.

## Foundational Learning

### Event Semantics
- **Why needed**: Understanding how events are structured and related is fundamental to natural language comprehension
- **Quick check**: Can you identify the core components of an event (agent, action, object, time, location)?

### Causal and Intentional Relations
- **Why needed**: These relations capture how events influence each other and the purposes behind actions
- **Quick check**: Given two events, can you determine if one causes or enables the other?

### Semantic Similarity
- **Why needed**: Assessing event similarity is crucial for tasks like information retrieval and summarization
- **Quick check**: Can you identify when two different descriptions refer to the same underlying event?
