# State of the Art and Potentialities of Graph-level Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05860
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05860
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Comprehensive survey of graph-level learning techniques covering traditional methods, GL-DNNs, GL-GNNs, and graph pooling with taxonomy, benchmarks, and future directions

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive survey of graph-level learning techniques, spanning traditional methods through modern deep learning approaches. The authors propose a systematic taxonomy that organizes methods into four categories: traditional learning, graph-level deep neural networks, graph-level graph neural networks, and graph pooling. They review representative algorithms, provide comparative analysis, and compile extensive resources including benchmark datasets and evaluation metrics. The survey identifies 13 important future research directions and addresses the gap in comprehensive literature that covers both traditional and deep learning approaches to graph-level learning.

## Method Summary
The survey employs a systematic taxonomy-based approach to organize and review graph-level learning methods. The methodology involves categorizing algorithms into four main branches: traditional learning methods (graph kernels, subgraph mining, graph embeddings), graph-level deep neural networks (RNN-based, CNN-based, CapsNet-based), graph-level graph neural networks (MPNNs, subgraph-based, kernel-based, contrastive learning-based, spectral-based), and graph pooling operations. For each category, representative algorithms are selected and reviewed with comparative analysis. The survey also compiles benchmark datasets, evaluation metrics, and application domains while identifying future research directions through comprehensive literature analysis.

## Key Results
- Proposes a systematic taxonomy covering traditional learning, GL-DNNs, GL-GNNs, and graph pooling

- Maps applications across bioinformatics, chemistry, social networks, and other domains

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can graph-level learning algorithms effectively handle imbalanced label distributions in real-world datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenge of imbalanced learning in graph-level tasks and mentions some preliminary approaches like oversampling minority classes.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as a future direction but doesn't provide concrete solutions. Existing oversampling methods have limitations like overfitting and changing the original distribution.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive evaluation of different imbalance handling techniques specifically designed for graph-level learning, including both traditional and deep learning approaches, with benchmark results on multiple imbalanced graph datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective approaches for graph-level anomaly detection in real-world applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies graph-level anomaly detection as an underexplored area with applications in protein function identification and other domains.
- Why unresolved: While some preliminary work exists combining GL-GNNs with traditional anomaly detection methods, the paper notes that current approaches may not be specifically designed for anomaly detection and could smooth out anomalous information.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic comparison of different graph-level anomaly detection approaches, including both traditional and deep learning methods, evaluated on benchmark datasets with known anomalies and real-world applications.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can graph-level learning algorithms achieve better out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses OOD learning as a critical challenge, noting that existing methods assume training and test data have the same distribution.
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions some recent approaches like invariant learning and invariant subgraph identification, but these are still in early stages and haven't been thoroughly evaluated.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive benchmark for evaluating OOD generalization in graph-level learning, along with a comparison of different techniques (invariant learning, meta-learning, data augmentation, etc.) across multiple datasets with varying distribution shifts.
