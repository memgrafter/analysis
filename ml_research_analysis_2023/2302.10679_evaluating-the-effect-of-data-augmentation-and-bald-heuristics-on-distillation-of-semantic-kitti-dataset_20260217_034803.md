# Evaluating the effect of data augmentation and BALD heuristics on distillation of Semantic-KITTI dataset

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10679
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10679
- Reference count: 4
- This paper evaluates Bayesian active learning (AL) methods for dataset distillation on the full Semantic-KITTI dataset, extending prior work on a subset. It investigates the impact of data augmentation (DA) and the BALD heuristic within an AL framework. Experiments with SqueezeSegV2 and SalsaNext models show that DA and BALD do not significantly improve label efficiency over random sampling on the full dataset, unlike the subset results. This is attributed to dataset redundancy, inappropriate DA, and aggregation method limitations. The study highlights challenges in real-world AL, including redundancy handling, DA relevance, aggregation choice, outlier filtering, diversity integration, uncertainty estimation, and sequential data processing. It concludes that careful DA design, better aggregation, and diversity-aware methods are crucial for effective AL on large-scale LiDAR datasets.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the effectiveness of Bayesian Active Learning by Disagreement (BALD) heuristics and data augmentation in dataset distillation for the full Semantic-KITTI dataset. The research extends prior work that demonstrated success on a subset of Semantic-KITTI to the complete dataset, using SqueezeSegV2 and SalsaNext models for semantic segmentation of LiDAR point clouds. The key finding is that neither BALD-based sampling nor data augmentation significantly improves label efficiency compared to random sampling on the full dataset, contrasting with results from the subset. This ineffectiveness is attributed to dataset redundancy, inappropriate augmentation strategies, and limitations in the aggregation methods used.

The paper highlights several critical challenges in applying active learning to large-scale LiDAR datasets, including the need for redundancy-aware sampling, domain-appropriate data augmentation, better aggregation methods, diversity-promoting strategies, robust outlier filtering, and uncertainty estimation techniques suitable for sequential data. The authors conclude that effective active learning on such datasets requires careful design of augmentation strategies, improved aggregation methods, and integration of diversity-aware sampling techniques to overcome the limitations identified in their experiments.

## Method Summary
The study employs a Bayesian Active Learning framework with BALD heuristics to select informative samples for labeling from the Semantic-KITTI dataset. Data augmentation techniques are applied to the training data to improve model generalization. The AL process involves training models on initially labeled data, evaluating uncertainty on unlabeled data using BALD, selecting the most uncertain samples for labeling, and repeating this cycle. Two segmentation models (SqueezeSegV2 and SalsaNext) are evaluated on the full Semantic-KITTI dataset. The effectiveness of AL with BALD and DA is compared against random sampling baselines. Key challenges identified include dataset redundancy, inappropriate DA for LiDAR data, and limitations in aggregation methods for handling sequential data and outliers.

## Key Results
- BALD heuristics and data augmentation do not significantly improve label efficiency over random sampling on the full Semantic-KITTI dataset
- Previous success of BALD and DA on a subset of Semantic-KITTI does not generalize to the full dataset
- Dataset redundancy, inappropriate augmentation strategies, and aggregation method limitations are identified as key factors limiting AL effectiveness
- The study highlights the need for domain-appropriate DA, better aggregation methods, and diversity-aware sampling for effective AL on large-scale LiDAR datasets

## Foundational Learning
1. **Bayesian Active Learning by Disagreement (BALD)**: A query strategy that selects samples where different model hypotheses disagree most, aiming to maximize information gain. Why needed: To identify the most informative samples for labeling to improve model performance efficiently. Quick check: Verify that BALD selects samples with high epistemic uncertainty and that model disagreement is properly measured.

2. **Data Augmentation for LiDAR Data**: Techniques to artificially expand training data by applying transformations like rotation, scaling, or noise injection to point clouds. Why needed: To improve model robustness and generalization, especially when labeled data is scarce. Quick check: Ensure augmentations preserve semantic meaning and are appropriate for the 3D nature of LiDAR data.

3. **Dataset Redundancy in LiDAR Sequences**: The presence of highly similar or duplicate samples in sequential LiDAR data due to sensor movement and scene repetition. Why needed: Redundancy can reduce the effectiveness of active learning by causing the selection of uninformative similar samples. Quick check: Analyze dataset diversity and similarity metrics to quantify redundancy levels.

## Limitations
- The study does not provide sufficient evidence to distinguish between the ineffectiveness of BALD/DA as fundamental limitations versus suboptimal implementation choices
- No comparison with alternative uncertainty estimation methods or diversity-promoting sampling strategies
- Limited investigation of different model architectures or training procedures that might yield different results
- Lack of quantitative metrics for analyzing dataset redundancy and its systematic mitigation

## Confidence
- Claims about the ineffectiveness of BALD and DA are Low confidence findings due to uncertainty about implementation adequacy
- Claims about dataset redundancy and sequential data processing challenges are Medium confidence due to lack of quantitative analysis
- Generalizability of findings to other semantic segmentation tasks is Medium confidence due to limited model and method comparisons

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies comparing different data augmentation strategies specifically designed for LiDAR data (e.g., rotation, scaling, noise injection) to determine if domain-appropriate augmentations can improve AL performance.

2. Implement and evaluate diversity-promoting sampling methods (e.g., CoreSet, uncertainty-diversity trade-offs) in combination with BALD to assess whether diversity-aware selection improves label efficiency on the full Semantic-KITTI dataset.

3. Analyze dataset redundancy quantitatively using clustering or coverage metrics, and test whether pre-processing to reduce redundancy improves the effectiveness of AL methods.
