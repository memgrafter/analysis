# Accelerated Training via Incrementally Growing Neural Networks using Variance Transfer and Learning Rate Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12700
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12700
- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method for incrementally growing neural networks by adding new neurons while preserving the functionality of the original network. The approach addresses the challenge of imbalanced training effort across subnetworks added at different growth phases through a learning rate adaptation mechanism. The authors introduce a variance transfer parameterization scheme that dynamically stabilizes weight, activation, and gradient scaling as the architecture evolves. Experimental results demonstrate that their method achieves comparable or better accuracy than training large fixed-size models while saving a substantial portion of the original computation budget.

## Method Summary
The method involves three key components: (1) variance transfer for initialization that rescales old weights to match new network width while breaking symmetry through noise injection, (2) learning rate adaptation that rebalances gradient contributions of subnetworks added at different growth phases, and (3) a flexible growth scheduler that adjusts model size and training epochs at each growth phase based on a coupling between model size and training epoch allocation. The framework allows networks to grow incrementally while preserving functionality and achieving faster training through more efficient resource utilization.

## Key Results
- Achieves comparable or better accuracy than training large fixed-size models
- Saves a substantial portion of the original computation budget for training
- Demonstrates real wall-clock training speedups in experiments
