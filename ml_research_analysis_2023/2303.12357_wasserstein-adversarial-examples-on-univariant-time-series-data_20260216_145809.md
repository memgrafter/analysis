# Wasserstein Adversarial Examples on Univariant Time Series Data

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.12357
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12357
- **Reference count**: 33
- **Primary result**: Proposes the first Wasserstein PGD attack for univariant time series with high success rates (up to 100%) and more imperceptible perturbations than L∞ PGD

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first adversarial attack framework specifically designed for univariant time series data using Wasserstein distance as the metric. The authors argue that traditional L_p norms fail to capture perceptual similarity in temporal sequences, making Wasserstein distance more appropriate for measuring indistinguishability. They develop an efficient two-step projection method that first constrains perturbations to an L∞ ball and then projects to the Wasserstein ball, leveraging the closed-form solution available for 1D distributions. Experiments demonstrate superior attack success rates and more imperceptible perturbations compared to traditional L∞ PGD attacks across multiple ECG datasets and neural network architectures.

## Method Summary
The proposed Wasserstein Projected Gradient Descent (WPGD) attack generates adversarial examples by optimizing perturbations within a Wasserstein ball rather than traditional L_p balls. The key innovation is a two-step projection process: first projecting to an L∞ norm ball for computational tractability, then efficiently projecting to the Wasserstein ball using the closed-form solution for 1D distributions. This approach maintains attack effectiveness while producing perturbations that are less perceptible to humans. The paper also evaluates Wasserstein smoothing as a defense mechanism, though it finds limited effectiveness, suggesting the need for stronger certified robustness methods tailored to time series data.

## Key Results
- WPGD achieves up to 100% attack success rates on ECG datasets
- Wasserstein-bounded perturbations are more imperceptible than L∞-bounded perturbations
- Two-step projection method guides the search more effectively than direct Wasserstein projection
- Wasserstein smoothing defense provides limited protection against Wasserstein adversarial examples

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input data → Wasserstein distance calculator → Gradient descent optimizer → Projection module → Adversarial example output
- **Critical path**: Generate perturbation → Apply gradient descent step → Project to L∞ ball (for two-step) → Project to Wasserstein ball → Check attack success → Iterate until success or max iterations
- **Design tradeoffs**: Two-step projection trades increased computational cost for better attack success rates and more imperceptible perturbations
- **Failure signatures**: Low attack success rates despite high computational cost, perceptible perturbations despite satisfying Wasserstein bounds, or defenses completely neutralizing attack

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: Can certified robustness methods be developed specifically for Wasserstein adversarial examples on time series data?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper evaluates Wasserstein smoothing but finds limited effectiveness, stating "there is space for developing stronger certified robustness method"
  - Why unresolved: Only tested Wasserstein smoothing adapted from image data, which performed poorly against proposed attack
  - What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing new certified robustness methods specifically designed for time series Wasserstein space

- **Open Question 2**: How can computational efficiency of Wasserstein PGD be improved while maintaining attack effectiveness?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper notes Wasserstein PGD has more time cost than L∞ PGD, especially with large Wasserstein bounds
  - Why unresolved: Two-step projection helps but WPGD is still slower than traditional PGD
  - What evidence would resolve it: Developing more efficient algorithms for Wasserstein projection that reduce computation time while maintaining or improving attack success rates

- **Open Question 3**: How do Wasserstein adversarial examples generalize to multivariate time series data?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper states "it would be interesting to study Wasserstein adversarial examples on multivariant time series under Gaussian distributions"
  - Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates attack on univariant time series data
  - What evidence would resolve it: Testing Wasserstein PGD attack on multivariate time series datasets and evaluating effectiveness and computational challenges

## Limitations
- Limited to univariant time series data where closed-form Wasserstein solution exists
- Defense evaluation only tested single baseline method (Wasserstein smoothing)
- No comparison to more advanced certified robustness techniques
