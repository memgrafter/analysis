# Towards Interpretability in Audio and Visual Affective Machine Learning: A Review

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08933
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08933
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper conducts a systematic literature review to examine the use of interpretability methods in affective machine learning, focusing on studies using audio, visual, or audiovisual data. The review identified 29 research articles published between 2018-2022 that employed interpretability methods. Most studies used feature attribution techniques like LIME or SHAP to generate local explanations via saliency maps, with a smaller number using interpretable-by-design models with attention mechanisms or transparent architectures. However, the authors found that interpretability was often limited to visualizing feature importance without deeper evaluation. They recommend more rigorous human-centered evaluation of interpretability methods in different affective computing contexts, considering factors like input representation, modality interactions, and intended end-users of explanations.

## Executive Summary
This systematic literature review examines how interpretability methods have been applied in affective machine learning research using audio, visual, and audiovisual data. The authors analyzed 29 papers published between 2018-2022 to understand the current state of interpretability in this domain. They found that while interpretability is increasingly being adopted, most approaches remain limited to basic feature attribution techniques and qualitative evaluations. The review identifies significant gaps in multimodal interpretability, human-centered evaluation, and the diversity of methods employed, offering concrete recommendations for advancing the field.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a systematic literature review using structured searches across IEEE, PubMed, and Web of Science databases with specific query terms related to interpretability in affective computing. They applied inclusion criteria to select papers published between 2018-2022 that used machine learning for affect or emotion recognition with audio/visual data and explicitly listed interpretability as a contribution. The selected papers were then analyzed to extract information about tasks, modalities, interpretability methods employed, and evaluation approaches.

## Key Results
- Most interpretability work in affective ML relies on feature attribution methods like LIME or SHAP to create saliency maps
- Only four of the 29 reviewed papers performed user-based evaluations of their interpretability methods
- Multimodal interpretability remains underdeveloped, with most approaches focusing on identifying dominant modalities rather than explaining modality interactions
- Local explanations were more common than global explanations in the reviewed literature

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can interpretability methods be systematically evaluated to ensure their explanations are both understandable and meaningful for specific affective computing applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the current over-reliance on qualitative reviews and calls for more rigorous human-centered evaluation of interpretability methods.
- Why unresolved: Different affective computing contexts have varying needs for interpretability, and existing methods are often evaluated only anecdotally or qualitatively without standardized metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of standardized evaluation frameworks for interpretability methods in affective computing, including user studies with diverse participant groups and application scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can multimodal affective computing systems better account for modality interactions when generating explanations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that current methods focus on identifying dominant modalities while largely neglecting modality interactions, which are crucial for understanding model decisions in multimodal contexts.
- Why unresolved: Most existing interpretability methods are developed primarily for unimodal systems, and multimodal explanation techniques remain underdeveloped.
- What evidence would resolve it: Novel interpretability methods that explicitly model and explain modality interactions in multimodal affective computing systems, validated through systematic evaluation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What alternative interpretability methods beyond feature attribution could be more effective for affective computing applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests exploring methods like concept activation vectors (CAVs) that provide explanations in terms of high-level task-specific concepts rather than low-level features.
- Why unresolved: The field has been dominated by feature attribution methods, and there's limited research on alternative approaches that might be more intuitive or meaningful for users.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of different interpretability methods in various affective computing contexts, demonstrating the effectiveness of alternative approaches for specific applications.

## Limitations

- The small sample size of 29 papers may not fully represent the current state of interpretability research in affective computing
- The review's focus on papers published between 2018-2022 may miss important foundational work or recent developments beyond this timeframe
- The review relies on authors' self-reporting of interpretability methods, which may lead to incomplete or inconsistent documentation across papers
