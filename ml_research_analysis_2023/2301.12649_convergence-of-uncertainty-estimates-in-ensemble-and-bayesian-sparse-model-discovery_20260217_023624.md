# Convergence of uncertainty estimates in Ensemble and Bayesian sparse model discovery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12649
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12649
- Authors: [Not specified in input]
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Bagging inclusion probability sequential thresholding least-squares (BIP-STLS) achieves exponential convergence rates for sparse model discovery with computationally efficient uncertainty quantification

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes theoretical foundations for bootstrapping-based sparse model discovery, particularly focusing on the bagging inclusion probability sequential thresholding least-squares (STLS) estimator. The key results include proving that this method achieves correct sparse model identification with exponential convergence rates for both false discovery probability and true discovery probability under milder regularity conditions than existing methods. The authors also demonstrate that this approach provides computationally efficient uncertainty quantification, with asymptotic equivalence to Bayesian methods with spike-and-slab priors but with significantly lower computational complexity (O(n²) vs O(n³)). Experimental results on synthetic sparse linear regression and physical model discovery (Lotka-Volterra equations) validate the theoretical findings, showing accelerated variable selection and valid uncertainty quantification that converges to the true values as sample sizes increase. The method is particularly robust to hyperparameter selection under varying noise and sparsity levels.

## Method Summary
The method uses bootstrap resampling to estimate inclusion probabilities for variable selection in sparse linear regression and sparse nonlinear dynamics discovery (SINDy). The algorithm generates multiple bootstrap samples, performs sequential thresholding least-squares on each sample to identify active variables, and counts the frequency of inclusion for each variable across all bootstrap replicates. Variables with inclusion probabilities exceeding a threshold are selected as active features. The method includes an optional re-weighting scheme based on out-of-bag prediction error to improve performance. The theoretical analysis establishes exponential convergence rates for false discovery probability (FDP) and true discovery probability (TDP) under Gaussian noise assumptions and regularity conditions on the design matrix.

## Key Results
- BIP-STLS achieves exponential convergence rates for FDP ≤ (p-q)exp((1/3-npc/3)) and TDP ≥ 1-qexp(-n(1-2pc)²/6)
- Asymptotic equivalence to spike-and-slab Bayesian methods with O(n²) complexity versus O(n³) for MCMC
- Empirical validation shows 2-10× speedup over Bayesian SINDy with comparable or better model selection accuracy
- Robust performance across varying noise levels (σ = 0.25-1.0) and sparsity levels (q/p = 0.3-0.5)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bagging inclusion probability STLS achieves exponential convergence in both false discovery probability (FDP) and true discovery probability (TDP)
- Mechanism: The bootstrap replicates create an empirical distribution of least-squares estimates where non-active variables have Gaussian tails centered at zero with variance decreasing as 1/n. The inclusion probability threshold acts as a soft selection criterion that concentrates probability mass on the correct model components.
- Core assumption: The noise is Gaussian with known variance and the design matrix satisfies regularity conditions (bounded eigenvalues, sample size scales appropriately with model dimension)
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "this bootstrapping-based ensembling technique can perform a provably correct variable selection procedure with an exponential convergence rate of the error rate"
  - [section 4.2]: Theorem 4.1 shows FDP ≤ (p-q)exp((1/3-npc/3)) and TDP ≥ 1-qexp(-n(1-2pc)²/6)
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence from related work on bootstrap stability selection [59] but no direct citation of exponential rates
- Break condition: If the Gaussian noise assumption fails or eigenvalues become ill-conditioned (ρ₁ → 0), the tail bounds no longer hold and convergence degrades to polynomial rates

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Bagging inclusion probability STLS provides computationally efficient uncertainty quantification asymptotically equivalent to Bayesian spike-and-slab methods
- Mechanism: The empirical distribution of bootstrap estimates converges in Wasserstein-2 distance to the posterior distribution of spike-and-slab MCMC, but with O(np²) complexity per replicate versus O(n²p) per MCMC iteration
- Core assumption: The spike-and-slab posterior mean converges to OLS as λₙ/n → 0, and bootstrap resampling approximates the posterior sampling
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "asymptotically equivalent to the Bayesian method with lower computational cost" and "computationally much more efficient" with specific runtime comparison
  - [section 5.2]: Theorem 5.2 establishes W₂²(L(β̂*),L(β̃SSL)) → 0 and Remark 1 provides complexity comparison
  - [corpus]: Weak connection to [46] on spike-and-slab convergence but no explicit Wasserstein analysis in cited works
- Break condition: If the spike-and-slab posterior is multimodal or the bootstrap distribution is too concentrated, the Wasserstein equivalence breaks down

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Re-weighting bootstrap replicates by out-of-bag error improves variable selection performance
- Mechanism: Bootstrap samples with lower prediction error on held-out data receive higher weights, reducing variance from poor bootstrap fits while maintaining the exponential convergence properties
- Core assumption: The out-of-bag error is a reliable proxy for the quality of the subset selection in each bootstrap replicate
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.1]: Describes re-weighting scheme using out-of-bag MSE and claims "empirically achieves the best results"
  - [Algorithm 2]: Shows explicit implementation with weight calculation exp(-MSEs)
  - [corpus]: No direct citation for this specific weighting scheme; appears to be novel contribution
- Break condition: If all bootstrap replicates have similar errors (flat MSE landscape), re-weighting provides no benefit and adds computational overhead

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing → Bootstrap resampling → Least-squares estimation → Inclusion probability calculation → Subset selection → Uncertainty quantification
- Critical path: Bootstrap resampling → Least-squares → Thresholding → Inclusion counting → Final selection
  - Bottleneck: Matrix inversion in least-squares (O(p³) per replicate, but can be optimized with Woodbury identity)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Inclusion probability threshold vs. false discovery control: Lower thresholds increase TDP but may increase FDP
  - Bootstrap sample size vs. computational cost: More samples improve stability but increase runtime quadratically
  - Out-of-bag weighting vs. simplicity: Improves performance but adds hyperparameter tuning burden
- Failure signatures:
  - All inclusion probabilities clustered near 0.5: Indicates model identifiability issues or poor regularization
  - Runtime increases superlinearly with p: Suggests inefficient matrix operations; check Woodbury optimization
  - Uncertainty estimates too narrow/wide: Verify bootstrap sample size adequacy and residual distribution assumptions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Synthetic sparse regression with known support: Verify exponential convergence rates empirically across sample sizes
  2. High-noise regime (σ >> signal): Test robustness of inclusion probability gaps and threshold selection
  3. Comparison with Bayesian spike-and-slab on same synthetic data: Measure Wasserstein distance convergence empirically

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis relies heavily on Gaussian noise assumptions that may not hold in physical systems with non-Gaussian measurement errors
- Exponential convergence rates are asymptotic and their practical relevance for finite sample sizes remains to be fully validated
- Computational advantage assumes favorable scaling between p and n, which may break down for extremely high-dimensional problems

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** The theoretical framework for BIP-STLS convergence under idealized conditions, and the empirical demonstration of improved FDP/TDP ratios over LASSO methods
- **Medium Confidence:** The Wasserstein distance equivalence between bootstrap and spike-and-slab posteriors, due to limited empirical validation and the strong regularity conditions required
- **Low Confidence:** The practical robustness of the exponential convergence rates in non-ideal settings (correlated features, non-Gaussian noise, model misspecification)
