# Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.15004
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** Recent claims of emergent abilities in large language models are likely artifacts of metric choice rather than true scale-dependent model behavior.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the notion of emergent abilities in large language models—sudden, unpredictable performance gains at specific scales. Through mathematical modeling, experiments with InstructGPT/GPT-3 on arithmetic tasks, and meta-analysis of BIG-Bench results, the authors demonstrate that nonlinear or discontinuous metrics (e.g., accuracy, multiple choice grade) create the illusion of emergence by sharply deforming per-token error rates. In contrast, linear or continuous metrics (e.g., token edit distance, Brier score) reveal smooth, predictable scaling. The study further shows that similar metric-induced "emergence" can be created in vision models of varied architectures, suggesting that emergent abilities vanish under different metrics or better statistics.

## Method Summary
The researchers investigated emergent abilities by testing InstructGPT/GPT-3 models (350M to 175B parameters) on arithmetic tasks (2-digit multiplication, 4-digit addition) using both accuracy and linear metrics (token edit distance). They conducted a meta-analysis of BIG-Bench tasks to identify which metrics showed emergent abilities and tested whether switching to continuous metrics removed the apparent emergence. Additionally, they trained vision models (LeNet on MNIST, autoencoders on CIFAR100, Transformers on Omniglot) and induced emergent abilities by redefining metrics, demonstrating the phenomenon across domains.

## Key Results
- Nonlinear metrics (accuracy, multiple choice grade) create the illusion of emergence by geometrically transforming smooth per-token error rates
- Linear metrics (token edit distance, Brier score) reveal smooth, predictable scaling behavior
- Increasing test dataset size eliminates apparent emergence under nonlinear metrics
- Similar metric-induced emergence can be created in vision models across diverse architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Nonlinear or discontinuous metrics create the illusion of emergence by sharply deforming per-token error rates.
- **Mechanism:** When accuracy or multiple-choice grade is used, the probability of scoring correctly drops geometrically with sequence length. This causes smooth underlying improvements to appear as sharp jumps.
- **Core assumption:** Per-token error probability decreases smoothly with model scale.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "nonlinear or discontinuous metrics (e.g., accuracy, multiple choice grade) create the illusion of emergence by sharply deforming per-token error rates"
  - [section] "if the researcher scores models’ outputs using a nonlinear metric such as Accuracy...the researcher's measurement choice nonlinearly scales performance, causing performance to change sharply"
  - [corpus] "Found 25 related papers... Average neighbor FMR=0.502" (weak support, corpus focuses on retrieval rather than emergence mechanisms)
- **Break condition:** The illusion disappears when switching to linear or continuous metrics such as token edit distance or Brier score.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Limited test data resolution causes smaller models to appear incapable even when they have above-chance performance.
- **Mechanism:** With insufficient samples, the estimated accuracy of smaller models can round to zero due to resolution limits of 1/(test dataset size), masking gradual improvements.
- **Core assumption:** The true performance of smaller models is above chance but not easily measurable with limited data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "having insufficient resolution to estimate model performance in the smaller parameter regime, with resolution set by 1/test dataset size"
  - [section] "insufficiently sampling the larger parameter regime...having insufficient resolution to estimate model performance in the smaller parameter regime"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this specific mechanism.
- **Break condition:** Increasing the test dataset size reveals smooth, continuous improvements even under nonlinear metrics.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Sparse sampling of model scales amplifies apparent discontinuities by missing intermediate performance values.
- **Mechanism:** If only a few model sizes are evaluated, particularly skipping intermediate scales, the resulting performance curve can look discontinuous even if underlying behavior is smooth.
- **Core assumption:** Model performance changes smoothly with scale but is only evaluated at widely spaced parameter values.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "partially by evaluating too few large-scale models"
  - [section] "insufficiently sampling the larger parameter regime...causes smaller models to appear wholly unable to perform the task"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus evidence does not directly address sampling density effects.
- **Break condition:** Evaluating models at more densely spaced parameter values reveals smooth transitions.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Resolution limits in statistical estimation**
  - Why needed here: Understanding how limited test data affects performance measurement accuracy is crucial for interpreting why smaller models appear to have zero accuracy
  - Quick check question: If you have 100 test examples and a model has 1% error rate, what is the resolution-limited estimate of its accuracy?
- **Concept: Nonlinear transformations of probabilities**
  - Why needed here: Accuracy and multiple-choice metrics apply geometric decay to underlying probabilities, converting smooth improvements into sharp jumps
  - Quick check question: If a model's per-token accuracy improves from 0.9 to 0.99, how does this affect sequence accuracy for length-5 sequences?
- **Concept: Proper scoring rules vs discontinuous metrics**
  - Why needed here: Distinguishing between metrics like Brier score (continuous) and accuracy (discontinuous) explains why some metrics reveal smooth scaling while others create emergence illusions
  - Quick check question: What is the key mathematical property that makes Brier score a "proper" scoring rule while accuracy is not?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Model family with varying parameter counts -> Task definition -> Metric for scoring outputs -> Test dataset for evaluation -> Parameter-scaling analysis
- **Critical path:** Model generation → Output scoring via chosen metric → Performance aggregation across test examples → Parameter-scaling analysis. The metric choice critically determines whether emergence appears.
- **Design tradeoffs:** Linear metrics (token edit distance, Brier score) reveal true scaling but may be less interpretable; nonlinear metrics (accuracy, multiple-choice grade) align better with human judgment but can create false emergence signals.
- **Failure signatures:** Apparent emergence under accuracy/multiple-choice metrics but smooth scaling under linear metrics; emergence disappearing when test dataset size increases; emergence appearing only at sparse parameter sampling points.
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Reproduce a claimed emergent ability using accuracy metric on a simple task (e.g., 2-digit addition) with the smallest and largest available models
  2. Repeat the same task and models using token edit distance metric to verify smooth scaling appears
  3. Increase test dataset size by 10x and re-measure performance to check if emergence persists under the nonlinear metric

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the sharpness of emergent abilities correlate with specific nonlinear or discontinuous metrics, or are there other factors contributing to the observed abrupt transitions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that emergent abilities vanish under linear or continuous metrics, suggesting they are artifacts of nonlinear or discontinuous metrics like accuracy and multiple choice grade.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows a strong correlation between nonlinear metrics and emergent abilities, it does not conclusively rule out other factors such as task complexity or model architecture influencing the sharpness of transitions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with a broader range of tasks and metrics, while controlling for model architecture and task complexity, could help determine if nonlinear metrics are the sole cause of emergent abilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can emergent abilities be induced in vision models using the same metrics that create them in language models, or are there domain-specific differences in how metrics affect model behavior?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that similar metric choices can induce emergent abilities in vision tasks across diverse network architectures, but it does not explore whether the same metrics work universally across domains.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focus on language and vision tasks separately, leaving open the question of whether the same metrics would induce emergent abilities in other domains like reinforcement learning or multimodal models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the same nonlinear/discontinuous metrics on emergent abilities in other domains, such as reinforcement learning or multimodal models, would clarify if the phenomenon is domain-specific.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the resolution of test data affect the detection of emergent abilities, and is there a threshold resolution below which emergent abilities cannot be reliably identified?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights that insufficient test data can lead to the false detection of emergent abilities, as smaller models may appear to have zero performance due to limited resolution.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that increasing test data improves resolution and reveals smooth scaling, it does not quantify the minimum resolution required to avoid false positives in emergent ability detection.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically varying the test data size and analyzing its effect on the detection of emergent abilities across different tasks and metrics would help establish a resolution threshold.

## Limitations

- **Metric Definition Ambiguity:** The paper doesn't fully address when certain tasks naturally align with specific metrics, and assumes linear metrics are universally appropriate even for tasks requiring discontinuous evaluation.
- **Model Family Generalization:** Findings may not extend to all large language model families or training paradigms beyond InstructGPT/GPT-3 and the tested vision architectures.
- **Test Data Resolution Threshold:** The paper suggests increasing test dataset size resolves artifacts but doesn't specify the minimum sample size needed for reliable measurement across different model scales and task difficulties.

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The mathematical demonstration that nonlinear metrics geometrically transform smooth per-token error rates into sharp performance jumps is robust and well-supported by both theoretical analysis and empirical validation.

**Medium Confidence:** The claim that all emergent abilities are metric artifacts is strong but relies on testing a finite set of tasks and models, warranting caution in generalizing to all possible emergent behaviors.

**Medium Confidence:** The assertion that insufficient parameter sampling creates apparent emergence is plausible but under-specified regarding the required sampling density for different scaling regimes.

## Next Checks

1. **Task-Specific Metric Validation:** Test the same model scaling on tasks where discontinuous evaluation is genuinely appropriate (e.g., exact sequence generation) to determine if emergence persists even under linear metrics.

2. **Architecture and Training Diversity:** Replicate the findings across diverse model families (sparse models, mixture-of-experts, different pretraining objectives) to verify the metric dependence of emergent abilities generalizes beyond the tested architectures.

3. **Resolution Threshold Quantification:** Systematically vary test dataset sizes across multiple tasks and model scales to identify the minimum sample size required to reliably distinguish above-chance performance from zero accuracy.
