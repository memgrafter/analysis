# Exploring Contrast Consistency of Open-Domain Question Answering Systems on Minimally Edited Questions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14441
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14441
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 13

## Executive Summary
This work introduces minimally edited questions (MEQs) as a challenging evaluation framework for open-domain question answering systems. MEQs are questions that are semantically similar to original questions but yield different answers. The authors construct two contrast sets - MEQ-GPT and MEQ-AmbigQA - through a combination of GPT-4 generation and human annotation. Their experiments reveal that dense passage retrievers (DPR) show significant performance degradation on MEQs compared to standard test sets, indicating a fundamental limitation in contrast consistency. To address this, the authors propose a query-side contrastive loss that trains models to distinguish between paraphrase questions and MEQs, improving performance on MEQs without sacrificing accuracy on standard questions.

## Method Summary
The authors create minimally edited questions by first generating candidate questions using GPT-4 with instructions to create questions semantically similar to source questions but with different answers. Human annotators then validate these candidates, ensuring they meet strict criteria for semantic similarity while maintaining different answers. This process produces two contrast sets: MEQ-GPT derived from SQuAD questions and MEQ-AmbigQA from the AmbigQA dataset. The evaluation framework tests whether QA models can maintain consistent performance when faced with questions that are paraphrases or near-paraphrases but require different answers. The proposed solution involves a contrastive loss that penalizes the model for retrieving the same passages for questions that should yield different answers, effectively training the model to recognize subtle distinctions between semantically similar questions.

## Key Results
- DPR performance drops significantly on MEQs: from 72.7% to 26.3% on SQuAD and from 44.8% to 15.3% on AmbigQA
- The proposed contrastive loss improves DPR performance on MEQ-AmbigQA by 4.7% for the 3xReranker system
- Models struggle to distinguish between paraphrase questions and MEQs despite high semantic similarity
- Query-side contrastive training successfully improves contrast consistency without harming standard test performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed contrastive loss works by explicitly training the model to distinguish between semantically similar questions that should yield different answers. Standard QA training treats all semantically similar questions as equivalent, but MEQs expose the limitation of this approach by requiring models to recognize subtle contextual differences that determine answer selection. The loss function penalizes the model when it retrieves the same passages for MEQs that should have different answers, forcing the model to learn finer-grained distinctions in question semantics beyond simple paraphrase detection.

## Limitations
- Heavy reliance on GPT-4 for MEQ generation with limited independent human creation raises questions about dataset representativeness
- Performance improvements from contrastive loss are modest (4.7%) relative to the significant baseline gap
- Study focuses exclusively on DPR-based systems without exploring other retrieval architectures
- Small human evaluation sample size (40 participants, 200 samples) limits generalizability of qualitative findings

## Confidence
- High confidence: MEQ construction methodology and basic performance degradation findings
- Medium confidence: Effectiveness of the proposed contrastive loss approach
- Medium confidence: Characterization of MEQs as exposing fundamental limitations in current retrieval systems
