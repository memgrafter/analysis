# The Split Matters: Flat Minima Methods for Improving the Performance of GNNs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09121
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09121
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Flat minima methods can improve GNN performance by over 2 points with randomized splits; weight averaging techniques are recommended for stable improvements.

## Executive Summary
This work investigates the application of flat minima methods to graph neural networks (GNNs), which operate on non-i.i.d. data due to edge-connected vertices. The authors evaluate a variety of flat minima methods including SAM, ASAM, PGN, GSAM, SWA, EWA, Anti-PGD, and SAF on GCN, GAT, and Graph-MLP architectures across multiple datasets and both transductive and inductive settings. The key finding is that randomized train-test splits are essential for fair evaluation, with flat minima methods showing significant performance improvements (>2 points) in these settings. Weight averaging techniques, particularly EWA, are recommended for practitioners due to their robustness to hyperparameters and preservation of the original model.

## Method Summary
The paper applies flat minima methods to GNNs by first optimizing base models without flat minima techniques, then tuning hyperparameters for each method and evaluating performance with early stopping. The methods include adversarial training (SAM, ASAM), gradient-based sharpness minimization (PGN, GSAM), and weight averaging (SWA, EWA). Experiments are conducted on benchmark datasets with both fixed (Planetoid) and randomized splits, measuring classification accuracy improvements over base models.

## Key Results
- Flat minima methods improve GNN performance by over 2 points when using randomized train-test splits
- No single method consistently outperforms others, but weight averaging techniques (especially EWA) are broadly beneficial
- Combining multiple flat minima methods can further enhance performance
- Randomized splits are crucial for fair evaluation, as fixed Planetoid splits can introduce bias

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Flat minima methods improve GNN performance when using randomized train-test splits.
- Mechanism: Flat minima methods reduce the sharpness of the loss landscape around model parameters, leading to better generalization on unseen data. Randomized splits prevent overfitting to specific partitions, making generalization benefits more consistent.
- Core assumption: Flat minima in the loss landscape correspond to better generalization performance.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states flat minima methods improve performance by over 2 points with randomized splits; results show at least one method improves performance for each model-dataset combination.
- Break condition: If flat minima don't correlate with generalization, or if loss landscape is inherently unstable due to dataset characteristics.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Weight averaging techniques (SWA, EWA) are less sensitive to hyperparameters and preserve the original model.
- Mechanism: Weight averaging averages model weights over training epochs, creating a model in the center of a flat region. This reduces reliance on finding optimal weights and is less affected by hyperparameter choices. EWA adapts to early stopping by averaging over epochs.
- Core assumption: Averaging weights from a flat region improves generalization.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract recommends EWA for practitioners; EWA shows highest improvement (2.09 points) on CiteSeer with GCN and ra-pl split.
- Break condition: If model doesn't traverse flat region during training, or if averaging introduces instability.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining multiple flat minima methods can further improve performance.
- Mechanism: Different flat minima methods optimize different aspects of the loss landscape (e.g., SAM focuses on adversarial points, PGN penalizes gradient norm, SWA averages weights). Combining them addresses multiple failure modes.
- Core assumption: Different flat minima methods complement each other's strengths and weaknesses.
- Evidence anchors: Combining EWA and GASAM increases performance by 2.89 points on CiteSeer ra-pl split; paper notes no single method consistently works best.
- Break condition: If combined methods have conflicting objectives or computational cost outweighs benefits.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: Why do randomized splits consistently lead to larger performance gains from flat minima methods compared to fixed Planetoid splits?
  - Basis in paper: Authors show flat minima methods improve performance more on random splits than on fixed Planetoid split, referencing Shchur et al. on bias of fixed splits
  - Why unresolved: Paper demonstrates the effect but doesn't explain why random splits make flat minima methods more effective
  - What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying split randomness while measuring flat minima method performance, or theoretical analysis of how split characteristics interact with flat minima optimization dynamics

- Question: What is the optimal combination strategy for flat minima methods when applied to GNNs?
  - Basis in paper: Authors experiment with combining up to three methods (e.g., EWA+GSAM+ASAM) and find performance improvements, but note no single method consistently works best
  - Why unresolved: While combinations show promise, paper only explores limited combinations without systematic analysis of which combinations work best for which architectures/datasets
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic combinatorial testing across all method pairs/triples with statistical analysis of which combinations consistently outperform others across different GNN architectures and datasets

- Question: Why does Graph-MLP perform significantly worse on PPI compared to other GNN architectures?
  - Basis in paper: Graph-MLP fails on PPI with 45-point F1 score drop, attributed to PPI's inductive nature preventing use of validation/test edges during training
  - Why unresolved: Paper identifies symptom and offers hypothesis but doesn't test whether alternative architectures or modifications could address this limitation
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing whether message-passing variants of Graph-MLP or hybrid approaches that incorporate edge information during training can recover performance on inductive tasks like PPI

## Limitations

- Focus exclusively on node classification tasks, leaving open questions about flat minima methods' effectiveness on link prediction or graph classification
- Computational expense of certain methods (particularly SAM and GSAM) may limit practical applicability
- Does not investigate why fixed splits like Planetoid consistently fail to show improvements with flat minima methods

## Confidence

- High confidence: Weight averaging techniques (especially EWA) are broadly beneficial and less sensitive to hyperparameters
- Medium confidence: Flat minima methods can improve GNN performance by over 2 points with randomized splits
- Medium confidence: Combining multiple flat minima methods can further improve performance
