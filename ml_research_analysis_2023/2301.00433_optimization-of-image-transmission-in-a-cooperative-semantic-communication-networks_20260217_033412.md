# Optimization of Image Transmission in a Cooperative Semantic Communication Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00433
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00433
- Reference count: 39
- Proposes a semantic communication framework using scene graphs and a novel ISS metric with VD-ERL optimization for image transmission

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a semantic communication framework for image transmission in cooperative multi-server wireless networks. The framework extracts semantic information from images in the form of scene graphs and proposes a novel image-to-graph semantic similarity (ISS) metric to evaluate semantic similarity. To minimize transmission latency while satisfying ISS requirements, a value decomposition based entropy-maximized multi-agent reinforcement learning (VD-ERL) algorithm is proposed. The algorithm enables servers to cooperatively optimize resource allocation with improved action exploration and convergence speed. Simulation results show that the proposed VD-ERL algorithm reduces transmission delay by up to 16.1% and improves convergence speed by up to 100% compared to traditional multi-agent RL algorithms.

## Method Summary
The method involves semantic information extraction using scene graph generation models, ISS metric computation based on cosine similarity between image and semantic triple vectors, and VD-ERL for resource allocation optimization. The VD-ERL algorithm decomposes global Q-value functions into individual server Q-values while using entropy maximization to encourage exploration. The system iteratively selects semantic triples based on scores to minimize transmission time while meeting ISS constraints.

## Key Results
- VD-ERL algorithm reduces transmission delay by up to 16.1% compared to traditional multi-agent RL algorithms
- Convergence speed improves by up to 100% with VD-ERL approach
- The ISS metric effectively captures semantic similarity between original images and transmitted information

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: VD-ERL improves valuable action exploration and convergence speed by enabling cooperative training and distributed execution
- Mechanism: VD-ERL decomposes the global Q-value function into individual Q-value functions for each server, allowing them to learn from local observations while coordinating to achieve globally optimal performance. The entropy-maximization component encourages exploration of diverse actions by maximizing the policy entropy.
- Core assumption: The sum of individual Q-values equals the global Q-value, enabling effective coordination despite partial observability
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The proposed algorithm enables servers to cooperatively optimize resource allocation with improved action exploration and convergence speed."
  - [section III-A]: "The global Q value function is defined as Qtot (s, α), which is used to estimate the total expected reward of all distributed servers... the global Q value of all servers is equal to the sum of the individual Q value of each servers"
  - [section III-B]: "the proposed VD-ERL algorithm utilizes a value network to evaluate and promote the policy network and hence, the minor change of value function can not change the action choose directly"
- Break condition: If the assumption about Q-value decomposition does not hold, the coordination mechanism fails and servers may not achieve globally optimal performance

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The proposed image-to-graph semantic similarity (ISS) metric captures the correlation of meaning between the original image and its semantic information, enabling more efficient transmission
- Mechanism: ISS measures semantic similarity by computing the cosine similarity between vectorized representations of the original image and the received semantic information. It considers the included angle cosine between an image vector and its normalized semantic triple vectors.
- Core assumption: The vectorized representations preserve semantic meaning and the cosine similarity accurately reflects semantic similarity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "To evaluate the performance of studied semantic communication system, a multimodal metric is proposed to measure the correlation between the extracted semantic information and the original image."
  - [section II-C]: "The proposed ISS metric is defined as the included angle cosine between an image vector and its normalized semantic triple vectors, which is calculated by the projection of image vector on semantic information vector set."
  - [section IV]: "Figure 10 shows the correlation between the transmitted semantic information and RB allocation policies... we can see that the semantic score can describe the importance of each semantic triple with small error"
- Break condition: If the vectorization process fails to preserve semantic meaning or the cosine similarity does not correlate with human perception of semantic similarity, the metric becomes ineffective

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The semantic triple selection algorithm optimizes the trade-off between semantic reliability and transmission latency by iteratively selecting the most important semantic triples
- Mechanism: The algorithm sorts semantic triples by their scores (which represent the probability of extracting that triple from the original image) and iteratively selects triples with the highest scores until the minimum semantic similarity threshold is met.
- Core assumption: The semantic triple scores accurately reflect the importance of each triple to the overall semantic meaning of the image
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II-A]: "The score of each semantic triple ψn vk represents the probability of extracting triple ψn vk from the original image and will be used to the selection of the partial semantic information to be transmitted."
  - [section III-A]: "Based on the state sv and action αv, we can sort the semantic triples Ψvk according to the score vector βvk... we use an iterative algorithm to select several triples to satisfy constraint (8e) while minimizing the transmission time."
  - [section IV]: "Figure 9 shows the relationships between the semantic score distribution of semantic triples and the original image... we can see that the semantic triple with high semantic scores is more critical"
- Break condition: If the semantic triple scores do not accurately reflect importance (e.g., due to errors in the scene graph generation model), the algorithm may select unimportant triples or miss important ones

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image servers -> Scene graph extraction -> Semantic triple scoring -> VD-ERL resource allocation -> Transmission -> ISS evaluation -> Feedback
- Critical path: Image → Scene graph extraction → Semantic triple scoring → VD-ERL resource allocation → Transmission → ISS evaluation → Feedback
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Complexity vs. performance: More sophisticated semantic extraction and similarity metrics may improve performance but increase computational complexity
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: The entropy-maximization component balances between exploring new actions and exploiting known good actions
  - Centralization vs. decentralization: The VD-ERL approach enables distributed execution while achieving coordination, trading off some performance for scalability and robustness
- Failure signatures:
  - High semantic similarity but poor image reconstruction: Indicates the ISS metric may not capture all relevant semantic aspects
  - Low convergence speed: Suggests the exploration mechanism is not effective or the reward structure needs adjustment
  - High transmission latency despite optimization: May indicate constraints are too tight or the algorithm is getting stuck in local optima

## Limitations

- The proposed VD-ERL algorithm's performance is heavily dependent on the quality of semantic triple extraction and scoring, with no addressing of potential errors in scene graph generation
- The value decomposition assumption (that the sum of individual Q-values equals the global Q-value) may not hold in all network conditions, particularly with highly correlated channels or non-stationary user distributions
- The framework does not address the challenges of extracting semantic information from images with complex scenes or multiple objects

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of VD-ERL for distributed coordination and the basic structure of the ISS metric calculation
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of entropy-maximization for improving exploration and convergence speed, the relationship between semantic triple scores and semantic importance
- **Low Confidence**: The robustness of the framework to errors in scene graph generation and semantic triple scoring, the generalizability to network conditions beyond those tested
