# Understanding Finetuning for Factual Knowledge Extraction from Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.11293
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11293
- **Reference count**: 12
- **Primary result**: Finetuning can degrade factual knowledge extraction due to "Frequency Shock" and "Range Shift"; model mixing and mixture finetuning remedies achieve 12.4% Hit@1 improvement.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how finetuning language models for factual knowledge extraction can paradoxically degrade performance due to two side-effects: "Frequency Shock" where models over-predict rare entities and under-predict common ones, and "Range Shift" where models mostly predict entities seen during training. The authors demonstrate these effects across three controlled datasets with varying degrees of train/test mismatch. They propose two remedies—model mixing (combining finetuned and few-shot/zero-shot model scores) and mixture finetuning (combining finetuning with the LM's original pre-training task)—which together yield a 12.4% improvement in Hit@1 over vanilla finetuning.

## Method Summary
The authors investigate finetuning effects on factual knowledge extraction using T5 1.1 XXL. They construct three datasets with controlled train/test entity frequency mismatches (LowMismatch, MediumMismatch, HighMismatch) by manipulating entity overlap and frequency statistics. Models are evaluated using Hit@k metrics across manually crafted prompts from Petroni et al. (2019). The two proposed remedies are implemented as: (1) model mixing—averaging scores from finetuned models with few-shot/zero-shot baselines, and (2) mixture finetuning—combining factual knowledge extraction with the original masked language modeling task using fixed mixture ratios (1:15 optimal). Both approaches are tested across different mismatch levels to assess generalization.

## Key Results
- Frequency Shock causes over-prediction of rare entities and under-prediction of common entities seen during training
- Range Shift leads to models mostly predicting entities seen in training rather than unseen test entities
- Model mixing (FT+ZS or FT+FS) and mixture finetuning (1:15 ratio) combined achieve 12.4% improvement in Hit@1 over vanilla finetuning
- Both effects and remedies are observed consistently across Low, Medium, and High mismatch conditions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Finetuning for factual knowledge extraction disrupts the language model's original entity frequency distribution learned during pre-training. The model becomes biased toward entities encountered during finetuning, leading to Frequency Shock (shifting predictions toward rare training entities) and Range Shift (limiting predictions to training entities). The remedies work by: (1) model mixing restores the original LM's frequency distribution through score averaging with few-shot/zero-shot models, and (2) mixture finetuning maintains connection to pre-training objectives, preventing complete abandonment of the original entity distribution.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Over-prediction of rare entities relative to their true frequency
- Strong correlation between predicted entities and training set entities
- Degradation in performance on entities with frequency statistics differing from training data

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do Frequency Shock and Range Shift manifest in other types of knowledge extraction tasks beyond factual knowledge, such as commonsense reasoning or biomedical knowledge? The paper only provides empirical evidence for factual knowledge extraction and mentions potential extension to other domains without validation.

### Open Question 2
What is the optimal strategy for dynamically adjusting the mixture ratio in mixture finetuning during training, rather than using a fixed ratio? The paper uses static ratios (1:15 optimal) but doesn't explore adaptive adjustment strategies.

### Open Question 3
How does the scale of the language model (e.g., T5 Small vs. T5 XXL) interact with the severity of Frequency Shock and the effectiveness of the proposed remedies? While both small and large models show similar qualitative behavior, the magnitude of improvements differs and requires deeper analysis.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to T5 1.1 XXL model architecture, raising questions about generalizability to other LM architectures
- Multi-token prediction handling and probability aggregation details not fully specified, potentially affecting reproducibility
- Focus on entity-centric factual knowledge may not extend to other structured knowledge types or relation patterns

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Identification of Frequency Shock and Range Shift as systematic side-effects of finetuning
- **Medium Confidence**: Effectiveness of model mixing and mixture finetuning remedies
- **Medium Confidence**: Claims about inherent nature of side-effects to finetuning process

## Next Checks
1. Test Frequency Shock and Range Shift phenomena on other large language models (GPT-3, BERT variants) to verify generalizability
