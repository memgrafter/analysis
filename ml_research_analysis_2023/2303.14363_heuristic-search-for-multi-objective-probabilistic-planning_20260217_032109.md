# Heuristic Search for Multi-Objective Probabilistic Planning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14363
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14363
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: MOLRTDP with MOSSP abstraction heuristic solves 307.6 tasks on average

## Executive Summary
This paper extends heuristic search methods to multi-objective stochastic shortest path problems (MOSSPs), addressing the challenge of computing coverage sets of non-dominated policies. The authors introduce two new algorithms, MOLAO* and MOLRTDP, which extend LAO* and LRTDP to handle multiple objectives. They also develop a spectrum of domain-independent heuristics, including critical paths and abstraction heuristics, to guide the search. Experimental results show that MOLRTDP combined with the MOSSP abstraction heuristic (Hpdb2_mossp) performs best, demonstrating the effectiveness of heuristics that consider both multi-objective and stochastic aspects of MOSSPs.

## Method Summary
The paper presents two primary algorithms for solving MOSSPs: MOLAO* and MOLRTDP. MOLAO* extends LAO* to handle multiple objectives by maintaining a set of non-dominated policies, while MOLRTDP extends LRTDP with similar multi-objective considerations. Both algorithms use a weak improper policy assumption to guide the search toward optimal solutions. The authors also introduce a spectrum of domain-independent heuristics, including critical paths (Hplus_mossp) and abstraction heuristics (Hpdb2_mossp), which are designed to balance the trade-offs between multiple objectives and the stochastic nature of the problems.

## Key Results
- MOLRTDP combined with Hpdb2_mossp solves 307.6 tasks on average, outperforming other combinations
- Critical-path heuristics (Hplus_mossp) are incomplete but provide useful guidance in practice
- The proposed MOSSP abstraction heuristic (Hpdb2_mossp) effectively handles both multi-objective and stochastic aspects of the problems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of MOLRTDP and the proposed heuristics stems from their ability to balance exploration of the state space with exploitation of heuristic guidance. By extending LRTDP to handle multiple objectives, MOLRTDP can efficiently navigate the complex trade-offs between competing goals while accounting for uncertainty in the environment. The heuristics, particularly Hpdb2_mossp, incorporate both the multi-objective nature of the problem and the stochastic transitions, providing a more informed search direction than single-objective approaches.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Problem specification -> Heuristic computation -> MOLRTDP search -> Coverage set extraction

Critical path: MOSSP definition → Heuristic generation (Hpdb2_mossp) → MOLRTDP execution → Non-dominated policy set

Design tradeoffs: The choice between MOLAO* and MOLRTDP involves balancing completeness guarantees against computational efficiency. Hplus_mossp offers faster computation but incompleteness, while Hpdb2_mossp provides more comprehensive guidance at higher computational cost.

Failure signatures: Incomplete heuristics may lead to suboptimal coverage sets. Poor choice of upper bound vector ⃗b can cause premature convergence to dominated policies. Stochasticity underestimation in heuristics may result in overly optimistic policy evaluation.

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to a single benchmark domain (satellite mission planning), raising questions about generalizability
- The paper lacks theoretical convergence guarantees for the proposed algorithms
- Experimental results report average performance without variance measures, making it difficult to assess solution quality consistency

## Confidence
- High confidence: The algorithmic framework for extending LAO* and LRTDP to multi-objective settings is sound and well-implemented
- Medium confidence: The empirical superiority of MOLRTDP over MOLAO* is established but limited by single-domain evaluation
- Low confidence: The generalizability of the heuristic spectrum across diverse stochastic planning domains
