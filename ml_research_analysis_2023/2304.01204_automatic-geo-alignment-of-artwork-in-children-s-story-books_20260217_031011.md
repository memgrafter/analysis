# Automatic Geo-alignment of Artwork in Children's Story Books

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.01204
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01204
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: Prompt Augmentation using Keywords was the best method for culturally aligning children's book illustrations, outperforming CLIP Embedding Mask and Cross Attention Control approaches

## Executive Summary
This study explored using AI to automatically translate and generate culturally aligned illustrations in children's story books without human intervention. The research developed three distinct methods for modifying Stable Diffusion v1.5 prompts to incorporate cultural elements and tested them on books from UK, India, Japan, and Middle East cultures. Through both qualitative questionnaires with target culture participants and quantitative FID score comparisons, the research found that Prompt Augmentation using Keywords consistently generated images that conveyed the main ideas of the text while containing culturally relevant features. While AI-generated images were generally not as effective as traditional illustrations, they demonstrated potential as a faster and less expensive alternative for geo-aligning artwork in children's books.

## Method Summary
The study developed three methods to automatically translate and generate culturally aligned illustrations in children's story books using Stable Diffusion v1.5. First, Prompt Augmentation using Keywords involved prepending culture-specific keywords and style modifiers to text prompts. Second, CLIP Embedding Mask applied cultural masks to CLIP embeddings during generation. Third, Cross Attention Control with Editorial Prompts modified attention maps during the generation process. All methods used a fixed random seed (1234) for reproducibility and were tested on translated versions of UK books and original books from each target culture. Evaluation combined quantitative FID score analysis comparing generated images to originals with qualitative assessments from online questionnaires with native speakers from target cultures.

## Key Results
- Prompt Augmentation using Keywords achieved the best overall performance, with India scoring 60.9 and Japan scoring 61.3 on qualitative assessments
- CLIP Embedding Mask method showed the weakest performance, with India scoring only 28.3 and Japan scoring 25.0
- FID scores showed the generated images had moderate similarity to originals, with Method 1 (Prompt Augmentation) achieving 87.4 for India and 80.6 for Japan
- All methods struggled with maintaining character consistency across pages and handling complex language structures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of Prompt Augmentation stems from directly guiding the model's attention toward culturally relevant visual elements through keyword priming. By prepending culture-specific terms and artistic style modifiers to the original prompts, the method effectively steers Stable Diffusion's learned representations toward region-appropriate visual features while maintaining the semantic content of the original text. This approach works because it leverages the model's existing understanding of cultural visual concepts encoded in its training data, rather than attempting to modify the underlying embeddings or attention mechanisms.

## Foundational Learning
- **Frechet Inception Distance (FID)**: Metric measuring similarity between generated and original image distributions using Inception network features; needed to quantitatively assess cultural alignment quality and can be quickly checked by computing distance scores between reference and generated image sets
- **CLIP Embeddings**: Joint text-image representations that map both modalities to the same embedding space; required for the embedding mask method to apply cultural transformations and can be verified by testing cosine similarity between text and image embeddings
- **Cross Attention Maps**: Internal Stable Diffusion mechanism controlling how text tokens influence different spatial regions of the generated image; essential for the attention control method to modify cultural features and can be inspected by visualizing attention weight distributions
- **Seed-based reproducibility**: Using fixed random seeds ensures consistent image generation across runs; critical for fair method comparison and can be validated by generating multiple images with the same seed and parameters
- **Cultural keyword priming**: Strategic addition of culture-specific terms to prompts to influence visual style; fundamental to the best-performing method and can be tested by comparing generations with and without cultural prefixes
- **Stable Diffusion v1.5 architecture**: Diffusion model with U-Net backbone and text encoder for conditional image generation; provides the foundation for all three methods and can be confirmed by checking model version and architecture components

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Text Extraction -> Prompt Processing -> Stable Diffusion v1.5 -> Image Generation -> FID Score Calculation -> Questionnaire Evaluation

**Critical Path**: The most important sequence is Text Processing -> Prompt Augmentation with Keywords -> Stable Diffusion Generation -> Evaluation, as this represents the highest-performing method and the core workflow for culturally aligned image generation.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Fixed seed ensures reproducibility but may limit exploration of diverse outputs; keyword-based methods are simple but may not capture nuanced cultural elements; attention control offers fine-grained modification but requires complex implementation; CLIP mask approach theoretically sound but practically challenging due to embedding space complexity.

**Failure Signatures**: Distorted facial features (especially eyes), overly realistic instead of children's book style, incorrect number of facial features, inability to handle negative sentences, and failure to maintain character consistency across pages.

**Three First Experiments**:
1. Test Prompt Augmentation with basic culture keywords on simple descriptive sentences to verify cultural style transfer
2. Compare FID scores between generated and original images using different cultural keyword combinations
3. Conduct pilot questionnaire with 5-10 participants from target culture to validate qualitative assessment methodology

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well does Stable Diffusion handle maintaining character consistency across pages when translating illustrations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Since language is complex, words can have larger or smaller effects depending on the context in which they are used... Possessive pronouns such as “his” or “her” are usually superfluous when included in a sentence such as ‘The boy took his football to the park’ since the ideal of a male character is already encoded in the phrase 'the boy'..."
- Why unresolved: The paper notes that "each page of the books was input into the Stable Diffusion model separately, all three methods struggled with consistently drawing recurring characters between pages." However, the extent and impact of this issue is not fully explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing Stable Diffusion on a book with recurring characters across many pages, evaluating character consistency in the generated images, and comparing to the original illustrations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal way to balance between cultural localization and preserving the original meaning/style of the text?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "The success of this project is determined by how well the generated pictures assist with making a given story more understandable to children of different cultures. Each generated image will be assessed by how well it conveys the same scene as the corresponding text and whether it contains any distortions or inappropriate sections."
- Why unresolved: The paper discusses the challenges of balancing cultural sensitivity with accurate translation, but does not provide a clear framework or guidelines for achieving this balance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a rubric or set of criteria for evaluating the cultural appropriateness and semantic accuracy of generated images, and testing it on a diverse set of books and cultures.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the performance of Stable Diffusion be improved for non-English languages and cultures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "An explanation for this is could be that the Stable Diffusion model was trained on a subset of the Lioan-5B dataset [43]. As such any language which have limited representation in this dataset will perform poorly in stable diffusion. French, German, and Spanish were all in the top 5 most represented languages in this dataset."
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the bias towards Western cultures in Stable Diffusion, but does not explore potential solutions or workarounds for improving performance in other languages and cultures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimenting with fine-tuning Stable Diffusion on datasets from different cultures and languages, or developing new techniques for prompting and image generation that are more culturally inclusive.

## Limitations
- Small sample size (N=26 participants) limits statistical power and generalizability of qualitative findings
- Fixed seed approach (1234) may have introduced systematic biases that wouldn't generalize to other seeds
- All methods tested only use Stable Diffusion v1.5, potentially constraining performance of more advanced approaches
- Doesn't address temporal dynamics or character consistency across multiple story pages

## Confidence

| Claim | Confidence Level |

| AI-generated images can serve as "viable alternative" to traditional illustrations | Medium |

## Next Checks

1. **Replication with larger sample**: Conduct the questionnaire with N≥100 participants per culture to establish statistical significance of preference differences between AI-generated and traditional illustrations, with proper power analysis to detect meaningful effect sizes.

2. **Multi-seed validation**: Generate images using 5-10 different seeds (beyond just 1234) to assess whether the observed performance patterns hold across different random initializations, particularly for the identified failure modes around facial features and cultural style.

3. **Temporal consistency test**: Create a 10-page sequence using the best-performing method to evaluate character consistency, scene coherence, and narrative flow across multiple pages, as this represents a more realistic use case than single-page generation.
