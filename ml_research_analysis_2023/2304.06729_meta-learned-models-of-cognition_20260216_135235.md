# Meta-Learned Models of Cognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06729
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06729
- Reference count: 0
- One-line primary result: Meta-learning enables construction of Bayes-optimal cognitive models without explicit prior and likelihood specification

## Executive Summary
This article presents a comprehensive framework for meta-learned models of cognition, establishing a new research program that leverages meta-learning to construct Bayes-optimal learning algorithms. The approach addresses key limitations of traditional Bayesian cognitive models by enabling approximate optimal inference in computationally intractable scenarios, handling cases where inference problems cannot be explicitly defined, incorporating resource constraints, and integrating neuroscientific insights. The framework represents a significant advance in cognitive modeling by eliminating the need to specify prior and likelihood functions while maintaining normative optimality properties.

## Method Summary
The framework trains recurrent neural networks to approximate posterior predictive distributions by maximizing expected log-likelihood of future observations through meta-learning. The meta-learner optimizes network parameters using gradient-based methods on samples from the data-generating distribution, effectively learning to perform Bayesian inference without explicitly computing posteriors. This approach handles both supervised learning tasks (e.g., insect length prediction) and reinforcement learning scenarios, with performance evaluated against analytical Bayesian solutions or human behavioral data.

## Key Results
- Meta-learning can produce Bayes-optimal learning algorithms when neural networks are sufficiently expressive and optimization finds global optima
- The approach successfully extends rational analysis to computationally intractable inference problems by bypassing explicit posterior computation
- Resource-rational models emerge naturally through architectural constraints that manipulate algorithmic and computational complexity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Meta-learning produces Bayes-optimal learning algorithms
- **Mechanism:** Outer-loop meta-learning optimizes network parameters to maximize expected log-likelihood of future observations, mathematically equivalent to finding posterior predictive distribution per Aitchison's theorem
- **Core assumption:** Neural network is sufficiently expressive and optimization finds global optimum
- **Evidence anchors:** Abstract statement, section on meta-parameter maximization, weak corpus support
- **Break condition:** Insufficient network expressiveness or optimization failure results in approximate rather than exact Bayes-optimality

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Meta-learning extends rational analysis to computationally intractable problems
- **Mechanism:** Bypasses intractable exact Bayesian inference by training network to directly approximate observation-to-prediction mapping through supervised learning on data-generating distribution samples
- **Core assumption:** Representative samples from data-generating distribution are available
- **Evidence anchors:** Section on intractable inference, discussion of avoiding explicit posterior calculation, weak corpus support
- **Break condition:** Unavailability or insufficiency of data-generating distribution samples prevents appropriate approximation construction

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Meta-learning enables resource-rational models through complexity manipulation
- **Mechanism:** Architectural constraints (width, depth, parameter count) control algorithmic complexity (storage bits) and computational complexity (activation size/resources)
- **Core assumption:** Both complexity forms can be meaningfully manipulated through architectural choices
- **Evidence anchors:** Section on resource-rational models, Dasgupta et al. example of reducing hidden units, weak corpus support
- **Break condition:** Excessive constraints eliminate necessary representational capacity for capturing cognitive phenomena

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data generator → Recurrent network → Optimizer → Performance evaluation
- **Critical path:**
  1. Define task distribution and performance metric
  2. Initialize neural network architecture
  3. Implement meta-learning loop with data generation and parameter updates
  4. Validate convergence and performance
  5. Analyze learned behavior and compare to human cognition
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Network expressiveness vs. computational efficiency
  - Task distribution complexity vs. training feasibility
  - Interpretability vs. model capacity
  - Generalization vs. overfitting to meta-training distribution
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor convergence: Learning curves plateau at suboptimal loss
  - Overfitting: Excellent training performance but poor generalization
  - Underfitting: Consistently poor performance across all tasks
  - Mode collapse: Network predicts single mode rather than full distribution
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Simple synthetic task: Insect length prediction with known normal distributions to verify convergence to analytical Bayesian solution
  2. Complexity manipulation: Same task with varying network widths to observe resource-rational behavior emergence
  3. Intractable inference test: Non-conjugate prior (e.g., exponential prior) to verify meta-learning handles cases where analytical Bayesian inference fails

- What evidence would resolve it: Development of visualization and explanation techniques for meta-learned models, plus empirical studies demonstrating their effectiveness in cognitive science applications

## Limitations

- Theoretical framework assumes neural networks can sufficiently approximate posterior predictive distributions, unproven for complex cognitive tasks
- Computational complexity of meta-learning may limit scalability to real-world cognitive modeling applications
- Reliance on accurately specified data-generating distributions may not hold for many cognitive domains with unknown or complex generative processes
