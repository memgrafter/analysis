# Experimenting with Normalization Layers in Federated Learning on non-IID scenarios

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10630
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10630
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: GN and LN consistently outperform BN in non-IID federated learning scenarios

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how different normalization layers affect Federated Learning (FL) performance with non-IID data distributions. Through extensive experiments on MNIST and CIFAR10 datasets using ResNet-18 and EfficientNet-B0 architectures, the study compares Batch Normalization, Group Normalization, Instance Normalization, Layer Normalization, and Batch Renormalization across three non-IID scenarios. The results demonstrate that GN and LN significantly outperform BN in non-IID settings, with LN showing slight superiority in uniform cases. The study also reveals that batch size and epochs per round are critical hyperparameters affecting model performance and convergence.

## Method Summary
The study uses the OpenFL framework to conduct federated experiments with ResNet-18 and EfficientNet-B0 models. Five normalization layers (BN, GN, IN, LN, BRN) are evaluated across three non-IID scenarios (uniform, labels quantity skew, feature distribution skew) on MNIST and CIFAR10 datasets. Models are trained using Adam optimizer with learning rate 10^-3 and cross-entropy loss. The FedAvg algorithm is employed for model aggregation, with experiments varying batch sizes (8, 64, 256, 512) and epochs per round (E=1, 10, 100) to assess their impact on convergence and accuracy.

## Key Results
- GN and LN consistently outperform BN in non-IID federated learning settings
- Batch size critically affects performance, with degradation observed when batch size exceeds 256
- Optimal epochs per round (E=10) improves model accuracy and convergence
- IN shows baseline random performance, confirming its unsuitability for classification tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
In non-IID scenarios, local batch statistics in BN diverge from global statistics, causing poor generalization. GN and LN compute normalization statistics within each sample, eliminating dependence on batch composition. This is particularly important when data is non-IID, as batch statistics do not represent global statistics, leading BN-equipped networks to poor results.

### Mechanism 2
More local training steps allow clients to optimize their local models toward local minima before aggregation, improving global convergence when data is heterogeneous. However, excessive local training causes model divergence across clients. When E=1, BN converges quickly, while GN and LN require more training steps to converge, but when E increases to 10 or 100, BN also requires more rounds to reach convergence.

### Mechanism 3
Large batch sizes reduce gradient noise, causing optimization to converge to sharp minima that generalize poorly. This effect is amplified in non-IID settings where batch statistics are already unreliable. The accuracy degrades significantly when the batch size becomes too large, particularly when passing batch size 256 to 512.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: OpenFL framework (Director-Envoy workflow) -> Neural network models (ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0) -> Datasets (MNIST, CIFAR10) -> Training pipeline (local training, global aggregation)

- **Critical path**: 1) Director initializes model and sends to collaborators, 2) Collaborators train locally with specified normalization layer, 3) Collaborators send trained models back to Director, 4) Director aggregates models and redistributes updated model, 5) Repeat until convergence or maximum rounds reached

- **Design tradeoffs**: Normalization layer choice (BN vs GN/LN), Batch size (smaller vs larger), Epochs per round (fewer vs more)

- **Failure signatures**: BN performance degradation in non-IID scenarios, Performance drop when batch size exceeds 256, Degradation when epochs per round exceed optimal value, IN showing coin-toss performance

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Compare BN, GN, and LN performance on MNIST with labels quantity skew, varying epochs per round (E=1, 10, 100), 2) Test different batch sizes (8, 64, 256, 512) with GN on CIFAR10 in covariate shift scenario, 3) Evaluate scalability by varying number of clients (2, 4, 8, 10) with LN on MNIST in labels quantity skew setting

## Limitations
- Experiments focus only on image classification tasks with specific architectures (ResNet-18, EfficientNet-B0)
- Implementation details of the OpenFL framework are not fully specified
- Only five normalization variants and three non-IID scenarios are examined

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Consistent superiority of GN and LN over BN in non-IID settings
- **Medium Confidence**: Optimal epochs per round findings (E=10 showing best performance)
- **Medium Confidence**: Batch size degradation claims (performance drop at 512)
