# Better Diffusion Models Further Improve Adversarial Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04638
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04638
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Demonstrates significant improvements in adversarial training robustness using Elucidating Diffusion Models (EDM) on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores whether newer, more efficient diffusion models can further improve adversarial training. The authors propose using the Elucidating Diffusion Model (EDM), which is more efficient and higher quality than previous DDPM models, to generate synthetic training data. They demonstrate significant improvements in robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets under both ℓ∞ and ℓ2 threat models. The results show that EDM-generated data mitigates robust overfitting, and training with sufficient generated data reduces the generalization gap. Extensive ablation studies provide insights into the mechanisms by which diffusion models promote adversarial training.

## Method Summary
The method employs EDM to generate synthetic images, which are combined with original training data for adversarial training using the TRADES framework. The authors investigate various factors including data augmentation, sampling steps, and training details. They conduct extensive experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, using WRN-28-10 and WRN-70-16 architectures. The generated data is evaluated using FID scores, and robust accuracy is measured under ℓ∞ and ℓ2 threat models using PGD and AutoAttack.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 (70.69%) and CIFAR-100 (42.67%) using only generated data under ℓ∞-norm threat model (ϵ=8/255)
- Generated data mitigates robust overfitting and reduces generalization gap
- Class-conditional generation with 20 sampling steps yields optimal performance
- Lower FID scores correlate with higher clean and robust accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Higher quality diffusion models (lower FID) generate synthetic data that more closely matches the true data distribution, thereby improving adversarial training performance.
- **Mechanism:** The denoising diffusion model generates synthetic images that, when used as additional training data, reduce the domain gap between real and synthetic samples. This leads to better generalization and robustness during adversarial training.
- **Core assumption:** The synthetic images generated by EDM are sufficiently similar to real images in the true data distribution to be useful for training robust models.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our adversarially trained models achieve state-of-the-art performance on RobustBench using only generated data (no external datasets). Under the ℓ∞-norm threat model with ϵ=8/255, our models achieve 70.69% and 42.67% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100..."
  - [section 5.4] "We find that low FID of generated data leads to high clean and robust accuracy. FID is a popular metric for comparing the distribution of generated images to the true distribution of data. Low FID indicates a small difference between the generated and true data distributions."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing - no direct comparisons of FID and performance in corpus papers.
- **Break condition:** If the diffusion model's FID score does not significantly improve over DDPM, or if the synthetic data introduces significant artifacts that harm training.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Sufficient quantity of generated data mitigates robust overfitting by providing diverse training examples that prevent the model from memorizing specific patterns.
- **Mechanism:** As the number of generated images increases, the training data becomes more diverse, reducing the gap between training and test performance. This prevents the model from overfitting to the original training set and improves robust generalization.
- **Core assumption:** The diversity of generated images is sufficient to cover the adversarial vulnerability space and prevent overfitting.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2] "Generated data can help to close the generalization gap for both clean and robust accuracy... The results show that the images generated by EDM contain those that are difficult to classify robustly, which benefits the robustness of model."
  - [section 5.2] "The robust overfitting is alleviated with the increasing size of generated data... After 500K generated images, the added generated images provide no significant improvement."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing - corpus papers focus on other aspects of diffusion models, not specifically on data quantity for adversarial training.
- **Break condition:** If the generated data does not sufficiently cover the adversarial vulnerability space, or if the model's capacity is too low to utilize all generated data.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Class-conditional generation outperforms unconditional generation because it produces more targeted and relevant synthetic samples for each class.
- **Mechanism:** By conditioning the generation process on class labels, the diffusion model can generate images that are more representative of each specific class, leading to better performance during adversarial training.
- **Core assumption:** The class-conditional generation process can produce high-quality, class-specific images that are beneficial for training robust models.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.4] "Class-conditional generation consistently outperforms unconditional generation, with lower FID and better robust performance. With 20 sampling steps, both settings achieve the lowest FID and best performance."
  - [corpus] Weak or missing - corpus papers do not directly compare class-conditional vs unconditional generation for adversarial training.
- **Break condition:** If the class-conditional generation process fails to produce high-quality images for certain classes, or if the pseudo-labeling process introduces noise.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** EDM -> WRN -> TRADES -> PGD/AutoAttack
- **Critical path:**
  1. Generate synthetic images using EDM
  2. Combine original and synthetic images for training
  3. Train WRN using TRADES framework with combined dataset
  4. Evaluate robustness using PGD and AutoAttack
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Sampling steps in EDM: Higher steps = better quality but slower generation
  - Amount of generated data: More data = better robustness but higher computational cost
  - Original-to-generated ratio: Balancing real and synthetic data for optimal performance
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor FID scores from EDM indicating low-quality synthetic images
  - Robust overfitting despite using generated data
  - No improvement in robust accuracy compared to using only real data
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train WRN-28-10 on CIFAR-10 using only real data and evaluate robust accuracy.
  2. Train WRN-28-10 on CIFAR-10 using real data + 1M EDM generated data and compare robust accuracy.
  3. Vary the number of sampling steps in EDM and observe the effect on robust accuracy.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal sampling step size for generating data with diffusion models for adversarial training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the effect of sampling steps on generated data quality and model performance, finding that both class-conditional and unconditional generation achieve the best performance with 20 sampling steps for CIFAR-10.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers sampling steps up to 40 and on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets. The optimal sampling step may vary for different datasets or when using more sampling steps.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on a wider range of datasets and sampling steps to determine the optimal number of steps for generating data with diffusion models.

### Open Question 2

- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with different model architectures and capacities to determine how model capacity affects the utilization of generated data in adversarial training.

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't thoroughly investigate whether other modern diffusion variants would yield similar improvements to EDM
- The relationship between FID scores and robust accuracy could be confounded by other training factors
- The mechanism explaining how higher quality generated data improves robust accuracy lacks rigorous statistical validation

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The observation that generated data helps close the generalization gap and mitigates robust overfitting is well-supported by the ablation studies
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that EDM specifically provides advantages over DDPM is supported by results, but lacks thorough investigation of other diffusion variants
- **Medium Confidence:** The mechanism explaining how higher quality generated data improves robust accuracy through better distribution matching is logical but could benefit from more rigorous validation

## Next Checks

1. **Direct Comparison Validation:** Conduct a controlled experiment comparing EDM with other recent diffusion models (e.g., ADM, DDIM) using identical training setups to isolate the contribution of EDM's specific architectural improvements.

2. **Mechanism Validation:** Design an experiment varying the FID scores of generated data while keeping other factors constant, to establish a more direct causal relationship between data quality (as measured by FID) and robust accuracy improvements.

3. **Robustness to Noise Validation:** Test the trained models against adaptive attacks specifically designed to exploit potential weaknesses in synthetically generated training data, to verify that the improvements are not merely addressing standard attack patterns.
