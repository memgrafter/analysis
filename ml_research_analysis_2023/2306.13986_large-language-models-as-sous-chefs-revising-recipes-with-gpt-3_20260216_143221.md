# Large Language Models as Sous Chefs: Revising Recipes with GPT-3

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13986
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13986
- Reference count: 5
- Primary result: GPT-3.5-based recipe revisions preferred 62.5% over originals in human evaluation

## Executive Summary
This paper explores using large language models to revise recipes and improve their readability for home cooks. The authors develop a prompt-based approach using GPT-3.5 to enhance recipe instructions, making them more user-friendly and accessible. Through a detailed human evaluation process on Amazon Mechanical Turk, where annotators compared original and revised recipes step-by-step, the study demonstrates that LLM-revised recipes are preferred by a significant margin. The research establishes LLMs as effective "digital sous chefs" capable of enhancing recipe usability through strategic modifications and additions.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a prompt-based approach using GPT-3.5 to revise existing recipes with the goal of improving readability and user-friendliness. They design a comprehensive human evaluation task on Amazon Mechanical Turk, where annotators compare original and revised recipes step-by-step to determine preferences. The evaluation methodology is detailed and systematic, allowing for granular assessment of each modification made by the LLM. The study focuses on English-language recipes and examines the validity of added steps in the revised versions.

## Key Results
- Annotators preferred revised recipes 62.5% of the time over original recipes (33.3% preference for originals)
- 83.3% of added steps in revised recipes were deemed valid by human evaluators
- GPT-3.5 demonstrated the most effective performance among tested LLMs for recipe revision tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness stems from LLMs' ability to process natural language and identify patterns in recipe structures. GPT-3.5 can recognize when instructions are ambiguous or missing crucial steps, then generate clearer alternatives based on its training data. The model's understanding of cooking terminology and sequential processes enables it to add helpful intermediate steps or clarify confusing instructions, resulting in more accessible recipes for home cooks.

## Limitations
- Narrow focus on recipe revision rather than creation limits scope
- Evaluation restricted to English-language recipes, reducing generalizability
- Reliance on Amazon Mechanical Turk annotators may not represent experienced cooks
