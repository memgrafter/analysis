# Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14325
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Multiagent debate between LLM instances improves reasoning and factuality by having agents critique each other's responses over multiple rounds, leading to better consensus answers. This approach significantly outperforms single-agent baselines on arithmetic, GSM8K, chess move prediction, biographies, and MMLU tasks, while requiring only black-box access to models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a multiagent debate framework where multiple instances of a language model independently generate answers to a prompt, then iteratively critique each other's responses over several rounds. The process aims to improve both the factual accuracy and reasoning quality of the final answer by leveraging collective critique and consensus-building. Experiments across six benchmarks show that this approach consistently outperforms single-agent baselines, with improvements in arithmetic accuracy, GSM8K problem-solving, chess move prediction, biography factuality, and MMLU task performance. The method requires only black-box access to models, making it widely applicable.

## Method Summary
The approach initializes multiple agents with the same prompt and has each generate an initial answer. In each debate round, agents are given the other agents' responses as context and prompted to update their own answer based on critiques and alternative perspectives. This process repeats for a fixed number of rounds or until convergence. The final consensus answer is selected, typically by majority vote or as the last agent response. The method was evaluated on six tasks using ChatGPT-based models, comparing debate performance against single-agent baselines including zero-shot, reflection, and majority voting approaches.

## Key Results
- Multiagent debate significantly outperforms single-agent baselines on arithmetic, GSM8K, chess move prediction, biographies, and MMLU tasks
- Debate improves both mathematical reasoning and strategic reasoning capabilities compared to baseline methods
- The approach enhances factual validity and reduces hallucinations in generated content across multiple domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Debate enables iterative cross-examination of reasoning steps, reducing error propagation.
- Mechanism: Each agent independently generates an answer, then critiques all other agents' reasoning. Through repeated rounds, inconsistent or flawed reasoning paths are identified and corrected, converging toward a more robust final answer.
- Core assumption: Agents can meaningfully critique each other's reasoning when provided as context, and such critiques lead to measurable improvement in answer accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states that debate "significantly enhances mathematical and strategic reasoning" and that models "construct answers that are consistent with both their internal critic as well as sensible in light of the responses of other agents."
  - [section] shows quantitative improvements across arithmetic, GSM8K, and chess tasks, with debate outperforming single-agent baselines.
  - [corpus] includes related work such as "Debate Only When Necessary: Adaptive Multiagent Collaboration for Efficient LLM Reasoning," suggesting debate is recognized as a viable reasoning enhancement.
- Break condition: If agents' critiques are superficial or aligned with initial errors, convergence may stall or reinforce incorrect reasoning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multiagent debate improves factuality by exposing and eliminating inconsistent or uncertain facts.
- Mechanism: Different agents generate varied answers when uncertain. Debate forces consensus, and uncertain or inconsistent facts are dropped or corrected during critique rounds.
- Core assumption: When models are uncertain, they generate different answers; debate then filters out these inconsistencies, leaving more reliable facts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] notes that debate "improves the factual validity of generated content, reducing fallacious answers and hallucinations."
  - [section] shows debate improves biography factuality and MMLU accuracy over single-agent baselines.
  - [corpus] includes "MAD-Fact: A Multi-Agent Debate Framework for Long-Form Factuality Evaluation in LLMs," indicating research focus on factuality via debate.
- Break condition: If all agents share the same hallucination, debate may converge on incorrect facts with high confidence.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Debate leverages the diversity of reasoning paths to explore solution space more thoroughly.
- Mechanism: Multiple agents start with independent reasoning chains. Through debate, agents integrate alternative perspectives, uncovering reasoning paths they would not have found alone.
- Core assumption: Independent reasoning by multiple agents yields diverse approaches; integrating these improves final answer quality beyond any single path.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] mentions that debate induces models to "hold and maintain multiple chains of reasoning and possible answers simultaneously before proposing the final answer."
  - [section] provides qualitative examples where all agents initially err but arrive at the correct answer through debate.
  - [corpus] includes "Debating for Better Reasoning: An Unsupervised Multimodal Approach," suggesting debate's role in reasoning diversity.
- Break condition: If agents' reasoning diversity is low (e.g., all follow same flawed heuristic), debate gains diminish.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input Parser -> Prompt Generator -> Multi-Agent Executor -> Debate Loop -> Consensus Extractor -> Output Formatter
  - Supporting: Context Summarizer (optional), Performance Tracker

- Critical path:
  1. Generate initial answers from N agents
  2. For each round: concatenate other agents' responses, prompt each agent for updated answer
  3. Repeat until convergence or max rounds
  4. Select final answer (e.g., majority or consensus)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - More agents → higher accuracy but greater cost and context length
  - More debate rounds → deeper convergence but risk of diminishing returns
  - Summarization vs. full concatenation → cost vs. fidelity trade-off
  - Prompt length (short vs. long) → debate stubbornness vs. convergence speed

- Failure signatures:
  - No convergence after max rounds (agents keep oscillating)
  - All agents converge on wrong answer (shared hallucination)
  - Context overflow errors (responses too long for model limit)
  - Performance worse than single-agent baseline (ineffective critique)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run single-agent vs. 2-agent debate on 10 arithmetic problems; measure accuracy improvement.
  2. Vary debate rounds (1-4) on GSM8K; plot accuracy vs. rounds to find diminishing returns.
  3. Test summarization vs. concatenation on biography task with 5 agents; compare factuality scores.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions including whether the approach scales to more complex reasoning tasks beyond the evaluated benchmarks, the theoretical guarantees of convergence to correct answers, and how the debate approach compares to other ensemble methods that use model outputs without internal debate.

## Limitations
- Debate effectiveness depends on agent diversity and critique quality, which are not fully validated
- Convergence on wrong answers is possible if all agents share the same hallucination or heuristic flaw
- Higher computational cost and context window usage proportional to number of agents and debate rounds

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Multiagent debate consistently improves reasoning and factuality over single-agent baselines across diverse benchmarks (arithmetic, GSM8K, biographies, MMLU, chess), as supported by quantitative results and qualitative examples in the paper.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that debate works by iterative cross-examination and consensus-driven fact elimination is plausible but relies on assumptions about agent critique quality and diversity that are not fully validated in the paper.
- **Low confidence**: Specific prompt templates and debate strategies (short vs. long debate, agent count, round count) are underspecified, making exact reproduction difficult.

## Next Checks
1. **Test debate robustness**: Run debate with varying numbers of agents (2, 4, 6) on GSM8K and measure if accuracy gains plateau or if more agents significantly help, isolating the impact of agent diversity.
2. **Probe for hallucination convergence**: Construct synthetic biographies with known false facts and check if debate converges on these shared hallucinations more often than single-agent methods, quantifying the risk of collective error.
3. **Measure critique quality**: Log and analyze the critiques agents generate during debate; classify critiques as substantive vs. superficial and correlate with answer accuracy to validate the mechanism of error correction.
