# H-AES: Towards Automated Essay Scoring for Hindi

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14635
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14635
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: Fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa achieves QWK of 0.741, setting new state-of-the-art for Hindi AES

## Executive Summary
This study introduces H-AES, the first comprehensive benchmark for Automated Essay Scoring (AES) in Hindi. The authors address the low-resource challenge by leveraging machine-translated English essays and fine-tuning state-of-the-art multilingual transformer models. XLM-RoBERTa outperforms classical ML, neural networks, and other transformers, achieving a QWK of 0.741. The work demonstrates that English AES approaches can be effectively adapted to Hindi, establishing a foundation for future Hindi language processing in educational assessment.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a multi-pronged approach to Hindi AES, combining classical feature-based machine learning (Linear Regression, SVR, Random Forest, XGBoost), neural sequence models (BiLSTM, CNN, CNN+LSTM+Attention, SKIPFLOW), and fine-tuned multilingual transformers (mBERT, DistilmBERT, XLM-RoBERTa, MuRIL, IndicBERT). Features are extracted from translated English essays and a small real-world Hindi corpus, including essay length, readability scores, vocabulary, and semantic coherence. Models are trained on the translated ASAP corpus and validated on 126 real-world Hindi essays, with performance measured using Quadratic Weighted Kappa.

## Key Results
- XLM-RoBERTa fine-tuning achieves the highest average QWK of 0.741
- Classical feature-based models perform competitively, with XGBoost reaching QWK of 0.673
- SKIPFLOW neural model and CNN+LSTM+Attention follow XLM-RoBERTa in performance
- IndicBERT and MuRIL underperform, likely due to architectural and training data limitations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Fine-tuning large multilingual transformer models on translated English essays can produce competitive AES performance in Hindi. The models learn cross-lingual representations and linguistic generalizations from large-scale multilingual pre-training, enabling knowledge transfer from English to Hindi even with limited target data. This relies on shared linguistic structure and preserved semantic/syntactic features through translation.

### Mechanism 2
Classical feature-based ML models can perform competitively on Hindi AES when enriched with language-specific readability and coherence features. Extracted features such as average sentence length, readability scores adapted for Hindi, and semantic overlap using mBERT capture essay quality dimensions that correlate with human judgments. This assumes handcrafted features effectively encode the criteria used in the Hindi scoring rubric and remain informative after machine translation.

### Mechanism 3
Neural sequence models can learn essay scoring patterns directly from text without explicit feature engineering. These models automatically discover local and global textual patterns through convolutional and recurrent layers, and attention mechanisms pool sentence representations to predict scores. This relies on the sequential nature of essays and their internal coherence being effectively modeled by these architectures even in a low-resource setting.

## Foundational Learning

- **Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK)**
  - Why needed here: QWK is the evaluation metric used to compare AES models, accounting for chance agreement and being sensitive to score differences.
  - Quick check question: How does QWK differ from simple accuracy or correlation in evaluating essay scoring models?

- **Multilingual transformer pre-training**
  - Why needed here: Understanding how models like mBERT and XLM-R learn cross-lingual representations is key to grasping why fine-tuning works on Hindi.
  - Quick check question: What is the role of translation language modeling (TLM) in models like XLM-R, and how might it help Hindi AES?

- **Feature engineering for readability**
  - Why needed here: The study uses Hindi-specific readability formulas adapted from Sinha et al. (2012); knowing how these are computed clarifies their role.
  - Quick check question: How is the Hindi readability score computed from average word length and polysyllabic word count?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data → Feature extraction / Tokenization → Model training/fine-tuning → Score prediction → QWK evaluation
- **Critical path**: Translated ASAP corpus and native Hindi essays → Feature extraction and tokenization → Model training/fine-tuning → Score prediction → QWK evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Classical models are interpretable but limited in nuance; neural models are more expressive but need more data; transformers offer best performance but are resource-heavy
- **Failure signatures**: Low QWK scores may indicate poor translation quality, insufficient training data, or architectural mismatch with Hindi's linguistic features
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train and evaluate a linear regression model using the extracted features on the ASAP-Hindi corpus
  2. Fine-tune mBERT on the translated essays and test on both ASAP-Hindi and the organic Hindi corpus
  3. Compare the performance of SKIPFLOW with a simple BiLSTM baseline on the same data splits

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the quality of machine-translated English essays compare to native Hindi essays in terms of features critical for AES performance? The study trains and evaluates models using translated English essays and validates them on a small-scale real-world Hindi corpus, acknowledging translation's potential impact on language-related attributes. This remains unresolved as the paper does not provide a detailed comparative analysis of feature distributions or model performance metrics between the translated and native Hindi corpora.

### Open Question 2
What specific linguistic or architectural factors cause IndicBERT and MuRIL to underperform compared to multilingual models like mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa in AES tasks? The paper observes that IndicBERT and MuRIL perform comparatively worse and attributes this to architectural limitations and training data differences, but does not empirically isolate or test these factors.

### Open Question 3
Can the development of a larger, more diverse Hindi essay corpus significantly improve the generalization and performance of AES models beyond what is achievable with translated data? The paper acknowledges the lack of a comprehensive Hindi essay corpus and suggests that a larger corpus would facilitate more nuanced learning and better generalization, but uses a small-scale native Hindi corpus (126 essays) for validation.

## Limitations

- Translation quality of the ASAP corpus to Hindi is unspecified and may affect semantic/syntactic fidelity
- Small size of the real-world Hindi corpus (126 essays) limits generalizability validation
- Lack of variance reporting or statistical significance testing for QWK scores due to small sample size
- Limited comparison with prior Hindi AES work makes contextualizing the state-of-the-art claim difficult

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: XLM-RoBERTa fine-tuning achieves the best QWK among the tested models
- **Medium confidence**: Multilingual transformers can effectively transfer from English to Hindi, but hinges on translation quality and cross-lingual representation fidelity
- **Low confidence**: IndicBERT and MuRIL perform poorly due to architectural and training data limitations, as this is plausible but not empirically validated

## Next Checks

1. Conduct a bilingual human evaluation of the machine-translated ASAP corpus to verify semantic and syntactic fidelity to the original English essays, focusing on features critical to essay scoring
2. Perform repeated random subsampling or cross-validation to report confidence intervals and statistical significance of QWK differences between models, given the small Hindi corpus size
3. Ensure that all transformer models use identical preprocessing, tokenization, and input formatting to isolate architectural effects from data handling differences
