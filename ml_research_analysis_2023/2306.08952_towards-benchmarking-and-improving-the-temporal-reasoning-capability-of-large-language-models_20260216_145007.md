# Towards Benchmarking and Improving the Temporal Reasoning Capability of Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08952
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08952
- Authors: [Not specified in source]

- Key outcome: Introduces TEMP REASON benchmark and TempT5 model showing improved temporal reasoning across time-time, time-event, and event-event relations

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitations of large language models (LLMs) in temporal reasoning by introducing a comprehensive benchmark and training framework. The authors identify three distinct levels of temporal understanding (time-time, time-event, event-event relations) and show that existing time-sensitive QA datasets are biased towards recent years and only cover time-event relations. They propose TEMP REASON, a benchmark spanning a longer time range and all three temporal reasoning types, along with a novel training framework combining temporal span extraction pre-training and time-sensitive reinforcement learning. Experiments demonstrate that LLMs perform well on contemporary years but poorly on low-resource years, with the proposed TempT5 model consistently outperforming strong baselines across all settings.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces TEMP REASON, a temporal reasoning benchmark covering time-time, time-event, and event-event relations across three settings: closed book QA (CBQA), open book QA (OBQA), and reasoning QA (ReasonQA). The proposed training framework consists of three components: temporal span extraction (TSE) pre-training on 100K Wikipedia articles to encourage attention to temporal and entity spans, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on TEMP REASON dataset, and time-sensitive reinforcement learning (TSRL) using a reward function that penalizes temporally incorrect predictions. The method is evaluated on the TempT5 model, which is built on T5-base or T5-large architectures and incorporates all three training components.

## Key Results
- LLMs perform well on contemporary years but poorly on low-resource years due to imbalanced term frequencies in pre-training corpora
- TEMP REASON dataset covers all three temporal reasoning types (time-time, time-event, event-event) and three settings (CBQA, OBQA, ReasonQA)
- TempT5 model consistently outperforms strong baselines across all settings, with TSRL showing 1-2% improvements over SFT
- Performance varies significantly across temporal reasoning levels, with models performing best when relevant facts and timestamps are explicitly provided

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Language models exhibit temporal reasoning biases due to imbalanced term frequencies in pre-training corpora, favoring contemporary years over past and future years. LLMs are pre-trained on text corpora from specific time snapshots (typically around 2018-2020), leading to higher term frequencies for years closer to the training data and lower frequencies for years further away. This causes models to perform better on contemporary years and poorly on past/future years.

### Mechanism 2
Temporal reasoning capability varies significantly across different temporal reasoning levels (time-time, time-event, event-event), with models performing best when relevant facts and timestamps are explicitly provided. When answering temporal questions, models must (1) retrieve possible answers, (2) ground events to specific time ranges, and (3) perform temporal reasoning. Performance improves when context provides facts and timestamps, reducing the reasoning burden.

### Mechanism 3
Temporal span extraction pre-training and time-sensitive reinforcement learning improve temporal reasoning by encouraging models to focus on temporal and entity spans and penalizing temporally incorrect predictions. TSE pre-training masks temporal and entity spans, forcing models to learn their importance. TSRL uses a reward function that rewards correct answers and penalizes temporally wrong answers, reinforcing temporal consistency.

  1. Pre-train on temporal and entity span extraction task
  2. Fine-tune on TEMP REASON dataset in chosen setting
  3. Apply time-sensitive reinforcement learning
  4. Evaluate on test sets

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Span extraction pre-training vs. standard pre-training: TSE may improve temporal reasoning but requires additional training data and compute
  - Closed book vs. open book vs. reasoning settings: Tradeoff between realism and evaluation control
  - Reinforcement learning vs. supervised learning: RL may improve temporal reasoning but is more complex and unstable

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on past/future years indicates vocabulary frequency bias
  - Large performance gaps between settings indicate insufficient reasoning capabilities
  - Inability to generalize temporal reasoning across different question types

## Limitations
- Temporal reasoning degradation for past/future years is inferred from model performance rather than directly measured through corpus frequency analysis
- Effectiveness of temporal span extraction pre-training is demonstrated through downstream improvements rather than direct ablation studies
- Generalization of the proposed framework to other temporal reasoning tasks and domains is not established

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Observation that LLMs perform better on contemporary years than on past/future years, supported by empirical evidence across multiple models
- Medium Confidence: Effectiveness of combined training framework (TSE pre-training + TSRL), though individual component contributions need further isolation
- Low Confidence: Mechanism explanation for temporal reasoning biases relies on assumptions about corpus composition without direct frequency evidence
