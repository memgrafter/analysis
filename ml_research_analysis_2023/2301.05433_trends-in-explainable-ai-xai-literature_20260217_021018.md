# Trends in Explainable AI (XAI) Literature

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.05433
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05433
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Analysis of 5,199 XAI papers reveals increasing multidisciplinary research, with significant growth in non-CS fields and cross-field collaboration.

## Executive Summary
This study provides a comprehensive analysis of trends in XAI literature by examining a dataset of 5,199 papers. The research reveals that XAI has become increasingly multidisciplinary, with significant growth in papers from non-CS fields, particularly in 2016, 2018, and 2021. The study also demonstrates increasing cross-field collaboration and citation activity, with XAI-CS frequently citing fields such as Mathematics, Medicine, and Psychology. The dataset serves as a valuable paper discovery engine for identifying influential works within and across fields.

## Method Summary
The study collected 5,199 XAI papers through keyword-based search, manual curation from existing lists, and citation tree expansion. Papers were classified by field using author affiliations and journal categories. The analysis examined publication trends, cross-field collaboration patterns, and citation networks. The dataset and analysis code are publicly available at https://github.com/alonjacovi/XAI-Scholar.

## Key Results
- XAI research shows significant growth in multidisciplinary contributions, with non-CS fields experiencing notable increases in 2016, 2018, and 2021
- Cross-field collaboration and citation activity are increasing, with XAI-CS frequently citing Mathematics, Medicine, and Psychology papers
- The dataset serves as an effective paper discovery engine for identifying influential works across different fields

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study's methodology combines multiple data collection approaches (keyword search, manual curation, and citation expansion) to create a comprehensive dataset. The classification system based on author affiliations and journal categories enables systematic analysis of field-specific contributions and collaboration patterns. The citation network analysis reveals the flow of ideas between fields and identifies influential works.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Keyword Search -> Manual Curation -> Citation Expansion -> Field Classification -> Trend Analysis -> Network Analysis

### Critical Path
The most critical path is: Keyword Search -> Citation Expansion -> Network Analysis, as this sequence determines the dataset's comprehensiveness and the validity of collaboration insights.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Keyword specificity vs. recall**: Narrow keywords may miss relevant papers, while broad keywords increase noise
- **Citation depth vs. computational efficiency**: Deeper citation trees provide more coverage but require more resources
- **Field classification granularity**: More detailed classifications provide better insights but may be less reliable

### Failure Signatures
- Incomplete keyword coverage leading to missing relevant papers
- Citation expansion bias toward highly-cited works
- Field misclassification affecting collaboration analysis accuracy

### First Experiments
1. Test keyword coverage by comparing results against known XAI literature lists
2. Validate field classification accuracy with manual review of random samples
3. Analyze citation network connectivity to identify potential data gaps

## Limitations
- Keyword-based search and citation expansion may miss relevant papers or create bias toward highly-cited works
- Field classification based on affiliations and journal categories may not accurately reflect interdisciplinary research
- Analysis focuses on quantitative trends without examining quality or impact of cross-field collaborations
- Dataset coverage completeness cannot be verified without access to all potential XAI literature sources

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The observed growth trends in XAI publications and the increase in cross-field collaboration are well-supported by the data collection methodology.

**Medium confidence**: The conclusions about XAI-CS citing patterns and field relationships are reasonable but depend on accurate field classification and citation data.

**Low confidence**: The assertion that 2016, 2018, and 2021 were particularly significant years for non-CS field contributions lacks detailed statistical validation in the current analysis.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct a random sample validation of the field classification accuracy to assess potential misclassifications in the dataset.
2. Perform a citation network analysis to verify the directionality and strength of cross-field citation patterns claimed in the study.
3. Implement a manual review of the collection process for a specific time period to identify potential systematic biases in paper selection.
