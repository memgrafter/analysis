# TOT: Topology-Aware Optimal Transport For Multimodal Hate Detection
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09314
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09314

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting implicit harmful content in multimodal memes where explicit text markers and visual cues are often missing or twisted. The proposed method, TOT (Topology-Aware Optimal Transport), formulates cross-modal alignment as an optimal transport problem, leveraging a Gaussian kernel method to capture complementary information across modalities and eliminate distributional gaps. A topology-aware optimal transport framework is then used to capture inter-modal correspondence through bipartite graph path reasoning, allowing comprehensive and representative information propagation. TOT achieves state-of-the-art performance on two publicly available benchmark datasets (Harm-C and Harm-P), with accuracy improvements of up to 5.66% over previous best models.

## Method Summary
TOT addresses implicit harmful content detection in multimodal memes by formulating cross-modal alignment as an optimal transport problem. The method employs a Gaussian kernel approach to capture complementary information across text and image modalities while eliminating distributional gaps. A topology-aware optimal transport framework captures inter-modal correspondence through bipartite graph path reasoning, enabling comprehensive information propagation. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on Harm-C and Harm-P benchmark datasets, demonstrating superior capability in capturing implicit cross-modal alignment for hate detection tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by transforming the cross-modal alignment problem into an optimal transport framework, which inherently handles the complex relationships between text and image modalities in multimodal memes. The Gaussian kernel method effectively captures complementary information while reducing distributional discrepancies between modalities. The topology-aware component with bipartite graph path reasoning enables comprehensive exploration of inter-modal correspondences, allowing the model to identify subtle harmful patterns that explicit markers might miss.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input Modalities -> Gaussian Kernel Processing -> Optimal Transport Layer -> Bipartite Graph Path Reasoning -> Topology-Aware Integration -> Classification Output

Critical path: The most performance-critical sequence is the Optimal Transport Layer, as it directly handles the core cross-modal alignment challenge. Bottlenecks here will cascade through the entire pipeline, affecting downstream classification accuracy.

Design tradeoffs: The method prioritizes accuracy through comprehensive inter-modal correspondence capture over computational efficiency. The Gaussian kernel and optimal transport components add significant computational overhead compared to simpler fusion approaches, but this is justified by the substantial accuracy gains on benchmark datasets.

Failure signatures: Performance degradation occurs when:
- Input modalities have extreme distributional gaps that overwhelm the Gaussian kernel's smoothing capabilities
- Graph path reasoning becomes computationally intractable with very large modality feature spaces
- Topology-aware integration fails to capture meaningful structural relationships in certain meme types
