# Evaluation of AI Chatbots for Patient-Specific EHR Questions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02549
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02549
- Reference count: 7
- ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude perform well in answering patient-specific questions from clinical notes

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates the performance of large language models (LLMs) including ChatGPT 3.5, ChatGPT 4, Google Bard, and Claude in answering patient-specific questions based on clinical admission notes. The authors assess accuracy, relevance, comprehensiveness, and coherence using a 5-point Likert scale across two session scenarios (1 question per session vs 1 topic per session) and three question types (general, specific, non-answerable). Results show that ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude perform well across different question types and scenarios, providing accurate, relevant, comprehensive, and coherent answers, though further research is needed to improve comprehensiveness and coherence.

## Method Summary
The study uses 30 de-identified clinical admission notes from the MIMIC-III dataset and creates three categories of questions (General, Specific, Non-answerable) with five questions each. The chatbots are presented with notes and asked questions under two scenarios: 1 Question per Session (1QpS) and 1 Topic per Session (1TpS). Medical professionals evaluate the generated answers using a 5-point Likert scale across four criteria. Statistical analysis using the Kruskal-Wallis test determines if performance differences across scenarios, question types, and models are significant.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude demonstrate strong performance in answering clinical questions with high accuracy, relevance, coverage, and coherence
- Session structure (1QpS vs 1TpS) does not significantly impact answer quality
- Question type (general, specific, non-answerable) does not significantly affect model performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs can accurately answer patient-specific clinical questions when given structured admission notes as context
- Mechanism: Models leverage natural language understanding to parse clinical jargon and abbreviations, generating relevant answers aligned with clinical facts
- Core assumption: Admission notes are sufficiently detailed and properly de-identified
- Evidence anchors: Study results show both ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude provide accurate, relevant, comprehensive, and coherent answers
- Break condition: Insufficient detail or excessive jargon/abbreviations in clinical notes

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Session structure does not significantly affect answer quality
- Mechanism: Models maintain internal state across sessions and can use prior context without substantial performance change
- Core assumption: Models have sufficient capacity to handle both single-question and multi-question sessions
- Evidence anchors: Kruskal-Wallis test shows no statistically significant difference across session scenarios
- Break condition: Session context becomes too large or complex

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Question type does not significantly affect model performance
- Mechanism: Models handle varying levels of question specificity while maintaining accurate and relevant answers
- Core assumption: Model understanding of question intent is robust across specificity levels
- Evidence anchors: Statistical analysis shows no significant differences across question types
- Break condition: Questions become too vague or rely heavily on external knowledge

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: De-identified clinical notes -> Question sets (General/Specific/Non-answerable) -> LLM models (ChatGPT 3.5, 4, Bard, Claude) -> Human evaluation (5-point Likert scale) -> Kruskal-Wallis statistical analysis

- Critical path: Prepare de-identified clinical notes → Generate question sets → Run models with two session scenarios → Have medical professionals evaluate answers → Analyze results statistically

- Design tradeoffs: Multiple models vs. focusing on one; Human evaluation vs. automated metrics; Small dataset for proof-of-concept vs. larger dataset for generalization

- Failure signatures: Low accuracy scores indicate model misunderstanding of clinical context; Low coherence scores suggest issues with answer generation or context handling; Non-significant statistical results may indicate insufficient sample size

- First 3 experiments: 1) Pilot with one clinical note and small question set to validate evaluation process; 2) Test two session scenarios with one model to confirm no significant performance difference; 3) Compare model performance on each question type to identify systematic weaknesses

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do LLMs perform on larger and more diverse clinical datasets compared to the MIMIC-III subset used in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the dataset used was relatively small and that further research is needed to evaluate performance on larger datasets
- Why unresolved: The current study is limited to a small, specific subset of MIMIC-III data
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive evaluation using a much larger and more varied set of clinical notes across different hospitals and specialties

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the coherence and comprehensiveness of LLM-generated answers be improved through fine-tuning or prompt engineering?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that further research is needed to improve the comprehensiveness and coherence of answers generated by LLMs
- Why unresolved: The study did not explore techniques like fine-tuning or advanced prompt engineering
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing improved coherence and comprehensiveness after applying specific fine-tuning strategies or optimized prompts

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different LLMs (beyond ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude) compare in terms of accuracy, relevance, and coherence for clinical QA tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluated only ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude, noting that experiments with other systems are ongoing
- Why unresolved: The study did not include a comprehensive comparison of multiple LLM models
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic evaluation of various LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Bard, Gemini) on the same clinical QA benchmark

## Limitations
- Small dataset of only 30 clinical notes may not capture full diversity of clinical scenarios
- Human evaluation introduces potential subjectivity and bias in assessment
- Specific questions used in the study are not provided, limiting reproducibility
- Results may not generalize to other LLM models or architectures

## Confidence

- High confidence: ChatGPT 3.5 and Claude perform well in answering questions from clinical notes
- Medium confidence: Session structure does not significantly affect answer quality
- Medium confidence: Question type does not significantly affect model performance
