# A Scale-Independent Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning with Convergence Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04179
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04179
- Reference count: 15
- One-line primary result: A scale-independent multi-objective A2C algorithm that converges in mean and outperforms scalarized single-objective approaches on edge caching tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a scale-independent multi-objective reinforcement learning algorithm based on the Advantage Actor-Critic framework. The method uses reward-specific state-value functions and optimizes the policy without predefined weights for combining objectives. The authors provide a convergence analysis guaranteeing convergence in mean under certain conditions. Experiments on a multi-task edge caching problem demonstrate superior sample efficiency and scale-invariance compared to single-objective A2C with scalarized rewards.

## Method Summary
The proposed MO-A2C algorithm employs reward-specific state-value functions in both actor and critic networks. Instead of using predefined weights to combine objectives, the method solves a convex combination problem at each update step to determine optimal weights for gradient updates. The algorithm maintains a single policy distribution while learning separate value functions for each reward component. Training involves computing reward-specific advantages, updating the critic by minimizing reward-specific losses with convex combination weights, and updating the actor by maximizing reward-specific policy losses with the same weights. The approach is evaluated on a multi-task edge caching problem with three competing objectives: QoS, bandwidth consumption, and backhaul cost.

## Key Results
- MO-A2C learns effectively after 2×10³ episodes, while SO-A2C requires over 5×10³ episodes for comparable performance
- The method demonstrates robustness to reward scaling across different weight combinations ([1,1,1], [0.1,1,1], [1,10,0.1])
- Convergence analysis provides theoretical guarantees for the actor update under specific learning rate conditions

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - MO-actor agent -> Parameterized policy network that outputs action distributions
  -> MO-critic agent -> Shared neural network that learns reward-specific state-value functions
  -> Advantage functions -> Reward-specific advantages used for policy updates
  -> Convex combination solver -> Solves for optimal weights in the multi-objective gradient update

- Critical path:
  1. Observe state and select action using current policy
  2. Execute action and observe rewards and next state
  3. Compute reward-specific advantages using learned value functions
  4. Update MO-critic by minimizing reward-specific losses with convex combination weights
  5. Update MO-actor by maximizing reward-specific policy losses with convex combination weights
  6. Repeat until convergence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-policy vs. multiple policy distributions: Single-policy approach simplifies architecture but may limit expressiveness
  - Shared vs. separate critic networks: Shared network reduces parameters but may introduce interference between objectives
  - Convex combination vs. other scalarization methods: Convex combination is simple but may not work well for non-convex Pareto frontiers

- Failure signatures:
  - Slow convergence or poor performance: May indicate issues with value function approximation or gradient combination
  - Unstable learning: Could be due to high variance in gradient estimates or poor choice of learning rates
  - One objective dominating: Might suggest that the convex combination is not finding a good balance between objectives

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare MO-A2C with scalarized A2C on a simple MO-MDP with known Pareto front
  2. Test MO-A2C's robustness to reward scaling by varying the relative importance of objectives
  3. Analyze the effect of different convex combination solvers on the quality of the learned policy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the convergence guarantee hold for the critic update (6) under the same assumptions as the actor update (9)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The convergence analysis in Theorem 4.1 only considers the actor update (9) and its learning rate. The critic update (6) is not analyzed for convergence.
- Why unresolved: The critic update (6) uses a different learning rate and objective function compared to the actor update (9). Its convergence properties are not established in the paper.
- What evidence would resolve it: A convergence theorem for the critic update (6) under appropriate assumptions on the critic learning rate and objective function.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is the MO-A2C algorithm to the choice of neural network architectures for the actor and critic?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses specific neural network architectures for the actor and critic (e.g., one hidden layer with 64 or 128 neurons). It does not explore the impact of different architectures on performance.
- Why unresolved: Different neural network architectures may lead to different levels of performance and sample efficiency. The optimal architecture may depend on the specific problem domain.
- What evidence would resolve it: An empirical study comparing the performance of MO-A2C with different neural network architectures on the multi-task problem or other benchmark problems.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the MO-A2C algorithm perform on problems with more than three objectives?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates MO-A2C on a multi-task problem with three objectives (QoS, BW, and BH). It does not explore its performance on problems with more objectives.
- Why unresolved: The performance of MO-A2C may degrade as the number of objectives increases due to the curse of dimensionality. It is unclear how well it scales to higher-dimensional objective spaces.
- What evidence would resolve it: An empirical study evaluating MO-A2C on problems with varying numbers of objectives and comparing its performance to other multi-objective RL algorithms.

## Limitations
- The convergence proof assumes a convex Pareto frontier, which may not hold in many practical problems
- Experimental validation is limited to a single application domain (edge caching) without comparison to other multi-objective RL baselines
- The reward-specific value functions may require careful tuning to avoid interference between objectives
