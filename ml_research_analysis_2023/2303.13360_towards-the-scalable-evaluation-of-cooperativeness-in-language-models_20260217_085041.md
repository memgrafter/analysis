# Towards the Scalable Evaluation of Cooperativeness in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13360
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13360
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: This paper introduces a method for generating and evaluating cooperative behavior in language models using game-theoretic scenarios, finding that larger instruct-tuned GPT-3 models tend to exhibit more cooperative behavior.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of generating diverse evaluation scenarios with specific game-theoretic structures for assessing the cooperative behavior of language models. The authors employ both crowdworkers and language models to generate instances of four experimental games: dictator game, ultimatum game, punishment game, and prisoner's dilemma. They find that both humans and models struggle to create and evaluate scenarios that accurately reflect the intended game-theoretic structures. The quality of generations is generally mediocre, with high rejection rates and false positive rates in the verification process. The authors release a dataset of 786 filtered scenarios and conduct quantitative and qualitative evaluations of UnifiedQA and GPT-3 models on this dataset. They observe that larger instruct-tuned GPT-3 models tend to exhibit more cooperative behavior, while other models show flat scaling trends. The study highlights the difficulty of generating structured evaluation data and emphasizes the need for further research in this area.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a method for generating and evaluating cooperative behavior in language models using game-theoretic scenarios. They first crowdsource and model-generate instances of four experimental games: dictator game, ultimatum game, punishment game, and prisoner's dilemma. These scenarios are then verified by humans and models to ensure they accurately reflect the intended game-theoretic structures. The verified scenarios are used to create a dataset of 786 filtered instances, which is then used to evaluate the cooperative behavior of UnifiedQA and GPT-3 models. The evaluation involves measuring the models' propensity to cooperate in the generated scenarios, with larger instruct-tuned GPT-3 models showing more cooperative behavior.

## Key Results
- Both humans and models struggle to create and evaluate scenarios that accurately reflect the intended game-theoretic structures, with high rejection rates (41.4% for human-generated and 52.2% for model-generated) and false positive rates (16.7% for human-verified and 25.7% for model-verified).
- Larger instruct-tuned GPT-3 models tend to exhibit more cooperative behavior, while other models show flat scaling trends.
- The quality of generated scenarios is generally mediocre, highlighting the difficulty of generating structured evaluation data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The authors acknowledge several significant limitations in their study. First, they note that the proposed method for evaluating cooperativeness through human- or model-generated scenarios may not be comprehensive, as some scenarios could be more challenging than others and may not fully capture the intended game-theoretic structures. The quality of generated scenarios was generally mediocre, with high rejection rates (41.4% for human-generated and 52.2% for model-generated) and false positive rates (16.7% for human-verified and 25.7% for model-verified) in the verification process. This suggests that both humans and models struggle to create and evaluate scenarios that accurately reflect the intended game-theoretic structures. Additionally, the authors emphasize that their dataset, while the largest to date for evaluating cooperative behavior in PLMs, is still relatively small compared to the complexity of the problem. The study also notes that language models may have difficulty with certain game-theoretic structures, such as positive reciprocity in the dictator game, which could be attributed to the ambiguity of the prompt. Finally, the authors acknowledge that their evaluation focuses on a limited set of games and may not capture the full range of cooperative behaviors exhibited by language models.

## Limitations
- The proposed method for evaluating cooperativeness may not be comprehensive, as some scenarios could be more challenging than others and may not fully capture the intended game-theoretic structures.
- The quality of generated scenarios is generally mediocre, with high rejection rates and false positive rates in the verification process.
- The dataset, while the largest to date for evaluating cooperative behavior in PLMs, is still relatively small compared to the complexity of the problem.
- Language models may have difficulty with certain game-theoretic structures, such as positive reciprocity in the dictator game, which could be attributed to the ambiguity of the prompt.
