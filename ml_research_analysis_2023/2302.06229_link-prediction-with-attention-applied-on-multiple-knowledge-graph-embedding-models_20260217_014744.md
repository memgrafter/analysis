# Link Prediction with Attention Applied on Multiple Knowledge Graph Embedding Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06229
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06229
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: A novel approach combining multiple KGE models using spherical geometry and attention outperforms individual models, especially in low-dimensional settings, achieving significant improvements in Hits@1 and MRR metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for link prediction in knowledge graphs by combining multiple knowledge graph embedding (KGE) models using spherical geometry and attention mechanisms. The approach addresses the limitation that different KGE models excel at capturing different types of relational and structural patterns. By learning to focus on the most suitable model for each query through attention, the method effectively captures heterogeneous patterns like symmetry, antisymmetry, and hierarchy. The combined embeddings are projected onto the Poincaré ball to handle structural patterns, resulting in state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks, particularly in low-dimensional settings.

## Method Summary
The proposed method combines query representations from multiple KGE models (TransE, DistMult, ComplEx, RotatE, and AttE/AttH) using spherical geometry and attention mechanisms. For each query, the method creates hyperspheres with centers at the query embeddings from different models and radii determined during training. The convex hull formed by these centers contains most plausible answers, allowing the combined model to leverage the strengths of each individual model. An attention mechanism learns per-relation weights to dynamically select which KGE model's query representation to emphasize. The combined query embeddings are then projected onto the Poincaré ball using the exponential map, enabling the model to capture hierarchical structural patterns through hyperbolic geometry. The method is trained using standard KGE loss functions while optimizing both the embeddings and attention weights.

## Key Results
- The SEPA model outperforms individual KGE models and state-of-the-art methods on standard benchmarks (WN18RR, FB15k-237, NELL-995-h100)
- Significant improvements in low-dimensional settings (embedding dimension 32) with 10-20% relative gains in Hits@1
- The method effectively handles diverse relational patterns, showing superior performance on both symmetric and antisymmetric relations
- Attention mechanism successfully learns to select the most appropriate model for different relation types

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Combining query representations from multiple KGE models through spherical geometry and attention enables the model to capture heterogeneous relational and structural patterns more effectively than individual models.
- **Mechanism:** The approach creates hyperspheres for each model's query representation, with centers at the query embeddings and radii determined during training. The convex hull formed by these centers contains most plausible answers, allowing the combined model to leverage the strengths of each individual model.
- **Core assumption:** The optimal embedding for an answer to a query lies within the convex hull of the query embeddings given by different models.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our combination uses attention to select the most suitable model to answer each query"
  - [section 3] "Proposition 3.1. Let 𝒒1 and 𝒒2 be two query embeddings for a query 𝑞. Then, the following two statements are equivalent for every vector 𝒂 in the vector space: 𝒂 = argmin𝒆 (𝑝 (𝒒1, 𝒆) + 𝑝 (𝒒2, 𝒆)), 𝒂 lies in the convex hull of vectors 𝒒1 and 𝒒2."
- **Break condition:** If the query representations from different models are too dissimilar or incompatible, the convex hull may not contain optimal answers, reducing the effectiveness of the combination.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The attention mechanism allows the model to focus on the most suitable query representation for each specific relation, improving performance for both symmetric and antisymmetric patterns.
- **Mechanism:** Attention weights are learned per relation type, allowing the model to dynamically select which KGE model's query representation to emphasize based on the characteristics of the relation in the query.
- **Core assumption:** Different KGE models are better suited for different types of relational patterns, and attention can effectively learn to select the appropriate model for each relation.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Our combination uses attention to select the most suitable model to answer each query"
  - [section 3] "Figure 3: Comparison between the importance given by each model to a symmetric (in green) and antisymmetric (in blue) relation"
- **Break condition:** If the attention mechanism fails to learn meaningful weights or if relations are too mixed in their patterns, the attention-based selection may not provide significant benefits over uniform averaging.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Projecting the combined model onto the Poincaré ball enables effective capture of hierarchical structural patterns in addition to relational patterns.
- **Mechanism:** The combined query embeddings are projected from Euclidean space to the Poincaré ball using the exponential map, allowing the model to leverage hyperbolic geometry's ability to represent hierarchical structures.
- **Core assumption:** Hyperbolic geometry is better suited for representing hierarchical structures than Euclidean geometry.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The models are also mapped onto a non-Euclidean manifold, the Poincaré ball, to capture structural patterns, such as hierarchies, besides relational patterns, such as symmetry."
  - [section 3] "We compute the score as 𝑝 (𝑞, 𝑎) = 𝑑 (𝒒𝑀 𝐸 ⊕ 𝒓, 𝒂) , where 𝒉, 𝒓, 𝒕, 𝒒 are points on a manifold M, exp0 (·) is the exponential map from origin, and ⊕ is Möbius addition."
- **Break condition:** If the dataset lacks significant hierarchical patterns, the benefits of hyperbolic projection may be minimal, and the additional complexity may not be justified.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Triple → Query split → Multiple KGE representations → Spherical embeddings → Attention-weighted combination → Poincaré projection (SEPA) → Scoring → Loss computation

- **Critical path:** Triple → Query split → Multiple KGE representations → Spherical embeddings → Attention-weighted combination → Poincaré projection (SEPA) → Scoring → Loss computation

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Combining multiple models increases expressiveness but also computational complexity
  - Using attention allows dynamic selection but requires learning additional parameters
  - Poincaré ball projection captures hierarchy but may not be necessary for all datasets

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor performance on specific relation types may indicate attention weights are not learning correctly
  - Degraded performance compared to individual models could suggest incompatibility between KGE representations
  - Convergence issues might indicate problems with the loss function or optimization process

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement and test individual KGE models (TransE, RotatE, etc.) on a small dataset to establish baseline performance
  2. Implement the spherical embedding and attention mechanism without Poincaré projection, combining two KGE models
  3. Add Poincaré ball projection and test on a dataset with known hierarchical patterns (e.g., WN18RR)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How would the proposed model perform if it combined embeddings from non-KGE models, such as language models or graph neural networks, alongside traditional KGE models?
- **Open Question 2:** How does the attention mechanism handle situations where multiple models perform equally well for a given query, and what are the implications for model performance?
- **Open Question 3:** What is the impact of the choice of geometric space (Euclidean, Hyperbolic, etc.) on the model's ability to capture different types of relational and structural patterns?

## Limitations
- **Computational overhead**: Combining 5 KGE models with attention and Poincaré projection significantly increases training and inference time compared to individual models
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The method introduces additional hyperparameters (attention weights, radius parameters, projection settings) that may require careful tuning for different datasets
- **Limited model diversity**: The study focuses on a specific set of KGE models; performance with other model combinations remains unexplored

## Confidence
**Medium-High**: The paper presents a theoretically grounded approach with clear mechanisms for combining multiple KGE models. The mathematical foundations (convex hull properties, attention mechanisms, and Poincaré ball projection) are well-established. However, the empirical validation, while showing improvements, lacks statistical significance testing across runs.

## Next Checks
1. **Statistical significance testing**: Perform multiple runs with different random seeds to establish confidence intervals for performance improvements
2. **Ablation on model diversity**: Test the method with different combinations of KGE models (e.g., excluding TransE or adding newer models like TuckER) to assess sensitivity to model selection
3. **Scalability analysis**: Evaluate performance and training time on larger datasets (e.g., YAGO3-10) to assess practical utility beyond benchmark datasets
