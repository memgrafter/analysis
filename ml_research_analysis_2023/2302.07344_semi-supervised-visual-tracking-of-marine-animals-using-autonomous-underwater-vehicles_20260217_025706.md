# Semi-Supervised Visual Tracking of Marine Animals using Autonomous Underwater Vehicles

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07344
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07344
- Reference count: 40
- We present VMAT dataset with 33 sequences of 17 marine animal types and evaluate 13 semi-supervised trackers for underwater AUV tracking applications.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces VMAT, a novel dataset for semi-supervised visual tracking of marine animals in underwater environments. The dataset contains 33 sequences across 17 marine animal types with dense annotations for benchmarking. The authors evaluate 13 state-of-the-art semi-supervised tracking algorithms on this dataset and demonstrate real-world performance using an autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) to autonomously track marine animals. Their results show that online discriminative trackers, particularly KeepTrackFast, achieve the highest accuracy, while transformer-based methods excel in midwater environments.

## Method Summary
The authors developed VMAT (Visual Marine Animal Tracking) dataset containing 33 video sequences across 17 marine animal types captured in diverse underwater habitats. They evaluated 13 semi-supervised visual tracking algorithms including discriminative online methods (MDNet, SiamFC, ECO), transformer-based approaches (TransT, KeepTrack, KeepTrackFast), and other state-of-the-art methods. The evaluation was conducted using both offline benchmarking on the VMAT dataset and online testing with an actual AUV platform tracking marine animals in real-world conditions. The study provides comprehensive performance metrics including success rate, precision, and normalized precision across different marine habitats and behavior types.

## Key Results
- KeepTrackFast achieved the highest tracking accuracy among evaluated semi-supervised trackers on the VMAT dataset
- Transformer-based methods showed superior performance in midwater environments compared to other habitats
- Online discriminative trackers outperformed other approaches in overall tracking accuracy
- Real-world AUV deployment successfully tracked 6 marine animals across different species and behaviors

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of semi-supervised visual tracking for marine animals relies on the combination of pre-trained models that can generalize across diverse underwater species and the ability to adapt to specific tracking targets through limited labeled data. Online discriminative trackers maintain templates of the target appearance and update them during tracking, allowing adaptation to changes in pose, lighting, and partial occlusions common in underwater environments. Transformer-based methods leverage self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies and handle occlusions better than traditional correlation-based approaches. The semi-supervised nature allows the system to benefit from both labeled training data and unlabeled frames during tracking, improving robustness to the challenging conditions of underwater visual tracking.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Camera -> Preprocessing -> Feature Extraction -> Tracking Algorithm -> Pose Estimation -> Control System -> Actuators

Critical path: Camera captures video frames → Preprocessing (color correction, enhancement) → Feature extraction (CNN/Transformer) → Target localization → Pose estimation → Control commands to AUV

Design tradeoffs: The study balances tracking accuracy against computational efficiency for real-time AUV operation. Online discriminative trackers offer good accuracy with moderate computational cost, while transformer methods provide better handling of occlusions but require more processing power. The semi-supervised approach reduces annotation burden but may sacrifice some precision compared to fully supervised methods.

Failure signatures: Tracking failures typically manifest as template drift (when appearance changes exceed update model capacity), complete target loss (when target exits frame or becomes severely occluded), and mislocalization (when background features are incorrectly associated with the target). Underwater-specific failures include tracking failure due to backscatter, color distortion affecting feature matching, and performance degradation in low-light conditions.

## Limitations
- Limited environmental diversity with dataset collected from only two geographic locations (HIMB and Moku o Lo'e)
- Small sample size for midwater sequences (n=4) limiting conclusions about transformer performance in this habitat
- Limited real-world testing with only 6 tracked animals during AUV deployment
- Absence of established baselines specifically for underwater transformer tracking

## Confidence
- **High** confidence in overall tracking accuracy measurements due to dense annotations and standardized protocols
- **Medium** confidence in habitat-specific performance conclusions due to limited sequences per habitat type
- **Low** confidence in transformer performance claims for midwater environments due to small sample size
- **Medium** confidence in real-world AUV deployment results due to limited number of tracked animals
- **High** confidence in dataset's marine animal diversity representation
- **Low** confidence in dataset's environmental variability comprehensiveness
