# All Roads Lead to Rome? Exploring the Invariance of Transformers' Representations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.14555
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14555
- **Reference count:** 8
- **Primary result:** BERT-INN achieves significantly lower L2 distances than existing bijective methods on both synthetic and real NLP datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether transformer models learn isomorphic or seed-sensitive representation spaces. The authors propose the Bijection Hypothesis, suggesting that bijective methods can align different models' representation spaces. They introduce BERT-INN, an invertible neural network-based method using affine coupling layers, which demonstrates superior performance in aligning BERT embeddings compared to existing methods like CCA. The work provides insights into representation invariance across different transformer models and layers.

## Method Summary
The authors propose BERT-INN, an invertible neural network that uses affine coupling layers to learn bijective mappings between BERT embeddings from different models. The method is designed to align representation spaces more effectively than existing approaches. The architecture employs multiple affine coupling layers to capture complex transformations between embeddings. The approach is evaluated on both synthetic data and real NLP datasets (MNLI, SST-2, MRPC) using L2 distance as the primary alignment metric.

## Key Results
- BERT-INN achieves significantly lower L2 distances (0.0059) than other bijective methods (0.1396-0.3060) on synthetic data
- BERT-INN shows superior performance on real datasets, with 0.0461 L2 distance compared to 0.1534 for CCA
- Shallower layers are more invariant than deeper layers across different models
- Attention weights are more invariant than representations
- Finetuning increases variance in representation spaces

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BERT-INN's superior performance stems from its use of affine coupling layers, which can capture complex non-linear transformations between representation spaces. The invertible nature ensures that the mapping is bijective, allowing for precise alignment. The architecture's depth and configuration of coupling layers enable it to model intricate relationships between different BERT embeddings, outperforming simpler bijective methods that cannot capture such complexity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Invertible Neural Networks:** Bijective mappings that preserve information and enable precise alignment
  - Why needed: To establish one-to-one correspondence between representation spaces
  - Quick check: Verify invertibility through reconstruction accuracy

- **Affine Coupling Layers:** Components that split input, transform one part using the other, then recombine
  - Why needed: To build expressive yet invertible transformations
  - Quick check: Confirm coupling layer operations preserve invertibility

- **Representation Invariance:** The degree to which different models learn similar representations
  - Why needed: To understand generalization and transferability across models
  - Quick check: Measure alignment quality using L2 distance

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Input Embeddings -> Affine Coupling Layers (multiple) -> Output Embeddings

**Critical Path:** The sequence of affine coupling layers forms the core transformation pipeline, with each layer building upon the previous one's output to progressively align the representation spaces.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The method trades computational complexity for alignment accuracy. More coupling layers increase expressiveness but also computational cost and training time.

**Failure Signatures:** Poor alignment manifests as high L2 distances between mapped representations, indicating the model fails to learn effective bijective mappings.

## Limitations
- Focus on L2 distance may not fully capture semantic alignment quality
- Performance on datasets with more diverse linguistic phenomena remains unclear
- Computational overhead compared to simpler methods like CCA is not discussed in detail

## Confidence
- **High:** Claims about layer-wise invariance and attention representation stability are consistent across multiple experiments
- **Medium:** BERT-INN's superiority over existing methods is demonstrated but may depend on specific experimental conditions and metrics
- **Medium:** Generalizability to other transformer architectures and tasks requires further validation

## Next Checks

2. Test the method on multilingual BERT variants and compare invariance patterns across languages
