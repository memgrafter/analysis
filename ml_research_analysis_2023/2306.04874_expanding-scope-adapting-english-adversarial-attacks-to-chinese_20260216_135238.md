# Expanding Scope: Adapting English Adversarial Attacks to Chinese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04874
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04874
- Reference count: 38
- This paper adapts state-of-the-art adversarial attack methods from English to Chinese, demonstrating high success rates when combined with proper text segmentation and linguistic constraints.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the adaptation of English adversarial attack methods to Chinese NLP models. The authors propose four attack strategies—Open HowNet, Masked Language Model, Homophone, and Morphonym transformations—along with a composite attack that integrates all four. Experiments on Chinese sentiment classification and entailment models show that these attacks achieve high success rates (up to 98.16%) while maintaining fluency and semantic consistency. Human evaluation confirms the quality of the generated adversarial examples, and adversarial training significantly improves model robustness, reducing attack success rates by up to 49.32%.

## Method Summary
The authors adapt English adversarial attack methods to Chinese by combining them with proper text segmentation (using Jieba) and linguistic constraints. They introduce four attack strategies: Open HowNet for synonym replacement, Masked Language Model for contextually appropriate substitutions, Homophone for pronunciation-based replacements, and Morphonym for character component-based substitutions. A composite attack integrates all four methods. The approach uses greedy search with word importance ranking to select words for perturbation, applying linguistic constraints (stop word modification, repeat modification, MUSE similarity) to filter candidates. The method is evaluated on Chinese sentiment classification and entailment tasks using BERT and RoBERTa models.

## Key Results
- The adapted attack methods achieve high success rates of up to 98.16% on Chinese sentiment classification and entailment models.
- Human evaluation confirms that the generated adversarial examples maintain fluency and semantic consistency.
- Adversarial training with successfully attacked examples significantly improves model robustness, reducing attack success rates by up to 49.32%.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adapting English adversarial attack methods to Chinese works when combined with proper text segmentation and linguistic constraints.
- Mechanism: The attack methods search for word substitutions that fool the model while maintaining fluency and semantic consistency. Text segmentation ensures that multi-character Chinese words are treated as units rather than being split into individual characters, preventing out-of-context substitutions. Linguistic constraints (stop word modification, repeat modification, MUSE similarity) filter out undesirable perturbations.
- Core assumption: The Chinese language's morphology and phonology can be leveraged to create high-quality adversarial examples while preserving readability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our experiments show that attack methods previously applied to English NLP can generate high-quality adversarial examples in Chinese when combined with proper text segmentation and linguistic constraints."
  - [section] "We use a Chinese segmentation tool provided by Jieba to segment an input text into a list of words. This means we obtain perturbed text x′ by replacing some xi with its synonym x′i."
- Break condition: If text segmentation fails to properly identify word boundaries, or if linguistic constraints are too restrictive or too permissive, the quality of generated adversarial examples will degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Chinese-specific transformations (homophone and morphonym) significantly improve attack success rates compared to English-adapted methods.
- Mechanism: Homophone transformations replace characters with others that share the same pronunciation (Pinyin), while morphonym transformations replace characters with others sharing similar pictographic components. These transformations exploit Chinese language characteristics to create perturbations that are semantically consistent for human readers but can fool NLP models.
- Core assumption: The phonological and morphological similarities in Chinese characters can be used to create effective adversarial examples that maintain human readability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We introduce morphonym and homophone word-substitution attacks that are specific to the Chinese language; they function as a benchmark to the English adapted attack methods."
  - [section] "Because there exist many homophones in modern Chinese, the same spoken syllable may map to one of many characters with different meanings... Replacing Chinese characters with the same pronunciation may serve as an additional attack method to test the adversarial robustness of NLP models while preserving the semantics for human readers."
- Break condition: If the homophone or morphonym substitutions are too obvious or the NLP model becomes robust to these specific types of transformations, the attack success rates will decrease.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adversarial training with generated adversarial examples significantly improves model robustness against attacks.
- Mechanism: By finetuning the target model on successfully attacked examples, the model learns to recognize and correctly classify adversarial inputs, reducing the attack success rate in subsequent evaluations.
- Core assumption: The adversarial examples generated by the attack methods are representative of the types of perturbations the model will encounter in real-world scenarios.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In addition, we demonstrate that adversarially trained models significantly decrease attack success rate by up to 49.32%."
  - [section] "To evaluate how adversarial examples generated by the attack methods could improve the adversarial robustness of a target model, we attacked the target BERT model... and finetune the target model with the successfully attacked examples."
- Break condition: If the adversarial training set is not diverse enough or if the model overfits to the specific attack patterns, the robustness gains may be limited or temporary.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text segmentation (Jieba) -> Word importance ranking (greedy search) -> Synonym generation (Open HowNet, MLM, Homophone, Morphonym) -> Language constraints (stop word, repeat modification, MUSE) -> Victim models (BERT, RoBERTa) -> Datasets (online shopping reviews, Chinanews)

- Critical path:
  1. Segment input text into words
  2. Rank words by importance
  3. For each important word, generate synonym candidates using all four transformation methods
  4. Apply language constraints to filter candidates
  5. Select the best substitution that maximizes the score function
  6. Check if the model's prediction changes
  7. If not, repeat with the next important word

- Design tradeoffs:
  - High attack success rate vs. maintaining fluency and semantic consistency
  - Using English-adapted methods vs. developing Chinese-specific transformations
  - Comprehensive constraints vs. allowing more diverse perturbations

- Failure signatures:
  - Low attack success rate despite high perturbation percentage (constraints too restrictive)
  - High attack success rate but poor human evaluation scores (constraints too permissive)
  - Segmented words being split incorrectly, leading to out-of-context substitutions
  - Model becoming robust to specific attack patterns after adversarial training

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the Open HowNet transformation method on a small subset of the online shopping review dataset to verify it generates semantically similar word substitutions.
  2. Evaluate the homophone transformation on a set of Chinese characters with multiple pronunciations to ensure it selects appropriate substitutions based on context.
  3. Perform a pilot adversarial training experiment with the BERT model using 100 successfully attacked examples to measure the initial impact on robustness.

## Limitations
- The study focuses on two specific tasks (sentiment classification and entailment) and may not generalize to other Chinese NLP applications.
- The human evaluation sample size is not specified, raising questions about statistical significance.
- The paper doesn't address computational efficiency or the scalability of the attack methods to larger datasets or models.

## Confidence
- High confidence: The general approach of combining English-adapted methods with Chinese-specific transformations (homophone, morphonym) is sound and supported by linguistic theory. The framework for integrating text segmentation and linguistic constraints is well-established in Chinese NLP.
- Medium confidence: The specific implementation details and hyperparameter choices (e.g., MUSE similarity thresholds, MLM top-k predictions) are not fully specified, which may affect reproducibility. The human evaluation methodology for assessing fluency and semantic consistency lacks detail.
- Low confidence: The long-term effectiveness of adversarial training on Chinese models is uncertain, as the paper doesn't address potential catastrophic forgetting or the need for continuous retraining as new attack methods emerge.
