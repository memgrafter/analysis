# Dual-sPLS: a family of Dual Sparse Partial Least Squares regressions for feature selection and prediction with tunable sparsity; evaluation on simulated and near-infrared (NIR) data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07206
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07206
- Reference count: 40
- Dual-sPLS achieves competitive prediction accuracy while providing interpretable feature selection for chemometric applications

## Executive Summary
Dual-sPLS introduces a family of sparse partial least squares regression methods that combine dimension reduction with variable selection for chemometric applications. The method uses dual norms (inspired by lasso, group lasso, least squares, and ridge regression) to enforce sparsity in PLS weight vectors, enabling interpretable feature localization alongside accurate prediction. Evaluated on both simulated data (Gaussian mixtures) and real near-infrared spectroscopy data, Dual-sPLS variants achieve competitive or superior prediction accuracy compared to standard PLS, lasso, and other sparse PLS methods, while providing sparser and more interpretable regression coefficients.

## Method Summary
Dual-sPLS is a family of sparse PLS regression methods that use dual norms to enforce sparsity in weight vectors during the PLS iteration. The core algorithm computes weight vectors under different norm constraints (pseudo-lasso, pseudo-least squares, pseudo-ridge) with an adaptive shrinking ratio parameter that controls sparsity by thresholding variables based on the empirical distribution of |XTy|. The method handles both invertible and singular design matrices through appropriate norm selection, and includes deflation steps to compute multiple latent components. The regression coefficients are derived from the sparse weight vectors and can be evaluated using standard metrics (RMSE, MAE, R²) while providing interpretable feature localization.

## Key Results
- Dual-sPLS pseudo-lasso and pseudo-ridge variants achieve lower RMSE and MAE than standard PLS and lasso on NIR spectroscopy data
- The method provides sparser regression coefficients (lower ℓ0 norm) while maintaining or improving prediction accuracy
- Sparse coefficients localize to chemically meaningful regions of the NIR spectrum, enhancing interpretability
- The shrinking ratio parameter allows intuitive control of sparsity without sacrificing predictive performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dual-sPLS achieves both high prediction accuracy and interpretable feature localization by combining dimension reduction (PLS) with sparse penalties via dual norms.
- Mechanism: The algorithm optimizes PLS weight vectors under a dual norm constraint, which allows explicit sparsity control. This enables simultaneous reduction of dimensionality and selection of relevant features, yielding interpretable coefficients without sacrificing predictive performance.
- Core assumption: The dual norm framework can be solved analytically for the specified penalties, and the resulting sparse PLS models generalize well to unseen data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Dual-sPLS favorably compares to similar regression methods, on simulated and real chemical data."
  - [section] "This formally provides a unified formulation for regression methods in the spirit of the lasso mentioned above, and also least-squares or ridge, all blended in a PLS formalism."
- Break condition: If the dual norm optimization becomes intractable or the sparsity parameter selection fails, the model may overfit or lose interpretability.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The shrinking ratio parameter allows intuitive control of sparsity, leading to chemically meaningful feature localization.
- Mechanism: By adaptively selecting a threshold based on the empirical cumulative distribution of |XTy|, Dual-sPLS ensures that a user-defined proportion of variables is set to zero. This targets sparsity directly at the weight vector, which propagates to the regression coefficients.
- Core assumption: The distribution of |XTy| is a good proxy for variable importance, and the chosen shrinking ratio aligns with chemically relevant features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We obtain it adaptively, according to the proportion of variables that we would like to keep in the active set at each iteration."
  - [section] "The rationale behind constrainting the direction w instead of the regression coefficients β is their collinearity."
- Break condition: If the empirical distribution is not representative (e.g., in highly correlated data), the shrinking ratio may select irrelevant features or miss important ones.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Dual-sPLS can handle both invertible and singular design matrices by offering different norm options (lasso, least squares, ridge).
- Mechanism: The method uses appropriate regularization (e.g., ridge-like penalties) when X is singular, ensuring the optimization remains solvable. This flexibility allows application to a wider range of chemometric datasets.
- Core assumption: The choice of norm and regularization parameter can be adapted to the data's invertibility status without harming predictive performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "When X is singular, the above cannot hold. Meanwhile, this case can be addressed with a regularization inspired by the ridge [17]."
  - [section] "Dual-sPLSLS requires data to be represented by a non-singular matrixX, as explained in Section 3.2.3."
- Break condition: If the regularization is too weak (for singular X) or too strong (for invertible X), the model may either fail to converge or oversmooth the coefficients.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data preprocessing (mean-centering, Savitzky-Golay filtering) -> Core algorithm (Dual-sPLS variants) -> Hyperparameter tuning (shrinking ratio, components) -> Evaluation (RMSE, MAE, R², ℓ0) -> Comparison to baselines

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess data (centering, optional smoothing)
  2. For each component: compute weight vector under chosen dual norm, apply adaptive sparsity threshold, update X via deflation
  3. Compute regression coefficients
  4. Evaluate and tune hyperparameters
  5. Compare to baselines

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sparsity vs. predictive accuracy: higher sparsity may reduce interpretability but hurt prediction
  - Norm choice: lasso for variable selection, ridge for handling singularity, least squares for non-singular data
  - Shrinking ratio: higher ratio → sparser model, but risk of missing important features

- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: poor validation performance despite good calibration
  - Underfitting: high error on both calibration and validation
  - Non-convergence: failure to find sparse weight vectors
  - Poor interpretability: sparse coefficients not located in chemically relevant regions

## Limitations
- The paper lacks explicit sensitivity analysis for hyperparameter selection, particularly for the shrinking ratio and number of latent components.
- While Dual-sPLS is evaluated on NIR data, the method's generalizability to other high-dimensional chemometric applications remains untested.
- The adaptive thresholding based on |XTy| distribution assumes that variable importance correlates with this measure, which may not hold in all scenarios.

## Confidence
- High: Dual-sPLS framework and algorithm (Sections 3.1-3.3) - well-defined and reproducible.
- Medium: Prediction accuracy claims (RMSE, MAE, R²) - supported by simulated and real data, but lack of hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.
- Low: Interpretability claims - sparse coefficients are shown, but chemical relevance is not explicitly validated.
