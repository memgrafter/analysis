# Adaptive Knowledge Distillation between Text and Speech Pre-trained Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03600
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03600
- Reference count: 0
- Proposes Prior-informed Adaptive knowledge Distillation (PAD) to align text and speech pre-trained models, achieving better performance on SLU tasks than other metric-based distillation approaches.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of transferring linguistic knowledge from text pre-trained models to speech pre-trained models for spoken language understanding tasks. The proposed Prior-informed Adaptive knowledge Distillation (PAD) method aligns text-speech representations at adaptive granularity, informed by prior knowledge reflecting semantic significance. By combining Attention-based Significance Priors (ASP) and Anchor-based Adaptive Span Aggregation (AASA), PAD achieves better global and local alignments between text and speech pre-trained models, outperforming other metric-based distillation approaches on three spoken language understanding benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The method proposes a metric-based distillation approach that aligns text and speech embedding spaces through adaptive granularity alignment. It uses Attention-based Significance Priors (ASP) to extract semantic significance from self-attention maps, weighting the alignment process. Anchor-based Adaptive Span Aggregation (AASA) generates multi-scale speech spans to narrow the granularity gap between speech tokens and text tokens. The method is trained on 10 hours of Librispeech data and evaluated on Fluent Speech Commands (Intent Classification), IEMOCAP (Emotion Recognition), and Audio SNIPS (Slot Filling) benchmarks using wav2vec2-base and bert-base-uncased models.

## Key Results
- PAD outperforms other metric-based distillation approaches on three SLU benchmarks
- The method achieves better global and local alignments between text and speech pre-trained models
- ASP and AASA components effectively address semantic and granularity gaps between modalities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Attention-based Significance Priors (ASP) for Semantic Alignment
ASP helps align speech and text embeddings by weighting tokens based on their semantic significance, reducing noise from irrelevant features like blank or noise signals. It extracts significance scores from self-attention maps of pre-trained transformer models, using these scores to weight the alignment loss. This ensures that only the most semantically relevant tokens/frames are aligned, ignoring non-informative ones.

### Mechanism 2: Anchor-based Adaptive Span Aggregation (AASA) for Granularity Alignment
AASA narrows the granularity gap between speech and text representations by adaptively generating local spans, where each span is aggregated by several speech embeddings. It uses anchor points selected based on their significance scores to generate local spans of varying scales. Each span pool consists of several spans, and the alignment is performed between text tokens and these span pools, maximizing the sum of maximum text-span similarities.

### Mechanism 3: Prior-informed Adaptive Knowledge Distillation (PAD)
PAD combines ASP and AASA to achieve better global and local alignments between text and speech pre-trained models. It applies ASP to both global and local alignments to focus on semantically relevant tokens/frames. For local alignments, it uses AASA to generate adaptive spans, narrowing the granularity gap. The method is evaluated on three spoken language understanding benchmarks, showing superior performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Speech module (wav2vec2-base) -> ASP for significance extraction -> AASA for span aggregation -> Text module (bert-base-uncased) -> Global and local alignment losses -> SLU downstream tasks

- **Critical path**: 1. Extract significance scores from self-attention maps using ASP 2. Generate adaptive spans using AASA 3. Perform global and local alignments, incorporating ASP and AASA 4. Evaluate the method on SLU benchmarks

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using ASP vs. traditional methods like inverse document frequency (idf), Adaptive spans vs. fixed spans in AASA, Global vs. local alignments, and their combination

- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on SLU benchmarks, High alignment loss during training, Incompatibility between global and local alignments

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Evaluate the impact of ASP on global and local alignments separately 2. Assess the effectiveness of AASA in narrowing the granularity gap 3. Test the combined performance of ASP and AASA in PAD on SLU tasks

## Limitations

- Implementation details for critical components like ASP layer selection and AASA hyperparameters are unspecified
- Evaluation is limited to English benchmarks, with no testing on multilingual or cross-lingual tasks
- Computational overhead of adaptive components is not analyzed

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Core contribution of addressing granularity and semantic gaps through adaptive alignment is well-supported
- **Medium Confidence**: Superiority over baseline methods demonstrated but could benefit from more ablation studies
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about ASP's noise-filtering capability are not empirically validated
