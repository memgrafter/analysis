# Erasure of Unaligned Attributes from Neural Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02997
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02997
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: AMSAL algorithm achieves effective bias reduction while preserving task performance on multiple datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces AMSAL (Assignment-Maximization Spectral Attribute removaL), an algorithm for removing guarded attributes from neural representations when those attributes are not perfectly aligned with each input instance. The method addresses a critical gap in bias mitigation by handling unaligned attribute scenarios through an alternating optimization approach. Experiments demonstrate significant reductions in bias metrics across multiple datasets while maintaining main task performance, with particular success on sentiment analysis and profession classification tasks.

## Method Summary
AMSAL operates through an alternating optimization framework that alternates between finding optimal alignment between input representations and guarded attributes, then projecting both into a joint latent space to maximize covariance. The algorithm consists of two main steps: (1) an alignment step that discovers relationships between representations and attributes, and (2) a projection step that maximizes covariance in a learned latent space. This approach is specifically designed to handle scenarios where protected attributes are not perfectly aligned with each input instance, making it more applicable to real-world situations where attribute information may be incomplete or noisy.

## Key Results
- On Twitter sentiment dataset, AMSAL reduced gender and age prediction gaps with minimal impact on sentiment prediction accuracy
- On BiasBios dataset, reduced True Positive Rate gap by up to 0.13 while maintaining profession classification accuracy
- Method effectiveness depends on separability of guarded attributes from main task labels, with degraded performance when highly entangled

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
AMSAL's effectiveness stems from its ability to jointly optimize attribute alignment and latent space projection through alternating maximization. By finding optimal mappings between representations and attributes before projection, the method can more effectively separate protected attributes from task-relevant information in the learned space.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Input representations → Alignment step → Joint latent space projection → Output representations

**Critical path:** The alignment step directly impacts the quality of the subsequent projection, making it critical for overall performance. Poor alignment leads to ineffective attribute removal.

**Design tradeoffs:** AMSAL trades computational complexity for improved handling of unaligned attributes compared to simpler projection-based methods. The alternating optimization increases runtime but provides better performance on challenging alignment scenarios.

**Failure signatures:** When guarded attributes are highly entangled with main task labels, AMSAL shows degraded performance and may fail to effectively remove protected attributes without harming task accuracy.

**Three first experiments:** 1) Test on synthetic data with known attribute alignment properties, 2) Evaluate convergence speed across different dataset sizes, 3) Compare performance against baseline methods on simple aligned attribute scenarios

## Limitations
- Effectiveness strongly depends on separability of guarded attributes from main task labels
- Experimental scope limited to specific bias types (gender, age, race)
- Computational efficiency and scalability not thoroughly examined
