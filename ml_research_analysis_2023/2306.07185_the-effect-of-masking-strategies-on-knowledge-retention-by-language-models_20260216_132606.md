# The Effect of Masking Strategies on Knowledge Retention by Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07185
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07185
- Reference count: 12
- Key outcome: Salient span masking (+9.4%) and PMI masking (+8.9%) both outperform random token masking (+4.4%) in knowledge retention

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how different pre-training masking strategies affect factual knowledge retention in language models. The authors train T5-small models on Wikipedia passages with three masking strategies (random token masking, salient span masking, and PMI masking) and evaluate knowledge retention using QA pairs from the PAQ dataset. Their results show that PMI and salient span masking significantly outperform random masking in knowledge retention. They also demonstrate that catastrophic forgetting occurs during fine-tuning for downstream tasks, but this can be mitigated using elastic weight consolidation or multi-task learning, with multi-task learning showing slightly better performance.

## Method Summary
The paper uses a three-step process: (1) additional pre-training on Wikipedia passages using one of three masking strategies (random token masking, salient span masking, or PMI masking) with cross-entropy loss, (2) CBQA fine-tuning on QA pairs with cross-entropy loss, and (3) testing on hold-out QA pairs to measure knowledge retention. The authors use T5-small models and the PAQ dataset containing 10K Wikipedia passages with 49K QA pairs. They evaluate performance using exact match and F1 scores, and test mitigation strategies for catastrophic forgetting including elastic weight consolidation (with regularization strength λ=1000) and multi-task learning.

## Key Results
- Salient span masking outperforms random token masking by 9.4% in exact match scores
- PMI masking outperforms random token masking by 8.9% in exact match scores
- Both EWC and multi-task learning significantly mitigate catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PMI masking outperforms random token masking in knowledge retention by capturing contextual relationships between frequently co-located tokens.
- Mechanism: PMI masking jointly masks n-grams that frequently appear together in the training corpus, forcing the model to understand broader context rather than relying on local features. This approach reduces the likelihood of masking non-informative tokens and requires understanding of entity context (e.g., masking "the united states" as one token).
- Core assumption: Contextual relationships between tokens are crucial for factual knowledge retention, and masking these relationships together improves learning efficiency.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "masking entities and principled masking of correlated spans based on pointwise mutual information lead to more factual knowledge being retained than masking random tokens"
  - [section] "PMI masking jointly masks frequently co-located n-grams in the training corpus (Levine et al., 2021)"
- Break condition: If the PMI calculation doesn't accurately capture meaningful correlations in the corpus, or if the masking rate disrupts learning too severely.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Salient span masking (SSM) improves knowledge retention by prioritizing entity-related information during pre-training.
- Mechanism: SSM identifies and masks named entities first, then fills remaining masking quota with random tokens. Since QA answers are often entity-based, this creates an inductive bias toward entity-related knowledge acquisition.
- Core assumption: Entities are the primary carriers of factual knowledge in text, and focusing pre-training attention on them improves downstream knowledge retention.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "masking entities and principled masking of correlated spans based on pointwise mutual information lead to more factual knowledge being retained"
  - [section] "SSM prioritizes masking named entities (Zhang et al., 2019). As the answers in QA are mostly related to entities (e.g., 'Who is the current French president?'), SSM could have a positive inductive bias"
- Break condition: If entity identification fails or if entities are not the primary knowledge carriers in the specific domain.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) and Multi-Task Learning (MTL) prevent catastrophic forgetting by maintaining important parameters from the pre-training stage.
- Mechanism: EWC adds a regularization term based on the Fisher Information Matrix to preserve parameters important for the initial task. MTL trains on both pre-training and fine-tuning objectives simultaneously, forcing the model to maintain dual-task capabilities.
- Core assumption: Parameters important for knowledge retention can be identified and protected during fine-tuning without severely compromising downstream task performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "catastrophic forgetting occurs when models are fine-tuned for downstream tasks, but this can be mitigated using elastic weight consolidation (EWC) or multi-task learning (MTL)"
  - [section] "we apply elastic weight consolidation and multi-task learning, two common strategies to counter catastrophic forgetting. We find catastrophic forgetting of knowledge indeed to be a problem, as both applying EWC and MTL leads to substantial improvements"
- Break condition: If the regularization strength is mis-tuned, causing either insufficient protection or excessive interference with fine-tuning.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Catastrophic forgetting in sequential learning
  - Why needed here: The paper demonstrates that knowledge acquired during pre-training is lost during fine-tuning, which is a classic example of catastrophic forgetting that must be understood to appreciate the mitigation strategies.
  - Quick check question: What happens to a model's performance on task A after it's fine-tuned on task B, and why does this occur?

- Concept: Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) for token correlation
  - Why needed here: PMI masking relies on identifying tokens that frequently appear together, requiring understanding of how PMI quantifies co-occurrence strength in text corpora.
  - Quick check question: How does PMI differ from simple co-occurrence counting, and why is this distinction important for masking strategies?

- Concept: Fisher Information Matrix in parameter importance estimation
  - Why needed here: EWC uses the Fisher Information Matrix to identify which parameters are most important for the pre-training task, requiring understanding of how this matrix quantifies parameter sensitivity.
  - Quick check question: What does a high value in the Fisher Information Matrix diagonal indicate about a parameter's importance to task performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-training module (Wikipedia passages + PAQ QA pairs) -> Masking strategies (RTM, SSM, PMI) -> Knowledge infusion pipeline (cross-entropy loss) -> Fine-tuning module (CBQA training + QA pairs) -> Evaluation module (Exact match scoring on held-out QA pairs) -> Regularization modules (EWC, MTL)

- Critical path: Pre-training → CBQA fine-tuning → Evaluation → (Optional) Regularization mitigation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Masking strategy complexity vs. performance gain (PMI > SSM > RTM but with increasing computational overhead)
  - Regularization strength vs. task interference (EWC regularization strength of 1000 found experimentally optimal)
  - Joint training efficiency vs. sequential training simplicity (MTL requires careful batch scheduling)

- Failure signatures:
  - High variance between runs suggests unstable masking strategy or insufficient training epochs
  - No improvement over baseline indicates pre-training is not effectively encoding knowledge
  - Degradation during fine-tuning confirms catastrophic forgetting is occurring
  - Over-regularization shows as poor fine-tuning performance despite knowledge retention

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline comparison: Run only CBQA fine-tuning without any pre-training to establish performance floor
  2. Masking strategy comparison: Implement all three masking strategies (RTM, SSM, PMI) with identical hyperparameters to measure knowledge retention differences
  3. Regularization effectiveness: Apply EWC to the best-performing masking strategy to quantify catastrophic forgetting mitigation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different pre-training task combinations (e.g., random token masking + salient span masking vs. PMI masking + salient span masking) compare in terms of knowledge retention and downstream task performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares three masking strategies (random token masking, salient span masking, and PMI masking) and their effects on knowledge retention, but does not explore combinations of these strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on individual masking strategies and their standalone effects, without investigating potential synergies or trade-offs when combining different pre-training tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that train models using combinations of different masking strategies and comparing their performance in terms of knowledge retention and downstream task performance would provide insights into the effectiveness of task combinations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the long-term stability of knowledge retention when using elastic weight consolidation (EWC) and multi-task learning (MTL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that EWC and MTL can mitigate catastrophic forgetting of factual knowledge, but does not investigate the long-term stability of these methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on short-term effects of EWC and MTL on knowledge retention, without exploring how these methods perform over extended periods or with repeated fine-tuning cycles.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting long-term studies that repeatedly fine-tune models using EWC and MTL and measure knowledge retention over time would provide insights into the long-term stability of these methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different regularization strengths in EWC and task weighting in MTL affect the balance between retaining pre-training knowledge and adapting to downstream tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses specific regularization strengths for EWC and task weighting for MTL, but does not explore the impact of varying these hyperparameters on knowledge retention and task performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses fixed hyperparameters for EWC and MTL without investigating how different settings might affect the trade-off between retaining pre-training knowledge and adapting to downstream tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying regularization strengths for EWC and task weighting for MTL, and analyzing their effects on knowledge retention and downstream task performance, would provide insights into the optimal balance between these factors.

## Limitations
- The PAQ dataset may not represent all factual knowledge domains, potentially limiting generalizability
- Comparison between masking strategies doesn't control for pre-training compute budget
- Catastrophic forgetting analysis only examines T5-small, leaving uncertainty about scalability
- Regularization techniques lack ablation studies to determine which components are most critical

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core finding that different masking strategies produce different knowledge retention outcomes is well-supported by the experimental results. The EM and F1 score improvements for SSM (+9.4%) and PMI (+8.9%) over RTM (+4.4%) are statistically significant and reproducible.

**Medium Confidence**: The mechanism explanations for why PMI and SSM outperform RTM are plausible but not definitively proven. The relative performance of EWC vs MTL for forgetting mitigation shows clear differences, but the optimal regularization strength and training dynamics require further investigation.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about the specific reasons for masking strategy effectiveness (e.g., exact PMI calculation methodology, entity prioritization benefits) lack detailed experimental validation.
