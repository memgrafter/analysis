# A negation detection assessment of GPTs: analysis with the xNot360 dataset

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16638
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16638
- Reference count: 28
- GPT-4 achieved 0.7833 accuracy and 0.7706 F1-score on negation detection task

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates negation detection capabilities of GPT models (GPT-2, GPT-3, GPT-3.5, GPT-4) using a custom xNot360 dataset with 360 sentence pairs. Models were tested using a zero-shot prediction approach to determine if the second sentence negates the first. Results show significant performance disparities: GPT-4 achieved 0.7833 accuracy and 0.7706 F1-score, while GPT-3.5 performed poorly at 0.4306 accuracy and 0.2705 F1-score. GPT-2 achieved 0.5000 accuracy with perfect recall, and GPT-3 reached 0.6056 accuracy. The findings indicate that even advanced models struggle with negation detection, highlighting the need for improved logical reasoning capabilities in high-stakes domains like healthcare and law.

## Method Summary
The study employed a zero-shot prediction approach to evaluate GPT models' negation detection capabilities using the xNot360 dataset containing 360 sentence pairs. The dataset was constructed using logical rules to ensure effective testing of negation understanding. Models were prompted to determine if the second sentence negates the first, with performance measured using accuracy and F1-score metrics. Confusion matrices were used to analyze error patterns and identify systematic weaknesses in handling different types of negation.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieved the highest performance with 0.7833 accuracy and 0.7706 F1-score
- GPT-3.5 showed notably poor performance at 0.4306 accuracy and 0.2705 F1-score
- GPT-2 achieved 0.5000 accuracy with perfect recall but likely many false positives
- All models struggled particularly with conditional statements and complex negation structures

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-4's superior performance stems from its enhanced architectural capacity to model complex logical relationships beyond simple pattern matching.
- Mechanism: The larger model size and more sophisticated training process enable GPT-4 to better understand the logical structure of negation, particularly in conditional statements where surface-level negation cues can be misleading.
- Core assumption: Model size and training sophistication directly correlate with logical reasoning capability, not just memorization.
- Evidence anchors: GPT-4 achieved 0.7833 accuracy and 0.7706 F1-score, while GPT-3.5 performed poorly at 0.4306 accuracy and 0.2705 F1-score; The overall proficiency of the GPT models in negation detection remains relatively modest.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) can actually degrade logical reasoning performance when user preferences conflict with logical consistency.
- Mechanism: RLHF optimizes for user satisfaction rather than logical correctness, leading models to prioritize responses that "feel right" over those that are logically sound, especially in high-stakes domains.
- Core assumption: User feedback reflects preference for plausibility over logical accuracy, particularly in ambiguous contexts.
- Evidence anchors: The adage 'Bitter pills may have blessed effects' is an appropriate metaphor, suggesting that offering users more palatable information can occasionally undermine the potency of the message; constantly optimizing for user satisfaction will not always yield favorable outcomes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The xNot360 dataset's construction using logical principles exposes models' fundamental limitations in handling negation beyond surface-level pattern recognition.
- Mechanism: By creating sentence pairs based on logical negation rules rather than just adding negation words, the dataset reveals whether models understand the semantic content or merely recognize negation keywords.
- Core assumption: Logical consistency in dataset construction provides a more rigorous test of negation understanding than surface-level negation detection.
- Evidence anchors: By employing logical rules, we can at least ensure that our dataset effectively tests a model's ability to understand and handle negation in text; Conditional statements are particularly prone to errors when negated with truth table evidence.

## Limitations

- The xNot360 dataset contains only 360 sentence pairs, which may not capture the full complexity of negation in natural language
- Zero-shot evaluation approach doesn't explore whether few-shot learning or fine-tuning could substantially improve performance
- The study focuses on English language negation, and results may not translate to other languages with different negation structures
