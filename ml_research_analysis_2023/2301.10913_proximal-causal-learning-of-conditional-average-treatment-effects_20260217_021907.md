# Proximal Causal Learning of Conditional Average Treatment Effects

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the P-learner, a method for estimating heterogeneous treatment effects when unmeasured confounding is present but proxy variables are available. The approach extends the R-learner framework by using doubly robust scores based on proximal causal inference assumptions, enabling two-stage loss minimization with flexible machine learning tools. Theoretical analysis establishes oracle error bounds when using penalized kernel regression, provided nuisance components are reasonably estimated. Empirical results on simulated and real data demonstrate the method's effectiveness in recovering true treatment effects and identifying heterogeneity.

## Method Summary
The P-learner estimates heterogeneous treatment effects under unmeasured confounding by leveraging proxy variables that influence either treatment assignment or outcomes. The method uses a two-stage approach: first estimating nuisance components (bridge functions) that relate proxies to unmeasured confounders, then using doubly robust scores to estimate conditional average treatment effects. The framework allows flexible machine learning methods for both stages while maintaining statistical guarantees through cross-fitting and oracle error bounds.

## Key Results
- The P-learner achieves oracle bounds on estimated error when using penalized kernel regression for nuisance estimation
- Empirical results show the method successfully recovers true treatment effects in simulations with unmeasured confounding
- The RATE metric effectively identifies treatment heterogeneity even when ground truth CATEs are unavailable

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: The P-learner achieves an oracle bound on estimated error when using penalized kernel regression, provided nuisance components are reasonably estimated.
- **Mechanism**: The method decouples nuisance estimation (bridge functions h and q) from treatment effect estimation via a two-stage loss function. The cross-fitted doubly robust scores form pseudo-outcomes that can be regressed on covariates using flexible ML tools. The oracle bound shows the P-learner's regret matches what would be achieved if the bridge functions were known a priori.
- **Core assumption**: The bridge functions h* and q* satisfy certain completeness conditions (Assumptions 4-5) and can be estimated with error o(n^{-1/2}) (Assumption 8).
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Our proposed estimator can be implemented by off-the-shelf loss-minimizing machine learning methods, which in the case of kernel regression satisfies an oracle bound on the estimated error as long as the nuisance components are estimated reasonably well."
  - [section]: "Our contribution is to extend this flexible CATE estimation strategy to the proximal causal inference framework... The proposed loss relies on doubly robust scores which can also be re-purposed to enable semiparametric efficient estimation and inference"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct mention of oracle bounds or kernel regression in related papers
- **Break condition**: If the nuisance components cannot be estimated with sufficient accuracy (o(n^{-1/2})), the oracle bound no longer holds and the P-learner may perform worse than traditional methods.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The doubly robust scores enable both efficient CATE estimation and semiparametric inference on lower-dimensional summaries.
- **Mechanism**: The scores combine outcome regression and propensity score-like terms to create a pseudo-outcome that is insensitive to first-order estimation errors in the nuisance components. This allows flexible ML methods to be used in the second stage while maintaining statistical guarantees.
- **Core assumption**: The completeness conditions ensure the bridge functions exist and are identifiable from the observed data.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Our proposed estimator can be implemented by off-the-shelf loss-minimizing machine learning methods... Theoretical analysis shows the P-learner achieves an oracle bound on estimated error"
  - [section]: "The proposed loss relies on doubly robust scores which can also be re-purposed to enable semiparametric efficient estimation and inference on lower-dimensional summaries of the CATEs"
  - [corpus]: Weak - related papers discuss doubly robust methods but not in the proximal causal inference context
- **Break condition**: If the completeness conditions are violated (proxies don't contain sufficient information about unmeasured confounders), the bridge functions cannot be consistently estimated, breaking the doubly robust property.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The RATE metric provides a practical way to evaluate treatment heterogeneity when ground truth CATEs are unavailable.
- **Mechanism**: RATE uses the doubly robust proximal scores to construct a targeting operator characteristic (TOC) curve that ranks units by estimated CATEs and compares the ATE for the top quantiles to the overall ATE. The area under this curve measures how well the estimator stratifies the population by treatment benefit.
- **Core assumption**: The doubly robust scores satisfy E[Γi|Xi] ≈ E[Yi(1) - Yi(0)|Xi], allowing the RATE to detect heterogeneity even with unmeasured confounding.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section]: "Recent developments in the statistics literature offer an attractive solution to this problem in the form of a family of metrics called rank-weighted average treatment effects (RATE)"
  - [section]: "The RATE is the area under this curve and measures how well the CATE estimator stratifies the population in terms of units that benefit differently from treatment"
  - [corpus]: Weak - RATE is mentioned in the paper but not in related papers in the corpus
- **Break condition**: If the doubly robust scores have substantial bias (due to poor nuisance estimation), the RATE may not accurately reflect true heterogeneity and could lead to incorrect conclusions about treatment effects.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data preprocessing (partition covariates into X, Z, W) -> Nuisance estimation (estimate bridge functions h*, q*) -> Cross-fitting (avoid overfitting) -> Score construction (doubly robust proximal scores) -> CATE estimation (regress scores on covariates) -> Inference (RATE metric and bootstrap CIs)

- **Critical path**: Nuisance estimation → Cross-fitting → Score construction → CATE estimation → Heterogeneity assessment
  The method's performance critically depends on accurate estimation of the bridge functions h* and q*. Poor nuisance estimation will propagate through the entire pipeline.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Flexibility vs. interpretability: The method allows flexible ML models but may sacrifice interpretability compared to parametric approaches
  - Computational cost: Kernel methods for nuisance estimation scale poorly (O(n^3)), while neural network approaches may scale better but require careful tuning
  - Sample size requirements: The method requires sufficient data to estimate both nuisance components and CATEs accurately

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High variance in CATE estimates across different cross-fitting folds
  - Low RATE values suggesting inability to detect heterogeneity
  - Instability in bridge function estimates (large standard errors)
  - Sensitivity to hyperparameter choices in nuisance estimation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the P-learner perform when the proxy variables Z and W are weakly informative about the unmeasured confounder U?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the proxy variables must satisfy completeness conditions (Assumptions 4 and 5) to carry sufficient information about the confounders, but does not explore scenarios where these conditions are only partially met.
- Why unresolved: The theoretical analysis assumes the completeness conditions hold, but real-world proxy variables may not satisfy them strongly, leaving uncertainty about performance degradation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies varying the strength of the proxy-confounder relationship, measuring how performance metrics like MSE and RATE degrade as proxy informativeness decreases.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the P-learner be extended to handle time-varying treatments and dynamic confounding in longitudinal settings?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on static treatment effects and mentions that panel data extensions exist (Imbens et al. 2021), but does not develop methods for dynamic treatment regimes with time-varying confounding.
- Why unresolved: The current framework assumes static settings, and extending it to longitudinal data would require new bridge function constructions and potentially different identification strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical validation of P-learner variants for longitudinal settings, demonstrating improved performance over existing methods for dynamic causal inference.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between model flexibility in the final stage (τ estimation) and the accuracy of nuisance component estimation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The theoretical analysis shows oracle bounds when nuisance components are reasonably estimated, but does not provide guidance on how to optimally tune this trade-off in practice.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that flexible learners can work well but doesn't characterize when simpler models might be preferable given estimation error in h and q.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic empirical studies varying model complexity across both stages, identifying conditions under which simpler or more complex final stage models perform better given different levels of nuisance estimation error.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the P-learner scale to high-dimensional covariate settings where p >> n?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The empirical examples use moderate-dimensional settings (d=5 to 7), and while the framework could theoretically handle high dimensions, the kernel-based nuisance estimation may not scale well.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges computational challenges with kernel methods (O(n^3)) but does not explore alternative scalable approaches or regularization strategies for high-dimensional settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparisons of the P-learner using different nuisance estimation methods (kernel vs neural network vs sparse regression) in high-dimensional settings, measuring both computational efficiency and statistical performance.

## Limitations
- The oracle bound relies on strong completeness assumptions that may not hold when proxy variables are weakly informative about unmeasured confounders
- Kernel-based nuisance estimation scales poorly (O(n^3)), limiting applicability to large datasets
- The RATE metric, while innovative, lacks validation against ground truth heterogeneity in diverse real-world applications
