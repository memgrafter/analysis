# A New Dataset and Empirical Study for Sentence Simplification in Chinese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04188
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04188
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: Introduces CSS, the first open-source Chinese sentence simplification dataset with 766 human-annotated simplifications showing distinct characteristics from English

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CSS (Chinese Sentence Simplification), the first open-source Chinese sentence simplification dataset containing 766 human-annotated simplifications of 383 sentences from the PFR Chinese corpus. The dataset includes multiple rewriting operations such as lexical simplification, sentence splitting, compression, and paraphrasing. Through comprehensive experiments, the authors demonstrate that Chinese sentence simplification has distinct characteristics from English, particularly in compression and sentence splitting operations. The paper establishes baselines using unsupervised, zero-shot, and few-shot learning methods, and evaluates large language models on this task, finding that GPT-3.5-turbo and ChatGLM-6B perform well in Chinese sentence simplification.

## Method Summary
The authors collected manual simplifications from human annotators who performed sentence simplifications while explicitly labeling operations (lexical simplification, sentence splitting, compression, paraphrasing). The dataset was then used to test several unsupervised and zero/few-shot learning methods. Unsupervised learning used English-to-Chinese translation to create pseudo-parallel data, while zero/few-shot methods leveraged pre-trained language models. Large language models including GPT-3.5-turbo and ChatGLM-6B were evaluated on CSS. Both automatic metrics (SARI, BLEU) and human evaluation were employed to assess quality.

## Key Results
- CSS contains 766 simplifications with operation tags for 383 original sentences
- Chinese simplification shows distinct patterns from English, particularly lower compression rates and higher sentence splitting frequency
- GPT-3.5-turbo and ChatGLM-6B perform well in Chinese sentence simplification, especially in few-shot settings
- Unsupervised and zero/few-shot methods provide reasonable baselines for Chinese simplification
- Automatic and human evaluation results confirm high quality of CSS dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Manual human simplification with labeled operations yields high-quality simplification data that captures distinct Chinese simplification patterns
- Mechanism: Human annotators perform simplifications while explicitly labeling operations, providing rich supervision for model training and evaluation
- Core assumption: Human annotators can consistently apply and label simplification operations when given clear guidelines and examples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We collect manual simplifications from human annotators and perform data analysis to show the difference between English and Chinese sentence simplifications"
  - [section 3.1]: Detailed annotation process with operation definitions, qualification tests, and quality control measures
  - [corpus]: CSS contains 766 simplifications with operation tags for 383 original sentences
- Break condition: If annotator agreement on operation labels drops below threshold or quality control fails to catch inconsistent annotations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Unsupervised learning with pseudo-parallel corpora can train Chinese simplification models without requiring labeled simplification data
- Mechanism: Machine translation of English simplification data to Chinese creates pseudo-parallel data for training simplification models, leveraging cross-lingual transfer
- Core assumption: English-Chinese translation can adequately bridge the gap between English and Chinese simplification patterns
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Furthermore, we test several unsupervised and zero/few-shot learning methods on CSS and analyze the automatic evaluation and human evaluation results"
  - [section 4.1]: Description of unsupervised method using English-to-Chinese translation to create pseudo-simplification data
  - [corpus]: Results show unsupervised method achieves reasonable performance but falls short of supervised baselines
- Break condition: If translation quality is poor or Chinese simplification patterns diverge too significantly from English patterns

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Large Language Models can serve as high-quality Chinese sentence simplification systems, especially in few-shot settings
- Mechanism: LLMs leverage in-context learning and pre-training on multilingual data to perform Chinese simplification without explicit fine-tuning or with minimal examples
- Core assumption: LLMs contain sufficient Chinese language knowledge and simplification capabilities in their pre-training
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "In the end, we explore whether Large Language Models can serve as high-quality Chinese sentence simplification systems by evaluating them on CSS"
  - [section 6]: Evaluation of multiple LLMs (GPT-3.5-turbo, Vicuna-13B, ChatGLM-6B) showing strong performance, especially in few-shot settings
  - [corpus]: GPT-3.5-turbo outperforms all baselines in few-shot setting according to automatic metrics
- Break condition: If LLM outputs fail to preserve meaning or produce fluent simplifications despite strong automatic metrics

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection → Human annotation pipeline → Dataset creation → Multiple model training approaches (unsupervised, zero-shot, few-shot) → Evaluation (automatic + human) → LLM experiments
- Critical path: Dataset creation → Baseline model development → Comprehensive evaluation → LLM exploration
- Design tradeoffs: Manual annotation (high quality but expensive) vs. unsupervised methods (cheaper but lower quality); English transfer vs. direct Chinese approaches
- Failure signatures: Poor SARI scores with high BLEU scores indicating copying; human evaluation revealing meaning preservation issues despite strong automatic metrics; LLM outputs containing English text or failing to follow instructions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the unsupervised translation-based method and evaluate on CSS to establish baseline performance
  2. Implement zero-shot transfer from English simplification dataset and compare results
  3. Fine-tune mT5 with the small additional dataset for few-shot learning and evaluate improvement over zero-shot

## Limitations

- Dataset size (383 source sentences, 766 simplifications) is relatively small compared to established English simplification datasets
- Limited scale of human evaluation (21 raters evaluating 50 examples each) may not capture full diversity of quality issues
- Heavy reliance on automatic metrics that can disagree with human judgments, particularly for semantic preservation
- Unsupervised approach using English-to-Chinese translation may not fully capture unique Chinese simplification characteristics

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: CSS is the first open-source Chinese sentence simplification dataset; Chinese simplification shows distinct characteristics from English (particularly in compression and sentence splitting operations)

**Medium Confidence**: Large language models can serve as high-quality Chinese sentence simplification systems; effectiveness of unsupervised learning methods

**Low Confidence**: Specific differences in operation distributions between English and Chinese simplification may be influenced by annotation guidelines and small dataset size

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset Expansion and Diversity Analysis**: Expand CSS to include more diverse domains and sentence types, then re-evaluate operation distribution claims and model performance to verify patterns hold across larger, more varied dataset

2. **Multi-Rater Human Evaluation Scaling**: Implement larger-scale human evaluation with 50-100 raters evaluating 200-300 examples using both pairwise comparisons and holistic scoring to establish more robust baselines and identify systematic quality issues

3. **Cross-Lingual Transfer Robustness Testing**: Design controlled experiments comparing different unsupervised approaches (direct translation vs. pivot languages vs. multilingual pretraining) to quantify contribution of English-Chinese translation quality to simplification performance and identify most effective transfer strategies for Chinese simplification
