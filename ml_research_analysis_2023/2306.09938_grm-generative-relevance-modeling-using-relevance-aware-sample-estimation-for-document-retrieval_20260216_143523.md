# GRM: Generative Relevance Modeling Using Relevance-Aware Sample Estimation for Document Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09938
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09938
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GRM with RASE improves MAP by 6-9% and Recall@1k by 2-4% over prior methods, achieving state-of-the-art results on TREC Robust04 and CODEC datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GRM (Generative Relevance Modeling), a novel approach that leverages large language models (LLMs) to generate documents for query expansion in document retrieval. The key challenge addressed is that LLMs can produce irrelevant content (hallucinations) that harms retrieval effectiveness. GRM introduces RASE (Relevance-Aware Sample Estimation), which estimates the relevance of generated documents by comparing them to relevant documents in the target collection using semantic similarity and neural re-ranking. Experiments demonstrate that GRM with RASE significantly outperforms existing query expansion methods, achieving state-of-the-art results on two standard retrieval datasets.

## Method Summary
GRM generates multiple diverse documents per query using an LLM (ChatGPT) through a multi-step process: first generating subtopics for each query, then generating documents for each subtopic. RASE estimates the relevance of these generated documents by computing similarity to relevant real documents in the target collection and using neural re-rankers to estimate their query likelihood scores. The most relevant generated documents are then used to expand the original query using RM3-style weighting, improving retrieval effectiveness while mitigating the impact of irrelevant generated content.

## Key Results
- GRM with RASE achieves 6-9% MAP improvement and 2-4% Recall@1k improvement over prior methods
- Neural re-ranker-based relevance estimation (GRM-T5) outperforms BM25 and uniform weighting approaches
- Diverse subtopic generation (50 documents per query) effectively mitigates hallucination impact
- State-of-the-art performance on both TREC Robust04 and CODEC datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RASE effectively weighs generated documents by estimating their relevance based on semantic similarity to relevant real documents.
- Mechanism: RASE computes the query likelihood score of generated documents by aggregating relevance scores from semantically similar real documents in the target collection, weighted by their similarity.
- Core assumption: Generated documents that are semantically similar to relevant real documents contain more useful expansion terms.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Specifically, we identify similar real documents for each generated document and use a neural re-ranker to estimate their relevance."
  - [section 3] "RASE focuses on estimating the query likelihood score, P(Q|D_LM), by modeling the relevance of documents in a collection that are similar to the generated document D_LM."
  - [corpus] Weak - only general relevance modeling papers found, no specific RASE implementations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Diverse subtopic generation mitigates the impact of hallucinations by providing multiple angles on the query.
- Mechanism: LLM is prompted to generate multiple subtopics for each query, then generates documents for each subtopic, creating a diverse set of synthetic documents that collectively cover different facets of the information need.
- Core assumption: Complex queries have multiple distinct subtopics, and generating documents for each subtopic will produce more relevant expansion terms than a single document.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We generate 50 documents per topic to give a reasonable sample and observe a significant variance in expansion effectiveness."
  - [section 3] "Specifically, given the initial query Q, we utilize ChatGPT with chain-of-thought reasoning to first generate K subtopics for the query Q. Then, we prompt the LLM to generate documents based on these subtopics."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in corpus of similar multi-subtopic generation strategies.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Neural re-ranker-based relevance estimation outperforms BM25 and uniform weighting for RASE.
- Mechanism: GRM-T5 uses T5-3B re-ranker with max-passage aggregation to compute P(Q|D_C) for similar documents, providing more accurate relevance signals than BM25 or uniform weighting.
- Core assumption: Neural re-rankers trained on relevance judgments can better estimate document relevance than lexical similarity measures like BM25.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "For our relevance estimate function, P(Q|D_C), we use the following four formulations and normalize scores: GRM-Uniform, GRM-BM25, GRM-T5, GRM-Gold."
  - [section 5] "Utilizing a neural re-ranker [29], such as in GRM-T5, reveals that better RASE can more effectively weigh beneficial generated documents for query expansion."
  - [corpus] Weak - only general T5 re-ranking papers found, no specific RASE implementations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: LLM generator → RASE module (similarity search + relevance estimator) → GRM expansion module → Retrieval system
- Critical path: Document generation → RASE relevance estimation → Term weighting → Query expansion → Retrieval
- Design tradeoffs: Diversity vs. quality in generated documents; complexity of RASE vs. simplicity of uniform weighting; neural re-ranker accuracy vs. computational cost
- Failure signatures: Low recall despite high precision (overly selective RASE); high variance in expansion effectiveness (poor subtopic generation); degraded performance vs. baselines (miscalibrated relevance estimator)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare RASE with uniform weighting across different query types to measure effectiveness gain
  2. Vary number of generated documents (K) and generations (G) to find optimal diversity-quality tradeoff
  3. Test different similarity measures (BM25 vs. semantic embeddings) in RASE to evaluate semantic capture

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several are implied from the limitations and discussion sections.

## Limitations

- Computational overhead of neural re-ranking for each generated document could limit scalability
- Lack of ablation studies on individual RASE components makes it difficult to isolate performance drivers
- Reliance on similarity-based document selection assumes semantic similarity correlates with relevance

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (RASE relevance weighting): Medium - Strong theoretical foundation but limited empirical validation of individual components
- Mechanism 2 (Diverse subtopic generation): Medium - Effectiveness demonstrated but subtopic quality and hallucination filtering not thoroughly evaluated
- Mechanism 3 (Neural re-ranker superiority): High - Clear performance improvements over baselines with consistent results across datasets
