# Online Label Shift: Optimal Dynamic Regret meets Practical Algorithms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19570
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19570
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Achieves optimal dynamic regret for online label shift by reducing adaptation to online regression

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the online label shift problem where class marginals change over time while class-conditionals remain invariant. The authors propose algorithms that reduce label shift adaptation to online regression, achieving optimal dynamic regret without prior knowledge of drift extent. For unsupervised settings, they estimate label marginals online and reweight an initial classifier. For supervised settings, they use weighted empirical risk minimization with weights tracked by online regression. Experiments demonstrate consistent accuracy improvements of 1-3% over baselines while being sample and computationally efficient.

## Method Summary
The core approach reduces label shift adaptation to online regression by estimating label marginals and reweighting classifier predictions. In the unsupervised setting, an initial classifier trained on offline data is adapted online by tracking label marginals using online regression oracles and reweighting predictions by the ratio of estimated to training marginals. In the supervised setting, weighted empirical risk minimization is used with importance weights derived from estimated label marginals. The key innovation is using online regression algorithms (like FLH-FTL and Aligator) that adapt to the smoothness of label marginal sequences without requiring prior knowledge of total variation bounds.

## Key Results
- Achieves optimal dynamic regret bounds of Õ(T^(2/3)V^(1/3) + √T) for both unsupervised and supervised settings
- Outperforms baselines (ROGD, UOGD, ATLAS, FTH, FTFWH) by 1-3% accuracy on multiple datasets
- Sample-efficient, requiring only 1-5% holdout data to estimate confusion matrix
- Computationally efficient, avoiding expensive full retraining through reweighting mechanism

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Label shift adaptation reduces to online regression by estimating label marginals and reweighting an initial classifier. The algorithm tracks label marginals using online regression oracles, then reweights the initial classifier's predictions by estimated marginals divided by training marginals. This works when class-conditionals remain invariant and the initial classifier's confusion matrix is invertible.

### Mechanism 2
Dynamic regret is controlled without prior knowledge of total variation bounds. Online regression oracles adapt their switching behavior to the smoothness of label marginal sequences, achieving near-optimal rates without knowing the total variation ahead of time. This works when label marginals form a sequence with bounded total variation.

### Mechanism 3
Weighted empirical risk minimization with online-estimated importance weights achieves optimal dynamic regret in supervised settings. The algorithm estimates label marginals online, then uses weighted ERM where each example's contribution is weighted by the ratio of estimated to previous label marginal. This works when label marginals are bounded away from zero.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Online data stream → label marginal estimation → classifier adaptation → classification output
- **Critical path**: 1) Estimate label marginals using online regression oracle, 2) Reweight initial classifier predictions, 3) Update classifier via reweighting/weighted ERM, 4) Repeat for each online round
- **Design tradeoffs**: Reweighting is computationally cheaper but may be less accurate if initial classifier is poor; retraining allows full adaptation but is more expensive
- **Failure signatures**: Poor label marginal estimation → biased predictions; too frequent switching → high variance; insufficient holdout data → incorrect reweighting
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Test FLH-FTL tracking accuracy on synthetic data with known marginals, 2) Measure accuracy improvement on CIFAR-10 with controlled label shift, 3) Compare computational cost of reweighting vs retraining on Fashion-MNIST

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the discussion section implies several areas for future work including extending the reduction to other types of distribution shifts beyond label shift, analyzing performance with varying numbers of classes, and handling continuous label shifts.

## Limitations
- Reduction relies on accurate label marginal estimation, which may be challenging with rapidly changing distributions or poorly conditioned confusion matrices
- Theoretical guarantees assume bounded total variation in label marginals, but real-world shifts may exhibit more complex patterns
- Supervised setting requires sufficient holdout data for confusion matrix estimation, limiting applicability in data-scarce scenarios

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Theoretical framework connecting online regression to label shift adaptation is well-established and optimality proofs appear sound
- **Medium confidence**: Empirical results show consistent improvements across multiple datasets, though magnitude varies significantly
- **Low confidence**: Assumption that class-conditionals remain invariant while only marginals shift may not hold in many practical scenarios
