# Prescriptive PCA: Dimensionality Reduction for Two-stage Stochastic Optimization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02223
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02223
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: PPCA achieves optimality gaps as low as 2-3% with low-dimensional representations (k < 5), while standard PCA requires much higher k to achieve similar performance

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of aligning dimensionality reduction with downstream stochastic optimization, where standard PCA may fail because it maximizes data variance rather than optimization-relevant features. The authors propose Prescriptive PCA (PPCA), which minimizes the degree of suboptimality in the downstream stochastic program by formulating it as a bi-convex optimization problem solvable via an alternating algorithm involving semidefinite programming. The method uses a distributionally-robust bound on the expected reconstruction error, which can be efficiently computed. Computational experiments on synthetic and real data (NYC taxi repositioning) demonstrate that PPCA significantly outperforms standard PCA, achieving near-optimal solutions with much lower dimensional representations.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes Prescriptive PCA (PPCA) as a dimensionality reduction technique specifically designed for two-stage stochastic optimization problems. PPCA formulates the dimensionality reduction as a bi-convex optimization problem that minimizes a distributionally-robust bound on the expected reconstruction error in the downstream stochastic program. The solution is obtained through an alternating algorithm that solves semidefinite programming subproblems. The key insight is that standard PCA maximizes data variance without considering the structure of the downstream optimization problem, potentially projecting onto subspaces orthogonal to the dual feasible region of the recourse problem. PPCA instead optimizes a surrogate bound on the optimality gap by aligning the projection with the dual feasible region of the stochastic program.

## Key Results
- PPCA achieves optimality gaps as low as 2-3% with low-dimensional representations (k < 5)
- On NYC taxi repositioning problem, PPCA maintains near-optimal performance with k=2 while PCA requires k=15
- PPCA significantly outperforms PCA across various signal-to-noise ratios and regularization parameter settings
- The alternating algorithm converges reliably to local minima that improve upon PCA solutions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PPCA aligns dimensionality reduction with downstream stochastic optimization by minimizing a distributionally-robust bound on the expected reconstruction error.
- Mechanism: Standard PCA maximizes data variance without regard to downstream optimization relevance, potentially projecting onto a subspace orthogonal to the dual feasible region. PPCA instead optimizes a surrogate bound on the optimality gap in the downstream stochastic program by solving a bi-convex optimization problem.
- Core assumption: The quality of the downstream stochastic program solution depends critically on the alignment between the data subspace and the dual feasible region of the recourse problem.
- Evidence anchors: The limitation of PCA is demonstrated through examples where PCA projections are orthogonal to the dual feasible region, resulting in zero recourse objective. Corpus lacks direct evidence of this specific alignment mechanism.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PPCA uses a distributionally-robust optimization framework to compute a tight upper bound on the expected reconstruction error without requiring distributional assumptions.
- Mechanism: The approach formulates the problem as minimizing a worst-case bound on the expected value of the recourse function, computed via semidefinite programming. This bound depends only on the covariance matrix and is invariant to the mean of the data.
- Core assumption: The second moments (covariance matrix) contain sufficient information to bound the expected reconstruction error in the downstream stochastic program.
- Evidence anchors: Proposition 3 shows that a bound on the expected value can be computed by solving a semidefinite program using only the covariance matrix. The bound is derived to be independent of the mean μ, making it invariant to first-stage decisions. Weak corpus evidence for distributionally-robust optimization in this context.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The alternating algorithm in PPCA converges to a local minimum that improves upon PCA by iteratively optimizing over the projection and the error covariance.
- Mechanism: The algorithm alternates between fixing Σe and optimizing over Λ + θΣ1, then fixing Λ + θΣ1 and optimizing over Σ1 and Σe. This yields monotonic improvement in the objective value.
- Core assumption: The bi-convex problem structure allows for effective alternating optimization to find a locally optimal solution.
- Evidence anchors: The algorithm is described as guaranteeing convergence to a local minimum with monotonic improvement in each iteration. It can be initialized with the PCA solution, ensuring the PPCA solution is at least as good as PCA in terms of the worst-case bound. No corpus evidence of alternating algorithms for dimensionality reduction.

## Limitations
- The distributional-robust bound's tightness in practice is not extensively validated
- The paper does not provide extensive sensitivity analysis on regularization hyperparameters
- Limited comparison with other dimensionality reduction methods beyond standard PCA
- The approach assumes static data without temporal dependencies or sequential decision-making

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The core mechanism of aligning dimensionality reduction with downstream optimization through distributionally-robust bounds is well-supported by theoretical derivations and synthetic experiments. The alternating algorithm for solving the bi-convex problem appears sound based on the monotonic improvement guarantee.

**Medium confidence**: While computational experiments show PPCA significantly outperforms PCA on both synthetic and real data, the specific choice of regularization parameters and algorithm stopping criteria could materially affect results. The paper does not provide extensive sensitivity analysis on these hyperparameters.

**Low confidence**: The distributional-robust bound's tightness in practice is not extensively validated. The paper assumes second moments contain sufficient information for the bound, but higher-order moments could be important in some applications. The comparison with other dimensionality reduction methods beyond PCA is limited.
