# Interactive Editing for Text Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03067
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03067
- Reference count: 28
- One-line primary result: Interactive framework for text summarization that enables iterative human editing of draft summaries

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces REVISE, an interactive framework for text summarization that enables human writers to iteratively edit and refine draft summaries. The core method uses a fill-in-the-middle model based on encoder-decoder architecture, which generates contextually relevant suggestions when users modify unsatisfactory segments. The system supports two editing modes: users can either provide optional starting phrases or let the model generate alternatives. Extensive experiments on the CNN/Daily Mail dataset show that REVISE significantly improves both annotation efficiency and summary quality compared to traditional summarization approaches.

## Method Summary
REVISE uses a fill-in-the-middle (FIM) model trained on randomly split summaries to handle edits at any position. The model architecture includes encoder-decoder components with sentinel tokens to mark insertion points. During training, summaries are split into prefix, middle, and suffix segments, with special handling for corner cases at the beginning and end. The system allows users to edit any segment of a summary and receive contextually appropriate suggestions, with the option to provide starting phrases for guided generation. Human evaluation was conducted across 120 documents spanning news, conversations, and blogs to validate both efficiency gains and quality improvements.

## Key Results
- Users complete annotations 32% faster with REVISE compared to traditional summarization
- REVISE produces higher quality summaries with fewer hallucinations
- The system significantly improves annotation quality while reducing editing time

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The fill-in-the-middle model improves summary editing efficiency by allowing users to modify any segment without regenerating the entire summary.
- Mechanism: The model uses encoder-decoder architecture with sentinel tokens to identify insertion points, allowing seamless integration of edited segments with existing content.
- Core assumption: Users can identify specific segments needing improvement and provide optional starting phrases for better generation.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "writers can effortlessly modify unsatisfactory segments at any location or length and provide optional starting phrases"; [section 3] "The input of the model is a source document sequence, a prefix sequence containing the summary before the deleted part and an optionally human start, a suffix sequence containing the summary after the deleted part."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Training with randomly split summaries improves model's ability to handle edits at any position.
- Mechanism: The model is trained on data where summaries are split into prefix, middle, and suffix segments, allowing it to learn context-aware generation across different positions.
- Core assumption: Random splitting during training exposes the model to diverse editing scenarios including beginning, middle, and end modifications.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.1] "During training, we randomly divide the summary ti into three parts: the prefix pi, the middle mi, and the suffixsi"; [section 3.2] "Therefore, we sample a proportion γ of data for the edits in the beginning and the end."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Human evaluation shows significant improvements in both efficiency and quality when using interactive editing versus traditional summarization.
- Mechanism: Users complete annotations 32% faster with REVISE while producing higher quality summaries with fewer hallucinations.
- Core assumption: Interactive suggestions reduce cognitive load and provide better starting points for human editors.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Human evaluation results demonstrate that users complete annotations 32% faster with REVISE while producing higher quality summaries with fewer hallucinations"; [section 4.4] "REVISE can significantly save editing time and improve annotation quality."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Document → Encoder → Context processing → Decoder → Generated suggestions → User selection → Final summary
- Critical path: Document → Encoder → Context processing → Decoder → Generated suggestions → User selection → Final summary
- Design tradeoffs: Faster editing vs. potential for suboptimal suggestions; Flexibility in editing positions vs. complexity in model training; Human control vs. automated efficiency
- Failure signatures: Suggestions that don't connect coherently with existing content; Model fails to generate ending tokens for edits at summary boundaries; Users reject most suggestions, indicating poor quality
- First 3 experiments: 1) Test basic FIM generation with controlled prefix-suffix pairs; 2) Evaluate corner case handling for beginning/end edits; 3) Measure user interaction patterns and suggestion acceptance rates

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model handle extremely long documents (e.g., 10,000+ tokens) and what are the performance implications?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions using CNN/Daily Mail dataset which typically contains shorter documents, but doesn't discuss performance on longer documents.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide any experiments or analysis on document length scalability, which is crucial for real-world applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing performance metrics (ROUGE scores, latency, memory usage) on documents of varying lengths up to 10,000+ tokens.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different prompt engineering strategies on the quality of generated suggestions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions users can provide "optional starting phrases" but doesn't systematically study the impact of different prompt strategies.
- Why unresolved: The paper only briefly mentions this feature but doesn't provide any empirical analysis of how different prompt strategies affect suggestion quality or user satisfaction.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing different prompt strategies (length, specificity, style) with quantitative and qualitative user feedback.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the system handle documents with multiple topics or perspectives, and does it maintain coherence across edits?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses coherence evaluation but doesn't specifically address multi-topic documents or how edits affect overall summary coherence.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation focuses on local coherence (ℓ1, ℓ2 metrics) but doesn't examine how edits affect the summary's ability to maintain coherence across different topics or perspectives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of summary coherence before and after edits on multi-topic documents, with user studies on perceived coherence.

## Limitations

- Human evaluation methodology lacks detailed statistical analysis, sample sizes, and inter-rater reliability metrics
- Corner case training approach (γ=0.5) appears heuristic rather than theoretically grounded
- Implementation details of sentinel tokens and generation order are not fully specified

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core premise that interactive editing can improve summarization workflows is well-supported by existing literature and the experimental design is reasonable. The use of FIM architecture for this task is methodologically sound.

**Medium Confidence**: The reported efficiency gains (32% time reduction) and quality improvements are plausible given the interactive nature of the system, but the lack of detailed statistical analysis and potential confounding factors in the human evaluation reduce confidence in these specific quantitative claims.

**Low Confidence**: The effectiveness of the corner case training approach and the generalizability of the model to domains beyond news articles remain uncertain due to limited validation and lack of ablation studies on the training methodology.

## Next Checks

1. **Statistical Validation**: Re-run the human evaluation with proper statistical analysis including confidence intervals, inter-rater reliability metrics (Cohen's kappa), and significance testing between REVISE and baseline conditions to verify the claimed 32% efficiency improvement and quality gains.

2. **Architecture Transparency**: Implement and test the FIM model with detailed architectural specifications including token generation order, attention mechanism configuration, and sentinel token handling to reproduce the reported performance and identify potential failure modes.

3. **Domain Generalization**: Evaluate the model on non-news domains (conversations, blogs) with controlled experiments to verify whether the corner case training approach (γ=0.5) effectively handles beginning and end edits across different text types, and whether the efficiency/quality improvements transfer beyond the CNN/Daily Mail dataset.
