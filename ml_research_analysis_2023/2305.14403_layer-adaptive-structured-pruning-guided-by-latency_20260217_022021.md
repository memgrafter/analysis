# Layer-adaptive Structured Pruning Guided by Latency

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14403
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14403
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: SP-LAMP achieves 1.28×/8.45× faster inference on ResNet50/ResNet18 with minimal accuracy loss (+1.7%/-1.57% top-1 accuracy change)

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of structured network pruning with latency constraints, proposing SP-LAMP (Structured Pruning Layer-Adaptive Magnitude-based Pruning). SP-LAMP extends the LAMP score from unstructured pruning to structured pruning, accounting for inter-layer connections by incorporating the magnitude of filters in adjacent layers. The method uses a group knapsack solver to maximize the SP-LAMP score under latency constraints, ensuring at least one filter remains per layer. Latency contributions are calculated considering skip connections and filter positions, improving accuracy over previous methods. Experiments on ResNet50/18, ResNet56, and MobileNetV2 demonstrate significant latency reductions with minimal accuracy loss.

## Method Summary
SP-LAMP performs structured pruning by computing importance scores that account for inter-layer connections, using a group knapsack solver to select filters under latency constraints. The method extends LAMP by incorporating magnitudes of filters in adjacent layers into the SP-LAMP score, then groups filters within each layer for knapsack optimization to ensure minimum layer coverage. Latency is profiled comprehensively, considering skip connections and filter positions, before solving the optimization problem and fine-tuning the pruned network.

## Key Results
- Achieves 1.28× faster inference on ResNet50 with only +1.7% top-1 accuracy change
- Achieves 8.45× faster inference on ResNet18 with -1.57% top-1 accuracy change
- Outperforms state-of-the-art pruning approaches in balancing speed and accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SP-LAMP improves pruning efficiency by extending LAMP to structured pruning with inter-layer connection awareness.
- Mechanism: The SP-LAMP score incorporates both the magnitude of filters in the current layer and the magnitude of corresponding channels in the adjacent layer, using the formula: `score(u, l) := ∥Wl,u∥2F × ∥cWl+1,u∥2F / Σv≥u ∥Wl,v∥2F × ∥cWl+1,v∥2F`. This accounts for how removing a filter affects downstream layer operations.
- Core assumption: The Frobenius norm of a filter and its corresponding channel in the next layer are good indicators of importance when pruned together.

  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct mention of inter-layer connection importance in related works.
- Break condition: If the correlation between filter/channel magnitudes and actual importance breaks down in deeper networks or different architectures.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Group knapsack solver prevents complete layer elimination while maximizing SP-LAMP scores under latency constraints.
- Mechanism: Instead of treating each filter as an independent item (0-1 knapsack), SP-LAMP groups filters within each layer and solves a group knapsack problem. This ensures at least one filter remains per layer while optimizing the selection of filter groups based on SP-LAMP scores and latency contributions.
- Core assumption: Preserving at least one filter per layer is essential for maintaining network functionality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We utilize a group knapsack solver to maximize the SP-LAMP score under latency constraints, ensuring at least one filter remains per layer."
  - [section]: Algorithm 1 and Equation 10 describe the group knapsack solver implementation.
  - [corpus]: Weak - related works don't explicitly discuss group knapsack approaches to prevent layer elimination.
- Break condition: If the latency constraint becomes so tight that even the minimum required filters exceed the budget.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Improved latency collection strategy accounts for skip connections and filter positions, making latency estimates more accurate.
- Mechanism: SP-LAMP individually removes filters from each layer and measures the resulting latency impact on all affected layers, considering skip connections and position-dependent effects. This generates a more accurate latency lookup table than methods that only consider current and next layer impacts.
- Core assumption: Latency is not solely determined by the immediate upstream and downstream layers but depends on the global network structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We improve the strategy of collect the latency to make it more accurate. In particular, for ResNet50/ResNet18 on ImageNet and CIFAR10, SP-LAMP is 1.28×/8.45× faster..."

- Break condition: If the latency measurement process becomes prohibitively expensive or if hardware characteristics change significantly.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - SP-LAMP Score Calculator -> Latency Profiler -> Group Knapsack Solver -> Pruning Engine -> Evaluation Module

- Critical path:
  1. Compute SP-LAMP scores for all filters
  2. Profile latency contributions for all filter groups
  3. Solve group knapsack problem to select filters
  4. Apply masks and fine-tune pruned network
  5. Evaluate accuracy and latency

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. Latency: Tighter latency constraints lead to more aggressive pruning and potential accuracy loss
  - Measurement Cost vs. Precision: More thorough latency profiling improves accuracy but increases computational overhead
  - Group Size vs. Flexibility: Larger filter groups simplify knapsack solving but reduce pruning granularity

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy collapse: Indicates insufficient filters retained in critical layers
  - Unexpected latency: Suggests inaccurate latency profiling or hardware-specific effects not captured
  - Knapsack infeasibility: Indicates latency constraints are too tight for even minimum required filters

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline pruning on ResNet18 with SP-LAMP (no latency constraints) to verify accuracy preservation
  2. Latency-constrained pruning on ResNet18 with increasing constraint tightness to identify breaking point
  3. Comparison of SP-LAMP vs. HALP on ResNet50 with identical latency constraints to quantify accuracy-latency tradeoff improvements

## Limitations
- The effectiveness depends heavily on accurate latency profiling, which may not generalize well across diverse accelerator architectures
- The SP-LAMP score's assumption of fixed inputs during pruning may not hold when multiple layers are pruned sequentially
- The group knapsack solver's performance could degrade when latency constraints become extremely tight

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The mathematical formulation of SP-LAMP scores and their extension from LAMP is well-founded and theoretically sound
- **Medium Confidence**: The latency collection methodology is reasonable but may require hardware-specific calibration for optimal results
- **Medium Confidence**: The experimental results demonstrate improvements, though the sample size of tested architectures and datasets is relatively limited
