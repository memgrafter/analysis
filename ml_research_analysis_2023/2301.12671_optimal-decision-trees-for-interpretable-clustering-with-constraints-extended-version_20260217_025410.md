# Optimal Decision Trees For Interpretable Clustering with Constraints (Extended Version)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12671
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12671
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces the first approach for interpretable and constrained clustering using decision trees.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel SAT-based approach for interpretable clustering with pairwise constraints. The method constructs decision trees that approximate two well-known clustering objectives (minimum diameter and minimum split) while supporting must-link and cannot-link constraints. The key innovation lies in encoding clustering objectives and constraints into Boolean variables and clauses, then using a MaxSAT solver to find optimal tree structures. The approach employs smart pairs processing and distance classes to improve efficiency and approximation guarantees. Experiments demonstrate that the method can produce high-quality, interpretable clustering solutions that satisfy user-provided constraints, with tree-based clustering outperforming unconstrained clustering baselines when constraints are present.

## Method Summary
The approach encodes clustering objectives and pairwise constraints into Boolean variables and clauses for a MaxSAT solver. It constructs distance classes to enable efficient approximation of clustering objectives and uses a smart pairs procedure to reduce computational complexity. The method supports two clustering objectives (minimum diameter and a bi-criteria objective combining minimum split and maximum diameter) and pairwise instance-level constraints. The SAT encoding represents the decision tree structure, clustering assignments, and optimization objectives, with the MaxSAT solver finding solutions that satisfy hard constraints while optimizing soft clauses.

## Key Results
- First approach for interpretable and constrained clustering using decision trees
- Outperforms unconstrained clustering baselines in terms of clustering quality when constraints are present
- Demonstrates trade-off between interpretability and constraint satisfaction
- Achieves high-quality, interpretable clustering solutions that satisfy user-provided pairwise constraints

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The SAT-based encoding enables exact optimization of interpretable clustering with constraints.
- Mechanism: The approach encodes clustering objectives and pairwise constraints into Boolean variables and clauses, then uses a MaxSAT solver to find a tree structure that satisfies all hard constraints (clustering constraints) while optimizing soft clauses (diameter and split objectives). This ensures that the final clustering respects user-provided constraints while being represented as a decision tree.
- Core assumption: The decision tree depth is sufficient to represent any clustering that satisfies the constraints (Proposition 1), and the MaxSAT solver can find feasible solutions within reasonable time limits.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our framework is the first approach for interpretable and constrained clustering."
  - [section] "Our formulation supports two well-known clustering objectives as well as pairwise instance-level clustering constraints."
- Break condition: The approach becomes infeasible when the tree depth is insufficient to represent any clustering that satisfies the constraints, or when the MaxSAT solver cannot find a feasible solution within the time limit.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Distance classes enable efficient approximation of clustering objectives.
- Mechanism: The algorithm divides all pairs of points into distance classes based on their pairwise distances. It then uses these classes to encode the approximation guarantees for the minimum split and maximum diameter objectives. By focusing on optimizing the indices of these classes rather than individual distances, the approach significantly reduces the problem size.
- Core assumption: The distance classes are constructed such that the minimum and maximum distances within each class are less than the approximation parameter apart.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To encode the /u1D716-approximation... we divide the set of all pairs of data points based on their distance into /u1D707non-overlapping intervals called distance classes"
  - [section] "All pairs in the same class are treated similarly w.r.t. being clustered together or not."
- Break condition: The approximation guarantee breaks if the distance classes are not constructed correctly, or if the optimization does not properly account for the relationship between distance classes and clustering objectives.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Smart pairs procedure reduces computational complexity without changing solution quality.
- Mechanism: The approach builds a graph where points are nodes and edges represent clustering constraints. It then processes pairs in order of distance, classifying them as "inner" or "crossing" based on connected components and mutual exclusivity. This allows the algorithm to avoid adding redundant clauses and detect infeasibility early.
- Core assumption: The ordering of pairs based on distance and the connected component structure can be used to determine which clauses are redundant or which cases are infeasible.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Since /u1D438+ (/u1D438− ) represents the set of pairs that are forced to be clustered together (separately), an inner pair is forced to have the same label and a crossing pair to have different labels."
  - [section] "We make use of this fact to avoid adding co-clustering clauses... or separating clauses... when it is redundant to do so."
- Break condition: The procedure may miss valid solutions if the connected component analysis is incorrect, or it may incorrectly declare infeasibility if the mutual exclusivity detection is flawed.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input preprocessing -> Distance class construction -> SAT encoding -> Smart pairs processing -> MaxSAT solver -> Postprocessing

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess input data and constraints
  2. Construct distance classes for approximation
  3. Build SAT encoding with smart pairs optimization
  4. Run MaxSAT solver with time limit
  5. Extract and validate solution

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Tree depth vs. expressiveness: Deeper trees can represent more complex clusterings but increase computational complexity
  - Approximation parameter vs. solution quality: Larger approximation parameters reduce problem size but may yield lower-quality solutions
  - Constraint strictness vs. feasibility: More constraints may make the problem infeasible for shallow trees

- Failure signatures:
  - Infeasible: No solution found within time limit or solver reports infeasibility
  - Unknown: Solver times out without finding any solution
  - Poor quality: Solution found but with low ARI/NMI scores
  - Memory issues: Out-of-memory errors during encoding or solving

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run on Iris dataset with 0.1 approximation parameter and default tree depth, verify that solution satisfies constraints and has reasonable ARI score
  2. Test with increasing numbers of constraints (0.1 to 1.0) on Wine dataset, observe trade-off between interpretability and constraint satisfaction
  3. Compare [MD,MS] objective vs. MD-only objective on Glass dataset, verify that Pareto optimization yields better solutions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the trade-off between interpretability and constraint satisfaction change as dataset dimensionality increases?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that in high-dimensional datasets with complex patterns, a large number of univariate splits may be needed to fit ground-truth constraints, leading to infeasibility for a given tree depth.
- Why unresolved: The experiments show this trend but don't systematically explore the relationship across varying dimensionalities or provide a theoretical framework for understanding when this trade-off becomes most severe.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying dataset dimensionality while keeping other factors constant, combined with theoretical analysis of the relationship between dimensionality, tree depth, and constraint satisfiability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of converting hard clustering constraints into soft constraints on solution quality and interpretability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests investigating strategies to overcome infeasibility by converting hard clustering constraints into soft constraints that are encouraged rather than required to be satisfied.
- Why unresolved: This remains a future work direction mentioned in the conclusion without experimental validation or theoretical analysis of the trade-offs involved.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of hard vs. soft constraint formulations across various datasets, including measures of both solution quality and interpretability, plus analysis of how constraint relaxation affects the tree structure.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of approximation parameter ε affect the Pareto front exploration and selection of optimal solutions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that there are potentially many Pareto-optimal solutions and that investigating strategies for exploring the Pareto front and selecting promising solutions is an interesting direction for future work.
- Why unresolved: The experiments fix ε at 0.1 without exploring how different values affect the distribution of solutions along the Pareto front or the interpretability of the resulting trees.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying ε values, visualization of the Pareto front for different ε settings, and analysis of how ε affects both solution quality metrics and tree interpretability measures.

## Limitations

- Scalability concerns for larger datasets (10K+ points) due to exponential complexity in tree depth
- Limited exploration of hyperparameter sensitivity (approximation parameter, tree depth)
- Insufficient analysis of how constraint distribution patterns affect the interpretability-constraint satisfaction trade-off

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core SAT-based encoding methodology and the theoretical foundations (Proposition 1, approximation guarantees) are well-established and clearly presented. The experimental results show consistent improvements over baselines in constrained clustering scenarios.

**Medium Confidence**: The practical effectiveness of the smart pairs pruning procedure and the distance class construction method for reducing computational complexity. While theoretically sound, the actual impact on solution quality and runtime may vary with different datasets and constraint distributions.

**Low Confidence**: The scalability of the approach to significantly larger datasets (10K+ points) or higher-dimensional spaces, as these scenarios are not explored in the experiments. The sensitivity analysis for key hyperparameters (approximation parameter, tree depth) is also limited.
