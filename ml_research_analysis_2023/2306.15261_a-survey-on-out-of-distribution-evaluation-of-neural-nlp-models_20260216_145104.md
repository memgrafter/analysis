# A Survey on Out-of-Distribution Evaluation of Neural NLP Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15261
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15261
- Reference count: 16
- One-line primary result: Unifies adversarial robustness, domain generalization, and dataset bias evaluation under distribution shift framework

## Executive Summary
This survey paper unifies three research lines in out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation of neural NLP models - adversarial robustness, domain generalization, and dataset biases - under the common framework of distribution shift. The authors categorize OOD data generation methods into natural domain shift, debiased data, and adversarial examples, while evaluation protocols are divided into data-based and method-based approaches. The paper identifies key challenges including the need for comprehensive benchmarks and improving general OOD performance across different types of distribution shifts.

## Method Summary
The survey analyzes three OOD evaluation research lines by comparing their data-generating processes and evaluation protocols. It categorizes OOD data generation into natural domain shift (NDS), debiased data, and adversarial examples, and evaluation methods into data-based and method-based approaches. The framework unifies these lines under covariate shift assumptions, examining how each type shifts different feature categories (semantic, background, biased). The method involves systematic review of existing literature, categorization of approaches, and identification of connections and challenges across the three research domains.

## Key Results
- Unifies adversarial robustness, domain generalization, and dataset bias evaluation under distribution shift framework
- Categorizes OOD data generation into three types: natural domain shift, debiased data, and adversarial examples
- Identifies feature shift mechanisms across OOD types (semantic, background, biased features)
- Highlights challenges including breaking covariate shift assumptions and need for comprehensive benchmarks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a comprehensive benchmark that effectively evaluates models across all three types of out-of-distribution (OOD) data: adversarial robustness, domain generalization, and dataset biases?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the lack of a unified benchmark that can evaluate models across all three OOD types, due to the idiomatic lock-in in each research line, different data-generating processes, and evaluation protocols.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of a benchmark that includes diverse OOD data types, clear evaluation metrics, and demonstrated effectiveness in identifying model weaknesses across all three OOD types.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the implications of data-generating processes that break the assumptions of covariate shift, and how can we mitigate these effects in OOD evaluation?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights that some data-generating processes may break the assumptions of covariate shift (fixed P0(Y|X) and P0(Y)), which could lead to misleading evaluations. However, it does not provide specific solutions for addressing this issue.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing model performance under different data-generating processes that adhere to or violate covariate shift assumptions, and development of evaluation methods that account for these violations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we improve general OOD performance by enhancing the acquisition of general linguistic or semantic knowledge, and what role does dataset filtering play in this process?
- Basis in paper: Explicit
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that improving general OOD performance requires learning general linguistic or semantic knowledge, but it does not provide specific strategies for achieving this. Additionally, it notes that dataset filtering for bias mitigation may have unintended consequences on OOD performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental studies comparing the effectiveness of different data augmentation techniques and pretraining strategies on general OOD performance, and analysis of the trade-offs between bias mitigation and OOD generalization.

## Limitations
- The unification framework assumes all three research lines can be characterized as studying covariate shifts, which may break down when P0(Y|X) changes
- The three-category system for OOD data generation may not be exhaustive as new methods emerge
- The feature analysis framework might oversimplify complex real-world scenarios where multiple feature types interact

## Confidence

- High confidence: The categorization of OOD data generation methods and evaluation protocols is well-supported by existing literature and provides a useful organizing framework
- Medium confidence: The unification of three research lines under the distribution shift framework is conceptually sound but requires empirical validation across diverse NLP tasks
- Low confidence: The generalization argument that improving semantic feature acquisition will enhance performance across all OOD types needs more rigorous testing with real-world datasets
