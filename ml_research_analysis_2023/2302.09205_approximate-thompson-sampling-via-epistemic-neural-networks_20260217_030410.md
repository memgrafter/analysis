# Approximate Thompson Sampling via Epistemic Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09205
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09205
- Reference count: 9
- Epinets match large ensemble performance in Thompson sampling at orders of magnitude lower computational cost.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the use of epistemic neural networks (ENNs) for approximate Thompson sampling in decision-making tasks. The authors demonstrate that ENNs producing accurate joint predictive distributions in a synthetic testbed also excel in bandit and reinforcement learning environments. A key contribution is the epinet architecture—a small additive network that estimates uncertainty—which achieves performance comparable to large ensembles while drastically reducing computational cost. The results emphasize that high-quality joint predictive distributions are critical for effective decision-making, even when the generative model of the environment differs from the testbed. The accompanying open-source code facilitates further research on ENNs for deep reinforcement learning.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates several ENN architectures for approximate Thompson sampling, focusing on their ability to generate joint predictive distributions. A central innovation is the epinet, a compact additive network designed to estimate epistemic uncertainty efficiently. The authors compare epinets to larger ensemble methods across synthetic testbeds, bandit tasks, and reinforcement learning problems, measuring decision-making performance as a function of predictive quality. The experimental setup includes environments with varying levels of uncertainty and structural differences to test robustness. All code and benchmarks are made publicly available to support reproducibility and future work.

## Key Results
- Epinets achieve performance on par with large ensembles in Thompson sampling tasks, but at orders of magnitude lower computational cost.
- Better joint predictive distributions in the synthetic testbed correlate strongly with improved performance in downstream bandit and RL tasks.
- The quality of joint predictive distributions is crucial for effective decision-making, even when the environment's generative model differs from the testbed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper shows that ENNs, particularly epinets, are effective at capturing epistemic uncertainty in a way that translates directly to better decision-making under uncertainty. By producing high-quality joint predictive distributions, ENNs enable Thompson sampling to explore and exploit more effectively in bandit and RL settings. The epinet's compact architecture allows it to model uncertainty efficiently, avoiding the computational overhead of large ensembles while retaining their benefits. The experiments demonstrate that this approach is robust even when the environment's generative process is not perfectly aligned with the testbed.

## Foundational Learning
- **Epistemic Uncertainty**: Uncertainty due to lack of knowledge about the true model; crucial for decision-making in uncertain environments.
  - *Why needed*: Enables exploration and risk-aware decisions.
  - *Quick check*: Does the method quantify uncertainty over predictions?
- **Thompson Sampling**: A Bayesian approach to balancing exploration and exploitation by sampling from the posterior.
  - *Why needed*: Core algorithm for decision-making under uncertainty.
  - *Quick check*: Is sampling from the predictive distribution used for action selection?
- **Joint Predictive Distributions**: Modeling uncertainty over entire prediction vectors, not just individual outputs.
  - *Why needed*: Necessary for coherent uncertainty quantification in multi-action settings.
  - *Quick check*: Are dependencies between outputs captured?
- **Additive Networks**: Architectures where uncertainty is modeled by adding a small network to the base model.
  - *Why needed*: Enables efficient uncertainty estimation without full ensembles.
  - *Quick check*: Is the uncertainty model lightweight and additive?
- **Bandit and RL Tasks**: Sequential decision problems where actions have uncertain outcomes.
  - *Why needed*: Standard benchmarks for decision-making algorithms.
  - *Quick check*: Are both exploration and exploitation strategies evaluated?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Epinet (small additive network) -> Joint Predictive Distribution -> Thompson Sampling Action Selection

**Critical Path:**
Data → Base Network → Epinet → Joint Predictive Distribution → Thompson Sampling → Action

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Epinet vs. Ensemble: Computational efficiency vs. potential expressiveness.
- Additive uncertainty modeling vs. full Bayesian inference: Speed vs. theoretical rigor.

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor uncertainty estimates leading to over-exploration or under-exploration.
- Degradation in performance under domain shift or limited data.

## Limitations
- Performance under limited data or heavy domain shifts is not thoroughly explored.
- No investigation of overfitting risks with the small epinet architecture.
- Computational scaling in high-dimensional state-action spaces is not addressed.

## Confidence
- **High**: Epinets achieve comparable performance to large ensembles in tested settings.
- **Medium**: Joint predictive quality directly translates to RL performance across diverse environments.
- **Low**: Robustness guarantees under data scarcity or distribution shift.

## Next Checks
