# Skip Connections in Spiking Neural Networks: An Analysis of Their Effect on Network Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13563
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13563
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: Optimizing skip connections improves SNN accuracy by an average of +8% on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10-DVS, and DVS128 Gesture datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how skip connections affect spiking neural network (SNN) training and proposes a hyperparameter optimization method to adapt models from artificial neural networks (ANNs) to SNNs. The authors analyze two types of skip connections—DenseNet-like (DSC) and addition-type (ASC)—and demonstrate that optimizing their number, position, and type can significantly improve SNN accuracy and efficiency. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10-DVS, and DVS128 Gesture datasets show an average +8% accuracy increase when optimizing skip connections in resnet18, densenet121, and mobilenetv2 architectures. The results highlight the importance of skip connections for improving SNN performance and suggest that optimizing these architectural elements can help bridge the accuracy gap between SNNs and their ANN counterparts.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a hyperparameter optimization framework using Bayesian optimization to select the best number, position, and type of skip connections for SNNs. The method involves extracting block architectures from pre-trained ANNs, generating adjacency matrices to define skip connection topologies, and evaluating candidate SNN topologies via fine-tuning. The search space includes varying numbers of skip connections (nskip) and two types: DenseNet-like (DSC) and addition-type (ASC). The optimization process uses a Gaussian Process (GP) model with expected improvement (EI) acquisition to minimize accuracy drop. The approach is validated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10-DVS, and DVS128 Gesture datasets using resnet18, densenet121, and mobilenetv2 architectures.

## Key Results
- Optimizing skip connections in SNNs improves accuracy by an average of +8% on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10-DVS, and DVS128 Gesture datasets.
- DenseNet-like skip connections (DSC) enhance accuracy by increasing feature reuse and temporal spike integration.
- Addition-type skip connections (ASC) reduce average firing rate, improving energy efficiency without significant accuracy loss.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Skip connections alleviate vanishing gradients in SNNs by creating direct gradient paths.
- Mechanism: Skip connections bypass intermediate layers, allowing gradients to flow directly from output to earlier layers, counteracting the vanishing gradient problem inherent in unrolled SNN training.
- Core assumption: Gradient flow in SNNs is bottlenecked by temporal unfolding and non-differentiable spike functions.

- Break condition: If surrogate gradient approximations become exact or temporal unrolling is eliminated, skip connections may offer diminishing returns.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DenseNet-like skip connections improve SNN accuracy by increasing feature reuse and temporal spike integration.
- Mechanism: Concatenation of multiple layer outputs increases the temporal and spatial resolution of spike information, allowing later layers to benefit from richer, temporally diverse input patterns.
- Core assumption: Spiking neurons encode information in spike timing and density, and richer input patterns enhance classification accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "optimizing the position, type, and number of skip connections can significantly improve the accuracy and efficiency of SNNs"
  - [section] "We consider a generalized version where we vary the number of skip connections by randomly selecting only some channels for concatenation."
  - [corpus] Weak—corpus lacks direct studies on feature reuse via concatenation in SNNs.
- Break condition: If concatenation drastically increases MACs without proportional accuracy gain, the approach becomes inefficient.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Addition-type skip connections reduce average firing rate, improving energy efficiency without significant accuracy loss.
- Mechanism: Element-wise summation aggregates spikes from multiple layers, increasing spike density and enabling faster firing decisions, which reduces the number of timesteps needed for convergence.
- Core assumption: Lower firing rates correlate with reduced energy consumption on neuromorphic hardware, and summation can maintain signal integrity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The summation operation adds up the spikes of multiple inputs, which increases the overall firing rate of the network"
  - [abstract] "optimizing the position, type, and number of skip connections can significantly improve the accuracy and efficiency of SNNs"
  - [corpus] Weak—no corpus neighbor explicitly studies firing rate vs energy trade-offs in SNNs with skip connections.
- Break condition: If summation causes spike saturation or loss of temporal precision, accuracy may degrade despite lower firing rates.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input layer → Convolution blocks with configurable skip connections (DSC/ASC) → Output layer
  Adjacency matrix defines skip connection topology per block
  Bayesian optimization loop selects adjacency matrices to minimize accuracy drop

- Critical path:
  1. Extract block architecture from ANN
  2. Generate adjacency matrix search space
  3. Evaluate candidate SNN topologies via fine-tuning
  4. Update GP model and select next candidates

- Design tradeoffs:
  - DSC vs ASC: DSC improves accuracy but increases MACs; ASC reduces MACs but may lower firing rates
  - Number of skip connections: More connections improve accuracy but increase search space complexity
  - Fine-tuning epochs: Fewer epochs speed up search but may miss optimal configurations

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy plateaus or degrades with added skip connections (overfitting or saturation)
  - Average firing rate remains low despite DSC addition (ineffective concatenation)
  - Bayesian optimization converges slowly or oscillates (poor GP kernel or acquisition function)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary nskip from 0 to 3 in a single block with DSC only; measure accuracy and firing rate
  2. Replace DSC with ASC in the same block; compare MACs and firing rates
  3. Run Bayesian optimization with UCB acquisition on a two-block resnet18; record convergence speed and final accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do skip connections affect the energy efficiency of SNNs compared to ANNs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that skip connections can improve accuracy and efficiency of SNNs by enabling faster convergence and increasing information flow through the network. However, the specific energy efficiency gains compared to ANNs are not quantified.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on accuracy improvements and does not provide direct energy consumption measurements or comparisons with ANNs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the energy consumption of optimized SNNs with and without skip connections, and comparing these results to equivalent ANNs on the same tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal type and number of skip connections for different SNN architectures and tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the effects of different types of skip connections (densenet-like and addition-type) and varying numbers of skip connections on SNN performance. However, it does not provide a definitive answer on the optimal configuration.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents a hyperparameter optimization method to find the best configuration, but the results are specific to the tested architectures and datasets. Generalizing these findings to other architectures and tasks remains an open question.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing various combinations of skip connection types and numbers across a wide range of SNN architectures and diverse tasks, along with theoretical analysis to explain the observed trends.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do backward connections impact SNN performance and can they be optimized using hyperparameter tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention incorporating backward connections as a potential direction for future work in the conclusion section.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on forward skip connections and does not explore the effects of backward connections on SNN training and performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments incorporating backward connections in SNNs, optimizing their parameters using hyperparameter tuning methods, and comparing the results with forward-only skip connections and standard SNNs.

## Limitations

- The exact impact of skip connections on gradient propagation in SNNs remains theoretically underexplored, with limited empirical validation beyond reported accuracy gains.
- The trade-off between accuracy improvements and computational overhead (increased MACs) for DenseNet-style skip connections is not fully quantified across different datasets.
- The generalization of skip connection benefits to other SNN architectures beyond resnet18, densenet121, and mobilenetv2 is not established.

## Confidence

- High confidence in the mechanism of skip connections alleviating vanishing gradients due to direct gradient paths.
- Medium confidence in the efficiency gains from addition-type skip connections reducing firing rates, as the relationship between firing rate and energy consumption is not explicitly validated.
- Medium confidence in the overall accuracy improvements (+8% average) reported, given the lack of detailed ablation studies on individual architectural components.
