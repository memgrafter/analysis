# Appropriateness is all you need!

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14553
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14553
- Reference count: 1
- Primary result: Current chatbot safety approaches focusing on harm avoidance are insufficient; appropriateness frameworks (technical-discursive, social, moral) offer better guidance for normative chatbot behavior.

## Executive Summary
This paper argues that the prevailing approach to chatbot safety, which emphasizes avoiding harmful outputs, is inadequate for ensuring appropriate conversational behavior. The authors propose shifting from a safety-normativity framework to one centered on appropriateness, encompassing three dimensions: technical-discursive, social, and moral. They introduce the PAVA framework (Positionality, Acceptability, Value Alignment) as concrete criteria for implementing and evaluating this approach. The paper advocates for using challenge sets to validate chatbot responses against these appropriateness criteria, offering a more nuanced alternative to current safety-focused methodologies.

## Method Summary
The paper does not present a specific technical method or implementation. Instead, it proposes a conceptual framework for chatbot evaluation based on appropriateness rather than safety. The approach involves developing criteria for technical-discursive, social, and moral appropriateness through the PAVA framework, designing challenge sets to test these criteria, and implementing a method to evaluate chatbot responses against these standards. The paper outlines the need for this shift in perspective but does not provide detailed implementation or training procedures.

## Key Results
- Appropriateness frameworks offer better differentiation than safety-normativity alone for chatbot evaluation
- PAVA (Positionality, Acceptability, Value Alignment) provides actionable criteria for appropriateness
- Challenge sets can operationalize appropriateness requirements through targeted validation scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Appropriateness as a normative framework provides better differentiation than safety-normativity alone.
- Mechanism: Appropriateness introduces three distinct categories (technical-discursive, social, moral) that capture nuanced conversational expectations beyond mere harm avoidance.
- Core assumption: Conversational norms extend beyond harm prevention to include context-sensitive behavioral expectations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The paper argues that "rather than looking for 'safety' in a chatbot's utterances to determine what they may and may not say, we ought to assess those utterances according to three forms of appropriateness: technical-discursive, social, and moral."
  - [section] "We claim that 'appropriateness' (as defined below) is a much better suited approach to the normativity than safety to determine what chatbots may and may not say."
  - [corpus] Corpus shows related work on appropriateness frameworks and evaluations, supporting the novelty of this multi-dimensional approach.
- Break condition: If appropriateness criteria cannot be operationalized into measurable benchmarks, the framework becomes too abstract to implement.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Positionality, Acceptability, and Value Alignment (PAVA) provide concrete implementation criteria for appropriateness.
- Mechanism: PAVA transforms abstract appropriateness concepts into actionable design requirements for chatbot behavior and evaluation.
- Core assumption: Concrete metrics can be derived from philosophical concepts of positionality, acceptability, and value alignment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We elaborated appropriateness along three conditions (PAVA) to find reliable metrics to test general-purpose chatbots against to ensure their appropriateness in responding to even more challenging inputs."
  - [section] "Positionality refers to a specific and known subjectivity of a conversation participant... Acceptability could give more detailed guidance... Value Alignment plays into both positionality, acceptability, and into appropriateness at large."
  - [corpus] Corpus neighbors include papers on frameworks for evaluating appropriateness and trustworthiness, suggesting practical implementation is being explored.
- Break condition: If PAVA criteria conflict with each other or with practical chatbot functionality, implementation becomes intractable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Challenge sets provide operational validation for appropriateness requirements.
- Mechanism: Hand-crafted challenge sets can test chatbot responses against appropriateness criteria that safety metrics alone miss.
- Core assumption: Targeted test scenarios can reveal appropriateness failures that general safety metrics would miss.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To make these rather philosophical considerations applicable for validation and thus useful for improving the utterances of chatbots, we suggest the use of challenge sets."
  - [section] "Proposed in machine translation (MT) contexts, Isabelle, Cherry and Foster (2017) introduce hand-crafted challenges that reflect specific linguistic problems not easily overcome by pure machine-learning methods."
  - [corpus] Corpus includes papers on challenge sets and benchmarks for evaluating LLM behavior, supporting this approach.
- Break condition: If challenge sets cannot be designed to cover the full range of appropriateness contexts, they will provide incomplete validation.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Technical-discursive appropriateness
  - Why needed here: Forms the baseline conversational competence chatbots must demonstrate before social and moral appropriateness can be assessed.
  - Quick check question: Can a chatbot maintain logical consistency and conversational coherence even when discussing controversial topics?

- Concept: Social appropriateness
  - Why needed here: Captures context-sensitive behavioral norms that vary across cultures and relationships, which safety metrics typically ignore.
  - Quick check question: Does the chatbot adjust its communication style appropriately when role-playing different social contexts?

- Concept: Moral appropriateness
  - Why needed here: Addresses positive duties and ethical obligations beyond harm avoidance that are critical for trustworthy conversational agents.
  - Quick check question: Will the chatbot respond appropriately when a user discloses distress, rather than simply ending the conversation to avoid unsafe outputs?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input processing → Context analysis → Appropriateness evaluation (PAVA) → Response generation → Safety filtering → Output
- Critical path: User input → Context classification → PAVA appropriateness check → Response generation → Final safety validation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Granularity vs. performance: More nuanced appropriateness checks increase computational cost
  - Positionality vs. flexibility: Fixed chatbot persona improves consistency but may limit conversational range
  - Challenge set coverage vs. practical implementation: Comprehensive testing requires significant resources
- Failure signatures:
  - Positionality conflicts: Chatbot persona shifts inconsistently across conversations
  - Acceptability failures: Chatbot accepts clearly inappropriate requests
  - Value alignment drift: Chatbot behavior diverges from intended ethical framework over time
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test chatbot with counterfactual scenarios to evaluate moral appropriateness reasoning
  2. Implement positionality configuration and measure consistency across varied conversational contexts
  3. Design challenge sets targeting social appropriateness failures in cross-cultural communication scenarios

## Limitations

- The paper presents a conceptual framework without technical implementation or empirical validation
- Lacks concrete operational definitions and measurable criteria for PAVA components
- No specific metrics or experimental results to verify practical efficacy

## Confidence

- High confidence: The identification of current safety-normativity limitations in chatbot design
- Medium confidence: The proposed three-dimensional appropriateness framework (technical-discursive, social, moral)
- Low confidence: The operationalization of PAVA criteria and their effectiveness in real-world implementation
