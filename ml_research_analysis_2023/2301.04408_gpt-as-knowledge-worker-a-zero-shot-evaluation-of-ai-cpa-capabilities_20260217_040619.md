# GPT as Knowledge Worker: A Zero-Shot Evaluation of (AI)CPA Capabilities

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.04408
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04408
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: GPT-3.5 approaches human-level performance on CPA knowledge work tasks without calculations, scoring 57.6% accuracy significantly above 25% guessing baseline

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates OpenAI's text-davinci-003 and prior GPT models on the AICPA Uniform CPA Examination to assess their readiness for knowledge work tasks. The zero-shot evaluation reveals that while GPT-3.5 performs poorly on quantitative reasoning (14.4% correct), it approaches human-level performance on foundational knowledge and application tasks without calculations, achieving 57.6% accuracy overall. Performance varies significantly by section, with best results in Business Environment and Concepts (69.7%) and weakest in Financial Accounting and Reporting (51.0%). The study demonstrates substantial improvements across GPT-3 generations, with text-davinci-003 scoring 57.6% compared to 29.9% for text-davinci-001. These results suggest large language models are approaching human performance on many knowledge work tasks and could transform the quality and efficiency of future knowledge work.

## Method Summary
The study uses zero-shot prompting to evaluate GPT models on two CPA exam assessments: a sample Regulation exam (40 questions) and synthetic multiple-choice questions (208 total) based on AICPA Blueprints across four sections. The authors test various prompt engineering techniques including answer type specifications, contextualization variations, and justification requirements, using OpenAI API with different temperature settings (0.0, 0.5, 1.0) and best_of parameters (1, 2, 4). Performance is measured against a 25% guessing baseline and compared across GPT-3 model generations to assess capability improvements.

## Key Results
- GPT-3.5 achieves 57.6% accuracy on CPA exam questions without calculations, significantly above 25% guessing baseline
- Model performs poorly on quantitative reasoning tasks (14.4% correct) but excels in foundational knowledge sections
- Performance varies by section: BEC (69.7%), AUD (57%), REG (53%), FAR (51%)
- Substantial generational improvements: text-davinci-003 (57.6%) vs text-davinci-001 (29.9%)
- Strong non-entailment capabilities with top two answers correct 82.1% of the time

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-3.5 performs well on foundational knowledge work tasks when quantitative reasoning is excluded
- Mechanism: Large-scale pretraining enables pattern recognition and knowledge application across domains
- Core assumption: Training data includes sufficient domain coverage for professional knowledge work
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section performance data showing 57.6% accuracy and section-wise breakdowns
- Break condition: Performance drops significantly when tasks require quantitative reasoning

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Performance improves dramatically across GPT-3 model generations
- Mechanism: Architectural improvements and training scale enhance pattern recognition and contextual understanding
- Core assumption: Each generation represents substantial improvements over previous versions
- Evidence anchors: Abstract showing improvement from 30% to 57% accuracy across generations
- Break condition: Performance gains may stall if architectural improvements plateau

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Model demonstrates strong non-entailment capabilities with top two answers correct over 82% of the time
- Mechanism: Probabilistic ranking mechanisms effectively identify plausible answers
- Core assumption: Ranking mechanism is more reliable than single-best-answer selection
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section data on non-entailment performance
- Break condition: Ranking fails on questions requiring nuanced understanding

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Assessment preparation pipeline → Prompt engineering interface → OpenAI API integration → Response parsing and scoring engine → Experiment tracking system → Analysis and reporting tools
- **Critical path**: Question preparation → Prompt generation → API call → Response parsing → Scoring → Analysis → Reporting
- **Design tradeoffs**: Zero-shot evaluation maximizes generalizability but limits performance compared to fine-tuned approaches; prompt engineering requires extensive experimentation but no model modifications
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on quantitative questions, inconsistent results across temperature settings, hallucinated references in explanations, inability to follow complex instructions
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run baseline prompt (no context, no justification) across all temperature settings to establish performance baseline
  2. Test contextualization variations (different professional roles) to identify optimal framing
  3. Evaluate top-k answer generation to measure non-entailment capabilities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How will GPT-3.5's performance on knowledge work tasks change as new models are released?
- Basis in paper: The paper documents material improvements across GPT-3 generations from 30% to 57% accuracy
- Why unresolved: Paper only evaluates a few generations and cannot predict future improvements
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing newer generations on AICPA assessment and comparing performance

### Open Question 2
- Question: How will GPT-3.5's performance on knowledge work tasks change as new fine-tuning techniques are developed?
- Basis in paper: Paper focuses on zero-shot performance and acknowledges fine-tuning could improve results
- Why unresolved: Paper does not explore impact of fine-tuning on performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Fine-tuning GPT-3.5 on CPA assessment and comparing to zero-shot performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How will GPT-3.5's performance on knowledge work tasks change as new prompt engineering techniques are developed?
- Basis in paper: Paper experiments with different prompt engineering techniques and finds some improve performance
- Why unresolved: Paper only explores limited techniques and cannot predict future improvements
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing new prompt engineering techniques on GPT-3.5 and comparing performance

## Limitations
- Evaluation is zero-shot only, potentially underestimating model capabilities compared to fine-tuned approaches
- Proprietary nature of text-davinci-003 limits understanding of architectural and training details
- Sample size (248 questions) may not fully capture real-world knowledge work complexity
- Focus on multiple-choice formats may not represent diverse task types in actual professional practice

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Claims about relative performance across GPT-3 model generations showing clear improvement trends from 30% to 57% accuracy
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about approaching human-level performance on foundational knowledge tasks without calculations, given 57.6% accuracy and 25% guessing baseline comparison
- **Low confidence**: Claims about specific capability ceilings or limitations without further experimentation, particularly regarding quantitative reasoning performance (14.4% correct)

## Next Checks

3. **Human-in-the-loop comparison**: Conduct controlled experiment where human CPAs and GPT-3.5 answer same question sets under identical conditions, measuring both accuracy and time efficiency
