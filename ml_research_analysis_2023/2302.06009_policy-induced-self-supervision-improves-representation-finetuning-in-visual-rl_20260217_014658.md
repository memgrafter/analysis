# Policy-Induced Self-Supervision Improves Representation Finetuning in Visual RL

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06009
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06009
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Finetuning pretrained representations improves transfer learning in visual RL, and the proposed PiSCO self-supervised objective that clusters representations by induced policy outperforms frozen, standard finetuned, and other self-supervised alternatives across multiple benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how to transfer representations pretrained on source tasks to target tasks in visual reinforcement learning. It analyzes freezing versus finetuning pretrained representations, revealing that finetuning is required even when representations perfectly capture target task information, and that finetuned representations improve learnability and robustness to noise. The study shows that pretrained bottom layers are task-agnostic and readily transferable, while top layers encode task-specific information requiring adaptation. Based on these insights, the authors propose a self-supervised objective (PiSCO) that clusters representations according to the policy they induce, rather than traditional similarity measures. Combined with freezing bottom layers, PiSCO significantly outperforms frozen, finetuned, and self-supervised alternatives across multiple benchmarks including DeepMind Control and Habitat tasks.

## Method Summary
The method involves pretraining a visual feature encoder on source tasks or ImageNet, then transferring it to downstream RL tasks. The authors propose partial freezing where early layers are frozen (identified as task-agnostic) while top layers are finetuned with an auxiliary policy-induced self-consistency objective (PiSCO). PiSCO clusters representations based on the KL divergence between action distributions induced by different augmentations of the same state, encouraging representations that lead to consistent policy decisions. The approach is combined with standard RL algorithms like DrQ-v2 and evaluated across multiple domains including DeepMind Control, MSR Jump, and Habitat navigation tasks.

## Key Results
- Finetuning is required even when pretrained representations perfectly capture target task information
- Finetuned representations improve learnability by creating purer action clusters and being more robust to noise
- Pretrained bottom layers are task-agnostic and readily transferable, while top layers encode task-specific information requiring adaptation
- PiSCO significantly outperforms frozen, finetuned, and self-supervised alternatives across multiple benchmarks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Finetuning is required even when pretrained representations perfectly capture the information required to solve the target task
- Mechanism: Representations must be adapted to emphasize task-relevant information for decision making, not just capture raw information
- Core assumption: The downstream RL algorithm benefits from representations that cluster states according to the actions they induce, not just by visual similarity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "first, finetuning is required even when pretrained representations perfectly capture the information required to solve the target task"
  - [section 3.3] "both pretrained and finetuned representations can perfectly regress the distance and optimal actions: they get sub-pixel estimation error (0.94 and 0.79, respectively) and perfect accuracy (both 100%)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations, but related to "What Happens During Finetuning of Vision Transformers" which studies invariance during finetuning
- Break condition: If representations could be directly used without adaptation while maintaining learnability and robustness to noise

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Finetuned representations improve learnability by creating purer action clusters and being more robust to noise
- Mechanism: Finetuning refines representations so that states requiring similar actions are closer in representation space, making it easier for RL algorithms to learn policy boundaries
- Core assumption: RL algorithms learn more efficiently when similar states (in terms of optimal action) are represented more similarly
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.4] "Finetuned representations yield purer clusters... Finetuned representations (98.75%) than for Frozen (91.41%) or Random (82.98%)"
  - [section 3.4] "Finetuned representations are more robust to perturbations... Finetuned is more robust and degrades more slowly"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations, but related to "Adversarial Finetuning with Latent Representation Constraint" which addresses robustness trade-offs
- Break condition: If frozen representations already provide sufficient cluster purity and noise robustness for the RL algorithm

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Task-agnostic information is computed in early layers while task-specific information is refined in later layers
- Mechanism: Early layers capture general visual features that transfer well across tasks, while later layers adapt these features for specific task requirements
- Core assumption: Visual representations naturally decompose into transferable (early) and task-specific (late) components
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.5] "pretrained bottom layers are task-agnostic and readily transferable to new tasks, while top layers encode task-specific information and require adaptation"
  - [section 3.5] "both Frozen and Finetuned curves trend downwards and lie significantly lower than Random, indicating that representations specialize for decision making as we move up the feature encoder"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations, but related to "Efficient Finetuning for Dimensional Speech Emotion Recognition" which discusses layer-wise adaptation
- Break condition: If all layers equally contribute to both task-agnostic and task-specific information

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Visual feature encoder (CNN or ConvNeXt)
  - Policy head (MLP with GELU activations)
  - Value head (twin Q-networks for DrQ-v2)
  - Projector network (h(·)) for PiSCO
  - Data augmentation pipeline for state perturbations

- Critical path:
  1. Pretrain feature encoder on source tasks or ImageNet
  2. Identify transferable layers through linear probing
  3. Freeze early layers, finetune later layers with PiSCO
  4. Optimize RL objective with auxiliary PiSCO loss

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Freezing more layers increases stability but may limit task-specific adaptation
  - Stronger PiSCO weighting improves consistency but may slow RL convergence
  - Larger projectors capture more information but increase computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - If finetuning collapses to freezing behavior, learning rate is too low
  - If representations don't improve over random initialization, task gap is too large
  - If PiSCO hurts performance, weighting is too high or augmentation is too aggressive

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why does finetuning improve learnability even when pretrained representations perfectly capture task-relevant information?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that frozen representations can perfectly regress optimal actions and agent-obstacle distance on MSR Jump tasks, yet finetuned representations still outperform frozen ones during RL transfer
- Why unresolved: The paper hypothesizes that finetuning emphasizes task-relevant information for decision making, but doesn't provide a mechanistic explanation of how this happens at the representation level or why frozen representations with sufficient information still fail to transfer
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing frozen representations with selectively masked task-relevant features, or ablation studies showing which aspects of the representations are modified during finetuning and how they correlate with improved decision-making

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the policy-induced self-consistency objective (PiSCO) generalize to multi-step temporal dependencies and longer-horizon tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] PiSCO currently measures similarity between perturbed versions of the same state, but most RL tasks require reasoning about sequences of actions over time
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates PiSCO on relatively simple tasks with immediate action consequences, and doesn't address how the objective would handle delayed rewards or state transitions that depend on action sequences
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing PiSCO performance on tasks with sparse rewards, long time horizons, or requiring memory of past states, along with modifications to the objective to incorporate temporal consistency

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between the layers frozen during finetuning and the hierarchical structure of visual features in convolutional networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that early layers compute task-agnostic information while later layers encode task-specific information, and freezing early layers improves transfer performance
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore whether the optimal freezing strategy correlates with established theories of visual feature hierarchies (edges→textures→parts→objects), or whether this relationship holds across different architectures
- What evidence would resolve it: Layer-wise analysis showing which types of visual features are preserved vs. modified during finetuning, and whether freezing strategies optimized for one task generalize to others with different visual requirements

## Limitations
- The analysis focuses on a limited set of downstream tasks, leaving uncertainty about generalizability to other RL domains
- The study assumes access to a pretrained feature encoder, which may not always be available in practice
- Computational cost of PiSCO and partial freezing is not thoroughly analyzed

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Finetuning improves learnability and robustness compared to frozen representations, supported by clear quantitative evidence across multiple metrics (action accuracy, distance estimation, cluster purity).
- **Medium confidence**: Task-agnostic vs. task-specific layer decomposition, as the evidence shows trends but could benefit from more rigorous statistical analysis across different architectures.
- **Medium confidence**: PiSCO's superiority over traditional self-supervised methods, as results are compelling but could be affected by hyperparameter choices specific to each method.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-domain robustness test**: Apply PiSCO to transfer between substantially different visual domains (e.g., synthetic to real-world) to validate the robustness claims beyond the current experimental scope.

2. **Layer-wise ablation study**: Systematically vary the number of frozen layers (not just early vs. late) to precisely identify the optimal freezing point and validate the task-agnostic/task-specific layer hypothesis.

3. **Computational efficiency analysis**: Measure and compare the wall-clock training time and memory usage of PiSCO versus frozen and standard finetuning methods across different batch sizes and projector dimensions.
