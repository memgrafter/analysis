# ManyDG: Many-domain Generalization for Healthcare Applications

## Quick Facts

- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08834

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of domain generalization in healthcare applications where patient-specific characteristics hinder model performance on new patients. The authors propose ManyDG, a novel method that explicitly identifies and removes patient-specific covariates to improve generalization. By treating each patient as a separate domain and using mutual reconstruction with orthogonal projection, ManyDG learns domain-invariant features that better capture label-relevant information. Experiments on four healthcare datasets demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods, with ManyDG achieving up to 14.6% higher accuracy in seizure detection and 13.3% in drug recommendation tasks.

## Method Summary
ManyDG is a many-domain generalization method designed for healthcare applications that treats each patient as a separate domain. The method uses a Siamese-type architecture with a double data loader that outputs paired samples from the same patient. It employs four objective functions: supervised loss, maximum mean discrepancy (MMD), mutual reconstruction, and domain similarity. The key innovation is the use of orthogonal projection to extract label information by removing domain components from feature representations, combined with mutual reconstruction to learn domain-invariant features. The model is trained end-to-end to jointly optimize these objectives, resulting in improved generalization performance across multiple healthcare tasks.

## Key Results
- Achieves 14.6% higher accuracy than state-of-the-art methods on seizure detection task
- Improves drug recommendation accuracy by 13.3% compared to baselines
- Demonstrates consistent performance gains across all four healthcare datasets with varying patient counts (78 to 140,000 patients)
- Shows effectiveness in scenarios with limited data and continuous learning settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Patient-specific covariates act as spurious correlations that hurt generalization
- **Mechanism**: Unique patient characteristics (environment, identity) are independent of prediction targets but get learned as signal, causing overfitting to training patients
- **Core assumption**: Patient covariates are present in the data and do not causally influence the target labels
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "patient-level data unavoidably contains some unique personalized characteristics (namely, patient covariates)...These patient covariates can cause spurious correlations in learning a DNN model"
  - [section 1]: "patient-level data unavoidably contains some unique personalized characteristics...These patient covariates can cause spurious correlations in learning a DNN model"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus papers don't directly address patient covariates as spurious correlations
- **Break condition**: If patient covariates actually contain predictive signal for the target labels (i.e., they are not truly spurious)

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Mutual reconstruction enables learning domain-invariant features
- **Mechanism**: By reconstructing features using domain factor from one sample and label from another, the model learns to separate domain and label information
- **Core assumption**: Two samples from the same patient share domain but may have different labels
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.3]: "use the domain factor of x and the label of x′ to reconstruct the feature of x′...Conversely, we reconstruct v by the domain factor of x′ and the label of x"
  - [section 3.3]: "This mutual reconstruction design enables to learn better domain factors z, z′"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus papers don't discuss mutual reconstruction for domain generalization
- **Break condition**: If domain factors cannot be reliably extracted or if the reconstruction process doesn't force separation of domain and label information

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Orthogonal projection extracts label information by removing domain components
- **Mechanism**: The feature vector v is decomposed into components parallel (v||z) and orthogonal (v⊥z) to the domain factor z, with v⊥z containing label-relevant information
- **Core assumption**: Domain and label information are approximately aligned along different dimensions in the feature space
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 3.3]: "v and z are in the same embedding space, we decompose v into two parts...Another component orthogonal to z, denoted as v⊥z, which contains the remaining information, i.e., the label representation"
  - [section 4.4]: "we empirically find our universal feature extractorhθ(·) tends to generate v that contains both domain and label information approximately in different embedding dimensions"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus papers don't discuss orthogonal projection for domain generalization
- **Break condition**: If domain and label information are not separable along different dimensions, making orthogonal projection ineffective

## Limitations
- The orthogonal projection assumption requires domain and label information to be approximately separable along different dimensions, which may not hold for all healthcare datasets
- Mutual reconstruction depends on having sufficient paired samples from the same patients during training, which could be challenging with limited or heterogeneous patient data
- The approach assumes patient covariates are purely spurious correlations, which may not be universally true across all healthcare prediction tasks

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The experimental results showing ManyDG outperforms baselines on all four healthcare datasets, as these are directly measured and reported
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism of orthogonal projection for extracting label information, as this relies on the assumption that domain and label information are separable, which is empirically observed but not theoretically proven
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that patient covariates are purely spurious correlations, as this assumes patient characteristics never contain predictive signal for the target labels, which may not be universally true
