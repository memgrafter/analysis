# Multi-Objective Population Based Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01436
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01436
- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MO-PBT, a multi-objective extension of Population Based Training (PBT) for hyperparameter optimization. The key innovation is adapting PBT's population-based approach to handle multiple conflicting objectives by using non-dominated sorting and domination-based selection, rather than scalarizing objectives. Experiments on diverse tasks—Precision/Recall, Accuracy/Fairness, and Accuracy/Adversarial Robustness—demonstrate that MO-PBT consistently outperforms random search, single-objective PBT, and the state-of-the-art MO-ASHA algorithm. Specifically, MO-PBT achieves superior hypervolume scores and produces more diverse Pareto fronts, offering better trade-offs between objectives. The method scales well with population size and search space, making it a practical and efficient solution for multi-objective hyperparameter optimization in real-world applications.

## Method Summary
MO-PBT extends PBT to multi-objective optimization by maintaining a population of models and using domination-based ranking to guide selection. After each training phase, solutions are ranked using non-dominated sorting, partitioning them into fronts where solutions in earlier fronts dominate those in later ones. Within each front, greedy scattered subset selection further ranks solutions based on their distance from others in objective space, favoring diversity. The top performers replace the bottom performers via copying of both weights and hyperparameters, followed by exploration via local perturbation. The algorithm is evaluated on three multi-objective HPO tasks using hypervolume and coverage metrics, demonstrating superior performance compared to random search, single-objective PBT, and MO-ASHA.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: MO-PBT outperforms scalarization-based single-objective PBT because it directly handles the multi-objective structure rather than collapsing objectives.

- Core assumption: Scalarization can bias the search toward one objective, missing parts of the Pareto front.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "using non-dominated sorting and domination-based selection, rather than scalarizing objectives"

- Break Condition: If objectives are nearly aligned or one dominates the others, scalarization might perform comparably or better due to reduced computational overhead.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The explore operator's local mutation strategy maintains beneficial traits while exploring nearby hyperparameter space, improving convergence speed.
- Mechanism: The explore operator perturbs hyperparameters by shifting to nearby ordinal values (or resampling rarely), keeping changes small and preserving useful hyperparameter configurations.
- Core assumption: Hyperparameter spaces are structured such that small changes lead to gradual performance changes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The key idea of the operator is locality: the new value of a hyperparameter is chosen from the vicinity of the current value"

- Break Condition: If the search space is highly discrete or the optimal configuration is far from current ones, local exploration may be too slow.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Population container -> Training loop -> Evaluation & ranking -> Exploit operator -> Explore operator -> Ready condition
- Critical path:
  1. Initialize population with random hyperparameters
  2. Train all models for 2 epochs
  3. Evaluate and rank via non-dominated sort + greedy scattered subset
  4. Replace bottom τ% with copies of top τ%
  5. Perturb hyperparameters of copied models
  6. Repeat until budget exhausted
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Population size vs. wall-clock time: larger populations improve hypervolume but increase training cost
  - Explore strategy: local mutation preserves good traits but may slow global search
  - Ranking within fronts: greedy scattered subset favors diversity; crowding distance is an alternative
- Failure signatures:
  - Premature convergence: population collapses to a single front; diversity drops
  - Exploit inefficiency: bottom performers not improving; may indicate poor ranking
  - Explore stagnation: hyperparameters not changing; may indicate overly conservative perturbation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run MO-PBT on a simple synthetic bi-objective problem with known Pareto front to verify correct non-dominated sorting.
  2. Compare MO-PBT against scalarized PBT on a toy hyperparameter problem to observe front coverage differences.
  3. Measure hypervolume progression over time on Precision/Recall task to confirm scaling with population size.

## Limitations
- The claimed advantage over scalarization is inferred from general MO literature rather than demonstrated through controlled ablation studies.
- The explore operator's local mutation strategy, while intuitive, has no direct evidence in the corpus supporting its superiority over alternative exploration strategies.
- The core mechanism of domination-based selection is plausible but lacks direct experimental validation in the paper corpus.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The basic framework of extending PBT to multi-objective optimization using non-dominated sorting is sound and well-established in the literature.
- **Medium Confidence**: The empirical results showing MO-PBT outperforming baselines are promising, but the lack of direct comparisons with other MO-HPO methods in the corpus limits definitive conclusions.
- **Low Confidence**: The specific implementation details of the domination-based ranking and explore operator, which are critical for reproduction, are not fully specified in the paper.
