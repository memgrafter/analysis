# Diminishing Return of Value Expansion Methods in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03955
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03955
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Value expansion methods show diminishing returns in sample efficiency as rollout horizon increases, even with perfect dynamics models; the bottleneck is not model accuracy but likely variance in value targets or gradient instability

## Executive Summary
This paper empirically studies whether model-based value expansion methods in continuous control problems are limited by dynamics model accuracy. Using oracle (perfect) dynamics models, the authors compare sample efficiency across different rollout horizons and against learned models. They find that longer horizons yield diminishing returns in sample efficiency even with oracle models, and that oracle vs learned models provide similar performance. Model-free value expansion (Retrace) performs on par with model-based methods but with less computational overhead. The primary conclusion is that the bottleneck for model-based value expansion is not model accuracy, but rather something else—likely related to the value expansion targets themselves or policy gradients.

## Method Summary
The paper implements SAC and DDPG algorithms with value expansion methods (critic expansion and actor expansion) using horizons H ∈ [0, 1, 3, 5, 10, 20, 30]. Experiments compare learned neural network dynamics models (ensemble of 5 networks with 4 hidden layers, 256 neurons each) against oracle dynamics models across standard continuous control benchmark tasks. A model-free Retrace baseline is also evaluated. The primary metric is sample efficiency measured by learning curves (average episode undiscounted return vs. environment interaction steps).

## Key Results
- Longer horizons in value expansion methods increase sample efficiency but with diminishing returns, even with oracle dynamics models
- Oracle and learned dynamics models show similar performance, indicating model accuracy is not the bottleneck
- Model-free value expansion (Retrace) performs comparably to model-based methods with less computational overhead
- Actor expansion methods suffer from exploding gradients with longer horizons, leading to training instability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Value expansion methods suffer from diminishing returns on sample efficiency as rollout horizon increases, even with oracle dynamics models.
- **Mechanism:** Longer rollout horizons in value expansion methods lead to increased variance in target value estimates. This variance accumulates linearly with horizon length due to stochastic policy sampling, causing gradient instability and reduced learning performance.
- **Core assumption:** The variance of value expansion targets increases linearly with horizon length, and this variance dominates the potential benefits of longer horizons.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "longer horizons increase sample efficiency, but the gain in improvement decreases with each additional expansion step"
  - [section] "The second desirable property is that trajectories from the real environment or a dynamics model can be used... QH retrace reduces to the regular SAC H-step value expansion target"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this mechanism specifically

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Model-based value expansion methods provide only marginal sample efficiency improvements over model-free methods, even with perfect dynamics models.
- **Mechanism:** The computational overhead of learning and using dynamics models in value expansion methods outweighs the small gains in sample efficiency compared to model-free approaches like Retrace.
- **Core assumption:** The benefits of model-based value expansion are small relative to their computational cost.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "model-free value expansion methods... introduce no computational overhead, we find their performance to be on-par with model-based value expansion methods"
  - [section] "While the model-free variant only utilizes the collected experience, the model-based variant requires learning a parametric model and unrolling this model at each update of the critic and the actor"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this mechanism specifically

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Actor expansion methods are particularly vulnerable to gradient instability with longer horizons, limiting their effectiveness.
- **Mechanism:** Actor expansion methods backpropagate through entire rollout trajectories to compute policy gradients. This recursive computation graph is susceptible to vanishing or exploding gradients, especially for longer horizons, leading to training instability.
- **Core assumption:** The recursive nature of actor expansion gradients causes instability that scales with horizon length.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "For AE, building a very accurate differentiable model that predicts into the future might not be worth it due to gradient instability"
  - [section] "The second row of Figure 4 shows the actor gradient's standard deviation... the standard deviation of the Halfcheetah actor gradients explodes for horizons larger or equal to 3"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence supporting this mechanism specifically

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Real environment -> Data collection -> Dynamics model (oracle/learned) -> Rollout generator -> Value expansion module -> Q-function networks -> Policy network -> Training loop

- **Critical path:**
  1. Collect real environment transitions
  2. Generate H-step rollouts using dynamics model
  3. Compute value expansion targets QH
  4. Update Q-function using value expansion loss
  5. Update policy using either critic expansion or actor expansion loss
  6. Repeat

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Horizon length vs. computational cost: Longer horizons provide diminishing returns but increase computation time quadratically
  - Model accuracy vs. learning performance: Even perfect models provide limited benefits due to other bottlenecks
  - On-policy vs. off-policy rollouts: Off-policy methods like Retrace offer similar performance with less overhead

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Exploding gradients in actor expansion for longer horizons
  - Training collapse for longer horizons in both SAC and DDPG
  - Minimal performance difference between oracle and learned models
  - Similar performance between model-based and model-free value expansion methods

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement SAC with H=0, H=1, H=3, H=5 using oracle dynamics to observe diminishing returns
  2. Compare SAC with DDPG for the same horizons to test if variance reduction explains diminishing returns
  3. Implement Retrace as a model-free baseline and compare against model-based methods with identical horizons

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the fundamental bottleneck that causes diminishing returns in value expansion methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors conclude that model accuracy is not the limitation and state "the bottleneck lies elsewhere" - likely related to value expansion targets or policy gradients
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies that the bottleneck is not model accuracy but doesn't conclusively determine what the actual bottleneck is
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments isolating and testing the impact of value expansion targets vs policy gradients on sample efficiency

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would variance reduction techniques beyond particle averaging (e.g., control variates, baselines) eliminate diminishing returns in actor expansion?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors tested particle averaging but found it didn't resolve diminishing returns, and note that actor expansion is susceptible to exploding/vanishing gradients
- Why unresolved: Only simple variance reduction was tested; more sophisticated techniques weren't explored
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing actor expansion with advanced variance reduction methods and comparing sample efficiency across horizons

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the diminishing return phenomenon extend to model-based value expansion methods beyond continuous control tasks (e.g., image-based control, discrete action spaces)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study focuses specifically on "continuous control problems" with "inﬁnite horizon, continuous state- and action space problems"
- Why unresolved: The empirical study was limited to specific benchmark tasks with continuous actions
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying value expansion methods to image-based control tasks or discrete action spaces and measuring sample efficiency across horizons

## Limitations
- Conclusions rest on empirical observations without theoretical justification for why diminishing returns occur
- Analysis could be strengthened by testing on environments where dynamics are significantly harder to learn
- Limited to continuous control tasks; generalization to other domains (image-based, discrete actions) is unknown

## Confidence

- **Diminishing returns observation (High):** The empirical evidence across multiple environments and horizon lengths is robust and reproducible
- **Model accuracy not being the bottleneck (Medium):** While the comparison between oracle and learned models shows similar performance, the analysis could be strengthened by testing on environments where dynamics are significantly harder to learn
- **Variance accumulation mechanism (Low):** The paper suggests this as the primary explanation but does not provide direct evidence measuring variance across horizons or demonstrating that variance reduction techniques would mitigate the effect
