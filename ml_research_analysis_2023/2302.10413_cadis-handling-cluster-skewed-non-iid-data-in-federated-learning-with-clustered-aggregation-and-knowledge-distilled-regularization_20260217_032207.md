# CADIS: Handling Cluster-skewed Non-IID Data in Federated Learning with Clustered Aggregation and Knowledge DIStilled Regularization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10413
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10413
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Improves accuracy by up to 16% compared to FedAvg in cluster-skewed non-IID federated learning

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses federated learning with cluster-skewed non-IID data where clients can be grouped into clusters with similar data distributions. The proposed CADIS method combines clustered aggregation based on penultimate layer similarity with knowledge distillation-based regularization. By analyzing the improvement patterns in neural network penultimate layers, CADIS can identify client clusters without accessing raw data, then performs weighted aggregation that ensures fairness across clusters. The knowledge distillation regularization prevents overfitting during local training by encouraging local models to stay close to the global model's knowledge. Experimental results demonstrate significant accuracy improvements over FedAvg across multiple datasets and non-IID settings.

## Method Summary
CADIS operates through a client-server architecture where the server clusters clients based on penultimate layer similarity and performs weighted aggregation, while clients train locally with knowledge distillation regularization. The clustering process uses a novel similarity metric derived from the improvement in penultimate layer representations after local training, which indicates whether clients have similar class distributions. During local training, clients optimize both their local loss and a regularization term based on KL divergence between their model's output and the global model's output. The server then performs weighted aggregation where each client's contribution is determined by both their cluster size and local data quantity, ensuring fairness across clusters. This approach enables effective federated learning even when data distributions are highly skewed and clients cannot be easily grouped based on metadata.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 16% accuracy improvement over FedAvg across various non-IID settings
- Outperforms or matches other federated learning approaches on MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets
- Demonstrates robustness to different levels of cluster skew and data quantity skew
- Shows effective clustering ability with accuracy exceeding 90% in client group identification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Clients with similar penultimate layer representations can be clustered together even when their data is not shared.
- Mechanism: The penultimate layer of a neural network captures intermediate feature representations that are indicative of the underlying data distribution. By measuring the similarity of penultimate layer improvements between clients, the server can estimate how similar their data distributions are without accessing the raw data.
- Core assumption: The improvement pattern in the penultimate layer after local training is a reliable indicator of the training data's class distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By performing an in-depth analysis of the behavior of a classification model's penultimate layer, we introduce a metric that quantifies the similarity between two clients' data distributions without violating their privacy."
  - [section III-B1] "From this proposition we come up to the following observation: By analyzing the improvement of the penultimate layer, we may identify whether the training data comprises samples from a particular class."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for penultimate layer similarity as clustering metric.
- Break condition: If the penultimate layer doesn't capture enough information about class distributions, or if clients with similar data distributions produce different penultimate layer improvements due to model initialization differences.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge distillation-based regularization prevents local overfitting and helps preserve global model knowledge.
- Mechanism: During local training, clients compute a regularization term based on the KL divergence between their local model's output distribution and the global model's output distribution. This encourages local models to stay close to the global model's knowledge while still learning from their local data.
- Core assumption: The global model contains valuable generalizable knowledge that should be preserved during local training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we offer a novel local training regularization based on the knowledge-distillation technique that reduces the overfitting problem at clients"
  - [section IV] "We observe that the global model is an aggregation of multiple local models; as a result, it possesses more information and a higher generalizability."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for knowledge distillation in federated learning.
- Break condition: If the regularization term is too strong, it might prevent local models from learning their specific data patterns; if too weak, it won't prevent overfitting.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Weighted aggregation based on both cluster cardinality and data size ensures fair contribution from all clusters.
- Mechanism: Each client's weight in aggregation is inversely proportional to their cluster size and directly proportional to their data size. This prevents large clusters from dominating the aggregation while still giving more weight to clients with more data.
- Core assumption: Clients within the same cluster have similar data distributions, so their contributions should be balanced at the cluster level.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We then propose an aggregation scheme that guarantees equality between clusters."
  - [section III-A] "To equalize the contribution across the clusters, we employ the first term in (1), which is inversely proportional to cluster cardinality."
  - [corpus] Moderate - related to "Fairness and accuracy in federated learning" paper but not identical mechanism.
- Break condition: If clusters are incorrectly formed, or if the assumption that all clients in a cluster have similar data distributions doesn't hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Server -> Clients (global model distribution) -> Clients -> Server (trained models, penultimate layer info) -> Server (clustering, weighted aggregation) -> Global model update
- Critical path: 1. Server sends global model to clients 2. Clients train locally with KD regularization 3. Clients send trained models and penultimate layer info to server 4. Server computes similarity matrix and updates Q-matrix 5. Server clusters clients and performs weighted aggregation 6. Server sends updated global model to clients
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Clustering frequency vs. accuracy: More frequent clustering might be more accurate but increases communication overhead
  - Regularization strength vs. local learning: Strong regularization prevents overfitting but may limit local adaptation
  - Similarity threshold vs. cluster granularity: Lower thresholds create more clusters but may split similar clients
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor accuracy: Likely due to incorrect clustering or insufficient regularization
  - Slow convergence: Could indicate overly conservative clustering or insufficient local training
  - High variance in client performance: May suggest some clients are overfitting while others underfit
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run CADIS on MNIST with 10 clients and compare accuracy to FedAvg - should show basic functionality
  2. Test different similarity thresholds on CIFAR-10 to find optimal clustering granularity
  3. Vary regularization strength (λ parameter) to find balance between overfitting prevention and local learning

## Limitations
- The core claim about penultimate layer similarity as a clustering metric lacks direct empirical validation from the corpus
- Knowledge distillation-based regularization requires careful tuning of the λ parameter, which wasn't thoroughly explored across different datasets
- The weighted aggregation scheme assumes equal importance of all clusters, which may not hold in real-world scenarios where some clusters are more critical than others

## Confidence
- High: The overall framework combining clustering with knowledge distillation is technically sound and addresses a real problem in federated learning
- Medium: The experimental results showing accuracy improvements over FedAvg, though the 16% improvement figure needs context about baseline performance
- Low: The specific claim that penultimate layer similarity alone can reliably identify client clusters without ground truth cluster labels
