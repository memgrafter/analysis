# Evaluating the Logical Reasoning Ability of ChatGPT and GPT-4

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03439
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03439
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: ChatGPT and GPT-4 outperform RoBERTa fine-tuning on most logical reasoning benchmarks but struggle with out-of-distribution datasets

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the logical reasoning capabilities of ChatGPT and GPT-4 using multiple datasets and benchmarks. The models are tested on multi-choice reading comprehension and natural language inference tasks, including popular benchmarks like LogiQA and ReClor, as well as newly-released datasets like AR-LSAT. The study also constructs an out-of-distribution dataset to investigate the robustness of these models. Results show that ChatGPT and GPT-4 outperform traditional fine-tuning methods on most logical reasoning benchmarks, with GPT-4 achieving even higher performance. However, both models struggle with out-of-distribution datasets, and their performance on natural language inference tasks remains limited.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ChatGPT and GPT-4 using instruction-prompt schemes on multiple logical reasoning benchmarks including LogiQA, ReClor, AR-LSAT, ConTRoL, MED, HELP, ConjNLI, and TaxiNLI datasets. Results are compared against a RoBERTa fine-tuning baseline. The researchers also construct an out-of-distribution dataset to test model robustness and examine in-context learning capabilities by varying the number of examples provided in the same conversation window.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT and GPT-4 significantly outperform RoBERTa fine-tuning on in-distribution logical reasoning benchmarks
- Both models show substantial performance drops on newly released and out-of-distribution datasets
- GPT-4's accuracy increases with in-context learning, correctly answering more questions after seeing approximately eight conversation rounds
- Models struggle with natural language inference tasks despite performing better on multi-choice reading comprehension

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT and GPT-4 outperform traditional fine-tuning methods on logical reasoning benchmarks because their pre-training on massive corpora captures implicit logical patterns that fine-tuning methods must explicitly learn.
- Mechanism: Large language models encode statistical patterns of logical reasoning across diverse text, enabling them to recognize and apply these patterns during inference without task-specific training.
- Core assumption: Logical reasoning patterns are sufficiently represented in web-scale pre-training data for models to generalize to benchmark tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ChatGPT performs significantly better than the RoBERTa fine-tuning method on most logical reasoning benchmarks."
  - [section 3.1.1] "The accuracy of the LogiQA 2.0 test set is 53.37%, nearly 4 points higher than the RoBERTa base model."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - neighboring papers focus on systematic evaluation rather than explaining this performance gap.
- Break condition: If benchmark tasks require reasoning patterns not present in pre-training data, performance will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Both models struggle with out-of-distribution datasets because their reasoning abilities are based on pattern matching rather than true logical inference.
- Mechanism: Models succeed on familiar patterns but fail when encountering novel reasoning structures that don't match their training distribution.
- Core assumption: Performance drops on out-of-distribution data indicate pattern-matching rather than genuine logical reasoning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the performance drops significantly when handling newly released and out-of-distribution datasets."
  - [section 3.1.1] "On the AR-LSAT test set, the accuracy is only 20.42%, lower than the performance of RoBERTa base."
  - [section 3.1.2] "On LogiQA 2.0 ood data shows that GPT-4 only gets 48.21% correctness, which is significantly lower than that on the ReClor dataset."
- Break condition: If models could perform true logical reasoning, they would maintain performance across distribution shifts.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: In-context learning improves performance because models can adapt to task patterns through demonstration rather than requiring parameter updates.
- Mechanism: Providing examples in the same conversation window allows models to infer task structure and apply it to new instances.
- Core assumption: Models can generalize from a few examples to novel instances within the same reasoning domain.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "GPT-4 is prone to output more correct answers within the same conversation window after approximately eight conversation rounds"
  - [section 4.2] "For the LogiQA 2.0 ood dataset, GPT-4 yields 9 correct answers when the 20 examples are in the same conversation window. However, without the context, the number of correct answers drops to 5."
- Break condition: If models truly understood logical reasoning, they wouldn't require in-context examples to perform well.

- Critical path: Model selection → Prompt engineering → Dataset selection/creation → Evaluation execution → Result analysis

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies on commercial API access rather than reproducible local implementations
- Lack of detailed prompt specifications makes exact reproduction difficult
- The out-of-distribution datasets may not capture all forms of distributional shift that affect logical reasoning
- Comparison between instruction-tuned and fine-tuned models conflates architectural differences with training methodology

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The finding that ChatGPT and GPT-4 outperform RoBERTa on in-distribution logical reasoning benchmarks
- Medium Confidence: The claim that both models struggle with out-of-distribution data
- Medium Confidence: The observation that in-context learning improves performance

## Next Checks

1. **Prompt Ablation Study**: Systematically vary prompt formats, instruction clarity, and example inclusion to quantify the contribution of prompt engineering versus model capability to performance differences.

2. **Cross-Distribution Generalization**: Test models on a broader range of out-of-distribution scenarios including different reasoning types (causal, analogical, temporal) and text domains to better characterize generalization limits.

3. **Intermediate Layer Analysis**: Use probing techniques to examine whether model representations change systematically when processing logical reasoning tasks versus general text, providing evidence for or against genuine logical reasoning capabilities.
