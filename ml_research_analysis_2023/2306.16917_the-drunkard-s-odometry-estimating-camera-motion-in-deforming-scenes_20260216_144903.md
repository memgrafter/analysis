# The Drunkard's Odometry: Estimating Camera Motion in Deforming Scenes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16917
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16917
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Proposes a flow-based method for estimating camera motion in deforming scenes, outperforming baselines on synthetic and real medical datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenging problem of estimating camera motion in deforming scenes, particularly relevant for medical applications like endoscopy where a camera navigates inside a deformable environment. The authors introduce the Drunkard's Dataset, a large-scale synthetic dataset with ground truth annotations including camera poses, RGB images, depth, optical flow, and normal maps in deforming indoor scenes. They propose the Drunkard's Odometry, a flow-based method that decomposes optical flow into rigid-body camera motion and non-rigid scene deformations. The method is evaluated on the Drunkard's Dataset and shows superior performance compared to baselines like COLMAP, DROID-SLAM, and Endo-Depth-and-Motion (EDaM) in terms of Relative Position Error (RPE) and Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) metrics. Additionally, the authors introduce a novel ground-truth-free metric called Absolute Palindrome Trajectory Error (APTE) to validate odometry estimates in the absence of ground truth data.

## Method Summary
The Drunkard's Odometry is a flow-based method that estimates camera motion in deforming scenes by decomposing optical flow into rigid-body camera motion and non-rigid scene deformations. The method is trained on the Drunkard's Dataset, a large-scale synthetic dataset with ground truth annotations. The architecture consists of a pose network, feature encoder, 4D correlation pyramid, iterative update block, least-squares optimizer, and context encoder. The method is trained using a combination of optical flow, inverse depth, and relative camera pose loss terms. The Drunkard's Odometry shows superior performance compared to baselines on both the synthetic Drunkard's Dataset and the real Hamlyn dataset, which lacks ground truth camera poses.

## Key Results
- The Drunkard's Odometry outperforms baselines like COLMAP, DROID-SLAM, and Endo-Depth-and-Motion (EDaM) on the synthetic Drunkard's Dataset in terms of RPE and ATE metrics.
- The method shows promising results on the real Hamlyn dataset, despite the lack of ground truth camera poses, using the novel APTE metric.
- The paper introduces the Drunkard's Dataset, the first large-scale dataset with ground truth annotations for deforming scenes, enabling the development and evaluation of methods for camera motion estimation in such environments.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Drunkard's Odometry improves camera motion estimation in deforming scenes by decomposing optical flow into rigid-body camera motion and non-rigid scene deformations.
- **Mechanism:** The method uses a flow-based architecture that iteratively estimates a dense scene flow field Tk, which is composed of the rigid transformation from camera motion Tk_c and the non-rigid transformation from scene deformations Tk_d. This decomposition allows the model to isolate and account for deformations separately from camera motion.
- **Core assumption:** The optical flow can be effectively decomposed into rigid and non-rigid components, and this decomposition is learnable from synthetic data with ground truth annotations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "propose the Drunkard's Odometry, a flow-based method that decomposes optical flow estimates into rigid-body camera motion and non-rigid scene deformations"
  - [section] "Tk contains pixel-wise rigid-body transformations that ideally... maps every 3D point Pj... to its ground truth equivalent... As the estimated scene flow Tk might be affected by noise, we will denote as Pk_j' = Tk [uj] Pj the transformation of Pj to the local frame of I2, that in general will not coincide the true corresponding point Pj'. Tk [uj] is composed by the rigid transformation coming from the camera motion Tk_c and the one coming from the non-rigid surface deformations Tk_d [uj]"
  - [corpus] Weak. The related papers focus on deformable scene reconstruction but don't explicitly discuss optical flow decomposition as a mechanism for robust camera motion estimation.
- **Break condition:** If the scene deformations are too complex or rapid for the model to learn a meaningful decomposition, or if the optical flow estimation itself is unreliable due to noise or textureless regions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Training on synthetic data with perfect ground truth annotations enables the model to learn deformation patterns that generalize to real-world non-rigid scenes.
- **Mechanism:** The Drunkard's Dataset provides large-scale synthetic data with ground truth camera poses, depth, optical flow, and normal maps in deforming indoor scenes. The model is trained exclusively on this data, learning to predict scene flow and camera motion. The synthetic nature allows for perfect annotations, which are difficult to obtain in real-world medical applications like endoscopy.
- **Core assumption:** Features learned from synthetic deforming scenes can transfer to real non-rigid scenes, even when the synthetic data doesn't perfectly match the real domain (e.g., different textures, shapes).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "This dataset is the first large set of exploratory camera trajectories with ground truth inside 3D scenes where every surface exhibits non-rigid deformations over time"
  - [section] "As a proof, such data does not exist yet and motivates the use of the Drunkard's data. Our Drunkard's Odometry has all the limitations inherent to a frame-to-frame tracking method. Drift accumulates very quickly, and even if our sequences are loopy we do not either detect loop closures or correct our trajectories based on them. However, the SLAM literature shows that SLAM methods (e.g., [75]) can be build on top of odometry ones (e.g., [76])."
  - [corpus] Missing. The corpus doesn't provide evidence about synthetic-to-real generalization in deformable scenes.
- **Break condition:** If the domain gap between synthetic and real data is too large, or if the model overfits to the specific types of deformations present in the synthetic data.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The novel Absolute Palindrome Trajectory Error (APTE) metric allows validation of odometry methods in the absence of ground truth data by measuring cyclic consistency.
- **Mechanism:** APTE generates "palindrome videos" by reversing and concatenating a sequence to itself. A good odometry method should return to its starting position, so the metric measures the discrepancy between the start and end poses over various loop lengths. This provides a relative measure of tracking quality without requiring ground truth.
- **Core assumption:** In a real sequence, the camera trajectory is approximately cyclic or revisits similar areas, making the palindrome trick meaningful. The method assumes that drift is the main source of error and that cyclic consistency is a good proxy for overall accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "introduce a novel ground-truth-free metric called Absolute Palindrome Trajectory Error (APTE) to validate odometry estimates in the absence of ground truth data"
  - [section] "Since the Hamlyn dataset lacks ground truth camera poses we propose a novel ground truth-free trajectory metric to measure the quality of the estimated odometries. The key idea is to generate loopy videos by duplicating and reversing a given image sequence and concatenating it to the end of the original sequence... We then simply measure the discrepancy between start and end pose and accumulate it over various loop lengths"
  - [corpus] Weak. The related papers don't discuss palindrome-based validation metrics for odometry.
- **Break condition:** If the real sequence is not cyclic or doesn't revisit areas, making the palindrome assumption invalid. Also, if the method accumulates error in a way that coincidentally returns to the start, giving a false positive.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** RGB images -> Pose Network -> Feature Encoder -> 4D Correlation Pyramid -> Update Block (iterative refinement) -> Least-Squares Optimizer -> Scene Flow and Camera Pose

- **Critical path:** RGB images → Pose Network → Feature Encoder → 4D Correlation Pyramid → Update Block (iterative refinement) → Least-Squares Optimizer → Scene Flow and Camera Pose.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - **Synthetic vs. Real Data:** Synthetic data provides perfect ground truth but may have a domain gap. The tradeoff is between annotation quality and real-world applicability.
  - **Flow-Based vs. Feature-Based:** Flow-based methods can handle dense correspondences but may be sensitive to noise. Feature-based methods are more robust but sparser.
  - **Iterative Refinement:** Allows for gradual improvement but increases computational cost. The number of iterations is a hyperparameter to tune.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - **High RPE/ATE:** Indicates drift or poor pose estimation, possibly due to insufficient deformation modeling or feature tracking failures.
  - **Low recall (COLMAP):** Suggests the method fails to establish correspondences in many frames, possibly due to textureless regions or extreme deformations.
  - **APTE not improving with loop length:** Could indicate systematic bias or consistent error patterns in the odometry.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation on deformation levels:** Train and test the model on different difficulty levels of the Drunkard's Dataset to see how performance scales with deformation complexity.
  2. **Synthetic-to-real transfer:** Evaluate the model on the Hamlyn dataset to test generalization from synthetic to real non-rigid scenes.
  3. **Ablation on loss components:** Remove the optical flow or depth supervision from the loss function to see their impact on pose estimation accuracy.

## Limitations

- Reliance on synthetic data, which may not fully capture the complexity and variability of real-world medical scenes.
- Lack of loop closure detection and global optimization, limiting long-term SLAM applications.
- Performance on real endoscopies is promising but not directly comparable to other methods due to the absence of ground truth camera poses.

## Confidence

- **High confidence:** The method's effectiveness on the synthetic Drunkard's Dataset, as evidenced by the quantitative results (RPE and ATE) and qualitative visualizations. The APTE metric is also well-justified and provides a useful tool for evaluating odometry in the absence of ground truth.
- **Medium confidence:** The generalization of the method to real endoscopies, as the performance on the Hamlyn dataset is promising but not directly comparable to other methods. The effectiveness of the flow-based decomposition approach in handling complex deformations is also somewhat uncertain, as the paper does not extensively explore failure cases or challenging scenarios.
- **Low confidence:** The claim that the method is "superior" to baselines like COLMAP and DROID-SLAM. While the results on the Drunkard's Dataset are strong, the comparison is not entirely fair due to the different nature of the methods (feature-based vs. flow-based) and the synthetic data bias.
