# Reformulation Techniques for Automated Planning: A Systematic Review

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10079
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10079
- Reference count: 37
- This paper presents a systematic review of reformulation techniques for classical planning, classifying 54 studies into six main categories and providing a qualitative comparison of their benefits and drawbacks.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a systematic review of reformulation techniques for classical planning, analyzing 54 studies to classify and compare approaches that modify domain models to improve planning efficiency. The authors identify six main categories of reformulation techniques: entanglements, macro-operators, operators elimination, bagged representation, action schema splitting, and domain model configuration. The review finds that macro-operators are the most studied technique, while other approaches like bagged representation and action schema splitting show promise but are less explored. The paper provides valuable insights into how these techniques reduce search space complexity and discusses their applicability to non-classical planning problems.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a systematic literature review following Kitchenham and Charters (2017) methodology, searching Google Scholar, Scopus, and Summon using keywords related to classical planning reformulation. They identified 54 relevant studies through inclusion/exclusion criteria focusing on peer-reviewed, English-language papers dealing with domain-independent reformulation techniques for classical planning. The identified papers were classified into six main categories of reformulation techniques, and a qualitative comparison was provided based on the reported benefits, drawbacks, and empirical results across studies.

## Key Results
- Macro-operators are the most studied reformulation technique, with the largest body of research and empirical evidence
- Reformulation techniques primarily reduce search space complexity by eliminating actions, collapsing indistinguishable objects, or creating action shortcuts
- The effectiveness of reformulation techniques varies significantly across domains, with no single approach dominating all problem types
- Most reformulation techniques lack formal completeness guarantees, relying on empirical validation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Reformulation reduces search space size by eliminating actions or states that cannot contribute to a solution.
- Mechanism: Techniques like entanglements remove actions whose preconditions cannot be satisfied in the initial state or whose effects cannot lead to the goal. This prunes the search tree and reduces branching factor.
- Core assumption: The reformulation technique correctly identifies actions that are truly unreachable or unnecessary for solving the planning task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "re-formulate, re-represent or tune the domain model and/or problem description, while keeping to the same input language, in order to increase the efficiency of a plan generation."
  - [section 4.1] "Outer entanglements [Chrpa and McCluskey, 2012, Chrpa et al., 2018] identify useless actions on the basis of their relationships with predicates from the initial state or the goal state."
- Break condition: If the reformulation incorrectly identifies an action as useless when it is actually necessary, completeness is lost and valid solutions are missed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Macro-operators reduce the number of steps in a plan by encapsulating useful action sequences into single operators.
- Mechanism: By combining primitive operators into macros, the search algorithm can reach goal states in fewer transitions. This trades increased branching factor for reduced plan depth.
- Core assumption: The macro sequences are frequently used in solution plans and represent meaningful shortcuts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "macro-operators are the most studied technique"
  - [section 4.2] "Macros, informally speaking, provide short-cuts in the state space and, consequently, planning engines can generate plans in a smaller number of steps."
- Break condition: If macros are too specific or too general, they may not be used or may cause combinatorial explosion in the ground space.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Bagged representation reduces branching factor by collapsing indistinguishable objects into counts.
- Mechanism: When object identity is irrelevant, representing multiple identical objects as a single counter reduces the number of ground actions and states that must be considered.
- Core assumption: The planning problem can be solved without distinguishing between individual objects of the same type.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.4] "The main advantage is to reduce the branching factor, by basically pruning states that are identical but for the specific object."
  - [section 4.4] "In the presence of large sets of objects, this can lead to an explosion of the combinatorial problem, that needs to take into account the specific information of each individual object."
- Break condition: If the problem requires distinguishing between individual objects (e.g., for specific placement goals), the bagged representation loses completeness.

## Limitations
- Most reformulation techniques lack formal completeness and soundness guarantees, relying primarily on empirical validation
- The classification into six categories may oversimplify the relationships and interactions between different techniques
- Focus on classical planning limits generalizability to more expressive planning formalisms like temporal or probabilistic planning
