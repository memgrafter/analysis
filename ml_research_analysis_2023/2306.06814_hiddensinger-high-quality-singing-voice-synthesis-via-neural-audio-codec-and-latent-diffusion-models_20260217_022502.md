# HiddenSinger: High-Quality Singing Voice Synthesis via Neural Audio Codec and Latent Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06814
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06814
- Reference count: 40
- High-fidelity singing voice synthesis via neural audio codecs and latent diffusion models

## Executive Summary
HiddenSinger introduces a novel high-quality singing voice synthesis system that addresses limitations of conventional two-stage approaches. The system combines an audio autoencoder with residual vector quantization (RVQ) to compress audio into low-dimensional latent representations, followed by a latent diffusion model to generate these representations conditioned on musical scores. The authors demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across multiple metrics and extend the approach to an unsupervised framework (HiddenSinger-U) that can synthesize singing voices of unseen speakers using only unlabeled data.

## Method Summary
HiddenSinger employs a three-stage pipeline: first, an audio autoencoder encodes audio spectrograms into compressed latent representations using RVQ; second, a condition encoder extracts musical score information (lyrics and melody) and produces an enhanced condition; third, a latent diffusion model generates the latent representation conditioned on the enhanced condition. The model is trained with reconstruction loss and adversarial loss. HiddenSinger-U extends this framework by adding unsupervised encoders for lyrics and melody, incorporating contrastive learning to align representations without paired data.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art nMOS scores of 4.15 on the NUS-48E dataset, outperforming previous models by 0.1-0.3 points
- Demonstrates superior pitch accuracy with lower pitch error and periodicity error metrics compared to baseline systems
- Shows effective zero-shot adaptation to unseen speakers with HiddenSinger-U, maintaining high naturalness while preserving speaker identity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) enables high-fidelity audio reconstruction by distributing multi-dimensional audio features across multiple codebooks.
- Mechanism: The audio autoencoder first encodes high-dimensional linear-spectrograms into low-dimensional continuous latent representations. RVQ then discretizes these into multiple codebooks, each handling a subset of the audio features. This distribution reduces the representational burden on any single codebook, allowing finer-grained audio reconstruction.
- Core assumption: Multi-codebook RVQ can better approximate complex audio distributions than single-codebook VQ without excessive computational overhead.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we introduce an audio autoencoder that can efficiently encode audio into a compressed latent representation, resulting in a lower-dimensional representational space"
  - [section] "we apply RVQ [21] to the continuous latent representation z0 for efficient audio compression"

- Break condition: If the residual structure fails to converge during training, or if the codebook entries become too sparse, the audio reconstruction quality will degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Latent diffusion models conditioned on musical score representations can generate high-fidelity latent audio representations with controllable pitch and timing.
- Mechanism: The latent generator uses score-conditioned denoising to iteratively refine a noisy latent vector into a high-quality latent representation that preserves musical structure. The condition encoder extracts both lyrics and melody representations, which guide the diffusion process toward the desired output.
- Core assumption: The score representation contains sufficient information for the diffusion model to reconstruct the target latent audio structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we use the latent diffusion models to sample a latent representation from a musical score"
  - [section] "We adopt the latent diffusion models [26] in the latent generator to generate the latent representation of the audio autoencoder"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence on latent diffusion for SVS - weak evidence
- Break condition: If the conditioning information is insufficient or poorly encoded, the generated latent representations will lack proper pitch or timing alignment.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Unsupervised learning with contrastive objectives enables adaptation to unseen speakers without paired musical score data.
- Mechanism: HiddenSinger-U uses self-supervised representations for lyrics and quantized F0 for melody to encode speaker information without relying on ground-truth labels. Contrastive learning maximizes agreement between paired representations (e.g., lyrics and unsupervised lyrics) while penalizing mismatches.
- Core assumption: Self-supervised speech representations contain sufficient phonetic information, and quantized F0 preserves melody without speaker leakage.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our proposed model is extended to HiddenSinger-U, an unsupervised singing voice learning framework, to train the model using an unlabeled singing voice dataset"
  - [section] "We introduce two additional encoders into the condition encoder to model the unsupervised lyrics and melody representation"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence on unsupervised SVS with contrastive learning - weak evidence
- Break condition: If the self-supervised representations fail to capture phonetic content or if speaker information leaks through F0 quantization, the synthesized singing voice will be unintelligible or have incorrect speaker identity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Audio spectrogram -> Audio Autoencoder (Encoder + RVQ blocks + Decoder) -> Latent representation
  Musical score -> Condition Encoder (Lyrics encoder + Melody encoder + Enhanced condition encoder + Prior estimator) -> Enhanced condition
  Latent representation + Enhanced condition -> Latent Generator (Score estimation network with diffusion) -> Synthesized latent representation
  Synthesized latent representation -> Audio Autoencoder (Decoder) -> High-fidelity audio

- Critical path:
  1. Musical score → condition encoder → enhanced condition
  2. Enhanced condition + noise → latent generator → latent representation
  3. Latent representation → audio autoencoder → high-fidelity audio
  4. For unsupervised learning: audio → self-supervised representations → encoders → contrastive loss

- Design tradeoffs:
  - RVQ vs KL regularization: RVQ provides better reconstruction quality but increases codebook complexity
  - Latent diffusion vs direct waveform generation: Diffusion allows better conditioning but requires iterative sampling
  - Unsupervised vs supervised learning: Unsupervised enables zero-shot adaptation but may sacrifice some quality

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor audio quality: Check RVQ codebook convergence and diffusion model conditioning
  - Pronunciation errors: Verify condition encoder and enhanced condition encoder outputs
  - Speaker mismatch in unsupervised mode: Inspect self-supervised representations and contrastive loss terms

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train audio autoencoder with RVQ and evaluate reconstruction quality (PESQ, spectrogram MAE)
  2. Train latent generator with condition encoder and measure conditioning effectiveness (pitch error, periodicity error)
  3. Test unsupervised learning framework with varying unlabeled data ratios and evaluate naturalness (nMOS) and similarity (sMOS)
