# Conceptual Design Generation Using Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01779
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01779
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: GPT-3-generated design solutions show higher feasibility and usefulness than crowdsourced solutions, while crowdsourced solutions exhibit more novelty.

## Executive Summary
This study explores the use of large language models, specifically GPT-3, for generating design solutions across 12 different design problems. The research compares GPT-3-generated solutions with crowdsourced human solutions, finding that while LLM-generated solutions are more feasible and useful, human solutions demonstrate greater novelty. The paper investigates how prompt engineering, particularly few-shot learning, can be used to make GPT-3 solutions more similar to human-generated ones. Computational metrics are introduced to provide scalable evaluation of solution quality and diversity.

## Method Summary
The research methodology involves presenting 12 design prompts to both GPT-3 and human participants. GPT-3 is tested using various prompt engineering techniques, including zero-shot and few-shot learning approaches. Expert evaluators assess the generated solutions across three dimensions: feasibility, novelty, and usefulness. Computational metrics, including mean nearest generated sample and convex hull volume calculated using SentenceBERT embeddings, are used to provide additional quantitative analysis of the solution sets.

## Key Results
- GPT-3-generated solutions demonstrate higher average feasibility and usefulness compared to crowdsourced solutions
- Crowdsourced solutions exhibit greater novelty than GPT-3-generated solutions
- Few-shot learning prompt engineering significantly increases the similarity between GPT-3 and crowdsourced solutions
- Computational metrics reveal that GPT-3 solutions are less diverse but more similar to each other compared to human solutions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-3 can generate design solutions that are more feasible and useful than crowdsourced solutions, but less novel.
- Mechanism: GPT-3 leverages its pre-trained knowledge to produce solutions aligned with established design conventions, which increases feasibility and usefulness. However, its reliance on learned patterns reduces the generation of truly novel concepts.
- Core assumption: GPT-3's training data includes sufficient domain-relevant knowledge to produce feasible and useful design concepts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Expert evaluations indicate that the LLM-generated solutions have higher average feasibility and usefulness while the crowdsourced solutions have more novelty."
  - [section] "GPT-3 generates design solutions strictly based on the inputs it is provided and what data GPT-3 was trained on"
- Break condition: If GPT-3's training data lacks sufficient domain-specific knowledge, feasibility and usefulness scores would drop significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prompt engineering, specifically few-shot learning, can make GPT-3 solutions more similar to crowdsourced ones.
- Mechanism: Providing GPT-3 with examples of human-generated solutions as context helps it mimic human creative patterns and diversity in its outputs.
- Core assumption: GPT-3 can learn from a small number of examples to adapt its generation style to match human creativity patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We experiment with prompt engineering and find that leveraging few-shot learning can lead to the generation of solutions that are more similar to the crowdsourced solutions."
  - [section] "Table 3 reveals that, on average, when the few-shot method was utilized, similarity across all design prompts increased."
- Break condition: If the provided examples don't represent the diversity of human solutions, GPT-3 may simply mimic a narrow subset of patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Computational metrics (mean nearest generated sample and convex hull volume) can effectively evaluate design solution quality at scale.
- Mechanism: These metrics provide quantitative measures of similarity to human solutions and diversity of generated solutions, respectively, enabling large-scale evaluation without expert intervention.
- Core assumption: SentenceBERT embeddings capture semantic similarity relevant to design solution quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Computational metrics, including mean nearest generated sample and convex hull volume, provide additional insights into the differences between GPT-3-generated and crowdsourced design solutions."
  - [section] "We leverage a pre-trained textual embedding network, SentenceBERT, to embed each individual design solution into a 384-dimensional vector."
- Break condition: If SentenceBERT embeddings don't capture the semantic nuances important for design solution quality, the metrics would fail to correlate with human evaluations.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do computational metrics compare to human expert evaluations for assessing important characteristics of human and LLM-generated design solutions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly compares computational metrics (mean nearest generated sample and convex hull volume) with human expert evaluations (feasibility, novelty, usefulness) for both GPT-3 and crowdsourced solutions.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that computational metrics can provide insights into the differences between LLM-generated and crowdsourced solutions, it does not fully explore the relationship between these metrics and human evaluations. The paper notes that computational metrics do not translate well for engineering design problems, but does not provide a definitive answer on how to bridge this gap.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research could involve developing new computational metrics that more closely align with human evaluations, or conducting studies to determine the correlation between computational and human evaluations for various design tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what degree can prompt engineering be employed to vary the attributes of LLM-generated design solutions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper experiments with prompt engineering using zero-shot and few-shot learning methods, but notes that GPT-3 is highly sensitive to input changes and that results vary significantly based on the design prompt.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive understanding of how different prompt engineering techniques affect the attributes of LLM-generated design solutions. It also does not explore the potential limitations or optimal conditions for using prompt engineering in design tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Future research could involve systematically testing various prompt engineering techniques on a wider range of design problems, and analyzing the resulting solutions for changes in attributes such as feasibility, novelty, and usefulness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the limitations of GPT-3 generated design solutions, such as non-descriptive solutions, be addressed through prompt engineering or other methods?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that GPT-3 generated solutions are shorter and contain less information than crowdsourced solutions, which is a limitation that could be addressed through prompt engineering.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a definitive solution to this limitation, and it is unclear whether this is a unique issue to GPT-3 or common among all LLMs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research could involve testing different prompt engineering techniques, as well as exploring other LLMs, to determine the most effective methods for generating detailed and descriptive design solutions.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of computational metrics depends on SentenceBERT's ability to capture design-relevant semantic features, which remains unverified for this specific application
- The comparison between LLM-generated and crowdsourced solutions may be influenced by the specific design prompts chosen, limiting generalizability
- GPT-3's reliance on pre-trained knowledge may limit its ability to generate truly novel design concepts that fall outside its training data distribution

## Confidence

- Medium: GPT-3 generates more feasible and useful solutions than crowdsourced ones, supported by expert evaluations but relies on specific design prompts
- Medium: Prompt engineering improves similarity to crowdsourced solutions, with quantitative evidence but limited exploration of optimal configurations
- Low: Computational metrics effectively evaluate design solution quality, correlation with expert evaluations mentioned but not thoroughly validated
