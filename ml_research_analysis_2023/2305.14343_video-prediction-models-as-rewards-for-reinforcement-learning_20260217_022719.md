# Video Prediction Models as Rewards for Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14343
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14343
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: VIPER achieves expert-level control across 28 tasks without ground truth rewards or expert actions

## Executive Summary
VIPER introduces a novel approach to reinforcement learning by using pretrained video prediction models as reward signals. The method trains an autoregressive transformer on expert videos and uses the video prediction likelihoods as rewards for RL agents. This allows agents to learn complex behaviors without programmatic task rewards, achieving expert-level performance across multiple benchmarks including DeepMind Control Suite, Atari, and RLBench.

## Method Summary
VIPER employs an autoregressive transformer trained on expert demonstration videos to predict future frames. The key insight is that maximizing the video prediction likelihood encourages agents to match the trajectory distribution of the expert data. The trained video prediction model serves as a reward function, providing feedback to the RL agent based on how well its actions align with predicted expert trajectories. This approach eliminates the need for explicit reward engineering while enabling generalization to out-of-distribution environments and cross-embodiment tasks.

## Key Results
- Achieves expert-level control across 28 tasks without access to ground truth rewards or expert actions
- Demonstrates cross-embodiment generalization for tabletop manipulation tasks
- Outperforms baselines like AMP, particularly in environments with sparse rewards

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
VIPER leverages the video prediction model's ability to capture the distribution of expert trajectories. By using the likelihood of predicted frames as rewards, the RL agent is incentivized to produce actions that generate observations consistent with expert demonstrations. This alignment between the agent's trajectory distribution and the expert distribution emerges from maximizing the video prediction likelihood, effectively learning to imitate expert behavior without explicit reward signals or action labels.

## Foundational Learning
- Video prediction models: Needed to capture expert trajectory distributions; quick check: model can predict plausible future frames from expert videos
- Autoregressive transformers: Required for sequential frame prediction; quick check: model can generate coherent video sequences
- Reinforcement learning with learned rewards: Enables policy optimization using video prediction likelihoods; quick check: RL agent improves performance over training iterations

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Expert videos -> Video prediction model -> Likelihood scores -> RL agent -> Actions -> Observations -> Video prediction model

Critical path: Video prediction model training → Likelihood-based reward calculation → RL policy optimization → Action execution → Observation collection

Design tradeoffs: Video prediction accuracy vs. computational efficiency; model complexity vs. generalization capability

Failure signatures: Poor expert dataset coverage leading to reward signal misalignment; video prediction model collapse causing degraded reward signals

First experiments:
1. Train video prediction model on expert dataset and evaluate frame prediction accuracy
2. Test RL agent with fixed video prediction model as reward function on a simple task
3. Evaluate cross-embodiment generalization on a manipulation task with different robot morphologies

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
None

## Limitations
- Performance heavily dependent on quality and diversity of expert demonstrations
- Requires large number of expert demonstrations for effective video prediction model training
- Autoregressive transformer architecture may struggle with long-horizon predictions
- High computational cost of training large video prediction models and using them for reward estimation

## Confidence
- High confidence: Core methodology of using video prediction likelihoods as rewards is theoretically sound and experimentally validated
- Medium confidence: Cross-embodiment generalization results are promising but limited to a specific set of manipulation tasks
- Medium confidence: Comparison to baselines like AMP shows competitive performance, but exact replication is challenging due to unspecified implementation details

## Next Checks
1. Test VIPER's performance with varying amounts of expert demonstrations (10%, 50%, 100% of current dataset) to quantify data efficiency requirements
2. Evaluate robustness to different video prediction model architectures beyond autoregressive transformers
3. Conduct ablation studies to isolate contributions of different components, such as video prediction likelihoods versus other video-based reward signals and different pretraining strategies
