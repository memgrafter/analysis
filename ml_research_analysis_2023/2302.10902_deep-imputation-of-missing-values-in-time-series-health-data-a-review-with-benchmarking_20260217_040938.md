# Deep Imputation of Missing Values in Time Series Health Data: A Review with Benchmarking

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.10902
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10902
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Benchmark of six deep learning imputation methods across five health datasets shows no single method consistently outperforms others; performance depends on data characteristics and missingness patterns.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark study of state-of-the-art deep learning methods for imputing missing values in multivariate time series health data. The study evaluates six deep imputation methods (BRITS, CATSI variants, NAOMI, TAME) across five real-world health datasets including EHR and sensor data. Experiments systematically test varying time series lengths, missing value rates up to 80%, and missing data types (MCAR, MAR, MNAR). Results reveal that no single method consistently outperforms others across all datasets, with performance depending on data characteristics such as variable correlation, time series length, and missingness patterns.

## Method Summary
The study benchmarks six deep learning imputation methods: BRITS, CATSI (with LSTM and MLP variants), NAOMI, and TAME. Methods are evaluated on five health datasets (DACMI, Sepsis, Hypotension, IEEEPPG, Heartbeat) with varying time series lengths (5-25 steps), missing value rates (up to 80%), and missingness types (MCAR, MAR, MNAR). Imputation accuracy is measured using Normalized Root Mean Square Deviation (NRMSD) per variable, and downstream classification performance is assessed using weighted F1 scores. The study also examines the impact of different initialization methods and cross-sectional versus longitudinal imputation strategies.

## Key Results
- No single deep learning imputation method consistently outperforms others across all datasets and conditions
- Cross-sectional methods outperform hybrid/longitudinal approaches for highly correlated variables
- MNAR missingness is substantially more challenging than MCAR/MAR, with lower imputation accuracy
- Imputation performance degrades as missing value rates increase, particularly above 50%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Deep learning methods that jointly perform cross-sectional and longitudinal imputations outperform methods that use only one dimension.
- Mechanism: Combining both variable-to-variable dependencies (cross-sectional) and time-to-time dependencies (longitudinal) allows the model to capture richer structure in multivariate time series, especially when both patterns exist.
- Core assumption: The missingness structure is influenced by both inter-variable correlations and temporal trends, so modeling both improves estimation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Deep learning methods that jointly perform cross-sectional (across variables) and longitudinal (across time) imputations of missing values in time series data yield statistically better data quality than traditional imputation methods."
  - [section] "State-of-the-art methods jointly perform cross-sectional (across variables) and longitudinal (across time) imputations of missing values in time series data."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no corpus papers directly test this joint model claim, only survey-level mentions.
- Break condition: If variables are nearly independent (low cross-correlation) and time trends are weak, the hybrid approach offers no advantage and may overfit.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Missing value initialization method materially affects convergence and final imputation accuracy.
- Mechanism: Providing a better initial estimate reduces the number of training iterations needed and can lead the model to a better local optimum.
- Core assumption: The initialization is close enough to the true value to avoid poor local minima.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] No direct mention, but Table 3 shows performance varies with initialization.
  - [section] "Missing values require some initial values for the deep learning method to start learning the final estimates. It is hypothesized that initial values can affect the final convergence of the imputation algorithm."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; initialization strategies are rarely compared systematically in deep imputation literature.
- Break condition: If the data has strong temporal or cross-sectional patterns, poor initialization may be corrected during training; if patterns are weak, initialization dominates accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Performance degrades more sharply for missing not at random (MNAR) than for missing at random (MAR) or missing completely at random (MCAR).
- Mechanism: When missingness depends on unobserved values, the model cannot reliably infer the missing data, leading to higher imputation error.
- Core assumption: Missingness is informative and correlated with the missing values themselves.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Deep learning methods that jointly perform cross-sectional and longitudinal imputations of missing values in time series data yield statistically better data quality than traditional imputation methods."
  - [section] "It is shown that imputing missing values that are not random is far more challenging and error-prone than imputing MCAR type missing values."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; most corpus papers do not evaluate MNAR scenarios.
- Break condition: If the correlation between missingness and values is weak, the degradation is modest and hybrid methods still outperform single-dimension approaches.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Missing value initialization -> Deep learning model selection -> Training loop -> Evaluation
- Critical path:
  1. Load raw multivariate time series
  2. Apply missingness mask (MCAR/MAR/MNAR)
  3. Initialize missing values (mean/median/piecewise/interpolation)
  4. Train selected imputation model
  5. Impute test set and evaluate NRMSD
  6. Train downstream classifier and report weighted F1
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Hybrid models (CATSI, BRITS) have higher computational cost but better accuracy when both cross-sectional and longitudinal dependencies exist.
  - Cross-sectional-only models (CATSI-MLP) are faster but may fail when temporal patterns dominate.
  - Simple baselines (piecewise interpolation) can outperform complex models on strongly correlated signals.
- Failure signatures:
  - High NRMSD variance across variables indicates model bias toward certain patterns.
  - Degradation at high missing rates (>50%) suggests model overfits observed entries.
  - Poor F1 on imputed data indicates synthetic values disrupt predictive signal.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run each imputation method on DACMI with 5% MCAR and piecewise initialization; compare NRMSD.
  2. Vary time-series length (5, 10, 15, 20, 25 steps) for CATSI on sepsis; plot NRMSD vs length.
  3. Compare MAR, MCAR, MNAR missingness on heartbeat data at 20% missing; report NRMSD and F1.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do time series imputation methods perform on real-world EHR data with high rates of MNAR (missing not at random) missingness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows MNAR imputation accuracy is substantially lower than MCAR/MAR, and MNAR is the most common type in real EHR data
- Why unresolved: Existing benchmarking focuses on low missing rates and MCAR/MAR types; no comprehensive evaluation exists for high-MNAR scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking state-of-the-art methods on multiple real EHR datasets with naturally occurring MNAR patterns and high missing rates (>50%)

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the observed classification performance improvement from imputed heartbeat data (outperforming complete data) generalize to other time series signal domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The heartbeat dataset showed CATSI-LSTM imputation yielded better classification than complete data, suggesting imputed data may add relevant information
- Why unresolved: This is a single observation on one dataset; the phenomenon could be dataset-specific or method-specific
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing this hypothesis across multiple time series signal datasets (EEG, PPG, accelerometer) with varying correlation structures and missingness patterns

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the optimal imputation strategies for individual variables within a multivariate time series, and can these be predicted from variable statistics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study found cross-sectional methods outperform hybrid/longitudinal for highly correlated variables, and performance varies by variable and domain
- Why unresolved: While patterns were observed, a generalizable framework for variable-specific method selection based on statistical properties is not established
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing predictive models that recommend imputation methods based on variable correlation matrices, temporal characteristics, and domain context

## Limitations
- The study relies on a relatively small set of six deep learning methods, which may not fully represent the state-of-the-art in time series imputation.
- The evaluation focuses on healthcare datasets with specific characteristics (high inter-variable correlation, regular sampling), which may limit generalizability to other domains.
- The computational cost of hybrid models is not thoroughly analyzed, and the performance of simple baselines like piecewise interpolation on non-healthcare data is unclear.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Deep learning methods outperform traditional methods in healthcare time series imputation when cross-sectional and longitudinal dependencies exist.
- **Medium confidence**: No single method consistently outperforms others across all datasets; method selection should depend on data characteristics.
- **Low confidence**: The relative performance of initialization methods and the impact of initialization on convergence are not well established due to limited systematic comparison.

## Next Checks
1. **Systematic initialization comparison**: Conduct a controlled experiment varying only the initialization method across all imputation models to quantify its impact on convergence and final accuracy.
2. **Cross-domain generalization**: Test the best-performing methods from this study on non-healthcare time series data (e.g., financial or environmental datasets) to assess generalizability.
3. **Computational efficiency analysis**: Measure and compare the training time and resource usage of hybrid models versus simpler baselines to provide a more complete picture of their practical utility.
