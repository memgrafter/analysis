# Optimal Transport for Correctional Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01701
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01701
- Reference count: 33
- One-line primary result: Presents a generalized formulation of correctional learning using optimal transport for improved parameter estimation.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a generalized framework for correctional learning using optimal transport. The approach formulates the problem of improving parameter estimation through expert intervention as an optimal transport problem, allowing for more complex parameter estimation and consideration of multiple intervention policies. The method discretizes the continuous optimal transport problem and employs importance sampling to approximate integrals, demonstrating improved estimation accuracy over original data across three numerical experiments.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach discretizes the continuous optimal transport problem and uses importance sampling to approximate integrals. The teacher modifies original samples based on a computed transportation plan to minimize estimation error. The framework relies on the assumption that the estimator depends only on the empirical measure and is Fr\'echet-differentiable, enabling linearization of the problem for small intervention budgets. Conditional probability mass functions derived from the optimal transport plan enable stochastic generation of corrected sequences that satisfy budget constraints.

## Key Results
- Improved estimation accuracy compared to original data across all three experimental settings
- Absolute error decreases as sample size and intervention budget increase
- Framework enables estimation of more complex characteristics and consideration of alternative intervention strategies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Taylor approximation of the student's estimator enables linearization of the optimal transport problem for small intervention budgets.
- Mechanism: By assuming the estimator depends only on the empirical measure and is Fr\'echet-differentiable, the cost function is approximated as linear in the transport plan near the original empirical distribution.
- Core assumption: The budget B is small enough that most samples are not modified, so the first-order Taylor expansion is accurate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By assuming that the estimator depends on the samples only through their empirical measure, we can pose the correctional learning problem as an optimization program in terms of distribution functions – i.e., as an optimal transport problem."
  - [section] "Since the estimator in (7) depends on the samples only through their empirical distribution, it makes sense to pose the optimization problem to be solved by the teacher in terms of distribution functions, i.e., as an optimal transport problem."
- Break condition: If the budget is large, the Taylor approximation becomes inaccurate and the linearization fails to capture the true cost surface.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Discretizing the continuous sample space using observed unique values enables importance sampling to approximate integrals in the optimal transport formulation.
- Mechanism: The observed samples define a discrete state space; by constructing proposal distributions over this state space and using importance sampling, the infinite-dimensional integrals are approximated by finite sums over observed and candidate modified values.
- Core assumption: The samples are exchangeable and the empirical measure captures the essential information for estimation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "One of the most common approaches to solve the optimal transport problem in (11) is to discretize it [10]."
  - [section] "We start by defining a discretized sample space... For the continuous case, we note that with probability one, S = O and n = |S|= |O|= N, (16) since all the samples in O are distinct, with probability one."
- Break condition: If the observed samples are insufficient to represent the underlying distribution, the discretization may miss critical regions and importance sampling may have high variance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The conditional probability mass function derived from the optimal transport plan enables stochastic generation of corrected sequences that satisfy the budget constraint.
- Mechanism: After solving for the transport plan α, Bayes' theorem yields p(˜x |x), the probability of changing x to ˜x. Sampling from this conditional pmf and filtering sequences that meet the budget constraint generates feasible corrected sequences.
- Core assumption: The intervention budget is sufficient to allow meaningful corrections while keeping the number of modified samples within B.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Then, by applying Bayes' theorem, we compute the conditional probability mass function (pmf) as p(˜x |x) = αˆpN (˜x), which will give us the probability of changing an observation x into ˜x."
  - [section] "The teacher's intervention procedure is then as follows. For each sample in xi ∈ O, the teacher modifies it according to the pmf in (28)."
- Break condition: If the budget is too small relative to the estimation error, no corrected sequence may improve the estimate while staying within budget.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection -> Teacher module -> Solver -> Intervention generator -> Budget monitor -> Student module

- Critical path:
  1. Observe original samples ON
  2. Compute empirical measure ˆpN
  3. Discretize sample space S and construct proposal distributions
  4. Set up and solve the discretized optimal transport program for α
  5. Derive conditional pmf p(˜x |x)
  6. Generate M candidate corrected sequences
  7. Filter by budget constraint
  8. Select best sequence by lowest estimation error
  9. Pass corrected sequence to student estimator

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Discretization granularity vs. computational cost: Finer discretization improves accuracy but increases solver complexity
  - Proposal distribution choice: Independent sampling simplifies implementation but may require interpolation; using ˆpN directly aligns with empirical distribution but limits flexibility
  - Number of candidate sequences M: Higher M increases chance of finding budget-feasible improvements but costs more computation

- Failure signatures:
  - Solver fails to converge: May indicate poor discretization or ill-conditioned proposal distributions
  - No corrected sequence improves estimate: Budget too small or estimator not sensitive to the modifications
  - High variance in final estimate: Importance sampling variance too large; consider better proposal distributions or more samples

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the Taylor approximation accuracy for small B by comparing estimated corrections to the exact (brute-force) solution for low N
  2. Test the impact of discretization by solving the same problem with different numbers of states and measuring estimation error and solver time
  3. Evaluate different intervention policies (greedy vs. stochastic) by applying each to the same transport plan and comparing final estimation accuracy

## Limitations

- Taylor approximation validity is limited to small intervention budgets; accuracy degrades for large budgets or highly nonlinear estimators
- Discretization approach may inadequately represent underlying continuous distributions, especially in high-dimensional or multimodal cases
- Method's effectiveness for real-world applications with complex, high-dimensional data remains uncertain without extensive empirical validation

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The optimal transport formulation of correctional learning and the discretization approach are well-established methods with solid theoretical foundations
- **Medium confidence**: The Taylor approximation and importance sampling for integral approximation are standard techniques, but their application here requires careful validation
- **Low confidence**: The practical effectiveness of the framework for real-world applications with high-dimensional data or large intervention budgets is uncertain without extensive empirical validation
