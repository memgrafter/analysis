# Capturing Conversion Rate Fluctuation during Sales Promotions: A Novel Historical Data Reuse Approach

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12837
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12837
- Reference count: 40
- Key result: HDR improved 9% RPM and 16% CVR during Double 11 Sales in 2022

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of deteriorated conversion rate (CVR) prediction during sales promotions due to data distribution shifts. The authors propose a Historical Data Reuse (HDR) approach that retrieves historically similar promotion data and fine-tunes the CVR prediction model using this data. HDR consists of three components: automated data retrieval from historical promotions, distribution shift correction through re-weighting, and TransBlock module for quick model fine-tuning. The approach has been successfully deployed in Alibaba's display advertising system, demonstrating significant performance improvements during major promotional events.

## Method Summary
The HDR approach tackles CVR prediction challenges during promotional periods by leveraging historical promotion data. The method employs an automated retrieval system to find similar past promotions, applies distribution shift correction to align retrieved data with the current promotion context, and uses a TransBlock module for efficient model adaptation. This framework allows the model to quickly adapt to promotion-specific patterns while maintaining the generalization capabilities learned from regular traffic data. The approach addresses the fundamental issue of distribution shift between regular and promotional traffic patterns.

## Key Results
- HDR achieved 9% RPM lift and 16% CVR lift during Double 11 2022 promotion
- Significant improvements in both ranking and calibration metrics
- Successful deployment in Alibaba's display advertising system
- Demonstrated effectiveness on real-world e-commerce promotion data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HDR works by recognizing that promotional periods create distinct data distribution shifts that degrade standard CVR prediction models. By retrieving historically similar promotions, the system can leverage patterns learned from past promotional events. The distribution shift correction ensures retrieved data aligns with current promotion characteristics, while TransBlock enables rapid model adaptation without complete retraining. This approach effectively bridges the gap between regular traffic patterns and promotional behavior, allowing the model to maintain prediction accuracy during critical high-traffic periods.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Data Retrieval -> Distribution Shift Correction -> TransBlock Fine-tuning -> CVR Prediction

Critical Path: The core workflow involves retrieving relevant historical promotion data, correcting for distribution shifts through re-weighting, and applying TransBlock fine-tuning to the base CVR model. The system must balance retrieval accuracy with computational efficiency to ensure real-time serving capabilities during high-traffic promotional periods.

Design Tradeoffs: The approach trades off some computational overhead for improved prediction accuracy during critical promotional periods. The system must balance the granularity of historical data retrieval with serving latency requirements. The TransBlock design prioritizes rapid adaptation over comprehensive model retraining, accepting potential limitations in capturing complex promotion-specific patterns.

Failure Signatures: Poor retrieval accuracy may lead to ineffective adaptation, while excessive distribution shift between retrieved and target data could result in suboptimal correction. Over-aggressive fine-tuning might cause overfitting to limited promotional data, while under-tuning may fail to capture promotion-specific patterns adequately.
