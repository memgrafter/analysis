# Adaptive Coordination in Social Embodied Rearrangement

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00087
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00087
- Reference count: 39
- Key outcome: BDP achieves 35% higher success rate and 32% higher efficiency compared to prior zero-shot coordination methods in multi-agent household rearrangement tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Social Rearrangement, a novel task for studying coordination in multi-agent embodied AI systems where two robots must collaborate on household tasks using only egocentric observations. The authors propose Behavior Diversity Play (BDP), a method that uses a shared policy architecture and a discriminability objective to encourage behavioral diversity across a population of agents. This approach enables training coordination agents that can adapt to unseen partners zero-shot. Evaluated across 10,000 rearrangement problems in 60 environments, BDP demonstrates substantial improvements in both success rate and efficiency compared to existing zero-shot coordination methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed BDP approach uses a shared policy architecture across all agents in a population, trained with a discriminability objective that encourages behavioral diversity. The key innovation is learning a coordination policy that can generalize to unseen partners by maximizing the discriminability between different agent behaviors while maintaining coordination effectiveness. The method operates under realistic constraints of egocentric observations without privileged information about the environment or partner. During training, a population of agents is developed with diverse behaviors through the discriminability objective, enabling the final policy to adapt to novel partners during zero-shot deployment.

## Key Results
- BDP achieves 35% higher success rate compared to prior zero-shot coordination methods
- BDP demonstrates 32% higher efficiency in task completion
- The method shows strong generalization capabilities across 10,000 rearrangement problems in 60 diverse environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BDP works by encouraging behavioral diversity within a population of agents through a discriminability objective. This diversity allows the shared policy architecture to learn robust coordination strategies that are not overfit to specific partner behaviors. The discriminability objective ensures that agents develop distinct yet complementary behaviors, which enables the coordination policy to handle the variability encountered when working with unseen partners. By maximizing the differences between agent behaviors while maintaining coordination effectiveness, BDP creates a policy that can adapt to new coordination scenarios without requiring retraining.

## Foundational Learning

**Egocentric observations** - Agents only perceive the environment from their own perspective without global information. Needed because real-world robots have limited sensor coverage and cannot access privileged information. Quick check: Verify agents can complete tasks using only local visual inputs.

**Behavioral diversity** - Population of agents develops distinct yet complementary behaviors. Needed to ensure robustness when coordinating with novel partners. Quick check: Measure pairwise behavioral differences between agents in the population.

**Discriminability objective** - Loss function that encourages behavioral differences while maintaining coordination. Needed to balance diversity with task effectiveness. Quick check: Monitor discriminability metrics during training to ensure sufficient behavioral separation.

**Zero-shot coordination** - Ability to coordinate with unseen partners without additional training. Needed for practical deployment where pre-training all possible partnerships is infeasible. Quick check: Test coordination success rate with held-out partner types.

**Shared policy architecture** - Single policy network used across all agents in population. Needed to enable generalization to new partners. Quick check: Verify policy can be deployed across different physical agent embodiments.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Environment Generator -> Task Distribution -> Population of Agents (shared policy + discriminability objective) -> Coordination Policy

**Critical path**: Environment Generator provides diverse rearrangement tasks → Population training with shared policy and discriminability objective → Coordination policy learns to handle behavioral diversity → Zero-shot evaluation with unseen partners

**Design tradeoffs**: The shared policy architecture enables generalization but may limit specialization for specific partner types. The discriminability objective promotes diversity but requires careful balancing to maintain coordination effectiveness. The egocentric observation constraint increases realism but reduces information availability compared to privileged-view methods.

**Failure signatures**: Poor discriminability leading to homogeneous behaviors, overfitting to training partners resulting in low zero-shot performance, or task failure due to insufficient coordination despite behavioral diversity. Monitoring success rate drops when introducing novel partners indicates coordination breakdown.

**First 3 experiments to run**: 1) Test coordination with systematically varied partner types to identify generalization boundaries. 2) Ablate the discriminability objective to measure its contribution to zero-shot performance. 3) Evaluate performance under communication constraints or sensor noise to test robustness.
