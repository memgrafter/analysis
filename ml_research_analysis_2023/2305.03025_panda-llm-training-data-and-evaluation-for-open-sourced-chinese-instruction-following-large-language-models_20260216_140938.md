# Panda LLM: Training Data and Evaluation for Open-Sourced Chinese Instruction-Following Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03025
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03025
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: Panda LLM achieves state-of-the-art performance on Chinese reasoning benchmarks, outperforming existing open-source Chinese LLMs

## Executive Summary
Panda LLM presents an open-source Chinese instruction-following large language model developed through a two-stage training approach. The model is built by first fine-tuning LLaMA on Chinese corpora, then instruction tuning on the COIG dataset. This approach addresses the lack of high-quality Chinese instruction datasets for open-source LLMs. Panda LLM demonstrates superior performance on Chinese reasoning benchmarks compared to existing open-source models, with the model's code and checkpoints publicly available.

## Method Summary
Panda LLM employs a two-stage training methodology using LLaMA as the base model. First, the model undergoes fine-tuning on five Chinese corpora (Chinese-Wiki-2019, Chinese-News-2016, Chinese-Baike-2018, Chinese-Webtext-2019, Translation-2019). Second, instruction tuning is performed on the COIG dataset containing diverse instruction types. Training uses 16 NVIDIA A100-80G GPUs with DeepSpeed ZERO-1, bfloat16 precision, and gradient checkpointing. The model sizes range from 7B to 65B parameters, with specific hyperparameters including learning rate 1e-5, batch sizes 8192/4096 for 7B/13B models, and 1% warm-up steps.

## Key Results
- Panda LLM achieves state-of-the-art performance on Chinese reasoning benchmarks (LogiQA-v2, C3-d, C3-m)
- Using just 4.2% of the COIG dataset improves reasoning capability, with 13.38 gain on C3-m dataset
- Panda-13B outperforms other open-source Chinese LLMs on benchmark evaluations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Two-stage training (pre-training + instruction-tuning) is more effective than direct training on mixed instruction/non-instruction data.
- Mechanism: Pre-training on large-scale general Chinese corpora builds foundational language understanding, while instruction-tuning on a smaller, diverse instruction dataset fine-tunes the model to follow instructions and generalize across domains.
- Core assumption: The model benefits from first learning general language patterns before being specialized for instruction-following tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our Panda LLM model has been trained on Chinese-Wiki-2019, Chinese-News-2016, Chinese-Baike-2018, Chinese-Webtext-2019 and Translation-2019 (Xu, 2019) and COIG datasets (Zhang et al., 2023) with instruction-tuning (Wei et al., 2021) based on the LLaMA model (Touvron et al., 2023)."
  - [section 3.3] "To address this issue, we turned to the COIG dataset, which contains instruction data from a vast array of domains, including exam instructions, human value alignment instructions, Leetcode instructions, and more."
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct corpus evidence comparing mixed training vs. staged training, but related papers suggest staged training is a common approach.
- Break condition: If instruction-tuning data is not sufficiently diverse or if pre-training corpus is too narrow, the two-stage approach may not yield significant improvements.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning on a diverse, high-quality instruction dataset (COIG) significantly improves reasoning performance on Chinese benchmarks.
- Mechanism: The COIG dataset contains varied instruction types (exam instructions, human value alignment, Leetcode, etc.), which exposes the model to a wide range of reasoning and problem-solving scenarios during fine-tuning, leading to better generalization.
- Core assumption: Diverse instruction data leads to better generalization than narrow, domain-specific instruction data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "even using just 4.2% of the COIG dataset dramatically improves our model's reasoning capability, particularly on the C3-m dataset, with an impressive gain of 13.38."
  - [section 3.3] "To enhance the instruction-following capability of Panda LLM, we further incorporate the Chinese Open Instruction Generalist (COIG) dataset (Zhang et al., 2023) into our corpus."
  - [corpus] Weak. While the COIG dataset is mentioned, there's no direct corpus evidence quantifying the diversity or quality of the instruction data.
- Break condition: If the COIG dataset lacks diversity or contains low-quality instructions, the performance gains may be minimal or non-existent.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Starting with a powerful base model (LLaMA) and fine-tuning it on Chinese data yields better results than training a Chinese LLM from scratch.
- Mechanism: LLaMA provides a strong foundation in general language understanding and reasoning. Fine-tuning on Chinese data adapts this foundation to the Chinese language and instruction-following tasks, leveraging the pre-trained knowledge.
- Core assumption: Pre-trained models like LLaMA have learned general language and reasoning capabilities that are transferable to Chinese and instruction-following tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our Panda LLM model has been trained on Chinese-Wiki-2019, Chinese-News-2016, Chinese-Baike-2018, Chinese-Webtext-2019 and Translation-2019 (Xu, 2019) and COIG datasets (Zhang et al., 2023) with instruction-tuning (Wei et al., 2021) based on the LLaMA model (Touvron et al., 2023)."
  - [section 2.1] "Our Panda LLM is established based on various LLaMA (Large Language Model Meta AI) models (Touvron et al., 2023), including Meta's recently released LLaMA-7B, LLaMA-13B, LLaMA-33B, and LLaMA-65B, as our base models."
  - [corpus] Weak. No direct corpus evidence comparing fine-tuning vs. training from scratch, but related papers suggest fine-tuning pre-trained models is a common and effective approach.
- Break condition: If the base model (LLaMA) is not sufficiently powerful or if the Chinese data is too different from the pre-training data, the fine-tuning approach may not yield significant improvements.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: LLaMA base model -> Chinese corpus fine-tuning -> COIG instruction tuning -> Evaluation on Chinese benchmarks
- Critical path: The critical path for training Panda LLM involves: 1) Pre-training on large-scale Chinese corpora, 2) Fine-tuning on the COIG dataset, 3) Evaluating on Chinese reasoning benchmarks
- Design tradeoffs: The main design tradeoff is between model size and performance. Larger models (e.g., Panda-13B, Panda-33B) may achieve better performance but require more computational resources. Another tradeoff is between the diversity of instruction data and the risk of overfitting to specific instruction types.
- Failure signatures: Potential failure modes include: 1) Poor performance on Chinese reasoning benchmarks, indicating issues with the fine-tuning or instruction-tuning process, 2) Overfitting to the COIG dataset, leading to poor generalization on other Chinese tasks, 3) Suboptimal performance compared to larger models, indicating limitations of the chosen model size.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal sampling proportion of COIG dataset to maximize instruction-following performance without over-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions COIG makes up 4.2% of training samples and significantly boosts performance, but doesn't explore optimal proportions
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested one fixed proportion (4.2%) and didn't experiment with different sampling ratios
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying COIG proportion from 1% to 20% while measuring performance on reasoning benchmarks

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does Panda LLM's performance compare to commercial Chinese LLMs like GPT-4 or Claude when evaluated on the same benchmarks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only compares Panda to other open-source models, not commercial alternatives
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on open-source models and doesn't have access to commercial model evaluations
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct benchmark comparisons between Panda and commercial models on LogiQA-v2, C3-d, and C3-m datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relative contribution of each Chinese corpus (Chinese-Wiki-2019, Chinese-News-2016, Chinese-Baike-2018, Chinese-Webtext-2019, Translation-2019) to the final model performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses all five corpora together but doesn't analyze their individual contributions
- Why unresolved: The ablation study only shows results with and without COIG, not individual corpus contributions
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies training on different combinations of the five corpora while measuring performance impact

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the two-stage training approach (pre-training followed by instruction-tuning) compare to other training strategies like interleaved pre-training and instruction-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions they tried mixing datasets indiscriminately but doesn't explore other training strategies
- Why unresolved: Only two training approaches were tested - sequential pre-training then instruction-tuning, and mixing all data
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparison of Panda's two-stage approach with interleaved training and other alternative training schedules on the same benchmarks

## Limitations
- The paper lacks direct empirical evidence comparing the two-stage training approach against alternative methods like mixed training on instruction and non-instruction data.
- Evaluation is limited to three Chinese reasoning benchmarks without detailed analysis of model performance across different instruction types or failure case studies.
- The paper does not provide independent replication or comparison with recent models to validate the magnitude of claimed improvements.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The overall approach of developing an open-source Chinese instruction-following LLM using LLaMA as a base model and the COIG dataset for instruction tuning is well-established and technically sound.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that Panda LLM achieves state-of-the-art performance on Chinese reasoning benchmarks is supported by reported results, but without comparison to other recent models or independent replication, the magnitude of improvement remains uncertain.
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of the two-stage training methodology is theoretically sound but lacks direct empirical validation through ablation studies.
