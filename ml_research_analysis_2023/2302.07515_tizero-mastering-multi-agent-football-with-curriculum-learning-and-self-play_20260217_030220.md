# TiZero: Mastering Multi-Agent Football with Curriculum Learning and Self-Play

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07515
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07515
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TiZero outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods by over 30% in win rate in 11 vs. 11 football

## Executive Summary
TiZero presents a self-evolving multi-agent system for playing 11 vs. 11 football in the Google Research Football environment without demonstrations. The system introduces three key innovations: joint-policy optimization for coordinated learning, adaptive curriculum learning to address sparse rewards, and a novel self-play strategy combining challenge and generalization phases. Trained at scale using a distributed framework across 800 CPUs and 2 A100 GPUs for 45 days, TiZero demonstrates superior performance with significantly more assists and passes leading to goals compared to previous methods.

## Method Summary
TiZero uses joint-ratio Policy Optimization (JRPO) to optimize policies across multiple agents jointly through decentralized factorization, enabling coordinated actions. The system employs curriculum learning with 10 difficulty levels, starting with simplified scenarios and progressively advancing to full 11v11 football. Self-play combines challenge (80% probability) against the strongest previous version with generalization against the entire opponent pool weighted toward stronger opponents. The distributed framework uses actors for rollouts, learners for training, and a data server for communication, with policies conditioned on player IDs and using LSTM memory.

## Key Results
- Outperforms previous state-of-the-art methods by over 30% in win rate
- Demonstrates superior coordination with significantly more assists and passes leading to goals
- Shows effectiveness of algorithmic innovations across multiple environments beyond football

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint-policy optimization enables better coordination among agents than independent policy updates.
- Mechanism: By optimizing the joint policy using a decentralized factorization, all agents' policies are improved jointly with respect to a shared advantage function, encouraging coordinated actions across agents.
- Core assumption: The joint policy factorization captures meaningful dependencies between agents' actions while still allowing decentralized execution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "an objective that optimizes the policies of multiple agents jointly"
  - [section 4.2] "we optimize the joint-policy using a decentralized factorization"
  - [section 5.2] "JRPO's performance is equal or better than MAPPO, MAT and QMIX across all tasks"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Curriculum learning addresses the sparse reward problem in 11v11 football.
- Mechanism: Agents start training in simplified scenarios with denser rewards and gradually progress to more complex scenarios, allowing them to learn basic behaviors efficiently before tackling the full game.
- Core assumption: Early curriculum stages provide meaningful learning signals that transfer to later stages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "adaptive curriculum learning"
  - [section 4.3] "we design a curriculum self-play mechanism, in which agents are trained on a sequence of progressively more difficult scenarios"
  - [section 5.1] "the curriculum self-play stage allows TiZero to rapidly improve early in training to a level just below the 'Built-in Hard' agent"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The Challenge & Generalize self-play strategy produces more diverse and robust strategies than simple self-play.
- Mechanism: Agents first challenge the strongest previous version (80% probability) to ensure monotonic improvement, then generalize by playing against the entire opponent pool with probability weighted toward stronger opponents.
- Core assumption: Playing against a diverse set of opponents prevents overfitting to specific strategies and improves robustness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a novel self-play strategy"
  - [section 4.3] "we design an algorithm that produces a monotonically-improving sequence of policies"
  - [section 5.3] "Results show that our method outperforms other baselines in both training efficiency and final performance"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Observation encoder: Splits 268D vector into 6 parts, processes with MLPs, concatenates, feeds to LSTM
  - Policy network: Outputs 19-action softmax distribution, conditioned on player ID
  - Value network: Centralized estimate of team value, uses 5-part observation
  - Distributed framework: Actors (rollouts), Learners (training), Data server (communication)
  - Opponent pool: Dynamic collection of saved agent versions for self-play

- Critical path:
  1. Actors collect rollouts with LSTM hidden states
  2. Data server forwards to Learners
  3. Learners compute joint-policy objective and value loss
  4. Gradients averaged via NCCL reduction
  5. Updated parameters synchronized back to Actors
  6. Self-play module samples opponents based on performance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Joint-policy optimization vs. MAPPO: Better coordination but requires centralized value network
  - Curriculum progression: Faster early learning but requires careful threshold tuning
  - Self-play sampling: Balances exploration of diverse strategies with exploitation of strong opponents

- Failure signatures:
  - No improvement in TrueSkill rating: Check curriculum progression thresholds
  - High variance in win rates: Verify opponent sampling distribution
  - GPU memory exhaustion: Monitor joint-policy optimization memory usage

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare JRPO vs. MAPPO on Overcooked (simpler than GFootball)
  2. Test curriculum progression on simplified GFootball scenario (5v5)
  3. Validate self-play diversity on Tic-Tac-Toe before scaling to GFootball
