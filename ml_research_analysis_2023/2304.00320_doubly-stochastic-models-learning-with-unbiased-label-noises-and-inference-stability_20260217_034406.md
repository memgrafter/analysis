# Doubly Stochastic Models: Learning with Unbiased Label Noises and Inference Stability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00320
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00320
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper investigates how unbiased random label noises in mini-batch stochastic gradient descent (SGD) act as an implicit regularizer. The authors model SGD dynamics as a doubly stochastic differential equation (SDE) with two noise terms: one from gradient sampling and another from label noise sampling. They prove this continuous-time approximation converges to the discrete-time SGD iterates. Their analysis shows the label noise term encourages convergence to solutions with lower neural network gradient norms, improving inference stability. Experiments on self-distillation with ResNet-56 and linear regression validate that adding unbiased label noise reduces gradient norms and can improve generalization, with the noise level tunable via variance. This is the first work to rigorously characterize the regularization effects of unbiased label noise in SGD.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel theoretical framework for understanding how unbiased label noise in stochastic gradient descent (SGD) acts as an implicit regularizer. The authors model SGD dynamics as a doubly stochastic differential equation (SDE) that captures both the gradient sampling noise and label noise, proving this continuous-time approximation converges to the discrete-time SGD iterates. Their analysis demonstrates that the label noise term encourages convergence to solutions with lower neural network gradient norms, thereby improving inference stability. Through experiments on self-distillation with ResNet-56 and linear regression, they validate that adding unbiased label noise reduces gradient norms and can improve generalization, with the noise level tunable via variance.

## Method Summary
The authors model SGD with unbiased label noise as a doubly stochastic differential equation, where two noise terms are incorporated: one from gradient sampling and another from label noise sampling. They prove that this continuous-time SDE approximation converges to the discrete-time SGD iterates, establishing theoretical grounding for their approach. The analysis shows that the label noise term acts as an implicit regularizer by encouraging convergence to solutions with lower neural network gradient norms, which improves inference stability. The method is validated through experiments on self-distillation tasks using ResNet-56 and linear regression, demonstrating that adding unbiased label noise reduces gradient norms and can improve generalization. The noise level is tunable via variance, allowing for controlled experimentation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by modeling SGD with unbiased label noise as a doubly stochastic differential equation that captures both gradient sampling noise and label noise. The label noise term acts as an implicit regularizer by encouraging the optimization process to converge to solutions with lower neural network gradient norms. This regularization effect improves inference stability by preventing the model from fitting to noisy or unstable regions of the loss landscape. The continuous-time SDE approximation provides a rigorous mathematical foundation that converges to the discrete-time SGD iterates, explaining why the label noise has a regularizing effect that can improve generalization.

## Limitations
- The theoretical analysis assumes unbiased label noise, which may not hold in all practical scenarios
- The continuous-time SDE approximation may not capture all discrete dynamics of practical SGD implementations
- Experimental validation is limited to specific architectures (ResNet-56) and tasks (CIFAR-10 classification, linear regression)
- The relationship between optimal noise level and dataset characteristics requires further investigation

## Confidence
Theoretical convergence proof: High - The mathematical derivation of the doubly stochastic SDE and its convergence to discrete SGD iterates appears rigorous within the stated assumptions
Label noise as implicit regularizer: Medium - While theoretically supported, the practical magnitude and consistency of this effect across diverse architectures and tasks needs verification
Generalization improvements: Medium - Experimental results are promising but based on limited scenarios; the relationship between gradient norm reduction and generalization requires more extensive validation
