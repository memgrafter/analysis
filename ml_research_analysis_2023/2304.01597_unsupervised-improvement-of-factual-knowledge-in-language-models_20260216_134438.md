# Unsupervised Improvement of Factual Knowledge in Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01597
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01597
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: Significant improvements on factual recall benchmarks like LAMA through PMI-based token prioritization during pretraining

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method to improve language model performance on knowledge-intensive tasks without using external knowledge bases. The approach modifies masked language modeling by applying higher masking rates and larger penalties to tokens with higher informative relevance, computed using pointwise mutual information. Experiments show significant improvements on factual recall benchmarks, extractive QA, and zero-shot tasks like sentiment analysis and natural language inference using AutoPrompt. The method outperforms baselines in most cases, especially on factual recall tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors propose modifying the masked language modeling objective by computing pointwise mutual information (PMI) between tokens to determine their informative relevance. Tokens with higher PMI scores receive higher masking rates (15-50%) and larger penalty weights (normalized to [1,5]) during pretraining. The approach uses Wikipedia corpus with wordpiece tokenization, computes skip-gram co-occurrence statistics within a window of 10, and trains BERT-base models for 10 epochs with weighted cross-entropy loss. The method is fully unsupervised and does not require external knowledge bases during pretraining.

## Key Results
- Significant improvement on LAMA factual recall benchmark across all metrics
- Enhanced performance on SQuAD extractive QA task
- Better zero-shot results on sentiment analysis (SST2) and natural language inference using AutoPrompt
- Outperforms baseline BERT models in most knowledge-intensive tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Prioritizing informative tokens during pretraining improves factual knowledge retention by forcing the model to focus on predicting and correctly reconstructing important factual tokens rather than common function words. This is achieved by increasing masking rates and penalty weights for tokens with higher informative relevance computed via PMI.

### Mechanism 2
Weighted cross-entropy loss penalizes mistakes on informative tokens more severely, creating a stronger gradient signal when the model makes errors on important factual tokens. This reinforces factual knowledge storage without degrading general language understanding.

### Mechanism 3
Variable masking rates based on token importance lead to more efficient pretraining for knowledge-intensive tasks. Instead of uniform 15% masking, tokens with higher informative relevance are masked at rates between 15-50%, forcing the model to develop stronger representations for factual knowledge.

  - Quick check question: How does PMI measure the relationship between two tokens in a corpus?

  - Why needed here: The paper's goal is to improve performance on tasks that require factual knowledge recall rather than just language understanding
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes knowledge-intensive tasks from general language understanding tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Wikipedia corpus → WordPiece tokenization → Skip-gram co-occurrence statistics → PMI computation → Informative relevance scores → Variable masking rates → Weighted loss calculation → Model training → Knowledge retention evaluation

- Critical path: PMI computation → Masking rate assignment → Weighted loss calculation → Model training → Knowledge retention evaluation

- Design tradeoffs: Higher masking rates for informative tokens vs. maintaining general language understanding, computational cost of PMI computation vs. performance gains, vocabulary size (100k) vs. inclusion of most entities

- Failure signatures: Poor performance on non-knowledge-intensive tasks (CoLA underperforms baseline), degradation in general language modeling capabilities, computational overhead becoming prohibitive for larger corpora

- First 3 experiments: 1) Compare BERTuu (baseline) vs. BERTvw (proposed) on LAMA factual recall benchmark, 2) Evaluate zero-shot performance on SQuAD portion of LAMA, 3) Test prompt-tuned performance on SST2 sentiment analysis and NLI tasks using AutoPrompt

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How does the proposed method perform on multilingual knowledge-intensive tasks compared to existing multilingual language models?
- What is the impact of different skip-gram window sizes on the informative relevance computation and downstream task performance?
- How does the proposed method scale to larger language models like GPT-3 or T5-XXL in terms of both performance and computational requirements?
- What is the effect of the proposed pretraining strategy on the model's ability to generalize to out-of-distribution data or domain adaptation?
- How does the proposed method impact the model's performance on generation tasks compared to BERT-style models?

## Limitations

- Computational overhead from PMI computation (2 hours, 11GB memory) may become prohibitive for larger vocabularies
- Effectiveness on truly zero-shot knowledge retrieval not thoroughly evaluated as AutoPrompt experiments require prompt tuning
- Lacks ablation studies showing individual contributions of variable masking rates versus weighted penalties
- Vocabulary size of 100k may miss rare but important factual tokens
- Skip-gram window size of 10 not empirically validated through ablation studies

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Improving factual recall on LAMA benchmark through PMI-based token prioritization
- Medium Confidence: Working for zero-shot knowledge-intensive tasks using AutoPrompt
- Low Confidence: Fully unsupervised nature without external knowledge bases

## Next Checks

1. Run ablation studies comparing variable masking rates versus weighted penalties independently to determine individual contributions and potential synergies.

3. Test pretrained models on diverse knowledge-intensive tasks including open-domain QA, entity linking, and fact verification to assess generalization beyond specific benchmarks.
