# Structural hierarchical learning for energy networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03978
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03978
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 34
- Primary result: Structural hierarchical learning with partitioned neural networks and topological bridges improves accuracy and coherency in energy network forecasting while reducing model complexity.

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the challenge of achieving accurate and coherent forecasts across hierarchical energy networks, where disparate predictions at different levels lead to inconsistent decision-making. Building on recent advances in hierarchical learning, the authors propose a novel approach that leverages custom neural network designs inspired by the topological structures of hierarchies. By partitioning large models into smaller, node-specific components and connecting them through tailored topological bridges, the method reduces model complexity and improves data efficiency. Evaluated on real-world building energy data, the method demonstrates that structural models with fewer connections perform best, and that coherency information enhances both accuracy and coherency of forecasts, provided individual predictions are sufficiently accurate.

## Method Summary
The method proposes custom neural network architectures for hierarchical forecasting that partition large models into smaller, node-specific components connected through topological bridges. These partitions are designed to reflect hierarchical structures (fully disconnected, leaf-connected, or k-level partitions), while bridges enable targeted information exchange across levels (bottom-up, top-down, or both). The approach incorporates coherency requirements directly into learning through a structurally-scaled loss function, avoiding the biases of a priori normalization. Batch normalization handles scale differences across hierarchical levels, and the method is evaluated using a rolling window cross-validation setup on building energy data from the BDG2 dataset.

## Key Results
- Structural hierarchical models with fewer connections (tree and cutree partitions) outperform fully connected models in both accuracy and coherency.
- Incorporating coherency information improves both accuracy and coherency of forecasts, but only when individual predictions are sufficiently accurate.
- Structural models with top-down and bottom-up-top-down bridges perform better than disconnected models, demonstrating the value of information exchange across hierarchical levels.
- The method achieves data efficiency by reducing the number of weights to update compared to fully connected models, leading to fewer iterations required for calibration.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Structural partitioning reduces model complexity and improves data efficiency by decreasing the number of weights to update.
- Mechanism: By dividing a large hierarchical model into separate partitions, each tailored to node-specific elements, the total number of weights is significantly reduced compared to a fully connected model. This reduction leads to fewer iterations required for calibration and fewer data instances needed for training.
- Core assumption: The hierarchical structure of the data allows for meaningful separation into partitions without losing critical inter-node information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Building on recent advances in hierarchical learning, the authors propose a novel approach that leverages custom neural network designs inspired by the topological structures of hierarchies. By partitioning large models into smaller, node-specific components and connecting them through tailored topological bridges, the method reduces model complexity and improves data efficiency."
  - [section]: "We propose three partition variations, serving as alternatives to the fully-connected hierarchical model of Ref. [21], echoing established tree topologies, namely, fully-disconnected, leaf-connected, and k-level partitions... In a hierarchical forecasting setting, however, it becomes on the contrary rather beneficial to gather produced outputs under the hood of one model."
- Break condition: If the hierarchical structure does not allow for meaningful separation or if the partitions become too isolated, leading to loss of important inter-node information.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Topological bridges facilitate targeted information exchange across hierarchical layers, enhancing forecast accuracy and coherency.
- Mechanism: By creating connections between separated model partitions, information can be shared in a targeted manner. Different types of bridges (e.g., bottom-up, top-down, disconnected) allow for varying levels of information flow, enabling the model to capture both local and global patterns within the hierarchy.
- Core assumption: The hierarchical structure of the data implies meaningful relationships between different levels that can be exploited through these bridges.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Building on recent advances in hierarchical learning, the authors propose a novel approach that leverages custom neural network designs inspired by the topological structures of hierarchies. By partitioning large models into smaller, node-specific components and connecting them through tailored topological bridges, the method reduces model complexity and improves data efficiency."
  - [section]: "We propose three partition variations... Two topological bridges have already been presented, namely disconnected partitioning, or disc, and fully connected ones. To complement these we propose two well-established hierarchical connections, namely, (d) bottom-up and (e) top-down..."
- Break condition: If the bridges are not properly designed to reflect the hierarchical relationships, they may introduce noise or irrelevant information, hindering model performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Structurally-scaled loss functions address scale differences in hierarchical time series, avoiding a priori normalization and its associated biases.
- Mechanism: By incorporating structural scaling into the loss function, the model can handle the inherent scale differences across hierarchical levels without requiring explicit normalization of the input data. This prevents the biases introduced by normalization that were identified as problematic in previous work.
- Core assumption: The scale differences in hierarchical time series are inherent and can be effectively managed through structural scaling rather than normalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The approach incorporates coherency requirements directly into the learning process using a structurally-scaled loss function, avoiding the need for a priori normalization and its associated biases."
  - [section]: "To propose a regressor that is robust to scale differences and avoids normalization requirements and its subsequent identified faulty-coherency learning [21], we leverage the Mean Structurally-Scaled Square Error (MS3E)..."
- Break condition: If the structural scaling is not accurately reflecting the true scale differences or if the model cannot effectively learn from unscaled data, the approach may fail to improve performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of structural hierarchical learning scale with the size of the hierarchy (number of nodes and levels)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the proposed approach reduces model complexity and improves data efficiency, but does not provide a systematic analysis of scalability with hierarchy size.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a specific case study (F oxsite with 140 nodes) and does not explore how the method performs on larger or deeper hierarchies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of nodes and levels in the hierarchy, reporting accuracy and coherency metrics across different sizes.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different topological bridge designs on forecast performance for various types of hierarchical structures (e.g., balanced vs. unbalanced trees)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces different topological bridge designs (disconnected, bottom-up, top-down, etc.) and evaluates them on a single hierarchical structure, but does not explore how these designs perform on different types of hierarchies.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers a single hierarchical structure (power distribution-like) and does not investigate the performance of different topological bridges on other types of hierarchies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the method to different hierarchical structures (e.g., balanced, unbalanced, deep, shallow) and comparing the performance of different topological bridge designs across these structures.

### Open Question 3

- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the impact of different covariance matrix approximation methods on the performance of structural hierarchical learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of structural hierarchical learning using different covariance matrix approximation methods, reporting accuracy and coherency metrics for each method.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness depends heavily on the hierarchical structure of the data, with unclear generalizability to non-hierarchical or irregularly structured networks.
- The study uses a single real-world dataset (BDG2 F oxsite), limiting generalizability across different energy network types and scales.
- The choice of partitioning strategy and bridge types appears somewhat arbitrary, with limited justification for why certain configurations work better than others.

## Confidence

**High confidence:** The core mechanism of using structural partitioning to reduce model complexity and improve data efficiency is well-supported by the empirical results and theoretical reasoning.

**Medium confidence:** The effectiveness of topological bridges for information exchange across hierarchical layers, while demonstrated, could benefit from more systematic analysis of different bridge types.

**Medium confidence:** The structural scaling approach for handling scale differences is innovative, but its advantages over traditional normalization methods need further validation across diverse datasets.
