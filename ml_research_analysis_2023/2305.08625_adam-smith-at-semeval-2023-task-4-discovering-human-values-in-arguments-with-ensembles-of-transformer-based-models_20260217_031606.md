# Adam-Smith at SemEval-2023 Task 4: Discovering Human Values in Arguments with Ensembles of Transformer-based Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08625
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08625
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- Best F1-score of 0.56 on Main dataset

## Executive Summary
Adam Smith presents a transformer-based ensemble system for detecting human values in argumentative text, achieving top performance in the SemEval-2023 Task 4 competition. The approach combines 12 transformer models (DeBERTa and IBM-RoBERTa variants) trained with different objectives and thresholds optimized on a separate Leave-Out-Dataset. While demonstrating strong performance on the primary dataset, the system showed significant performance degradation on out-of-domain text, particularly from the New York Times.

## Method Summary
The system uses an ensemble of 12 transformer-based models, each consisting of a pretrained language model with an additional fully connected layer. Models are trained using either loss minimization or F1-score maximization objectives, with early stopping based on validation performance. The final predictions are generated by averaging individual model outputs and applying a global threshold optimized on a 300-sample Leave-Out-Dataset to maximize macro F1-score. Models are pretrained on IBM-ArgQ-Rank-30kArgs using masked language modeling before fine-tuning on the task data.

## Key Results
- Achieved highest F1-score of 0.56 on the Main test dataset
- Ablation study showed 3-model ensemble (EN-Deberta-F1) slightly outperformed the full 12-model system on some datasets
- Significant performance drop on New York Times dataset (F1-score of 0.25 vs 0.34 for 3-model ensemble)
- Outperformed baseline models by large margin on all test sets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training models to optimize F1-score instead of loss improves multi-label classification performance on imbalanced datasets.
- Mechanism: By selecting decision thresholds that maximize F1-score on a validation set, the ensemble achieves better precision-recall balance for rare labels.
- Core assumption: The validation set distribution is representative of the test set for threshold selection.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Instead of calculating the loss, we could binarize the predictions according to a threshold and use them to calculate the f1-score... Algorithm 1 to determine the threshold that maximizes the f1-score."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citation, but task description confirms multi-label imbalance.
- Break condition: If the Leave-Out-Dataset distribution differs significantly from test data, threshold selection fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Ensembling diverse transformer models reduces variance and improves generalization.
- Mechanism: Combining predictions from models trained with different objectives (F1 vs Loss) and different random seeds creates a more robust decision boundary.
- Core assumption: Model diversity captures different aspects of the data distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Ensembling the models by selecting one global decision threshold that maximizes the f1-score leads to the best-performing system in the competition."
  - [section] "The final system is an ensemble of 12 individual models... Each model has the same structure: It consists of a transformer-based language model with one additional fully connected linear layer on top."
- Break condition: If all models converge to similar solutions, ensembling provides little benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pretraining on domain-specific datasets (IBM-ArgQ-Rank-30kArgs) improves model adaptation to argument text.
- Mechanism: Masked language modeling on argument data shifts the language model's understanding toward the specific terminology and structure used in the task.
- Core assumption: The pretraining corpus shares sufficient linguistic features with the target task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We pretrain our models on the IBM-dataset using masked language modeling... Table 2 shows that pretraining only improves the roberta model but not the deberta model."
  - [corpus] Missing - no external validation of pretraining benefits.
- Break condition: If pretraining data distribution differs too much from target task, performance degrades.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does reducing ensemble size improve robustness across different domains?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that an ensemble of 3 models (EN-Deberta-F1) performs better than a larger ensemble of 12 models on the New York Times dataset (F1-score of 0.34 vs 0.25), suggesting smaller ensembles may be more robust to domain shifts.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates improved performance on one out-of-domain dataset, it doesn't systematically test whether this holds across multiple domains or different types of domain shifts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the 3-model ensemble against multiple diverse out-of-domain datasets to confirm consistent robustness improvements, and comparing performance degradation rates across domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific components in the larger ensemble are counterproductive to performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The ablation study shows that the full 12-model ensemble performs worse than a 3-model ensemble on the New York Times dataset, but doesn't identify which specific models or configurations are causing this degradation.
- Why unresolved: The paper only shows that smaller ensembles work better but doesn't analyze which individual models drag down performance or whether certain combinations of model types are problematic.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of each model's contribution to ensemble performance through techniques like leave-one-out ablation, and identifying whether certain model configurations consistently harm ensemble performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the self-training approach with silver labels affect model generalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper implemented self-training to create additional training data but found that the resulting ensemble performed worse than other approaches, despite showing slight improvement during training.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't investigate why the self-training approach failed - whether it was due to noise in the silver labels, domain mismatch, or other factors that could potentially be addressed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of silver label quality, comparison of silver label distribution to original training data, and experiments varying the amount and quality of self-generated training data.

## Limitations
- Significant performance degradation on out-of-domain text (New York Times dataset)
- Computationally expensive ensemble requiring 12 individual models
- Non-standard macro F1-score calculation differs from typical implementations
- Limited ablation testing on final test performance

## Confidence
- High confidence: The basic ensemble methodology and threshold optimization approach are well-documented and technically sound
- Medium confidence: The performance claims are supported by competition results, but limited ablation testing and lack of external validation reduce certainty
- Low confidence: The pretraining benefits and domain generalization capabilities remain poorly characterized
