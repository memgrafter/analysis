# Less is More: Removing Text-regions Improves CLIP Training Efficiency and Robustness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05095
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05095
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Removing text regions from CLIP training data improves zero-shot classification accuracy and robustness against typographic attacks, achieving 68.78% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-Atr versus <50% for prior models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a simple yet effective method to enhance CLIP training efficiency and robustness by filtering out images containing text regions. The authors argue that text-heavy images often introduce noise and typographic vulnerabilities, distracting the model from learning pure visual semantics. By removing such images from the training set, the model becomes more robust to typographic attacks and achieves higher zero-shot classification accuracy on standard benchmarks like ImageNet and CoCo. The proposed ImageNet-Atr dataset specifically benchmarks robustness against typographic attacks, where the filtered CLIP model significantly outperforms previous approaches.

## Method Summary
The core method involves preprocessing the training corpus to exclude images containing text regions before training CLIP. This is achieved using an off-the-shelf text detection model (such as CRAFT or similar) to identify and filter out images with substantial textual content. The filtered dataset is then used to train CLIP as usual, with no changes to the model architecture or optimization procedure. The authors demonstrate that this preprocessing step leads to more efficient training (higher accuracy within the same optimization budget) and improved robustness against typographic attacks, as measured on their newly proposed ImageNet-Atr benchmark.

## Key Results
- Achieves 68.78% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-Atr, significantly outperforming previous models with accuracy below 50%.
- Improves zero-shot classification accuracy on ImageNet and CoCo benchmarks compared to standard CLIP training.
- Demonstrates more efficient training within a fixed optimization budget, reaching higher accuracy faster.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
By removing text-heavy images from training, the model is forced to focus on visual semantics rather than relying on co-occurring text as a shortcut. Text regions in images can introduce typographic attacks, where adversarial text overlays mislead the model. Filtering these out reduces such vulnerabilities and encourages the model to develop stronger visual representations.

## Limitations
- May remove images where text is semantically important (e.g., signs, labels), potentially losing useful visual-text correlations.
- Evaluation focuses on zero-shot classification; impact on other vision-language tasks (retrieval, captioning) is not explored.
- No ablation study on optimal amount of text removal or validation across different CLIP backbones.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Filtering text regions improves CLIP training efficiency and robustness against typographic attacks, as evidenced by ImageNet-Atr results and baseline comparisons.
- **Medium confidence**: Filtering improves zero-shot classification accuracy on standard benchmarks, but generalizability across CLIP variants needs further validation.
- **Low confidence**: The claim of "significant" robustness improvement is based on a single new evaluation set (ImageNet-Atr), whose representativeness for real-world scenarios is unclear.

## Next Checks
1. Test the filtering approach on diverse CLIP backbones (e.g., ResNet-50, ViT-B/16) to assess generalizability beyond the evaluated ViT-L/14 model.
2. Conduct an ablation study varying the percentage of text-filtered images to identify the optimal trade-off between robustness and retained visual semantics.
3. Evaluate the impact on downstream tasks beyond zero-shot classification, such as image-text retrieval accuracy, dense captioning, or multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
