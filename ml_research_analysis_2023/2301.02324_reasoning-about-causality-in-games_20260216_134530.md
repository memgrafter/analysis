# Reasoning about Causality in Games

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.02324
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02324
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: Introduces causal games as a unifying framework supporting both causal and game-theoretic reasoning

## Executive Summary
This paper presents causal games, a framework that extends multi-agent influence diagrams (MAIDs) to the causal domain, enabling the computation of predictions, interventions, and counterfactuals in games. The authors introduce mechanised MAIDs to encode dependencies between agents' decision rules and the distributions governing the game, allowing for more efficient computation by exploiting conditional independencies. By generalizing Pearl's causal hierarchy to the game-theoretic domain, causal games support richer queries than existing formalisms while maintaining computational benefits.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce mechanised MAIDs by adding mechanism variables (Π for decision rules, Θ for parameters) to standard MAIDs, creating explicit dependencies between agents' choices and game parameters. They define R-relevance graphs using graphical criteria to identify conditional independencies, enabling more efficient computation of equilibria. The framework is then extended to support interventions and counterfactuals by viewing causal games as an extension of Pearl's causal hierarchy. The paper provides formal definitions for predictions, interventions, and counterfactuals in the game-theoretic context, demonstrating how standard causal inference techniques can be adapted to strategic settings.

## Key Results
- Mechanised MAIDs successfully encode dependencies between decision rules and game parameters
- R-relevance graphs enable identification of conditional independencies for efficient computation
- Causal games support both causal and game-theoretic reasoning by generalizing MAIDs to Pearl's causal hierarchy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Mechanised MAIDs encode dependencies between agents' decision rules and the distributions governing the game
- Mechanism: By introducing mechanism variables (Π for decision rules, Θ for parameters) and edges from these into decision rule variables, the model explicitly represents how agents' choices depend on game parameters and other agents' choices
- Core assumption: Agents' rationality relations rD(paΠD) can be modelled as serial relations mapping parent mechanism variable instantiations to sets of rational decision rules
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "mechanised games, which encode dependencies between agents' decision rules and the distributions governing the game."
  - [section]: "For each decision variable D, a new parent ΠD representing its decision rule is added, and for each non-decision variable V, a new parent ΘV representing the parameters of its CPD."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: R-relevance graphs enable identification of conditional independencies in games, allowing for more efficient computation of equilibria
- Mechanism: By applying graphical criteria (R-reachability) to the mechanised graph, irrelevant edges can be pruned, creating a minimal representation where decision rules depend only on truly relevant mechanism variables
- Core assumption: Sound and complete graphical criteria exist for identifying R-relevance for natural choices of R (e.g., best responses, subgame perfectness)
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we introduce mechanised games, which encode dependencies between agents' decision rules and the distributions governing the game."
  - [section]: "We also generalise K&M's notion of strategic relevance to R-relevance to enable modelling many different decision-making principles."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Causal games support both causal and game-theoretic reasoning by generalising MAIDs to the causal hierarchy
- Mechanism: By viewing causal games as extending MAIDs to support interventions and counterfactuals (levels 2 and 3 of Pearl's hierarchy), queries about causal effects in games can be answered using standard causal inference techniques adapted to strategic settings
- Core assumption: The game-theoretic structure (rational agents, strategic interactions) can be integrated with causal models without violating causal assumptions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Causal games extend multi-agent influence diagrams (MAIDs) to the causal domain, allowing for the computation of predictions, interventions, and counterfactuals in games."
  - [section]: "This framework – (structural) causal games – can be seen as extending Pearl's causal hierarchy to the game-theoretic domain."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Mechanised MAID: Extends MAID with mechanism variables (Π, Θ) and edges between them
  - R-relevance graph: Minimal subgraph showing relevant dependencies for rationality relation R
  - Causal game: MAID extended to support interventions (level 2 of causal hierarchy)
  - Structural causal game: Causal game extended to support counterfactuals (level 3 of causal hierarchy)
  - Query engine: Computes predictions, interventions, and counterfactuals using the appropriate causal model

- Critical path:
  1. Build mechanised MAID from game specification
  2. Compute R-relevance graph using graphical criteria
  3. Select appropriate causal model (BN, CG, or SCG) based on query type
  4. Answer query using causal inference techniques adapted to game-theoretic setting

- Design tradeoffs:
  - DAG-based vs tree-based representation: DAGs are more compact and reveal conditional independencies but may be less natural for context-specific independencies
  - Mechanised vs standard MAID: Mechanised MAIDs explicitly represent dependencies between decision rules but add complexity
  - Causal vs non-causal models: Causal models support richer queries but require stronger assumptions about causal structure

- Failure signatures:
  - Incorrect R-relevance graph: May lead to wrong independence assumptions and inefficient computation
  - Cyclic dependencies without proper handling: May cause non-existence of solutions or incorrect query answers
  - Violation of causal assumptions: May lead to meaningless or incorrect causal queries in strategic settings

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement mechanised MAID for a simple game (e.g., Battle of the Sexes) and verify that the R-relevance graph correctly identifies relevant dependencies
  2. Compute Nash equilibria in a mechanised MAID and compare with standard MAID and EFG representations
  3. Answer a causal query (e.g., intervention on a decision rule) in a simple causal game and verify the result using manual calculation

## Limitations

- Scalability concerns for large, complex games with many agents and variables
- Potential breakdown of assumptions in settings with complex feedback loops or circular dependencies
- Lack of analysis on model misspecification and sensitivity to incorrect assumptions

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The basic formalism of mechanised MAIDs and their relationship to standard MAIDs is well-established and mathematically sound. The extension of Pearl's causal hierarchy to games follows logically from existing causal inference principles.
- **Medium confidence**: The computational advantages claimed for exploiting conditional independencies depend on the availability of sound and complete graphical criteria for R-reachability. While plausible, this requires empirical validation across diverse game types.
- **Medium confidence**: The integration of causal reasoning with game-theoretic concepts like Nash equilibrium and rationality relations appears theoretically sound, but practical implementations may face challenges in handling complex strategic interactions.
