# Pre-Pruning and Gradient-Dropping Improve Differentially Private Image Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11754
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11754
- Reference count: 10
- Pre-pruning and gradient-dropping improve DP-SGD accuracy-privacy trade-offs for image classification

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of scaling differential privacy to large neural networks, where DP-SGD struggles with the high dimensionality of gradients. The authors propose DP-SSGD, a training paradigm that combines pre-pruning (removing less important parameters before training) and gradient-dropping (selectively updating gradients during training) to reduce the effective parameter space. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet demonstrate that this approach improves accuracy compared to using either technique alone, particularly for pre-trained models.

## Method Summary
DP-SSGD combines two techniques: pre-pruning removes less important parameters before training using methods like random pruning, Synflow, or DP-SNIP; gradient-dropping selectively updates only a subset of gradients during each iteration. The approach leverages neural network overparameterization to reduce the effective dimensionality of gradients, improving the signal-to-noise ratio in DP-SGD. The combined method is tested on image classification tasks with WideResNet and ResNet architectures, showing improved accuracy-privacy trade-offs compared to baseline DP-SGD.

## Key Results
- DP-SSGD with ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10 (epsilon=1) achieves 6.78% accuracy vs 6.09% for baseline DP-SGD
- Combined pre-pruning and gradient-dropping on WideResNet-28-10 achieves 94.84% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (epsilon=1) vs 94.65% for baseline
- Synflow pre-pruning outperforms random and DP-SNIP at higher pruning rates; random gradient-dropping slightly better than magnitude-based

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Reducing gradient space through pre-pruning and gradient-dropping improves signal-to-noise ratio in DP-SGD. By removing parameters or selectively updating gradients, the effective dimensionality of gradients is reduced, meaning less noise needs to be added for the same privacy level. Core assumption: neural networks are over-parameterized. Evidence: [abstract] "neural networks are overparameterized, which allows us to improve neural network training with differential privacy." Break condition: too high pruning/dropping rates reduce model capacity.

### Mechanism 2
Combined approach creates privacy-accuracy trade-off where smaller models require less privacy budget. Pre-pruning reduces model size before training, requiring fewer parameters to be privatized during DP-SGD. Gradient-dropping further reduces effective parameter space during training. Core assumption: privacy loss accumulates with each epoch, so fewer parameters updated means lower total privacy loss. Evidence: [abstract] "training a smaller model requires less privacy budget for achieving good accuracy." Break condition: very low privacy budget where further model size reduction doesn't improve accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
Effectiveness varies by specific technique used. Different pre-pruning methods (random, Synflow, DP-SNIP) and gradient-dropping methods (random, magnitude-based) impact final accuracy differently. Core assumption: choice of method significantly impacts performance. Evidence: [section] "While random and Snip-DP-based pre-pruning performs better for smaller pruning rates, Synflow outperforms other benchmarks when the rates are higher." Break condition: wrong method choice for specific task leads to accuracy decrease.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal trade-off between pre-pruning and gradient-dropping rates for maximizing accuracy under different privacy budgets (ε values)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "a form trade-off between pre-pruning and gradient-dropping" and shows in Figure 3 that combining these techniques creates a diagonal pattern where certain combinations perform better than others
- Why unresolved: The paper shows the general pattern but doesn't provide a mathematical framework or precise guidelines for determining optimal rates based on specific ε values or dataset characteristics
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study mapping pre-pruning/gradient-dropping combinations to accuracy-privacy trade-offs across multiple datasets and model architectures, ideally with a closed-form expression or decision rule

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of DP-SSGD scale when applied to larger datasets beyond CIFAR and ImageNet, such as JFT-300M or WebVision?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, but acknowledges that "achieving high accuracies for large neural network trained in a differentially private manner remains difficult"
- Why unresolved: The experiments were limited to relatively small-scale datasets, leaving questions about scalability to truly large-scale vision datasets with billions of images
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct application of DP-SSGD to larger-scale datasets with comprehensive benchmarking against current state-of-the-art DP training methods on these datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the pruning rate, gradient-dropping rate, and the resulting privacy guarantee (ε, δ)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions "our training paradigm introduces a tension between the rates of pre-pruning and gradient-dropping, privacy loss, and classification accuracy" but doesn't provide a theoretical framework connecting these elements
- Why unresolved: The paper empirically demonstrates trade-offs but lacks a formal analysis of how these parameters affect the privacy budget calculation
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis deriving how pre-pruning and gradient-dropping rates affect the composition of privacy guarantees in DP-SGD, potentially leading to more efficient privacy accounting

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does DP-SSGD perform when applied to non-image domains such as natural language processing or graph neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper is specifically focused on "image classification" and experiments are conducted on image datasets with CNN architectures
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore whether the pre-pruning and gradient-dropping techniques transfer effectively to other domains with different data structures and model architectures
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical evaluation of DP-SSGD on language models, transformer architectures, or graph neural networks, comparing performance against domain-specific DP training methods

## Limitations
- Experimental validation limited to CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet datasets with specific architectures
- Hyperparameter tuning for optimal pruning and gradient-dropping rates not extensively explored
- Limited ablation studies on comparative analysis between different pre-pruning and gradient-dropping methods

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of reducing gradient space through pre-pruning and gradient-dropping is theoretically sound and aligns with established DP-SGD principles
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific performance improvements reported are based on limited experiments and may not generalize across all settings
- **Low Confidence**: The scalability claims to ImageNet and effectiveness for pre-trained models are based on limited experimental evidence
