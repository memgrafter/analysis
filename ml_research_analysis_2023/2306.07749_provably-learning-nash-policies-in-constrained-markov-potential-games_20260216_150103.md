# Provably Learning Nash Policies in Constrained Markov Potential Games

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07749
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07749
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces Constrained Markov Potential Games (CMPGs) and proposes CA-CMPG, an algorithm that provably converges to ε-Nash policies with sample complexity bounds.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of learning safe multi-agent policies in Constrained Markov Potential Games (CMPGs). The authors show that while Nash policies in CMPGs can theoretically be found via constrained optimization, strong duality does not hold, making Lagrangian approaches inapplicable. They propose CA-CMPG, a coordinate ascent algorithm that provably converges to ε-Nash policies in tabular, finite-horizon CMPGs. Additionally, they provide the first sample complexity bounds for learning Nash policies in unknown CMPGs, ensuring safe exploration under certain assumptions.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces Constrained Markov Potential Games (CMPGs) and proposes CA-CMPG, a coordinate ascent algorithm for finding Nash policies. CA-CMPG works by having agents take turns solving CMDPs while maintaining feasibility, with convergence guaranteed by showing the potential function increases by at least ε/2 per iteration. For unknown transitions, CA-CMPG-E extends this approach with sample complexity bounds that depend on the Slater constant and availability of generative models. The method requires access to a feasible initial policy and sample-efficient CMDP solvers.

## Key Results
- Strong duality does not hold in CMPGs, making Lagrangian approaches inapplicable and potentially unsafe
- CA-CMPG converges to ε-Nash policies in tabular, finite-horizon CMPGs
- Sample complexity bounds provided for learning Nash policies in unknown CMPGs, with O(H^8/ε^3ζ^2) using generative models and O(H^10/ε^5c^2) without

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Nash policies in CMPGs can be found via constrained optimization, but strong duality does not hold, making Lagrangian approaches inapplicable.
- Mechanism: The paper shows that while a Nash policy can theoretically be found by maximizing the potential function subject to constraints, using Lagrangian duality to convert this to an unconstrained MPG fails because strong duality does not hold in CMPGs. This is demonstrated via a concrete counterexample where the dual solution violates constraints.
- Core assumption: The CMPG has a well-defined potential function and coupled constraints that make the constrained optimization intractable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "One tempting approach is to solve it by Lagrangian-based primal-dual methods. As we show, in contrast to the single-agent setting, however, CMPGs do not satisfy strong duality, rendering such approaches inapplicable and potentially unsafe."
  - [section]: "Unfortunately, in the following proposition, however, we show that strong duality, i.e., D∗ = P ∗, does not hold in general for CMPGs."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus contains related work on safe MARL and Nash equilibria but does not directly address the strong duality failure in CMPGs.
- Break condition: If the CMPG happens to satisfy strong duality (which the paper shows is not generally true), then Lagrangian methods might work, but this would be a special case rather than the general rule.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: CA-CMPG algorithm converges to an ε-Nash policy by having agents take turns solving CMDPs while maintaining feasibility.
- Mechanism: Agents coordinate by keeping their policies fixed while one agent solves a CMDP to maximize its value function. This process is repeated, and the potential function increases by at least ε/2 per iteration until convergence to an ε-Nash policy. Feasibility is maintained by requiring the CMDP solver to return feasible policies.
- Core assumption: Agents have access to a feasible initial policy and can solve CMDPs with ε/2 accuracy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "To solve the CMPG problem, we propose our algorithm Coordinate-Ascent for CMPGs (CA-CMPG), which provably converges to a Nash policy in tabular, finite-horizon CMPGs."
  - [section]: "In CA-CMPG, the agents start with the feasible policy πS. In every iteration, the agents take turns to maximize their own value function. While one agent is maximizing its value function, the other agents keep their policy fixed."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus contains related work on MARL and CMDPs but does not directly validate the specific CA-CMPG convergence mechanism.
- Break condition: If the CMDP solver cannot guarantee feasibility or ε/2 accuracy, the algorithm may get stuck in an infeasible policy that is not a Nash policy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sample complexity bounds for learning Nash policies in unknown CMPGs depend on the Slater constant and whether a generative model is available.
- Mechanism: The paper provides sample complexity bounds for CA-CMPG-E, showing that with a generative model, the bound is O(H^8/ε^3ζ^2), while without a generative model but with safe exploration, it is O(H^10/ε^5c^2), where ζ is the Slater constant and c relates to the constraint set.
- Core assumption: The CMPG is strictly feasible (ζ > 0) and the agents have access to sample-efficient CMDP solvers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Furthermore, we provide the first sample complexity bounds for learning Nash policies in unknown CMPGs, and, which under additional assumptions, guarantee safe exploration."
  - [section]: "To apply this CMDP solver in CA-CMPG-E, we need to ensure that in every iteration, the agents have access to a strictly feasible policy. We state a stronger condition in the following assumption."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus contains related work on sample complexity in RL but does not directly validate the specific CMPG sample complexity bounds.
- Break condition: If the Slater constant ζ is very small or the constraint set c is very large, the sample complexity bounds become worse, potentially making learning impractical.

## Limitations

- The theoretical guarantees rely heavily on the assumption of a strictly feasible initial policy, which may not hold in practice for complex CMPGs
- Sample complexity bounds depend critically on the Slater constant ζ, which could be very small for tightly constrained problems, making bounds pessimistic
- The convergence analysis assumes perfect CMDP solvers with ε/2 accuracy, but real-world solvers may introduce additional approximation errors

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The core theoretical results showing that strong duality fails in CMPGs and the convergence proof of CA-CMPG to ε-Nash policies
- Medium Confidence: The sample complexity bounds for CA-CMPG-E, as they rely on several intermediate results about CMDP sample complexity
- Low Confidence: The practical performance and scalability of the algorithm to non-tabular settings or continuous state spaces
