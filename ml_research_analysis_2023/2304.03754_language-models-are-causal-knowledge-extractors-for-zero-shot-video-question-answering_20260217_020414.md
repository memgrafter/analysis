# Language Models are Causal Knowledge Extractors for Zero-shot Video Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03754
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03754
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel framework CaKE-LM achieves 4-6% improvement in zero-shot causal video question answering by extracting causal knowledge from language models

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses causal video question answering (CVidQA), which requires understanding cause-and-effect relationships in videos rather than just temporal or associative reasoning. Existing methods struggle because they rely on question generation systems pre-trained on reading comprehension datasets that only learn to ask association questions. The authors propose CaKE-LM, a framework that leverages language models' implicit causal commonsense knowledge acquired during pre-training to generate causal questions for CVidQA training. By prompting LMs with video descriptions to retrieve intentions and creating question-answer pairs, CaKE-LM significantly outperforms conventional methods on NExT-QA and Causal-VidQA datasets.

## Method Summary
CaKE-LM extracts causal knowledge from language models through a prompting-based approach. The framework first decomposes CVidQA questions into event pairs (event X triggers event Y), then prompts LMs with video descriptions to retrieve the causal intentions. These responses are transformed into questions with grammar correction and distractor sampling via clustering to create multiple-choice options. The method supports both zero-shot prompting for larger LMs and few-shot prompting for smaller models. Additionally, the framework investigates knowledge transferability through distillation, training smaller LMs on question-answer pairs generated by larger LMs. The generated data is then used to train video QA models for causal reasoning tasks.

## Key Results
- CaKE-LM outperforms conventional methods by 4-6% in zero-shot CVidQA accuracy on NExT-QA and Causal-VidQA datasets
- Zero-shot prompting works well with GPT-3 while smaller LMs benefit from few-shot examples
- Knowledge distillation enables smaller LMs to effectively learn causal knowledge from larger LMs
- Generated questions show improved causal reasoning compared to association-based questions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Language models capture causal commonsense knowledge through causal language modeling (CLM) objective during pre-training
- Mechanism: By predicting the next word based on previous context, LMs implicitly learn cause-and-effect relationships between events, which can be extracted through prompting
- Core assumption: The CLM objective naturally leads to acquisition of causal knowledge without explicit supervision
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Language models are witnessed to capture general knowledge from immense data and can be utilized in understanding chat-bot user intentions with dialogues"
  - [section 3.1] "Intuitively, LMs acquire causal commonsense knowledge through training on causal language modeling (CLM) objective, as understanding the cause-and-effect relationships improves next word prediction"
- Break condition: If the LM was not pre-trained on sufficient diverse text data containing causal relationships, or if the CLM objective was modified to ignore causality

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Decomposing CVidQA questions into event pairs enables effective knowledge extraction from LMs
- Mechanism: CVidQA questions naturally consist of two events with causal relations (event X triggers event Y), which can be mapped to question-answer pairs by prompting LMs with the first event to retrieve the second event's intention
- Core assumption: Most CVidQA questions follow this two-event causal structure that can be explicitly decomposed
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "CaKE-LM generates causal questions containing two events with one triggering another (e.g., 'score a goal' triggers 'soccer player kicking ball')"
  - [section 3.2] "Noticing that a CVidQA question typically consists of two causal-associated events Ex and Ey, where Ex is triggered by Ey"
- Break condition: If CVidQA questions don't follow the two-event causal structure or contain complex multi-step causal chains that can't be captured in simple prompt-response pairs

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Causal knowledge is transferable between LMs through distillation with LM-generated responses
- Mechanism: A smaller LM can be trained on question-answer pairs generated by a larger LM, effectively transferring causal knowledge without requiring the smaller LM to have been pre-trained on diverse causal text
- Core assumption: Causal knowledge is sufficiently generalizable that training data from one LM can effectively teach another LM
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also investigate the knowledge transferability between different LMs by Distillation with LM Answer"
  - [section 3.4] "we inspect a straightforward way to distill LM knowledge: Training with LM-generated responses"
- Break condition: If the smaller LM lacks sufficient capacity to represent the causal knowledge, or if the causal knowledge is too idiosyncratic to the source LM

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Causal reasoning and temporal reasoning distinction
  - Why needed here: CVidQA specifically requires understanding causal relations (why X happened) versus temporal relations (what happened before/after), which conventional VidQA methods don't address
  - Quick check question: What's the difference between "What happened after the car stopped?" and "Why did the car stop?" in terms of required reasoning?

- Concept: Zero-shot vs few-shot prompting strategies
  - Why needed here: The paper demonstrates different prompting approaches work better for different LMs, with GPT-3 working well zero-shot while smaller LMs need few-shot examples
  - Quick check question: When would you use zero-shot prompting versus few-shot prompting with an LM?

- Concept: Multi-choice question generation with distractors
  - Why needed here: The method generates distractors by clustering LM responses and sampling from relevant pools to create challenging multiple-choice questions for CVidQA training
  - Quick check question: How do you ensure distractors are plausible but incorrect answers in multiple-choice question generation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Knowledge Source (Pre-trained LMs) -> Knowledge Extraction (Prompting System) -> Question Generation (Caption Transformation, Grammar Correction) -> Distillation (LM-generated Training Data) -> CVidQA Training (Video QA Models)

- Critical path:
  1. Video caption → LM prompt → LM response (causal intention)
  2. Caption + response → question transformation → grammar correction
  3. Response → distractor sampling via clustering → multiple-choice options
  4. Generated QA pairs → CVidQA model training → evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using LM-generated data avoids expensive human annotation but may introduce noise from irrelevant or non-causal responses
  - Zero-shot prompting is more scalable but less reliable than few-shot prompting
  - Distillation enables knowledge transfer but may lose some nuance from the larger LM

- Failure signatures:
  - Context Irrelevant Error: LM generates completely unrelated responses to prompts
  - Non-causal Correlation Error: LM generates relevant but non-causal responses
  - Visual Mismatch Error: Generated answers are reasonable but don't align with video content

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate QAs using GPT-3 zero-shot prompting on NExT-QA captions and evaluate CVidQA performance
  2. Compare zero-shot vs few-shot prompting effectiveness across different LM sizes (GPT-2, GPT-Neo variants)
  3. Test knowledge distillation by training T5-large on GPT-3-generated responses and evaluating on CVidQA tasks

## Limitations

- Data Quality and Domain Transfer Issues: The method relies on LMs trained on general text data rather than video-specific or causal-domain data, raising uncertainty about whether textual causal knowledge transfers effectively to visual understanding
- Evaluation Methodology Concerns: The paper evaluates on NExT-QA and Causal-VidQA datasets without addressing potential biases or whether generated questions adequately represent the full spectrum of CVidQA challenges
- Generalizability to Complex Causal Scenarios: The framework appears optimized for simple two-event causal chains but may not handle multi-step causal chains or complex counterfactual scenarios

## Confidence

- LM-Captured Causal Knowledge: Medium confidence - supported by CLM objective theory but not directly validated
- Zero-shot Performance: Medium confidence - demonstrated on specific datasets but may not generalize
- Knowledge Distillation Effectiveness: Medium confidence - shown to work but with performance gaps versus larger LMs
- Distractor Generation Quality: Low confidence - clustering-based method may produce implausible distractors

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset Validation**: Evaluate the generated questions and CVidQA performance on additional video QA datasets (e.g., TGIF-QA, MSRVTT-QA) that weren't used in the original training or evaluation to test generalizability across different video domains and question types

2. **Human Evaluation of Generated Content**: Conduct human studies to assess the quality of generated questions, correctness of causal relationships, and plausibility of distractors to validate whether LM-generated content actually captures meaningful causal knowledge

3. **Error Analysis on Failure Cases**: Systematically analyze cases where the method fails, categorizing errors into types (visual mismatch, non-causal correlation, context irrelevance) and examining whether failures stem from LM limitations, prompt design issues, or fundamental mismatches between textual causal knowledge and visual reasoning requirements
