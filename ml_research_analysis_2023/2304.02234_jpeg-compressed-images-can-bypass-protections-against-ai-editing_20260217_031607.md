# JPEG Compressed Images Can Bypass Protections Against AI Editing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.02234
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02234
- **Reference count**: 4
- **Primary result**: JPEG compression can effectively bypass photoguard's imperceptible perturbations, allowing adversaries to edit protected images using diffusion models.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates a significant vulnerability in photoguard's approach to protecting images from AI-powered editing. The authors show that JPEG compression can remove the imperceptible perturbations added by photoguard, enabling adversaries to successfully edit protected images using diffusion models while maintaining the original subject and incorporating visual features from text prompts. Through systematic experiments with various image transformations, the researchers establish that JPEG compression is particularly effective at bypassing photoguard protection, while other transformations like Gaussian blur and rotations are less successful. This finding raises concerns about the robustness of perturbation-based protection methods in real-world scenarios where JPEG compression is commonly used.

## Method Summary
The researchers applied photoguard protection to images using both encoder and diffusion attack methods, then systematically tested various image transformations including JPEG compression at multiple quality levels (100%, 95%, 85, 75%, 65%), Gaussian blur, median blur, rotations, and flips. They used Stable Diffusion Model v1.5 to attempt editing the transformed images according to specific text prompts and evaluated whether the original subjects were maintained while incorporating visual features from the prompts. The effectiveness of each transformation in bypassing photoguard protection was assessed by comparing the edited images to the originals.

## Key Results
- JPEG compression effectively removes photoguard perturbations, allowing successful image editing while maintaining original subjects
- Lower JPEG quality levels (e.g., 65%) are more effective at bypassing protection than higher quality levels
- JPEG compression outperforms other tested transformations like Gaussian blur, median blur, rotations, and flips
- The diffusion model can generate realistic edited images from JPEG-compressed, photoguard-protected images

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: JPEG compression can remove the adversarial perturbations added by photoguard.
- Mechanism: JPEG compression acts as a lossy transformation that removes high-frequency noise while preserving semantic content.
- Core assumption: The photoguard perturbations are primarily high-frequency components that JPEG can filter out.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "imperceptible perturbations are not robust to JPEG compression"
  - [section] "JPEG compression allows an adversary to modify a protected image found online"
- Break condition: If photoguard optimizations are modified to include JPEG resistance, or if the perturbations are designed to survive JPEG compression.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Diffusion models rely on image encoders that are sensitive to perturbations but robust to JPEG compression.
- Mechanism: The encoder in the diffusion model processes the image and when JPEG-compressed photoguard images are fed in, the encoder can still extract meaningful features, allowing realistic image generation.
- Core assumption: The image encoder is more robust to JPEG compression than to the photoguard perturbations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the aforementioned perturbations are not robust to JPEG compression"
  - [section] "JPEG compression allows an adversary to edit the photoguard image while maintaining the original subject"
- Break condition: If the encoder is retrained or modified to be less sensitive to the photoguard perturbations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: JPEG compression is a widely used and often incidental transformation that adversaries can exploit.
- Mechanism: JPEG is commonly used for image storage and sharing, making it an accessible and effective tool for adversaries to bypass protections without specialized knowledge.
- Core assumption: JPEG compression is prevalent enough that its use can be considered a baseline attack vector.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "poses a major weakness because of the common usage and availability of JPEG"
  - [section] "widespread use of JPEG transformations (w3t), which may occur inadvertently"
- Break condition: If alternative image formats become more prevalent or if images are stored in formats that don't use JPEG compression.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Original image -> photoguard perturbations -> JPEG compression -> diffusion model (encoder + denoising network) -> edited image
- Critical path: 1) Original image is protected with photoguard perturbations 2) JPEG compression is applied to the protected image 3) Adversary uses diffusion model to edit the JPEG-compressed image 4) Diffusion model generates realistic edited image
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Perturbation strength vs. imperceptibility: Stronger perturbations may be more effective but less imperceptible
  - JPEG quality settings: Lower quality removes more perturbations but also degrades image quality more
  - Encoder robustness: Making the encoder more robust to perturbations may make it less effective at image editing
- Failure signatures:
  - If JPEG compression doesn't bypass photoguard: The perturbations may be robust to JPEG or not primarily high-frequency
  - If edited images don't maintain the original subject: The JPEG compression may not have removed enough of the perturbations
  - If other transforms (Gaussian blur, rotations) work: The photoguard perturbations may not be as robust as claimed
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply photoguard to an image and then apply JPEG compression at varying qualities; attempt to edit the image with a diffusion model
  2. Apply photoguard to an image and then apply Gaussian blur or other transforms; attempt to edit the image with a diffusion model
  3. Modify the photoguard algorithm to include JPEG resistance and test if JPEG compression still bypasses the protection
