# Deep neural networks architectures from the perspective of manifold learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03406
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03406
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: DNN architectures transform data manifolds in distinct ways observable through persistent homology and PH dim, with topological descriptors at final layer correlating with generalization ability.

## Executive Summary
This study systematically investigates deep neural network architectures through the lens of manifold learning and topological data analysis. The authors analyze how different architectures (CNNs, Vision Transformers, LLMs) transform data topology during training using persistent homology and persistent homological fractal dimension. They demonstrate that CNNs progressively simplify topological descriptors while Vision Transformers maintain more stable topology, and that these topological changes correlate with model performance and generalization ability.

## Method Summary
The authors train multiple DNN architectures (ResNet, SE-ResNet, MobileNetV2, VGG, Vision Transformers, ConvMixer, BERT, RoBERTa) on image and text classification tasks. For each model, they extract intermediate representations at every layer and compute topological descriptors using persistent homology (E⁰, E¹) and persistent homological fractal dimension (PH dim). They track these metrics across training epochs and correlate final-layer descriptors with test accuracy. The methodology involves creating simplicial complexes from embedding manifolds, computing persistent diagrams, and estimating fractal dimension through power-law scaling analysis.

## Key Results
- CNNs progressively simplify topological descriptors across depth during training, while Vision Transformers exhibit more stable topology
- PH dim typically decreases through CNN layers, indicating learning of low-dimensional representations
- Different activation functions affect topological simplification patterns
- Topological descriptors at the final layer show statistically significant inverse correlation (r = -0.9) with test accuracy error, suggesting predictive power for generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Different DNN architectures transform data manifolds in fundamentally distinct ways, observable through topological descriptors and PH dim. Convolutional architectures progressively simplify topological descriptors across depth, while Vision Transformers maintain more stable topology due to architectural differences in information processing and aggregation.

### Mechanism 2
The performance and generalization ability of deep models can be predicted from topology and geometry of internal representation at final layer. Models with simpler topological descriptors (lower Eᵦ) and lower PH dim at output layer generalize better because they represent more compact, disentangled representations.

### Mechanism 3
The learning process of DNNs is associated with gradual simplification of topological descriptors and decrease of fractal dimension in internal representation. As training progresses, models learn to transform complex data manifolds into simpler representations, reducing cycles and connectivity while lowering PH dim.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data input → Layer-wise transformations → Topological descriptor computation (Eᵦ, PH dim) → Performance correlation analysis
- **Critical path**: 1) Preprocess data and create batches, 2) Pass batches through each layer of network, 3) Compute topological descriptors at each layer, 4) Track changes across training epochs, 5) Correlate final-layer descriptors with test performance
- **Design tradeoffs**: Computational cost vs. topological resolution (higher-dimensional homology is more informative but computationally expensive); batch size vs. statistical significance (larger batches provide more stable estimates but require more memory); depth of analysis vs. architectural coverage (more layers analyzed provides better understanding but increases complexity)
- **Failure signatures**: Topological descriptors that don't change across layers may indicate architectural issues or insufficient training; unexpected increases in PH dim during training may signal memorization rather than generalization; lack of correlation between topological descriptors and performance may indicate need for different metrics
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Compare topological descriptor evolution across ResNet, ViT, and BERT on CIFAR-10 to validate architecture-dependent patterns, 2) Test correlation between final-layer Eᵦ and test accuracy across different activation functions on same architecture, 3) Evaluate PH dim as generalization predictor by training multiple models with different hyperparameters and measuring correlation with test performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does the observed difference in topological simplification between CNNs and Vision Transformers reflect fundamental architectural differences in how these models process information, or could it be explained by training procedures and dataset characteristics? The paper shows CNNs exhibit topological simplification during training while Vision Transformers show more stable topology, but doesn't definitively establish whether this is inherent to architectures or influenced by training dynamics and data properties.

### Open Question 2
Can persistent homology-based topological descriptors reliably predict generalization ability across diverse architectures and tasks without requiring a separate test set? The authors propose using topological descriptors at final layer to estimate generalization ability and show promising correlations, but acknowledge this needs further validation across diverse architectures, tasks, and domains.

### Open Question 3
What is the theoretical explanation for why fractal dimension (PH dim) increases in intermediate layers of CNNs and Vision Transformers during training, and what does this reveal about the learning process? The authors observe increase in PH dim in intermediate layers during training forming a "hump" shape, but note this requires theoretical understanding connecting observed behavior to principles of information propagation, feature abstraction, or optimization dynamics.

## Limitations
- Computational constraints limit analysis to 0D and 1D homology, potentially missing important higher-dimensional topological features
- Correlation between topological descriptors and generalization was observed across limited set of architectures and datasets
- Analysis of large language models was less comprehensive than for vision models, with only two transformer architectures examined

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: CNNs progressively simplify topological descriptors during training (consistent across multiple architectures and datasets); PH dim decreases through CNN layers and correlates with performance

**Medium Confidence**: Architectural differences between CNNs and Vision Transformers are compelling but require further validation with more diverse architectures

**Low Confidence**: Claim that topological descriptors at final layer can reliably predict generalization without separate test set is promising but preliminary
