# $\rm A^2Q$: Aggregation-Aware Quantization for Graph Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00193
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00193
- Authors: Multiple
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Up to 18.6x compression ratio with <1% accuracy loss on GNNs

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of reducing latency and memory consumption during inference of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) on large-scale graphs. The proposed Aggregation-Aware Quantization (A2Q) method learns node-specific quantization parameters, including bitwidth and step size, based on the topology of the graph. To mitigate the vanishing gradient problem in semi-supervised tasks, a Local Gradient method is introduced, which uses quantization error as supervised information. Additionally, a Nearest Neighbor Strategy is developed to generalize the approach to unseen graphs. Extensive experiments on eight public datasets demonstrate that A2Q achieves up to 18.6x compression ratio with negligible accuracy degradation compared to FP32 models.

## Method Summary
A2Q introduces a novel quantization framework specifically designed for GNNs that accounts for graph topology. The method learns quantization parameters (bitwidth and step size) that are node-specific, adapting to the local graph structure. To address the vanishing gradient problem in semi-supervised settings, the authors propose a Local Gradient method that uses quantization error as a supervisory signal. The approach also includes a Nearest Neighbor Strategy to enable generalization to unseen graphs without retraining. The quantization process is optimized through a learnable quantization step size and a discrete quantization step size that are trained simultaneously.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 18.6x compression ratio compared to FP32 models
- Improves accuracy by up to 11.4% and 9.5% over state-of-the-art quantization methods on node-level and graph-level tasks respectively
- Achieves up to 2x speedup on dedicated hardware implementations
- Maintains negligible accuracy degradation (<1%) compared to full-precision models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core innovation lies in making quantization aware of graph aggregation patterns. By learning node-specific quantization parameters based on local topology, A2Q preserves the structural information that is crucial for GNN performance. The Local Gradient method addresses the vanishing gradient problem by using quantization error as a supervisory signal, ensuring that the quantization process doesn't degrade the model's ability to learn from limited labeled data. The Nearest Neighbor Strategy enables the model to adapt to new graphs by leveraging learned quantization patterns from similar structures.
