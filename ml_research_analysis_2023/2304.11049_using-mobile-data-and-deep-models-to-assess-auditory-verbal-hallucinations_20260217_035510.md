# Using Mobile Data and Deep Models to Assess Auditory Verbal Hallucinations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11049
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11049
- Reference count: 40
- Top-1 F1 score: 54% in predicting AVH valence across four categories

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel method for assessing the valence of auditory verbal hallucinations (AVH) using mobile sensing data and deep learning models. The researchers collected multimodal data including self-reports, audio diaries, and passive mobile sensing from 435 participants experiencing hearing voices. By leveraging transfer learning with VGGish and BERT, and introducing a new approach to transform mobile sensing time series into spectrograms, they developed a hybrid neural network model that achieves moderate predictive performance. The study demonstrates the potential of mobile data and deep learning for assessing AVH, which could support timely interventions for individuals with mental illnesses.

## Method Summary
The authors collected multimodal data from 435 participants experiencing auditory verbal hallucinations, including ecological momentary assessments (EMAs), audio diaries, and passive mobile sensing data (location, phone usage, microphone). They employed transfer learning with pre-trained VGGish and BERT models to extract features from audio and text data respectively. A novel approach was introduced to transform mobile sensing time series data into spectrograms, enabling feature extraction through convolutional neural networks. These multimodal features were then combined in a hybrid neural network architecture to predict the valence of hallucinated voices across four categories. The model achieved a top-1 F1 score of 54% and top-2 F1 score of 72% in classifying AVH valence.

## Key Results
- Top-1 F1 score of 54% in predicting AVH valence across four categories
- Top-2 F1 score of 72% when considering top two predictions
- Hybrid neural network combining mobile sensing, audio, and text features
- Demonstrated potential for mobile data and deep learning in AVH assessment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages the rich, multimodal nature of data available through mobile devices to capture various aspects of an individual's experience with auditory verbal hallucinations. By combining passive sensing data (location, phone usage, audio environment) with active self-reports and audio diaries, the model can potentially identify patterns that correlate with different valence states of hallucinated voices. The use of transfer learning with pre-trained models like VGGish and BERT allows for efficient feature extraction from audio and text data, while the innovative transformation of mobile sensing time series into spectrograms enables the application of convolutional neural networks to this data type. This multimodal approach captures both the contextual information from passive sensing and the subjective experiences reported by participants, providing a comprehensive view of AVH experiences that can inform valence prediction.

## Limitations
- Moderate predictive performance (54% top-1 F1 score) raises questions about clinical utility
- Lack of detailed information on class imbalance in valence categories
- Pre-trained models (VGGish and BERT) not specifically trained on AVH populations, potentially limiting feature relevance

## Confidence

**Medium Confidence:** The hybrid deep learning approach combining multimodal features is technically sound, but the moderate performance metrics suggest limitations in either data quality, model architecture, or feature relevance for this specific application.

**Medium Confidence:** The collection of multimodal data from 435 participants provides reasonable sample size, but the paper lacks details on participant demographics, clinical diagnoses, and longitudinal consistency of self-reports that would strengthen confidence in findings.

**Low Confidence:** The clinical applicability and practical utility of the model for real-world intervention support is not well-established, given the moderate predictive performance and lack of validation in clinical settings.
