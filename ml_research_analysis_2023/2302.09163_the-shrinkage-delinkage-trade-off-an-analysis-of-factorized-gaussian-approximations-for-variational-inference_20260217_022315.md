# The Shrinkage-Delinkage Trade-off: An Analysis of Factorized Gaussian Approximations for Variational Inference

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.09163
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09163
- **Reference count:** 34
- **Primary result:** FG-VI always underestimates both componentwise variance and entropy of multivariate Gaussian targets, with a trade-off between variance shrinkage and delinkage effects.

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the uncertainty deficit in factorized Gaussian variational inference (FG-VI) by comparing how it underestimates both componentwise variance and entropy of multivariate Gaussian targets. The authors prove that FG-VI systematically underestimates both uncertainty measures, though the degree of underestimation differs. They introduce a key theoretical insight: the shrinkage-delinkage trade-off, where entropy underestimation results from two competing effects - variance shrinkage reduces entropy while factorized approximation delinks correlated nodes, potentially increasing entropy. A particularly striking finding is that in high dimensions, per-component entropy gaps can vanish even when all componentwise variances are underestimated by a constant factor. The paper derives theoretical bounds on entropy gaps and average variance shrinkage in terms of dimensionality and the condition number of the correlation matrix, with empirical validation on both Gaussian and non-Gaussian targets.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a theoretical framework to analyze factorized Gaussian approximations by deriving bounds on entropy gaps and variance shrinkage. They prove that FG-VI always underestimates componentwise variance and entropy for multivariate Gaussian targets. The analysis decomposes the entropy underestimation into two components: variance shrinkage (which decreases entropy) and delinkage (which increases entropy by ignoring correlations). The theoretical results are validated through experiments on both Gaussian and non-Gaussian targets, comparing the predicted bounds with empirical observations.

## Key Results
- FG-VI always underestimates both componentwise variance and entropy of multivariate Gaussian targets, though not necessarily to the same degree
- The shrinkage-delinkage trade-off shows entropy is decreased by variance shrinkage but increased by delinkage of correlated nodes
- In high dimensions, per-component entropy gaps can vanish even when all componentwise variances are underestimated by a constant factor
- Theoretical bounds on entropy gap and average variance shrinkage are derived in terms of dimensionality and condition number of the correlation matrix

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The analysis reveals that factorized Gaussian approximations introduce systematic uncertainty underestimation through two mechanisms. First, the variance shrinkage effect occurs because the factorized approximation cannot capture the full covariance structure, leading to reduced variance estimates. Second, the delinkage effect occurs because the factorized approximation treats variables as independent, which can paradoxically increase entropy by removing negative correlations that would otherwise reduce uncertainty. The balance between these two effects determines the net entropy underestimation, and this balance shifts dramatically with dimensionality.

## Limitations
- Theoretical results rely heavily on Gaussianity assumption with non-Gaussian cases only empirically validated
- Derived bounds depend on condition number of correlation matrix, which may be intractable for real-world problems
- The paper does not fully characterize when shrinkage or delinkage dominates in practical scenarios

## Confidence
- **Theoretical bounds validity (Gaussian case): High** - Proofs are rigorous and mathematically sound
- **High-dimensional entropy vanishing claim: Medium** - Theoretically derived but limited empirical validation across diverse problem classes
- **Non-Gaussian empirical validation: Medium** - Supportive but without formal theoretical guarantees
- **Generalizability to real-world problems: Low** - Many practical assumptions may not hold
