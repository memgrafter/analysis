# Investigating Pre-trained Audio Encoders in the Low-Resource Condition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17733
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17733

## Executive Summary
This work investigates the performance of three state-of-the-art pre-trained audio encoders—Wav2vec2, WavLM, and Whisper—in low-resource scenarios across seven speech understanding and generation tasks. The study focuses on how pre-training protocols influence model performance and representational properties when fine-tuning with limited labeled data. Experiments are conducted with 1%, 5%, and 10% of training data for tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR), speaker diarisation (SD), intent classification (IC), slot filling (SF), keyword spotting (KS), speaker identification (SID), and speech translation (ST).

Key findings show that Whisper encoders outperform Wav2vec2 and WavLM on content-driven tasks (e.g., IC, SF, ASR, KS) in terms of both performance and convergence speed. For example, on the IC task with 1% data, Whisper BASE surpasses WavLM by 150% in F1 score with faster convergence. Whisper also excels on KS, achieving over 96% accuracy with far fewer updates than baselines. However, Whisper performs poorly on SID, likely due to its pre-training focus on content-to-text mapping rather than speaker-specific features. Analysis of representational properties reveals that Whisper mitigates the anisotropy problem more effectively than baselines, with isotropy scores approaching those of text embeddings. Visualization of layer-wise contributions indicates that Whisper distributes task-relevant information differently across layers, especially for SID, where intermediate layers carry more speaker information. Overall, the study demonstrates Whisper's superior effectiveness for content-based tasks in low-resource settings, with implications for optimizing encoder use in constrained data regimes.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three pre-trained audio encoders (Wav2vec2, WavLM, Whisper) on 7 speech understanding and generation tasks in low-resource settings. The method uses randomly sampled 1%, 5%, and 10% of training data from each task, with performance measured by WER, DER, accuracy, F1 score, and BLEU score. The SUPERB benchmark pipeline is employed with frozen encoders and lightweight task-specific heads, trained with identical hyperparameters across all experiments.

## Key Results
- Whisper outperforms Wav2vec2 and WavLM on content-driven tasks (IC, SF, ASR, KS) with faster convergence in low-resource settings
- Whisper BASE achieves 150% higher F1 score than WavLM on IC with 1% data and fewer updates
- Whisper mitigates the anisotropy problem more effectively, with isotropy scores approaching those of text embeddings
- Whisper performs poorly on SID due to pre-training focus on content-to-text mapping rather than speaker-specific features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Whisper's pre-training with audio-transcript pairs creates representations that are more isotropically distributed in semantic space than Wav2vec2 or WavLM.
- Mechanism: During pre-training, Whisper learns to map audio to text, which inherently requires disentangling content from irrelevant variation. This process produces embeddings with better spherical uniformity (isotropy) compared to self-supervised models trained without text supervision.
- Core assumption: Isotropy in the embedding space directly correlates with faster convergence and better performance in low-resource fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Analysis of representational properties reveals that Whisper mitigates the anisotropy problem more effectively than baselines, with isotropy scores approaching those of text embeddings."
  - [section] "The average isotropy scores of W2V2, WavLM, and Whisper... are 1e-300, 1e-14, and 1e-2, respectively."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence provided; this is measured experimentally.
- Break condition: If the downstream task does not benefit from isotropic embeddings (e.g., SID), the advantage disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Whisper's content-focused pre-training makes it excel on tasks requiring semantic understanding but underperform on speaker-specific tasks.
- Mechanism: By learning to map audio to transcripts during pre-training, Whisper optimizes its internal representations for capturing content and semantic features, while speaker-specific characteristics are not emphasized. This specialization transfers well to content-driven tasks (IC, SF, ASR, KS) but not to SID.
- Core assumption: The pre-training objective shapes the representational bias, making certain types of information more accessible during fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Whisper performs poorly on SID, likely due to its pre-training focus on content-to-text mapping rather than speaker-specific features."
  - [section] "Our hypothesis is that Whisper pre-training places emphasis on capturing content... rather than speaker information... W2V2 and WavLM, which are pre-trained only on speeches, are better positioned to tackle this task."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence; this is inferred from task performance patterns.
- Break condition: If fine-tuning data is abundant enough to override pre-training biases, or if task heads can extract speaker information from intermediate layers.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Whisper's representational information is distributed differently across layers compared to Wav2vec2 and WavLM, with content information concentrated in later layers.
- Mechanism: Visualization of layer-wise contributions shows that Whisper places more emphasis on later layers for content tasks, while speaker information resides more in intermediate layers. This distribution pattern aligns with its pre-training objective of content-to-text mapping.
- Core assumption: The layer-wise distribution of information reflects the pre-training objective and influences task-specific performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Visualization of layer-wise contributions indicates that Whisper distributes task-relevant information differently across layers, especially for SID, where intermediate layers carry more speaker information."
  - [section] "For Wav2vec2, WavLM and Whisper MEDIUM encoders on 4 tasks we observe different patterns of layer contribution layers."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence; this is observed through experimental visualization.
- Break condition: If the task requires information distributed across all layers rather than concentrated in specific regions.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Self-supervised learning for speech
  - Why needed here: All three encoders use different self-supervised pre-training approaches (Wav2vec2 uses contrastive loss, WavLM uses denoising masked modeling, Whisper uses audio-transcript mapping). Understanding these differences explains their performance gaps.
  - Quick check question: What is the fundamental difference between Wav2vec2's InfoNCE loss and Whisper's audio-to-text mapping objective?

- Concept: Representation isotropy and anisotropy
  - Why needed here: The paper explicitly measures isotropy scores and links them to performance. Understanding this geometric property is crucial for interpreting why Whisper outperforms other encoders.
  - Quick check question: How does an isotropic embedding space differ from an anisotropic one in terms of information distribution?

- Concept: Layer-wise information distribution in Transformers
  - Why needed here: The paper analyzes how different encoders distribute task-relevant information across layers, which affects both performance and fine-tuning strategies.
  - Quick check question: Why might speaker information be concentrated in intermediate layers while content information is in later layers for certain models?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Raw audio → Encoder feature extraction → Task-specific head → Prediction
- Critical path: Raw audio → Encoder feature extraction → Task-specific head → Prediction. The encoder quality and layer selection strategy are the critical differentiators.
- Design tradeoffs: Whisper trades speaker modeling capability for superior content representation, uses smaller parameter count for faster inference, and relies on pre-training data quality rather than task-specific fine-tuning.
- Failure signatures: Poor SID performance indicates content-over-speaker bias; slow convergence on ASR with Wav2vec2 indicates anisotropic representations; memory issues with Whisper MEDIUM during fine-tuning.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare isotropy scores of all three encoders on the same dataset to verify the quantitative advantage.
  2. Test weighted-sum vs. last-layer output strategies on SID to confirm speaker information location in intermediate layers.
  3. Fine-tune Whisper with increasing amounts of data on IC to measure the point where fine-tuning surpasses vanilla frozen performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why does Whisper perform poorly on speaker identification tasks compared to content-driven tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that Whisper performs poorly on SID and hypothesize this is because its pre-training emphasizes content-to-text mapping rather than speaker-specific features.
- Why unresolved: The analysis is based on performance observations rather than detailed investigation of what speaker information Whisper captures or fails to capture.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of speaker-specific features in Whisper's representations, ablation studies on speaker-related components, or experiments modifying Whisper's pre-training to emphasize speaker information.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the layer-wise information distributions differ between Whisper and baseline models for various task types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors visualize layer contribution weights and observe that SID places more emphasis on intermediate layers for Whisper compared to last layers for W2V2 and WavLM.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides qualitative observations but doesn't systematically quantify or explain why different tasks utilize different layers across models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Quantitative analysis of layer utilization patterns across all tasks, experiments testing the impact of using different layer combinations for each task type.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the anisotropy mitigation in Whisper compare to other pre-trained audio encoders and what causes this difference?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors report that Whisper's isotropy scores approach those of text embeddings (1e-2) while baselines have much lower scores, and attribute this to Whisper's pre-training protocol.
- Why unresolved: The paper measures isotropy but doesn't explain the specific mechanisms in Whisper's pre-training that lead to better isotropy or compare these properties systematically across models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of representation geometry across all models, investigation of how specific pre-training objectives affect anisotropy, or experiments modifying pre-training to improve isotropy.

## Limitations
- The study relies on a fixed evaluation protocol (SUPERB benchmark) that uses frozen encoders with task-specific heads, which may not represent all possible fine-tuning strategies
- The isotropy measurements lack direct causal evidence linking this property to performance improvements
- The layer-wise contribution analysis uses visualization rather than quantitative measurement, making it difficult to generalize findings

## Confidence
*High confidence:* The core finding that Whisper outperforms Wav2vec2 and WavLM on content-driven tasks in low-resource settings is well-supported by quantitative results across multiple metrics and tasks. The observation that Whisper converges faster with fewer updates is directly measurable and consistently observed.

*Medium confidence:* The explanation that Whisper's pre-training objective creates more isotropic representations is supported by measurements but lacks mechanistic validation. The claim about layer-wise information distribution being tied to pre-training objectives is based on visualization patterns rather than quantitative analysis.

*Low confidence:* The specific assertion that isotropy directly causes faster convergence and better performance requires more rigorous experimental validation. The hypothesis about Whisper's poor SID performance being solely due to pre-training bias is plausible but not definitively proven.

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies varying the amount of pre-training data for each encoder to determine whether the performance gap persists across different pre-training regimes.

2. Implement and test alternative fine-tuning strategies (partial unfreezing, layer-wise learning rates) to assess whether the observed advantages of Whisper persist beyond the frozen-encoder protocol.

3. Perform controlled experiments measuring the causal relationship between isotropy scores and convergence speed by artificially manipulating embedding distributions while keeping other factors constant.
