# How good are variational autoencoders at transfer learning?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10767
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10767
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: VAE encoders learn generic representations shared across domains, while decoders learn dataset-specific representations

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how well VAE representations transfer across different datasets and tasks using Centred Kernel Alignment (CKA) to measure representational similarity. The authors find that VAE encoders learn generic, dataset-independent features while decoders learn dataset-specific ones. This has important implications for transfer learning: for unsupervised tasks like image generation, the encoder can be frozen while the decoder needs retraining, whereas for supervised tasks, visual inspection of reconstructions can help identify shared latent variables between source and target domains. The study provides practical guidelines for selecting which VAE components to retrain based on the target task.

## Method Summary
The authors train multiple VAEs with different learning objectives (β-VAE, β-TC VAE, Annealed VAE, DIP-VAE II) and regularization strengths on source datasets. They compute CKA and Procrustes similarity scores between all pairs of layers across models trained on different datasets. The similarity patterns between encoder and decoder representations are analyzed to identify generic versus specific representations. The study uses dSprites, Cars3D, SmallNorb, Celeba, and Symsol datasets with 5,000 examples each for similarity measurements. Visual reconstruction experiments are used to validate findings and propose a method to assess shared latent variables for classification tasks.

## Key Results
- CKA reveals that VAE encoders learn generic representations shared across datasets, while decoders learn dataset-specific ones
- Encoder representations show high CKA similarity across different datasets and training regimes, but decoder representations show low similarity
- Visual inspection of reconstructions can identify shared latent variables between source and target domains without retraining

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** CKA can reliably measure representational similarity in VAEs because it aligns with known learning dynamics such as encoder-first learning and posterior collapse.
- **Mechanism:** By comparing centered kernel matrices, CKA captures geometric similarity in activation spaces; this matches empirical behaviors like encoders learning abstract features before decoders and decoder representations collapsing under high regularization.
- **Core assumption:** High-dimensional neural activations are sufficiently stable and centered for linear kernel alignment to be meaningful across different VAE architectures and training regimes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] shows the paper uses CKA to evaluate VAE representational similarity.
  - [section 2.2] explains CKA as normalized HSIC, measuring alignment between kernel matrices.
  - [section 4.1] demonstrates CKA results are consistent with encoder-first learning and posterior collapse facts.
- **Break condition:** If the number of samples is too small or representations are extremely high-dimensional, Procrustes may overestimate similarity while CKA underestimates it, leading to misleading comparisons.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Encoders learn generic, dataset-independent representations while decoders learn dataset-specific ones.
- **Mechanism:** During training, encoders extract abstract features (edges, curves) shared across domains; decoders specialize to reconstruct domain-specific details, making their activations more specific to the training data.
- **Core assumption:** The inductive bias of VAE architectures causes encoders to map inputs to a shared latent manifold regardless of domain, while decoders must invert that manifold back to domain-specific pixel space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] states "encoders' representations are generic but decoders' specific."
  - [section 4.2] shows high CKA similarity in encoder quadrants across datasets but low similarity in decoder quadrants.
  - [corpus] neighbor papers discuss VAE generalization and representation learning, supporting the idea of generic vs specific features.
- **Break condition:** If datasets share very little structural similarity, encoder genericness may degrade, leading to lower cross-dataset CKA scores.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Visual inspection of reconstructions can identify shared latent variables between source and target domains without retraining.
- **Mechanism:** Because decoders are dataset-specific, a VAE trained on a source domain will reconstruct target domain inputs using only the shared latent variables; comparing input and reconstruction reveals which variables are preserved.
- **Core assumption:** The VAE's decoder will faithfully map latent representations to realistic reconstructions in the target domain, so differences between input and reconstruction highlight domain-specific information not captured in the latent space.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] proposes a method to visually assess transferability for classification tasks.
  - [section 4.3] describes case studies where reconstruction comparisons reveal shared variables like color or position.
  - [corpus] papers on interpretable representations support using reconstructions to analyze latent variable semantics.
- **Break condition:** If the decoder's reconstruction quality is poor or the shared variables are subtle, visual inspection may fail to identify them accurately.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Centred Kernel Alignment (CKA)**
  - Why needed here: CKA is the primary metric used to compare representational similarity across different VAEs and datasets; understanding its formulation and limitations is critical to interpreting results.
  - Quick check question: How does CKA differ from simple Pearson correlation when comparing high-dimensional neural activations?

- **Concept: Posterior collapse in VAEs**
  - Why needed here: The paper uses posterior collapse as a test case to validate CKA; knowing what causes and indicates collapse helps understand the experimental design.
  - Quick check question: What happens to the sampled latent representation when regularization is too high in a VAE?

- **Concept: Transfer learning taxonomy**
  - Why needed here: The paper distinguishes unsupervised vs self-taught transfer learning; understanding these settings clarifies which VAE components to retrain.
  - Quick check question: In what scenario would you freeze the encoder and retrain only the decoder in a VAE transfer learning setup?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - Input -> Encoder (conv/fc layers) -> Latent space (10-dim Gaussian) -> Decoder (conv/fc layers) -> Reconstruction

- **Critical path:**
  1. Load and preprocess datasets (dSprites, Cars3D, SmallNorb, Celeba, Symsol).
  2. Train base VAEs with multiple objectives and regularization strengths.
  3. Save intermediate models for CKA analysis at different epochs.
  4. Compute CKA scores between all layer pairs across models.
  5. Analyze similarity heatmaps to infer generic vs specific representations.
  6. Validate findings via reconstruction experiments and classification tasks.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - High regularization encourages disentanglement but risks posterior collapse.
  - Convolutional vs fully connected architectures trade off spatial feature extraction for simplicity.
  - More epochs improve reconstruction but increase computational cost and risk overfitting.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - CKA scores near zero between encoder layers suggest poor feature extraction.
  - Very high CKA between decoder layers of different datasets may indicate overfitting to source domain.
  - Posterior collapse manifests as near-zero similarity between sampled latent and encoder/decoder layers.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train a β-VAE on dSprites with β=1 and β=8; compute CKA between their activations to confirm encoder similarity and decoder specificity.
  2. Transfer a VAE encoder from dSprites to Symsol without fine-tuning; compare CKA scores to a VAE trained directly on Symsol.
  3. Visually inspect reconstructions of Celeba images using a VAE trained on Cars3D; identify shared latent variables (e.g., color, orientation).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the transferability of VAE representations vary across different model architectures beyond the convolutional and fully-connected networks studied?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that "similarity metrics comparing the geometry of representations were overly sensitive to differences in neural architectures" and only study two architectures.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests convolutional and fully-connected architectures, leaving open whether their findings generalize to other architectures like transformers or graph neural networks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing CKA similarity scores across multiple architectures (CNN, transformer, GNN) on the same transfer learning tasks would show if the encoder/decoder specificity pattern holds.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different datasets on the representational similarity of VAE encoders and decoders, beyond the five datasets used in the study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state they used "increasingly challenging transfer learning configurations using the two pairs of datasets (dSprites, Symsol) and (Cars3D, Celeba)" and note that "the representational similarity of the decoder seems to vary depending on the dataset."
- Why unresolved: The study only uses five datasets, which may not represent the full diversity of possible data domains and complexities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing representational similarity across a wider range of datasets (e.g., medical images, natural language, tabular data) would show how universal the encoder/decoder patterns are.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different learning objectives and regularization strengths beyond those tested affect the transferability of VAE representations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors test four learning objectives (β-VAE, β-TC VAE, Annealed VAE, DIP-VAE II) with five regularization strengths each, but note that "the representational similarity of the decoder seems to vary depending on the dataset" and "CKA of the representations of fully-connected decoders may not be a good predictor of reconstruction quality."
- Why unresolved: The study tests a limited range of learning objectives and regularization strengths, and the relationship between these parameters and transferability is not fully characterized.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing a broader range of learning objectives (e.g., Wasserstein autoencoders, adversarial autoencoders) and regularization strategies (e.g., spectral regularization, dropout) would show how these choices affect transferability.

## Limitations

- The study relies heavily on representational similarity metrics (CKA and Procrustes) as proxies for transferability, which may not capture all relevant aspects of transfer learning performance
- The visual inspection method for identifying shared latent variables requires more rigorous validation across diverse dataset pairs
- The findings may need adjustment for domains with different characteristics than the tested datasets (shapes, faces, cars, 3D objects)

## Confidence

- High confidence: The finding that encoders learn generic representations while decoders learn dataset-specific ones is well-supported by the CKA analysis and reconstruction experiments
- Medium confidence: The practical guidelines for selecting which VAE components to retrain are sound but may need adjustment for domains with different characteristics than the tested datasets
- Low confidence: The visual inspection method for identifying shared latent variables, while promising, requires more rigorous validation across diverse dataset pairs

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-architectural validation**: Test the transferability findings with different VAE architectures (e.g., deeper networks, different activation functions) to ensure the generic vs specific representation pattern holds.

2. **Dataset diversity expansion**: Evaluate the visual inspection method on datasets with varying levels of semantic similarity to confirm its robustness and identify failure conditions.

3. **Downstream task benchmarking**: Compare the proposed transfer learning approach against standard fine-tuning methods on real-world classification and generation tasks to quantify practical benefits.
