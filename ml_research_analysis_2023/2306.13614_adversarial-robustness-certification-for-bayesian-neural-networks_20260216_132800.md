# Adversarial Robustness Certification for Bayesian Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13614
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13614
- Reference count: 40
- Presents a unified framework for bounding robustness of Bayesian neural networks to adversarial perturbations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for certifying the robustness of Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) to adversarial attacks. The method addresses the challenge of verifying two key robustness properties: probabilistic robustness (probability that a sampled BNN is safe for all inputs in a set) and decision robustness (worst-case expected output over posterior distribution). By reformulating these properties as integrations over weight-space intervals, the authors develop a framework that computes certified bounds using weight interval sampling, posterior integration, and bound propagation. The approach is inference-agnostic and scales to large networks including convolutional architectures.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a unified framework that bounds BNN robustness by reformulating probabilistic and decision robustness as weight-space interval integrations. The method employs weight interval sampling to explore the posterior distribution, posterior integration to compute bounds, and bound propagation to track how uncertainties flow through the network. This approach is designed to be compatible with various inference techniques (HMC, VI) and can handle large-scale networks. The framework provides sound but incomplete guarantees, meaning the bounds are valid but may underestimate true robustness.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates improved or comparable robustness guarantees compared to state-of-the-art adversarial training
- Successfully verifies robustness and uncertainty in BNNs across regression and classification tasks
- Shows applicability to large networks including convolutional architectures on datasets like MNIST and GTSRB

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by translating robustness verification into a problem of integrating over the posterior distribution of weights within specified intervals. By sampling these intervals and propagating bounds through the network, the method captures how uncertainty in weights affects output robustness. The inference-agnostic design allows the framework to leverage any Bayesian inference method, making it broadly applicable across different BNN implementations.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs)**: Neural networks with weights represented as probability distributions rather than point estimates
   - Why needed: Provides the foundation for modeling uncertainty in neural networks
   - Quick check: Verify understanding of how BNNs differ from standard deterministic networks

2. **Probabilistic Robustness**: The probability that a randomly sampled BNN is robust for all inputs in a given set
   - Why needed: Captures the likelihood of BNN safety under uncertainty
   - Quick check: Confirm understanding of how this differs from worst-case guarantees

3. **Decision Robustness**: The worst-case expected output over the posterior distribution of weights
   - Why needed: Provides conservative guarantees about BNN behavior
   - Quick check: Verify ability to distinguish between probabilistic and decision robustness

4. **Weight Interval Sampling**: Technique for exploring the posterior distribution by sampling from weight intervals
   - Why needed: Enables efficient exploration of the weight space for bound computation
   - Quick check: Confirm understanding of how interval sampling differs from standard Monte Carlo sampling

5. **Bound Propagation**: Method for tracking how uncertainties propagate through network layers
   - Why needed: Allows computation of output bounds given input and weight uncertainties
   - Quick check: Verify understanding of how bounds are maintained through nonlinear activations

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Bayesian Inference Method -> Weight Interval Sampler -> Bound Propagation Layer -> Output Bounds

Critical Path:
The critical path involves sampling weight intervals from the posterior, propagating these through each network layer while maintaining bounds, and computing final output bounds. Each layer's computation must maintain certified bounds while handling the increased uncertainty from the previous layer.

Design Tradeoffs:
- Soundness vs completeness: The method provides valid bounds but may underestimate true robustness
- Computational complexity vs bound tightness: Tighter bounds require more samples, increasing computation time
- Inference method flexibility vs optimization for specific techniques: The inference-agnostic design trades off potential optimizations for specific inference methods

Failure Signatures:
- Loose bounds indicating insufficient sampling or integration resolution
- Computational bottlenecks at deeper layers due to accumulated uncertainty
- Incompatibility with certain activation functions that don't preserve bound properties

First Experiments:
1. Verify bound computation on a simple linear BNN with known analytical solutions
2. Test weight interval sampling effectiveness on a small network with synthetic posterior
3. Validate bound propagation through a single nonlinear layer with controlled inputs

- Performance heavily dependent on quality of weight interval sampling and posterior integration
