# The Life Cycle of Knowledge in Big Language Models: A Survey

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive framework for understanding how knowledge circulates within large language models throughout their learning, tuning, and application processes. The authors divide the knowledge life cycle into five key periods: acquisition, representation, probing, editing, and application, systematically reviewing the current state-of-the-art methods for each stage. A significant focus is placed on identifying open questions and limitations in each area, particularly around knowledge acquisition mechanisms, the encoding of factual versus linguistic knowledge in transformer architectures, and the challenges of reliable knowledge editing. The survey serves as both a roadmap for researchers and a critical analysis of the current understanding of knowledge in language models.

## Method Summary
The survey employs a comprehensive review methodology to systematically categorize and analyze existing studies on knowledge in large language models. It organizes the literature into five distinct periods of the knowledge life cycle, examining the methods, challenges, and limitations for each period. The approach involves synthesizing findings from multiple related papers, identifying common themes and divergences, and proposing future research directions. While the survey does not provide specific implementation details or code, it serves as a valuable synthesis of the current state of knowledge in this field, highlighting key mechanisms and open questions for further investigation.

## Key Results
- Knowledge acquisition in language models primarily occurs through self-supervised learning on large-scale text corpora, with knowledge implicitly encoded in model parameters
- Factual knowledge is primarily encoded in feed-forward layers while linguistic knowledge is distributed across attention heads in the transformer architecture
- Current knowledge probing methods are susceptible to biases and inconsistencies, highlighting the need for more robust approaches
- Knowledge editing techniques exist but face challenges in modifying specific knowledge without affecting unrelated information or introducing unintended side effects

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Language models acquire knowledge through self-supervised learning on large-scale text corpora.
- Mechanism: By predicting masked tokens or next tokens, models implicitly capture and encode factual, linguistic, and semantic knowledge within their parameters.
- Core assumption: Knowledge is implicitly encoded in the model's parameters through exposure to diverse textual data.
- Evidence anchors:

- Break condition: If the model is trained on a corpus lacking diversity or sufficient scale, the knowledge acquisition may be incomplete or biased.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge is represented in specific layers and components of the transformer architecture.
- Mechanism: Factual knowledge is primarily encoded in the feed-forward layers, while linguistic knowledge is distributed across attention heads. The representation varies across layers, with lower layers encoding coarse-grained information and higher layers encoding fine-grained, task-specific knowledge.
- Core assumption: Different types of knowledge are encoded in distinct parts of the model architecture.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1]: "Dai et al. (2022a) first introduce the concept of knowledge neurons, which are neurons in transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) related to certain factual knowledge."
  - [section 4.1]: "Clark et al. (2019); Htut et al. (2019) investigate the linguistic knowledge encoded in attention heads, and find that while some individual attention heads are associated with specific aspects of syntax, the linguistic knowledge is distributed and represented by multiple attention heads."

- Break condition: If the model architecture is significantly altered or the knowledge types are not well-defined, the representation mechanism may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Knowledge can be edited in pre-trained language models by modifying specific parameters or using memory-based approaches.
- Mechanism: Methods include constrained fine-tuning, memory-based editing, meta-learning-based editing, and locating and editing specific knowledge neurons.
- Core assumption: Knowledge is locally stored in the model and can be modified without significantly affecting other knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6]: "Current approaches include constrained fine-tuning (Zhu et al., 2020), memory-based (Mitchell et al., 2022; Madaan et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2022), meta-learning inspired (De Cao et al., 2021; Hase et al., 2021; Mitchell et al., 2021) and location-based methods (Dai et al., 2022a; Meng et al., 2022)."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input text data -> Transformer encoder layers -> Feed-forward layers (factual knowledge) + Attention heads (linguistic knowledge) -> Probing/Editing methods -> Knowledge applications
- Critical path: Pre-training → Knowledge Representation → Knowledge Probing/Editing → Knowledge Application
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pre-training data size vs. model size and computational cost
  - Knowledge representation granularity vs. model complexity
  - Probing accuracy vs. bias and consistency
  - Editing effectiveness vs. unintended side effects
- Failure signatures:
  - Incomplete knowledge acquisition: Poor performance on downstream tasks
  - Inconsistent knowledge representation: Unpredictable model behavior
  - Unreliable knowledge probing: Inaccurate assessment of model capabilities
  - Ineffective knowledge editing: Failure to modify specific knowledge without affecting others
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a language model on a diverse corpus and evaluate its knowledge acquisition on a downstream task.
  2. Analyze the knowledge representation in the model's layers using probing techniques.
  3. Attempt to edit specific knowledge in the model and assess the impact on related knowledge and downstream tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop more reliable and robust knowledge probing methods for pre-trained language models that are not susceptible to biases and inconsistencies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the limitations of current knowledge probing methods, such as prompt-based probing and feature-based probing, and highlights the need for more robust and reliable approaches.
- Why unresolved: Current probing methods are often sensitive to prompt selection, instance verbalization, negation, and other factors, leading to inconsistent and unreliable results. Additionally, the attribution of results to the knowledge in PLMs or the probing task itself remains unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating new probing methods that address the identified limitations, such as incorporating causal analysis to identify and eliminate biases, or designing more comprehensive and unbiased benchmarks for knowledge probing.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we achieve effective and consistent knowledge editing in pre-trained language models without affecting unrelated knowledge or introducing unforeseen side effects?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenges of knowledge editing in PLMs, including the difficulty of modifying parameters without affecting other knowledge, and the need for methods that satisfy generality, reliability, and consistency desiderata.
- Why unresolved: Current knowledge editing approaches have limitations, such as potential unforeseen side effects on unrelated knowledge, and the difficulty of ensuring consistency across paraphrases and related knowledge.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating new knowledge editing approaches that address the identified limitations, such as incorporating ideas from meta-learning, continual learning, or life-long learning, and designing comprehensive benchmarks to assess the capabilities of editing strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we leverage the knowledge in pre-trained language models more effectively for downstream tasks while addressing the limitations of current application paradigms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the limitations of current knowledge application paradigms, such as the consistency and reliability issues of LMs-as-KBs, and the catastrophic forgetting, computational cost, and inconsistent predictions of fine-tuning, prompt learning, and in-context learning methods.
- Why unresolved: Current knowledge application paradigms have limitations that hinder their effectiveness and practicality in real-world applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating new knowledge application strategies that address the identified limitations, such as new tuning-free methods to address computational cost, or black-box tuning methods to tune pre-trained language models without access to their parameters.

## Limitations

- The survey primarily focuses on transformer-based models, limiting generalizability to other architectures
- While mechanisms are identified, the exact processes of knowledge acquisition and long-term stability of edited knowledge remain partially understood
- The taxonomy may not fully capture the complexity of knowledge interactions across different model scales and domains

- Low Confidence: The exact processes of knowledge acquisition from text data and the long-term stability of edited knowledge require further investigation
