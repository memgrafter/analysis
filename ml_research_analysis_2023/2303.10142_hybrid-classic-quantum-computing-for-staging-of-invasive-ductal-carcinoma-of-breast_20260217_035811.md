# Hybrid Classic-Quantum Computing for Staging of Invasive Ductal Carcinoma of Breast

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10142
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10142
- Reference count: 14
- One-line primary result: Hybrid classical-quantum computing successfully stages breast cancer with results comparable to human experts

## Executive Summary
This study presents a hybrid classical-quantum computing approach for staging Invasive Ductal Carcinoma (IDC) of the breast. The method combines a classical subroutine for TNM classification with a quantum subroutine for IDC staging, using quantum rule-based systems to represent medical knowledge and reasoning. The quantum routine employs 15 input qubits for TNM states, 10 additional qubits for logical operations, and produces an 8-bit output string corresponding to cancer stages I-A through IV. When tested with the IBM quantum simulator, the system successfully matched expected cancer stages for all 15 test cases, demonstrating the feasibility of using quantum computing for medical diagnosis tasks while maintaining results comparable to human experts.

## Method Summary
The hybrid approach separates cancer staging into two phases: a classical subroutine processes patient clinical examination data to produce a TNM classification, which is then encoded as activation of a single qubit in the quantum subsystem. The quantum routine implements staging rules using reversible quantum gates (CN and CCN gates) to process the TNM classification through 10 auxiliary qubits, producing an 8-bit output string representing cancer stages I-A through IV. The system was validated using the IBM quantum simulator with 15 test cases covering all possible TNM classifications.

## Key Results
- The hybrid system successfully staged all 15 test cases, matching expected cancer stages for each TNM classification
- Quantum rule-based implementation required 15 input qubits, 10 auxiliary qubits for logical operations, and produced 8-bit output strings
- Classical pre-processing of TNM classification reduced quantum complexity by encoding categorical classifications into single-qubit activations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The hybrid approach reduces qubit requirements by offloading classical TNM classification before quantum staging.
- Mechanism: The system separates the problem into two distinct phases: a classical subroutine processes patient data to produce a TNM classification, which is then encoded as activation of a single qubit in the quantum subsystem. This encoding strategy means only one of the 15 input qubits needs to be active at a time, drastically reducing the quantum state space that must be managed simultaneously.
- Core assumption: The TNM classification is categorical and deterministic enough that a classical approach is both sufficient and optimal for this step.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method combines a classical subroutine for TNM classification with a quantum subroutine for IDC staging"
  - [section] "The results of the clinical examination... is transferred to a classical system for TNM classification"
- Break condition: If TNM classification becomes probabilistic or requires quantum advantage for optimal performance, the classical pre-processing step becomes a bottleneck rather than an optimization.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Quantum rule-based inference can represent complex staging rules with fewer logical operations than classical equivalents.
- Mechanism: The system encodes medical staging rules as quantum circuits using reversible gates (CN and CCN gates) that implement AND/OR/NOT operations. These quantum circuits process the TNM classification through a network of 10 auxiliary qubits to produce an 8-bit output string representing cancer stages. The quantum representation allows parallel evaluation of multiple staging possibilities through superposition principles.
- Core assumption: The staging rules can be decomposed into a finite set of Boolean operations that map cleanly onto reversible quantum gates without requiring additional ancilla qubits beyond the 10 specified.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We need reversible quantum gates... such as the CN (controlled-not gate) and the CCN gate"
  - [section] "we are going to need 10 extra qubits to solve the defined inferential circuits"
- Break condition: If staging rules involve non-Boolean reasoning (probabilistic, fuzzy, or continuous variables), the quantum rule-based approach may not capture the necessary nuance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The 8-bit output encoding directly maps to cancer staging categories, enabling immediate clinical interpretation.
- Mechanism: The quantum routine produces an 8-bit string where each bit pattern corresponds to a specific cancer stage (I-A through IV). This encoding is designed so that after measurement, the resulting classical bit string can be directly interpreted as a cancer stage without additional processing. The system tests all 15 possible TNM classifications and verifies that each produces the expected stage pattern.
- Core assumption: The mapping between TNM classifications and cancer stages is deterministic and can be exhaustively enumerated in advance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we need an eight-bit string in the output... Table 5 illustrates the matching between the output bit string and the different stages of cancer"
  - [section] "The experiment was carried out with the IBM quantum simulator... the inferred cancer stages are also compared with the expected cancer stages"
- Break condition: If new staging categories are introduced or if the TNM-to-stage mapping becomes context-dependent, the fixed 8-bit encoding would require redesign.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the quantum staging system perform on real-world patient data rather than simulated cases?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge they tested with IBM quantum simulator but note this is at the "dawn of QC" and "there is still a lot to do"
- Why unresolved: The study only tested with simulated cases using the IBM quantum simulator, not with actual clinical data from real patients
- What evidence would resolve it: Clinical trials using the quantum staging system with real patient data, comparing results to traditional staging methods and expert diagnosis

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the computational and energy efficiency advantage of this hybrid classical-quantum approach compared to pure classical staging methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention quantum advantage could mean "more than just performing calculations faster" and "much greater energy savings," but do not provide quantitative comparisons
- Why unresolved: The paper presents the approach as potentially more efficient but does not provide benchmarks or energy consumption measurements comparing the hybrid system to existing classical methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies measuring execution time, computational resources, and energy consumption of the hybrid system versus traditional staging methods

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would the system handle cases where TNM classification is uncertain or ambiguous, and how would this uncertainty propagate through the quantum staging subroutine?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses categorical reasoning but acknowledges "uncertainty is inherent to human reasoning" and mentions the need to handle inaccurate knowledge in quantum systems
- Why unresolved: The current design assumes a clear TNM classification as input, but real clinical scenarios often involve uncertainty in classification, which the paper doesn't address for the quantum component
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and testing of the system with fuzzy or probabilistic TNM inputs, and demonstration of how uncertainty is handled in the quantum staging results

## Limitations
- Quantum staging was tested only through simulation rather than on actual quantum hardware, leaving noise and error effects unverified
- The system assumes deterministic TNM-to-stage mappings that may not capture clinical uncertainty or borderline cases
- Fixed 8-bit encoding cannot easily accommodate evolving staging criteria or additional diagnostic factors

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The hybrid architecture design is sound and the separation of classical TNM classification from quantum staging is a reasonable optimization strategy given current NISQ constraints
- **Medium confidence**: The quantum rule-based implementation can correctly represent Boolean staging rules using reversible gates, though real-world noise effects remain untested
- **Low confidence**: The system's performance with ambiguous or borderline clinical cases, and its scalability to more complex diagnostic scenarios beyond the 15 tested TNM states
