# SaDI: A Self-adaptive Decomposed Interpretable Framework for Electric Load Forecasting under Extreme Events

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.08299
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08299
- **Reference count**: 0
- **Primary result**: 22.14% improvement in daily mean normalized RMSE over state-of-the-art models for electric load forecasting during extreme events

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SaDI, a novel framework for electric load forecasting during extreme events like heatwaves, where traditional models often struggle due to rare extreme samples and dramatic load fluctuations. The SaDI framework decomposes the load series into long-term trend, short-term trend, and periodic components, modeling each separately for improved accuracy. By incorporating an External Variable Triggered Loss (ETL) that emphasizes extreme conditions and leveraging the interpretability of Generalized Additive Models (GAM), SaDI achieves significant improvements in forecasting accuracy while providing insights into feature contributions. Experiments on real-world Chinese grid data and public building energy datasets demonstrate the framework's effectiveness, with average 22.14% improvement in daily mean normalized RMSE compared to state-of-the-art models.

## Method Summary
The SaDI framework tackles electric load forecasting during extreme events by first decomposing the load series into long-term trend, short-term trend, and periodic components. Each component is modeled separately: the long-term trend is captured using linear regression, the short-term trend is modeled via Generalized Additive Models (GAM) enhanced with an External Variable Triggered Loss (ETL) to emphasize extreme conditions, and the periodic component is handled using LightGBM. The ETL uses external factors like temperature with a piecewise function to assign higher weights to samples with extreme values. GAM provides interpretability by showing how each feature contributes to predictions. Experiments on real-world Chinese grid data and public building energy datasets show SaDI achieves average 22.14% improvement in daily mean normalized RMSE compared to state-of-the-art models, with better handling of extreme events and interpretable results.

## Key Results
- SaDI achieves average 22.14% improvement in daily mean normalized RMSE compared to state-of-the-art models
- The framework demonstrates better handling of extreme events like heatwaves
- SaDI provides interpretable results through the use of Generalized Additive Models (GAM)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The SaDI framework's effectiveness stems from its decomposed approach to electric load forecasting, which separately models long-term trends, short-term trends, and periodic components. By using linear regression for long-term trends, GAM with ETL for short-term trends, and LightGBM for periodic components, SaDI can capture the unique characteristics of each aspect of the load series. The ETL mechanism, which uses external factors like temperature with a piecewise function to assign higher weights to extreme samples, helps the model better handle rare extreme events that traditional models often struggle with. Additionally, the use of GAM provides interpretability by showing how each feature contributes to predictions, allowing for insights into the factors driving load fluctuations during extreme events.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component Map**: Load Series -> Decomposition (Long-term Trend, Short-term Trend, Periodic) -> Individual Modeling (Linear Regression, GAM with ETL, LightGBM) -> Ensemble Prediction
- **Critical Path**: Load Series Decomposition -> GAM with ETL for Short-term Trend -> Ensemble with Linear Regression and LightGBM outputs
- **Design Tradeoffs**: Decomposing the load series allows for specialized modeling of each component but adds complexity; using GAM provides interpretability but may be less flexible than deep learning models.
- **Failure Signatures**: Poor decomposition may lead to inaccurate modeling of individual components; ETL may overfit to extreme samples if not properly regularized.

## Limitations
- The framework's performance on datasets from China and public building energy systems may not generalize to other geographic regions or different types of extreme events beyond heatwaves.
- The paper does not discuss computational efficiency or scalability of the SaDI framework for real-time deployment.
- The interpretability benefits of GAM, while demonstrated, lack quantitative metrics for assessing the quality and actionability of the feature contributions.
