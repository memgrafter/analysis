# Evaluating the overall sensitivity of saliency-based explanation methods

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13682
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13682
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 8
- All seven explanation methods pass the sensitivity test with SI scores above 0.01 threshold

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes an extension to the Independent Parameter Randomization (IPR) test for evaluating saliency-based explanation methods in convolutional neural networks. The key innovation is introducing a formal Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) threshold of 0.99 to determine when saliency maps are considered similar, providing a more rigorous sensitivity assessment. The extended test is applied to seven explanation methods across five CNN architectures using ImageNet, demonstrating that all methods pass the sensitivity test while revealing interesting correlations between certain methods. This work establishes a standardized methodology for comparing explanation methods' sensitivity to model perturbations.

## Method Summary
The paper extends the IPR test by introducing an SSIM threshold of 0.99 to determine when saliency maps are similar, then defines dataset-level sensitivity scores by aggregating individual image sensitivity scores. The method involves randomizing one layer at a time in five pre-trained CNN architectures (InceptionV3, MobileNetV2, VGG16, ResNet18, ResNet50) while keeping other layers fixed, then generating saliency maps using seven explanation methods (Gradients, InputXGradient, Guided Backpropagation, Guided GradCAM, DeepLIFT, Integrated Gradients, and LIME). SSIM scores are calculated between original and randomized-layer saliency maps, and sensitivity scores are aggregated across 900 ImageNet validation images to determine overall sensitivity for each method-architecture combination.

## Key Results
- All seven explanation methods pass the sensitivity test with SI scores above the 0.01 threshold
- LIME shows maximum sensitivity (SI = 1.0) for ResNet50 architecture specifically
- High correlations exist between Guided Backpropagation and Guided GradCAM, and between DeepLIFT, InputXGradient, and Integrated Gradients
- The extended IPR test successfully provides a standardized methodology for comparing explanation methods' sensitivity across different model architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The extension improves sensitivity assessment by introducing a formal SSIM threshold to determine when two saliency maps are similar.
- Mechanism: The paper uses a well-established mapping between SSIM scores and human-perceived image similarity (Wang et al., 2004) to select 0.99 as the threshold. This ensures that saliency maps are considered similar only when they are visually nearly identical, making the sensitivity test more rigorous.
- Core assumption: Human-perceived similarity mapping from Wang et al. (2004) is valid for saliency maps in the context of explanation methods.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By defining an SSIM threshold to determine when two saliency maps are similar"
  - [section 4] "Based on Table 1, we choose 0.99 as the threshold SSIM score to define when two saliency maps are 'similar enough'"
- Break condition: If the SSIM threshold of 0.99 is too strict for certain applications, it may fail to capture meaningful but slightly different saliency maps, leading to false negatives in sensitivity assessment.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The extension enables overall sensitivity evaluation across datasets by aggregating individual image sensitivity scores.
- Mechanism: The paper defines a dataset-level sensitivity score (S_I) by averaging individual image sensitivity scores (S_i) across all images in the dataset. This provides a single metric to compare explanation methods across different model architectures and datasets.
- Core assumption: Averaging individual image sensitivity scores is a valid way to represent overall sensitivity for a dataset.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By determining when an explanation method is sensitive to layer randomizations for a dataset"
  - [section 4] "We state that an explanation method E is sensitive to a set of images I if: S_I ≥ 0.01"
- Break condition: If the dataset contains images with very different characteristics, averaging may mask important variations in sensitivity across different types of images.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The extension provides a rigorous methodology to compare multiple explanation methods simultaneously.
- Mechanism: By applying the extended IPR test to seven explanation methods across five model architectures, the paper generates S_I scores that can be directly compared. This allows for systematic comparison of explanation methods' sensitivity to model perturbations.
- Core assumption: The S_I score is a valid and comprehensive metric for comparing explanation methods' overall sensitivity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The extended IPR test provides a rigorous methodology to compare explanation methods' sensitivity to model perturbations"
  - [section 7] "Figure 3 shows the S_I scores of each explanation method across all model architectures"
- Break condition: If the S_I score does not capture all relevant aspects of explanation quality (e.g., faithfulness, plausibility), it may lead to incomplete or misleading comparisons.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: SSIM (Structural Similarity Index)
  - Why needed here: SSIM is used to quantitatively measure the similarity between saliency maps generated by the original model and those generated by randomized layer models.
  - Quick check question: What does an SSIM score of 0.99 represent in terms of image similarity?

- Concept: Model perturbation through parameter randomization
  - Why needed here: Parameter randomization is the core mechanism used to test the sensitivity of explanation methods to changes in the model architecture.
  - Quick check question: How does randomizing a single layer while keeping other layers unchanged help assess the sensitivity of an explanation method?

- Concept: Saliency maps
  - Why needed here: Saliency maps are the explanations generated by the explanation methods, showing which parts of the input image contributed most to the prediction.
  - Quick check question: What information does a saliency map provide about a model's decision-making process?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: 
  ImageNet dataset -> CNN architectures (InceptionV3, MobileNetV2, VGG16, ResNet18, ResNet50) -> Layer randomization -> Saliency map generation -> SSIM calculation -> S_i scores -> S_I aggregation

- Critical path:
  1. Generate randomized layer models for each architecture
  2. Generate saliency maps for original and randomized models
  3. Calculate SSIM scores between saliency maps
  4. Compute S_i scores for individual images
  5. Aggregate S_i scores to obtain S_I for the dataset
  6. Compare S_I scores across explanation methods

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost vs. comprehensiveness: Testing on a subset of 900 images with 90% confidence interval balances thoroughness with feasibility
  - Threshold selection: The choice of 0.99 SSIM threshold prioritizes visual similarity but may be too strict for some applications
  - Model coverage: Five architectures provide good diversity but may not capture all possible model types

- Failure signatures:
  - All explanation methods having S_I scores very close to 0.01 (threshold) suggests the threshold may be too low
  - High variance in S_I scores across architectures for a single explanation method indicates architecture-specific behavior
  - Unexpected correlations between explanation methods may reveal shared implementation issues

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify SSIM threshold: Test the 0.99 SSIM threshold on a small set of visually similar and dissimilar saliency maps to confirm it aligns with human perception
  2. Validate sensitivity scoring: Check that S_i scores correctly identify sensitive and insensitive explanation methods on a known dataset
  3. Compare with original IPR: Run the extended IPR test on a small dataset and compare results with the original IPR test to validate the improvements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the exact magnitude of sensitivity thresholds required for explanation methods to be considered faithful across different domains?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper establishes a sensitivity threshold (SSIM < 0.99, resulting in SI threshold of 0.01) but notes this may vary by use case and domain.
- Why unresolved: The authors explicitly state that "certain use cases might require a higher threshold because observing a minimal amount of sensitivity would not suffice" but don't specify what those thresholds might be or how to determine them for different applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing explanation method performance at varying sensitivity thresholds across multiple domains (NLP, healthcare, finance) to establish domain-specific threshold guidelines.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different reference inputs affect the sensitivity scores of Integrated Gradients and DeepLIFT methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "It would be interesting to observe how different reference inputs affect the sensitivities of IG and DeepLIFT. In this work, we use an image where all pixels are zero as a reference but there are other types of reference inputs too, namely, maximum distance, blurred, uniform, and Gaussian."
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses a zero-pixel reference image for these methods but acknowledges that other reference types exist and may yield different sensitivity results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative sensitivity analysis of IG and DeepLIFT using multiple reference inputs (zero, maximum distance, blurred, uniform, Gaussian) across various model architectures and datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does LIME show maximum sensitivity (SI = 1.0) for ResNet50 but not for other architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "LIME has consistently high SI scores, comparable to Gradients, except for ResNet50, for which it has an SI score of 1.0... We hypothesize that this high sensitivity can be attributed to the surrogate model... This could be because the surrogate model is a local and simpler approximation of the original CNN-based model architecture."
- Why unresolved: The authors only provide a hypothesis without testing or confirming the mechanism behind this architecture-specific behavior.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of LIME's surrogate model behavior across different CNN architectures, including examination of how layer randomizations affect surrogate model training and explanation generation specifically for ResNet50 versus other architectures.

## Limitations
- The choice of 0.99 SSIM threshold, while justified by human perception studies, may not generalize to all types of saliency maps or explanation methods
- The averaging approach for dataset-level sensitivity scores could mask important variations across different image types
- The evaluation focuses solely on CNNs and may not capture the behavior of explanation methods on other architectures like transformers

## Confidence
**High confidence**: The methodology for extending the IPR test with SSIM thresholds is clearly defined and the implementation appears sound. The comparison of seven explanation methods across five architectures is comprehensive.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that the extended IPR test provides a more rigorous methodology is supported by the results, but the specific threshold choice (0.99) and its generalizability remain uncertain. The interpretation of high correlations between certain explanation methods could have alternative explanations.

**Low confidence**: The assertion that the S_I score is a comprehensive metric for comparing explanation methods' overall sensitivity may be overstated, as it doesn't capture all aspects of explanation quality.

## Next Checks
1. **Threshold sensitivity analysis**: Systematically test different SSIM thresholds (0.95, 0.97, 0.99, 0.995) to evaluate how the choice affects S_I scores and explanation method rankings.

2. **Architecture diversity validation**: Apply the extended IPR test to additional model architectures beyond CNNs (e.g., vision transformers) to assess the method's generalizability across different model types.

3. **Qualitative validation**: Conduct human studies to verify that saliency maps with SSIM scores above 0.99 are indeed perceived as similar by human observers, particularly for the explanation methods that showed high correlations.
