## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of uncertainty quantification in visual odometry for resource-constrained autonomous edge robotics. Traditional VO systems lack computationally efficient methods to estimate predictive uncertainties, which are essential for risk-aware decision-making in mission-critical applications. The authors propose a family of lightweight conformalized VO frameworks that extract uncertainty bands with guaranteed statistical validity while maintaining low computational overhead. Four novel frameworks are presented, ranging from univariate to multivariate approaches, providing a spectrum of solutions adaptable to different resource constraints. The methods combine interval scoring, calibration loss, and traditional metrics to achieve optimal uncertainty estimates while maintaining accuracy across varying noise conditions and model architectures.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach leverages conformal inference (CI) to extract uncertainty bands with guaranteed statistical validity while maintaining low computational overhead. Four novel frameworks are presented: univariate conformalized quantile regression (UCQR), multivariate conformalized quantile regression (MCQR), conformalized set prediction (CSP), and a novel training loss function that combines interval scoring and calibration loss with traditional metrics (MSE and KL-divergence). These frameworks provide varying levels of computational complexity, interval adaptiveness, and data dependency. The conformalized joint prediction (CJP) method achieves optimal uncertainty estimates with tunability and maintains accuracy even under varying noise conditions and model architectures. The approach addresses the fundamental limitation of existing VO systems that lack computationally efficient uncertainty estimation mechanisms.

## Key Results
- Consistent capture of true uncertainty in pose estimations across different datasets, estimation models, and applied noise types
- Conformalized joint prediction (CJP) method achieves optimal uncertainty estimates with tunability while maintaining accuracy under varying noise conditions
- Four frameworks provide a spectrum of solutions balancing computational complexity and uncertainty quantification capability
- Demonstrated statistical validity of uncertainty bands while maintaining low computational overhead suitable for edge deployment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging conformal inference, which provides distribution-free uncertainty quantification with guaranteed coverage under exchangeability assumptions. By constructing prediction intervals or sets that contain the true value with a user-specified probability, the approach captures epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty without requiring complex Bayesian inference. The combination of interval scoring, calibration loss, and traditional metrics during training ensures that the learned uncertainty estimates are both accurate and statistically valid. The modular framework design allows adaptation to different computational constraints while maintaining the core statistical guarantees.

## Foundational Learning

**Conformal Inference (CI)**: Distribution-free uncertainty quantification method that provides guaranteed coverage under exchangeability assumptions. Needed because traditional Bayesian methods are computationally expensive for edge deployment. Quick check: Verify coverage probability on held-out calibration data.

**Quantile Regression**: Predicts conditional quantiles of the target distribution rather than point estimates. Needed to capture asymmetric uncertainty in pose estimations. Quick check: Evaluate calibration of predicted quantiles on validation set.

**Interval Scoring**: Loss function that penalizes both interval width and miscoverage. Needed to balance between uncertainty quantification and prediction accuracy. Quick check: Monitor both interval width and coverage rate during training.

**Exchangeability Assumption**: Data points are assumed to be identically distributed and order-independent. Needed for the theoretical guarantees of conformal inference. Quick check: Test robustness to temporal correlations in sequential data.

**Interval Scoring Rules**: Proper scoring rules for evaluating probabilistic predictions with interval estimates. Needed to ensure calibrated uncertainty estimates. Quick check: Compare interval scores across different conformalization methods.

## Limitations
- Conformal inference guarantees assume exchangeability, which may be violated in sequential VO applications with temporal correlations
- Limited real-world dataset testing, with most evaluations on synthetic or simulated data
- Computational requirements for multivariate methods may still exceed strict edge constraints
- No explicit handling of multi-modal uncertainty distributions

## Confidence
