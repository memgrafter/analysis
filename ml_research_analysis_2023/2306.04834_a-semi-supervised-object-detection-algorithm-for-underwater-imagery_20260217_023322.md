# A Semi-supervised Object Detection Algorithm for Underwater Imagery

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04834
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04834
- Reference count: 21
- One-line primary result: Achieves 0.64 average precision on unlabelled underwater datasets using VAE-based anomaly detection

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a semi-supervised object detection algorithm for identifying artificial objects in underwater imagery by treating them as anomalies. The method uses a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to learn a compressed representation of natural seafloor imagery, then identifies artificial objects through reconstruction error analysis and latent space clustering. The approach is evaluated on real seafloor imagery collected by an Autonomous Underwater Vehicle at Jervis Bay, Australia, demonstrating effective detection of artificial objects without requiring labeled training data for these anomalies.

## Method Summary
The method trains a VAE on inlier (natural seafloor) images to learn a compressed latent representation. During inference, the VAE's reconstruction error is used to generate anomaly heatmaps highlighting poorly reconstructed regions. Regions of Interest (ROIs) are extracted from high-error areas and filtered based on size thresholds. Simultaneously, latent vectors are reduced via t-SNE and density is estimated using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) to identify low-density regions likely containing anomalies. The two approaches are combined to detect artificial objects, with performance evaluated using precision, recall, and F1-score across different latent dimensionalities.

## Key Results
- Achieves 0.64 average precision on unlabelled underwater datasets
- Demonstrates sensitivity to latent dimensionality, with performance plateauing beyond d=512
- Shows effective separation of anomalies in latent space using density estimation techniques

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Improves anomaly detection by reducing search space through clustering in VAE latent space
- Mechanism: Images embedded into low-dimensional latent space by VAE encoder; KDE applied to identify low-density regions likely containing anomalies
- Core assumption: Anomalous images are sparsely distributed in latent space
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states method clusters image data in learned latent space to extract anomalous features; Section VI-C shows anomalous images gather in low-density regions
- Break condition: If anomalies are not sparsely distributed in latent space, density-based clustering will not isolate them effectively

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Uses reconstruction error in poorly reconstructed regions to identify anomalies
- Mechanism: Computes squared pixel-wise difference between input and VAE reconstruction to form anomaly heatmap; extracts ROIs from high-error areas
- Core assumption: Anomalous objects cause localized high reconstruction errors since VAE trained only on natural images
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions anomaly score based on extracting poorly reconstructed regions; Section VI-B describes ROI extraction from reconstruction error heatmaps
- Break condition: If VAE can reconstruct anomalous objects well (overfitting), reconstruction error won't discriminate between inliers and outliers

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Performance depends on VAE's latent dimensionality, with higher dimensions improving separation
- Mechanism: Trains models with varying latent dimensionalities (8 to 2048) and evaluates performance to find optimal d
- Core assumption: Higher latent dimensionality allows VAE to learn richer feature representations, improving anomaly separability
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions evaluating precision-recall tradeoff with appropriate latent dimensionality; Section VII shows ROI-based approach plateaus beyond d=512
- Break condition: If optimal d is too high, computational cost increases without meaningful performance gain; if too low, anomalies may not be separable

  - Why needed here: Method relies on VAEs to learn compressed representation of inlier images and identify anomalies through reconstruction error and latent space clustering
  - Quick check question: How does the KL divergence term in VAE loss function encourage latent space to be well-behaved for clustering?

  - Why needed here: Used to identify low-density regions in latent space assumed to contain anomalies
  - Quick check question: Why might KDE be preferred over DBSCAN in this application, given need for probabilistic anomaly scores?

- Concept: Region of Interest (ROI) extraction and image segmentation
  - Why needed here: ROIs extracted from anomaly heatmaps to focus on areas with high reconstruction error, with properties used as anomaly scores
  - Quick check question: How does choice of contour area thresholds for ROI extraction affect false positive and false negative rates?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data pipeline (underwater imagery acquisition → preprocessing → train/test split) → VAE model (encoder → latent sampling → decoder → reconstruction) → Anomaly detection (L2 reconstruction error → anomaly heatmap → ROI extraction → ROI anomaly score; Latent space → t-SNE reduction → KDE density estimation → clustering) → Evaluation (precision, recall, F1-score)

- Critical path: VAE training on inliers → inference on test set → anomaly heatmap generation → ROI extraction and scoring → latent space clustering → combined anomaly detection

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Latent dimensionality: Higher d increases model capacity and potentially better anomaly separation, but also computational cost and risk of overfitting
  - KDE bandwidth: Affects sensitivity of density-based clustering; too low leads to overfitting, too high to underfitting
  - ROI area thresholds: Must be set based on expected object size; too strict may miss anomalies, too lenient increases false positives

- Failure signatures:
  - High false positives: Likely due to overly permissive ROI thresholds or poor KDE bandwidth selection
  - High false negatives: May indicate insufficient VAE capacity (low d) or that anomalies are not well-represented by reconstruction error
  - Poor clustering: Could result from inappropriate t-SNE perplexity or insufficient latent dimensionality

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train VAEs with d=64, 256, 1024; compare reconstruction quality and latent space separability via t-SNE plots
  2. Apply KDE with varying bandwidths on latent vectors (d=256); evaluate impact on anomaly detection precision/recall
  3. Vary ROI area thresholds; plot precision-recall curves to select optimal tradeoff for dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: What is the impact of using pre-trained models as feature extractors in VAE encoder?
  - Basis in paper: Authors mention as future work possibility of applying transfer-learning approach using pre-trained models as feature extractors in VAE encoder
  - Why unresolved: Current study uses custom VAE architecture without pre-trained components, so potential benefits or drawbacks not evaluated
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing anomaly detection performance using pre-trained feature extractors versus current custom VAE architecture

- Question: How would incorporating temporal dependencies between images or their latent representations affect online anomaly detection performance?
  - Basis in paper: Authors suggest extending approach to image sequences would potentially support online anomaly detection
  - Why unresolved: Current method processes images independently without considering temporal context, so potential benefits of temporal modeling not assessed
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing anomaly detection performance on sequential data using current method versus extended method incorporating temporal dependencies

- Question: How can reconstruction error thresholds be adaptively adjusted across different datasets to improve robustness to environmental changes?
  - Basis in paper: Authors propose this as future work, suggesting adapting reconstruction error thresholds rather than maintaining static thresholds could develop more robust system
  - Why unresolved: Current method uses fixed percentile thresholds for both density scores and ROI scores, which may not be optimal across varying datasets or environmental conditions
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating improved anomaly detection performance using adaptive thresholds compared to fixed thresholds across datasets with varying environmental conditions

## Limitations

- Method relies heavily on assumption that artificial objects are anomalous and sparsely distributed in learned latent space, which may not hold for all datasets
- Evaluation performed on single dataset from Jervis Bay, Australia, limiting generalizability to other underwater environments with different lighting, sediment types, or target characteristics
- Choice of KDE bandwidth and ROI area thresholds not systematically optimized, potentially impacting detection performance

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Latent Space Clustering): Medium - Supported by qualitative t-SNE visualizations but limited quantitative validation
- Mechanism 2 (Reconstruction Error ROI): Medium - Demonstrated effectiveness but threshold selection lacks rigorous optimization
- Mechanism 3 (Latent Dimensionality Impact): Low - Performance plateau observed but optimal dimensionality not clearly established
