# Small Temperature is All You Need for Differentiable Architecture Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06855
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06855
- Reference count: 21
- The paper proposes using small temperature to sparsify continuous architecture parameters in DARTS, eliminating performance collapse and achieving superior searching results.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the architecture mismatch problem in differentiable architecture search (DARTS), where the distribution of architecture parameters during training differs from evaluation, leading to performance discrepancies and potential model collapse. The authors propose using small temperature to sparsify the continuous distribution of architecture parameters during training, effectively closing the gap between training and evaluation phases. The method introduces sparse-noisy softmax to alleviate gradient saturation and an exponential temperature schedule for better distribution control. Extensive experiments across multiple search spaces and datasets demonstrate that this approach effectively eliminates performance collapse while achieving superior searching results compared to baseline DARTS methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed method addresses DARTS' architecture mismatch problem by introducing a temperature-based approach to control the sparsity of architecture parameters during training. Small temperature values are used to sharpen the softmax distribution, encouraging the selection of stronger operations while suppressing weaker ones. To prevent gradient saturation issues that arise from extreme temperature values, the authors introduce sparse-noisy softmax, which adds controlled noise to the architecture weights. An exponential temperature schedule gradually reduces temperature during training, allowing the architecture to evolve from exploration to exploitation phases. Additionally, an entropy-based adaptive scheme monitors and adjusts the temperature dynamically based on the distribution's entropy, ensuring robust performance across different scenarios. This approach maintains a continuous relaxation of the architecture space while effectively mimicking the discrete selection that occurs during evaluation.

## Key Results
- Eliminates performance collapse that commonly occurs in standard DARTS approaches
- Achieves higher accuracy in searched architectures across multiple datasets and search spaces
- Demonstrates improved stability and robustness compared to baseline DARTS methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of the small temperature approach stems from its ability to better align the training and evaluation phases of DARTS. During training, architecture parameters exist as continuous distributions over possible operations, while evaluation requires discrete selection. By using small temperature, the softmax distribution becomes more peaked, effectively mimicking the discrete selection process. This alignment reduces the discrepancy between how operations are weighted during training versus how they are ultimately selected during evaluation. The sparse-noisy softmax prevents the optimization from getting stuck in poor local minima by maintaining sufficient gradient flow even when the temperature is low. The exponential decay schedule allows the search process to start with broader exploration and gradually focus on the most promising architectures, while the entropy-based adaptation ensures the temperature remains optimal throughout training, preventing both premature convergence and excessive randomness.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Search Space -> Architecture Weights -> Softmax (with Temperature) -> Operation Selection -> Validation Loss -> Architecture Optimization

Critical Path: Architecture weights are initialized and optimized through gradient descent, with temperature controlling the softmax distribution that determines operation importance. The validation loss guides weight updates, while the temperature schedule and adaptive scheme ensure the distribution remains optimal throughout training.

Design Tradeoffs: The method trades computational simplicity for improved stability and performance. While standard DARTS uses fixed temperature (typically 1.0), the proposed approach requires careful temperature scheduling and entropy monitoring, adding complexity but significantly improving search quality and robustness.

Failure Signatures: Performance collapse manifests as the selection of weak operations dominating the final architecture. This occurs when the training distribution becomes too flat or when gradient saturation prevents proper weight updates. The method addresses these by maintaining appropriate temperature levels and using noisy softmax to ensure gradient flow.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of small temperature may vary across different search spaces and architectures, potentially requiring problem-specific tuning.
- The introduction of sparse-noisy softmax adds hyperparameters (noise level, temperature decay rate) that could affect stability and require careful calibration.
- While the exponential temperature schedule provides good control, it may need adjustment for different search scenarios to achieve optimal performance.
