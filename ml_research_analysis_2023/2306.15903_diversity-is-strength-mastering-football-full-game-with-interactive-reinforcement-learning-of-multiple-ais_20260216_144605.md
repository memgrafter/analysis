# Diversity is Strength: Mastering Football Full Game with Interactive Reinforcement Learning of Multiple AIs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.15903
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15903
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Primary result**: Novel DRL framework that won both 5v5 and 11v11 tracks in IEEE CoG 2022 Football AI Competition

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Diversity is Strength (DIS), a novel DRL training framework that simultaneously trains three distinct AI agents in complex multi-agent environments like football games. The framework uses an interconnected history model pool structure to enhance the AIs' capabilities and strategy diversity. DIS incorporates model evaluation and screening schemes to select the best models for the final AI. The trained AI won the 5v5 and 11v11 tracks in the IEEE CoG 2022 Football AI Competition, achieving high performance in both tracks for the first time.

## Method Summary
The DIS framework trains three distinct AI types simultaneously: a main agent using PPO, a policy explorer that tweaks reward shaping and inputs, and a method explorer using different algorithms (RND-PPO, MAPPO). These AIs are linked through an interconnected history model pool structure with five pools (SHMP, LHMP, SMP, DPMP, DMMP) that store models from different AIs and time periods. Periodic model evaluation using Elo-based ranking selects top performers to enrich the shared pools, ensuring both strength and diversity in training opponents.

## Key Results
- Won both 5v5 and 11v11 tracks in IEEE CoG 2022 Football AI Competition
- Achieved high performance in both tracks for the first time
- Demonstrated rich strategy diversity through behavior analysis
- Ablation experiments proved designed modules benefit the training process

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Diversity among opponents in the interconnected history model pool structure prevents catastrophic forgetting and strategy collapse.
- **Mechanism**: By maintaining five different pools and linking three AI types, the framework ensures the main agent is constantly challenged by both past versions of itself and distinct strategies from other AIs. This variety of opponents forces the model to retain older strategies while adapting to new ones.
- **Core assumption**: Diverse opponent strategies in training lead to more generalizable and robust AI performance.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "These AIs are linked through an interconnected history model pool structure, which enhances their capabilities and strategy diversities."
- **Break condition**: If the model sampling method overly favors high-performing models, diversity may decrease, leading to overfitting and forgetting.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Simultaneous training of three distinct AI types with different algorithms and reward settings enriches strategy diversity while maintaining strength.
- **Mechanism**: The main agent uses PPO with stable hyperparameters, the policy explorer tweaks reward shaping and inputs to generate variants, and the method explorer uses entirely different algorithms (RND-PPO, MAPPO). This creates a "strategy zoo" where each AI explores different parts of the strategy space.
- **Core assumption**: Different algorithms and reward designs naturally produce distinct strategies without explicit coordination.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.2] "The diverse training settings and algorithms are the primary reasons for producing varied model effects."
- **Break condition**: If reward shaping is too extreme or algorithms are too similar, the diversity benefit diminishes.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Model evaluation and screening with Elo-based ranking ensures only high-quality, diverse strategies enter the shared pools.
- **Mechanism**: Periodically, models from each AI type are evaluated against each other and against historical models. Top performers are added to the superior model pool and shared pools.
- **Core assumption**: Elo-based ranking correlates with strategic diversity and robustness.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 3.3] "We rank the results and select the top three models from the top model pool, eliminating the lower-ranked models."
- **Break condition**: If evaluation only measures win rate without considering strategy novelty, the pools may converge to similar strategies.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Non-transitivity in self-play training
  - **Why needed here**: Explains why simply training against the latest version of oneself leads to strategy collapse and forgetting.
  - **Quick check question**: Why does playing only against your most recent self-model cause the model to forget older strategies?

- **Concept**: History model pools (HMP) as a mitigation strategy
  - **Why needed here**: Provides the foundation for why maintaining a pool of past models helps maintain strategy diversity.
  - **Quick check question**: How does including older versions of the model in training opponents prevent catastrophic forgetting?

- **Concept**: Reward shaping and its impact on strategy diversity
  - **Why needed here**: Clarifies how different reward functions can bias the AI toward different playstyles (e.g., ball possession vs. aggressive attacking).
  - **Quick check question**: What is the trade-off between using hand-crafted rewards to guide behavior and maintaining model generalization?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Sampler -> Trainer -> History Model Pools (SHMP, LHMP, SMP, DPMP, DMMP) -> Evaluator -> Scheduler
- **Critical path**: 1. Sampler generates data using current model or sampled history model as opponent. 2. Trainer updates model parameters. 3. Evaluator ranks new models and updates HMPs. 4. Best models from each AI type are shared across pools. 5. Repeat.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Resource allocation: Balancing training resources among three AI types vs. depth in one.
  - Diversity vs. performance: Including weaker but diverse models in pools vs. only strong ones.
  - Complexity vs. maintainability: Interconnected pools add complexity but improve diversity.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Elo scores plateau early → possible overfitting or insufficient diversity.
  - One AI type dominates the pools → imbalance in strategy exploration.
  - Training instability → check learning rates or gradient clipping.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train only the main agent with self-play and SHMP to establish baseline performance.
  2. Add policy explorer with ball possession reward to test diversity impact.
  3. Introduce method explorer with RND-PPO to evaluate cross-algorithm strategy enrichment.

## Limitations
- The Elo-based model evaluation mechanism lacks rigorous validation and may not adequately capture strategy diversity
- Resource allocation strategy between three AI types is not justified
- Claims about specific strategy diversity benefits from the three-AI structure are primarily based on qualitative analysis

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The competition results (winning both 5v5 and 11v11 tracks) provide strong empirical evidence for the framework's effectiveness in practical settings.
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism of using interconnected history model pools to prevent catastrophic forgetting is theoretically sound but lacks direct experimental validation in this paper.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about specific strategy diversity benefits from the three-AI structure are primarily based on qualitative analysis rather than quantitative metrics.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation on evaluation metric**: Run experiments comparing Elo-based screening versus alternative diversity metrics (e.g., strategy embedding distance) to verify that the current evaluation captures true strategic diversity.

2. **Resource allocation sensitivity**: Systematically vary the computational resources allocated to each AI type to determine optimal distribution and validate the claimed benefits of the three-AI structure.

3. **Strategy diversity quantification**: Implement automated strategy clustering analysis on the trained models to provide quantitative evidence of the claimed "rich strategies" rather than relying solely on qualitative descriptions.
