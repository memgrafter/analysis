# Detecting Fake Job Postings Using Bidirectional LSTM

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02019
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02019
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Bidirectional LSTM achieved ROC AUC of 0.91 and 98.71% accuracy in detecting fake job postings

## Executive Summary
This study presents a Bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) approach for detecting fraudulent job postings in online job markets. The model processes both numeric and text features from job advertisements, leveraging bidirectional LSTM layers to capture contextual information from past and future time steps in the text data. The approach outperforms traditional ensemble models including LightGBM, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting, demonstrating superior performance in identifying fake job postings.

## Method Summary
The study implemented a Bidirectional LSTM model that processes both numeric and text features from job advertisements. The bidirectional architecture captures contextual information from both past and future time steps in the text data, providing a more comprehensive understanding of the job posting content. The model was trained and evaluated on a dataset of job advertisements, with performance measured using ROC AUC score and accuracy metrics.

## Key Results
- Achieved ROC AUC score of 0.91 for fake job posting detection
- Attained 98.71% accuracy on test data
- Outperformed ensemble models (LightGBM, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) in comparative analysis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The bidirectional LSTM architecture captures contextual information from both past and future time steps in the text data, enabling the model to understand the full context of job posting content. This comprehensive temporal understanding allows the model to identify subtle patterns and inconsistencies that distinguish fake postings from legitimate ones, which unidirectional models might miss.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input features -> Embedding layer -> Bidirectional LSTM layers -> Dense layers -> Output layer
Critical path: Text features pass through embedding and Bi-LSTM layers, while numeric features are processed separately, then both are concatenated and passed through dense layers for final classification
Design tradeoffs: Bi-LSTM captures more context than unidirectional LSTM but requires more computational resources and training time
Failure signatures: May overfit on small datasets, struggle with rare words not in vocabulary, or fail to capture domain-specific fraud patterns
