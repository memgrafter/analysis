# Bayesian Reinforcement Learning with Limited Cognitive Load

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03263
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03263
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces capacity-limited Bayesian reinforcement learning framework that balances information acquisition with reward optimization through an information bottleneck

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a framework for capacity-limited Bayesian reinforcement learning that integrates Bayesian inference, reinforcement learning, and rate-distortion theory to model processing constraints on learning and action selection. The core innovation is introducing an information bottleneck between an agent's beliefs and actions, formalizing cognitive limitations as a trade-off between expected utility and mutual information. The authors develop BLASTS (Blahut-Arimoto Limited Thompson Sampling), a variant of Thompson Sampling that optimizes this trade-off, and demonstrate through theoretical analysis and simulations that capacity-limited agents can achieve more efficient exploration in multi-armed bandit and episodic reinforcement learning settings.

## Method Summary
The framework modifies standard Bayesian decision-making by introducing a capacity constraint that limits the information flow from beliefs to actions through a rate-distortion function. The BLASTS algorithm approximates optimal capacity-limited decision rules by combining Monte Carlo sampling to approximate environment distributions with the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm to compute the optimal decision rule that trades off rewards and information rate. The method provides principled bounds on Bayesian regret that improve with reduced capacity constraints, enabling resource-aware agent design that balances computational efficiency with performance.

## Key Results
- Capacity-limited agents achieve better long-term performance by avoiding overfitting to noisy observations through information bottleneck
- BLASTS provides a computationally tractable method for approximating optimal capacity-limited decision rules
- Theoretical bounds show Bayesian regret can be controlled by the rate-distortion function, enabling resource-aware design

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Capacity-limited Bayesian reinforcement learning achieves better long-term performance by balancing information acquisition with reward optimization through an information bottleneck.
- Mechanism: The framework introduces a rate-distortion function that optimizes the trade-off between expected utility and the mutual information between beliefs and actions. By limiting the information flow from beliefs to actions, the agent avoids overfitting to noisy observations and maintains computational efficiency.
- Core assumption: The mutual information between beliefs and actions serves as a valid proxy for cognitive load, and minimizing this information while maintaining reward maximization leads to more efficient learning.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract explicitly states the core idea is introducing an information bottleneck formalizing capacity limitations as trade-off between expected utility and mutual information; section describes decision-rule as estimate-to-action channel with limited capacity.
- Break condition: If mutual information doesn't accurately measure cognitive load (e.g., in domains where actions require minimal processing regardless of belief complexity), the framework may not provide benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The BLASTS algorithm provides a computationally tractable method to approximate optimal capacity-limited decision rules by generalizing Thompson Sampling.
- Mechanism: BLASTS uses Monte Carlo sampling to approximate the environment distribution and the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm to iteratively compute the optimal decision rule that trades off rewards and information rate. This allows the agent to focus computational resources on the most informative actions.
- Core assumption: Monte Carlo approximation of environment distribution is sufficient for practical computation, and Blahut-Arimoto algorithm converges to good approximation of optimal decision rule.
- Evidence anchors: Section describes BLASTS performing three procedures including using Blahut-Arimoto to iteratively compute globally optimal decision-rule; corpus shows weak support with no direct citations of BLASTS or Blahut-Arimoto in related papers.
- Break condition: If environment distribution cannot be adequately approximated with reasonable samples, or if Blahut-Arimoto algorithm fails to converge in practice, computational benefits disappear.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The framework provides principled bounds on Bayesian regret that improve with reduced capacity constraints, enabling resource-aware agent design.
- Mechanism: Theoretical results show Bayesian regret can be bounded in terms of the rate-distortion function, allowing designers to trade off performance against computational capacity. Lower capacity constraints lead to simpler learning targets but higher regret.
- Core assumption: Information-theoretic bounds on regret are tight enough to be practically useful for agent design decisions.
- Evidence anchors: Theorem and Corollary provide specific bounds relating BayesRegret to rate-distortion function R1(D) and information rate D1(R); section mentions providing both novel simulations and previously-established theoretical results in several learning settings.
- Break condition: If theoretical bounds are too loose to guide practical decisions, or if real-world performance deviates significantly from bounds, framework loses prescriptive value.

  - Quick check question: What is the fundamental limit that rate-distortion theory establishes for lossy compression, and how does this relate to the agent's decision-making process?

  - Why needed here: Forms basis for how agents update beliefs about environment and make decisions under uncertainty, which capacity-limited framework modifies.
  - Quick check question: How does Thompson Sampling use posterior sampling to achieve probability matching, and why is this property important for exploration?

  - Why needed here: Information bottleneck formalizes capacity constraint by limiting mutual information between beliefs and actions, directly modeling cognitive limitations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Environment model (E, Θ, observation function ρ) -> Belief estimator (ηt, Bayesian posterior updating) -> Decision rule (δt, capacity-limited policy) -> Rate-distortion optimizer (Blahut-Arimoto algorithm) -> Performance metric (expected utility, Bayesian regret) -> Capacity constraint (λ parameter, rate bound R)

- Critical path:
  1. Agent observes history Ht
  2. Update belief distribution ηt using Bayesian inference
  3. Compute optimal decision rule δt using rate-distortion optimization
  4. Sample action from δt and execute in environment
  5. Observe outcome and update history
  6. Repeat

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. computational efficiency: More Monte Carlo samples improve approximation quality but increase computation time
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: Higher capacity constraints allow more information processing but may lead to overfitting
  - Simplicity vs. performance: Lower capacity constraints yield simpler models but potentially lower rewards

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor convergence of Blahut-Arimoto algorithm (check iteration count and tolerance)
  - Rapidly increasing regret over time (indicates insufficient exploration)
  - Computational bottlenecks in Monte Carlo sampling (monitor sample count and runtime)
  - Disconnection between theoretical bounds and empirical performance (validate assumptions)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement BLASTS for a simple 2-armed bandit and verify it recovers Thompson Sampling when λ=0
  2. Test sensitivity to λ parameter by plotting action distributions for different values in a 3-armed bandit
  3. Compare regret curves of BLASTS vs. Thompson Sampling on Bernoulli and Gaussian bandits with varying capacities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do capacity-limited agents balance exploration and exploitation in complex environments with a large number of actions or states?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses how rate-distortion theory can be used to identify learning targets that balance simplicity and utility, but does not provide concrete guidance on how to apply this framework to environments with high-dimensional action or state spaces.
- Why unresolved: Theoretical results are primarily focused on simpler environments (multi-armed bandits and episodic MDPs) and do not address challenges of scaling to more complex settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating performance of capacity-limited agents in complex environments with large action or state spaces, comparing their performance to traditional exploration strategies.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do capacity-limited agents handle uncertainty in the environment that is not captured by their current beliefs or models?
- Basis in paper: The paper assumes agents have access to a prior distribution over the environment, but does not address how agents should update beliefs or models when faced with unexpected or novel information.
- Why unresolved: Framework does not explicitly consider possibility of model uncertainty or need for agents to adapt beliefs in response to new information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing how capacity-limited agents perform in environments with significant model uncertainty, and how they adapt beliefs and learning strategies in response to new information.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do capacity-limited agents compare to other resource-rational models of decision-making, such as those based on heuristics or satisficing?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses relationship between capacity-limited agents and other models of bounded rationality, but does not provide direct comparison of their performance or behavior.
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on theoretical foundations and does not provide empirical evidence to support claims about advantages over other models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing performance of capacity-limited agents to other resource-rational models in variety of decision-making tasks, measuring factors such as accuracy, efficiency, and robustness.

## Limitations
- Assumes mutual information between beliefs and actions is valid proxy for cognitive load, which may not hold in domains requiring minimal processing regardless of belief complexity
- Computational tractability relies on Monte Carlo approximations that may fail in high-dimensional or complex environments
- Theoretical regret bounds may be too loose for practical guidance in real-world applications

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Information bottleneck optimization): Medium - Strong theoretical foundation but limited empirical validation across diverse domains
- Mechanism 2 (BLASTS algorithm): Low - Novel computational approach with weak corpus support and unknown convergence properties
- Mechanism 3 (Regret bounds): Medium - Theoretically sound but practical utility depends on tightness of bounds
