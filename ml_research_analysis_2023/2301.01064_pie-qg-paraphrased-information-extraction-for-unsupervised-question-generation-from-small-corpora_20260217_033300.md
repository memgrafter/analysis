# PIE-QG: Paraphrased Information Extraction for Unsupervised Question Generation from Small Corpora

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.01064
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01064
- **Reference count**: 9
- **Key outcome**: PIE-QG achieves on-par performance with state-of-the-art unsupervised methods while requiring an order of magnitude fewer passages, improving EM by 2.5% and F1 by 1.9% over sentence retrieval baselines.

## Executive Summary
PIE-QG introduces a method for generating synthetic training data for extractive question answering (EQA) systems using only a small corpus of text, eliminating the need for external knowledge sources or large training datasets. The approach combines paraphrasing to avoid lexical overlap, Open Information Extraction (OpenIE) to extract subject-predicate-object triples, and multiple filtering steps to produce high-quality question-answer pairs. Experiments across five EQA datasets demonstrate that PIE-QG achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art unsupervised methods while requiring significantly fewer passages.

## Method Summary
PIE-QG operates through a pipeline of paraphrasing, information extraction, and filtering. First, it paraphrases each sentence in the corpus multiple times using a multilingual paraphraser to create lexical diversity. Then, OpenIE extracts subject-predicate-object triples from these paraphrased sentences. A filtering process removes low-quality triples and generates answer candidates by identifying named entities or noun phrases. Finally, it generates questions from the remaining triples using a pre-trained model fine-tuned on SQuAD, creating a dataset of high-quality question-answer pairs for training EQA models.

## Key Results
- Achieves 2.5% improvement in Exact Match (EM) and 1.9% improvement in F1 scores compared to sentence retrieval-based baselines
- Produces comparable results to iterative data refinement methods while using 8× fewer passages
- Matches state-of-the-art unsupervised method performance on five EQA datasets while requiring an order of magnitude fewer passages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PIE-QG works by generating diverse paraphrases of input sentences, then extracting structured information triples from these paraphrases. This approach avoids the lexical overlap between questions and answers that can make questions too easy to answer. The filtering process ensures only high-quality triples with appropriate answer candidates are retained, while the question generation step transforms these triples into natural language questions. By leveraging pre-trained models and focusing on quality over quantity, PIE-QG creates effective training data without requiring large corpora or external knowledge sources.

## Foundational Learning
- **Paraphrasing**: Needed to create lexical diversity and avoid simple pattern matching between questions and answers; quick check: compare vocabulary overlap between original and paraphrased sentences
- **Open Information Extraction (OpenIE)**: Extracts structured triples from text without requiring predefined schemas; quick check: verify extracted triples contain valid subject-predicate-object relationships
- **Named Entity Recognition (NER)**: Identifies answer candidates within extracted triples; quick check: ensure all named entities and noun phrases are correctly tagged
- **Question Generation**: Transforms structured triples into natural language questions; quick check: validate generated questions are grammatically correct and answerable from the provided context
- **Extractive QA Evaluation**: Measures model performance using Exact Match and F1 metrics; quick check: ensure evaluation scripts correctly identify answer spans in context

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Paraphraser -> OpenIE -> Triple Filter -> Answer Candidate Selector -> Question Generator -> QA Model

**Critical Path:**
Paraphraser → OpenIE → Triple Filter → Answer Candidate Selector → Question Generator → QA Model Training

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Balance between paraphrase diversity (more paraphrases = better coverage but higher computational cost) vs. quality
- OpenIE system selection (accuracy vs. computational efficiency)
- Filtering strictness (removing low-quality triples vs. preserving potentially useful examples)
- Question generation model choice (pre-trained vs. custom-trained)

**Failure Signatures:**
- Low-quality questions indicate poor OpenIE extraction or inadequate filtering
- Questions too similar to answers suggest insufficient paraphrasing diversity
- Missing valid questions indicates overly strict filtering criteria
- Poor QA performance despite good question quality may indicate mismatch between generated data distribution and target domain

**3 First Experiments:**
1. Test paraphraser quality by measuring vocabulary overlap between original and paraphrased sentences
2. Validate OpenIE extraction by manually checking triple quality on a sample of paraphrased sentences
3. Assess filtering effectiveness by comparing number and quality of triples before and after filtering

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How would PIE-QG perform on corpora in languages other than English where the required external modules (paraphrasing, OpenIE, NER) may not be available?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the downside of PIE-QG is the use of external modules that may not exist in languages other than English, and suggests this is future work to apply these modules to other languages.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not present any experiments or results on corpora in languages other than English.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying PIE-QG to corpora in other languages with comparable language-specific models for paraphrasing, OpenIE, and NER, and analyzing the performance outcomes.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal number of paraphrases to generate per sentence to maximize QA performance without over-fitting?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions generating "ten different paraphrases" per sentence but does not explore whether this is optimal or how it affects performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not conduct experiments varying the number of paraphrases per sentence to determine if fewer or more would yield better results.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments systematically varying the number of paraphrases per sentence (e.g., 1, 5, 10, 20) and measuring the resulting QA performance to identify an optimal number.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of PIE-QG change when using different pre-trained language models (e.g., T5, XLNet) instead of BERT?
- Basis in paper: The paper uses BERT-based models but does not explore whether other pre-trained language models would yield better or worse results.
- Why unresolved: The paper only experiments with BERT-based models and does not compare performance with other pre-trained language models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments fine-tuning different pre-trained language models (T5, XLNet, etc.) with the PIE-QG generated training data and comparing their QA performance.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses primarily on extractive question answering datasets, limiting generalizability to other QA formats
- Performance claims lack comparison with supervised baselines, making it difficult to assess absolute performance ceiling
- Heavy dependence on OpenIE quality, which can vary significantly across domains and text types

## Confidence
- Claims about performance improvements: Medium confidence (limited baseline comparison, no absolute performance metrics)
- Claims about reduced data requirements: Medium confidence (needs more specific comparison details)
- Claims about quality improvements through filtering: Low confidence (limited analysis of filtering impact)
