# Probing Out-of-Distribution Robustness of Language Models with Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.11660
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11660
- **Reference count:** 9
- **Primary result:** Parameter-efficient transfer learning methods (Adapter, LoRA, Prefix-tuning) outperform fine-tuning on large auto-regressive language models for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution intent classification tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness of large language models using parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) methods. The study evaluates fine-tuning, Adapter, LoRA, and prefix-tuning on auto-regressive language models (GPT2 variants, GPT-Neo, GPT-J) across three intent classification tasks. Key findings include: (1) Model size correlates with OOD detection performance without parameter updates, but remains inferior to fine-tuned smaller models; (2) Parameter-efficient methods (PETLs) outperform fine-tuning on large models for both in-distribution (IND) and OOD tasks; (3) Traditional representation-based OOD detection methods (e.g., Mahalanobis distance) perform poorly on auto-regressive models compared to logit-based approaches, likely due to the absence of [CLS] embeddings. The research demonstrates that scaling language models improves OOD robustness but requires specialized detection methods for optimal performance.

## Method Summary
The study compares four parameter-efficient transfer learning methods (Fine-tuning, Adapter, LoRA, Prefix-tuning) on auto-regressive language models (GPT2-S/M/L/XL, GPT-Neo, GPT-J) for intent classification. Three datasets are used: CLINC150 (far-OOD) and Banking77 (close-OOD). Models are evaluated using in-distribution classification accuracy and OOD detection metrics (FPR@95, AUROC) with four detection methods (MSP, Energy, Mahalanobis, Cosine). Experiments test different model sizes and parameter ratios (0.1%, 0.5%, 1%) to identify optimal configurations for OOD robustness.

## Key Results
- Larger PLM sizes improve OOD detection without parameter updates, but fine-tuned smaller models still outperform
- PETL methods outperform fine-tuning on large PLMs for both IND and OOD tasks
- Logit-based OOD detection methods are more effective than representation-based methods for auto-regressive models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Scaling PLM size improves OOD detection without parameter updates.
- **Mechanism:** Larger models capture richer representations from training data, leading to more discriminative features for outlier detection.
- **Core assumption:** The increased model capacity allows better extraction of distributional characteristics without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Model size correlates with OOD detection performance without parameter updates, but remains inferior to fine-tuned smaller models"
  - [section] "As the size of the PLM increases, the performance improves without any update of model parameters"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support for this specific mechanism; most related work focuses on parameter-efficient adaptation rather than pure scaling effects.
- **Break condition:** If the training data lacks diversity or the OOD distribution is too far from IND, scaling alone may not provide sufficient discrimination.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) methods outperform full fine-tuning on large PLMs for OOD detection.
- **Mechanism:** PETL methods preserve pre-trained knowledge while adapting task-specific features, maintaining better distributional awareness for OOD detection.
- **Core assumption:** The regularization effect of PETL prevents overfitting to IND data, preserving generalization to unseen distributions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Parameter-efficient methods (PETLs) outperform fine-tuning on large models for both in-distribution (IND) and OOD tasks"
  - [section] "PETLs outperform fine-tuning with sufficiently large PLMs in both IND and OOD metrics"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support; related work focuses on parameter efficiency for performance, not specifically OOD robustness.
- **Break condition:** If PETL methods use too few parameters or inappropriate adaptation strategies, they may lose critical discriminative features.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Logit-based OOD detection methods work better than representation-based methods for auto-regressive PLMs.
- **Mechanism:** Auto-regressive PLMs lack [CLS] embeddings, making the last token embedding less suitable for holistic representation-based detection.
- **Core assumption:** The final token in auto-regressive models is optimized for next-token prediction rather than capturing sentence-level semantics.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "leveraging the information of the last hidden representation... does not transfer well in auto-regressive PLM, requiring a novel representation extracting technique"
  - [section] "decoder models (e.g., GPT and its variants) do not have [CLS] embedding, which assembles the token embeddings to capture holistic information"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus support; most related OOD detection work focuses on bidirectional models with [CLS] tokens.
- **Break condition:** If alternative pooling strategies or representation extraction methods are developed for auto-regressive models, representation-based methods might become viable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the size of pre-trained language models (PLMs) directly correlate with improved out-of-distribution (OOD) detection performance when model parameters are not updated during fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "As the size of the PLM increases, the performance improves without any update of model parameters" but also notes that "their performances still lag far behind compared to the fine-tuned small PLM (i.e., BERT-base)."
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows correlation, it does not definitively establish causation or determine the exact point of diminishing returns for OOD detection as model size increases.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying PLM sizes and measuring OOD performance with and without parameter updates, controlling for other variables, would establish the relationship.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can logit-based evaluation methods for OOD detection be effectively adapted for use with auto-regressive PLMs like GPT variants?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "leveraging the information of the last hidden representation...does not transfer well in auto-regressive PLM, requiring a novel representation extracting technique" and that logit-based methods outperform representation-based ones.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but does not provide a solution for extracting meaningful representations from auto-regressive models for OOD detection.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing new pooling or representation extraction methods specifically designed for auto-regressive models, then comparing their OOD detection performance to existing methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal number of trainable parameters for parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) methods when applied to large PLMs for OOD detection tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper conducts experiments with 0.1%, 0.5%, and 1% of PLM parameters but notes "With sufficient parameters to reach maximum performance, there is no meaningful difference or improvement within each methodology."
- Why unresolved: While the paper tests different parameter ratios, it does not determine the optimal balance between parameter efficiency and OOD detection performance across different model scales and tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying parameter ratios across different PLM sizes and tasks, measuring both computational efficiency and OOD detection performance, would identify optimal parameter ratios.

## Limitations
- Limited to intent classification tasks, which may not generalize to other NLP domains
- Does not explore alternative representation extraction methods for auto-regressive models
- Hyperparameter sensitivity of PETL methods is not thoroughly investigated

## Confidence
- **High Confidence:** Larger model sizes improve OOD detection without parameter updates; PETL methods outperform fine-tuning on large models; Logit-based methods show better performance than representation-based methods for auto-regressive PLMs
- **Medium Confidence:** The specific ranking of PETL methods; the magnitude of performance improvements across different model sizes; the generalizability of findings to other NLP tasks
- **Low Confidence:** The exact mechanisms by which model scaling improves OOD detection; the long-term stability of PETL-based OOD detection across model updates; the performance of alternative representation extraction methods not explored in this study

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain validation:** Evaluate the OOD detection performance on non-intent classification tasks (e.g., sentiment analysis, question answering) to assess generalizability beyond the current experimental setup.

2. **Alternative representation methods:** Implement and test advanced pooling strategies (e.g., attention-based pooling, hierarchical representations) for auto-regressive models to determine if representation-based methods can match or exceed logit-based performance.

3. **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis:** Conduct systematic experiments varying learning rates, bottleneck dimensions, and prefix lengths across all PETL methods to quantify their impact on OOD detection performance and identify optimal configurations.
