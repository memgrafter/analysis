# A Comprehensive Survey on Pretrained Foundation Models: A History from BERT to ChatGPT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09419
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09419

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive survey of Pretrained Foundation Models (PFMs), tracing their evolution from BERT to ChatGPT. It examines the fundamental components and pretraining methods used in natural language processing, computer vision, and graph learning. The survey covers advanced PFMs for different data modalities and unified approaches that consider data quality and quantity. It highlights PFMs' remarkable achievements across AI fields while discussing critical challenges in model efficiency, security, and privacy. The paper aims to guide future research toward developing PFMs with improved scalability, reasoning ability, cross-domain learning, user-friendly interaction, and security-preserving capabilities for artificial general intelligence.

## Method Summary
The survey follows a comprehensive review methodology, synthesizing research on Pretrained Foundation Models across multiple modalities. The approach involves analyzing the historical development of PFMs from early transformer-based models like BERT through to more recent ChatGPT architectures. The survey examines pretraining methods including self-supervised learning tasks such as masking, contrastive learning, and denoising autoencoders. It covers implementation across text, image, graph, speech, and video data, discussing both modality-specific approaches and unified PFMs. The review methodology includes examining transfer learning effectiveness, where models pretrained on large-scale unlabeled data are fine-tuned for downstream tasks using task-specific metrics.

## Key Results
- PFMs have achieved remarkable performance improvements across diverse AI domains through transfer learning from large-scale pretraining
- Advanced PFMs demonstrate capabilities across multiple data modalities including text, image, graph, speech, and video
- Key challenges remain in model efficiency, security, privacy preservation, and development of artificial general intelligence capabilities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Pretrained Foundation Models work by leveraging large-scale unlabeled data to learn general-purpose representations that can be transferred to specific downstream tasks. The self-supervised pretraining process allows models to capture rich semantic and structural patterns without requiring manual annotations. When fine-tuned on smaller labeled datasets for specific tasks, these pretrained representations provide strong inductive biases that accelerate learning and improve generalization. The transformer architecture's attention mechanisms enable the models to capture long-range dependencies and complex relationships within the data. This transfer learning paradigm effectively amortizes the cost of learning fundamental patterns across many tasks, making it computationally efficient to adapt to new applications.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Input data → Pretraining task → Transformer backbone → Learned representations → Fine-tuning → Downstream task

**Critical Path:**
The critical path is the pretraining phase, where the model learns general-purpose representations from large-scale data. This phase determines the quality of features available for subsequent fine-tuning and directly impacts downstream task performance.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Model size vs. computational efficiency: Larger models capture more complex patterns but require more resources
- Pretraining data diversity vs. domain specificity: General pretraining enables broad applicability but may lack specialized knowledge
- Task complexity vs. pretraining objectives: Simple tasks train faster but may not capture nuanced relationships
- Fine-tuning strategy vs. task adaptation: Full fine-tuning offers better performance but risks overfitting with limited data

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor downstream performance despite successful pretraining indicates mismatch between pretraining tasks and target domain
- Catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning suggests inadequate regularization or insufficient pretraining
- Slow convergence on downstream tasks indicates suboptimal pretraining data or objectives
- Security vulnerabilities reveal insufficient consideration of adversarial robustness during pretraining
