# Revealing Similar Semantics Inside CNNs: An Interpretable Concept-based Comparison of Feature Spaces

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07663
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07663
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Methods for quantifying semantic concept similarity across CNN layers reveal that similar concepts emerge at similar relative depths regardless of architecture

## Executive Summary
This work introduces interpretable methods to compare semantic information encoded in CNN latent spaces across different architectures. By leveraging concept activation vectors (CAVs) and non-negative CAVs (NCAVs), the authors develop both supervised and unsupervised approaches to estimate layer-wise similarity of semantic concepts. Experiments on three object detectors and two datasets demonstrate that semantic concepts emerge at similar relative depths across architectures, enabling meaningful cross-model comparisons.

## Method Summary
The method extracts or generates concept-related training data, then mines CAVs/NCAVs for each layer in each network using TCAV and ICE techniques. Supervised similarity is measured via cosine similarity and Pearson correlation coefficient of concept activation rankings, while unsupervised similarity uses Jaccard index on binarized concept masks. The approach produces heatmaps of layer-wise semantic similarity across architectures, enabling insights into how different CNNs process semantic information.

## Key Results
- Semantic concepts emerge at similar relative depths across different CNN architectures, independent of total network depth
- Similar semantic concepts are learned regardless of the CNN architecture used
- Datasets with high semantic diversity lead to lower quality of automatic concept extraction results

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Layer-wise similarity of semantic concepts emerges independent of total network depth
- Mechanism: Semantic concepts are learned in a relative depth position within the CNN backbone, regardless of the total number of layers in the architecture
- Core assumption: The CNN architecture preserves the hierarchical flow of semantic information such that similar concepts emerge at similar relative depths
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "similar concepts emerge in similar relative layer depth, independent of the total number of layers"
  - [section]: "Therefore, to compare different CNN backbones, it seems to be sufficient to compare only several layers that are uniformly distributed in the backbone"
- Break condition: If a network uses a fundamentally different information flow (e.g., transformers or recurrent architectures), the relative depth assumption may fail

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Semantic comparability can be quantified using concept activation vectors (CAVs) and non-negative CAVs (NCAVs)
- Mechanism: CAVs and NCAVs act as global vector representations in the latent space, enabling quantitative comparison of semantic concept responses across layers and architectures
- Core assumption: The latent space directions corresponding to semantic concepts are stable and comparable across different CNN backbones
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we use two renowned explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques, which are used to obtain concept activation vectors"
  - [section]: "CAV is the normal vector to the separation hyperplane and indicates the direction of the concept in the latent space"
- Break condition: If the concept representations are highly non-linear or distributed across many dimensions, linear CAVs may not capture semantic similarity accurately

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Datasets with semantically homogeneous samples yield higher quality concept extraction than highly diverse datasets
- Mechanism: When input samples contain a narrow set of concepts, ICE can more reliably mine interpretable concepts and their corresponding layers
- Core assumption: The semantic diversity of the test dataset directly affects the quality of automatic concept extraction
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "datasets with high semantic variability may lead to a lower quality of automatic concept extraction results"
  - [section]: "we recommend using datasets with semantically homogeneous samples, similar to those found in CelebA"
- Break condition: If concept mining techniques are adapted to handle high diversity (e.g., clustering-based approaches), this limitation may be mitigated

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing (concept mining, synthetic concept generation) -> Core comparison engine (supervised/unsupervised similarity) -> Output (heatmaps of layer-wise similarity)
- Critical path: 1) Extract or generate concept-related training data, 2) Mine CAVs/NCAVs for each layer in each network, 3) Generate concept masks or compute cosine similarities on test data, 4) Compute layer-wise similarity scores, 5) Visualize and interpret results
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using 1D vs 3D CAVs: 1D is computationally efficient and translation invariant but may lose spatial detail
  - Synthetic vs real concept data: Synthetic data is easier to generate but may not reflect real-world distributions
  - Binarization threshold BT: Affects mask size and similarity scores; needs tuning per dataset
- Failure signatures:
  - Low similarity scores across all layers: May indicate poor concept mining or mismatched datasets
  - Inconsistent patterns across architectures: Could signal different semantic learning dynamics or need for relative depth normalization
  - High sensitivity to BT: Suggests concept masks are not robust; consider non-binary comparison methods
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run ICE on CelebA and MS COCO to compare concept mask quality and interpretability
  2. Compute supervised similarity using synthetic concepts on both datasets to validate relative depth hypothesis
  3. Test robustness of results to different BT values and dimensionality choices (1D vs 3D CAVs)

## Limitations
- Reliance on synthetic concept data may not reflect real-world semantic distributions
- Assumption of similar relative depth emergence may not hold for non-CNN architectures
- Performance depends on quality of concept mining, which degrades with dataset semantic diversity

## Confidence
- High confidence in the methodological framework for comparing CNN latent spaces
- Medium confidence in the relative depth independence claim, pending broader architecture testing
- Medium confidence in dataset semantic diversity effects, as corpus evidence is limited
