# Self-Paced Absolute Learning Progress as a Regularized Approach to Curriculum Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05769
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05769
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: SPALP combines Absolute Learning Progress with self-paced regularization, achieving performance comparable to standard ALP-GMM while often reaching it faster

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Self-Paced Absolute Learning Progress (SPALP), a curriculum learning method that combines Absolute Learning Progress (ALP) with self-paced regularization to improve reinforcement learning efficiency. SPALP applies a squashing function to rewards based on their magnitude, favoring tasks with higher rewards during curriculum sampling. The regularization strength is dynamically controlled by parameter α, which decreases as the mean reward approaches a target bound. Experiments across three environments demonstrate that SPALP achieves performance comparable to standard ALP-GMM, often reaching the same level faster. The method's effectiveness depends heavily on the reward landscape structure and the choice of reward bound hyperparameter.

## Method Summary
SPALP extends ALP-GMM by applying a squashing function to rewards that compresses low rewards more than high rewards, amplifying the ALP calculated from high-reward tasks. The squashing function f_α(x) = -α(1 - exp(x/α)) focuses sampling on tasks where the agent already performs well. The regularization parameter α is dynamically adjusted based on mean reward, with the constraint that mean squashed reward equals target bound rb. When mean rewards approach rb, α increases, weakening squashing and allowing broader exploration. SPALP uses the same reward history, GMM fitting, and sampling mechanism as ALP-GMM but modifies the ALP calculation through reward squashing.

## Key Results
- SPALP achieves performance comparable to standard ALP-GMM across three tested environments
- SPALP often reaches ALP-GMM performance faster, particularly in environments where high-reward tasks correlate with learning progress
- Performance is highly sensitive to the reward bound hyperparameter rb, with different values needed for optimal results in different environments

- Claim: SPALP improves curriculum learning efficiency by applying a squashing function to rewards, making learning progress more sensitive to high-reward tasks.
- Mechanism: The squashing function compresses low rewards more than high rewards, amplifying ALP calculated from high-reward tasks. This focuses sampling distribution on tasks where agent already performs well.
- Core assumption: High-reward tasks are more likely to yield genuine learning progress than low-reward tasks.

- Mechanism: α chosen such that mean squashed reward equals target bound rb. As mean rewards approach rb, α increases, weakening squashing and allowing broader exploration.
- Core assumption: As agent learns, it should transition from focused exploitation to broader exploration.

- Break condition: If rb is poorly chosen, agent may oscillate between exploration and exploitation or get stuck in local optima.

- Claim: SPALP achieves performance comparable to ALP-GMM by maintaining same semantic structures while improving sampling efficiency.
- Mechanism: SPALP uses same reward history and GMM-based sampling as ALP-GMM but modifies ALP calculation through reward squashing.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Reward History -> ALP Calculator -> GMM Fitter -> Task Sampler -> Policy Optimizer

- Critical path: 1. Sample task from current GMM or uniformly, 2. Execute task, receive reward, 3. Update reward history, 4. Calculate ALP (with or without squashing), 5. Fit new GMM based on ALPs, 6. Update policy

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Reward squashing vs. exploration: Stronger squashing focuses on high-reward tasks but may miss important low-reward tasks
  - Reward bound rb: Higher values allow more exploration but may slow learning; lower values focus learning but may get stuck
  - Reward history size: Larger histories provide more stable ALPs but may slow adaptation to new tasks

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance despite tuning: May indicate inappropriate reward squashing or poorly chosen rb
  - Oscillating between exploration and exploitation: May indicate rb is too close to achievable rewards
  - Slow learning: May indicate insufficient exploration or overly strong reward squashing

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic ALP-GMM and verify it matches published results on toy environment
  2. Add reward squashing with fixed α and verify it focuses sampling on high-reward tasks
  3. Implement dynamic α adjustment based on mean reward and test different rb values on bipedal walker environment

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of SPALP depend on the specific structure and reward landscape of the environment?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that SPALP's advantage depends on the structure of the reward landscape in each environment, and that it works better in some environments than others.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide systematic analysis or criteria for predicting when SPALP will outperform ALP-GMM based on environment's characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive study across diverse set of environments with varying reward structures, coupled with formal analysis of conditions under which SPALP's regularization leads to improved learning efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for dynamically adjusting the reward bound hyperparameter rb during training to maximize performance?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses possibility of lowering rb each time it is reached, like a ladder, to lessen sampling disadvantage of lower reward subspaces, but does not implement or evaluate this strategy.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores fixed values of rb and does not investigate dynamic adjustment strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance of SPALP with various dynamic rb adjustment strategies against fixed rb and ALP-GMM across multiple environments.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size and implementation of the reward history buffer affect the performance of SPALP, and what is the optimal configuration?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests that longer reward history might help teacher remember progress in different parts of parameter space, potentially mitigating on-off problem, but does not systematically investigate impact of buffer size or alternative implementations.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses fixed-size FIFO buffer without exploring how different buffer sizes or implementations influence SPALP's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Series of experiments varying reward history buffer size and implementation, measuring impact on SPALP's learning efficiency and comparing results to ALP-GMM.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness highly sensitive to reward bound hyperparameter rb, requiring careful tuning for each environment
- Assumes high-reward tasks correlate with learning progress, which may not hold in environments with deceptive reward structures
- Experiments show performance comparable to ALP-GMM but don't demonstrate clear superiority, raising questions about whether added complexity is justified

## Confidence

- **High**: SPALP's mechanism of applying reward squashing to amplify high-reward tasks is clearly specified and mathematically sound
- **Medium**: SPALP achieves performance comparable to ALP-GMM in tested environments, though advantage depends on reward landscape structure
- **Low**: SPALP's superiority over ALP-GMM is claimed but not conclusively demonstrated across all tested scenarios
