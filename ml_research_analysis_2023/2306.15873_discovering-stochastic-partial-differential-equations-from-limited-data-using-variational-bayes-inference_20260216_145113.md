# Discovering stochastic partial differential equations from limited data using variational Bayes inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15873
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15873
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First framework for discovering SPDEs from limited noisy data using variational Bayes with spike-and-slab priors

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first framework for discovering stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) from limited noisy data. The method combines extended Kramers-Moyal expansion for SPDEs with variational Bayesian sparse learning using spike-and-slab priors. The approach independently identifies drift and diffusion terms by expressing them as weighted combinations of candidate basis functions and applying sparse Bayesian inference. Tested on three canonical SPDEs (stochastic heat equation, Allen-Cahn equation, and Nagumo equation), the method accurately recovers the underlying equations with limited data.

## Method Summary
The proposed method extends Kramers-Moyal expansion to handle limited noisy data and applies variational Bayesian inference with spike-and-slab priors to achieve sparse representation of SPDE terms. The framework represents drift and diffusion terms as weighted combinations of candidate basis functions, then uses sparse Bayesian learning to identify the most relevant terms. This approach enables simultaneous discovery of both deterministic (drift) and stochastic (diffusion) components of SPDEs from limited observations.

## Key Results
- Successfully recovered three canonical SPDEs (stochastic heat equation, Allen-Cahn equation, Nagumo equation) from limited data
- Variational Bayesian framework achieved better or comparable accuracy to Gibbs sampling
- Demonstrated significant computational efficiency improvements over existing approaches
- Method accurately identified both drift and diffusion terms independently

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging the extended Kramers-Moyal expansion to connect statistical moments of the stochastic process to SPDE terms, while the variational Bayesian framework with spike-and-slab priors enables sparse identification of relevant basis functions. This combination allows the algorithm to distinguish signal from noise and identify the most important terms even with limited data.

## Foundational Learning
- Kramers-Moyal expansion: Essential for connecting stochastic process moments to SPDE terms; quick check: verify moment calculations match theoretical expectations
- Spike-and-slab priors: Enable sparse selection of relevant basis functions; quick check: confirm sparsity pattern matches true model structure
- Variational Bayes inference: Provides computational efficiency over sampling methods; quick check: compare convergence speed with Gibbs sampling
- Sparse Bayesian learning: Critical for identifying relevant terms from candidate basis functions; quick check: validate coefficient recovery accuracy

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Data → Preprocessing → Kramers-Moyal expansion → Basis function generation → Variational Bayes inference → SPDE discovery

**Critical Path:** Noisy data → Moment estimation → Basis function selection → Sparse inference → Final equation recovery

**Design Tradeoffs:** Variational Bayes vs Gibbs sampling (computational efficiency vs theoretical guarantees), spike-and-slab priors vs other sparse priors (sparsity vs flexibility)

**Failure Signatures:** Overfitting with too many basis functions, poor moment estimation with limited data, incorrect noise model assumptions

## Limitations
- Validation limited to three canonical equations with specific noise characteristics
- Assumption of Gaussian noise structure may not generalize to all physical systems
- Computational scalability to high-dimensional SPDEs remains untested
- Spike-and-slab prior selection may introduce bias toward certain functional forms
