# Self-Distillation for Gaussian Process Regression and Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02641
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02641
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces data-centric and distribution-centric approaches to self-distillation for Gaussian Process Regression and Classification.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first formulation of knowledge distillation specifically for Gaussian Process models. The authors introduce two approaches: data-centric, where models are refitted using deterministic predictions from previous iterations, and distribution-centric, where the full probabilistic posterior is reused as the prior. For Gaussian Process Regression, the data-centric approach relates closely to self-distillation of kernel ridge regression, while the distribution-centric approach corresponds to ordinary GPR with a specific hyperparameter choice. For Classification, the data-centric method requires redefining the model to use continuous Bernoulli distribution for well-specification.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces two distillation approaches for Gaussian Processes: data-centric and distribution-centric. In data-centric distillation, the model is iteratively refitted using deterministic predictions (mean predictions for GPR, continuous Bernoulli targets for GPC) from the previous iteration. For GPR, this corresponds to kernel ridge regression self-distillation with progressive regularization. For GPC, it requires switching from Bernoulli to continuous Bernoulli likelihood. In distribution-centric distillation, the full posterior from each iteration becomes the prior for the next, which for GPR is equivalent to a single fit with scaled noise parameter, and for GPC can be efficiently approximated by scaling the covariance function.

## Key Results
- Data-centric GPR relates to self-distillation of kernel ridge regression with progressive regularization through eigenvalue shrinkage
- Distribution-centric GPR is equivalent to ordinary GPR with a scaled noise parameter 1/γ⁻ᵗ⁻¹
- Data-centric GPC requires continuous Bernoulli likelihood to handle continuous predictions in [0,1]
- Distribution-centric GPC can be efficiently approximated by scaling the covariance function

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Data-centric self-distillation for GPR iteratively regularizes the mean prediction by progressively shrinking basis functions with smallest eigenvalues the most. Each distillation step uses mean predictions from the previous step as training targets, creating a recursion where the posterior mean at step t is a weighted combination of kernel evaluations with coefficients that depend on the eigenvalues of the kernel matrix and the noise parameters γs.

Core assumption: The kernel matrix K is positive semi-definite and has a spectral decomposition K = OΛO⊺ with eigenvalues λ_i.

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract] "the data-centric approach for GPR closely relates to known results for self-distillation of kernel ridge regression"
- [section] "It can be shown that the behavior of yt as t increases corresponds to a progressively sparsifying the underlying basis functions by shrinking the basis functions with smallest eigenvalues the most"

Break condition: If K is singular or has eigenvalues very close to zero, the spectral decomposition approach may fail or become numerically unstable, breaking the progressive regularization effect.

### Mechanism 2
Distribution-centric self-distillation for GPR is equivalent to performing ordinary GPR with a scaled noise parameter 1/γ⁻ᵗ⁻¹ where γ⁻ᵗ = Σₛ₌₀ᵗ 1/γₛ. The posterior at each step is a GP with updated mean and covariance functions. By recursively applying the update equations, the solution can be shown to match a single GPR fit with a composite noise parameter that accumulates the inverse noise parameters from all previous steps.

Core assumption: The posterior distribution of a GP is itself a GP, allowing the posterior to be used as a prior in the next iteration.

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract] "the distribution-centric approach for GPR corresponds to ordinary GPR with a very particular choice of hyperparameters"
- [section] "performing multiple steps of distribution-centric self-distillation is equivalent to fitting an ordinary GP with a particular choice of noise parameter"

Break condition: If the noise parameters γt vary significantly across steps, the accumulated parameter γ⁻ᵗ⁻¹ may become dominated by small values, leading to numerical instability or over-regularization.

### Mechanism 3
Data-centric self-distillation for GPC requires switching from Bernoulli to continuous Bernoulli likelihood to handle continuous predictions in [0,1]. When using mean predictions from a GPC model as targets, the values are continuous in [0,1] rather than binary. The continuous Bernoulli distribution has a normalizing constant C(λ) that allows it to model such continuous targets, whereas the standard Bernoulli is undefined for non-binary values.

Core assumption: The continuous Bernoulli distribution is a valid likelihood for modeling continuous values in [0,1] with the appropriate normalizing constant.

Evidence anchors:
- [abstract] "the data-centric approach for GPC requires redefining the model from a Binomial likelihood to a continuous Bernoulli likelihood to be well-specified"
- [section] "since the original observations, y0, are in {0, 1}^N and the predictions, ŷ1, are in [0, 1]^N, directly reusing the predictions... yields a misspecified model"

Break condition: If the continuous predictions from the teacher model fall outside [0,1] due to numerical issues or model misspecification, the continuous Bernoulli likelihood becomes undefined.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data-centric GPR: Spectral decomposition of kernel matrix → iterative mean prediction updates → regularization through eigenvalue shrinkage
  - Distribution-centric GPR: Recursive posterior-to-prior updates → equivalent to single GPR with scaled noise parameter
  - Data-centric GPC: Laplace approximation → continuous Bernoulli likelihood → iterative model fitting on continuous predictions
  - Distribution-centric GPC: Approximate method using scaled covariance function → iterative posterior updates

- Critical path:
  1. For data-centric GPR: Compute spectral decomposition of K once, then perform efficient iterative updates using diagonal matrices
  2. For distribution-centric GPR: Single GPR fit with appropriately scaled noise parameter
  3. For data-centric GPC: Iteratively fit models using Laplace approximation and continuous Bernoulli likelihood
  4. For distribution-centric GPC: Use scaled covariance approximation method

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Data-centric vs distribution-centric: Data-centric offers explicit regularization control through γt parameters but requires iterative fitting; distribution-centric is computationally equivalent to single fit but offers less direct control
  - Continuous Bernoulli vs thresholding: Continuous Bernoulli handles information in continuous predictions but may overfit; thresholding simplifies to binary targets but loses information
  - Naïve vs efficient implementation: Naïve iterative fitting scales linearly with steps; efficient spectral method is constant time per step after initial decomposition

- Failure signatures:
  - Numerical instability in spectral decomposition (eigenvalues too small or negative)
  - Over-regularization when γt values accumulate to very large γ⁻ᵗ⁻¹
  - Misspecification when continuous predictions fall outside [0,1] in data-centric GPC
  - Poor approximation quality in distribution-centric GPC when Laplace approximation is inadequate

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement data-centric GPR with synthetic data and verify that mean predictions show progressive regularization across distillation steps
  2. Implement distribution-centric GPR and confirm equivalence to single fit with scaled noise parameter by comparing results
  3. Implement data-centric GPC with continuous Bernoulli and demonstrate that it handles continuous predictions better than standard Bernoulli with thresholding

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of kernel function affect the convergence and properties of self-distillation in Gaussian Processes?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on Gaussian Processes with a radial basis kernel, but does not extensively explore how different kernel choices impact self-distillation outcomes.
- Why unresolved: The paper primarily uses a scaled radial basis kernel for its experiments, leaving the effects of other kernel types (e.g., Matérn, polynomial) unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing self-distillation results across various kernel functions and their hyperparameters would provide insights into how kernel choice influences the distillation process.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the data-centric self-distillation approach be extended to work with non-Gaussian likelihoods, such as Poisson or Gamma distributions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the extension of data-centric self-distillation to Gaussian Process Classification (GPC) using the continuous Bernoulli distribution, but does not explore other non-Gaussian likelihoods.
- Why unresolved: The paper's focus on Gaussian Processes with Bernoulli and continuous Bernoulli likelihoods leaves the applicability of data-centric self-distillation to other non-Gaussian likelihoods untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing data-centric self-distillation methods for other non-Gaussian likelihoods, such as Poisson or Gamma, would demonstrate the approach's versatility.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the theoretical guarantees for the convergence of self-distillation in Gaussian Processes, and under what conditions do these guarantees hold?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper presents empirical results and theoretical connections between self-distillation and known results for kernel ridge regression, but does not provide formal convergence guarantees.
- Why unresolved: While the paper establishes some connections and empirical observations, it does not derive formal convergence theorems or conditions under which self-distillation in Gaussian Processes is guaranteed to converge.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving convergence theorems for self-distillation in Gaussian Processes, possibly under specific assumptions on the kernel, data, or hyperparameters, would provide theoretical guarantees for the method's effectiveness.

## Limitations
- The equivalence between distribution-centric distillation and single GPR fit with scaled noise parameter lacks empirical validation on real-world datasets
- Continuous Bernoulli likelihood implementation details for GPC are not fully specified, particularly boundary behavior
- Optimal scheduling of noise parameters γt across distillation steps is left to user discretion without clear guidance

## Confidence
- High confidence: GPR data-centric distillation mechanism and its relationship to kernel ridge regression
- Medium confidence: GPR distribution-centric equivalence claims and GPC continuous Bernoulli implementation
- Low confidence: Empirical performance claims on real datasets and optimal hyperparameter scheduling
