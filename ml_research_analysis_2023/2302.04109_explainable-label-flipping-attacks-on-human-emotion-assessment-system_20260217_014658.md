# Explainable Label-flipping Attacks on Human Emotion Assessment System

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04109
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04109
- Reference count: 0

## Executive Summary
This study investigates label-flipping poisoning attacks on EEG-based emotion assessment systems using Adaptive Boosting (AdaBoost) and Random Forest classifiers. The attacks systematically mislabel training samples across four emotion categories at poisoning rates of 5%, 25%, 50%, and 75%. Results demonstrate that AdaBoost significantly outperforms Random Forest in both baseline accuracy and resilience to poisoning attacks. The research employs Explainable AI techniques, particularly Permutation Importance, to analyze how attacks affect model behavior and feature contributions, revealing that Pz electrode features have the greatest impact on emotion classification.

## Method Summary
The study uses EEG data from 17 participants collected via Emotiv Insight 5-channel device, extracting 25 features from five brainwave bands across five electrodes. Data is split 80/20 for training and testing. Label-flipping attacks are implemented by randomly reassigning labels in the training set at specified poisoning rates. Both AdaBoost and Random Forest classifiers are trained on poisoned and clean data, with performance evaluated using accuracy, recall, precision, F1-score, and log loss metrics. Permutation Importance analysis is applied to understand feature contributions under different attack scenarios.

## Key Results
- AdaBoost maintains significantly higher accuracy than Random Forest across all poisoning rates
- AdaBoost shows superior resilience to label-flipping attacks, degrading from 99.68% to 21.61% versus Random Forest's 90.97% to 14.52%
- Pz electrode features demonstrate the largest impact on emotion classification outcomes
