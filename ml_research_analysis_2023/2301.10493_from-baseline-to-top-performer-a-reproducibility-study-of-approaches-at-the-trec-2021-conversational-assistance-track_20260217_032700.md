# From Baseline to Top Performer: A Reproducibility Study of Approaches at the TREC 2021 Conversational Assistance Track

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10493
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10493
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Neither baseline nor top system could be fully reproduced due to missing implementation details; best-effort implementation achieved 12-21% relative margin of reported results

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the reproducibility of TREC systems by attempting to reproduce both the organizers' baseline and the top-performing participant submission from the 2021 TREC Conversational Assistance track. The study reveals that key practical implementation details were missing from the published descriptions, making full reproduction impossible. The authors implemented best-effort versions of both systems and conducted additional experiments to understand the impact of different pipeline components on end-to-end performance.

## Method Summary
The authors attempted to reproduce two systems: a baseline using BM25 retrieval with T5-based query rewriting, and a top system using dense retrieval (ANCE) with query rewriting. Due to missing information about specific algorithms, models, and input construction procedures, they implemented best-effort versions. They then conducted controlled experiments varying query rewriting methods (T5 CANARD vs T5 QReCC), retrieval techniques (BM25 vs ANCE/BM25 fusion), and reranking approaches (monoT5 vs duoT5) to understand their impact on conversational search performance.

## Key Results
- Neither baseline nor top system could be fully reproduced due to missing implementation details
- Best-effort implementation achieved 12-21% relative margin of reported results
- Advanced retrieval techniques (ANCE/BM25 fusion, duoT5 reranking) consistently improved performance by 3-29% across metrics
- Stage-specific query rewriting benefits were observed: T5 CANARD for first-pass retrieval, T5 QReCC for re-ranking

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Different query rewriting methods can be beneficial in different stages of the retrieval pipeline
- Mechanism: T5 CANARD improves first-pass retrieval recall while T5 QReCC yields better final NDCG when used in re-ranking
- Core assumption: The rewriter's dataset (CANARD vs QReCC) influences its effectiveness differently for retrieval vs reranking
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we measure the impact of the dataset used for fine-tuning the query rewriter and find that employing different query rewriting methods in different stages of the retrieval pipeline might be beneficial"
  - [section 5.2]: "Using T5 CANARD for first-pass retrieval results in the highest recall. However, the overall best combination in terms of final ranking (NDCG@3) is when T5 QReCC is employed in first-pass retrieval and T5 CANARD is used in re-ranking"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus neighbors discuss query rewriting but not stage-specific differences
- Break condition: If the rewriter's effectiveness is primarily determined by architecture rather than training data, the stage-specific benefit may not hold

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Advanced retrieval techniques improve end-to-end performance when applied at either stage
- Mechanism: Using ANCE/BM25 fusion and duoT5 reranker consistently improves recall and NDCG over BM25+monoT5 baseline
- Core assumption: Dense retrieval and pairwise reranking provide complementary signal to sparse retrieval
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1]: "using more advanced retrieval methods (ANCE/BM25 instead of BM25 for first-pass ranking and mono/duoT5 instead of monoT5 for re-ranking) does yield consistent improvements across metrics and datasets: +12%–29% in recall and +3%–12% in NDCG@3"
  - [abstract]: "end-to-end system performance can indeed benefit from advanced retrieval techniques in either stage of a two-stage retrieval pipeline"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus neighbors don't discuss retrieval technique combinations
- Break condition: If the collection statistics or query distribution don't benefit from dense retrieval signal, improvements may not materialize

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Missing practical implementation details significantly impact reproducibility
- Mechanism: Variations in preprocessing, tokenization, parameter tuning, and model inputs create performance gaps
- Core assumption: Small differences in implementation details compound across pipeline stages
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4]: "neither approach could be fully reproduced due to key information missing... key missing information includes the names of specific algorithms and models used, and detailed-enough descriptions of procedures of constructing inputs to neural models"
  - [section 4.4]: "Overall, according to the track overview paper, the relative differences between BaselineOrganizers and WaterlooClarke are 18% and 37% in terms of NDCG@3 and Recall@500, respectively... However, the respective differences in our reproduced approaches are 5% and 7%"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus neighbors don't discuss reproducibility challenges
- Break condition: If all implementation details were shared, reproducibility would be complete regardless of other factors

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Query rewriting module → First-pass retrieval → Reranking stage → Final ranking
- Critical path: Query rewriting → First-pass retrieval → Reranking → Final ranking
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single vs different query rewriters per stage (complexity vs performance)
  - Dense vs sparse vs hybrid first-pass retrieval (speed vs effectiveness)
  - Pointwise vs pairwise reranking (simplicity vs ranking quality)
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor first-pass recall indicates query rewriting or retrieval configuration issues
  - Low NDCG despite good recall suggests reranking stage problems
  - Large gaps between reported and reproduced results point to missing implementation details
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reproduce baseline with reported parameters to establish baseline performance
  2. Switch query rewriter dataset (CANARD → QReCC) to measure impact on each stage
  3. Replace BM25 with ANCE/BM25 fusion in first-pass retrieval to test effectiveness gains

## Limitations
- Neither baseline nor top system could be fully reproduced due to missing implementation details
- Missing information includes specific algorithm names, model configurations, and input construction procedures
- Best-effort implementation achieved only 12-21% relative margin of reported results

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The finding that key practical information was missing, making full reproducibility impossible. This is directly supported by the authors' systematic comparison with original system descriptions.

**Medium confidence**: The performance gap measurements (12-21% relative margin) are reliable within the constraints of best-effort implementation, though exact baselines remain uncertain.

**Medium confidence**: The experimental results showing benefits of advanced retrieval techniques and stage-specific query rewriting approaches, though these conclusions are based on the authors' implementations rather than exact reproductions.
