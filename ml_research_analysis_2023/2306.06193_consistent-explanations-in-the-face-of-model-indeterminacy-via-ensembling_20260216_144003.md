# Consistent Explanations in the Face of Model Indeterminacy via Ensembling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06193
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06193
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Ensemble methods significantly improve explanation consistency across identically performing models by exploring model indeterminacy through local perturbations and global mode connectivity paths

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the critical problem of inconsistent explanations arising from model indeterminacy, where multiple models with identical performance produce divergent explanations. The authors demonstrate that even small variations in random seed initialization can lead to dramatically different LIME explanations, undermining trust and interpretability in machine learning systems. To combat this, they propose ensemble methods that systematically explore the underspecification set of models through two complementary strategies: local weight perturbations and global mode connectivity paths in the loss landscape. Their approach achieves significant improvements in explanation similarity while maintaining test accuracy, with the combined method reducing the number of required pre-trained models by up to fivefold compared to naive ensembling.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach combines two exploration strategies to generate explanation-consistent ensembles. First, local weight perturbations involve adding small random noise to the weights of a pre-trained model, exploring nearby points in the loss landscape. Second, global mode connectivity uses linear interpolation between pre-trained models to find paths of low loss connecting different optima. The ensemble then aggregates predictions and explanations from this diverse set of models. For explanation aggregation, they employ a majority voting scheme weighted by each model's loss on a held-out set, ensuring that better-performing models have greater influence on the final explanation. This dual exploration strategy systematically samples the underspecification set to find models that agree on important features while maintaining competitive predictive performance.

## Key Results
- Explanation similarity (cosine distance) improved by up to 40% on financial datasets using ensemble methods
- Combined local and global exploration reduced required pre-trained models by up to fivefold compared to naive ensembling
- Maintained test accuracy while significantly improving explanation consistency across LIME, SHAP, and gradient-based explanations
- Demonstrated that model indeterminacy leads to inconsistent explanations even among identically performing models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Model indeterminacy creates an underspecification set where multiple models achieve identical performance but make different predictions on specific instances due to random initialization and optimization paths. These subtle differences in decision boundaries lead to varying importance attributions when generating explanations. By exploring this underspecification set through controlled perturbations and connectivity paths, the ensemble method identifies models that agree on which features are important for specific predictions. The aggregation process then emphasizes features that receive consistent attribution across the ensemble, effectively filtering out noise and artifacts that arise from individual model idiosyncrasies.

## Foundational Learning

**Model indeterminacy**: The phenomenon where multiple models achieve identical performance metrics but differ in their internal representations and predictions on specific instances. Why needed: Understanding this concept is crucial because it explains why identically performing models can produce contradictory explanations. Quick check: Verify that two models with the same test accuracy produce different predictions on at least some validation samples.

**Loss landscape exploration**: Systematic examination of the parameter space to find alternative optima and paths between them. Why needed: Provides the theoretical foundation for why perturbing weights or interpolating between models can yield new models with similar performance. Quick check: Confirm that interpolated models maintain low loss values between known optima.

**Explanation similarity metrics**: Quantitative measures like cosine distance to compare feature importance attributions across different models. Why needed: Enables objective evaluation of whether ensemble methods actually improve explanation consistency. Quick check: Calculate cosine similarity between LIME explanations from different models on the same instances.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Pre-trained models -> Weight perturbation module -> Mode connectivity interpolator -> Explanation generator (LIME/SHAP/gradient) -> Similarity calculator -> Weighted voting aggregator -> Final explanation and prediction

**Critical path**: Model training -> Exploration (local perturbations + global interpolation) -> Explanation generation -> Similarity computation -> Weighted aggregation -> Output

**Design tradeoffs**: Local perturbations offer fine-grained exploration but require careful tuning of perturbation magnitude; global interpolation provides broader exploration but depends on availability of diverse pre-trained models. The weighted voting scheme balances explanation consistency against model performance but introduces additional hyperparameters.

**Failure signatures**: If perturbation magnitude is too large, explanations become inconsistent and performance degrades; if too small, insufficient exploration occurs. Poor mode connectivity paths can lead to high-loss intermediates that harm both accuracy and explanation quality. Incorrect weighting can cause majority voting to amplify noisy explanations from underperforming models.

**First experiments**: 1) Compare explanation consistency between single models with different random seeds on a simple binary classification task; 2) Apply local perturbations with varying magnitudes and measure impact on explanation similarity; 3) Test global interpolation between two pre-trained models and evaluate whether intermediate models maintain both performance and improved explanation consistency.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to five financial datasets, potentially limiting generalizability to other domains
- No user studies to validate whether improved explanation similarity translates to better interpretability or trust
- Computational overhead of ensembling not addressed, particularly during inference time
- Theoretical connections between loss landscape properties and explanation consistency remain largely empirical

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Model indeterminacy leads to inconsistent explanations across identically performing models (robust empirical demonstration across five datasets and multiple explanation methods)

**Medium confidence**: Ensemble strategies effectively improve explanation consistency (results show improvement in similarity metrics, but practical significance unclear without user validation)

**Low confidence**: Combined approach reduces required pre-trained models by up to fivefold (comparison baseline unclear, savings depend heavily on specific dataset characteristics and hyperparameter choices)
