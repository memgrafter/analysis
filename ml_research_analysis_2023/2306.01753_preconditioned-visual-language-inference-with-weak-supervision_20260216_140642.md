# Preconditioned Visual Language Inference with Weak Supervision

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01753
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01753
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: SOTA VLMs perform below random on preconditioned visual language inference, showing the task's difficulty and novelty

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PVLIR, a task for preconditioned visual language inference and rationalization that requires models to determine whether a precondition (represented as an image) allows or prevents a commonsense statement from being true. The authors propose three weak supervision strategies to generate training data from image-caption datasets without costly manual annotation. Experiments show that while fine-tuning improves performance above random baselines, state-of-the-art VLMs still fall significantly short of human-level performance, highlighting the challenging nature of visual language reasoning with preconditions.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce PVLIR (Preconditioned Visual Language Inference and Rationalization), a task requiring models to reason about whether preconditions (visual representations) allow or prevent commonsense statements from being true. To address the lack of training data, they propose three weak supervision strategies: Extraction from Captions (extracting instances from PNLI datasets), Caption Querying (matching PNLI statements to similar captions), and Image Querying (matching images to PNLI statements). These strategies generate large-scale training data from image-caption datasets. The authors then fine-tune four state-of-the-art VLMs (ViLBERT, ViLT, FLAVA, CLIP) on this data and evaluate performance on a human-verified test set of 261 instances, showing that models perform below random before fine-tuning but improve significantly afterward, though still below human performance.

## Key Results
- SOTA VLMs (ViLBERT, ViLT, FLAVA, CLIP) perform below random on PVLIR before fine-tuning
- Fine-tuning on weak supervision data improves performance above random baselines
- Models still fall short of human-level performance even after fine-tuning
- Providing ground-truth rationales boosts FLAVA accuracy from 80.43% to 94.2%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Weak supervision strategies enable scalable data generation for preconditioned visual language tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Three strategies (Extraction from Captions, Caption Querying, Image Querying) extract or match PNLI instances from large image-caption datasets, producing large volumes of training examples without costly manual annotation.
- **Core assumption:** Image captions and PNLI preconditions/actions have sufficient semantic overlap for matching via linguistic patterns or vector similarity.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "propose a learning resource based on three strategies to retrieve weak supervision signals for the task"
  - [section] "Extraction from Captions method that results in 34K extracted instances"
  - [corpus] Corpus signals show related work on affordance and visual-language alignment, but no direct evidence that captions contain precondition-action pairs.
- **Break condition:** If captions rarely encode preconditions/actions, matching strategies yield noisy or sparse supervision.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning SOTA VLMs on weak supervision data improves performance on preconditioned inference.
- **Mechanism:** Pre-trained VLMs (ViLBERT, ViLT, FLAVA, CLIP) are fine-tuned on extracted PVLIR instances; performance improves from random to above-chance levels.
- **Core assumption:** VLMs have learned transferable multimodal representations that can be adapted to new inference tasks with modest supervision.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Fine-tuning improves performance, but models still fall short of human-level performance"
  - [section] "After fine-tuning, the models' performance improves above the random baseline"
  - [corpus] Corpus signals indicate these VLMs are strong in general vision-language tasks but not preconditioned inference.
- **Break condition:** If the weak supervision is too noisy or mismatched, fine-tuning may overfit spurious patterns without learning the intended reasoning.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Generated rationales can improve preconditioned inference accuracy.
- **Mechanism:** Free-text rationale generation (using Ayyubi et al. 2020) produces textual explanations; providing ground-truth rationales boosts FLAVA accuracy to 94.2% vs 80.43% without rationales.
- **Core assumption:** Rationales capture task-relevant constraints that guide model inference beyond raw visual-text pairs.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "state-of-the-art visual language models perform below random on the task"
  - [section] "The inference accuracy of the FLAVA-rationale-gold is 94.2"
  - [corpus] Corpus signals lack evidence of visual-language rationale generation models, suggesting novelty of this approach.
- **Break condition:** If rationales are not faithful or relevant, they may mislead the model rather than help.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Precondition-action relations in commonsense knowledge
  - **Why needed here:** PVLIR requires understanding that a precondition (e.g., broken glass) can prevent or allow an action (e.g., drinking water).
  - **Quick check question:** Given "A glass is used for drinking water" and an image of a shattered glass, is the action allowed or prevented?

- **Concept:** Weak supervision and labeling functions
  - **Why needed here:** The work relies on automatically extracting training data from image captions using regex patterns.
  - **Quick check question:** What pattern would extract "Swimming pools have cold water in winter unless heated" as an action-precondition pair?

- **Concept:** Visual-language model fine-tuning
  - **Why needed here:** VLMs are adapted from pretraining to the PVLIR task using weak supervision data.
  - **Quick check question:** Why does fine-tuning on PVLIR improve accuracy compared to zero-shot inference?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  PNLI datasets (ANION, PaCo, ATOMIC, δ-NLI, WINOVENTI) -> Image-caption datasets (CC12M, CC3M, MS COCO, VizWiz) -> Weak supervision strategies (Extraction from Captions, Caption Querying, Image Querying) -> PVLIR instances -> VLMs (ViLBERT, ViLT, FLAVA, CLIP) -> Fine-tuning -> Human-verified test set (261 instances) -> Performance evaluation

- **Critical path:**
  1. Preprocess PNLI and captions
  2. Apply weak supervision strategies
  3. Filter by precision thresholds
  4. Fine-tune VLMs
  5. Evaluate on human-verified test set

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Large weak supervision → more data but noisier
  - Multiple labeling functions → better coverage but lower precision
  - Rationale generation adds complexity but improves performance

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Random or near-random model accuracy → weak supervision too noisy or mismatched
  - High bias toward text modality → insufficient visual grounding
  - Rationale generation fails → modality misalignment in training

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run extraction from captions, measure precision and yield
  2. Fine-tune FLAVA on noisy PVLIR, track accuracy per epoch
  3. Generate rationales with Ayyubi et al. 2020, evaluate impact on inference accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the impact of using automatically generated captions versus human-annotated captions in the weak supervision methods?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper mentions the limitation of image captioning datasets in breadth and depth, and suggests investigating the use of automatically generated captions as a future extension.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not provide any experimental results or comparisons between automatically generated captions and human-annotated captions in the weak supervision methods.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experimental results comparing the performance of the weak supervision methods using automatically generated captions versus human-annotated captions, and analysis of the trade-offs between data quality and quantity.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the performance of the PVLI task change with different levels of visual-linguistic alignment in the training data?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper discusses the importance of grounding preconditions and actions in images, and mentions the use of approximate nearest neighbors to find semantically similar captions for PNLI statements. However, it does not explicitly investigate the impact of varying levels of visual-linguistic alignment on task performance.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not conduct experiments to measure the effect of different levels of visual-linguistic alignment on the PVLI task performance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experimental results showing the performance of the PVLI task with training data that has varying degrees of visual-linguistic alignment, and analysis of the relationship between alignment and task performance.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How can the counterfactual debiasing technique be extended to handle multi-modal inputs in the PVLI task?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper discusses the use of counterfactual debiasing to identify and mitigate biases in the data and models, but only applies it to the text modality in the PVLI task.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore how the counterfactual debiasing technique can be adapted to handle both visual and textual inputs simultaneously in the PVLI task.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Development and experimental evaluation of a multi-modal counterfactual debiasing technique for the PVLI task, along with analysis of its effectiveness in reducing biases and improving model performance.

## Limitations
- Reliance on weak supervision signals that may introduce significant noise into training data
- Human-verified test set remains relatively small at 261 instances
- Challenging comparison between VLM performance and human-level reasoning due to limited human annotations

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** The observation that state-of-the-art VLMs perform below random on the PVLIR task is well-supported by experimental results across multiple model architectures
- **Medium confidence:** The effectiveness of weak supervision strategies in generating useful training data is supported by the improvement in model performance after fine-tuning, though the noisiness of the data remains a concern
- **Medium confidence:** The claim that rationale generation improves inference accuracy is supported by the FLAVA-rationale-gold results (94.2% vs 80.43%), but the generalizability of this approach across different model architectures requires further validation

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a detailed error analysis on the human-verified test set to identify whether model failures stem from weak supervision noise, visual modality limitations, or genuine reasoning gaps in understanding preconditions.
2. Test the fine-tuning approach with progressively cleaner subsets of the weak supervision data to determine the optimal precision-recall tradeoff and establish the minimum quality threshold needed for effective learning.
3. Implement a cross-modal ablation study where models are trained with either visual or textual inputs alone to quantify the contribution of each modality to preconditioned inference performance.
