# Wave-U-Net Discriminator: Fast and Lightweight Discriminator for Generative Adversarial Network-Based Speech Synthesis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13909
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13909
- Reference count: 0
- Replaces ensemble of discriminators with single U-Net structure while maintaining comparable speech quality

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Wave-U-Net discriminator, a novel single-discriminator architecture designed to replace traditional ensemble discriminators in GAN-based speech synthesis systems. The architecture leverages a U-Net structure with encoder-decoder components and skip connections to assess waveforms in a sample-wise manner while extracting multi-level features. Experimental results demonstrate that the Wave-U-Net discriminator achieves comparable speech quality to ensemble discriminators while being 2.31x faster and 14.5x more lightweight in HiFi-GAN neural vocoder applications, and 1.90x faster and 9.62x more lightweight in VITS end-to-end TTS systems.

## Method Summary
The Wave-U-Net discriminator employs a U-Net architecture with encoder-decoder structure and skip connections to process waveforms. Unlike traditional ensemble discriminators that use multiple discriminators operating at different scales, this single architecture can assess waveforms sample-wise while simultaneously extracting multi-level features. The encoder progressively downsamples the input waveform to capture hierarchical representations, while the decoder reconstructs features at multiple scales. Skip connections between corresponding encoder and decoder layers preserve fine-grained details lost during downsampling. This design enables the discriminator to evaluate both local waveform characteristics and global structural patterns within a single forward pass.

## Key Results
- Achieves comparable speech quality to ensemble discriminators in both HiFi-GAN and VITS systems
- Provides 2.31x faster inference speed compared to ensemble discriminators in HiFi-GAN
- Reduces parameter count by 14.5x in HiFi-GAN and 9.62x in VITS systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Wave-U-Net discriminator works by leveraging the inherent multi-scale feature extraction capability of U-Net architectures. The encoder-decoder structure with skip connections allows the discriminator to maintain both high-level semantic information and fine-grained local details in a single network. This eliminates the need for multiple specialized discriminators while preserving the ability to evaluate speech quality across different temporal scales. The sample-wise assessment capability ensures that the discriminator can provide detailed feedback on waveform characteristics at each time step, enabling more precise gradient updates during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input Waveform -> Encoder -> Bottleneck -> Decoder -> Multi-scale Feature Outputs

Critical path: The encoder processes the waveform through successive downsampling layers, extracting hierarchical features. These features are bottlenecked at the lowest resolution, then progressively upsampled through the decoder. Skip connections directly connect corresponding encoder and decoder layers, ensuring fine details are preserved throughout the network.

Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoff involves balancing the depth of the U-Net (which increases feature extraction capability) against computational efficiency. Deeper networks can capture more complex patterns but increase latency and parameter count. The skip connections improve feature preservation but add to the parameter count.

Failure signatures: Potential failure modes include discriminator collapse (where the discriminator becomes too strong and provides uninformative gradients), mode collapse in generated speech, and loss of fine-grained temporal details if skip connections are insufficient.
