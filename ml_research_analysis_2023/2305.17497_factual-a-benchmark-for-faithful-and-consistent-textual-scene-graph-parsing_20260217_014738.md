# FACTUAL: A Benchmark for Faithful and Consistent Textual Scene Graph Parsing

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.17497
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17497
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Key outcome**: FACTUAL significantly improves faithfulness and consistency in scene graph parsing, leading to better performance on downstream tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FACTUAL, a benchmark designed to address the critical issues of unfaithfulness and inconsistency in textual scene graph parsing. The authors propose FACTUAL-MR, a novel intermediate representation that uses rigorously defined literals (objects, attributes, quantifiers, verb+preposition pairs) and strict quality control during annotation. By re-annotating captions from the Visual Genome dataset with FACTUAL-MR, the paper creates a large-scale benchmark dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that a parser trained on FACTUAL significantly outperforms existing approaches in terms of faithfulness and consistency metrics. Additionally, the authors introduce SoftSPICE, a novel graph similarity metric that achieves state-of-the-art results when combined with the improved scene graph parser.

## Method Summary
The FACTUAL method involves creating a novel intermediate representation called FACTUAL-MR, which uses rigorously defined literals (objects, attributes, quantifiers, verbs, prepositions) to ensure consistency in annotations. The annotation process involves crowd workers who are trained to use predefined closed sets of labels for each semantic element, with predicates decomposed into verb+preposition pairs. Expert annotators perform post-processing and verification to ensure high-quality annotations. The FACTUAL-MR representation can be deterministically mapped to scene graphs, avoiding error propagation from syntactic parsing. The method is evaluated using both intrinsic metrics (SPICE F-score, Exact Set Match, faithfulness, consistency) and extrinsic metrics (image caption evaluation, zero-shot image retrieval).

## Key Results
- FACTUAL-MR improves faithfulness and consistency in scene graph parsing by using rigorously defined literals and strict quality control
- The parser trained on FACTUAL significantly outperforms existing approaches in faithfulness and consistency metrics
- FACTUAL leads to significant performance improvements in image caption evaluation and zero-shot image retrieval tasks
- The novel SoftSPICE metric achieves state-of-the-art results when combined with the improved scene graph parser

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FACTUAL-MR's strict definitions of literals eliminate semantic ambiguity in annotations
- Mechanism: Predefined closed sets of labels for semantic elements and decomposition of predicates into verb+preposition pairs prevent inconsistent representations
- Core assumption: Semantic ambiguity directly causes parser errors
- Evidence: Abstract and section 4.1 support the precision of FACTUAL-MR definitions
- Break condition: Overly restrictive label sets may fail to cover linguistic variations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Deterministic mapping preserves complete semantic information
- Mechanism: Semantic-first representation avoids error propagation from syntactic parsing
- Core assumption: Syntactic parsing introduces errors that propagate into scene graphs
- Evidence: Abstract and section 4.2 support deterministic mapping benefits
- Break condition: Complex linguistic constructions may not be fully captured

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Expert post-processing ensures high annotation quality
- Mechanism: Three expert annotators review all annotations to correct systematic errors
- Core assumption: Initial crowd annotations contain errors that experts can identify
- Evidence: Section 4.3 describes expert review process
- Break condition: Expert biases or subtle semantic errors may persist

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: FACTUAL-MR creation → Expert verification → Parser training → Evaluation on downstream tasks

- Critical path: FACTUAL-MR creation → Expert verification → Parser training → Evaluation on downstream tasks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Closed label sets vs linguistic coverage: Predefined labels ensure consistency but may miss rare expressions
  - Semantic-first vs syntactic approaches: Semantic representation avoids parsing errors but requires more complex annotation
  - Expert review vs scale: Expert verification improves quality but limits dataset size and increases cost

- Failure signatures:
  - Low parser faithfulness despite clean annotations → Mapping rules may not capture semantic nuances
  - High lexical diversity in parser outputs → Predefined label sets may be too broad or inconsistent
  - Poor downstream task performance → Scene graph representations may not capture task-relevant information

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify deterministic mapping: Test FACTUAL-MR to scene graph conversion on 100 examples
  2. Parser sensitivity analysis: Train parsers with varying proportions of FACTUAL data
  3. Consistency benchmark: Compare lexical diversity metrics between FACTUAL and baseline datasets

- Question: Is the parser robust enough to handle complex linguistic variations?
  - Basis: VG dataset mainly contains short sentences with simple patterns
  - Why unresolved: Robustness to complex variations hasn't been thoroughly investigated
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive evaluation on dataset with wide range of linguistic variations

## Limitations

- Label set coverage may be insufficient for rare or domain-specific expressions
- Limited empirical validation of expert review effectiveness through inter-annotator agreement metrics
- Performance improvements may be partially attributed to specific datasets and evaluation protocols
- Complex linguistic phenomena involving implicatures or metaphors may not be fully captured

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Core methodology and deterministic mapping are well-specified and theoretically sound
- Medium Confidence: Expert review effectiveness is supported but lacks empirical validation
- Medium Confidence: Downstream task improvements are significant but may be partially dataset-specific
