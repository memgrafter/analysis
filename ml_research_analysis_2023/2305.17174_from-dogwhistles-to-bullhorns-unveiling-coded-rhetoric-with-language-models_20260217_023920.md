# From Dogwhistles to Bullhorns: Unveiling Coded Rhetoric with Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17174
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17174
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: Computational investigation of dogwhistles using language models reveals limited detection capabilities and consistent toxicity evasion

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first large-scale computational investigation of dogwhistles—coded expressions that convey different meanings to broad and narrow audiences. The authors develop a new taxonomy of dogwhistles, curate a glossary of over 300 dogwhistles with contextual information, and analyze their historical usage in U.S. political speeches. They find that GPT-3 has limited ability to identify dogwhistles and their meanings, with performance varying by dogwhistle type and targeted group. The study also shows that hateful content containing dogwhistles consistently evades toxicity detection by Perspective API, highlighting online risks of such coded language.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a comprehensive taxonomy of dogwhistles through expert analysis and curate a dataset of over 300 dogwhistles with contextual information. They analyze historical usage patterns in U.S. political speeches and evaluate GPT-3's ability to identify dogwhistles using controlled prompts. The study also tests whether dogwhistles enable hateful content to evade detection by the Perspective API, using systematic testing of speech samples containing various dogwhistle types.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 demonstrates limited ability to identify dogwhistles and their meanings, with varying performance across different dogwhistle types and targeted groups
- Hateful content containing dogwhistles consistently evades detection by Perspective API, highlighting risks of coded language online
- The curated taxonomy and glossary of 300+ dogwhistles provides a foundational resource for future computational research on coded rhetoric

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study works by systematically combining computational methods with expert-curated knowledge. Language models like GPT-3 can process large amounts of political text to identify patterns in dogwhistle usage over time. The curated glossary provides ground truth for evaluation, while the taxonomy enables systematic classification of different dogwhistle types. By testing detection evasion across multiple dogwhistle categories, the research reveals systematic vulnerabilities in toxicity detection systems.

## Limitations
- Curated dataset of 300+ dogwhistles may introduce sampling bias toward certain types of coded rhetoric
- Taxonomy relies on expert judgment, making comprehensiveness difficult to assess
- Analysis limited to U.S. political speeches, limiting generalizability to other contexts
