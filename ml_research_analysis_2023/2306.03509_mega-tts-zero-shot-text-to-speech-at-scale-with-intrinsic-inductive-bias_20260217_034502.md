# Mega-TTS: Zero-Shot Text-to-Speech at Scale with Intrinsic Inductive Bias
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03509
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03509
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Mega-TTS outperforms state-of-the-art TTS systems on zero-shot TTS, speech editing, and cross-lingual TTS tasks, achieving speaker similarity score of 0.936 and pitch distance of 35.46 on LibriSpeech test-clean dataset

## Executive Summary
Mega-TTS introduces a zero-shot text-to-speech system that decomposes speech into content, timbre, prosody, and phase attributes, each modeled by specialized modules. The approach leverages a VQGAN-based acoustic model for spectrogram generation and a latent code language model (P-LLM) for prosody modeling, with phase information handled by a GAN-based vocoder. Trained on a large-scale multi-domain dataset (20K hours), the system demonstrates superior performance on zero-shot TTS tasks, speech editing, and cross-lingual synthesis compared to existing state-of-the-art approaches.

## Method Summary
The method decomposes speech into four attributes and models each with specialized modules. A VQGAN-based acoustic model generates spectrograms from text and speaker embeddings, while a latent code language model (P-LLM) captures prosody variations. Phase information is separated and reconstructed using a GAN-based vocoder. The system is trained on a large-scale multi-domain dataset (20K hours) and evaluated on unseen speakers, demonstrating zero-shot capabilities across multiple TTS tasks.

## Key Results
- Achieved speaker similarity score of 0.936 on LibriSpeech test-clean dataset
- Obtained pitch distance of 35.46, outperforming existing TTS systems
- Demonstrated superior performance on zero-shot TTS, speech editing, and cross-lingual TTS tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system's effectiveness stems from the decomposition of speech into independent attributes (content, timbre, prosody, phase) that are modeled separately with specialized architectures. The VQGAN-based acoustic model efficiently generates high-quality spectrograms while the P-LLM captures prosody variations in latent space. The separation of phase information and its reconstruction by a GAN-based vocoder ensures high-fidelity speech synthesis. This modular approach with appropriate inductive biases allows the model to generalize effectively to unseen speakers and languages in zero-shot scenarios.

Design tradeoffs: Modular decomposition vs. end-to-end training complexity
Failure signatures: Poor speaker similarity indicates timbre modeling issues; unnatural prosody suggests P-LLM problems

## Limitations
- Scalability of VQGAN-based acoustic model to larger datasets remains uncertain
- Limited perceptual evaluation details raise questions about consistency across diverse speaker populations
