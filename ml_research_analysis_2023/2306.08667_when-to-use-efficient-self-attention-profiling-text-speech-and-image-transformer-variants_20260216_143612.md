# When to Use Efficient Self Attention? Profiling Text, Speech and Image Transformer Variants

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.08667
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08667
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 38
- This paper investigates the efficiency of Transformer variants across text, speech, and vision modalities, identifying input length thresholds where efficient variants outperform vanilla models.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically profiles Transformer variants across text, speech, and vision modalities to determine when efficient attention mechanisms become beneficial. The study introduces L-HuBERT, a local-attention variant for speech, and conducts comprehensive profiling using latency, throughput, and memory metrics. The key finding is that efficient Transformers only outperform vanilla models at much longer sequence lengths than typically encountered in practice, with tipping points ranging from 1750-2000 tokens for text and speech, and 512 pixels for images.

## Method Summary
The study profiles vanilla and efficient Transformer variants (BERT, Longformer, Nyströmformer, HuBERT, L-HuBERT, ViT, Swin) using synthetic inputs of varying sequence lengths. Text sequences range from 10-560 tokens, speech from 1-50 seconds, and images from 32-1024 pixels. Profiling measures latency, throughput, and memory usage using Pytorch CUDA Events, torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated(), and torchinfo for parameter counts. The analysis includes layerwise breakdowns to identify bottlenecks and compares efficiency across different resource constraints.

## Key Results
- Efficient Transformer variants only become more efficient than vanilla models at sequence lengths much higher than typical dataset lengths (1750-2000 tokens for text/speech, 512 pixels for images)
- Non-self-attention components contribute significantly to computational costs, especially in speech architectures where they account for 58.8% of latency
- The optimal model choice depends on resource constraints and modality, with different efficiency metrics being relevant for different use cases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Efficient Transformer variants only outperform vanilla models at much longer sequence lengths than typical benchmarks.
- Mechanism: The quadratic complexity of self-attention creates a tipping point where efficient variants with linear or sub-quadratic complexity become faster. However, this tipping point is much higher than typical sequence lengths in existing datasets.
- Core assumption: The computational overhead of efficient attention mechanisms is negligible compared to the savings at longer sequences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We observe that these thresholds are (a) much higher than typical dataset sequence lengths"
  - [section 5.2] "We define the tipping point for each modality: the input length at which the variant becomes more efficient than the vanilla model. For text and speech, it is 1750 − 2000 tokens for inference latency and max-memory, greater than typical input lengths (Table 3)."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains related work on efficient Transformers but no direct tipping point analysis.
- Break condition: If the overhead of efficient mechanisms is too high, or if typical sequence lengths increase significantly, this mechanism may not hold.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Non-self-attention components contribute significantly to overall computational costs, especially in speech architectures.
- Mechanism: While self-attention has quadratic complexity, other components like embedding layers and feed-forward networks also have substantial computational costs that scale differently with sequence length.
- Core assumption: The cost of non-self-attention components is significant enough to impact the overall efficiency of the model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1] "However, since the average seq length for most text and speech tasks is less than 1000 tokens and most image datasets are used at a max dimension of 512, at these points, non-self-attention components take up 35%, 58.8% and 43.75% latency for NLP, speech and images."
  - [section 5.1] "HuBERT uses a CNN feature extractor with different strides and kernel sizes and consumes more time in the earlier CNN layers as opposed to later ones, as is visible in Figure 2, which shows darker shades i.e. earlier layers dominating the computation."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains related work on efficient Transformers but no direct analysis of non-self-attention components.
- Break condition: If self-attention becomes the dominant cost at typical sequence lengths, this mechanism may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The choice of the right model depends on resource constraints and modality.
- Mechanism: Different efficiency metrics (latency, throughput, memory) are relevant for different use cases, and the optimal model varies based on these constraints and the modality (text, speech, vision).
- Core assumption: Different use cases have different priorities for efficiency metrics, and these priorities vary by modality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We observe that these thresholds are (a) much higher than typical dataset sequence lengths and (b) dependent on the metric and modality"
  - [section 5.2] "Suppose that one is training models under a time constraint; then, throughput is the bottleneck and efficient models would not be a good fit. On the other hand, efficient models are useful for long context memory-constrained inference."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains related work on efficient Transformers but no direct analysis of resource constraints.
- Break condition: If all use cases prioritize the same efficiency metric, or if the optimal model is the same across modalities, this mechanism may not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Vanilla Transformer: Input embedding -> Positional embedding -> Self-attention -> Feed-forward (intermediate and output layers) -> Other layers (activations, layer normalization, etc.)
  - Efficient Transformer variants: Same as vanilla, but with different self-attention mechanisms (local attention, approximate attention, etc.)
  - Profiling components: Pytorch CUDA Events for time-based metrics -> torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated() for memory usage -> torchinfo for parameter counts

- Critical path:
  1. Understand the Transformer architecture and self-attention mechanism
  2. Familiarize yourself with the different efficiency metrics used in the paper
  3. Review the profiling methodology and the models being compared
  4. Analyze the results, focusing on the tipping points and the contribution of non-self-attention components

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Efficient attention mechanisms vs. vanilla self-attention: Linear/sub-quadratic complexity vs. quadratic complexity, but potentially higher overhead
  - Different efficiency metrics: Latency, throughput, and memory have different trade-offs and are relevant for different use cases
  - Modality-specific considerations: Speech models have additional components (CNN feature extractors) that impact efficiency

- Failure signatures:
  - Inefficient models: High latency, low throughput, high memory usage
  - Tipping points not reached: Efficient variants not outperforming vanilla models at typical sequence lengths
  - Non-self-attention components dominating: High latency or memory usage not explained by self-attention costs

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Profile a vanilla Transformer and an efficient variant at different sequence lengths, comparing latency, throughput, and memory usage
  2. Analyze the layerwise efficiency of the models, focusing on the contribution of non-self-attention components
  3. Vary the resource constraints (e.g., memory-limited vs. time-limited) and observe how the optimal model changes

## Limitations
- Analysis is primarily focused on HuggingFace and fairseq implementations, which may not capture the full spectrum of available efficient Transformer variants
- Synthetic data approach may not fully represent real-world data distributions and their impact on efficiency metrics
- Study focuses on specific model configurations, and scaling effects may alter the identified thresholds

## Confidence
**High Confidence:** The observation that efficient Transformer variants only become advantageous at sequence lengths significantly longer than typical benchmarks is well-supported by the empirical data presented.

**Medium Confidence:** The claim that non-self-attention components contribute significantly to computational costs, especially in speech architectures, is supported by layerwise analysis.

**Low Confidence:** The generalizability of tipping points across different hardware setups and model sizes remains uncertain.
