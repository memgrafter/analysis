# How do different tokenizers perform on downstream tasks in scriptio continua languages?: A case study in Japanese

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09572
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09572
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Morphological analyzers improve downstream performance in scriptio continua languages, with BPE and Unigram outperforming WordPiece as subword tokenizers.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how different tokenizer configurations affect the downstream performance of pretrained language models in scriptio continua languages, using Japanese as a case study. The authors conduct an extensive study comparing 15 different tokenizer combinations (4 morphological analyzers × 3 subword tokenizers + 3 without morphological analyzers) on seven downstream tasks. Their findings reveal that each task benefits from different morphological analyzers, and that Byte-Pair-Encoding and Unigram consistently outperform WordPiece as subword tokenizers regardless of the task type.

## Method Summary
The authors train 15 different tokenizers using Japanese Wikipedia data (10M sentences) with 30k vocabulary size. They pretrain BERT-base models (125M parameters) for 500k steps using masked language modeling on Japanese Wikipedia and CC-100 datasets. The tokenizers combine four morphological analyzers (MeCab, Juman++, Sudachi, Vaporetto) with three subword tokenizers (BPE, WordPiece, Unigram), plus three configurations without morphological analysis. They then evaluate all models on seven downstream tasks: MARC-ja, JSTS, JNLI, JSQuAD, JCommonsenseQA, NER, and Universal Dependencies Japanese-GSD.

## Key Results
- Morphological analyzers significantly improve downstream performance in scriptio continua languages
- Different downstream tasks have different optimal morphological analyzers (e.g., Juman++ for NER, Sudachi for UD)
- BPE and Unigram consistently outperform WordPiece as subword tokenizers across all tasks
- Tokenizers without morphological analysis suffer from high [UNK] token ratios and unstable pretraining

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Morphological analyzer -> Subword tokenizer -> Pretrained language model (BERT) -> Downstream tasks

- Critical path:
  1. Preprocess input text with a morphological analyzer.
  2. Apply subword tokenization to the morphologically analyzed text.
  3. Train or fine-tune a PLM using the tokenized text.
  4. Evaluate the PLM on downstream tasks.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a morphological analyzer vs. not using one: Morphological analyzers improve performance but add preprocessing complexity.
  - Choice of subword tokenizer: BPE and Unigram generally outperform WordPiece, but the optimal choice may depend on the specific task and corpus.
  - Vocabulary size: Larger vocabularies can capture more linguistic diversity but may increase computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor downstream performance: May indicate issues with the morphological analyzer, subword tokenizer, or their combination.
  - High proportion of [UNK] tokens: May suggest that the subword tokenizer is struggling to segment the input text effectively, especially when used without morphological analysis.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare the performance of a PLM using a morphological analyzer vs. one without on a simple downstream task like text classification.
  2. Evaluate the impact of different subword tokenizers (BPE, WordPiece, Unigram) on a task-specific PLM.
  3. Investigate the relationship between the vocabulary similarity of subword tokenizers and their downstream performance differences.

## Limitations

- Limited scope of morphological analyzers (only four Japanese-specific tools) may not generalize to other scriptio continua languages
- Exclusive focus on BERT-base architecture restricts conclusions about other PLM architectures
- Absence of statistical significance testing across downstream tasks makes it difficult to determine meaningful performance differences
- Lack of investigation into corpus domain specificity effects on tokenizer performance

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The finding that morphological analyzers generally improve downstream performance in scriptio continua languages is well-supported by the consistent improvements observed across multiple tasks. The superiority of BPE and Unigram over WordPiece for subword tokenization is also robustly demonstrated.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that different downstream tasks benefit from different morphological analyzers is supported by observed task-specific optimal choices, but lacks statistical validation. The correlation between vocabulary similarity and performance differences is observed but not rigorously tested.

**Low Confidence**: The suggestion that tokenizer effectiveness may vary by corpus domain is based on anecdotal evidence from limited task performance patterns rather than systematic investigation.
