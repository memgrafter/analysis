# BayLing: Bridging Cross-lingual Alignment and Instruction Following through Interactive Translation for Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10968
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10968
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 95% of GPT-4's translation quality and 89% of GPT-3.5-turbo's performance on general tasks through interactive translation fine-tuning

## Executive Summary
BayLing introduces a novel approach to enhance non-English language capabilities of large language models by leveraging interactive translation tasks. The method fine-tunes LLaMA on 160K interactive translation instruction pairs, successfully bridging cross-lingual alignment with instruction-following capabilities. The approach demonstrates that English proficiency and knowledge can be effectively transferred to other languages through high-quality language alignment, achieving near-parity with GPT-4 on translation tasks and strong performance on general instruction-following benchmarks.

## Method Summary
BayLing employs interactive translation fine-tuning on LLaMA using 160K proprietary instruction pairs to align languages while preserving instruction-following capabilities. The approach treats translation as an interactive task where the model learns to align languages through bidirectional instruction pairs. This alignment enables the transfer of English proficiency and knowledge to non-English languages, creating a model that performs well on both translation tasks and general instruction-following benchmarks across multiple languages.

## Key Results
- Achieves 95% of GPT-4's translation quality
- Matches 89% of GPT-3.5-turbo's performance on general tasks
- Demonstrates 96% interactive translation capability compared to GPT-3.5-turbo in human evaluation
- Strong performance on Chinese GaoKao and English SAT standardized tests

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BayLing's interactive translation approach works by creating bidirectional alignment between languages through paired instruction fine-tuning. This alignment process allows the model to map knowledge structures and reasoning patterns from English into target languages, effectively transferring capabilities rather than just translating text. The interactive nature of the translation tasks helps the model understand contextual nuances and maintain instruction-following capabilities across languages, creating a more robust multilingual model.

## Foundational Learning

**Cross-lingual Alignment**: Understanding how to map semantic structures between languages is essential for transferring capabilities. Quick check: Verify alignment quality through parallel sentence similarity metrics.

**Instruction Following Transfer**: The ability to transfer instruction-following patterns across languages determines generalization. Quick check: Test zero-shot instruction following in target languages.

**Interactive Translation**: Moving beyond static translation to interactive dialogue helps capture context and nuance. Quick check: Evaluate contextual consistency in multi-turn translation scenarios.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: LLaMA Base Model -> Interactive Translation Fine-tuning -> Aligned Multilingual Model -> Evaluation on Translation and General Tasks

**Critical Path**: The core innovation lies in the interactive translation fine-tuning stage, where bidirectional instruction pairs create the alignment that enables capability transfer. This step is critical as it determines whether English knowledge structures successfully map to target languages.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The approach trades the simplicity of direct translation fine-tuning for the complexity of interactive alignment, but gains stronger capability transfer. The proprietary nature of the 160K instruction pairs limits reproducibility but enables controlled evaluation.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor cross-lingual transfer may manifest as English-centric reasoning patterns or translation that preserves form but loses semantic equivalence. Models may also struggle with idiomatic expressions that don't align well between languages.

## Limitations
- Heavy focus on Chinese-English language pairs with limited coverage of other linguistic families
- Proprietary 160K instruction pairs restrict reproducibility and external validation
- Comparison primarily against GPT models without benchmarking against other open multilingual models
- Limited ablation studies to isolate the specific contribution of interactive alignment versus instruction-following improvements
