# Baselines for Identifying Watermarked Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18456
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18456
- Reference count: 2
- Key outcome: Introduces framework and baseline algorithms for detecting watermarks in large language models (LLMs) using statistical analysis of output tokens and logits.

## Executive Summary
This paper formalizes the problem of watermark identification in large language models and proposes three baseline algorithms to detect watermarks without requiring knowledge of underlying watermarking parameters. The authors develop methods based on analyzing distributions of output tokens and logits, demonstrating effectiveness at identifying watermarked models even at low watermark strengths. The work establishes a foundation for future research on watermark detection in LLMs, addressing the growing need for accountability and content authenticity in AI-generated text.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three watermark detection algorithms: RNG divergence using Kolmogorov-Smirnov test on token distributions, mean adjacent token differences on Lorenz curves, and δ-Amplification with bimodality testing. These methods analyze statistical properties of LLM outputs to identify watermarks without requiring access to watermarking parameters. The approaches exploit the fact that watermarking schemes modify token probability distributions in predictable ways, creating detectable statistical signatures across different prompts and model outputs.

## Key Results
- δ-Amplification algorithm successfully identified watermarked models at δ ≥ 5 using diverse prompts
- Statistical tests (Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Hartigan dip) effectively detected watermark-induced distributional differences
- Detection methods work across different prompt types while maintaining consistent performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The δ-Amplification algorithm detects watermarks by averaging logit distributions across prompts with a fixed suffix, amplifying the watermark's δ-mask effect while suppressing natural variation.
- Mechanism: By prompting with varied prefixes ending in "Now write me a story:", the algorithm exploits the fact that watermarking schemes only consider a fixed-size window of previous tokens (e.g., 5 tokens). This ensures the same green list partition across all prompts, while earlier tokens cause logit variation. Averaging these distributions amplifies the δ-induced band separation, making bimodality detectable via statistical tests.
- Core assumption: The watermark's green list depends only on a fixed-size previous token window, and natural variation across prompts is uncorrelated with the watermark's δ-mask.
- Break condition: If the watermarking scheme uses a variable-sized context window or the natural variation correlates with the watermark's δ-mask, the amplification effect will fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The mean adjacent token difference metric detects watermarks by measuring the average increase in logit value between adjacent tokens, which is directly influenced by the watermark's γ and δ parameters.
- Mechanism: For a Kirchenbauer-watermarked model, the watermark adds δ to a randomly selected subset of token logits (green list). This creates a predictable average logit increase that is proportional to γ and δ. By comparing this metric to an unmarked model, the watermark can be detected.
- Core assumption: The watermark's effect on logit values is consistent and measurable through the mean adjacent token difference.
- Break condition: If the watermark's effect on logit values is subtle or inconsistent, the mean adjacent token difference may not be sensitive enough to detect it.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The RNG divergence approach detects watermarks by measuring the statistical difference between random number distributions generated by watermarked and unmarked models.
- Mechanism: By prompting the model to generate random numbers and comparing the resulting distributions using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, the watermark's effect on the model's output distribution can be detected.
- Core assumption: The watermark introduces a statistically significant shift in the model's output distribution that can be detected by comparing distributions.
- Break condition: If the watermark's effect on the model's output distribution is too subtle or if the model's natural variation is too large, the statistical test may not be able to detect the watermark.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Prompt generator -> Model interface -> Distribution analyzer -> Statistical tester -> Result aggregator

- Critical path:
  1. Generate diverse prompts with fixed suffixes
  2. Query the LLM and retrieve logits or samples
  3. Compute statistics on the model's output distribution
  4. Apply statistical tests to detect the watermark
  5. Aggregate and interpret the results

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sensitivity vs. robustness: More sensitive methods may be more prone to false positives from natural variation
  - Logit access vs. black-box: Methods requiring logit access may be more powerful but less widely applicable
  - Single-shot vs. multi-shot: Single-shot methods are more efficient but may be less reliable

- Failure signatures:
  - False positives: The watermark detection method incorrectly identifies a non-watermarked model as watermarked
  - False negatives: The watermark detection method fails to identify a watermarked model
  - Performance degradation: The watermark detection method is too slow or resource-intensive for practical use

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate random number distributions from a known watermarked and unmarked model, and compare their statistical properties using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test
  2. Compute the mean adjacent token difference metric for a known watermarked and unmarked model, and compare the results
  3. Apply the δ-Amplification algorithm to a known watermarked model with varying δ strengths, and measure the detection rate and false positive rate

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation to one watermarking scheme (Kirchenbauer) with specific parameter ranges
- Reliance on statistical assumptions that may not hold across all LLM architectures and prompt types
- No analysis of robustness against potential adversarial countermeasures or adaptive attacks

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The core claim that watermark detection is feasible through statistical analysis of token distributions and logits. The mathematical foundations (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, Hartigan dip test) are well-established, and the three proposed algorithms follow logically from the watermarking mechanism described.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that δ-Amplification specifically can detect watermarks at δ ≥ 5 across diverse prompts. While the theoretical mechanism is sound, the paper provides limited empirical validation across different LLM architectures and prompt types.

**Low confidence**: The assertion that these methods constitute comprehensive "baselines" for watermark detection. The evaluation is limited to one watermarking scheme and does not explore robustness against adaptive countermeasures or varying model scales.

## Next Checks

1. Cross-scheme validation: Test the detection algorithms against at least two alternative watermarking schemes (beyond Kirchenbauer) to verify generalizability across different watermarking approaches.

2. Adversarial robustness testing: Evaluate detection performance when watermarked models employ countermeasures such as prompt-specific watermarking, context-aware watermark strength adjustment, or distribution smoothing techniques.

3. Real-world deployment assessment: Measure false positive rates on a large corpus of publicly available LLM outputs without known watermarking status to establish practical detection reliability thresholds.
