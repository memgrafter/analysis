# Understanding the World to Solve Social Dilemmas Using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11358
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11358
- Reference count: 6
- Multi-agent RL with world models achieves higher efficiency in sustainable resource management than model-free approaches

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether world models can help multi-agent reinforcement learning agents solve social dilemmas, specifically the common-pool resource appropriation problem. The authors compare DreamerV2, a world model-based algorithm, against on-policy (PPO) and off-policy (R2D2) model-free algorithms in a grid-world environment with apple resources. The key finding is that DreamerV2 achieves higher population efficiency through sustainable resource management by learning to encode both environmental dynamics (apple regrowth) and social dynamics (predicting other agents' intentions). This supports the hypothesis that world models facilitate the emergence of complex coordinated behaviors by allowing agents to understand both environmental and social dynamics.

## Method Summary
The study compares three RL algorithms in a multi-agent setting where agents must manage a shared resource (apples) sustainably. DreamerV2 uses a recurrent state-space model to learn a low-dimensional latent representation of the environment's dynamics, including other agents' behaviors. PPO and R2D2 serve as model-free baselines. All algorithms are trained for 2000 episodes in an environment with two agents and a single apple patch. The efficiency metric is calculated as the population's per-capita consumption (sum of rewards divided by number of agents). The environment is based on DeepMind's Melting Pot framework, with apple regrowth probability proportional to uneaten apples and laser beam interactions between agents.

## Key Results
- DreamerV2 achieves higher efficiency metrics (per-capita consumption) than both PPO and R2D2 over 2000 training episodes
- World models learned by DreamerV2 encode both environmental dynamics (apple regrowth patterns) and social dynamics (predicting other agents' intentions and behaviors)
- The learned representations enable agents to understand threats from other agents and coordinate behavior to prevent resource depletion

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: World models enable agents to predict environmental and social dynamics, which facilitates sustainable cooperation in social dilemmas.
- Mechanism: DreamerV2 learns a low-dimensional latent state representation of the environment's dynamics, including the behavior of other agents. This allows each agent to simulate future scenarios and make decisions that prevent resource depletion by coordinating actions with others.
- Core assumption: Agents can learn accurate world models that encode both environmental and social dynamics in a compact, low-dimensional latent space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "world models learnt by DreamerV2 were able to encode both environmental dynamics (e.g., apple regrowth patterns) and social dynamics (e.g., predicting other agents' intentions and behaviors)."
  - [section] "DreamerV2 encodes these dynamics in a sparse low dimensional discrete latent state and learns optimal behavior policies by using these representations instead of the real environment's observations."
- Break condition: If the world model fails to accurately predict other agents' intentions or environmental changes, the coordination mechanism breaks down, leading to resource depletion.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Discrete and sparse latent representations improve generalization and decision-making in complex social environments.
- Mechanism: By using discrete latent states, DreamerV2 ensures parsimony and simplicity, which allows agents to generalize better to novel situations and make more efficient decisions in dynamic social dilemmas.
- Core assumption: Discrete and sparse representations are more effective than continuous ones for encoding and utilizing world dynamics in multi-agent settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Ma et al. (2022) argue that parsimony is a cornerstone for the emergence of intelligence, and Gomez et al. (2022) showed the benefits of using discrete representations to facilitate the ability to generalize to novel situations."
- Break condition: If the discrete representation becomes too coarse, losing critical information needed for decision-making, the agent's performance will degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Understanding the intentions of other agents reduces conflicts and promotes cooperative behavior.
- Mechanism: DreamerV2's world model predicts other agents' future actions (e.g., consuming apples or using laser attacks), allowing an agent to adjust its strategy to avoid conflicts and coordinate resource use.
- Core assumption: Agents can learn to model and predict the behavior of other agents as part of their world model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The world models learnt by DreamerV2 were able to encode both environmental dynamics (e.g., apple regrowth patterns) and social dynamics (e.g., predicting other agents' intentions and behaviors)."
  - [section] "DreamerV2 is also able to model other agents' behavior by predicting their intentions of consuming the apples... the model also predicts future attacks to the other agents, proving that the model understands that it is beneficial to reduce the effective population."
- Break condition: If agents cannot accurately predict others' intentions, they may resort to non-cooperative strategies, leading to resource depletion.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Environment (grid-world with apple patches) -> Agent observations -> World Model (DreamerV2) -> Latent states -> Policy -> Actions -> Environment
- Critical path:
  1. Agent observes environment
  2. World model encodes observation into latent state
  3. Latent state is used to predict future states and rewards
  4. Policy selects action based on predicted outcomes
  5. Action is executed in the environment
  6. Feedback updates world model and policy
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Discrete vs. continuous latent states: Discrete states are more interpretable and may generalize better but might lose some information.
  - Model-based vs. model-free: Model-based (DreamerV2) can plan ahead but requires accurate world models; model-free is simpler but may not capture long-term dynamics.
  - Observation space size: Larger observations increase model complexity but provide more information.
- Failure signatures:
  - Inefficient resource use (high depletion rate)
  - Agents failing to predict each other's actions
  - Latent states not clustering by environmental or social features
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a single agent with DreamerV2 in the apple environment to verify basic functionality.
  2. Compare DreamerV2's performance with PPO and R2D2 on a simplified version of the environment.
  3. Visualize the latent states using t-SNE to confirm clustering by environmental and social features.

## Limitations

- The study only tests on a simple two-agent environment with one resource patch, limiting generalizability to more complex social scenarios
- The claimed benefits of discrete latent representations lack direct empirical validation within this work
