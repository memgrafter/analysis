# Disentangled Multi-Fidelity Deep Bayesian Active Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04392
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04392
- Reference count: 11
- Key outcome: D-MFDAL achieves superior prediction accuracy and sample efficiency on PDE benchmarks, particularly in early active learning stages with limited high-fidelity data

## Executive Summary
Disentangled Multi-Fidelity Deep Bayesian Active Learning (D-MFDAL) introduces a novel framework for learning surrogate models across multiple fidelities. The key innovation is a disentangled neural process architecture that separates latent observations into global and local components, avoiding error propagation from low-fidelity to high-fidelity representations. The method learns distributions over functions at each fidelity level and includes a multi-fidelity regularization term to align global observations across fidelities. An efficient greedy algorithm is designed for budget-constrained batch active learning.

## Method Summary
D-MFDAL builds on neural process architecture but introduces a disentangled approach where latent observations are separated into global components (shared across fidelity levels) and local components (specific to each fidelity). The model learns distributions over functions using Bayesian neural processes and includes a multi-fidelity regularization term based on Jensen-Shannon divergence to align global observations. A unified evidence lower bound (ELBO) incorporates both standard likelihood terms and the regularization term. For active learning, D-MFDAL employs a multi-fidelity look-ahead greedy (MF-LIG) acquisition function that considers the budget constraint and selects batches of informative samples across fidelity levels.

## Key Results
- D-MFDAL achieves lower normalized root mean squared error (nRMSE) compared to state-of-the-art methods on heat, Poisson, and fluid simulation tasks
- The method demonstrates superior sample efficiency, particularly in early active learning stages with limited high-fidelity data
- D-MFDAL shows improved robustness to unbalanced data distributions across fidelity levels compared to hierarchical approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Disentangled latent observations avoid error propagation
D-MFDAL separates latent observations into global (shared across fidelities) and local (fidelity-specific) components. This separation ensures that errors in low-fidelity representations don't contaminate high-fidelity representations, as information flows through the global component rather than through hidden representations at each fidelity level.

### Mechanism 2: Bayesian learning provides uncertainty quantification
By learning distributions over functions rather than point estimates, D-MFDAL provides uncertainty estimates that help avoid overfitting, especially when training data is limited. This probabilistic approach is more robust than deterministic methods that try to fit limited data with point estimates.

### Mechanism 3: Multi-fidelity regularization aligns cross-fidelity representations
The regularization term minimizes Jensen-Shannon divergence between posterior distributions across fidelity levels for the same scenarios. This encourages similar global latent representations across fidelity levels, improving the model's ability to transfer knowledge between different fidelities.

## Foundational Learning

  - Quick check: How does the ELBO formulation in D-MFDAL differ from standard NP ELBO formulations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Context sets -> Disentangled encoders (global and local) -> Bayesian aggregators -> Latent variables -> Decoders -> Predictions
- **Critical path**: Data flows from context sets through encoders to produce local and global latent observations, which are aggregated using Bayesian aggregation to produce latent variables zk, which are then passed to decoders to produce predictions, with the entire process trained using the unified ELBO
- **Design tradeoffs**: The disentanglement approach trades off some potential information sharing for robustness against error propagation; the Bayesian approach trades computational efficiency for uncertainty quantification; the multi-fidelity regularization trades model flexibility for better cross-fidelity alignment
- **Failure signatures**: If latent observations don't properly disentangle (global observations still contain fidelity-specific information), if Bayesian aggregation fails to properly combine observations, if the regularization term is too strong or too weak, or if the computational cost becomes prohibitive
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test the disentanglement by comparing latent observation similarity across fidelity levels for identical inputs
  2. Validate the Bayesian aggregation by checking whether uncertainty estimates make sense given the amount of training data
  3. Test the multi-fidelity regularization by measuring how well aligned global observations are across fidelity levels for reference data pairs

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the disentangled neural process architecture in D-MFDAL compare to hierarchical architectures in terms of error propagation when the number of high-fidelity data points is extremely limited (e.g., fewer than 10)?

### Open Question 2
Can the multi-fidelity regularization term in D-MFDAL be further improved by incorporating task-specific similarity metrics between fidelity levels, rather than relying solely on Jensen-Shannon divergence?

### Open Question 3
How does the performance of D-MFDAL scale with the number of fidelity levels, and are there diminishing returns or computational bottlenecks as the number of levels increases?

## Limitations
- The paper doesn't provide ablation studies showing individual contributions of disentanglement, Bayesian aggregation, and multi-fidelity regularization
- Experiments focus primarily on PDE benchmarks, leaving unclear how well the approach generalizes to other multi-fidelity scenarios
- The comparison methods may not represent the full state-of-the-art in multi-fidelity active learning

## Confidence
- Disentanglement mechanism: Medium - theoretical justification provided but limited empirical validation of benefits
- Multi-fidelity regularization: High - well-justified theoretically and empirically validated
- Computational scalability: Medium - theoretical claims made but limited empirical validation for large-scale problems
