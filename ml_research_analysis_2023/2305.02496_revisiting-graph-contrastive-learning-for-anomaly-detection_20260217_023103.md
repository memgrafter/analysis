# Revisiting Graph Contrastive Learning for Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02496
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02496
- Reference count: 30
- This paper revisits the problem of graph anomaly detection using contrastive learning, proposing a unified framework MAG that combines graph augmentation and multi-GNN modules.

## Executive Summary
This paper revisits graph anomaly detection using contrastive learning and proposes a unified framework MAG that combines graph augmentation and multi-GNN modules. Through comprehensive experiments, the authors discover that the benefits of multi-scale contrast modules in existing methods are actually due to the multi-GNN modules. They propose two variants of MAG, L-MAG and M-MAG, with L-MAG achieving state-of-the-art performance on Cora and Pubmed datasets with low computational cost.

## Method Summary
The MAG framework combines graph augmentation (masked features and removed edges) with multi-GNN modules to learn robust node representations for anomaly detection. The framework uses contrastive learning to distinguish between normal and anomalous nodes through different contrast patterns including node-node, subgraph-subgraph, and node-subgraph contrasts. The authors propose two variants: L-MAG (lightweight) and M-MAG (with enhanced multi-GNN modules). The model is trained for 100 epochs with a learning rate of 1e-3 and evaluated using AUC scores on citation network datasets.

## Key Results
- Multi-GNN modules, not multi-scale contrast modules, are the primary drivers of performance gains in graph anomaly detection
- Graph augmentation combining masked features and removed edges shows significant competitiveness
- Normal node-subgraph contrast pattern achieves the best performance among different contrast patterns
- L-MAG achieves state-of-the-art results on Cora and Pubmed datasets with low computational cost

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Multiple graph neural networks learn different representations of the same graph structure, capturing diverse interaction patterns. These diverse representations enhance the model's ability to distinguish between normal and anomalous nodes. Evidence shows that gains in detection performance derive from multi-GNN modules rather than additional node-node contrast.

### Mechanism 2
Graph augmentation with masked features and removed edges creates diverse views of the graph, providing additional training signals that help the model learn robust representations. This augmentation strategy is particularly effective for identifying anomalies that might be missed with the original graph alone.

### Mechanism 3
Normal node-subgraph contrast is more effective than other contrast patterns because it creates a strong signal for identifying anomalies. Anomalous nodes show weaker consistency between their representations and their subgraph representations compared to normal nodes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the MAG framework perform on heterogeneous or dynamic graphs compared to its performance on static homogeneous graphs? The authors suggest that transferring MAG to more realistic scenes deserves more attention, but the current study only focuses on static homogeneous graphs.

### Open Question 2
What is the optimal combination of contrast patterns in the MAG framework for graph anomaly detection? The authors mention that MAG has a vast amount of combinations, and finding a better contrast combination is a worthwhile subject.

### Open Question 3
How does the performance of the MAG framework compare to other state-of-the-art graph anomaly detection methods on larger and more complex graphs?

## Limitations

- The study focuses only on static homogeneous graphs, limiting generalizability to real-world heterogeneous or dynamic graphs
- The specific architectural details of the multi-GNN modules are not fully specified, making it difficult to assess the exact mechanism of performance gains
- The optimal combination of contrast patterns remains unexplored due to the vast number of possible combinations

## Confidence

- Multi-GNN mechanism: Medium - Ablation studies provide evidence but lack detailed architectural specifications
- Graph augmentation effectiveness: Medium - Empirical results show competitiveness but underlying reasons are not fully explored
- Normal node-subgraph contrast: High - Clear evidence from average AUC scores demonstrates superiority
