# Revisiting Robustness in Graph Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00851
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00851
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: This paper introduces semantics-aware adversarial robustness and over-robustness, finding that common GNNs show significant over-robustness, and that including label-structure information reduces over-robustness without harming accuracy or adversarial robustness.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental gap in understanding what constitutes small, semantics-preserving perturbations in graph machine learning. The authors introduce a new semantics-aware notion of adversarial robustness and over-robustness using Contextual Stochastic Block Models (CSBMs) to generate principled synthetic graphs. Their key finding is that common perturbation sets contain a large fraction of graphs violating the unchanged semantics assumption, and that all assessed GNNs show significant over-robustness. The paper demonstrates that including label-structure information into GNN inference reduces over-robustness without harming accuracy or adversarial robustness. Theoretically, they prove that optimal robustness and accuracy are achievable for inductive node classification, with no tradeoff.

## Method Summary
The authors use Contextual Stochastic Block Models (CSBMs) to generate synthetic graphs with analytically tractable properties, combined with real-world graphs including Cora-ML, Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, and ogbn-arxiv. They train various GNN architectures (GCN, SGC, GAT, GATv2, APPNP, GraphSAGE, MLP) with and without Label Propagation (LP) integration. The evaluation involves applying multiple attack algorithms including Nettack, DICE, GR-BCD, SGA, ℓ2-weak, and ℓ2-strong, measuring semantic-aware adversarial robustness (R(f,g)), conventional adversarial robustness (R(f)), over-robustness (Rover), and F1-robustness score (F1^rob). The experiments are conducted on both synthetic CSBMs with varying homophily levels and real-world datasets.

## Key Results
- Common perturbation sets contain a large fraction of graphs violating the unchanged semantics assumption, revealing that many "adversarial" examples are actually semantically different graphs
- All assessed GNNs show significant over-robustness, where they incorrectly classify semantically perturbed graphs
- Including label-structure through Label Propagation (LP) into GNN inference significantly reduces over-robustness while maintaining or improving test accuracy and adversarial robustness
- Theoretical proof that there is no robustness-accuracy tradeoff for inductive node classification when using the Bayes optimal classifier

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CSBMs enable principled measurement of semantic content preservation under graph perturbations
- Mechanism: CSBMs define edge probabilities between same-class and different-class nodes, enabling calculation of the Bayes optimal classifier which serves as a semantic-content oracle
- Core assumption: CSBM parameters (p, q, µ, σ) can be set to match real-world graph properties
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section] - Weak corpus evidence for CSBM parameter fitting

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Label Propagation (LP) significantly reduces over-robustness without harming accuracy or adversarial robustness
- Mechanism: LP incorporates label-structure information into GNN inference by combining node features with label-spreading information
- Core assumption: The combination of LP with GNNs preserves the benefits of both feature learning and label-structure information
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section] - Weak corpus evidence for LP+GNN combinations

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: No robustness-accuracy tradeoff exists for inductive node classification using semantic-aware robustness
- Mechanism: The Bayes optimal classifier for inductive node classification achieves both optimal accuracy and robustness by making predictions based on the true data-generating distribution
- Core assumption: The reference classifier g (Bayes optimal) accurately represents semantic content preservation
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section] - Weak corpus evidence for theoretical proof's practical implications

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: CSBM graph generation -> GNN models (including LP variants) -> Attack algorithms (ℓ2-weak, Nettack, DICE, etc.) -> Evaluation metrics (R, Rover, Frob)
- **Critical path**: Generate CSBM → Train GNN models → Apply attacks → Measure robustness metrics → Analyze semantic preservation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Using CSBMs provides analytical tractability but may not capture all real-world graph properties; LP integration improves robustness but may reduce feature-based learning
- **Failure signatures**: High Rover values indicate over-robustness; low Radv values indicate poor adversarial robustness; discrepancies between conventional and semantic-aware robustness suggest measurement issues
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Generate CSBM with K=1.0 and measure Rover for GCN vs GCN+LP
  2. Apply ℓ2-weak attack with Bdeg budget and compare R and R(f,g) values
  3. Train MLP vs GCN on CSBM and compare test accuracy vs robustness tradeoffs

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: Can a reference classifier be derived from real-world data to measure semantic content preservation?
  - Basis: The paper acknowledges deriving a reference classifier for real-world datasets is challenging
  - Why unresolved: Difficulty of creating a gold standard reference classifier for real-world graphs
  - What evidence would resolve it: Development of a reliable method to create a reference classifier from real-world graph data

- **Open Question 2**: How does the degree distribution of real-world graphs affect the prevalence of over-robustness?
  - Basis: The paper shows low-degree nodes in CSBMs are particularly susceptible to over-robustness
  - Why unresolved: While the phenomenon is demonstrated, comprehensive analysis of different degree distributions is lacking
  - What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies on a wide range of real-world graphs with varying degree distributions

- **Open Question 3**: What is the impact of over-robustness on the generalization performance of GNNs in real-world applications?
  - Basis: The paper shows a significant portion of conventional robustness can be attributed to over-robustness
  - Why unresolved: The paper focuses on existence but not impact on overall performance and reliability
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of GNN performance with and without over-robustness mitigation on real-world datasets

## Limitations

- Findings are based on synthetic CSBMs and specific real-world datasets, limiting generalizability
- Theoretical proof assumes access to the true data-generating distribution, which may not hold in practice
- Semantic-aware robustness metrics rely on the assumption that CSBM parameters can accurately capture real-world graph properties
- Results focus on node classification tasks and may not generalize to other graph learning tasks

## Confidence

- **High**: Theoretical proof of no robustness-accuracy tradeoff for inductive node classification using Bayes optimal classifiers
- **Medium**: Empirical findings on over-robustness in common GNN architectures and effectiveness of LP integration
- **Low**: Generalizability of CSBM-based results to all real-world graph datasets
