# Neural Spline Search for Quantile Probabilistic Modeling

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Neural Spline Search (NSS), a non-parametric, data-driven approach for quantile probabilistic modeling that addresses the challenge of accurate quantile estimation across diverse applications. The method uses monotonic spline regressions guided by symbolic operators to transform inputs into target distributions without assuming specific parametric forms. NSS-sum excels in regression tasks while NSS-chain performs particularly well in time-series forecasting. Empirical results demonstrate consistent superiority over existing methods across synthetic, UCI regression, and M5 time-series benchmarks, with notable improvements in both MAE and pinball loss metrics.

## Method Summary
NSS employs a novel approach using monotonic spline regressions guided by symbolic operators (sum, scale, chaining) to transform inputs into target distributions. The method builds a series of spline functions that collectively map input data to the desired output distribution through iterative transformations. This non-parametric framework avoids assumptions about underlying distribution forms while maintaining expressiveness through the combination of multiple spline operations. The approach is designed to be flexible enough to capture complex data patterns while remaining computationally tractable for practical applications in regression and time-series forecasting.

## Key Results
- NSS consistently outperforms existing methods with 3.5%-7.0% improvements in MAE on UCI benchmarks
- NSS-sum shows superior performance for regression tasks
- NSS-chain excels in time-series forecasting applications
- Method demonstrates strong performance across synthetic, UCI regression, and M5 forecasting tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of NSS stems from its ability to capture complex, non-linear relationships between inputs and output distributions through flexible spline transformations. By avoiding parametric assumptions, the method can adapt to various data patterns without being constrained by predetermined distribution families. The use of monotonic splines ensures valid probability distributions while maintaining computational efficiency. The symbolic operators provide a structured way to compose multiple transformations, allowing the model to capture both simple and complex distributional shapes through hierarchical combinations of basic operations.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Input features -> Monotonic spline layers -> Symbolic operators (sum/scale/chain) -> Output distribution parameters

**Critical Path:**
The critical path involves transforming input features through monotonic spline layers, applying symbolic operations to combine multiple transformations, and producing the final quantile estimates. The monotonic spline layers ensure valid probability distributions while the symbolic operators provide compositional flexibility.

**Design Tradeoffs:**
The method trades computational complexity for distributional flexibility. While parametric methods are faster, NSS achieves better accuracy by avoiding distributional assumptions. The choice of symbolic operators (sum vs chain) affects both performance and computational requirements, with chain operations being more expressive but potentially more expensive.

**Failure Signatures:**
Potential failures include poor performance on very high-dimensional data due to spline complexity, convergence issues when symbolic operations create ill-conditioned transformations, and computational bottlenecks when chaining many operations. The method may also struggle with extremely skewed distributions if the spline basis is insufficient.
