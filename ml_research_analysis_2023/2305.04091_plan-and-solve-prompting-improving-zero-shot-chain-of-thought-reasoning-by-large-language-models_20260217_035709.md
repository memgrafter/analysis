# Plan-and-Solve Prompting: Improving Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought Reasoning by Large Language Models
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04091
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04091
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Plan-and-Solve (PS) and PS+ prompting improve zero-shot reasoning accuracy, outperforming Zero-shot-CoT and matching few-shot methods on 10 datasets spanning arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning.

## Executive Summary
Plan-and-Solve (PS) prompting is a zero-shot chain-of-thought approach that instructs large language models to first devise a plan for breaking down a problem into smaller subtasks, then solve each subtask step-by-step. The PS+ variant adds explicit instructions for extracting variables, computing intermediate results, and focusing on calculation accuracy. Evaluated on 10 reasoning datasets, PS+ consistently outperforms zero-shot baselines and is competitive with or better than few-shot methods, achieving up to 76.7% accuracy on math reasoning tasks.

## Method Summary
Plan-and-Solve prompting guides LLMs to first generate a plan for decomposing a problem into smaller subtasks, then execute the plan step-by-step. The PS+ variant adds explicit instructions for extracting relevant variables, computing intermediate results, and paying close attention to calculations. Both methods aim to address common errors in zero-shot chain-of-thought reasoning, such as missing steps or incorrect calculations, by making the reasoning process more explicit and structured.

## Key Results
- PS+ prompting achieves up to 76.7% accuracy on math reasoning tasks, outperforming zero-shot baselines.
- PS+ is competitive with or better than few-shot methods across 10 diverse reasoning datasets.
- PS+ improves over PS by explicitly prompting for variable extraction and detailed intermediate calculations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PS and PS+ prompting improve reasoning by structuring the problem-solving process: first, the model plans how to break down the problem into manageable subtasks; second, it executes each subtask with explicit focus on extracting relevant variables and computing intermediate results. This reduces errors due to missing steps or incorrect calculations, which are common in standard zero-shot chain-of-thought approaches.

## Foundational Learning
- Chain-of-thought reasoning: Why needed—guides models through step-by-step problem solving; Quick check—does the model produce intermediate reasoning steps?
- Zero-shot prompting: Why needed—enables reasoning without task-specific examples; Quick check—does performance match or exceed few-shot baselines?
- Variable extraction: Why needed—ensures all relevant information is captured before computation; Quick check—are all necessary variables identified and used?
- Intermediate result computation: Why needed—reduces cascading calculation errors; Quick check—are all intermediate values correct and used in subsequent steps?

## Architecture Onboarding
- Component map: Problem statement -> Plan generation -> Variable extraction -> Intermediate computation -> Final answer
- Critical path: Plan generation and variable extraction are essential for correct problem decomposition and accurate intermediate calculations.
- Design tradeoffs: PS+ adds explicit instructions for variable extraction and computation, which increases prompt length but reduces reasoning errors.
- Failure signatures: Missing or incorrect variable extraction, skipped subtasks, or arithmetic errors in intermediate steps.
- First experiments: 1) Run PS+ on a simple arithmetic problem and check for correct variable extraction and intermediate steps; 2) Compare PS vs PS+ on a commonsense reasoning task to observe differences in plan generation; 3) Evaluate PS+ on a symbolic reasoning dataset to assess robustness to non-arithmetic reasoning.

None

## Limitations
- Experiments limited to 10 English-language datasets, reducing generalizability to other languages or broader domains.
- Most results are on GPT-3.5 and GPT-4; performance on open-source LLMs is untested.
- The relative contribution of each PS+ instruction is not isolated; ablation studies are limited.

## Confidence
- Main claims: Medium
- Reasoning: PS and PS+ are clearly novel and improve over zero-shot baselines, but the evaluation is limited in scope (10 datasets, English only, few model families), and the performance gains are often modest.

## Next Checks
1. Replicate experiments with multiple open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, Mistral) to test model independence.
2. Evaluate PS/PS+ on multilingual and non-English datasets to assess cross-lingual generalization.
3. Conduct an ablation study isolating each PS+ instruction to measure its individual contribution to performance.
