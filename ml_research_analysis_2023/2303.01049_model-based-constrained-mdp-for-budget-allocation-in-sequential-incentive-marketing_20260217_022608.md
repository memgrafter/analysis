# Model-based Constrained MDP for Budget Allocation in Sequential Incentive Marketing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01049
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01049
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed CMDP approach achieves up to 16.11% improvement over random allocation on synthetic data and 13.61% on real data under fixed budget constraints

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of sequential incentive marketing by formulating it as a constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) to maximize return under budget constraints using logged counterfactual data. The authors propose an efficient learning algorithm that combines bisection search and model-based planning to solve the dual problem obtained through Lagrangian relaxation. The approach is designed to work with offline data where only logged actions and outcomes are available, making it practical for real-world marketing applications where online experimentation is costly or infeasible.

## Method Summary
The method formulates sequential incentive marketing as a CMDP where the objective is to maximize expected return subject to budget constraints. The authors convert this constrained problem to its dual form using Lagrangian relaxation, with the optimal dual variable found efficiently via bisection search due to the monotonicity of the dual objective. The model-based planning component accelerates the process by training the policy only once using estimated transition dynamics, rather than requiring repeated optimization steps. This combination of dual optimization and model-based planning enables efficient budget allocation while respecting constraints.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 16.11% improvement over random allocation on synthetic marketing datasets
- Demonstrates 13.61% improvement on real marketing data under fixed budget constraints
- Outperforms alternative methods in both synthetic and real-world experimental settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by transforming the constrained optimization problem into an unconstrained dual problem through Lagrangian relaxation. The bisection search efficiently finds the optimal dual variable because the dual objective is monotonic, while model-based planning accelerates policy learning by leveraging estimated transition dynamics. This combination allows for efficient optimization of incentive allocation policies under budget constraints using only logged counterfactual data, avoiding the need for costly online experimentation.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Data -> Transition Model Estimation -> Lagrangian Dual Formulation -> Bisection Search -> Model-based Policy Optimization -> Budget-Constrained Incentive Allocation
**Critical Path**: Logged data → transition dynamics estimation → dual problem formulation → bisection search for optimal dual variable → single policy training with model-based planning → constraint-satisfying incentive allocation
**Design Tradeoffs**: The model-based approach trades potential model misspecification risk for computational efficiency compared to model-free methods; the bisection search assumes monotonicity which may not hold with imperfect models
**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance when transition dynamics are poorly estimated, when logging policies are non-stationary, or when strong duality does not hold for the specific problem instance
