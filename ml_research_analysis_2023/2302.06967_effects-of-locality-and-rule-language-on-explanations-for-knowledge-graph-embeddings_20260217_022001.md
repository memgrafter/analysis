# Effects of Locality and Rule Language on Explanations for Knowledge Graph Embeddings

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.06967
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06967
- **Reference count**: 36
- **Primary result**: Bounded atoms and local scopes improve fidelity and coverage of rule-based explanations for KG embedding link predictors

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how locality and rule language design impact the quality of explanations for knowledge graph embedding-based link predictors. The author proposes a method that mines Horn rules with bounded atoms from local regions of knowledge graphs to construct rule-based surrogates for black-box link predictors. Through experiments on FB15K-237, the study demonstrates that incorporating bounded atoms and local scopes significantly improves explanation fidelity and coverage. The results show a symbiotic relationship between bounded atoms and local scopes, with more specific rules and local scopes leading to more accurate explanations that provide insights into the inner workings of KG embeddings for link prediction.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves mining Horn rules with bounded atoms from local regions of the knowledge graph, rather than using unbounded rules across the entire graph. The approach first identifies a local scope around the target entity pair, then mines Horn rules within this constrained region where atoms are bounded by a specified limit. These mined rules are used to construct surrogate models that approximate the behavior of black-box embedding-based link predictors. The bounded atoms constrain the number of variables in each rule body, while the local scope limits the search space to relevant subgraphs, both contributing to more focused and interpretable explanations.

## Key Results
- Bounded atoms and local scopes significantly improve explanation fidelity and coverage
- Bounded atoms and local scopes exhibit a symbiotic relationship in improving explanation quality
- More specific rules and local scopes provide deeper insights into KG embedding mechanisms

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness stems from aligning the explanation language with the actual decision boundaries learned by KG embeddings. By restricting explanations to local regions and bounded atoms, the method captures the specific regularities and patterns that the embedding model actually uses for predictions in that context, rather than generating overly general rules that may not reflect the model's true reasoning process.
