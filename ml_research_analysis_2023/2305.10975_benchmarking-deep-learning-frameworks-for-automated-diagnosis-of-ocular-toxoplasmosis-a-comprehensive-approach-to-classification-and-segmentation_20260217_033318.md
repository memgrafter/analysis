# Benchmarking Deep Learning Frameworks for Automated Diagnosis of Ocular Toxoplasmosis: A Comprehensive Approach to Classification and Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10975
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10975
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: MobileNetV2 achieved highest classification accuracy (98.9%) while MobileNetV2/U-Net delivered best segmentation results (Dice score 92.1%)

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive benchmarking study evaluating deep learning frameworks for automated diagnosis of ocular toxoplasmosis from fundus images. The study focuses on two tasks: classification of OT and segmentation of OT lesions. Five pre-trained CNN models were evaluated for classification, with MobileNetV2 achieving the highest accuracy (98.9%), recall (99.3%), and F1 score (98.9%). For segmentation, U-Net architectures with different encoders were assessed, with MobileNetV2/U-Net delivering the best results in accuracy (98.1%) and Dice score (92.1%). The study demonstrates that transfer learning with pre-trained models is effective for OT diagnosis and lesion segmentation.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated five pre-trained models (VGG16, MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet50, DenseNet121) for OT classification using transfer learning with frozen backbone layers. For segmentation, U-Net architectures with different encoders (MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet34, VGG16) were trained using Dice and Jaccard loss functions. The dataset consisted of 412 fundus images from the OTFID dataset with manual lesion segmentation masks. Preprocessing included green channel inversion, illumination equalization, CLAHE, Gaussian smoothing, and normalization. Data augmentation used rotation, zoom, and flips. Models were trained using TensorFlow/Keras on NVidia K80 GPUs with Adam optimizer (lr=1e-4, batch size=32, epochs=200).

## Key Results
- MobileNetV2 achieved highest classification accuracy (98.9%), recall (99.3%), and F1 score (98.9%)
- DenseNet121 excelled in classification precision (98.6%)
- MobileNetV2/U-Net delivered best segmentation results: accuracy (98.1%) and Dice score (92.1%)
- Dice and Jaccard loss functions outperformed cross-entropy for segmentation tasks
- Transfer learning with ImageNet-pretrained models proved effective for both classification and segmentation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MobileNetV2/U-Net architecture outperforms other encoder-decoder combinations for OT lesion segmentation.
- Mechanism: The depthwise separable convolutions in MobileNetV2 reduce computational cost while preserving spatial resolution, enabling finer segmentation boundaries without overfitting the small OT dataset.
- Core assumption: Reduced parameter count does not compromise segmentation quality on small datasets with limited lesion variety.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The success of MobileNetV2/U-Net can be attributed to its depthwise separable convolution-based encoder architecture..."
  - [abstract] "MobileNetV2/U-Net delivering the best results in accuracy (98.1%) and Dice score (92.1%)"
- Break condition: If the dataset grows substantially and lesion diversity increases, the model may underfit compared to denser architectures.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Transfer learning with ImageNet-pretrained backbones accelerates convergence and improves accuracy for OT classification.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained models bring general feature extractors that map well to fundus image characteristics, reducing the number of trainable parameters needed for the small OT dataset.
- Core assumption: Visual features useful for general object recognition overlap sufficiently with lesion detection features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This research seeks to provide a guide for future researchers looking to utilise DL techniques..."
  - [section] "This research investigates the feasibility of utilizing DL techniques for the diagnosis of Ocular Toxoplasmosis (OT)."
- Break condition: If domain shift between ImageNet and fundus images becomes too large (e.g., color, texture differences), fine-tuning may yield better results.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Dice and Jaccard loss functions yield better segmentation than standard cross-entropy in imbalanced lesion regions.
- Mechanism: These loss functions explicitly optimize for overlap between prediction and ground truth, handling the high class imbalance between lesion and background pixels more effectively.
- Core assumption: The ground truth masks are sufficiently accurate and consistent for overlap-based metrics to be meaningful.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The MobileNetV2/U-Net outperformed ResNet34 by 0.5% and 2.1% in terms of Acc and Dice Score, respectively when Jaccard loss function is employed during the training."
  - [section] "All the models were trained on binary cross-entropy loss function as well which did not produce any satisfactory results."
- Break condition: If lesion masks are noisy or inconsistent, Dice/Jaccard may overfit to mask artifacts.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Transfer learning and fine-tuning
  - Why needed here: The OT dataset is small (412 images), so using ImageNet-pretrained models avoids training from scratch and mitigates overfitting.
  - Quick check question: What happens if you freeze all layers of a pre-trained model and only train the final classification layer?

- Concept: Loss functions for segmentation (Dice/Jaccard vs. cross-entropy)
  - Why needed here: Lesion masks are highly imbalanced (small lesion area vs. large background), so overlap-based losses better capture segmentation quality.
  - Quick check question: How does Dice loss behave when the prediction is empty but the ground truth contains a lesion?

- Concept: Encoder-decoder architecture for segmentation
  - Why needed here: U-Net's skip connections preserve spatial detail, which is critical for precise lesion boundary detection in fundus images.
  - Quick check question: Why does U-Net include concatenation of encoder and decoder feature maps instead of just upsampling?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input images -> Preprocessing (green channel + illumination equalization + CLAHE + Gaussian smoothing + normalization) -> Backbone (pre-trained encoder) -> Task-specific head (Dense layers for classification, Decoder for segmentation) -> Output (class probabilities or segmentation mask)

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained encoder weights
  2. Freeze all encoder layers except final classification layer (classification task)
  3. Attach decoder for segmentation or dense layers for classification
  4. Train with chosen loss function
  5. Evaluate using Acc, Precision, Recall, F1 (classification) or Dice/IoU (segmentation)

- Design tradeoffs:
  - MobileNetV2: Fewer parameters, faster inference, slightly lower accuracy than DenseNet121 for classification
  - DenseNet121: Higher accuracy, more parameters, slower inference, better precision
  - VGG16: Lowest inference time, but lowest segmentation accuracy
  - ResNet34: Balanced parameter count, slower than MobileNetV2 but faster than DenseNet121

- Failure signatures:
  - Low Dice/IoU but high classification accuracy: Model finds lesions but cannot delineate boundaries well
  - High variance across CV folds: Model overfits to small dataset; consider stronger regularization or more data augmentation
  - Training loss plateaus early: Learning rate too low or model capacity too small for task complexity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train MobileNetV2 classifier with frozen backbone; evaluate 5-fold CV metrics
  2. Replace MobileNetV2 encoder with VGG16; compare Dice scores on segmentation task
  3. Swap Dice loss for Jaccard loss in MobileNetV2/U-Net; measure improvement in IoU

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- How would the performance of these models change with a larger, more balanced dataset containing more diverse OT cases? The authors acknowledge their dataset is limited in size and unbalanced, suggesting collecting more images for better generalization.

- Would self-supervised learning using large fundus image datasets from other domains improve OT detection performance? The paper mentions leveraging existing large fundus image datasets for self-supervised learning to improve solution performance.

- How would more advanced architectures like image transformer classifiers perform on the OT dataset compared to traditional CNNs? The authors suggest deploying more advanced architecture models like image transformer classifiers to examine effectiveness of SOTA architectures.

## Limitations
- Dataset size remains small (412 images), limiting generalizability to other populations or imaging conditions
- Limited ablation studies on preprocessing pipeline components prevent isolating their individual contributions
- No comparison with clinical baselines or existing automated systems for OT diagnosis
- The segmentation masks were manually created but quality metrics are not reported

## Confidence
- High confidence: Classification task results, particularly MobileNetV2 achieving superior accuracy/precision/recall metrics
- Medium confidence: Segmentation task results, as Dice scores are strong but dataset size limits statistical power
- Medium confidence: Claims about MobileNetV2/U-Net superiority, based on consistent performance across both tasks

## Next Checks
