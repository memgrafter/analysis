# SPENSER: Towards a NeuroEvolutionary Approach for Convolutional Spiking Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10987
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10987
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: SPENSER evolves CSNNs achieving 99.42% MNIST and 91.65% Fashion-MNIST accuracy

## Executive Summary
SPENSER introduces a neuroevolutionary framework for designing Convolutional Spiking Neural Networks (CSNNs) using Grammatical Evolution. The approach addresses the challenge of optimizing SNN architectures and parameters by evolving both network structure and key parameters through a combination of Genetic Algorithms and grammar-based representation. Experiments on standard image classification datasets demonstrate that SPENSER can discover architectures competitive with hand-designed SNNs, offering a promising automated design methodology for this emerging neural network paradigm.

## Method Summary
SPENSER combines Genetic Algorithms (GA) for macro network structure evolution with Structured Grammatical Evolution (DSGE) for fine-tuning layer parameters. The framework evolves CSNN architectures including layer types, neuron dynamics, and optimizer choices. Networks are trained using Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) with surrogate gradients to handle non-differentiable spiking activations. The evolutionary search uses one-pass fitness evaluation (1 epoch training) followed by extended training (50 epochs) of the best individuals. The method operates on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST datasets converted to spike trains using rate encoding.

## Key Results
- Test accuracy of 99.42% on MNIST dataset
- Test accuracy of 91.65% on Fashion-MNIST dataset
- Performance on par with state-of-the-art hand-designed SNNs
- Demonstrates viability of neuroevolution for automated SNN architecture design

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SPENSER's neuroevolutionary approach can discover CSNN architectures competitive with hand-designed models without manual parameter tuning.
- Mechanism: Genetic Algorithms evolve macro network structure while Structured Grammatical Evolution fine-tunes individual layer parameters, allowing joint exploration of architecture and neuron dynamics.
- Core assumption: The grammar encodes all valid CSNN configurations, and the search space is rich enough to include competitive architectures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SPENSER generates competitive performing networks with a test accuracy of 99.42% and 91.65% respectively."
  - [section] "These results are on par with state-of-the-art hand-designed SNNs, demonstrating the potential of neuroevolution for SNN design."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related papers focus on SNN optimization but not neuroevolution-based architecture search.
- Break condition: If the grammar excludes critical architectural patterns or neuron dynamics, the search cannot find optimal configurations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) with surrogate gradients enables gradient-based learning in SNNs despite non-differentiable spiking activations.
- Mechanism: Surrogate gradients approximate the spiking function as continuous around the threshold during backpropagation, allowing gradient flow.
- Core assumption: The surrogate gradient function chosen (ATan or Fast-Sigmoid) provides sufficient gradient signal for learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In order to bypass the non-differentiability of the spiking neuron's activation function, one can use surrogate gradients, by approximating the activation function with continuous functions centered at the activation threshold during the backward pass of backpropagation."
  - [section] "For this experimental study, we considered rate encoding for inputs and outputs, and as such, chose the Mean Square Error Spike Count described in Section 2.2."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; corpus neighbors discuss SNN training but not specifically BPTT with surrogate gradients.
- Break condition: If the surrogate gradient approximation is poor near the threshold, learning gradients vanish and training fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: One-pass fitness evaluation during evolution trades off training accuracy for search speed while still selecting good architectures.
- Mechanism: Networks trained for only 1 epoch on EvoTrain data are evaluated on Fitness data to estimate future performance, reducing computational cost per generation.
- Core assumption: Short training captures enough architectural quality signal to guide evolution toward better solutions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Because we are dealing with an image recognition problem, we defined a grammar that contains primitives allowing for the construction of CSNNs."
  - [section] "During the evolutionary search, individuals are trained on the EvoTrain split for 1 epoch and tested against the Fitness split for fitness assignment."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; corpus neighbors do not discuss evolutionary training strategies for SNNs.
- Break condition: If architecture quality is not reflected in short training performance, evolution selects suboptimal models.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GA level (macro structure encoding) -> DSGE level (layer parameter expansion) -> CSNN model generation -> BPTT training with surrogate gradients -> fitness evaluation
- Critical path: Define grammar → Set GA macro structure → Run evolutionary search (1 epoch training per individual) → Select best → Train 50 more epochs → Evaluate on test set
- Design tradeoffs: Short training speeds search but may miss architectures needing longer training; fixed loss function limits exploration of alternative learning signals
- Failure signatures: Evolution plateaus with low variance (search space too constrained), or best accuracy lags hand-designed models significantly (architecture search ineffective)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify grammar generates valid CSNN models by decoding random genotypes and checking syntax.
  2. Run evolution for 10 generations on a subset of MNIST to observe fitness progression.
  3. Compare test accuracy of the best evolved model with a randomly initialized CSNN of similar size.

## Limitations

- High computational demands due to BPTT training, limiting exploration of large search spaces
- Reliance on short training for fitness evaluation may not reliably predict final performance
- Framework depends on specific grammar rules and neuron dynamics, constraining discoverable architectures

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Architecture discovery): **Medium** - Results show competitive accuracy, but computational constraints and grammar limitations may restrict optimal solutions.
- Mechanism 2 (Surrogate gradients): **Medium** - BPTT with surrogate gradients is well-established, but the specific choice of gradient function and its effectiveness for CSNNs is not fully validated.
- Mechanism 3 (One-pass fitness): **Low** - Short training may not capture architectural quality, and the paper lacks ablation studies on training duration.
