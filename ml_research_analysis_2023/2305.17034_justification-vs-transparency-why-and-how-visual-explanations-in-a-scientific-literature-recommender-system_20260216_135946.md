# Justification vs. Transparency: Why and How Visual Explanations in a Scientific Literature Recommender System

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17034
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17034
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Providing both Why and How explanations together in a recommender system creates a trade-off between transparency/trust and user satisfaction

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how different types of visual explanations (Why and How) affect user perceptions in a scientific literature recommender system called RIMA. The authors developed interactive visual explanations using a human-centered design approach and conducted a qualitative user study (N=12) to explore how these explanations influence transparency, trust, and satisfaction. The research reveals that Why explanations enhance user satisfaction while How explanations improve transparency and trust, with a notable trade-off when both are provided together. The findings highlight the importance of tailoring explanation types to user characteristics and specific explanation goals.

## Method Summary
The authors followed a human-centered design approach to develop interactive visual explanations for the RIMA scientific literature recommender system. They first designed Why explanations (highlighting relevant keywords and showing similarity scores) and How explanations (revealing system inner workings through flow charts). A qualitative user study was then conducted with 12 participants who explored the system and provided feedback through think-aloud sessions and interviews. User perceptions of transparency, trust, and satisfaction were measured through questionnaires and qualitative analysis.

## Key Results
- How explanations improved perceived system transparency and trust compared to Why explanations
- Why explanations were more effective for user satisfaction than How explanations
- A trade-off exists between transparency/trust and satisfaction when both explanation types are provided together

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Why explanations improve user satisfaction while How explanations improve transparency and trust
- Mechanism: Why explanations provide abstract, user-oriented justifications (e.g., "similar users liked this") that satisfy users' need for simple reasoning, while How explanations reveal algorithmic details that enhance understanding of system functionality, building trust through transparency
- Core assumption: Users have different information needs based on their technical background and goals
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The results showed that How explanations improved perceived system transparency and trust, while Why explanations were more effective for user satisfaction"
  - [section] "The How explanation is the right choice, if the goal is to increase objective transparency" and "Why explanations, by contrast, are perceived as less transparent and trustworthy, but can contribute to increased satisfaction"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly address this specific trade-off between Why and How explanations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: User type (background knowledge) determines which explanation type is more effective
- Mechanism: Users with technical backgrounds benefit from How explanations because they can understand algorithmic details, while non-technical users prefer Why explanations that provide simple justifications without overwhelming technical information
- Core assumption: Background knowledge is the primary factor determining explanation effectiveness
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The study revealed a trade-off between transparency/trust and satisfaction when both explanations were provided together, and highlighted the importance of tailoring explanation types to user characteristics"
  - [section] "The user perceptions of Why and How explanations in terms of transparency and trust depend on the user type (e.g., background knowledge)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss XAI broadly but don't specifically address this user-type-dependent mechanism

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Providing both Why and How explanations together creates a trade-off between transparency/trust and satisfaction
- Mechanism: When both explanation types are available, users who understand How explanations gain transparency and trust but may become less satisfied due to complexity, while users who prefer Why explanations remain satisfied but miss out on transparency benefits
- Core assumption: Users cannot simultaneously optimize for both transparency/trust and satisfaction when given both explanation types
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The study revealed a trade-off 'transparency/trust vs. satisfaction' when both explanations were provided together"
  - [section] "How explanations can lead to higher transparency and trust, but lower satisfaction. Why explanations, by contrast, are perceived as less transparent and trustworthy, but can contribute to increased satisfaction"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss explanations generally but don't specifically address this combined provision trade-off

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - RIMA application (content-based recommender system) -> User interest model generation (from Semantic Scholar publications) -> Recommendation engine (semantic similarity calculation) -> Why explanation module (color bands, keyword highlighting, word clouds) -> How explanation module (flow charts, navigation panels) -> User interface (overview and detailed views)

- Critical path:
  1. User authenticates with Semantic Scholar
  2. System generates interest model from user's publications
  3. Recommendation engine retrieves and scores publications
  4. User views recommendations with color-coded relevance
  5. User can request Why or How explanations
  6. System displays appropriate explanation visualizations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Simplicity vs. detail: Providing both explanation types increases complexity but serves different user needs
  - Static vs. interactive: Interactive explanations require more development but allow personalized exploration
  - Algorithm transparency vs. intellectual property: Revealing too much may expose proprietary algorithms

- Failure signatures:
  - Users unable to understand How explanations → may indicate overly technical content or poor design
  - Users not engaging with explanations → may indicate explanations not visible enough or not perceived as useful
  - Users confused by multiple explanation types → may indicate need for clearer distinction between explanation purposes

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test explanation comprehension: Have users complete tasks requiring understanding of recommendations, measure success rate with different explanation types
  2. Measure explanation usage patterns: Track which explanation type users choose and how frequently they access detailed views
  3. A/B test explanation design: Compare user satisfaction and trust metrics between versions with different explanation presentation styles (e.g., always visible vs. on-demand)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal level of transparency that maximizes both user trust and satisfaction in recommender systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses a trade-off between transparency/trust and satisfaction when providing both Why and How explanations together, and references previous work suggesting there should be an optimal level of transparency for highest perceived understanding and trust.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies the trade-off and references the concept of an optimal transparency level, it does not empirically determine what this optimal level is or how it varies across different user types and contexts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the level of detail in explanations (Why vs. How) across different user groups, measuring trust, satisfaction, and perceived transparency to identify the point of maximum combined benefit.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do personal characteristics (e.g., background knowledge, expertise) influence the effectiveness of Why versus How explanations in recommender systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that users' background knowledge affects their perception of explanations, with some finding How explanations overwhelming while others find them transparent. It also references related work showing personal characteristics impact explanation perception.
- Why unresolved: The study identifies this relationship but doesn't systematically investigate which specific personal characteristics matter most or how to tailor explanations based on these characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Large-scale studies measuring user characteristics (education level, domain expertise, technical background) and correlating them with preferences for explanation types, then developing user models to predict optimal explanation strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are the findings about Why and How explanation trade-offs generalizable across different application domains beyond scientific literature recommendation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges this as a limitation, stating the analysis was performed in a single domain and findings need validation in other domains.
- Why unresolved: The study's qualitative user study was domain-specific, and while it provides insights into explanation design, it doesn't establish whether these insights transfer to e-commerce, social media, entertainment, or other recommendation contexts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Replication studies in multiple domains (e.g., movie recommendations, product recommendations, social media content) measuring similar transparency, trust, and satisfaction metrics to determine if the Why/Why trade-off persists across contexts.

## Limitations
- Small sample size (N=12) limits generalizability of findings
- Qualitative nature of study makes it difficult to establish causal relationships between explanation types and user perceptions
- Single domain focus (scientific literature recommendation) restricts applicability to other recommendation contexts

## Confidence
- Claim that Why explanations improve satisfaction while How explanations improve transparency/trust: Medium confidence
- Claim that user type determines explanation effectiveness: Low confidence (limited systematic evidence)
- Claim of trade-off between transparency/trust and satisfaction: Medium confidence (well-documented but mechanisms speculative)
