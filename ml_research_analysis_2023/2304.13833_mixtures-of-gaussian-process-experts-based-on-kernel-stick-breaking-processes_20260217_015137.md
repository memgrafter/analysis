# Mixtures of Gaussian process experts based on kernel stick-breaking processes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13833
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13833
- Reference count: 36
- This paper proposes a new mixture of Gaussian process experts model based on kernel stick-breaking processes to improve predictive performance

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel mixture of Gaussian process experts model that uses kernel stick-breaking processes for gating. The key innovation is a gating function that leverages kernel similarity between inputs to determine expert assignments, replacing heuristic approaches with a principled similarity measure. The model employs slice sampling for tractable posterior computation, avoiding the complexity of retrospective MCMC methods. Experiments on six datasets demonstrate superior predictive performance compared to baseline models.

## Method Summary
The proposed model uses kernel stick-breaking processes to create a mixture of Gaussian process experts. Each expert is a GP with squared exponential covariance, and the gating function uses kernel similarity (scaled Euclidean distance) to determine mixture weights. The posterior sampling employs a within-Gibbs sampler with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for continuous parameters, slice sampling for truncation, and rejection sampling for hyperparameters. The model automatically determines the number of experts through the dependent Dirichlet process framework.

## Key Results
- Superior predictive performance compared to baseline RG model across six datasets
- Improved RMSE, NLPD, and CRPS metrics demonstrate better uncertainty quantification
- Effective capture of complex data-generating processes with non-stationary behavior
- Demonstrated scalability with efficient slice sampling approach

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Kernel stick-breaking processes (KSBP) improve expert gating by directly comparing input locations to expert centers using scaled Euclidean distance
- Mechanism: The gating function computes mixture weights as `wn,i = vi * exp(-||xn - hi||^2 / r^2) * (1 - sum of previous weights)`. This replaces the heuristic occupation-number-based approach with a principled similarity measure
- Core assumption: Input similarity can be effectively captured by a kernel that decays exponentially with scaled Euclidean distance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The model uses a novel gating function that leverages the similarity between inputs captured by kernels"
  - [section 2]: "the input-dependent mixture weight is defined as wn,i = vi * κ(xn, hi) * (1 - sum of previous weights)"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus shows related work on mixtures of experts but doesn't directly validate the kernel similarity approach
- Break condition: If the true data-generating process doesn't exhibit local smoothness or if the kernel width r is poorly calibrated, the similarity measure may fail to capture appropriate expert assignments

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Slice sampling avoids the complexity of retrospective MCMC while achieving random truncation of the infinite GP expert sequence
- Mechanism: Instead of retrospective MCMC, slice sampling uses auxiliary variables un to control the sufficient number of candidate experts. The stopping criterion is modified to handle input-dependent weights: stop when un > 1 - sum(wn,i) for all n
- Core assumption: The slice sampling approach can adequately truncate the infinite stick-breaking process while maintaining the probabilistic properties of the dependent DP
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "we take this approach in designing the sampler" and explains the modified stopping criterion
  - [section 3.2]: "we cannot sample un given wn,sn for all n at once; rather, we must interweave the sampling"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors discuss related sampling techniques but don't validate the specific slice sampling modification
- Break condition: If the slice sampling truncation fails to capture enough experts for complex data, or if the interweaving sampling creates poor mixing in the Markov chain

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using geometric priors for stick-breaking probabilities (α and β) enables tractable posterior computation via rejection sampling
- Mechanism: The geometric prior leads to a posterior that can be sampled using rejection sampling with a carefully constructed envelope. The conjugacy properties of the geometric-beta relationship simplify the sampling process
- Core assumption: The geometric prior maintains conjugacy properties that allow rejection sampling to be efficient

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data -> GP Experts -> Kernel Gating Function -> Slice Sampling -> Posterior Inference -> Predictions
- Critical path: The critical path is the Gibbs sampling loop that updates (1) kernel width r, (2) stick-breaking probabilities v and expert locations h with slice sampling, (3) hyper-parameters α and β, (4) expert assignments s, and (5) GP hyper-parameters θ
- Design tradeoffs: Using slice sampling simplifies implementation but may be less efficient than retrospective MCMC for some problems. The geometric priors enable tractable computation but may not be optimal for all applications. The squared exponential covariance is simple but may not capture all types of non-stationarity
- Failure signatures: Poor predictive performance may indicate over-splitting of the gating function, insufficient truncation of experts, or poorly calibrated kernel width. Computational inefficiency may indicate problems with the slice sampling implementation or rejection sampling for hyper-parameters

## Limitations
- Performance heavily depends on kernel width parameter r, which lacks clear guidance for selection or calibration
- Slice sampling may be less efficient than retrospective MCMC for some problems, potentially limiting scalability
- Geometric priors for stick-breaking probabilities may not be optimal for all applications and could introduce bias if poorly calibrated

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mathematical formulation of the kernel stick-breaking process and its relationship to dependent Dirichlet processes is well-established and theoretically sound
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical improvements demonstrated on six datasets are encouraging, but the small sample sizes (30 training samples) and limited diversity of test functions make it difficult to assess generalizability
- **Medium confidence**: The slice sampling modification for input-dependent weights appears reasonable, but its effectiveness compared to retrospective MCMC is not thoroughly validated
