# Sample Average Approximation for Black-Box VI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06803
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06803
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: New SAA-based approach for black-box VI that achieves faster performance than Adam and batched quasi-Newton, with less than 200ms to achieve target ELBO on wells dataset vs almost a minute for Adam.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a sample average approximation (SAA) approach for black-box variational inference that transforms the stochastic optimization problem into a deterministic one, enabling the use of quasi-Newton methods with line search. The method uses a sequence of SAA problems with increasing sample sizes to mitigate overfitting and automate hyperparameter selection. Experiments show superior performance compared to Adam and batched quasi-Newton methods, particularly for complex models.

## Method Summary
The method transforms the stochastic optimization problem in variational inference into a sequence of deterministic problems using sample average approximation. For each iteration, a fixed sample of noise is drawn and the surrogate objective is optimized using L-BFGS with line search. The sample size doubles at each iteration to reduce overfitting, and a heuristic policy automates hyperparameter selection. A convergence checker using statistical tests on training vs testing noise determines when to stop.

## Key Results
- SAA approach achieves faster convergence than Adam and batched quasi-Newton on complex models
- On wells dataset, achieves target ELBO in less than 200ms compared to almost a minute for Adam
- Successfully applied to both diagonal and full-rank Gaussian approximating distributions on 5 UCI datasets
- Demonstrates effectiveness on models from Stan examples repository

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Replacing stochastic gradient updates with deterministic optimization over fixed samples (SAA) reduces variance in the gradient estimate, improving convergence stability.
- Mechanism: SAA fixes a sample of size n, constructs a deterministic objective function $\hat{L}_\epsilon(\theta)$, and uses deterministic optimization (L-BFGS + line search) to find a local optimum.
- Core assumption: Fixed sample is large enough that the surrogate objective is close in expectation to the true ELBO.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] and [section 3.1] show SAA transforms stochastic optimization into deterministic problems.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Doubling the sample size at each iteration mitigates overfitting to the training noise.
- Mechanism: Solving a sequence of SAA problems with increasing sample sizes progressively reduces the gap between surrogate and true ELBO.
- Core assumption: Sample size sequence grows fast enough to ensure convergence with probability one.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.1] references epi-convergence theory and [section 5.1] shows empirical superiority.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using line search in deterministic optimization avoids the need for hand-tuned step-size schedules.
- Mechanism: Exact gradient and function values enable strong Wolfe conditions for automatic step-size selection.
- Core assumption: Surrogate objective is smooth enough for line search to be effective.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.1] and [section 4] show line search advantages over manual step-size tuning.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: SAA for VI -> Opt -> Convergence checker
- Critical path: 
  1. Initialize parameters θ₀, sample size n₀, and max iterations τ₀
  2. For each iteration t: Draw training noise, optimize θ_t = Opt(θ_{t-1}, n_t, ϵ_{n_t}, τ_t), check convergence
  3. Return θ_t when converged
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sample size growth rate: Too slow → persistent overfitting; too fast → excessive computation
  - Initial sample size n₀: Too small → poor surrogate; too large → wasted computation
  - Stopping criteria: Too lax → premature termination; too strict → unnecessary iterations
- Failure signatures:
  - Slow convergence: Sample size growth too aggressive or initial parameters too far from optimum
  - Divergence: Sample size too small or line search failing to find valid step
  - Overfitting: Sample size growth too slow or insufficient testing noise
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run SAA for VI on Bayesian logistic regression with diagonal covariance, compare to Adam
  2. Vary initial sample size n₀ (16, 32, 64) and observe effect on convergence speed
  3. Replace L-BFGS with SGD in inner loop and measure impact on convergence stability

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Basis in paper: [explicit] "it is currently unknown how well these theoretical results can be applied to our specific setting"
- Why unresolved: Theoretical results by Mak et al. (1999) provide convergence insights but applicability to VI with increasing sample sizes and quasi-Newton methods is unclear
- What evidence would resolve it: Further theoretical analysis or empirical studies demonstrating convergence behavior and solution quality

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different sample size scheduling strategies on SAA for VI performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Uses geometric growth but doesn't explore alternatives
- Why unresolved: Choice of scheduling can significantly affect computational cost and convergence but paper doesn't compare strategies
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing geometric, harmonic, or adaptive schedules across models and datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does SAA for VI compare to other stochastic optimization methods like natural gradient methods?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Only compares to Adam and batched quasi-Newton
- Why unresolved: Paper demonstrates advantages over two methods but doesn't explore other stochastic optimization techniques
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies evaluating SAA against natural gradient methods or stochastic approximation methods

## Limitations
- Convergence checker implementation details are not fully specified, particularly IW-ELBO computation and t-tests
- Specific stopping criteria for SAA problems (max sample size, delta threshold) are not clearly defined
- Comparison limited to few models and datasets, generalizability to broader settings unclear
- Computational cost for very large sample sizes is not discussed, raising scalability concerns

## Confidence

- High confidence: Core mechanism of using SAA to transform stochastic VI into deterministic problem enabling deterministic optimization tools is well-established
- Medium confidence: Doubling sample size mitigates overfitting is supported empirically but relies on assumption about growth rate
- Low confidence: Claims of superior performance to Adam and batched quasi-Newton based on limited experiments may not generalize
