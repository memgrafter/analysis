# LLM-RM at SemEval-2023 Task 2: Multilingual Complex NER using XLM-RoBERTa

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03300
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03300
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa-base achieved F1-scores of 49.06-69.04 on development sets for 12 languages across 30 entity types

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a multilingual Named Entity Recognition (NER) approach using XLM-RoBERTa for the SemEval-2023 Task 2 MultiCoNER II challenge. The team fine-tuned XLM-RoBERTa-base on datasets of 12 languages (Bangla, Chinese, English, Farsi, French, German, Hindi, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish, Swedish, and Ukrainian) to recognize complex named entities across 30 entity types. The model achieved F1-scores ranging from 49.06 to 69.04 on development sets, with Hindi and German performing best. On the final test set, English, German, Spanish, and Hindi achieved overall F1-scores between 52.08 and 63.29. Creative Work entities consistently performed best, while Person and Product entities were most challenging. The results demonstrate XLM-RoBERTa's effectiveness for multilingual NER, particularly for low-resource languages.

## Method Summary
The team employed XLM-RoBERTa-base as the foundation model and fine-tuned it on multilingual NER datasets containing 12 languages and 30 entity types. The fine-tuning process involved training on the provided datasets for each language, with special attention to handling low-resource languages through transfer learning capabilities of the multilingual model. The approach leveraged the pre-trained multilingual representations from XLM-RoBERTa to enable cross-lingual transfer and knowledge sharing across languages. The evaluation was conducted on both development and test sets provided by the SemEval-2023 Task 2 organizers, with performance measured using standard NER metrics including precision, recall, and F1-score.

## Key Results
- Achieved F1-scores ranging from 49.06 to 69.04 on development sets across 12 languages
- English, German, Spanish, and Hindi achieved overall F1-scores between 52.08 and 63.29 on the final test set
- Creative Work entities performed best (F1: 80.14-90.48) while Person and Product entities were most challenging (F1: 45.93-57.22)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages XLM-RoBERTa's pre-trained multilingual representations, which capture cross-lingual semantic relationships and enable effective transfer learning across languages. The model's architecture allows it to share learned patterns across languages while maintaining language-specific distinctions. Fine-tuning on the NER task adapts these general representations to the specific entity recognition task while preserving the multilingual capabilities. The transformer-based architecture with self-attention mechanisms enables the model to capture long-range dependencies and contextual relationships necessary for complex entity recognition, particularly important for distinguishing between similar entity types.

## Limitations
- Evaluation only covers 12 out of 30 entity types, limiting generalizability of performance claims
- Results based on development set with artificial data that may not reflect real-world performance
- No cross-validation or robustness testing against out-of-distribution data reported

## Confidence
- High confidence in reported F1-scores for the 12 evaluated entity types on both development and test sets
- Medium confidence in comparative performance analysis across languages due to limited entity type coverage
- Low confidence in generalization to other entity types not evaluated (60% of entity types not assessed)

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate the model's performance on all 30 entity types to establish complete coverage metrics and identify potential performance variations across the full taxonomy
2. Conduct out-of-distribution testing using data from different domains or time periods to assess model robustness and potential performance degradation
3. Implement cross-validation across the available datasets to establish confidence intervals for the reported F1-scores and test for statistical significance between language performances
