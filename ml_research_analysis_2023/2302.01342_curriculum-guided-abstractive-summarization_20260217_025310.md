# Curriculum-Guided Abstractive Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01342
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01342
- Reference count: 36
- Primary result: BART augmented with sentence cross-attention and curriculum learning outperforms standard BART by 5.2%, 11.22%, and 5.4% in ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L respectively on Reddit TIFU

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenges of content selection and training efficiency in Transformer-based abstractive summarization models. The authors propose two complementary approaches: a modified BART architecture with an additional sentence cross-attention layer in the decoder, and a curriculum learning strategy using SUPER LOSS. These innovations are evaluated across multiple datasets including Reddit TIFU, Webis-TLDR-17, CNN/DM, and XSum, demonstrating significant improvements in summarization quality and faster convergence.

## Method Summary
The proposed method, CURR SUM, enhances the standard BART architecture by adding a sentence cross-attention layer to the decoder, enabling better content selection during the summarization process. Additionally, the authors incorporate a curriculum learning strategy using SUPER LOSS, which adjusts the learning difficulty during training to improve both efficiency and model performance. The approach is evaluated on multiple datasets, with results showing substantial improvements over the baseline BART model in terms of ROUGE scores and human evaluation metrics.

## Key Results
- CURR SUM outperforms standard BART by 5.2% in ROUGE-1, 11.22% in ROUGE-2, and 5.4% in ROUGE-L on Reddit TIFU
- Curriculum learning strategy leads to faster convergence compared to standard training
- Human evaluation demonstrates improved fluency, informativeness, and overall quality of generated summaries

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The enhanced content selection through sentence cross-attention allows the decoder to better identify and focus on important information from the source text. The curriculum learning approach using SUPER LOSS enables more efficient training by gradually increasing task difficulty, which helps the model learn more effectively and generalize better across different domains.

## Limitations
- The substantial ROUGE-1 improvement claims lack clarity on whether these are relative or absolute improvements
- Human evaluation methodology lacks detailed protocols and inter-annotator agreement metrics
- Generalization claims beyond tested domains are not adequately supported with empirical evidence

## Confidence
High: The experimental setup is reasonable and follows standard practices for abstractive summarization evaluation. The use of multiple datasets provides reasonable coverage of different summarization domains.

Medium: The ROUGE score improvements appear significant, though the exact baseline configurations and whether these are relative or absolute improvements need clarification. The curriculum learning approach shows promise but needs more extensive validation across different model architectures.

Low: The human evaluation claims lack sufficient methodological detail to fully assess their validity. The generalization claims beyond tested domains are not adequately supported with empirical evidence.
