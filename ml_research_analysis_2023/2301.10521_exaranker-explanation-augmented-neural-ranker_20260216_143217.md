# ExaRanker: Explanation-Augmented Neural Ranker

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10521
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10521
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Finetuning on a few thousand examples with synthetic explanations performs on par with models finetuned on three times more examples without explanations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ExaRanker, a method that augments retrieval datasets with explanations generated by large language models (LLMs) like GPT-3.5. The key idea is to train a sequence-to-sequence model to output both a relevance label and an explanation for each query-document pair, enabling the model to learn from richer supervision signals. The authors demonstrate that finetuning on a few thousand examples with synthetic explanations performs on par with models finetuned on three times more examples without explanations. Furthermore, ExaRanker incurs no additional computational cost during ranking and allows explanations to be requested on demand.

## Method Summary
ExaRanker augments retrieval datasets with explanations generated by large language models (LLMs) like GPT-3.5. The approach involves training a sequence-to-sequence model to output both a relevance label and an explanation for each query-document pair. This allows the model to learn from richer supervision signals. The authors finetune the model on a few thousand examples with synthetic explanations and demonstrate that it performs on par with models finetuned on three times more examples without explanations. During ranking, explanations are generated on demand, incurring no additional computational cost.

## Key Results
- Finetuning on a few thousand examples with synthetic explanations performs on par with models finetuned on three times more examples without explanations
- ExaRanker achieves an average nDCG@10 score of 0.474 in zero-shot evaluation on six BEIR datasets
- Outperforms a baseline model without explanations by 0.8 points

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper does not provide a detailed explanation of the mechanism behind why this approach works. However, it is likely that the synthetic explanations generated by LLMs provide additional context and semantic information that helps the model better understand the relevance of query-document pairs, leading to improved ranking performance.

- Large language models (LLMs) like GPT-3.5: Why needed - to generate high-quality synthetic explanations. Quick check - verify the quality and relevance of the generated explanations.
- Retrieval datasets: Why needed - to train and evaluate the ranking model. Quick check - ensure the datasets cover a diverse range of query-document pairs and relevance labels.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Query/Document -> Sequence-to-Sequence Model -> Relevance Label + Explanation

Critical path:
1. Input: Query and document pairs
2. Sequence-to-sequence model processes the input
3. Output: Relevance label and explanation

Design tradeoffs:
- Using synthetic explanations generated by LLMs vs. human-annotated explanations
- Finetuning on fewer examples with explanations vs. more examples without explanations

Failure signatures:
- Low-quality or irrelevant explanations generated by the LLM
- Overfitting to the synthetic explanations during finetuning
- Explanations not aligning with human reasoning for ranking relevance

First experiments:
1. Evaluate the quality of the synthetic explanations generated by the LLM
2. Compare the performance of ExaRanker with and without explanations on a small-scale retrieval task
3. Analyze the impact of the explanation generation order (labels first vs. explanations first) on ranking performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out any open questions. However, potential open questions include:
- How do the synthetic explanations compare to human-annotated explanations in terms of quality and relevance?
- Can the approach be extended to other ranking tasks beyond information retrieval?
- How does the explanation generation process scale to larger datasets and more complex ranking scenarios?

## Limitations
- Evaluation is limited to zero-shot transfer across six BEIR datasets; in-domain performance on the datasets used for finetuning is not reported
- Reliance on GPT-3.5 for generating explanations introduces potential concerns about reproducibility, bias, and cost if scaled to larger datasets or deployed in production systems
- The optimal order of generating labels and explanations is not definitively established

## Confidence
High confidence in the core claims regarding the effectiveness of explanation-augmented training for improving ranking performance and sample efficiency, given the reported results and ablation studies. Medium confidence in the claim of zero additional computational cost during ranking, as it depends on practical factors not fully explored in the paper.

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate ExaRanker's in-domain performance on the datasets used for finetuning to confirm the reported sample efficiency gains hold beyond zero-shot transfer.
2. Replicate the experiments using open-source LLMs for explanation generation to assess the impact on performance and practical deployment considerations.
3. Conduct a user study to determine whether the generated explanations are perceived as helpful and aligned with human reasoning in ranking tasks.
