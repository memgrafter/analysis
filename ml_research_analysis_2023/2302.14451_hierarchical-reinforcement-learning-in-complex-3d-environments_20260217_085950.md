# Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning in Complex 3D Environments

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14451
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14451
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: H2O2 achieves competitive performance on DeepMind Hard Eight tasks, matching strong flat Muesli baseline

## Executive Summary
This work introduces H2O2, a hierarchical deep reinforcement learning agent that learns to use temporally extended behaviors (options) from scratch in complex 3D environments. H2O2 combines primitive actions and goal-conditioned options in a continuous action space, training the low-level controller (LLC) offline from its own experience while using Muesli for the high-level controller (HLC). The agent demonstrates effective use of options for behaviors like navigating toward objects and carrying them, while using primitive actions for fine control like reorienting view.

## Method Summary
H2O2 is a hierarchical hybrid offline-online agent with a high-level controller using Muesli and a low-level controller trained offline via goal-conditioned RL. The HLC selects either primitive actions or latent goals from a learned distribution, while the LLC executes goal-conditioned primitive actions for multiple steps before termination. The system uses hindsight relabeling to train the LLC on achieved observations, with a KL-regularized multivariate normal prior encouraging goals to cluster around the origin. The LLC is trained independently of the HLC to avoid non-stationarity issues.

## Key Results
- H2O2 is competitive with a strong flat Muesli baseline on DeepMind Hard Eight tasks
- Options typically last 2-5 steps, used for navigation and object manipulation behaviors
- Increasing temporal abstraction or option space does not always improve performance
- KL regularization weight β=1 performs best, showing the importance of structured goal spaces

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Goal-conditioned RL and hindsight relabeling
  - Why needed here: H2O2 learns options by treating achieved observations as goals, requiring the ability to relabel trajectories and train goal-conditioned policies.
  - Quick check question: If you swap the achieved observation at time 𝑡𝑒 with the initial observation at 𝑡𝑠 in a trajectory, what reward does the LLC receive for that relabeled subtrajectory?

- Concept: Semi-MDP and option execution model
  - Why needed here: The HLC operates in an SMDP where options take multiple primitive steps; understanding call-and-return execution is critical to designing termination criteria.
  - Quick check question: What happens to the HLC's discount factor when an option takes 5 primitive steps but the SMDP step is discounted only once?

- Concept: Variational autoencoders for latent goal spaces
  - Why needed here: The Goal Encoder outputs a distribution over latent goals; regularization via KL divergence shapes the goal space geometry for the HLC.
  - Quick check question: If β=0 in the Goal Encoder's KL loss, what distribution does the latent goal 𝑔 follow during training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: HLC (Muesli) -> Goal/Action selection -> LLC (offline-trained goal-conditioned policy + value function + behavior cloning) -> Environment -> HLC observation update
- Critical path: HLC selects action or latent goal → LLC executes primitive actions conditioned on goal → Environment returns observation → HLC updates representation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Goal space dimensionality vs. expressiveness: Too large → sparse goals; too small → loss of useful options
  - KL regularization weight β: Controls compression; too high → collapse, too low → noisy HLC
  - Timeout length: Longer → more temporal abstraction but harder learning; shorter → less abstraction
- Failure signatures:
  - LLC timeout rate spikes → goals are unattainable or LLC policy is weak
  - HLC primitive action frequency dominates → goal space too noisy or options ineffective
  - Value estimates diverge → KL regularization too weak or behavior cloning weight mis-tuned
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Sweep β from 0 to 1 to observe impact on goal space smoothness and HLC performance
  2. Test different timeout lengths (7, 16, 32) to measure option duration vs. task success
  3. Compare H2O2 with and without the behavior cloning regularizer in the LLC to quantify stability impact

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we structure the goal space to accelerate the HLC learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "we expect that the performance of H2O2 will improve with goal sampling distributions that incorporate principled techniques for option discovery."
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore specific methods for structuring the goal space beyond the basic approach used.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing H2O2 performance with different goal space structures, such as hierarchical goal spaces or goal spaces based on learned abstractions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is it possible to learn effective HLCs with a general-purpose RL algorithm?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses Muesli, a state-of-the-art RL algorithm, for the HLC but does not explore whether other general-purpose RL algorithms could work as well.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one specific RL algorithm for the HLC.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing H2O2 performance with different RL algorithms for the HLC, such as PPO or A3C.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the HLC agent learn with a very large number of complex options, but remain competitive with a flat baseline?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how increasing the number of options does not always improve performance, suggesting that the agent may struggle to learn effectively with too many options.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore methods for efficiently learning with a large number of options.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing H2O2 performance with varying numbers of options, and comparing it to a flat baseline with the same number of parameters.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Are image goals good enough, or what other goal modalities can we use?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses image goals for the LLC but does not explore whether other goal modalities, such as text or vector descriptions, could be more effective.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests image goals for the LLC.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing H2O2 performance with different goal modalities for the LLC, such as text descriptions or vector representations of desired states.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Which goals should we train the LLC to achieve?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "we used a KL regularizer term with weight β to encourage the multivariate normal to stay close to a standard normal distribution," suggesting that the choice of goals is important for LLC training.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore how the choice of goals affects LLC performance or how to select goals that are most beneficial for learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing LLC performance with different sets of goals, and analyzing how the choice of goals affects the learned options and overall task performance.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness of temporal abstraction and offline training is empirically shown but lacks theoretical guarantees
- Optimal balance between temporal abstraction and option complexity remains task-dependent
- Current framework may not generalize well to domains with fundamentally different state-action structures
- Study focuses on specific DeepMind Hard Eight tasks, limiting broader applicability claims

## Confidence
- High confidence: H2O2 achieves competitive performance on DeepMind Hard Eight tasks, matching the strong flat Muesli baseline
- Medium confidence: The mechanisms of temporal abstraction and offline LLC training contribute to improved credit assignment and stability
- Low confidence: The specific architectural choices (goal space dimension, KL regularization weight) will generalize across diverse tasks
