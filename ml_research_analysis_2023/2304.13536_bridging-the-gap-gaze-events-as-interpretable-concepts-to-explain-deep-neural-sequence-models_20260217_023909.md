# Bridging the Gap: Gaze Events as Interpretable Concepts to Explain Deep Neural Sequence Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13536
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13536
- Reference count: 40
- The paper presents a method for explaining deep neural sequence models for oculomotoric biometric identification using gaze event detection

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to interpreting deep neural sequence models by leveraging gaze events as interpretable concepts for oculomotoric biometric identification. The method bridges the gap between complex neural network predictions and human-understandable gaze patterns by detecting fixations and saccades, then measuring their concept influence on model predictions. The study demonstrates that saccadic peak velocity phases are most influential for biometric identification, providing insights into which gaze patterns the model relies on. This work advances explainable AI by connecting abstract neural representations to interpretable oculomotor events.

## Method Summary
The method involves using established gaze event detection algorithms (I-VT) to identify fixations and saccades in gaze sequences, then calculating concept influence by measuring the overlap between the highest feature attributions and the event segmentation. The approach evaluates how different gaze events (fixations vs. saccades) influence model predictions for biometric identification. Concept influence is quantified by comparing the model's feature importance attributions with the detected gaze event boundaries, allowing researchers to determine which specific gaze patterns are most relevant for the model's decisions.

## Key Results
- Saccades demonstrate substantially higher concept influence than fixations for biometric identification
- Saccadic peak velocity phases are identified as the most influential components
- The method successfully links neural network predictions to interpretable gaze concepts
- Results provide insights into which parts of gaze sequences are most relevant for biometric identification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by connecting human-interpretable gaze events (fixations and saccades) to the abstract feature representations learned by deep neural networks. By using established gaze event detection algorithms to segment gaze sequences and then measuring concept influence through feature attribution overlap, the method reveals which specific gaze patterns the model relies on for biometric identification. The biological relevance of saccadic peak velocity as a distinguishing biometric feature likely explains why these events show higher concept influence compared to fixations.

## Architecture Onboarding

The core architecture follows this component path:
Raw Gaze Data -> I-VT Event Detection -> Deep Neural Sequence Model -> Feature Attribution Map -> Concept Influence Calculation

Critical path: Gaze sequences must be properly segmented into events before concept influence can be calculated, making event detection the most critical preprocessing step.

Design tradeoffs: The method trades off computational complexity of gaze event detection against interpretability gains. Using I-VT provides speed but may miss nuanced events compared to more sophisticated algorithms.

Failure signatures: Poor gaze event detection (due to noisy data or inappropriate parameters) will propagate errors to concept influence calculations. Model architectures with very distributed feature importance may show low concept influence scores even for relevant events.

## Limitations
- Findings are specific to oculomotoric biometric identification and may not generalize to other sequence modeling tasks
- Results depend on the I-VT gaze event detection algorithm and its parameter settings
- The relatively small dataset (N=60 subjects) may limit robustness of findings
- Assumes neural network representations align with human-interpretable gaze concepts, which may not always hold

## Confidence

High confidence in the validity of gaze event detection and concept influence calculation methodology
Medium confidence in the biological interpretation of saccadic peak velocity importance
Medium confidence in comparative saccade vs. fixation influence findings
Low confidence in direct application to other biometric or sequence modeling domains
