# Syntactic Complexity Identification, Measurement, and Reduction Through Controlled Syntactic Simplification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07774
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07774
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: Proposed approach achieved 90%+ accuracy in syntactic complexity measurement and 94.5% cosine similarity in controlled simplification using GPT-3 re-annotation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an approach for syntactic complexity measurement and reduction through controlled simplification for knowledge graph (KG) creation. The authors propose an algorithm that assigns weights to sentence complexity signs (tokens, verbs, conjunctions) and uses a threshold to classify sentences as simple or complex. For complex sentences, a syntactic simplification (SynSim) process splits and rephrases based on dependency parsing while preserving original mentions and at least one triple (subject-verb-object) per simplified sentence. Evaluation on a benchmark corpus showed their SC measurement achieved 90%+ accuracy. GPT-3 re-annotation of the corpus produced 2,277 simplified sentences vs 3,565 originally, with 94.5% and 90.4% similarity (cosine and Jaccard) to human annotations.

## Method Summary
The approach consists of two main algorithms: a syntactic complexity measurement algorithm that assigns weighted scores to tokens, verbs, and conjunctions using weights 0.07, 0.3, and 0.4 respectively, and a controlled syntactic simplification (SynSim) process that splits complex sentences based on dependency parsing while preserving original mentions and ensuring each simplified sentence contains at least one triple. The system uses a threshold of SC > 1 to classify sentences, then applies GPT-3 (Text-Davinci-003) for re-annotation, followed by manual validation. The approach aims to improve KG creation by enabling accurate triple extraction without information loss from summarization or synonym substitution.

## Key Results
- SC measurement algorithm achieved 90%+ accuracy on the 'Split & Rephrase' benchmark corpus
- GPT-3 re-annotation produced 2,277 simplified sentences vs 3,565 originally
- Simplified sentences showed 94.5% cosine similarity and 90.4% Jaccard similarity to human annotations
- Each simplified sentence preserved at least one subject-verb-object triple

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The syntactic complexity measurement algorithm can accurately classify complex and simple sentences with a threshold-based approach.
- Mechanism: The algorithm assigns weighted scores to tokens, verbs, and conjunctions, and uses a threshold (SC > 1) to classify sentences. This leverages the structural patterns of compound and complex sentences identified in linguistics research.
- Core assumption: Sentence complexity can be effectively captured through a linear combination of token count, verb count, and conjunction count with appropriate weights.
- Evidence anchors: The evaluation on the 'Split & Rephrase' corpus showed 90%+ accuracy for the SC measurement algorithm.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The controlled syntactic simplification (SynSim) process can split complex sentences into simpler ones while preserving factual information and original mentions.
- Mechanism: SynSim identifies conjunctions, splits sentences into independent clauses, and performs entity co-reference resolution to maintain original mentions. Each simplified sentence contains at least one triple (subject-verb-object).
- Core assumption: Complex sentences can be decomposed into independent clauses that maintain semantic relationships without losing factual content.
- Evidence anchors: The evaluation showed 94.5% and 90.4% similarity to human annotations using cosine and Jaccard similarity metrics.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-3 re-annotation can effectively simplify complex sentences while maintaining factual accuracy and improving the quality of the corpus.
- Mechanism: GPT-3 (specifically Text-Davinci-003) is used to generate simplified versions of complex sentences, which are then manually validated to ensure quality.
- Core assumption: Large language models can understand complex sentence structures and generate grammatically correct, simplified alternatives that preserve meaning.
- Evidence anchors: The re-annotated corpus showed improved performance with 94.34% accuracy compared to the original 90.15%.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Unstructured text -> Syntactic Complexity Measurement -> Threshold Classification -> Controlled Syntactic Simplification (SynSim) -> Output
- Critical path: 1. Text preprocessing (tokenization, POS tagging) 2. SC measurement using Algorithm 1 3. Classification based on threshold 4. SynSim process for complex sentences 5. Entity co-reference resolution 6. Output generation
- Design tradeoffs: Accuracy vs. computational cost (threshold-based approach reduces computational load but may miss some complex sentences), Simplification depth vs. information preservation (deeper simplification may lose some contextual information), Manual validation vs. automation (human validation ensures quality but increases time and cost)
- Failure signatures: Low similarity scores between simplified and original sentences, Loss of factual information in simplified sentences, Incorrect classification of sentence complexity, Inconsistent entity mentions across simplified sentences
- First 3 experiments: 1. Test the SC measurement algorithm on a small set of manually annotated sentences to verify accuracy. 2. Evaluate the SynSim process on compound sentences with clear clause boundaries. 3. Compare GPT-3 generated simplifications with human annotations for a subset of complex sentences.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the proposed syntactic complexity measurement algorithm compare to existing neural network-based approaches on the same benchmark datasets?
- Basis in paper: The authors state their algorithm achieved 90%+ accuracy on a benchmark corpus, but do not compare to neural approaches
- Why unresolved: The paper does not include any comparison with state-of-the-art neural network methods for syntactic complexity measurement
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison results showing accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 scores between the proposed algorithm and leading neural network approaches on the same benchmark datasets

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of syntactic simplification on downstream information extraction performance compared to other text simplification approaches?
- Basis in paper: While the authors argue their method is better for KG creation, they don't provide empirical evidence showing improved triple extraction rates compared to other simplification methods
- Why unresolved: The paper claims their approach preserves original mentions and triples, but doesn't measure actual IE performance improvement
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing information extraction performance metrics (precision, recall, F1) on complex sentences before and after SynSim simplification, compared to other simplification approaches

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of GPT-3 model parameters affect the quality and quantity of generated simplified sentences?
- Basis in paper: The authors used "Text-Davinci-003" model for re-annotation but don't explore parameter sensitivity
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't investigate how different GPT-3 model configurations or temperature settings impact the simplification output quality
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of simplification quality metrics across different GPT-3 model versions, temperature settings, and prompt formulations

## Limitations

- Limited evaluation scope based on a single benchmark corpus (720 sentences)
- Threshold sensitivity with no exploration of optimal thresholds for different domains
- Heavy dependence on dependency parsing accuracy without addressing parser failures
- Limited integration details with actual knowledge graph creation workflows

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The syntactic complexity measurement algorithm can achieve >90% accuracy on the Split & Rephrase benchmark corpus; Controlled syntactic simplification can preserve factual information while splitting complex sentences; GPT-3 can generate simplified sentences with high similarity to human annotations

**Medium Confidence**: The weighted linear combination approach effectively captures sentence complexity; Entity co-reference resolution maintains mention consistency across simplified sentences; The approach generalizes to other domains beyond the benchmark corpus

**Low Confidence**: GPT-3 will consistently produce high-quality simplifications across all text domains; The threshold-based classification works optimally without domain adaptation; The approach handles all types of complex sentence structures (deeply nested, ambiguous, etc.)

## Next Checks
