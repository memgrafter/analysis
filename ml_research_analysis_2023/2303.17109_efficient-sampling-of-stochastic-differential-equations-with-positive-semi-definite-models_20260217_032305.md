# Efficient Sampling of Stochastic Differential Equations with Positive Semi-Definite Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17109
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17109
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: PSD models achieve O(ε^{-(d+1)/(β-2s)}(log(1/ε))^{d+1}) dimension scaling for SDE sampling when solutions are sufficiently smooth

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel approach to sampling from high-dimensional stochastic differential equations (SDEs) by approximating the solution of the associated Fokker-Planck equation using positive semi-definite (PSD) models. The method circumvents the curse of dimensionality by leveraging the smoothness of the solution, requiring only O(ε^{-(d+1)/(β-2s)}(log(1/ε))^{d+1}) model dimension when the solution has smoothness β ≥ 4d+2. The approach combines theoretical analysis with practical algorithms, demonstrating both the mathematical foundation and computational feasibility of PSD-based sampling for SDEs.

## Method Summary
The approach constructs a PSD model of the form f(x) = φ(x)ᵀMφ(x) that approximates the solution of the Fokker-Planck equation up to error ε. This involves selecting base points in space-time, computing kernel matrices, and solving a semi-definite program to find the PSD matrix M. For fractional Fokker-Planck equations, the method approximates the fractional Laplacian using either Gaussian sampling or Bochner's theorem representation. The final sampling is performed using the efficient algorithm from Marteau-Ferey et al. The key insight is that as the true solution becomes smoother (higher β), the required model size grows more slowly with dimension, providing a dimension-free scaling regime.

## Key Results
- Achieves O(ε^{-(d+1)/(β-2s)}(log(1/ε))^{d+1}) model dimension for approximation error ε when β ≥ 4d+2
- Total computational complexity of O(m^3.5 log(1/ε)) for preparatory phase
- Sampling complexity of O(dε^(-2(d+1)/β-2) log(1/ε)^(2d+3)) per sample
- Demonstrates natural approximation of fractional Laplacian operator using PSD models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** PSD models allow efficient representation of probability densities that satisfy the Fokker-Planck equation with controllable approximation error.
- **Mechanism:** The PSD model represents a probability density as a quadratic form f(x) = φ(x)ᵀMφ(x), where M is a positive semi-definite matrix and φ is a feature map. This structure ensures non-negativity and allows exact computation of normalization constants for Gaussian kernels. By solving for M that approximates the solution of the associated PDE up to error ε, we obtain a density that can be sampled efficiently.
- **Core assumption:** The true solution p⋆ can be expressed as a sum of squares of smooth functions, satisfying Assumption 1.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The proposed approach consists in: first, computing the PSD model that satisfies the Fokker-Planck equation (or its fractional variant) associated with the SDE, up to error ε"
  - [section 4.1] "Given m base points pair (˜xi, ˜ti) ∈ ˜X for i ∈ {1,2,...,m} of space and time, let us consider the following approximation model for the solution of FPE"
- **Break condition:** If the true solution does not satisfy the sum-of-squares structure or has insufficient smoothness (β < 4d+2), the approximation error grows and the computational complexity deteriorates.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The PSD-based representation naturally approximates the fractional Laplacian operator acting on probability densities.
- **Mechanism:** For Gaussian kernels, the fractional Laplacian of a PSD model can be expressed as an expectation involving the model's base points and a Gaussian random variable. This allows empirical estimation without explicitly computing the non-local operator. For shift-invariant kernels, Bochner's theorem provides a spectral representation that similarly enables estimation.
- **Core assumption:** The kernel used in the PSD model admits a representation amenable to fractional Laplacian computation (Gaussian or shift-invariant).
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 4.2] "We demonstrate our result on Gaussian and shift-invariant kernels that can be represented by Bochner's theorem [68]"
  - [section 4.2] "Theorem 3. Consider probability density represented by a PSD model... then 1. if the kernel k is gaussian... we have (−∆)s p(x) = C ∑ Aije−η(∥xi∥2+∥xj∥2)Eξ∼N(µ,Σ)[∥ξ∥2se−jξ⊤x]"
- **Break condition:** If the kernel does not admit a suitable representation (e.g., non-shift-invariant or non-Gaussian), the fractional Laplacian approximation becomes intractable.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** As the true solution becomes smoother (higher β), the required model size grows more slowly with dimension, circumventing the curse of dimensionality.
- **Mechanism:** The approximation error bound shows that the model dimension m required to achieve error ε scales as m = O(ε^{-(d+1)/(β-2s)}(log(1/ε))^{d+1}). When β is large (high smoothness), the exponent -(d+1)/(β-2s) approaches zero, meaning the model size grows very slowly with dimension d. This contrasts with traditional methods where complexity typically scales exponentially with dimension.
- **Core assumption:** The true solution has sufficient smoothness (β ≥ 4d+2) and satisfies the geometric condition on zeros.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "This means that, if the probability associated with the SDE is somewhat regular, i.e. β ≥ 4d+2, then the algorithm requires O(ε^{-0.88} log(1/ε)^{4.5d}) in the preparatory phase"
  - [section 1] "Our results suggest that as the true solution gets smoother, we can circumvent the curse of dimensionality without requiring any sort of convexity"
- **Break condition:** If β is not sufficiently large relative to dimension d, the complexity reduction diminishes and the method loses its dimension-free advantage.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Positive Semi-Definite (PSD) Models**
  - Why needed here: PSD models provide a natural way to represent non-negative functions (probability densities) with efficient sampling properties, which is essential for approximating solutions of Fokker-Planck equations.
  - Quick check question: How does the quadratic form f(x) = φ(x)ᵀMφ(x) ensure that f(x) is non-negative for all x?

- **Concept: Sobolev Spaces and Smoothness**
  - Why needed here: The approximation error bounds depend critically on the smoothness of the true solution, measured in Sobolev spaces W^β_2. Understanding these spaces is essential for interpreting the β parameter and the convergence rates.
  - Quick check question: What does it mean for a function to belong to W^β_2(ℝ^d) for non-integer β?

- **Concept: Fractional Laplacian Operator**
  - Why needed here: The fractional Fokker-Planck equation involves a fractional Laplacian operator, which is non-local and challenging to compute. The PSD model's ability to approximate this operator is central to the method's effectiveness.
  - Quick check question: How does the Fourier multiplier definition F[(−∆)^s f](u) = ||u||^{2s}F[f](u) relate to the integral definition of the fractional Laplacian?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data preparation -> PSD model construction -> Error computation -> Sampling module -> Fractional Laplacian estimator
- **Critical path:**
  1. Generate m base points in (X×T) domain
  2. Compute kernel matrix K(X,T)(X,T)
  3. Solve semi-definite program for M (or Am for compressed model)
  4. Validate approximation error against PDE residual
  5. Sample from resulting PSD model
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Model dimension m vs. approximation accuracy: Larger m gives better accuracy but higher computational cost (O(m^3.5 log(1/ε)) for preparatory phase)
  - Kernel choice: Gaussian kernels allow exact normalization and efficient fractional Laplacian computation, but may be less expressive than other kernels
  - Compression vs. direct representation: The compressed model Am reduces storage but requires additional error analysis
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High residual in PDE constraint: Indicates insufficient model dimension or poor choice of base points
  - Numerical instability in solving for M: May occur if kernel matrix is ill-conditioned
  - Sampling quality degradation: Could indicate numerical errors in the sampling algorithm or poor approximation of the target distribution
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. 1D Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process: Compare PSD approximation with analytical solution for known smooth density
  2. 2D diffusion with Gaussian drift: Test dimension scaling by varying d while keeping β fixed
  3. Fractional FPE with known solution: Validate fractional Laplacian approximation using both Gaussian and shift-invariant kernels

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely heavily on Assumption 1 (PSD model structure) and Assumption 2 (Sobolev smoothness), which may not hold for all SDEs of practical interest
- The sampling phase complexity of O(dε^(-2(d+1)/β-2) log(1/ε)^(2d+3)) per sample still scales poorly with dimension when β is not sufficiently large
- The method loses its dimension-free advantage when the true solution has insufficient smoothness (β < 4d+2)

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** The PSD model representation for probability densities and its non-negativity properties; the computational complexity of the preparatory phase (O(m^3.5 log(1/ε))); the sampling algorithm based on Marteau-Ferey et al.
- **Medium confidence:** The approximation error bounds under Assumptions 1 and 2; the fractional Laplacian computation for Gaussian and shift-invariant kernels; the claim that β ≥ 4d+2 is sufficient for dimension-free scaling
- **Low confidence:** Practical performance on non-smooth or multimodal distributions; numerical stability of the SDP solver for large m; the actual observed dimension scaling in realistic high-dimensional settings

## Next Checks
1. **Smoothness sensitivity study:** Systematically vary β across the critical threshold 4d+2 and measure both approximation quality and computational cost to empirically verify the predicted transition from curse-of-dimensionality to dimension-free scaling.

2. **Robustness to smoothness violations:** Test the method on SDEs with known low-regularity solutions (e.g., discontinuous or multimodal densities) to quantify how quickly performance degrades when Assumptions 1-2 are violated.

3. **Numerical stability analysis:** Implement the full pipeline with varying kernel parameters and model dimensions to identify regimes where the kernel matrix becomes ill-conditioned or the SDP solver fails to converge, comparing against theoretical stability conditions.
