# Regularized EM algorithm

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14989
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14989
- Reference count: 0
- Proposed a regularized EM algorithm for Gaussian mixture models to handle low sample support settings

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a regularized EM (RG-EM) algorithm for Gaussian mixture models that addresses the challenges of low sample support settings where the number of samples is small relative to dimensionality. The method incorporates prior knowledge on covariance structure through a KL-divergence penalty term that shrinks covariance estimates towards user-specified target matrices. This regularization ensures positive definiteness of covariance matrices and improves clustering performance when sample size is limited. The algorithm uses cross-validation to select regularization parameters and demonstrates superior performance compared to vanilla EM and K-means in synthetic experiments.

## Method Summary
The RG-EM algorithm maximizes a penalized GMM likelihood with a KL-divergence penalty term that encourages covariance estimates to shrink toward user-specified target matrices. This regularization addresses the common issue of ill-conditioned covariance matrices in low sample support (LSS) settings where the number of samples is small relative to dimensionality. The method maintains theoretical convergence guarantees similar to the standard EM algorithm while incorporating prior knowledge about covariance structure. Cross-validation is employed to select the optimal regularization parameter λ. The algorithm alternates between E-step (computing posterior probabilities) and M-step (updating parameters with regularization), ensuring positive definiteness throughout the optimization process.

## Key Results
- RG-EM outperforms vanilla EM and K-means in LSS regimes, maintaining high NMI (near 1) even as dimensionality increases
- The method successfully prevents ill-conditioned covariance estimates through KL-divergence regularization
- Cross-validation effectively selects regularization parameters that balance bias and variance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The KL-divergence penalty term in the objective function acts as a regularizer that pulls estimated covariance matrices toward target matrices specified by the user. This shrinkage effect prevents the covariance estimates from becoming singular or ill-conditioned when the sample size is small relative to dimensionality. By incorporating prior knowledge about covariance structure, the algorithm effectively reduces model complexity and avoids overfitting. The regularization also ensures that the covariance matrices remain positive definite throughout the optimization, which is crucial for the numerical stability of the Gaussian mixture model. The cross-validation procedure then tunes the strength of this regularization to achieve optimal clustering performance.

## Foundational Learning
- Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs): Probabilistic models for clustering that assume data points are generated from a mixture of Gaussian distributions - needed to understand the baseline algorithm being regularized
- Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm: Iterative method for finding maximum likelihood estimates in models with latent variables - fundamental to understanding how RG-EM extends standard EM
- KL-divergence: Measure of difference between probability distributions used as regularization term - key to understanding the penalty mechanism
- Positive definite matrices: Symmetric matrices with all positive eigenvalues - critical for ensuring valid covariance matrices
- Cross-validation: Model selection technique for choosing regularization parameters - essential for understanding parameter tuning
- Low sample support (LSS) settings: Scenarios where the number of samples is small relative to dimensionality - the specific problem context addressed
