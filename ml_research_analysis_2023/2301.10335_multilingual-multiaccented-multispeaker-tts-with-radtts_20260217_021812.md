# Multilingual Multiaccented Multispeaker TTS with RADTTS

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10335
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10335
- Reference count: 0
- This paper introduces a multilingual, multiaccented, multispeaker TTS system called RADMMM that extends RADTTS with explicit control over accent, speaker, and fine-grained F0 and energy features.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RADMMM, a multilingual multiaccented multispeaker TTS system that extends RADTTS with explicit control over accent, speaker, and fine-grained F0 and energy features. The system uses a shared IPA token set and alignment learning framework to disentangle speaker, language, and accent attributes without requiring bilingual training data. Through speaker-adversarial loss, data augmentation, and embedding regularization, RADMMM achieves controllable synthesis of speech in any target language with specified accent while retaining the original speaker's voice characteristics.

## Method Summary
The authors extend RADTTS by adding accent conditioning to the text encoder and aligner, using IPA as a shared text token set across all languages. They implement several disentanglement strategies: speaker-adversarial loss to remove speaker information from text representations, data augmentation (formant, F0, and duration scaling) to create synthetic speakers while preserving accents, and VICReg-based embedding regularization to decorrelate accent and speaker embeddings. The RADMMM variant adds fine-grained frame-level control of F0 and energy features. The model is trained on a corpus with 7 languages and 1 speaker per accent, using open-source datasets.

## Key Results
- RADMMM outperforms controlled baselines in retaining speaker voice and accent quality while synthesizing fluent speech in all target languages and accents
- Human subjective evaluation shows improved accent similarity and speaker timbre retention compared to baseline RADTTS-ML
- The system successfully demonstrates multilingual synthesis without requiring bilingual training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Using IPA as a shared token set reduces language-speaker entanglement in low-resource multilingual TTS by forcing phonetic-level alignment learning across all languages, preventing separate encoders from creating language-specific feature spaces that entangle speaker identity with language.

### Mechanism 2
Data augmentation with transformed audio creates synthetic multilingual speakers, improving accent-speaker disentanglement by scaling formants, F0, and durations, then assigning new speaker IDs while keeping original accents, teaching the model that accent and speaker identity are independent attributes.

### Mechanism 3
VICReg-based embedding regularization decorrelates accent and speaker embeddings, improving controllable synthesis by constraining variance, covariance, and cross-correlation between these embeddings, forcing the model to learn independent representations for fine-grained control.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text encoding (IPA) -> Accent/speaker conditioning -> Alignment learning (text-mel with accent) -> Mel synthesis (with F0/energy conditioning) -> Vocoding
- **Critical path**: Text encoding (IPA) → Accent/speaker conditioning → Alignment learning (text-mel with accent) → Mel synthesis (with F0/energy conditioning) → Vocoding
- **Design tradeoffs**: Shared IPA vs. language-specific encoders (simplifies multilingual training but may miss language-specific nuances), explicit F0/energy conditioning (improves controllability but requires accurate prediction), augmentation strategy (improves disentanglement but may introduce artifacts)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor speaker retention (cosine similarity drops), increased CER (pronunciation errors), accent bleeding (speaker's native accent persists in target language), vocoder artifacts from over-smooth mels
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train baseline RADTTS-ML without disentanglement strategies to establish performance floor
  2. Add accent-conditioned alignment learning to test if accent conditioning improves alignment quality
  3. Apply embedding regularization with VICReg constraints to measure impact on speaker retention and accent control

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How would RADMMM perform with significantly more speakers per language/accent in the training dataset? The authors state "Scaling the model to large-resource conditions with more speakers per accent remains the subject of future work." This is unresolved because the paper focuses on a low-resource scenario with only 1 speaker per accent, so performance in a high-resource setting is unknown.

### Open Question 2
How does RADMMM's performance compare to state-of-the-art zero-shot voice conversion systems like YourTTS on the same dataset? The authors mention they "attempted to implement YourTTS but ran into issues reproducing the results on our dataset," so direct comparison is missing.

### Open Question 3
What is the impact of different data augmentation strategies on RADMMM's ability to disentangle accent and speaker attributes? The paper doesn't explore alternative augmentation approaches or analyze which types of augmentation are most beneficial, though it notes that augmentation "leads to a significant drop in both speaker retention as well as CER with augmentations, likely due to conditioning on noisy augmented features."

## Limitations

- Limited language coverage with only 7 languages and 1 speaker per accent, restricting generalizability to truly low-resource settings
- Incomplete ablation studies that don't isolate the contribution of individual disentanglement components
- Unclear whether improvements are due to genuine disentanglement or simply increased model capacity from additional conditioning and regularization

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Claims about overall architecture design and technical implementation of RADMMM, including IPA tokenization and accent conditioning.

**Medium Confidence**: Claims about effectiveness of specific disentanglement strategies (adversarial loss, augmentation, embedding regularization) due to incomplete ablation studies.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about superiority of RADMMM over all baselines in all aspects, as human evaluation is limited to a small set of speakers and languages.
