# Incomplete Multimodal Learning for Complex Brain Disorders Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16222
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16222
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 31
- Primary result: New transformer-based method with Riemannian GAN achieves superior prediction of cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease using incomplete multimodal data

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of predicting cognitive decline and disease outcomes in Alzheimer's disease using incomplete multimodal data, where genetic information is available during training but not inference. The authors propose a novel approach combining transformers and Riemannian GANs to enable a unimodal model trained on MRI data to effectively leverage complementary genetic information during training. The method demonstrates significant performance improvements over traditional machine learning and deep learning approaches across multiple evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The proposed method trains a multimodal teacher model (M-Model) on both MRI and genetic data, then uses a Riemannian GAN to guide a single-modality model (U-Model) to imitate the genetic component using only MRI data during inference. The Riemannian GAN projects real genetic representations onto a d-sphere and learns to generate fake genetic representations from MRI features. The U-Model is trained with an imitation loss that encourages it to learn a representation of MRI features consistent with the M-Model's representation of genetic data. This allows the U-Model to leverage complementary genetic information during training despite only having access to MRI data during inference.

## Key Results
- Achieves lower root mean squared error compared to related machine learning and deep learning methods
- Demonstrates higher accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score for classification tasks
- Effectively exploits auxiliary modalities available during training to improve unimodal model performance at inference

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Riemannian GAN component effectively bridges the gap between available MRI features and the missing genetic modality by learning a low-dimensional representation space that mimics the genetic modality during training.
- Mechanism: During training, the U-Model uses a Riemannian GAN to project real genetic representations from the M-Model onto a d-sphere and learn to generate fake genetic representations from MRI features. The discriminator scores samples based on their projection onto the largest hyper-section of the sphere, with normal vector v, encouraging the generator to produce samples that match the distribution of real genetic representations. This allows the U-Model to leverage complementary genetic information during training even though it only has access to MRI data during inference.
- Core assumption: The genetic and MRI modalities share complementary information relevant to the prediction task, and this shared information can be captured in a lower-dimensional representation space.

This encourages the U-Model to learn a representation of the MRI features that is similar to the representation learned by the M-Model for the genetic modality, effectively allowing the U-Model to leverage the complementary information from the genetic modality during training.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: M-Model (MRI backbone + Genetic backbone) -> Riemannian GAN (Generator + Discriminator) -> U-Model (MRI backbone + Riemannian GAN)
- Critical path: M-Model -> Riemannian GAN -> U-Model
  - The M-Model is trained first on both MRI and genetic data
  - The Riemannian GAN component learns to generate fake genetic representations from MRI features based on the representations learned by the M-Model
  - The U-Model is trained to imitate the genetic component of the M-Model using the generated fake genetic representations
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a transformer backbone vs. other architectures (e.g., MLP, ResNet)
  - Using a Riemannian GAN vs. other GAN variants (e.g., WGAN, LSGAN)
  - Using imitation loss vs. other loss functions (e.g., MSE, cross-entropy)
- Failure signatures:
  - If the U-Model fails to converge or produces poor results, it may indicate that the Riemannian GAN component is not effectively bridging the gap between the MRI and genetic modalities
  - If the M-Model produces poor results, it may indicate that the transformer backbone is not effectively capturing the relationships between features
  - If the imitation loss fails to converge, it may indicate that the representations learned by the M-Model and U-Model are not consistent
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the M-Model on both MRI and genetic data and evaluate its performance on the prediction task
  2. Train the Riemannian GAN component to generate fake genetic representations from MRI features and evaluate its ability to match the distribution of real genetic representations
  3. Train the U-Model to imitate the genetic component of the M-Model using the generated fake genetic representations and evaluate its performance on the prediction task compared to the M-Model and other baseline models
