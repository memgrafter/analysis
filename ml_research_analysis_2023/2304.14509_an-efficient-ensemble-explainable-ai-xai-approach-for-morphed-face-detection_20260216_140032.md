# An Efficient Ensemble Explainable AI (XAI) Approach for Morphed Face Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14509
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14509
- Reference count: 40
- The paper presents a novel ensemble Explainable AI (XAI) approach integrating saliency maps, Class Activation Maps (CAM), and Gradient-weighted Class Activation Maps (Grad-CAM) to enhance interpretability of deep learning models for morphed face detection.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an ensemble Explainable AI approach for morphed face detection that combines saliency maps, CAM, and Grad-CAM to provide comprehensive visual explanations of model predictions. The method is applied to an EfficientNet-B1 classifier and tested on three public datasets (FRLL, WMCA, MIFS). The ensemble approach generates interpretable heatmaps that highlight discriminative facial regions used by the model for morphed/unmorphed classification, achieving high accuracy (96.76-100%) while improving transparency in deep learning-based biometric presentation attack detection systems.

## Method Summary
The proposed ensemble XAI approach combines three interpretability techniques - saliency maps, CAM, and Grad-CAM - with an EfficientNet-B1 classifier for morphed face detection. The method involves preprocessing face images to 224x224 resolution, fine-tuning the EfficientNet-B1 model on each dataset with added dropout and output layers, then generating visual explanations by applying the three interpretability methods individually. These heatmaps are ensemble-stacked through concatenation and normalization to produce comprehensive explanations that highlight discriminative facial regions used for classification decisions.

## Key Results
- The ensemble XAI approach achieves high accuracy ranging from 96.76% to 100% across three public datasets (FRLL, WMCA, MIFS)
- Generated heatmaps effectively highlight discriminative facial regions including periocular and peri-nose areas used by the model for classification
- The approach provides interpretable explanations that improve understanding of model decision-making in morphed face detection scenarios

## Limitations
- Computational overhead of running three interpretability methods sequentially may limit real-time deployment in production systems
- Limited dataset sizes (particularly FRLL with only 102 faces) raise questions about robustness across diverse real-world conditions
- Lack of empirical evidence that ensemble explanations actually improve human understanding or trust in practical scenarios

## Confidence
- High confidence: The baseline EfficientNet-B1 model achieves strong detection accuracy (96.76-100%) on the three datasets, which is well-supported by the experimental results and aligns with established deep learning performance in biometric security.
- Medium confidence: The ensemble approach improves interpretability by combining multiple visualization techniques. While the heatmaps appear visually meaningful, there is limited quantitative validation that the ensemble provides meaningfully better explanations than individual methods.
- Low confidence: The claim that ensemble interpretability directly enhances trust in deep learning-based biometric systems lacks empirical support. No user studies or trust metrics are provided to substantiate this assertion.
