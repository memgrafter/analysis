# How Does Attention Work in Vision Transformers? A Visual Analytics Attempt

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13731
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13731
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: Visual analytics system for interpreting Vision Transformers through head importance, attention strength, and attention patterns

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a visual analytics approach to interpret Vision Transformers (ViTs) by analyzing three key aspects: head importance, attention strength distribution, and attention patterns. The system uses pruning-based metrics to identify important attention heads, profiles spatial attention distributions through k-hop analysis, and summarizes learned attention patterns using autoencoder clustering. Through case studies with deep learning experts, the system demonstrates effectiveness in revealing how different heads contribute to predictions and their distinct functional roles.

## Method Summary
The method employs multiple pruning-based metrics to assess head importance through leave-one-out ablation studies at both model and layer levels. Attention strength vectors are computed by averaging attention across k-hop spatial neighbors for each patch. An autoencoder learns compressed representations of binarized attention matrices, which are then clustered using t-SNE to identify distinct attention patterns. These components are integrated into an interactive visual analytics system for comprehensive ViT interpretation.

## Key Results
- System successfully identifies important attention heads using multi-metric ablation approach
- Reveals distinct spatial attention patterns through autoencoder-based clustering
- Enables interpretation of head functions through combined analysis of importance, strength, and patterns
- Validated through case studies with deep learning experts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The system effectively identifies important attention heads by using multiple pruning-based metrics that capture both local (layer-level) and global (model-level) impacts.
- Mechanism: By systematically ablating individual heads and measuring the resulting changes in both immediate layer activations and final model outputs, the system can distinguish between heads that are crucial for intermediate processing versus those that directly influence predictions.
- Core assumption: Head importance is context-dependent and cannot be captured by a single metric - different heads serve different functions at different layers.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We introduce multiple pruning-based metrics to assess the importance of individual attention heads"; [section] "We propose two model-level importance metrics for each head...We propose two layer-level importance metrics"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The system reveals attention patterns through autoencoder-based clustering of patch attention matrices.
- Mechanism: By binarizing patch attention matrices and training an autoencoder to learn latent representations, the system can cluster heads with similar attention patterns and reveal the underlying structure of how patches attend to each other.
- Core assumption: Attention patterns can be effectively captured and summarized through dimensionality reduction techniques, even though the original matrices are high-dimensional.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "using an autoencoder-based learning solution, we summarize all possible attention patterns that individual heads could learn"; [section] "we train an autoencoder for the heads' attention matrices, and summarize all possible attention patterns by clustering the latent representations"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The system effectively profiles spatial attention distributions through k-hop neighbor analysis.
- Mechanism: By computing attention strength vectors that average attention across k-hop neighbors in the 2D spatial context, the system can reveal how attention strength varies with spatial distance and how this varies across layers.
- Core assumption: Spatial attention in ViTs follows meaningful patterns that can be characterized by aggregating attention across spatial distances (k-hops).
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we profile the spatial distribution of attention strengths between patches inside individual heads"; [section] "We define a p-dimensional (pD) attention strength vector, s, for each head, which profiles the average attention strength of all patches to their k-hop neighbors"

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the attention patterns in vision transformers vary across different image classes or datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses analyzing attention patterns across multiple images but does not explore class-specific or dataset-specific trends.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on general patterns across all images without examining whether certain attention patterns are more prevalent in specific image classes or datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing attention patterns for individual classes or datasets separately to identify any unique patterns that emerge.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do the head importance metrics relate to the actual contribution of heads to model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces multiple head importance metrics but does not directly validate their relationship to model performance.
- Why unresolved: While the metrics quantify importance, their actual impact on model accuracy or other performance measures is not explicitly tested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing how pruning heads based on the importance metrics affects model performance metrics like accuracy, F1-score, etc.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the attention patterns change when the vision transformer is fine-tuned for specific tasks or domains?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper analyzes pre-trained vision transformers but does not explore how fine-tuning affects attention patterns.
- Why unresolved: Fine-tuning is a common practice to adapt models to specific tasks, but its impact on attention patterns is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing attention patterns before and after fine-tuning the model on a specific task or domain.

## Limitations
- The autoencoder-based pattern summarization lacks direct validation of whether the learned latent representations truly capture meaningful attention patterns
- Limited empirical evidence is provided for the claim that different heads serve distinct functional roles across layers
- The k-hop attention strength analysis assumes spatial relationships are meaningful, but this may not hold for all ViT architectures or datasets

## Confidence
- Head importance metrics: High - The ablation-based approach is well-established and the metrics are clearly defined
- Attention pattern clustering: Medium - While the methodology is sound, the quality of the learned patterns depends heavily on autoencoder architecture and training
- Spatial attention profiling: Medium - The k-hop analysis is novel but may not generalize across different ViT variants
