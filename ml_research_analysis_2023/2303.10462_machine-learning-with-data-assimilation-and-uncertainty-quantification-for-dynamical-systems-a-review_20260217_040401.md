# Machine learning with data assimilation and uncertainty quantification for dynamical systems: a review

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10462
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10462
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Reviews integration of ML, DA, and UQ for dynamical systems, highlighting improved forecasting accuracy and better handling of noisy data

## Executive Summary
This review paper examines the convergence of machine learning (ML), data assimilation (DA), and uncertainty quantification (UQ) in dynamical systems. The authors synthesize how these three approaches can be combined to address key challenges in modeling complex systems, including model error correction, parameter estimation, and error covariance specification. The review covers various ML architectures like autoencoders and recurrent neural networks, DA techniques including Kalman filtering and variational methods, and UQ approaches such as Bayesian neural networks and Monte Carlo simulations.

## Method Summary
The review synthesizes existing literature on hybrid approaches that combine ML, DA, and UQ for dynamical systems. The methodological framework involves using ML models (autoencoders, RNNs, deep learning) for dimensionality reduction, error correction, and uncertainty estimation, while DA techniques (Kalman filters, variational methods) provide state updates and uncertainty quantification, and UQ methods characterize prediction confidence. The review focuses on reduced-order modeling, latent assimilation, and physics-informed approaches for high-dimensional systems.

## Key Results
- ML surrogate models can learn to correct systematic model errors from DA analysis increments
- DA provides uncertainty estimates that complement ML's epistemic uncertainty quantification
- Latent assimilation reduces computational cost of DA in high-dimensional systems by performing updates in compressed latent spaces

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ML surrogate models can improve DA accuracy by learning to correct systematic model errors
- Mechanism: A neural network is trained on analysis increments (difference between DA analysis and background) to predict model errors, then used to correct forecasts before the next DA cycle
- Core assumption: Analysis increments contain useful information about systematic model errors, and this relationship is learnable
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] discusses "correction of forward model errors" as a key challenge addressed by ML in DA; [section] provides detailed explanation of resolvent and tendency corrections using neural networks trained on analysis increments
- Break condition: If analysis increments are dominated by observation operator errors or DA approximations, the learned corrections will be biased

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DA can provide uncertainty estimates for ML predictions in dynamical systems
- Mechanism: ML models are trained with Bayesian frameworks or ensembles to quantify epistemic uncertainty, while DA provides aleatoric uncertainty through error covariance matrices
- Core assumption: The combined uncertainty estimates from ML and DA are approximately independent and can be merged
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] highlights "improved handling of noisy and sparse data" as a key outcome; [section] explains how DA algorithms use Gaussian error assumptions to provide uncertainty quantification
- Break condition: If ML and DA uncertainties are strongly correlated, the combined estimate may be overconfident

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Latent assimilation (LA) can reduce computational cost of DA in high-dimensional systems
- Mechanism: Data are compressed into a reduced latent space using autoencoders, DA is performed in this space, then results are decoded back to physical space
- Core assumption: The latent space captures sufficient information about the dynamics for accurate DA
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions "reduced order surrogate modelling" as an application area; [section] describes various LA approaches including online methods and their trade-offs
- Break condition: If the latent space is too lossy or the encoding/decoding introduces significant errors, DA performance will degrade

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Observations -> ML models (autoencoders, RNNs) -> Latent space -> DA algorithms (Kalman filters, variational methods) -> State estimates -> UQ methods (Bayesian inference, Monte Carlo) -> Uncertainty quantification

- Critical path: 1. Obtain observations and model predictions; 2. Apply ML models for prediction/uncertainty estimation; 3. Perform DA to update state estimates; 4. Update ML models with DA analysis increments; 5. Propagate forward in time

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. computational cost: More complex ML models and DA methods improve accuracy but increase computational burden
  - Interpretability vs. performance: Bayesian ML methods provide uncertainty estimates but may be less accurate than point estimates
  - Online vs. offline learning: Online methods adapt to changing conditions but require more computational resources

- Failure signatures:
  - Degraded DA performance: If ML models are not properly trained or if the latent space is too lossy
  - Unstable predictions: If uncertainty estimates are not properly calibrated or if ensemble methods are underdispersed
  - Computational bottlenecks: If the model complexity exceeds available resources or if data pipelines are inefficient

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a simple autoencoder for dimensionality reduction on a toy dynamical system (e.g., Lorenz 96)
  2. Train a neural network to predict analysis increments from background states on a low-dimensional system
  3. Combine the trained autoencoder and error correction network in a latent assimilation framework and evaluate performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How can we effectively integrate machine learning and data assimilation techniques in high-dimensional, multimodal, and multi-scale systems like numerical weather prediction and ocean dynamics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that future research efforts could concentrate on the integration of ML and DA in high-dimensional, multimodal, and multi-scale systems, such as NWP and ocean dynamics
- Why unresolved: This is a complex challenge that requires further research and development to address the unique characteristics of these systems and the limitations of current ML and DA techniques
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful demonstrations of ML-DA integration in high-dimensional, multimodal, and multi-scale systems, along with performance comparisons and evaluations

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective methods for uncertainty quantification in deep learning models, particularly in the context of dynamical systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses various uncertainty quantification methods for ML, including Bayesian neural networks, Monte Carlo dropout, and deep ensembles, but highlights the need for further research in this area
- Why unresolved: Uncertainty quantification in deep learning models is a challenging task, especially when dealing with complex dynamical systems, and there is no consensus on the best approaches
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies and benchmarks of different uncertainty quantification methods in deep learning models for dynamical systems, along with theoretical and empirical analyses of their strengths and limitations

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we leverage the strengths of both data-driven and physics-based approaches in modeling and predicting complex dynamical systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the potential of combining data-driven and physics-based approaches, such as hybrid mechanistic-empirical models and physics-informed neural networks, but acknowledges the challenges in effectively integrating these methods
- Why unresolved: Balancing the strengths and limitations of data-driven and physics-based approaches is a complex task that requires further research and development to optimize their integration
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful demonstrations of hybrid models that effectively combine data-driven and physics-based approaches, along with performance comparisons and evaluations against purely data-driven or physics-based models

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of learned corrections depends critically on the assumption that analysis increments contain useful information about systematic model errors
- The assumption of independence between ML and DA uncertainty estimates may not hold in practice
- Computational cost remains a significant challenge, particularly for operational forecasting applications

## Confidence
- High confidence: The basic mechanisms of how ML can learn from DA analysis increments and how DA can provide uncertainty estimates for ML predictions are well-established and supported by multiple studies
- Medium confidence: The specific performance gains and optimal configurations for combining these approaches in operational settings remain empirical and system-dependent
- Medium confidence: While the theoretical foundations are sound, the practical implementation challenges and failure modes in real-world applications need further validation
