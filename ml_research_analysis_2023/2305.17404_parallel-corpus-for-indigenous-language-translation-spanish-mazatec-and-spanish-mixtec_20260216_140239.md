# Parallel Corpus for Indigenous Language Translation: Spanish-Mazatec and Spanish-Mixtec

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.17404
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17404
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: Introduced first parallel corpus for Spanish-Mazatec and Spanish-Mixtec translation; fine-tuning M2M100-48 achieved BLEU scores of 12.09-22.25 for Mazatec and 16.75-22.15 for Mixtec

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first parallel corpus for machine translation between Spanish and two indigenous Mexican languages, Mazatec and Mixtec, containing 9,799 and 13,235 sentences respectively. The authors evaluate three approaches—transformer, transfer learning, and fine-tuning—to assess translation quality. Fine-tuning the Facebook M2M100-48 model achieved the best results, with BLEU scores ranging from 12.09 to 22.25 for Mazatec-Spanish translations and 16.75 to 22.15 for Mixtec-Spanish translations. The study demonstrates that corpus size significantly impacts translation performance and that indigenous languages work better as target languages in translation tasks.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three machine translation approaches using a parallel corpus of Spanish-Mazatec (9,799 sentences) and Spanish-Mixtec (13,235 sentences) from religious, constitution, and textbook domains. The transformer baseline uses a base configuration with 6 encoder/decoder layers, 8 heads, and 512 hidden size. Transfer learning employs opus-mt models with max_seq_length=128, num_train_epochs=3, per_device_batch_size=4, and num_beams=5. Fine-tuning uses mBART50 and M2M100-48 pre-trained models with BPE tokenization. All approaches are evaluated using BLEU scores on the test sets.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuning M2M100-48 outperformed transformer and transfer learning approaches for both language pairs
- BLEU scores achieved: 12.09-22.25 for Mazatec-Spanish, 16.75-22.15 for Mixtec-Spanish translations
- Indigenous languages performed better as target languages than as source languages
- Corpus size directly correlated with translation performance, with Mixtec (larger corpus) outperforming Mazatec

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuning pre-trained multilingual MT models significantly improves translation performance for low-resource indigenous languages
- Mechanism: Pre-trained models like M2M100-48 capture rich linguistic representations from high-resource languages. Fine-tuning adapts these representations to the specific grammatical and lexical patterns of Mazatec and Mixtec, leveraging learned translation strategies even with limited parallel data
- Core assumption: The linguistic diversity of Mazatec and Mixtec shares enough structural similarity with the languages seen during pre-training to enable effective transfer
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Fine-tuning the Facebook M2M100-48 model outperformed the other approaches, with BLEU scores of 12.09 and 22.25 for Mazatec-Spanish and Spanish-Mazatec translations, respectively..." [section] "Fine-tuning approach outperformed the rest of the approaches used in our baseline experiment in both translation directions"
- Break condition: If the pre-training languages are too dissimilar in morphology or syntax, the transferred knowledge may be irrelevant or even harmful

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using indigenous languages as target languages yields higher BLEU scores than using them as source languages
- Mechanism: Translating into an indigenous language is less complex for the model because the source (Spanish) is high-resource with abundant parallel data, providing strong cues for target language structure. Conversely, translating from an indigenous language to Spanish requires the model to generate high-resource output from limited input patterns
- Core assumption: The source language (Spanish) provides sufficient linguistic context to guide generation into a low-resource target language
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The findings show that the dataset size (9,799 sentences in Mazatec and 13,235 sentences in Mixtec) affects translation performance and that indigenous languages work better when used as target languages." [section] "We discovered that utilizing indigenous languages as the target language surpasses the performance achieved when using Spanish as the target language"
- Break condition: If the target language has highly complex morphology or syntactic rules not well represented in the source language, the performance gap may narrow or reverse

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Dataset size has a direct impact on translation performance, especially for low-resource languages
- Mechanism: Larger parallel corpora provide more examples of word alignment, phrase structure, and domain-specific terminology, allowing the model to learn accurate translation patterns. Even with fine-tuning, limited data constrains the model's ability to generalize
- Core assumption: The quality and representativeness of the sentences in the corpus are consistent and cover the linguistic variability needed for robust translation
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The findings show that the dataset size (9,799 sentences in Mazatec and 13,235 sentences in Mixtec) affects translation performance..." [section] "This shows that the availability of the parallel corpora for the language pairs has a high impact on the performance of the translation models"
- Break condition: If the corpus is large but contains many errors, repetitions, or domain-specific bias, performance gains may plateau or degrade

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Parallel corpus alignment
  - Why needed here: Aligning Spanish texts with Mazatec and Mixtec sentences is the first step to creating usable training data; misalignment introduces noise and degrades translation quality
  - Quick check question: What preprocessing step ensures that each Mazatec sentence corresponds to the correct Spanish translation in the aligned corpus?

- Concept: Subword tokenization (BPE)
  - Why needed here: Low-resource languages may have rare or unseen words; BPE reduces vocabulary size and handles morphological richness by breaking words into subword units
  - Quick check question: How does BPE help when translating words that do not appear in the training corpus?

- Concept: Fine-tuning vs. transfer learning
  - Why needed here: Fine-tuning adapts an entire pre-trained model to a specific task, while transfer learning may only reuse parts of it; understanding the difference is crucial for selecting the right approach for low-resource settings
  - Quick check question: Why does fine-tuning generally outperform transfer learning when using a multilingual pre-trained model for a new language pair?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Corpus collection -> Text alignment -> Preprocessing (normalization, BPE) -> Train/validation/test split -> Model fine-tuning (M2M100-48) -> BLEU evaluation
- Critical path: Corpus alignment and preprocessing -> Fine-tuning with M2M100-48 -> BLEU evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: Using M2M100-48 (48M params) balances performance with computational cost; larger models may yield marginal gains but require more resources
- Failure signatures: Low BLEU scores despite fine-tuning may indicate poor corpus alignment, insufficient preprocessing, or mismatched tokenization schemes
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune M2M100-48 on the full Mazatec-Spanish corpus and evaluate BLEU in both directions
  2. Fine-tune M2M100-48 on the full Mixtec-Spanish corpus and compare performance with Mazatec
  3. Test zero-shot translation by evaluating M2M100-48 on unseen indigenous language pairs without fine-tuning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How do zero-shot and few-shot learning approaches compare to fine-tuning in improving translation quality for low-resource indigenous languages?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that future research will investigate zero-shot and few-shot learning approaches to further improve translation performance in low-resource settings
  - Why unresolved: The paper only reports results from transformer, transfer learning, and fine-tuning approaches, without evaluating zero-shot or few-shot methods
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimental comparison of zero-shot and few-shot learning approaches against fine-tuning methods using the same parallel corpora, measuring BLEU scores for translation quality

- Question: What is the impact of increasing corpus size on translation quality for Mazatec and Mixtec languages?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that "the dataset size affects translation performance" and observe that Mixtec (with a larger corpus) performs better than Mazatec
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not conduct experiments with systematically varied corpus sizes to quantify the relationship between corpus size and translation quality
  - What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments with incrementally larger training datasets for both languages, measuring changes in BLEU scores to establish the relationship between corpus size and performance

- Question: Why do indigenous languages perform better as target languages than as source languages in translation tasks?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that "indigenous languages work better when used as target languages" and that translating from Spanish to indigenous languages is "less complex" than the reverse direction
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed linguistic or computational analysis explaining this directional asymmetry in translation performance
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparative linguistic analysis of Spanish and indigenous language structures, error analysis of translation outputs, and computational experiments varying language directionality to identify specific factors contributing to this phenomenon

## Limitations

- BLEU scores (12.09-22.25 for Mazatec, 16.75-22.15 for Mixtec) remain relatively low for practical deployment despite being promising for low-resource language pairs
- Corpus domains (religious texts, constitution, textbooks) may not cover the full range of language use cases, potentially limiting model generalization to conversational or domain-specific content
- Analysis focuses primarily on BLEU as an evaluation metric without qualitative assessment of translation adequacy or fluency

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The finding that fine-tuning M2M100-48 outperforms both baseline transformer and transfer learning approaches is well-supported by experimental results and aligns with established practices in low-resource MT. The observation that indigenous languages perform better as target languages is also consistently demonstrated across both language pairs.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim about dataset size directly affecting performance is supported by the experimental setup, but the study doesn't systematically vary corpus size to quantify this relationship. The conclusion that indigenous languages benefit from being target languages assumes Spanish provides sufficient linguistic context, but doesn't explore why this asymmetry exists or test alternative explanations.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that fine-tuning works because of shared linguistic similarity between pre-training languages and Mazatec/Mixtec is speculative without detailed linguistic analysis. The study doesn't investigate what specific aspects of the pre-training data enable successful transfer or whether alternative pre-training strategies might yield better results.

## Next Checks

1. **Domain Generalization Test**: Evaluate the fine-tuned models on out-of-domain indigenous language texts (e.g., social media, news, conversational speech) to assess whether the reported BLEU scores reflect general translation capability or corpus-specific memorization

2. **Human Evaluation Study**: Conduct human assessments of translation quality using metrics like adequacy and fluency scores, particularly focusing on cases where BLEU scores are highest versus lowest to understand the practical utility of the translations

3. **Linguistic Transfer Analysis**: Perform detailed analysis of the pre-training languages in M2M100-48 to identify which linguistic features transfer successfully to Mazatec and Mixtec, and whether specific language families or typological features correlate with better performance
