# Models of symbol emergence in communication: a conceptual review and a guide for avoiding local minima

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04544
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04544
- Reference count: 9
- Many models portray communication as message transmission via signals, with meaning reduced to mapping symbols to referents, neglecting interactive and embodied aspects.

## Executive Summary
This paper reviews computational models of communication emergence, identifying pervasive assumptions that limit progress toward modeling truly emergent symbolic systems. The authors argue that most models reduce communication to information transmission through pre-defined discrete symbols, neglecting the interactive, embodied, and situated nature of real communication. They propose that advancing the field requires moving beyond the mapping metaphor, embracing continuous signals, and modeling meaning as emerging from ecological interactions rather than static symbol-referent associations.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct a conceptual review and classification of computational models from philosophy, linguistics, cognitive psychology, AI, and robotics. They analyze how models handle symbol emergence, communication function, and the mapping metaphor between symbols and referents. Rather than proposing new computational experiments, they synthesize patterns across existing literature to identify assumptions that create local minima in research progress. The classification scheme focuses on three key assumptions: whether symbols truly emerge or are predefined, whether communication serves descriptive versus imperative functions, and whether the mapping metaphor constrains model design.

## Key Results
- Most models assume pre-defined discrete symbols with arbitrary meanings rather than explaining symbol emergence
- Communication is typically modeled as descriptive message transmission, neglecting imperative/coordinative functions
- The mapping metaphor between symbols and referents is deeply embedded in current models, limiting exploration of alternative representational schemes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The paper demonstrates that many models share a hidden assumption of discrete, amodal symbols with pre-given meaning, which prevents true symbol emergence. By reframing meaning as arising from situated, embodied interactions rather than static symbol-referent mappings, models can explore richer forms of communication that include continuous signals and context-dependent meaning. This breaks when the model environment lacks situatedness or when the learning algorithm treats symbols as fixed categories rather than emergent relational structures.

### Mechanism 2
Multi-source complexity (perceptual grounding, sensorimotor coupling, physical signal properties, environmental dynamics) is necessary to move beyond simple symbol mapping. Embodied simulations that integrate continuous signals, movement, and rich sensory inputs allow communication to emerge from ecological interactions rather than from optimizing static rewards for correct symbol-to-referent mapping. This fails if the simulation treats the environment as static or fully observable, or if signals are still treated as discrete tokens.

### Mechanism 3
Communication function should not be limited to description; imperative and coordinative functions emerge naturally when agents have richer action repertoires and goals. By allowing agents to move, manipulate objects, and coordinate without explicit descriptive reward, models can evolve communication systems where signals control behavior dynamically rather than merely labeling referents. This mechanism breaks if the reward function remains tied to static descriptive success or if agent actions are decoupled from signal generation.

## Foundational Learning

- **Horizontal vs. vertical language evolution**: Many models mix or separate these two dynamics; understanding their roles clarifies which assumptions about symbol emergence are being made. Quick check: In a model where agents learn only from their parents and never from peers, which type of transmission is being modeled?

- **Embodied vs. abstract agent representations**: The review emphasizes that truly emergent communication requires agents with bodies and sensorimotor loops, not just abstract symbol processors. Quick check: What key capability does a robot with continuous movement have that a static token-passing agent lacks?

- **Symbol grounding problem and its proposed solutions**: The paper contrasts traditional symbol grounding (mapping tokens to referents) with grounding through interaction and ecological transformation. ## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Perceptual encoder (raw visual/audio input) -> Sensorimotor controller (continuous movement, action selection) -> Communication module (continuous or discrete signals) -> Reward/evaluation module (ecological fitness, not just message accuracy) -> Memory/history module (tracks interaction trajectories and concept evolution)

- **Critical path**: 1. Agent perceives environment → 2. Internal state updated via sensorimotor loop → 3. Signal emitted based on current context and history → 4. Signal received and interpreted in light of receiver's current state → 5. Receiver acts → 6. Environmental change updates both agents' states → 7. Communication system (vocabulary, grammar) adapts over time

- **Design tradeoffs**: Discrete vs. continuous signals (discrete are easier to analyze but limit meaning richness; continuous allow iconicity and gradience but require more complex learning); Full observability vs. partial observability (full observability simplifies learning but misses the ambiguity and context-dependency that shape real communication); Explicit reward for communication vs. emergent fitness (explicit rewards can shortcut emergence; emergent fitness better captures ecological pressures but is harder to tune)

- **Failure signatures**: Agents converge to one universal signal with no internal structure (indicates lack of compositionality pressure); Communication protocols overfit to training stimuli and fail to generalize (indicates lack of grounded sensorimotor context); Signals never stabilize or diverge across agents (indicates insufficient social or ecological pressure)

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Implement a simple LSG with continuous signals in a 2D grid with partial observability; observe if agents develop context-dependent meanings; 2. Add a secondary task (e.g., food gathering) that requires coordination; check if communication becomes imperative rather than purely descriptive; 3. Introduce variable signal transmission (distance decay, occlusion); observe if agents exploit physical signal properties (iconicity, redundancy)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can we effectively model the emergence of symbols without presupposing their existence, moving beyond the mapping metaphor? The authors argue that existing models assume symbols as discrete abstract entities with arbitrary meanings, rather than explaining their emergence. They suggest a shift towards embodied and situated models. This remains unresolved because the mapping metaphor is deeply ingrained in current models, and moving beyond it requires fundamentally rethinking the nature of symbols and their emergence in communication. Evidence would come from a successful model demonstrating the emergence of symbolic communication without relying on pre-defined symbols or the mapping metaphor, grounded in embodied and situated interactions.

### Open Question 2
What is the relative importance of different sources of communication complexity (perceptual grounding, sensorimotor grounding, properties of sign vehicles, environment complexity, agent architecture, communication function, social adaptations, and history of interactions) in shaping the emergence of structured symbolic communication? The authors identify multiple sources of communication complexity and suggest that models should explore their interplay, rather than isolating individual factors. This remains unresolved because the interplay of these factors is complex and not well understood, and their relative importance may vary depending on the specific context of communication emergence. Evidence would come from comparative studies of different models incorporating various combinations of complexity sources, demonstrating their impact on the emergence of structured symbolic communication.

### Open Question 3
How can we design agent architectures and learning mechanisms that facilitate the emergence of communication beyond the expectations of the modeler, promoting agent autonomy and avoiding the limitations of predefined reward functions? The authors argue that agents need certain cognitive preadaptations for social cooperation and that models should move beyond explicit reward functions to allow for more autonomous communication emergence. This remains unresolved because designing agent architectures and learning mechanisms that balance structure and flexibility to enable autonomous communication emergence is a challenging problem. Evidence would come from agent architectures and learning mechanisms that successfully generate complex communication systems without explicit reward functions, demonstrating agent autonomy and the emergence of unexpected communication properties.

## Limitations
- The classification of models across the three key assumptions is inherently qualitative and subjective
- The corpus analysis reveals limited direct empirical support from neighboring literature, suggesting this may be a novel analytical framework rather than a widely validated one
- The paper does not provide quantitative metrics for model comparison, making it difficult to assess the relative prevalence of different assumptions across the field

## Confidence
- High confidence: The identification of discrete symbol assumptions and mapping metaphors as common limitations in existing models
- Medium confidence: The argument that embodied, multi-source complexity models show more promise for true symbol emergence
- Low confidence: Specific predictions about which alternative assumptions will most effectively advance the field
