# Seasoning Model Soups for Robustness to Adversarial and Natural Distribution Shifts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10164
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10164
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Model soups (convex combinations of parameters) can smoothly trade-off robustness across different ℓp-norms and adapt to natural distribution shifts using only 100 labeled examples, often outperforming individual models and baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates model soups as a method for combining classifiers with different ℓp-norm adversarial robustness levels. The authors demonstrate that by fine-tuning a single ℓ∞-robust model to other threat models, soups can smoothly interpolate between different robustness profiles without additional adversarial training. These soups not only outperform individual models on adversarial robustness trade-offs but also achieve competitive results with multi-norm adversarial training methods. Additionally, the soups prove effective for adapting to natural distribution shifts (e.g., ImageNet variants) using only 100 labeled examples, with the best soups often surpassing individual models and baseline approaches.

## Method Summary
The authors create model soups by linearly combining parameters of models fine-tuned from a common ℓ∞-robust base model to different threat models (ℓ2, ℓ1, nominal). They use grid search to find optimal interpolation weights and evaluate these soups on both adversarial robustness (ℓ∞, ℓ2, ℓ1 attacks) and natural distribution shifts. For few-shot adaptation, they select the best soup using 1000 labeled examples from each shift. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet with various variants as distribution shifts.

## Key Results
- Model soups smoothly trade-off robustness across different ℓp-norms without additional adversarial training
- Soups outperform single models and achieve competitive results with multi-norm adversarial training
- Soups adapt to natural distribution shifts using only 100 labeled examples, often outperforming individual models
- Optimal soup weights vary across different shifts, highlighting the importance of model diversity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Model soups can smoothly trade-off robustness across different ℓp-norms without additional adversarial training.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning a single ℓ∞-robust model to other threat models creates compatible parameters that can be linearly combined, with interpolation weights controlling the balance between different robustness types.
- Core assumption: Parameters of models fine-tuned from the same base lie in a parameter space where linear combinations remain meaningful classifiers.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 3.2]
- Break condition: If fine-tuned models diverge too much in parameter space, linear combinations may lose robustness properties.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Soups can achieve robustness to all threats without jointly training on all of them.
- Mechanism: Combining models each specialized for a different threat model allows the soup to inherit robustness properties from each constituent model.
- Core assumption: Individual models retain their specialized robustness when combined with others in the soup.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 4.2]
- Break condition: If threat models are too diverse or incompatible, the soup may not effectively combine their robustness properties.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Soups enable adaptation to unseen distribution shifts using only a few labeled examples.
- Mechanism: Evaluating soups on a small sample of the new distribution allows selection of the optimal combination of weights for that specific shift.
- Core assumption: The optimal soup for a distribution shift can be identified from a small sample and will generalize to the full dataset.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 5.1]
- Break condition: If the few-shot sample is not representative of the full distribution shift, the selected soup may not generalize well.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Base robust model (ℓ∞-robust) -> Fine-tuned models (ℓ2, ℓ1, nominal) -> Soup combination layer (linear combination of parameters) -> Grid search for optimal weights -> Evaluation on distribution shifts

- Critical path:
  1. Train base robust model
  2. Fine-tune to other threat models
  3. Create soups with different weight combinations
  4. Evaluate soups on distribution shifts
  5. Select best soup for each shift

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of base models vs. soup diversity
  - Grid search granularity vs. computational cost
  - Soup weight range (convex vs. extrapolation) vs. performance

- Failure signatures:
  - Soups perform worse than individual models on all metrics
  - Optimal soup weights are at extremes (w=0 or w=1) for all shifts
  - Few-shot selection leads to poor generalization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Create soups with two models (ℓ∞, ℓ2) and evaluate robustness trade-off
  2. Create soups with three models (ℓ∞, ℓ2, ℓ1) and evaluate union robustness
  3. Select soups for ImageNet variants using 100 labeled examples and evaluate performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the robustness of model soups compare to ensembles of adversarially trained models when tested against stronger, more diverse attacks beyond AutoPGD?
- Basis in paper: The paper evaluates robustness using AutoPGD with 40 steps, but notes that stronger attacks could be used. They also mention that their soups are compared to MAX and SAT methods, which are known to be vulnerable to adaptive attacks.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates against AutoPGD, a standard attack. More powerful, adaptive attacks could potentially break the robustness of the soups, especially if they exploit the interpolation between models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the soups against a battery of strong, adaptive attacks (e.g., AutoAttack, adaptive attacks specifically designed to fool interpolated models) and comparing the results to ensembles of adversarially trained models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the interpolation framework be extended to other types of robustness beyond ℓp-norm bounded perturbations, such as spatial transformations or color jitter?
- Basis in paper: The paper focuses on ℓp-norm bounded perturbations as the threat model, but mentions that expanding the diversity of models might aid adaptation to new datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore robustness to other types of perturbations beyond ℓp-norms. It is unclear if the interpolation framework would be effective for these other threat models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Creating model soups by interpolating models trained on different types of robustness (e.g., spatial transformations, color jitter) and evaluating their performance on datasets with those types of shifts.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical explanation for why model soups can sometimes achieve higher robustness than the individual models they are composed of?
- Basis in paper: The paper observes that in some cases, intermediate soups have higher robustness than the extreme models trained on specific threat models. This is particularly notable for ℓ2-robustness when interpolating ℓ∞ and ℓ1-robust models.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a theoretical explanation for this phenomenon. It is unclear if this is due to the specific architectures used, the optimization process, or some other factor.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the decision boundaries of the individual models and the soups to understand how interpolation affects the geometry of the robust regions. Developing a theoretical framework to explain the improved robustness of soups.

## Limitations

- The method's effectiveness may degrade when applied to very different architectures or training paradigms due to parameter compatibility issues
- The few-shot adaptation mechanism assumes 100 labeled examples provide sufficient signal, which may not hold for highly complex or subtle distribution shifts
- Computational overhead of creating and evaluating multiple soups could become prohibitive for very large models or extensive hyperparameter grids

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Model soups can trade off robustness across different ℓp-norms (well-supported by empirical results showing smooth transitions); few-shot adaptation results on ImageNet variants are consistently positive.

**Medium Confidence**: Soups can achieve "universal robustness" without joint training (supported but could benefit from more extensive testing); soups outperforming baseline methods on distribution shifts (generally true but shows variation).

**Low Confidence**: Mechanism explanation for why linear parameter combinations preserve robustness properties lacks rigorous theoretical justification; claim that soup weights vary meaningfully across different shifts would benefit from more extensive statistical analysis.

## Next Checks

1. Test soup performance when fine-tuned models are initialized from different base architectures rather than a single model to assess parameter space compatibility limits.

2. Evaluate the few-shot selection mechanism with varying numbers of labeled examples (10, 50, 500) to determine the minimum sample size required for reliable soup selection.

3. Implement a theoretical analysis of the parameter space geometry to better understand conditions under which linear combinations preserve both accuracy and robustness properties.
