# GPTScore: Evaluate as You Desire

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.04166
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04166
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: GPTScore is a framework that leverages zero-shot instruction and in-context learning to evaluate text generation quality without annotated samples, achieving competitive performance across 37 datasets and 22 evaluation aspects.

## Executive Summary
GPTScore is a framework that leverages large generative pre-trained models' zero-shot instruction and in-context learning abilities to evaluate text generation quality without requiring annotated samples. The framework addresses the challenge of customized, multi-faceted evaluation by defining evaluation protocols through natural language instructions, supporting 22 different evaluation aspects. Experiments on 37 datasets across four text generation tasks demonstrate that GPTScore effectively allows users to evaluate texts as desired, achieving competitive performance compared to existing automated metrics.

The key innovation is using conditional generation probability to measure text quality, where higher-quality texts receive higher probabilities under the evaluation protocol. The framework supports vanilla, instruction-based, and instruction+demonstration variants, showing that combining instructions with in-context demonstrations further improves performance. Notably, smaller models with instructions can outperform larger supervised models, and incorporating highly correlated aspects enhances evaluation accuracy.

## Method Summary
GPTScore uses conditional generation probability from pre-trained models to evaluate text generation quality. The framework takes task specifications, aspect definitions, hypothesis text, and optional context as inputs, combining them into structured prompt templates. The generative model computes conditional probabilities of generating the hypothesis text, which are then aggregated into final scores. The method supports three variants: vanilla prompting (no instructions), instruction-based prompting, and instruction+demonstration prompting with in-context examples. GPTScore was evaluated on 37 datasets across four tasks: dialogue response generation, text summarization, data-to-text, and machine translation, measuring Spearman and Pearson correlation with human judgments.

## Key Results
- GPTScore achieves competitive performance compared to existing automated metrics across 37 datasets and 22 evaluation aspects
- Instruction-based prompting significantly improves evaluation performance compared to vanilla prompting across different model sizes
- Incorporating in-context demonstrations further enhances evaluation performance, especially for smaller models
- Combining definitions of highly correlated aspects can improve evaluation performance beyond individual aspect evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPTScore uses conditional generation probability to measure text quality, where higher-quality texts receive higher probabilities under the evaluation protocol.
- Mechanism: The framework calculates the log probability of generating the hypothesis text given a task specification, aspect definition, and optional context, using the generative pre-trained model's autoregressive decoding ability.
- Core assumption: A higher-quality generated text is more likely to be produced by the model when following the evaluation protocol than lower-quality texts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The core idea of GPTScore is that a generative pre-training model will assign a higher probability of high-quality generated text following a given instruction and context."
  - [section] "the higher-quality text of a certain aspect will be more likely generated than unqualified ones based on the given context, where the 'likely' can be measured by the conditional generation probability."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly address the conditional probability mechanism.
- Break condition: If the model's generative probability does not correlate with human judgment of quality, or if the model generates high-probability texts that are actually low-quality according to human evaluation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Instruction-based prompting significantly improves evaluation performance compared to vanilla prompting across different model sizes.
- Mechanism: Providing explicit task specifications and aspect definitions as instructions helps the model understand what quality dimensions to evaluate, leading to better alignment with human judgment.
- Core assumption: Large language models can effectively use natural language instructions to guide their evaluation behavior when provided with clear task and aspect definitions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experimental results... demonstrate that this approach can effectively allow us to achieve what one desires to evaluate for texts simply by natural language instructions."
  - [section] "Introducing instruction (IST) can significantly improve performance, and introducing demonstration (DM) will further improve performance."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly address instruction-based prompting performance.
- Break condition: If instructions are ambiguous, contradictory, or too complex for the model to interpret correctly, leading to degraded performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Incorporating in-context demonstrations with evaluation samples further enhances the evaluation performance, especially for smaller models.
- Mechanism: Adding a few exemplar evaluation samples to the prompt helps the model learn the desired evaluation pattern through few-shot learning, improving alignment with human judgment.
- Core assumption: Large language models can effectively learn evaluation patterns from a small number of demonstration samples through in-context learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The framework supports... and shows that combining instructions with in-context demonstrations further improves performance."
  - [section] "The generative pre-trained language model can better perform tasks when prefixed with a few annotated samples (i.e., demonstrations)."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus doesn't directly address in-context demonstration effectiveness.
- Break condition: If the demonstration samples are unrepresentative, misleading, or if the model overfits to the specific examples rather than learning the general evaluation pattern.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Task specification + Aspect definition + Hypothesis text + Optional context → Prompt template engine → Generative model → Probability calculation → Score aggregation → Optional demonstration handler

- Critical path: Task spec + Aspect def + Hypothesis text → Prompt template → Generative model → Probability calculation → Score aggregation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. cost: Larger models (GPT3-175B) may perform better but are more expensive to use
  - Instruction specificity vs. flexibility: More detailed instructions may improve performance but reduce adaptability
  - Demonstration quantity vs. efficiency: More examples improve performance but increase prompt length and cost

- Failure signatures:
  - Low correlation with human judgment despite high model confidence
  - Performance degradation when switching between different evaluation aspects
  - Inconsistent scores across similar texts or evaluation settings

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test baseline performance with vanilla prompting (no instructions) on a simple evaluation task
  2. Add instruction-only prompting and measure performance improvement
  3. Add in-context demonstrations and measure further performance gains, testing different numbers of examples

## Limitations

- Limited scope of evaluation aspects: The 22 evaluation aspects may not comprehensively cover all dimensions of text quality relevant to real-world applications.
- Dependency on model understanding of instructions: Performance heavily relies on the generative pre-trained model's ability to correctly interpret natural language instructions.
- Potential for circular evaluation: Using similar generative models for both evaluation and text generation may lead to overestimated performance on tasks where the model has inherent biases.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The core mechanism of using conditional generation probability for evaluation is well-grounded in the autoregressive nature of the models used. The experimental results showing performance improvements with instruction-based prompting and in-context demonstrations are supported by the data.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that GPTScore achieves competitive performance compared to existing automated metrics is supported by the experimental results, but the comparison is limited to specific datasets and evaluation aspects. The generalizability of these results to other text generation tasks or evaluation dimensions requires further validation.

**Low confidence**: The assertion that smaller models with instructions can outperform larger supervised models may be task-specific and not universally applicable. This claim requires more extensive testing across diverse tasks and evaluation aspects to establish its broader validity.

## Next Checks

2. **Instruction robustness analysis**: Systematically vary the complexity and specificity of instructions across multiple evaluation aspects to determine the threshold at which instruction-based prompting becomes ineffective or misleading for the model.
