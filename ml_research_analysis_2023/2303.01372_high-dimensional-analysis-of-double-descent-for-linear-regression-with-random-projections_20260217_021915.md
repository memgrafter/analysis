# High-dimensional analysis of double descent for linear regression with random projections

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01372
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01372
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: Asymptotic expressions for bias and variance in double descent phenomenon with random projections

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a rigorous high-dimensional analysis of double descent behavior in linear regression with random projections. Using random matrix theory, the authors derive asymptotic expressions for the bias and variance components of excess risk, showing how these terms behave as the number of random projections varies relative to the number of observations. The work bridges the gap between theoretical random matrix results and practical understanding of when and why double descent occurs in this specific setting.

## Method Summary
The authors employ high-dimensional random matrix theory tools to analyze the asymptotic behavior of ridge regression with random projections. They interpret the minimum norm least-squares fit using degrees of freedom concepts and derive precise asymptotic expressions for both bias and variance terms. The analysis focuses on the regime where both the number of observations and the number of random projections grow proportionally to infinity, allowing for clean mathematical characterization of the double descent phenomenon.

## Key Results
- Variance term explodes at m=n (where m is number of random projections and n is number of observations) and then decreases
- Bias term exhibits U-shaped behavior in under-parameterized regime, blows up at m=n, then decreases
- The double descent phenomenon is rigorously explained through the interplay between bias and variance terms
- Asymptotic expressions provide precise characterization of risk behavior across different regimes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on the fundamental properties of random projections in high dimensions. When the number of random projections is small, the model has limited capacity to fit the data, leading to high bias. As m approaches n, the random projection matrix becomes ill-conditioned, causing the variance to explode. Beyond this point, the additional projections provide redundant information, allowing the variance to decrease while maintaining low bias. The ridge regularization helps stabilize the solution but doesn't eliminate the fundamental double descent pattern.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Random Projections -> Ridge Regression -> Excess Risk Decomposition -> Double Descent Analysis

**Critical Path**: The core analysis flows from the random projection matrix through the ridge regression solution to the excess risk decomposition. The double descent behavior emerges from the interplay between bias and variance terms, which are determined by the spectral properties of the projected design matrix.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The choice of Gaussian random projections provides analytical tractability but may not reflect all practical scenarios. The asymptotic analysis assumes proportional growth of dimensions, which may not capture finite-sample effects. The ridge parameter introduces a regularization tradeoff that affects both bias and variance.

**Failure Signatures**: The theoretical predictions may break down near the critical regime where m ≈ n, where finite-sample effects become significant. Non-Gaussian projections may exhibit different behavior not captured by the current analysis. The linear regression setting limits applicability to nonlinear problems.
