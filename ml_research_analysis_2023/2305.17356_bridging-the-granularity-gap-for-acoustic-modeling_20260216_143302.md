# Bridging the Granularity Gap for Acoustic Modeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17356
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17356
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Progressive Down-Sampling (PDS) achieves 1.20× to 1.47× inference speedups in ASR while maintaining or improving performance, with competitive results on speech translation tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of modeling fine-grained frame-level acoustic features in speech processing by proposing Progressive Down-Sampling (PDS), which gradually compresses acoustic features into coarser-grained units containing more complete semantic information. The method also includes a representation fusion technique to mitigate information loss during high compression. Experimental results demonstrate that PDS achieves better or comparable performance on ASR tasks while yielding significant inference speedups, and shows competitive results on the more challenging speech translation task.

## Method Summary
The authors propose Progressive Down-Sampling (PDS) as a solution to bridge the granularity gap between frame-level acoustic features and coarser semantic units. PDS gradually compresses acoustic features through multiple stages, transforming fine-grained frame-level representations into coarser units that contain more complete semantic information similar to text-level representation. The method incorporates a representation fusion component that helps preserve information during the compression process, particularly at higher compression levels where information loss is most pronounced.

## Key Results
- Achieved inference speedups of 1.20× to 1.47× on ASR tasks while maintaining or improving performance
- Demonstrated better or comparable performance to baseline models on ASR task
- Showed competitive results on the more challenging speech translation task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Progressive Down-Sampling works by gradually transforming frame-level acoustic features into coarser-grained representations that better align with the semantic structure of target outputs like text. By progressively compressing information through multiple stages rather than applying aggressive compression in a single step, the method preserves more meaningful acoustic patterns while reducing computational complexity. The representation fusion component plays a critical role in mitigating information loss during high compression levels by intelligently combining features from different compression stages.
