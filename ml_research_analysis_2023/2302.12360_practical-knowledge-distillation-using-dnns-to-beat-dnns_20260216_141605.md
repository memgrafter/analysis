# Practical Knowledge Distillation: Using DNNs to Beat DNNs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.12360
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12360
- **Reference count**: 40
- **One-line primary result**: DNN ensembles with knowledge distillation achieve competitive or superior performance to XGBoost on tabular data, with up to 7.14% ROC AUC improvement.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the persistent challenge of improving deep learning model performance on tabular data, where gradient boosting methods like XGBoost typically outperform DNNs. The authors propose a practical method combining knowledge distillation, data denoising, and optimized ensembling to close this gap. Their approach distills ensembles of DNNs into simpler models suitable for high-performance real-time inference. Theoretically, the method is justified by proving its equivalence to classical cross-entropy knowledge distillation. Empirically, the authors demonstrate consistent improvements across several production applications deployed worldwide, with optimized DNN ensembles achieving competitive or superior performance to XGBoost, particularly on smaller datasets.

## Method Summary
The method employs TabNet as the primary DNN architecture for tabular data, enhanced through self-distillation where teacher and student models share the same architecture. Data denoising is performed by removing rows where predictions differ from labels by more than 0.99 threshold, and constant columns are eliminated from features. Hyperparameter tuning optimizes TabNet's attention layer dimension, prediction layer dimension, and decision steps. Multiple TabNet generations are created through iterative self-distillation, then combined into ensembles. Differential Evolution optimizes ensemble weights to maximize performance, and the final ensemble is distilled into simpler deployable models like XGBoost. This pipeline transforms TabNet ensembles into models that achieve performance competitive with or exceeding XGBoost on tabular datasets.

## Key Results
- DNN ensembles with knowledge distillation achieve competitive or superior performance to XGBoost on tabular data
- Gains of up to 7.14% in ROC AUC scores observed on tested datasets
- Method proves theoretically equivalent to classical cross-entropy knowledge distillation
- Consistent improvements demonstrated across multiple production applications worldwide

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge distillation using ensembles of DNNs can match or exceed the performance of XGBoost on tabular datasets.
- Mechanism: The approach leverages the diversity of DNN models to create robust ensembles that generalize well, especially on small datasets where XGBoost tends to overfit.
- Core assumption: DNN ensembles can capture patterns in data that individual models, including XGBoost, may miss.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "On tabular datasets, their optimized DNN ensembles achieve competitive or superior performance to XGBoost, with gains of up to 7.14% in ROC AUC scores."
  - [section] "Empirically, the authors demonstrate consistent improvements in model accuracy across several production applications deployed worldwide."
- Break condition: If the diversity of DNN models is insufficient or if the datasets are too large for DNNs to handle effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data denoising and distillation techniques can improve both gradient-boosting models and specialized DNN architectures.
- Mechanism: By removing noisy data and distilling knowledge from ensembles, the models are trained on cleaner, more representative data, leading to better performance.
- Core assumption: Noisy data negatively impacts model performance, and distilling knowledge from ensembles can provide a more robust learning signal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The authors explore knowledge distillation, data denoising, and optimized ensembling to close this gap."
  - [section] "We observe that TabNet underperforms XGBoost on larger datasets (100K rows)... With an eye on easy-to-implement optimizations of TabNet-based models, we assemble a series of practical improvements."
- Break condition: If the data denoising process removes too much information or if the distillation process fails to capture the essential knowledge from the ensembles.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Optimizing ensemble weights using techniques like Differential Evolution can significantly enhance model performance.
- Mechanism: By assigning optimal weights to each model in the ensemble, the combined predictions are more accurate than simple averaging.
- Core assumption: The performance of an ensemble depends on the optimal combination of its constituent models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Optimizing ensemble weights may look like a linear problem at the first sight, but the overall classifier performance depends on ensemble weights in nonlinear ways."
  - [section] "Tables 9 and 10 evaluate the optimization of ensemble weights on two datasets, using the default TabNet architecture and one with optimized hyperparameters."
- Break condition: If the optimization process overfits to the training data or if the ensemble models are too correlated to benefit from weighted averaging.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does knowledge distillation provide more benefit for DNNs than for XGBoost models on tabular data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "XGBoost still outperforms TabNet" initially, but after knowledge distillation and ensembling, "TabNet ensembles become competitive with XGBoost." It also notes that "DNNs gain more from knowledge distillation than XGBoost models do."
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates improved TabNet performance through distillation, it does not definitively quantify or compare the relative benefits for DNNs vs. XGBoost. The impact might depend on specific dataset characteristics or implementation details.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive empirical study comparing the performance gains from knowledge distillation on DNNs and XGBoost models across diverse tabular datasets, with detailed analysis of the factors influencing the benefits.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do TabNet and XGBoost models compare in terms of handling noisy labels in tabular data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses denoising methods and their impact on both TabNet and XGBoost, but does not specifically analyze how each model handles label noise.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that denoising improves performance for both models, it does not provide a detailed comparison of their robustness to label noise. The inherent noise-handling capabilities of the models might differ.
- What evidence would resolve it: An experiment where both TabNet and XGBoost are trained on datasets with varying levels of label noise, comparing their performance and resilience to noise.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the ensemble weight optimization technique be extended to other types of models beyond TabNet and XGBoost?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "ensemble models combine predictions of multiple constituent models, possibly of different types" and uses Differential Evolution to optimize weights. However, it does not explore the technique's applicability to other model types.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on TabNet and XGBoost, leaving open the question of whether the weight optimization approach is generalizable to other model types like decision trees, random forests, or other neural network architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the ensemble weight optimization technique to different combinations of model types, evaluating the impact on performance and generalizability.

## Limitations

- Claims rely heavily on proprietary datasets and internal production systems, making independent verification challenging
- Theoretical equivalence to classical cross-entropy knowledge distillation requires more rigorous mathematical proof
- Effectiveness of Differential Evolution for ensemble weight optimization is demonstrated empirically but lacks theoretical justification

## Confidence

- Claims about outperforming XGBoost on small datasets: **High** - supported by extensive production deployment evidence
- Theoretical equivalence to cross-entropy distillation: **Medium** - proof exists but lacks formal rigor
- Data denoising improvements: **Medium** - demonstrated empirically but threshold selection is arbitrary
- Differential Evolution ensemble optimization: **Low** - no comparison to simpler baseline methods like grid search
