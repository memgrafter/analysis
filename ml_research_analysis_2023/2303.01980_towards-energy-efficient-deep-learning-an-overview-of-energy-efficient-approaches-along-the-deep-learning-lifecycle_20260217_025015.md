# Towards energy-efficient Deep Learning: An overview of energy-efficient approaches along the Deep Learning Lifecycle

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01980
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01980
- Reference count: 0
- Primary result: Comprehensive overview of energy-efficient approaches for Deep Learning across its lifecycle phases

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically categorizes energy-efficient approaches for Deep Learning across six lifecycle phases: IT-Infrastructure, Data, Modeling, Training, Deployment, and Evaluation. The study synthesizes existing literature to identify techniques that reduce energy consumption and carbon emissions in DL models. By organizing these approaches along the DL lifecycle, the paper fills a critical gap in previous literature that often overlooked the evaluation phase and provided fragmented views of energy efficiency.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a literature review synthesizing information from four recent overviews of energy-efficient DL, with primary focus on two sources ([5] and [9]). The method involves mapping energy-efficient techniques to specific DL lifecycle phases, conducting a review of evaluation metrics and tools for measuring energy consumption and carbon footprint, and organizing findings into a structured overview. The approach relies on existing secondary sources rather than direct implementation or measurement of techniques.

## Key Results
- Energy efficiency can be systematically improved by applying specific techniques at each phase of the DL lifecycle
- Model compression techniques (pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation) significantly reduce inference energy consumption without substantial accuracy loss
- Pre-trained models and transfer learning reduce energy consumption by minimizing training iterations
- The paper identifies the need for standardized metrics for evaluating energy efficiency and carbon emissions across DL models

- Why unresolved: Different DL architectures, training approaches, and deployment scenarios make it challenging to create a universal metric that fairly compares energy efficiency across all contexts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of a comprehensive metric that accounts for various factors like model complexity, training duration, inference frequency, and hardware efficiency, tested across diverse DL applications.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between model accuracy and energy efficiency for different DL applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper emphasizes that "it is important to compress and accelerate these models before deploying them without compromising model accuracy" and discusses various compression techniques.
- Why unresolved: Different applications have varying requirements for accuracy and energy efficiency, making it difficult to establish a universal trade-off relationship.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing energy consumption and accuracy across various DL applications and compression techniques, leading to application-specific guidelines for optimal trade-offs.

- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies and pilot projects demonstrating successful integration of energy-efficient DL techniques into production environments, along with identified best practices and lessons learned.

## Limitations
- The analysis relies primarily on secondary sources rather than direct evaluation of techniques
- Absence of standardized metrics for energy efficiency and carbon emissions limits comparative analysis
- Some techniques could fit multiple lifecycle phases, potentially leading to overlap or misclassification

## Confidence

- Medium confidence: The relative effectiveness of specific techniques (e.g., model compression vs. training optimization)
- Low confidence: Precise energy savings quantification without standardized measurement tools
