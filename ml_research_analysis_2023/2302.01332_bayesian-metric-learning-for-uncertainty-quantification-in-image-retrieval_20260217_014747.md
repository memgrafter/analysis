# Bayesian Metric Learning for Uncertainty Quantification in Image Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01332
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01332
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: LAM achieves state-of-the-art predictive performance while providing well-calibrated uncertainties and reliable out-of-distribution detection across five datasets and three network architectures.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Laplacian Metric Learning (LAM), the first Bayesian approach for metric learning in image retrieval. The authors address the challenge of uncertainty quantification in metric learning by applying the Laplace Approximation to learn a distribution over network weights. They prove that the contrastive loss can be interpreted as an unnormalized log-posterior and propose three methods to ensure a positive definite Hessian. Empirical results demonstrate that LAM achieves state-of-the-art predictive performance while providing well-calibrated uncertainties and reliable out-of-distribution detection across five datasets, three network architectures, and varying latent space dimensions.

## Method Summary
LAM extends the Laplace Approximation to metric learning by interpreting the contrastive loss as an unnormalized negative log-posterior of a product of von Mises-Fisher distributions. The method learns a Gaussian distribution over network weights, either post-hoc or online during training, and uses this posterior to generate stochastic embeddings with calibrated uncertainties. Three Hessian approximation methods (positive, fixed, and full) ensure positive definiteness, which is critical for valid covariance estimates. The approach is evaluated on five image retrieval datasets using standard convolutional networks and ResNet50 architectures.

## Key Results
- LAM outperforms existing probabilistic retrieval models in both OOD detection (AUROC) and predictive performance (mAP@5)
- The method provides well-calibrated uncertainties across different latent space dimensions (3-2048)
- Online Laplace Approximation is more stable than post-hoc methods while maintaining comparable performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The contrastive loss can be interpreted as an unnormalized negative log-posterior, enabling Bayesian treatment via Laplace Approximation.
- Mechanism: The authors prove that the contrastive loss is equivalent to the negative log-likelihood of a product of von Mises-Fisher distributions, one attractive (centered at the embedding) and one repulsive (centered at the antipodal point), allowing the loss to be treated as a valid log-posterior.
- Core assumption: The contrastive loss with margin m > 2 ensures all negative pairs are within the margin, making the repulsive term a continuous von Mises-Fisher distribution rather than a discontinuous density.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We present a probabilistic interpretation of the contrastive loss (Hadsell et al., 2006) which justifies that it can be interpreted as an unnormalized negative log-posterior."
  - [section D]: "D. Probabilistic view" section provides the formal proof that the contrastive loss is equivalent to the negative log-likelihood of a product of von Mises-Fisher distributions.
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the margin m ≤ 2, the proof breaks down as the repulsive term becomes discontinuous and the normalization constant depends on both κ and m.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Three Hessian approximations ensure positive definiteness, which is critical for the Laplace Approximation to yield valid covariance estimates.
- Mechanism: The authors propose three methods to ensure a positive definite Hessian: (1) Positive approximation ignores the repelling term, (2) Fixed approximation ignores cross-derivatives, and (3) Full approximation applies ReLU to the Hessian. These methods guarantee that the inverse of the Hessian (covariance matrix) is well-defined.
- Core assumption: The Hessian of the contrastive loss wrt the model output has eigenvalues (0, 2) for positive pairs and (-2, 0) for negative pairs, which are not guaranteed to be positive definite.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We then propose three methods that ensure a positive definite Hessian."
  - [section]: "Ensuring positive definiteness of the Hessian" section describes the three approximations and their intuition.
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the Hessian is not positive definite, the Laplace Approximation fails as the covariance matrix cannot be inverted, leading to invalid uncertainty estimates.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Online Laplace Approximation improves model fit by marginalizing the Laplace Approximation during training, avoiding the instability of post-hoc Laplace.
- Mechanism: At each training step, the method keeps a Gaussian distribution on the parameters and updates them through an expected gradient step and a discounted Laplace update, recovering a solution where the Hessian reflects the loss landscape.
- Core assumption: Stochastic gradient-based training explores regions near local minima rather than isolated minima, making post-hoc Laplace unstable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We extend the Laplace Approximation (MacKay, 1992) for metric learning."
  - [section]: "Online Laplace approximations (Miani et al., 2022) avoids this instability by marginalizing the LA during training with Monte Carlo EM."
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the online updates do not properly track the loss landscape, the resulting posterior may still be unstable or poorly calibrated.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: von Mises-Fisher distribution
  - Why needed here: The von Mises-Fisher distribution is the natural choice for modeling uncertainty on the unit sphere, which is the latent space in metric learning with ℓ2-normalized networks.
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between the concentration parameter κ and the variance in a von Mises-Fisher distribution?
- Concept: Generalized Gauss-Newton (GGN) approximation
  - Why needed here: The GGN approximation is used to compute the Hessian of the contrastive loss efficiently, which is necessary for the Laplace Approximation.
  - Quick check question: How does the GGN approximation decompose the loss function, and why is this decomposition non-trivial for the contrastive loss?
- Concept: Hard negative mining
  - Why needed here: Hard negative mining is used to construct batches with an over-representation of hard negative pairs, which is necessary for computing the Hessian but leads to a biased estimate that must be corrected.
  - Quick check question: Why does hard negative mining lead to a biased Hessian estimate, and how is this bias corrected in the proposed method?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Images -> Backbone network -> ℓ2 normalization -> Contrastive loss -> Bayesian component -> Uncertainty estimation -> Stochastic embeddings

- Critical path:
  1. Train backbone network with contrastive loss
  2. Compute Hessian of contrastive loss using GGN approximation
  3. Apply one of three methods to ensure positive definite Hessian
  4. Perform Laplace Approximation (post-hoc or online) to obtain weight posterior
  5. Sample from weight posterior to obtain multiple embeddings
  6. Estimate von Mises-Fisher parameters from sampled embeddings
  7. Use uncertainty estimates for OOD detection and calibration

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Post-hoc vs online Laplace: Post-hoc is simpler but less stable; online is more stable but computationally expensive
  - Hessian approximation methods: Positive is simplest but ignores negatives; fixed is more accurate but still an approximation; full is most accurate but requires ReLU
  - Diagonal vs full Hessian: Diagonal is computationally efficient but less accurate; full is more accurate but memory-intensive

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor OOD detection: Indicates uncalibrated uncertainties or insufficient exploration of weight posterior
  - Unstable training: Suggests issues with Hessian approximation or Laplace updates
  - Degraded predictive performance: May indicate over-regularization from Laplace Approximation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement and train a simple convnet on FashionMNIST with contrastive loss to establish baseline performance
  2. Add post-hoc Laplace Approximation and evaluate uncertainty calibration on FashionMNIST vs MNIST
  3. Implement online Laplace Approximation and compare OOD detection performance against post-hoc and deterministic baselines

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the Hessian approximation scale with increasing latent space dimensions beyond 2048?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates performance on latent spaces ranging from 3 to 2048 dimensions but does not explore beyond this range.
- Why unresolved: The computational complexity of Hessian approximations grows quadratically with the number of parameters, making larger dimensions computationally challenging to evaluate.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing performance degradation or computational bottlenecks when scaling to 4096+ dimensional embeddings would clarify the practical limits of LAM.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different negative sampling strategies on the positive definiteness of the Hessian?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that hard negative mining leads to a biased Hessian approximation and introduces a scaling parameter wn to correct this.
- Why unresolved: While the paper proposes a correction factor, it doesn't systematically explore how different negative sampling strategies (e.g., semi-hard mining, distance-weighted sampling) affect the Hessian and model performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study comparing different negative sampling strategies with and without the scaling correction would reveal optimal sampling strategies for LAM.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does LAM perform on cross-modal retrieval tasks (e.g., text-to-image retrieval)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses exclusively on image retrieval tasks and does not explore cross-modal scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The Laplacian approximation and von Mises-Fisher distribution may behave differently when applied to heterogeneous data types with different distributional properties.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results on cross-modal datasets like Flickr30k or MSCOCO would demonstrate LAM's applicability beyond unimodal image retrieval.

## Limitations
- The margin constraint (m > 2) is critical for theoretical validity but may not be standard in practical metric learning applications
- Computational overhead of online Laplace Approximation is significant but not quantified relative to inference time
- Focus on retrieval-specific metrics without connecting to broader uncertainty quantification evaluation frameworks

## Confidence

- High confidence: The theoretical framework connecting contrastive loss to von Mises-Fisher distributions is mathematically rigorous and well-explained
- Medium confidence: The empirical superiority of LAM over existing probabilistic retrieval methods is demonstrated, but some baselines (Deep Ensemble) may not be optimally implemented for the retrieval setting
- Low confidence: The computational complexity analysis is incomplete, and the memory requirements for full Hessian approximation are not discussed
