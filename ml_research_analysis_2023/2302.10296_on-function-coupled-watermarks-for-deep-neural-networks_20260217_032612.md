# On Function-Coupled Watermarks for Deep Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10296
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10296

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel DNN watermarking method that addresses the problem of watermark removal attacks such as model fine-tuning and pruning. The key idea is to enhance the coupling of the watermark and model functionalities by using features from in-distribution data and applying random masking during training. This makes the watermark information spread across the network, making it difficult to remove. Experimental results on image classification tasks (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, and Tiny-ImageNet) show that the proposed method achieves a 100% watermark authentication success rate under aggressive watermark removal attacks, significantly outperforming existing solutions. The method also maintains high model accuracy, with perturbations within ±0.5% compared to non-watermarked models.

## Method Summary
The proposed function-coupled watermarking method enhances the coupling between watermark and model functionalities by utilizing in-distribution data features and random masking during training. The approach embeds watermark information into the model by making it depend on features from real data samples, rather than arbitrary patterns. Random masking during training ensures that the watermark information is distributed across multiple network layers and neurons, making it resilient to localized removal attacks like pruning or fine-tuning. This coupling ensures that removing the watermark would also degrade the model's performance on its primary task, creating a trade-off that deters attackers.

## Key Results
- Achieves 100% watermark authentication success rate under aggressive watermark removal attacks
- Maintains model accuracy within ±0.5% of non-watermarked models
- Outperforms existing watermarking solutions on multiple datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by creating a strong dependency between the watermark and the model's functional behavior. By embedding watermark information into features derived from in-distribution data, the watermark becomes integral to the model's decision-making process. Random masking during training ensures that this information is distributed across the network rather than localized in specific neurons or layers. This distribution makes it difficult for attackers to remove the watermark without significantly impacting the model's primary functionality, as any attempt to remove the watermark would likely degrade performance on the main task.

## Foundational Learning
- **Function coupling**: The process of intertwining watermark information with the model's primary functionality to make removal attacks costly. Why needed: Prevents attackers from easily removing watermarks without degrading model performance. Quick check: Verify that watermark removal attempts cause significant accuracy drops.
- **In-distribution feature utilization**: Using features from real training data to embed watermark information. Why needed: Makes the watermark more resistant to attacks that rely on out-of-distribution data. Quick check: Confirm that watermark is not detectable in arbitrary inputs.
- **Random masking**: Applying stochastic masking during training to distribute watermark information across the network. Why needed: Prevents localization of watermark in specific neurons, enhancing robustness. Quick check: Ensure watermark information is present across multiple layers.
- **Adversarial robustness**: The ability of the watermark to withstand targeted removal attacks. Why needed: Validates the practical security of the watermarking scheme. Quick check: Test against fine-tuning and pruning attacks.
- **Model functionality preservation**: Maintaining the primary task performance while embedding watermarks. Why needed: Ensures the watermarking process does not degrade the model's intended use. Quick check: Compare accuracy with and without watermarking.
- **Watermark authentication**: The process of verifying the presence of the embedded watermark. Why needed: Confirms the effectiveness of the watermarking scheme. Quick check: Test authentication success rate under various conditions.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Input data -> Feature extractor -> Random masking module -> Watermark embedding layer -> Classification head
- **Critical path**: The watermark embedding process relies on the interaction between feature extraction from in-distribution data and random masking during training.
- **Design tradeoffs**: The method trades some training complexity (due to random masking) for increased watermark robustness. This may increase training time but provides better security guarantees.
- **Failure signatures**: If the watermark is easily removable without significant accuracy loss, the function coupling is insufficient. If the model accuracy drops significantly, the watermark embedding may be too aggressive.

## Limitations
- The claim of 100% watermark authentication success rate needs careful interpretation, as specific attack parameters and threat models may not represent all practical scenarios.
- The experimental scope is limited to image classification tasks, leaving open questions about performance on other domains like natural language processing or speech recognition.
- The computational overhead introduced by random masking during training and its impact on convergence speed is not thoroughly discussed.

## Confidence
- Core contribution of function-coupled watermarking: Medium (methodology is sound but limited by scope of experiments)
- Claims about universal robustness: Low (given the narrow range of attacks tested)
