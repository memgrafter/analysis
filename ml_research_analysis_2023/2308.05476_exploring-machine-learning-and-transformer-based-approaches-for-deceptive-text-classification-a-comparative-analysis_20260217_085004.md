# Exploring Machine Learning and Transformer-based Approaches for Deceptive Text Classification: A Comparative Analysis

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2308.05476
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05476
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: RoBERTa achieved 91.3% accuracy in deceptive text classification, outperforming traditional ML models by 0.8%

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comparative analysis of machine learning and transformer-based approaches for detecting deceptive text in online reviews. The study evaluates five traditional ML algorithms (SVM, LSVM, Passive Aggressive, Logistic Regression, Multinomial Naive Bayes) against four transformer models (BERT, XLNET, DistilBERT, RoBERTa) using a dataset of 1600 hotel reviews. The results demonstrate that transformer-based models, particularly RoBERTa, significantly outperform traditional ML approaches in identifying deceptive text, with RoBERTa achieving 91.3% accuracy. The paper also explores the impact of TF-IDF feature extraction parameters on ML model performance and suggests future research directions involving collaborative filtering techniques.

## Method Summary
The study uses a deceptive detection dataset from Kaggle containing 1600 reviews (800 truthful, 800 fake) about Chicago hotels from TripAdvisor and Amazon Mechanical Turk. Data preprocessing includes removing special characters, punctuation, stopwords, lowercasing, and lemmatization. Feature extraction employs TF-IDF vectorization with n-gram range (2,2) and max features=1000 for ML models. The evaluation compares five traditional ML models and four transformer models using accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and AUC-ROC metrics through train-test split evaluation.

## Key Results
- RoBERTa achieved the highest accuracy of 91.3% with an F1 score of 90.6%, recall of 85.0%, precision of 97.1%, and AUC of 97.1%
- Passive Aggressive classifier, the best traditional ML model, achieved 90.5% accuracy, trailing RoBERTa by 0.8%
- TF-IDF parameters significantly influenced ML model accuracy, with n-gram range (2,2) and max features=1000 yielding optimal results

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Feature extraction (TF-IDF/word embeddings) -> Model training (ML/Transformer models) -> Model evaluation (accuracy, precision, recall, F1, AUC-ROC)
- Critical path: 1. Data preprocessing and feature extraction 2. Model training and hyperparameter tuning 3. Model evaluation and comparison
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Machine learning vs. transformer-based models: Machine learning models are simpler and faster to train but may not capture complex contextual relationships. Transformer-based models are more powerful but require more computational resources and larger datasets.
  - Feature extraction techniques: TF-IDF is effective for capturing word importance but may miss out on contextual information. Word embeddings capture semantic relationships but may require more sophisticated models to interpret.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy: Indicates that the model is not effectively distinguishing between deceptive and non-deceptive text. Possible causes include insufficient data, poor feature extraction, or inadequate model architecture.
  - High precision but low recall: Suggests that the model is overly conservative in identifying deceptive text, potentially missing many instances. This could be due to an imbalanced dataset or a model that is too sensitive to false positives.
  - Low precision but high recall: Implies that the model is flagging many instances as deceptive, including non-deceptive text. This may be caused by an overly aggressive model or a dataset with a high proportion of deceptive text.

- The study focuses exclusively on hotel reviews from two sources (TripAdvisor and Amazon Mechanical Turk), which may not capture the full diversity of deceptive text patterns across different domains
