# XploreNAS: Explore Adversarially Robust & Hardware-efficient Neural Architectures for Non-ideal Xbars

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.07769
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07769
- Reference count: 35
- This work addresses the problem of finding DNN architectures that are both adversarially robust and hardware-efficient when deployed on non-ideal memristive crossbar platforms.

## Executive Summary
XploreNAS introduces a two-phase algorithm-hardware co-optimization approach for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that targets non-ideal memristive crossbar platforms. The method trains a Supernet while incorporating crossbar-level noise and adversarial training, then derives optimal Subnets that balance robustness against adversarial attacks with hardware efficiency metrics like Energy-Delay-Area-Products (EDAP). Experiments demonstrate that Subnets identified by XploreNAS achieve 8-16% higher adversarial robustness compared to ResNet-18 baselines under PGD attacks, while also achieving 1.5-1.6× lower EDAPs when measured using Neurosim.

## Method Summary
XploreNAS employs a two-phase approach to hardware-aware Neural Architecture Search. In Phase-1, the Supernet is trained using clean data to update weights while maintaining a shared architecture across all operations. Phase-2 involves architecture parameter updates using crossbar noise and adversarial inputs (PGD-7 or PGD-20) to identify robust operations. After training, optimal Subnets are derived by selecting operations based on trained architecture parameters (threshold of 0.2), followed by fine-tuning the Subnet with adversarial training and crossbar noise. The method targets both adversarial robustness and hardware efficiency by incorporating crossbar-specific non-idealities like interconnect parasitics and NVM device variations into the search process.

## Key Results
- XploreNAS-derived Subnets achieve 8-16% higher adversarial robustness compared to ResNet-18 baseline under strong PGD attacks
- Subnets attain 1.5-1.6× lower Energy-Delay-Area-Products (EDAPs) as measured using Neurosim
- The method maintains robustness across different crossbar sizes (32×32 to 128×128) and device variations without re-training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
XploreNAS works by jointly optimizing for adversarial robustness and hardware efficiency during the Supernet training process. By incorporating crossbar-level noise and adversarial training directly into the architecture search, the method identifies operations that are both robust to attacks and efficient on non-ideal crossbar hardware. The two-phase approach allows for separate optimization of weights and architecture parameters, with crossbar noise modeling ensuring that selected operations perform well under real hardware constraints. The use of shared architecture parameters across all operations in the Supernet enables efficient search while maintaining diversity in operation selection.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input Data -> Supernet Training (Phase-1 & Phase-2) -> Architecture Parameter Selection -> Subnet Derivation -> Subnet Fine-tuning -> Hardware-efficient Robust Model

**Critical Path**: The most time-consuming aspect is the two-phase Supernet training, particularly Phase-2 which requires adversarial example generation and crossbar noise simulation. This dominates the computational cost but is necessary for identifying robust operations.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades increased training complexity (two-phase process with adversarial examples) for improved robustness and efficiency. There's also a tradeoff between hardware efficiency (minimizing crossbar underutilization) and model accuracy that is managed through the architecture search process.

**Failure Signatures**: If crossbar noise is not accurately modeled, Subnets may fail to achieve expected robustness in hardware deployment. Similarly, if the architecture search doesn't properly minimize crossbar underutilization, hardware efficiency gains may not materialize despite theoretical optimization.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

**Open Question 1**: How does the performance of XploreNAS compare to other hardware-aware NAS methods when applied to non-ideal crossbar platforms with different types of non-idealities beyond the ones studied? The paper focuses on specific non-idealities (interconnect parasitics and NVM device variations) and crossbar sizes (32x32 to 128x128). It would be valuable to understand the generalizability of XploreNAS to other types of non-idealities and crossbar configurations.

**Open Question 2**: Can the XploreNAS approach be extended to optimize neural architectures for other types of hardware platforms beyond memristive crossbar arrays, such as FPGAs or ASICs? The paper focuses on memristive crossbar arrays as the target hardware platform. It would be interesting to investigate the applicability of XploreNAS to other hardware platforms.

**Open Question 3**: How does the performance of XploreNAS change when considering different types of adversarial attacks beyond PGD, such as Carlini-Wagner or DeepFool attacks? The paper focuses on PGD attacks as the adversarial attack method. It would be valuable to understand the robustness of XploreNAS against other types of adversarial attacks.

## Limitations

- Crossbar noise modeling details, including parasitic resistances and device variations, are not fully specified, which could affect the accuracy of robustness measurements
- Specific hyperparameter values for training (learning rates, batch sizes, hardware efficiency weight) are not provided, making precise replication difficult
- The impact of crossbar size and device variation changes on model performance is not fully explored, limiting confidence in claims about maintaining robustness without re-training

## Confidence

**Adversarial Robustness Claims**: Medium confidence - The 8-16% improvement over ResNet-18 is reported, but the accuracy depends on precise crossbar noise modeling which is not fully detailed in the paper.

**Hardware Efficiency Claims**: Medium confidence - The 1.5-1.6× EDAP reduction is measured using Neurosim, but the accuracy of this measurement depends on accurate modeling of crossbar underutilization during architecture search, which is not fully specified.

**Crossbar Size Generalization**: Medium confidence - The claim about maintaining robustness across different crossbar sizes without re-training is made, but the impact of these variations on performance is not fully explored.
