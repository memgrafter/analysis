# Improving Out-of-Distribution Generalization of Neural Rerankers with Contextualized Late Interaction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06589
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06589
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: Adding contextualized late interaction to neural rerankers brings 5% average improvement on out-of-distribution datasets while maintaining in-domain performance with minimal latency increase.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether adding contextualized late interaction to neural rerankers can improve their out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. While multi-vector models like ColBERT have shown robustness in first-stage dense retrieval, it remains unclear whether similar benefits apply to rerankers that use only the [CLS] token for relevance scoring. The authors propose a simple method to add late interaction to rerankers by computing token-level similarities after the [CLS]-based score. Their experiments show significant gains on OOD datasets in BEIR while maintaining in-domain performance and adding minimal latency. The improvement is consistent across model sizes and diverse first-stage retrievers, and is more pronounced for longer queries.

## Method Summary
The authors extend monoBERT rerankers by adding a contextualized late interaction module that computes token-level similarities between query and document embeddings at the last layer. The [CLS] token representation is used to compute a base relevance score, and the late interaction module provides an additional score based on token-level similarities. The two scores are combined additively. The model is trained using LCE loss on the MS MARCO passage ranking dataset and evaluated on BEIR's 13 out-of-distribution datasets by reranking top-1k passages from first-stage retrievers.

## Key Results
- Adding contextualized late interaction improves average OOD performance by 5% on BEIR datasets
- The improvement is consistent across model sizes (MiniLM, ELECTRA base, ELECTRA large) and first-stage retrievers
- Gains are more pronounced for longer queries (gain increases from 1% to 12% as query length increases from 2-4 to 8-10 tokens)
- In-domain performance on MS MARCO remains unaffected while OOD robustness improves significantly

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adding late interaction to neural rerankers captures token-level interactions that the [CLS] token alone cannot fully represent, improving OOD generalization.
- Mechanism: Late interaction computes token-level similarities between query and document embeddings at the last layer, supplementing the [CLS]-based relevance score. This provides an additional 5% improvement on average for OOD datasets while maintaining in-domain performance.
- Core assumption: The [CLS] token representation, while capturing sequence-level semantics, loses some token-level granularity that is important for OOD robustness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we find adding late interaction still brings an extra 5% improvement in average on out-of-distribution datasets, with little increase in latency and no degradation in in-domain effectiveness."
  - [section] "late interaction at the last layer actually brings 'free' OOD capacity to rerankers."
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers; average neighbor FMR=0.462, suggesting moderate relevance to multi-vector retrieval and late interaction research.
- Break condition: If the token-level interactions are already fully captured by the attention mechanism in the previous layers, adding late interaction would provide no additional benefit.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The improvement from late interaction is not primarily due to exact-matched lexical signals but rather captures more nuanced semantic interactions.
- Mechanism: Late interaction considers token-level similarities beyond exact matches, allowing the model to capture semantic relationships that are not present in the exact-match tokens alone.
- Core assumption: Exact-matched tokens alone are insufficient for OOD generalization, and additional token-level interactions contribute to robustness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We found that OOD capacity improvement brought by the late interactions is not through the embedded exact-match signals but other factors instead."
  - [corpus] Weak corpus evidence; related papers focus on multi-vector retrieval but lack direct evidence for exact-match vs. semantic interactions.
- Break condition: If the exact-matched tokens were the primary source of OOD improvement, removing them would significantly degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Late interaction is more effective for longer queries, suggesting that it helps capture interactions in more complex query-document pairs.
- Mechanism: Longer queries have more tokens, providing more opportunities for meaningful token-level interactions that the late interaction mechanism can capture.
- Core assumption: Longer queries contain more complex information that benefits from token-level interaction beyond what the [CLS] token can represent.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "late interaction brings higher improvement on longer queries."
  - [corpus] Moderate corpus relevance (FMR=0.562); related work on multi-vector retrieval but limited specific evidence on query length effects.
- Break condition: If query length does not correlate with the effectiveness of late interaction, this mechanism would not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input -> Backbone Transformer -> CLS scorer -> Late interaction module -> Combined output

- Critical path:
  1. Encode query and document using Transformer backbone
  2. Extract [CLS] token representation and compute CLS-based score
  3. Extract token-level representations and project to lower dimensions
  4. Compute token-level similarities and aggregate
  5. Combine CLS and late interaction scores

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Latency vs. effectiveness: Adding late interaction increases latency slightly but significantly improves OOD performance
  - Model complexity: Simple addition of late interaction vs. more complex reranking architectures
  - Token dimensions: Higher dimensions provide marginal improvement but increase computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - No improvement on OOD datasets: May indicate that token-level interactions are already well-captured by the [CLS] token
  - Degradation in in-domain performance: Suggests that the late interaction is not complementary to the CLS-based scoring
  - Excessive latency increase: Indicates inefficient implementation of the token-level similarity computation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic monoBERT reranker with [CLS]-only scoring and evaluate on MS MARCO and BEIR
  2. Add late interaction module and compare OOD performance while monitoring latency
  3. Test different token dimensions (e.g., 1, 32, 128, 384) to find optimal balance between effectiveness and efficiency

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the content, the following questions arise:
- How do late interactions specifically improve robustness on out-of-distribution datasets compared to in-domain datasets?
- Is the improvement from late interactions consistent across all types of queries and documents, or does it vary based on specific characteristics like length or complexity?
- What is the optimal configuration for token dimensions in late interactions to balance effectiveness and computational efficiency?

## Limitations
- The paper focuses primarily on passage reranking, leaving open questions about whether similar benefits would be observed in document-level or cross-encoder reranking scenarios
- The exact implementation details of the late interaction module are not fully specified
- The analysis of failure cases where late interaction provides minimal benefit is limited

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The core finding that contextualized late interaction improves OOD performance on BEIR datasets (5% average improvement) is well-supported by experimental results across multiple model sizes and first-stage retrievers. The ablation studies showing consistent improvements across query lengths also have strong empirical backing.

**Medium Confidence:** The claim that improvements are not primarily driven by exact-matched lexical signals is supported by the evidence provided, but the analysis could be strengthened with more detailed breakdowns of which token-level interactions contribute most to OOD robustness.

**Low Confidence:** The assertion that late interaction is particularly beneficial for longer queries, while supported by data, lacks a theoretical explanation for why this relationship exists. The mechanism section provides some intuition but doesn't fully explain the underlying reasons.
