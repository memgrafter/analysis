# Multifactor Sequential Disentanglement via Structured Koopman Autoencoders

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17264
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17264
- Reference count: 34
- One-line primary result: Unsupervised multifactor sequential disentanglement method using Koopman autoencoders achieves competitive performance with weakly-supervised state-of-the-art approaches on benchmarks like Sprites and MUG.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to multifactor sequential disentanglement that extends beyond typical two-factor separation to handle complex sequential data with multiple semantically distinct components. The method leverages Koopman operator theory, assuming that underlying dynamics can be represented linearly in a learned latent space. A key innovation is a spectral loss term that structures the Koopman matrix's spectrum, enabling the identification of static and dynamic eigenvectors corresponding to disentangled factors. The approach is fully unsupervised and demonstrates significant improvements over unsupervised baselines while remaining competitive with weakly-supervised methods across multiple evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses a Koopman autoencoder architecture with an encoder-decoder structure augmented by a Koopman module that computes a linear operator on the latent space. The core innovation is a spectral loss term that enforces a structured spectrum in the Koopman matrix, separating static factors (eigenvectors with eigenvalue 1) from dynamic factors (eigenvectors with eigenvalues ≠ 1). The method processes sequential data through CNN and LSTM layers, applies the Koopman linearization, and uses spectral penalties during training to achieve multifactor disentanglement. After training, factors can be swapped between sequences by exchanging Koopman projection coefficients along identified eigenvectors.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance among unsupervised methods on Sprites dataset across accuracy (0.812), inception score (2.80), and entropy (0.560) metrics
- Demonstrates competitive performance with weakly-supervised methods like Fader Networks on multiple benchmarks
- Successfully performs incremental swaps of dynamic features and static factor exchanges between sequences

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Static and dynamic factors are disentangled by exploiting Koopman operator eigenvalues.
- Mechanism: Eigenvectors of the Koopman matrix with eigenvalue 1 encode static (time-invariant) factors, while eigenvectors with eigenvalues ≠ 1 encode dynamic (time-varying) factors.
- Core assumption: The underlying dynamics of the sequential data can be represented linearly in a learned latent space (Koopman theory assumption).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "eigenvectors of the approximate Koopman operator represent time-invariant and time-variant factors."
  - [section] "eigenvectors of the matrix C whose eigenvalue is 1 represent time-invariant factors."
- Break condition: If the true dynamics are highly nonlinear and cannot be well-approximated by a linear operator in any latent space, the Koopman assumption fails and the spectral structure collapses.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The spectral loss term enforces a structured Koopman matrix that separates static and dynamic factors.
- Mechanism: The loss term penalizes eigenvalues away from 1 for static factors (Lstat) and penalizes dynamic eigenvalues within an epsilon-ball around 1 (Ldyn), encouraging clear separation in the spectrum.
- Core assumption: Adding explicit spectral penalties during training will guide the Koopman matrix to adopt a form where static and dynamic factors are cleanly separated by eigenvalue magnitude.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we propose a novel spectral penalty term which splits the operator's spectrum to separate and clearly-defined sets of static and dynamic eigenvectors."
  - [section] "Ldyn penalizes dynamic factors whose modulus is outside an ϵ-ball."
- Break condition: If the dataset does not contain separable static/dynamic factors, the spectral loss may force an artificial separation that harms reconstruction quality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Swapping between factors is achieved by exchanging Koopman projection coefficients along identified eigenvectors.
- Mechanism: By replacing the projection coefficients of selected eigenvectors between two samples, the corresponding static or dynamic factors can be swapped while keeping the rest of the representation intact.
- Core assumption: The eigenvectors identified as corresponding to a particular factor remain stable and meaningful for swapping across samples in the same batch.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "swap the static features of the batch with those of the new samples, ¯Z[:, :,I stat] = ∑iαi ¯Z[i, :, Istat]."
- Break condition: If the eigenvector subspace assignments are inconsistent across samples or batches, swapping will produce incoherent outputs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input -> Encoder (CNN + LSTM) -> Latent code Z -> Koopman Module (splits Z into past/future, computes C, reconstructs future) -> Decoder (reverse of encoder) -> Output

- Critical path:
  Encode -> Koopman matrix computation (via least squares solve) -> Eigen-decomposition for factor identification -> Loss aggregation (reconstruction + prediction + spectral) -> Decode

- Design tradeoffs:
  - More static factors (larger ks) increases model flexibility but may weaken dynamic structure if the spectrum is over-constrained
  - Larger latent dimension k increases capacity but also computational cost of eigen-decomposition
  - Epsilon threshold in Ldyn controls strictness of dynamic factor separation

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor swapping results -> eigenvectors not stable or subspace identification incorrect
  - Collapse in reconstruction -> too strong spectral penalty pushing eigenvalues away from optimal
  - Indistinguishable static/dynamic factors -> spectrum not well-separated; adjust ks/kd or epsilon

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on Sprites with ks=4, kd=36, epsilon=0.5; verify static factor swapping works visually
  2. Remove Ldyn loss; observe if static/dynamic separation degrades (expected failure mode)
  3. Swap only dynamic factors in MUG; confirm expressions change while identity remains fixed

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: Can the proposed spectral loss term be further optimized to improve disentanglement quality, especially in cases with more than two factors?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that identifying individual factors like hair color or skin color in Sprites is challenging due to the unknown dimensionality of the subspaces encoding each factor. They propose a simple automatic procedure but leave further exploration for future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore alternative methods for optimizing the spectral loss term or investigate how it performs with a larger number of factors. The automatic identification procedure might not be optimal for all datasets and factor combinations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the performance of the proposed spectral loss term with other loss functions or optimization techniques on datasets with a larger number of factors. Evaluating the effectiveness of the automatic identification procedure on various datasets and factor combinations.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness is currently validated on relatively constrained datasets (Sprites, MUG, TIMIT), and its performance on more complex, real-world sequential data remains untested
- The automatic subspace identification for factor assignment is not fully specified and may not generalize well to datasets with different factor structures
- The reliance on linear Koopman assumptions may limit applicability to highly nonlinear dynamics where the spectrum cannot be meaningfully separated
