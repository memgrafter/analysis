## Executive Summary
This paper presents STEER (Stackelberg Decision Transformer), a novel approach for asynchronous action coordination in multi-agent systems. STEER leverages a Transformer-based architecture to model sequential decision-making processes, enabling efficient management of complex interactions among agents in both spatial and temporal contexts. By incorporating the hierarchical decision structure of Stackelberg games, STEER effectively handles leader-follower relationships and converges to Stackelberg equilibrium solutions.

The method is evaluated on various benchmarks, including multi-agent MuJoCo, Google Research Football, and Highway On-Ramp Merging tasks. STEER demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods, effectively managing decision-making processes in both spatial and temporal contexts. The approach shows high adaptability across different task types and environmental configurations, making it a promising solution for asynchronous action coordination in multi-agent systems.

## Method Summary
STEER is a novel approach that uses a Transformer-based architecture to model sequential decision-making processes in multi-agent systems. It incorporates the hierarchical decision structure of Stackelberg games, where leaders commit to actions first and followers respond optimally. The method employs an Inner Transformer Block (ITB) to process state information and an Outer Transformer Block (OTB) to generate actions autoregressively based on agent priorities. STEER is trained using a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, which updates actor and critic networks based on transitions stored in a replay buffer. The approach is designed to handle asynchronous action coordination efficiently and converges to Stackelberg equilibrium solutions.

## Key Results

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Inner Transformer Block (ITB): Processes state information (local observations, global state) into agent-specific embeddings
  - Outer Transformer Block (OTB): Processes decision information autoregressively to generate actions in leader-follower order
  - Actor Head: Generates actions for each agent based on sub-game state
  - Critic Head: Estimates value functions for each agent
  - Training Loop: Uses PPO with advantage estimation and entropy regularization

- Critical path:
  1. Environment provides state/observations to all agents
  2. ITB processes state information into embeddings
  3. OTB generates actions autoregressively based on priority
  4. Actions are executed, rewards are received
  5. Transitions are stored in replay buffer
  6. PPO updates actor and critic networks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Centralized training with decentralized execution vs fully centralized approach
  - Flexibility in handling different environmental configurations vs specialized encoding for specific tasks
  - Computational cost of Transformer blocks vs potential performance gains

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor convergence to Stackelberg equilibrium: may indicate incorrect modeling of leader-follower relationships
  - Inability to handle certain environmental configurations: may indicate limitations in ITB's state encoding
  - High variance in training: may indicate instability in advantage estimation or learning rate issues
