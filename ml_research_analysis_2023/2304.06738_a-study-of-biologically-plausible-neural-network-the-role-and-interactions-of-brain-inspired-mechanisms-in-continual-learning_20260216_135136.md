# A Study of Biologically Plausible Neural Network: The Role and Interactions of Brain-Inspired Mechanisms in Continual Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06738
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06738
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Biologically plausible framework incorporating excitatory-inhibitory neuron separation, dendritic computation, sparse representations, Hebbian learning, synaptic consolidation, and experience replay effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting in continual learning benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the role of biologically inspired mechanisms in continual learning for artificial neural networks. The authors propose a framework that incorporates Dale's principle with separate excitatory and inhibitory neuron populations, dendritic-like structures for context-dependent processing, sparse non-overlapping representations, Hebbian learning, synaptic consolidation, and experience replay. The framework is evaluated on Rot-MNIST, Perm-MNIST, and Seq-MNIST benchmarks, showing that these mechanisms effectively mitigate catastrophic forgetting while outperforming standard neural networks in challenging continual learning settings.

## Method Summary
The method combines several biologically plausible mechanisms within a two-layer MLP architecture. Dendritic segments receive context signals and modulate feedforward activity through k-winner-take-all (k-WTA) activations with 5% sparsity. Dale's principle is implemented with separate excitatory and inhibitory neuron populations, while Heterogeneous Dropout encourages task-specific subnetworks. Hebbian learning strengthens context-dendrite associations using Oja's rule, and synaptic consolidation protects important weights. Experience replay with reservoir sampling provides additional stability. The model is trained using SGD with learning rate 0.3, batch size 128, and 3 epochs per task.

## Key Results
- The biologically plausible framework effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting across multiple continual learning benchmarks.
- Performance matches or exceeds standard neural networks while maintaining biological plausibility.
- Sparse non-overlapping representations via k-WTA and Heterogeneous Dropout are critical for reducing interference between tasks.
- Experience replay provides additional benefits beyond biological mechanisms alone.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Incorporating Dale's principle with excitatory-inhibitory neuron separation reduces catastrophic forgetting in continual learning.
- Mechanism: Separate populations of excitatory and inhibitory neurons allow inhibitory units to selectively suppress certain excitatory units, facilitating the formation of task-specific subnetworks.
- Core assumption: The distinct connectivity patterns of excitatory and inhibitory neurons provide functional advantages in pattern learning and alleviating interference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "separate populations of exclusively excitatory and inhibitory neurons that adhere to Dale's principle"
  - [section] "Cornford et al. (2020) incorporate Dale's principle into ANNs... perform comparable to standard ANNs"
  - [corpus] "Counter-Current Learning: A Biologically Plausible Dual Network Approach" - supports biological plausibility of dual populations
- Break condition: If the learning rate disparity between excitatory and inhibitory weights is not maintained, the inhibitory suppression becomes ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Sparse, non-overlapping representations via k-WTA activations and Heterogeneous Dropout reduce interference between tasks.
- Mechanism: k-WTA enforces activation sparsity, while Heterogeneous Dropout encourages different neurons to be active for different tasks by dropping neurons inversely proportional to their activation counts.
- Core assumption: Sparse non-overlapping representations can reduce interference between tasks while allowing reusability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "sparse non-overlapping representations... can effectively control the overlap in representations"
  - [section] "Table 2 shows that sparsity plays a critical role in enabling CL in DNNs. Sparsity in activations effectively reduces interference by reducing the overlap in representations."
  - [corpus] "Brain-inspired learning in artificial neural networks: A review" - mentions sparsity benefits in biological networks
- Break condition: If dropout strength ρ is too low, representations remain overlapping; if too high, forward transfer is impaired.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hebbian updates strengthen context-dendrite associations, enhancing context-dependent gating.
- Mechanism: Following each supervised update, a Hebbian update step strengthens the connection between the context vector and the winning dendritic segment using Oja's rule.
- Core assumption: "Fire together, wire together" learning in the brain can be mimicked to strengthen context-specific processing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Mimicking the ubiquitous 'fire together, wire together' learning rule in the brain"
  - [section] "Figure 4 shows that the Hebbian update step increases the magnitude of the modulating signal from the dendrites"
  - [corpus] "Toward Practical Equilibrium Propagation: Brain-inspired Recurrent Neural Network" - discusses biologically plausible learning rules
- Break condition: If Hebbian learning rate is too high, it overwhelms error-based learning; if too low, context associations don't strengthen sufficiently.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input → Dendritic segments (context modulation) → k-WTA activation → Heterogeneous Dropout → Excitatory-inhibitory layers → Output
  Memory buffer for experience replay with consistency loss
  Context vector generation (average input image per task)

- Critical path: Dendritic context modulation → Sparse activation → Dropout enforcement → Inhibitory suppression → Synaptic consolidation → Replay integration

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sparsity level (k in k-WTA) vs forward transfer: higher sparsity reduces interference but may limit feature reuse
  - Dropout strength ρ vs modularity: higher ρ enforces more task-specific subnetworks but reduces transfer
  - Learning rate disparity for inhibitory weights: balances inhibition effectiveness vs stability

- Failure signatures:
  - Catastrophic forgetting persists: likely insufficient inhibitory suppression or inadequate synaptic consolidation
  - Poor forward transfer: sparsity/dropout too aggressive or context signal quality poor
  - Training instability: learning rates for Wie/Wei not properly adjusted

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement Dale's principle with balanced excitatory-inhibitory populations on Rot-MNIST; verify performance matches standard ANN baseline
  2. Add dendritic context modulation with k-WTA (k=5%); measure impact on forgetting in Perm-MNIST
  3. Integrate Heterogeneous Dropout with varying ρ; analyze activation overlap between tasks using KL divergence

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal way to encode context signals for continual learning tasks with varying degrees of similarity, beyond simple averaging of task samples?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that their context signal approach (averaging task samples) works well for Perm-MNIST but may not be effective for Rot-MNIST or natural images.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores one simple context signal method and does not investigate more sophisticated approaches that could capture task similarities and class relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing different context signal encoding methods (e.g., learned embeddings, prototype-based representations) on various CL benchmarks, showing improvements in performance and biological plausibility.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How would the biologically plausible mechanisms perform in more complex continual learning scenarios, such as online learning or reinforcement learning settings?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The experiments are limited to supervised learning on image classification tasks, which may not fully capture the challenges of more dynamic learning environments.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the applicability of their framework to other CL scenarios beyond the tested image classification tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending the biologically plausible framework to online learning and reinforcement learning settings, evaluating performance and biological plausibility in these more complex scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of different learning rules, beyond backpropagation, on the interactions between the biologically plausible mechanisms in continual learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that backpropagation is biologically implausible and suggest exploring more biologically plausible learning rules as a future direction.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses backpropagation throughout, which may not accurately reflect how the brain learns and could limit the effectiveness of the biological mechanisms.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing alternative learning rules (e.g., local learning rules, predictive coding) within the biologically plausible framework and comparing their performance and biological plausibility to backpropagation-based methods.

## Limitations

- The exact implementation details for dendritic context vector computation remain underspecified.
- Optimal sparsity levels appear task-dependent, with 5% sparsity potentially suboptimal for certain benchmarks.
- The study focuses on feedforward architectures, leaving questions about performance in recurrent or transformer-based models.
- Several key hyperparameter values (experience replay parameters α, β and synaptic consolidation λ values) are not fully specified across experiments.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The biological plausibility framework with Dale's principle and dendritic context modulation effectively reduces catastrophic forgetting, supported by consistent performance improvements across multiple benchmarks.
- **Medium confidence**: Sparse non-overlapping representations via k-WTA and Heterogeneous Dropout are critical for continual learning, though the optimal sparsity level appears task-dependent.
- **Medium confidence**: Experience replay provides additional benefits beyond biological mechanisms alone, but the relative contribution of replay versus biological mechanisms remains unclear.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study of biological mechanisms**: Remove each mechanism (dendritic computation, Dale's principle, Hebbian learning) individually to quantify their individual contributions to overall performance.

2. **Task-dependent sparsity optimization**: Systematically vary k in k-WTA across different CL benchmarks to identify optimal sparsity levels for different task types.

3. **Replay mechanism isolation**: Compare performance with biological mechanisms alone versus biological mechanisms + experience replay to quantify replay's marginal benefit in this framework.
