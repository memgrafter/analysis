# ComedicSpeech: Text To Speech For Stand-up Comedies in Low-Resource Scenarios

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12200
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12200
- Reference count: 0
- One-line primary result: Synthesizes expressive stand-up comedy speech in low-resource scenarios with 3.24 MOS score

## Executive Summary
ComedicSpeech addresses the challenge of synthesizing expressive stand-up comedy speech in low-resource scenarios by modeling personal prosody, rhythm, and fillers. The system introduces three key innovations: a prosody encoder with conditional layer normalization to capture personal expressiveness, a conditional duration predictor to model personal rhythm, and special tokens to represent comedian-specific fillers. Trained on a newly constructed Mandarin stand-up comedy dataset with only ~10 minutes of audio per speaker, the system achieves high voice quality and expressiveness scores.

## Method Summary
ComedicSpeech is a TTS system that builds upon FastSpeech 2 with three main innovations for stand-up comedy synthesis. First, it extracts prosody representation using a prosody encoder and integrates it into the model via conditional layer normalization in both encoder and decoder. Second, it enhances personal rhythm modeling through a speaker-dependent duration predictor that uses conditional layer normalization. Third, it models personal fillers by replacing them with special tokens in the phoneme set, allowing the model to learn speaker-dependent pronunciations. The system is trained on a Mandarin stand-up comedy dataset and pre-trained on AISHELL-3.

## Key Results
- Achieves 3.24 MOS score for overall voice quality in stand-up comedy synthesis
- Obtains 2.90 SMOS score for voice similarity to target speakers
- Scores 2.80 on Style MOS for expressiveness and personal style preservation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Conditional layer normalization (CLN) in the encoder and decoder flexibly integrates extracted personal prosody into the TTS model.
- Mechanism: The prosody encoder produces a weighted combination of shared prosody embeddings conditioned on reference speech. This prosody representation is then used as input to CLN, which dynamically adjusts the scale and bias of FFT blocks in the encoder and decoder, allowing the model to adapt to different input audio.
- Core assumption: The prosody representation can effectively capture and represent the diverse personal prosody of different comedians, and CLN can successfully integrate this information into the model.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prosody encoder -> Conditional duration predictor -> Special tokens -> TTS backbone (FastSpeech 2)

- Critical path:
  1. Extract personal prosody representation from reference speech using the prosody encoder.
  2. Condition the TTS model on the personal prosody representation using CLN in the encoder and decoder.
  3. Predict speaker-dependent phoneme durations using the conditional duration predictor.
  4. Synthesize speech using the conditioned TTS backbone, incorporating the predicted durations.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Prosody encoding: Using a prosody encoder with GST-like architecture allows for flexible and expressive prosody modeling, but it may require more computational resources compared to simpler approaches.
  - Duration prediction: The conditional duration predictor enables accurate modeling of personal rhythm, but it may be sensitive to the quality and diversity of the training data.
  - Special tokens: Introducing special tokens for personal fillers helps capture speaker-dependent pronunciations, but it may require careful handling of the phoneme set and potential conflicts with existing tokens.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor expressiveness: If the synthesized speech lacks the desired expressiveness or fails to capture the nuances of personal prosody and rhythm, it may indicate issues with the prosody encoding or duration prediction.
  - Incorrect filler pronunciation: If the personal fillers are not pronounced correctly or do not match the speaker's style, it may suggest problems with the special token representation or the model's ability to learn speaker-dependent pronunciations.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study on prosody encoding: Remove the prosody encoder and compare the synthesized speech quality and expressiveness to the full model.
  2. Ablation study on conditional duration prediction: Replace the conditional duration predictor with a standard duration predictor and evaluate the impact on personal rhythm modeling.
  3. Ablation study on special tokens: Replace the special tokens with ordinary phoneme sequences and assess the effect on the model's ability to capture speaker-dependent filler pronunciations.

## Limitations
- The relatively small training dataset (~10 minutes per speaker) may not capture the full diversity of stand-up comedy speech patterns
- Limited evaluation on cross-style generalization across different comedy subgenres and languages
- Subjective evaluation metrics (MOS, SMOS, Style MOS) may be influenced by factors unrelated to the proposed technical contributions

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The general architecture and approach of using prosody encoding, conditional duration prediction, and special tokens is sound and technically feasible. The reported MOS scores (3.24 overall) and improvements over baselines are consistent with the described methodology.

- **Medium Confidence**: The specific implementation details of the conditional layer normalization and its integration with the prosody encoder are plausible but lack sufficient technical depth for full verification. The effectiveness of special tokens for personal fillers is supported by the results but could benefit from more detailed analysis.

- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of results across different stand-up comedy styles and languages is unclear due to the limited dataset and single-language focus. The relative importance of each proposed component (prosody encoder, duration predictor, special tokens) to the overall performance is not rigorously established through ablation studies.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study on Component Importance**: Systematically remove each proposed component (prosody encoder, conditional duration predictor, special tokens) and measure the impact on MOS, SMOS, and Style MOS to quantify the individual contribution of each mechanism.

2. **Cross-Style Generalization Test**: Evaluate the system on stand-up comedy samples from different subgenres (observational, character-driven, storytelling) not present in the training data to assess how well the prosody and rhythm modeling generalizes across comedy styles.

3. **Statistical Significance Analysis**: Conduct statistical significance testing on the MOS and SMOS scores using appropriate sample sizes and confidence intervals to verify that the reported improvements over baselines are statistically significant rather than due to random variation.
