# Trained on 100 million words and still in shape: BERT meets British National Corpus

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09859
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09859
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Language models trained on 100M carefully curated words (BNC) outperform BERT trained on 3B+ words on GLUE, SuperGLUE, edge probing, and BLiMP benchmarks

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that language models can achieve superior performance when trained on a small, well-curated corpus rather than massive web crawls. By pre-training on the British National Corpus (100M words) with an optimized LTG-BERT architecture and span masking, they achieve better downstream performance than the original BERT model. The results challenge the assumption that larger training data always leads to better language models and highlight the importance of corpus quality and architectural choices in language model pre-training.

## Method Summary
The paper pre-trains a modified BERT architecture (LTG-BERT) on the British National Corpus (BNC) using masked language modeling with span-based masking. The model employs NormFormer architecture with pre-norm normalization, GEGLU activation functions, and disentangled attention mechanisms. The BNC corpus was preprocessed into Markdown format and tokenized using WordPiece with a 16,384 token vocabulary. Training used the LAMB optimizer with cosine learning rate decay and weight decay of 0.1. The model was evaluated on GLUE, SuperGLUE, edge probing, and BLiMP benchmarks, comparing performance against BERT trained on Wikipedia and BookCorpus.

## Key Results
- LTG-BERT trained on 100M words from BNC outperforms BERT-base trained on 3B+ words from Wikipedia + BookCorpus on GLUE, SuperGLUE, edge probing, and BLiMP benchmarks
- Span masking shows marginal improvements over subword masking on GLUE/SuperGLUE benchmarks
- Next sentence prediction objective is shown to be unnecessary for language modeling performance
- Training for half the original steps provides comparable performance, suggesting computational efficiency gains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: 100M carefully curated words can provide competitive linguistic knowledge compared to 3B+ web-crawled words
- Mechanism: Quality and balance of corpus enables better representation of linguistic phenomena than sheer quantity of noisy data
- Core assumption: British National Corpus's curated selection of diverse, balanced British English text captures sufficient linguistic variety to train competent language models
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We show that pre-training on this carefully curated corpus can reach better performance than the original BERT model"
  - [section]: "The British National Corpus is arguably more diverse and informative than a random subset of a web crawl"
  - [corpus]: The BNC contains newspapers, journals, books, letters, essays, informal conversations, business meetings, radio shows, and phone calls - covering most aspects of 20th century British English
- Break condition: If the curated corpus fails to represent important linguistic phenomena or domains needed for downstream tasks, or if the model architecture cannot extract sufficient information from the limited data

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Span masking is more effective than subword or whole-word masking for language modeling
- Mechanism: Masking spans forces the model to build longer-range non-local dependencies by making it harder to predict masked tokens using only local context
- Core assumption: The model can effectively learn to use context across masked spans rather than relying on local cues
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "SpanBERT (Joshi et al., 2020) iteratively samples random spans until 15% of subwords are masked... The original subword masking strategy is still a competitive baseline and it might be preferred in practice due to its simple implementation"
  - [section]: "Span-based masking performs marginally better than the other methods – it shows a clear improvement on (Super)GLUE benchmarks over the simple subword masking"
  - [corpus]: The masking strategy works with any text corpus, but span masking may be particularly effective with BNC's varied sentence structures
- Break condition: If the span sampling probability p=1/3 doesn't create useful masking patterns for the specific corpus, or if the model cannot effectively utilize longer-range context

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: NormFormer architecture with GEGLU activation and disentangled attention improves language modeling performance
- Mechanism: These architectural changes stabilize training and increase model expressiveness without significantly increasing parameters
- Core assumption: The combination of pre-norm normalization, GEGLU activation, and disentangled attention provides measurable benefits for masked language modeling
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "NormFormer. Pre-norm variation of the Transformer has been shown to lead to more stable convergence... We decided to use their so-called NormFormer architecture to stabilize the training"
  - [section]: "Preliminary experiments with this model showed that it tends to unpredictably diverge in the later stages of training... Accordingly, we follow some of the recent improvements of Transformer"
  - [corpus]: The architecture improvements are independent of corpus choice but may be particularly beneficial when training on limited data
- Break condition: If the additional complexity doesn't translate to improved downstream performance, or if the training becomes unstable despite the architectural changes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal vocabulary size for language models trained on small, curated corpora?
- Basis in paper: The authors use a vocabulary size of 16,384 tokens, noting that "we roughly follow Gowda and May (2020) and '... use the largest possible BPE vocabulary such that at least 95% of classes have 100 or more examples in training.'" They acknowledge that "we have not evaluated how it aligns with language modeling."
- Why unresolved: The authors did not systematically test different vocabulary sizes to determine their impact on performance for small corpora.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing models with varying vocabulary sizes (e.g., 8k, 16k, 32k, 64k) on the same small corpus would reveal the optimal balance between vocabulary size and downstream task performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of a pre-training corpus affect language model performance compared to its size?
- Basis in paper: The authors state "We believe this aspect is vital for the future research of effective and reliable language models" and compare their BNC model to one trained on a random 100M subset of Wikipedia and BookCorpus, finding "better quality leads to a noticeable difference in downstream performance."
- Why unresolved: While the authors demonstrate that a curated corpus (BNC) outperforms a random corpus of the same size, they don't quantify the relative importance of corpus quality versus size.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying both corpus quality (e.g., curated vs. random) and size (e.g., 10M, 50M, 100M, 500M words) would reveal the relative contributions of these factors to downstream performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How many training steps are truly necessary for effective language modeling on small corpora?
- Basis in paper: The authors test training for 1/4, 1/2, and 2x the original steps, finding that "training for half the time turns out to be enough to get comparable performance" but "decreasing the training steps further starts to degrade the downstream results too much." They conclude that "even with 1/4 steps, every token in BNC is seen about roughly 250 times during training."
- Why unresolved: The authors only test a limited range of training steps (0.25x to 2x) and don't explore whether even fewer steps could be sufficient with optimization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training for progressively fewer steps (e.g., 0.1x, 0.05x, 0.01x) while monitoring performance on downstream tasks would reveal the minimum number of steps required for effective language modeling on small corpora.

## Limitations

- The claim that curated corpora can universally match large web crawls depends heavily on the specific characteristics of the BNC corpus and may not generalize to other curated datasets
- The span masking improvements are marginal, and the paper doesn't provide mechanistic evidence for why it would be superior for learning longer-range dependencies
- The architectural improvements (NormFormer, GEGLU, disentangled attention) were introduced to address training instability but their individual contributions are not isolated through ablation studies

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The experimental results demonstrating that LTG-BERT trained on BNC outperforms BERT-base on the specific benchmarks tested (GLUE, SuperGLUE, edge probing, BLiMP). The methodology is sound, the code is available, and the results are reproducible.

**Medium Confidence**: The generalization claim that carefully curated 100M-word corpora can universally provide competitive linguistic knowledge compared to large web crawls. This depends on corpus selection criteria that are not fully specified or tested across multiple curated datasets.

**Medium Confidence**: The architectural improvements (NormFormer, GEGLU, disentangled attention) providing consistent benefits. While the paper shows these modifications helped stabilize training, the contribution of each individual component is not isolated through ablation studies.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that span masking fundamentally forces longer-range dependency learning in a way that subword masking cannot. The paper shows marginal performance improvements but does not provide mechanistic evidence for why span masking would be superior for this specific purpose.

## Next Checks

1. **Corpus Coverage Validation**: Systematically compare BNC's linguistic coverage against multiple 100M-word subsets from Common Crawl, including both random subsets and carefully selected domain-balanced subsets. This would test whether BNC's curation specifically, rather than corpus size alone, drives the performance gains.

2. **Architectural Ablation Study**: Conduct controlled experiments isolating the contribution of each NormFormer modification (pre-norm vs post-norm, GEGLU vs ReLU, disentangled attention vs standard attention) to determine which components are essential versus which are beneficial but not critical for the 100M-word training regime.

3. **Cross-Domain Generalization Test**: Evaluate LTG-BERT on American English benchmarks and non-news domain tasks (scientific writing, social media, technical documentation) to assess whether the British English focus of BNC creates domain-specific limitations, and compare this against BERT trained on more diverse web data.
