# Exploring the Impact of Instruction Data Scaling on Large Language Models: An Empirical Study on Real-World Use Cases

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14742
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14742
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: Instruction data scaling improves performance differently across task types, with translation and generation benefiting from smaller datasets while reasoning tasks show limited improvement even with more data

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how instruction data scaling affects large language model performance across 12 real-world Chinese use cases. The authors fine-tuned Bloomz-7B1-mt with datasets of 0.2M, 0.6M, 1M, and 2M examples to create BELLE models, then evaluated them using ChatGPT. Results show task-specific scaling patterns: translation, generation, and brainstorming benefit from small datasets (200K-2M), while extraction and classification improve continuously with more data. However, math, code, and chain-of-thought tasks show poor performance regardless of data size, suggesting architectural or training limitations. The findings highlight the need for task-specific approaches to instruction tuning.

## Method Summary
The study used Bloomz-7B1-mt as a base model and fine-tuned it on Chinese instruction datasets of varying sizes (0.2M, 0.6M, 1M, 2M) across 12 task types. Models were trained with 64 batch size, 2 epochs, constant learning rate 3e-6, and weight decay 0.001. ChatGPT served as the evaluator, scoring model outputs on a 0-1 scale for each task. The evaluation included three responses per instruction, with average scores used as the primary metric. This controlled scaling approach allowed comparison of performance across different task categories as data quantity increased.

## Key Results
- Translation, rewrite, generation, and brainstorming tasks achieve satisfactory performance with just 200K-2M training examples
- Extraction, classification, closed QA, and summarization tasks show continuous improvement with increased data size but with diminishing returns
- Math, code, and chain-of-thought tasks exhibit poor performance even with more data, indicating fundamental limitations in current instruction-tuning approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Increasing instruction data size improves model performance for tasks requiring factual recall and generalization
- Mechanism: More diverse instruction examples enable the model to better generalize to unseen instructions by expanding its knowledge base and learning patterns across different task types
- Core assumption: The base model has sufficient capacity to leverage additional training data effectively
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "merely increasing the amount of instruction data leads to continuous improvement in tasks such as open-ended generation"
  - [section] "For Open QA, the model's performance is continuously improved as the amount of data increases. Solving this task requires parametric knowledge of the model"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Tasks requiring reasoning and logical deduction show limited improvement with increased data size
- Mechanism: These tasks demand specific cognitive abilities (like chain-of-thought reasoning) that cannot be acquired through simple pattern matching from more examples
- Core assumption: The base model's reasoning capabilities are fixed and cannot be significantly enhanced through instruction tuning alone
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "in tasks such as math and code, the model performance curve remains quite flat while increasing data size"
  - [section] "The model's performance is still poor for math and code, and increasing data size no longer brings about performance improvement"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Translation tasks benefit significantly from even small amounts of instruction data due to transfer learning from multilingual pretraining
- Mechanism: The base model (Bloomz-7b1-mt) already has multilingual capabilities from pretraining, so instruction tuning only needs to adapt these existing capabilities to instruction-following format
- Core assumption: The base model has already learned relevant cross-lingual representations during pretraining
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In the translation task, Belle-7b-0.2m has achieved good performance, indicating that the model's translation ability may come from the multilingual ability of Bloomz-7b1-mt"
  - [section] "For translation, rewrite, generation, and brainstorming tasks, a dataset of 2 million samples, or even less, can enable the model to achieve satisfactory performance"

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal instruction data size for math and code tasks that would significantly improve model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that for math, code, and COT tasks, performance remains poor even with more data, suggesting the need for better training data quality, larger base models, or specialized training methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper indicates that simply increasing data size does not improve performance for these tasks, but does not provide specific data sizes or strategies that would be effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying data sizes and quality for math and code tasks, and evaluating the performance to determine the optimal data size and quality needed for significant improvement.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of training data impact the performance of instruction-tuned models across different task types?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests that the quality of training data is a potential reason for the poor performance in math, code, and COT tasks, indicating that data quality is a crucial factor.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how different qualities of training data affect model performance across various tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing experiments to compare model performance using training data of varying quality for different task types, and analyzing the impact on model performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of base model size on the performance of instruction-tuned models for tasks requiring reasoning abilities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that the model size may not be large enough to achieve the emergence of abilities for math, code, and COT tasks, indicating that base model size is a potential factor.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses a single base model size (Bloomz-7B1-mt) and does not explore the impact of different base model sizes on performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating models with different base model sizes for tasks requiring reasoning abilities, and comparing their performance to determine the impact of base model size.

## Limitations
- Limited task diversity (12 Chinese use cases) may not generalize to other languages or cultural contexts
- Exclusive use of ChatGPT as evaluator may introduce evaluator-specific bias in performance assessment
- Fixed base model capacity (7B parameters) constrains observed performance ceilings and may not reflect larger model behaviors

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** Translation tasks benefit from small amounts of instruction data due to multilingual pretraining transfer
- **Medium confidence:** Performance improves continuously for extraction, classification, closed QA, and summarization with increased data, but with diminishing returns
- **Low confidence:** Math, code, and chain-of-thought tasks will remain poor even with more data, requiring different training approaches

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the study using a different evaluator (e.g., human annotators or multiple LLM evaluators) to verify the robustness of the performance rankings across task types
2. Test the scaling patterns on a larger base model (e.g., 13B or 70B parameters) to determine if the observed performance ceilings are due to model capacity limitations rather than task difficulty
3. Conduct ablation studies on the instruction data quality by systematically varying data diversity and complexity within each task category to isolate the impact of data quality versus quantity
