# Plug-and-Play split Gibbs sampler: embedding deep generative priors in Bayesian inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11134
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11134
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: PnP-SGS outperforms state-of-the-art optimization and sampling methods, with PSNR improvements of up to 6 dB and significant gains in perceptual metrics like FID and LPIPS

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a stochastic plug-and-play (PnP) sampling algorithm called PnP-SGS that leverages variable splitting to efficiently sample from a posterior distribution. The method draws inspiration from the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) and divides the task of posterior sampling into two simpler problems: one depending on the likelihood function and the other interpreted as a Bayesian denoising problem. The proposed approach is implemented using state-of-the-art diffusion-based generative models as denoisers. Experiments on image processing problems show that PnP-SGS outperforms state-of-the-art optimization and sampling methods, with PSNR improvements of up to 6 dB and significant gains in perceptual metrics like FID and LPIPS. The method also provides uncertainty quantification through credibility intervals, which is not possible with deterministic optimization methods.

## Method Summary
PnP-SGS employs a variable splitting strategy inspired by ADMM to decompose the posterior sampling problem into two subproblems. The first subproblem involves sampling from a likelihood-based distribution, while the second is interpreted as a Bayesian denoising problem solved using a diffusion-based generative model. The algorithm iteratively alternates between these subproblems, with the denoiser providing high-quality samples that guide the sampling process. This stochastic approach allows for efficient exploration of the posterior distribution while leveraging the strong generative priors learned by diffusion models. The method is particularly effective for ill-posed inverse problems in imaging, where traditional optimization methods struggle to recover high-quality solutions.

## Key Results
- PnP-SGS achieves PSNR improvements of up to 6 dB compared to state-of-the-art optimization and sampling methods
- Significant gains in perceptual metrics, including FID and LPIPS, demonstrating superior visual quality of reconstructed images
- Provides uncertainty quantification through credibility intervals, enabling probabilistic interpretation of solutions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PnP-SGS works by exploiting the structure of the posterior distribution through variable splitting. By separating the likelihood and prior components, the algorithm can efficiently sample from each part using specialized methods. The diffusion-based denoiser serves as a powerful prior, guiding the sampling process toward high-probability regions of the posterior. This approach combines the strengths of optimization-based methods (fast convergence) with the flexibility of sampling methods (uncertainty quantification and exploration of multiple modes).

- Critical path: Denoiser output guides likelihood sampling, which updates the current estimate
- Design tradeoffs: Balancing denoiser quality vs. computational efficiency; choosing appropriate noise schedules
- Failure signatures: Mode collapse in the denoiser; poor generalization to out-of-distribution inputs; sensitivity to hyperparameter choices

## Limitations
- Relies heavily on the quality of the diffusion denoiser, making it sensitive to denoiser-specific failure modes such as mode collapse or poor generalization to out-of-distribution inputs
- Theoretical analysis assumes bounded gradients of the log-likelihood, which may not hold for all practical applications
- Current implementation requires tuning of hyperparameters such as the penalty parameter and noise schedule, which can be problem-specific and computationally expensive

## Confidence
- High confidence: Empirical results showing improved PSNR and perceptual metrics (FID, LPIPS) over competing methods; ability to provide uncertainty quantification
- Medium confidence: Theoretical convergence guarantees, which rely on strong assumptions that may not always hold in practice
- Low confidence: Claims about computational efficiency and scalability to large-scale problems, as the current evaluation focuses on relatively small image processing tasks
