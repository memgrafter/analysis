# Conveying the Predicted Future to Users: A Case Study of Story Plot Prediction

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.09122
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09122
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Key outcome**: GPT-2 variant enhanced with semantic frame representations (FGPT-2) outperforms baselines in story plot prediction consistency and storiability, with preliminary evidence of positive influence on user writing

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of creative writing assistance through automated story plot prediction. The authors propose a novel approach where novels are processed as sequences of fixed-size story blocks, with the goal of generating concise three-sentence summaries describing the next story block. Three models are evaluated: Fusion-based Seq2Seq, Plan-and-Write, and an enhanced GPT-2 variant incorporating semantic frame representations (FGPT-2). Human evaluations conducted via Amazon Mechanical Turk demonstrate that FGPT-2 produces the most consistent and storiable outputs, even surpassing human-written plots in certain evaluation metrics. A preliminary writing task study suggests that FGPT-2 positively influences participants' story continuations, supporting the "inspiration-through-observation" paradigm in creative writing assistance.

## Method Summary
The research treats novel writing as a sequence prediction problem where each novel is divided into fixed-size story blocks. The system generates three-sentence summaries for the next story block, providing writers with plot predictions. Three distinct models are adapted for this task: Fusion-based Seq2Seq (combining different input representations), Plan-and-Write (which plans the story structure before generation), and a GPT-2 variant enhanced with semantic frame representations (FGPT-2). The FGPT-2 model incorporates semantic frames to better capture narrative elements and relationships. Evaluation involves both human assessments through Amazon Mechanical Turk workers and a controlled writing task study with five participants per condition, examining how model outputs influence user-generated story continuations.

## Key Results
- FGPT-2 achieved the highest consistency and storiability scores in human evaluations among all tested models
- FGPT-2 outputs outperformed some human-written plot summaries in specific evaluation dimensions
- Preliminary writing task study (n=5 per condition) indicated FGPT-2 had positive influence on user story continuations despite being less favored by users compared to other baselines

## Limitations
- Human evaluation methodology relied on single annotator per instance without inter-rater reliability verification, limiting confidence in subjective judgments
- Writing task study had very small sample sizes (5 participants per condition), making results underpowered and limiting generalizability
- Evaluation framework assumes specific creative writing preferences for minimal, plot-focused suggestions rather than more elaborate or stylistically aligned continuations
- The study focuses on a specific format of three-sentence summaries, which may not align with all writers' needs or preferences
- Limited discussion of potential biases in the dataset or model outputs, which could impact the generalizability of results

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: FGPT-2 demonstrates technical improvements in consistency and storiability metrics compared to baselines
- **Medium confidence**: FGPT-2 outperforms human-written plots in certain evaluation dimensions, given AMT workers' single-annotator setup
- **Low confidence**: Claims about FGPT-2's positive influence on user writing are based on exploratory analysis with n=5 per group
