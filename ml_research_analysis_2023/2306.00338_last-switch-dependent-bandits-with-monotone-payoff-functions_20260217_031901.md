# Last Switch Dependent Bandits with Monotone Payoff Functions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00338
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00338
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Constant-factor approximation algorithm for LSD bandits with monotone payoffs, achieving (1-ε)(1-k/e^k·k!) guarantee

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the last switch dependent (LSD) bandit problem, where arm rewards depend on the time elapsed since the arm was last switched. The authors provide the first constant-factor approximation algorithm for this planning problem under monotone payoff assumptions. Their approach uses a novel LP relaxation based on "recurrent intervals" - minimal state-action sequences that start and end at the same state - combined with a randomized rounding procedure employing "mirroring virtual evolutions." The work also presents an online learning variant with sublinear regret, addressing a gap in existing restless bandit literature.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a constant-factor approximation algorithm for LSD bandits by first formulating a higher-dimensional LP relaxation using recurrent intervals as decision variables. The relaxation captures the temporal dependencies between actions and states through constraints that enforce valid schedules. They then design a randomized rounding procedure that constructs feasible schedules by mirroring virtual evolutions - essentially reflecting state trajectories to ensure consistency. The algorithm achieves a (1-ε)(1-k/e^k·k!) approximation ratio, which nearly matches state-of-the-art results for the special case of recharging bandits. For the online setting, they provide a sublinear regret algorithm based on an Explore-then-Commit strategy adapted to the time-correlated nature of the problem.

## Key Results
- First constant-factor approximation algorithm for LSD bandits under monotone payoffs
- (1-ε)(1-k/e^k·k!) approximation guarantee for the planning problem
- Sublinear regret algorithm for online learning variant
- Novel LP relaxation framework using recurrent intervals
- "Mirroring virtual evolutions" technique for randomized rounding

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm works by relaxing the temporal dependencies in the LSD bandit problem through a higher-dimensional LP formulation. The key insight is that recurrent intervals - minimal state-action sequences that start and end at the same state - can be used as decision variables in the relaxation. This allows the algorithm to capture the essential structure of valid schedules while making the problem tractable. The randomized rounding procedure then constructs feasible schedules by carefully "mirroring" virtual state trajectories, ensuring that the temporal constraints are satisfied while preserving the approximation guarantee.

## Foundational Learning
- **LP Relaxation**: A technique for converting hard combinatorial problems into continuous optimization problems by relaxing integrality constraints; needed because exact LP formulations are intractable for LSD bandits
- **Recurrent Intervals**: Minimal state-action sequences that start and end at the same state; needed as the fundamental building blocks for the relaxation
- **Randomized Rounding**: A technique for converting fractional LP solutions into integral solutions while preserving approximation guarantees; needed to construct feasible schedules from the relaxation
- **Mirroring Virtual Evolutions**: A technique for ensuring temporal consistency in constructed schedules by reflecting state trajectories; needed to satisfy the complex temporal dependencies in LSD bandits
- **Explore-then-Commit**: An online learning strategy where the algorithm explores for a fixed period before committing to a strategy; needed for the online learning variant
- **Restless Bandits**: A generalization of multi-armed bandits where the state of each arm evolves independently of the actions taken; needed as the broader problem class that LSD bandits belong to

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
LP Relaxation (recurrent intervals) -> Randomized Rounding (mirroring) -> Feasibility Check -> Approximation Guarantee

**Critical Path**
The critical path is: solve LP relaxation → apply randomized rounding → verify feasibility → bound approximation ratio. Each step depends on the previous one, with the rounding procedure being the most complex component.

**Design Tradeoffs**
The main tradeoff is between relaxation accuracy and computational tractability. Using recurrent intervals allows a compact relaxation but requires careful handling of temporal dependencies. The mirroring technique trades implementation complexity for stronger approximation guarantees.

**Failure Signatures**
- LP solution has high integrality gap → relaxation too loose
- Randomized rounding produces infeasible schedules → mirroring logic flawed
- Approximation ratio worse than claimed → rounding analysis incorrect
- Online regret not sublinear → exploration period or commitment strategy flawed

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the monotonicity assumption on payoff functions be removed while still maintaining constant-factor approximation guarantees for LSD bandits?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors conjecture that without monotonicity, the problem may not accept polynomial-time constant approximations under standard complexity assumptions.
- Why unresolved: The authors state that proving this conjecture falls beyond the scope of their work.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof showing inapproximability results for LSD bandits without monotonicity, or an algorithm achieving constant-factor approximation without this assumption.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the algorithm be adapted to achieve order-optimal regret in the online learning setting?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that the LP-based and time-correlated nature of their algorithm significantly complicates its adaptation into a dynamic online learning policy, such as one based on UCB.
- Why unresolved: The current online learning adaptation is based on Explore-then-Commit with sublinear regret, but not order-optimal.
- What evidence would resolve it: A learning adaptation of the algorithm with order-optimal regret guarantees, or a lower bound showing that order-optimal regret is impossible for this problem.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the "mirroring" technique developed in this work be extended to tackle richer subclasses of restless bandits that satisfy some analogous form of monotonicity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors believe that the "mirroring" technique could be used for tackling even richer subclasses of restless bandits which satisfy some analogous form of monotonicity.
- Why unresolved: The authors only demonstrate the technique for the specific case of LSD bandits and do not explore its applicability to other restless bandit models.
- What evidence would resolve it: An extension of the mirroring technique to a richer subclass of restless bandits, along with a proof of approximation guarantees or sublinear regret for the online learning setting.

## Limitations
- Restricted to monotone payoff functions, which may not capture all practical scenarios
- Theoretical guarantees depend on specific structural properties that may not generalize
- Online learning variant achieves sublinear regret but not order-optimal
- Complexity of the mirroring technique may limit scalability to large instances

## Confidence

- Randomized rounding procedure: High
- Sublinear regret for online learning: Medium
- Applicability to non-monotone payoffs: Low (conjectured negative)
