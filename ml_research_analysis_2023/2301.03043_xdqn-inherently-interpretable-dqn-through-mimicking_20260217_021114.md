# XDQN: Inherently Interpretable DQN through Mimicking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03043
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03043
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: XDQN achieves similar performance to DQN while providing global and local explanations through parallel mimic learning

## Executive Summary
XDQN is an interpretable variation of Deep Q-Network (DQN) that trains a Gradient Boosting Regressor as a mimic learner in parallel with the DQN's Q-network. Unlike post-hoc explanation methods, XDQN provides explanations during training by using the mimic learner to generate target Q-values and to analyze feature contributions. The approach replaces the traditional target Q-network with an inherently interpretable model, enabling both global policy understanding and local decision explanations. Experimental results on air traffic management scenarios demonstrate that XDQN maintains comparable performance to standard DQN while adding interpretability.

## Method Summary
XDQN trains a deep neural network Q-network and a Gradient Boosting Regressor in parallel using an experience replay buffer. The mimic learner (Gradient Boosting Regressor) updates every 9 episodes using minibatches from a prioritized experience replay buffer limited to the last K steps (K=4,536,000). The hyperparameter K controls the trade-off between using recent training data versus accumulated experience. During training, the mimic learner generates target Q-values for updating the deep Q-network and simultaneously provides feature importance analysis for explanations. The system uses ε-greedy policy with ε=0.9 decaying to 0.04 over 1,600 episodes.

## Key Results
- XDQN achieves similar play performance to DQN on air traffic management scenarios
- The approach maintains high fidelity between mimic learner predictions and Q-network values (low MAE)
- XDQN provides both global explanations (policy understanding) and local explanations (individual decision reasoning)
- The method demonstrates effectiveness across three real-world ATM scenarios with 6,676-6,773 flights each

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: XDQN enables inherently interpretable DQN by training a mimic learner in parallel during online training.
- Mechanism: Replace the target Q-network with a Gradient Boosting Regressor that mimics the Q-network's behavior while learning, enabling explanations during training, not just post-training.
- Core assumption: The mimic learner can approximate the Q-network's predictions with high fidelity (low MAE) while remaining interpretable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "XDQN trains the interpretable mimic learner in parallel with the DQN's Q-network"
  - [section 3] "we propose eXplainable Deep Q-Network (XDQN), which is an explainable variation of DQN [22]"

- Break condition: If MAE between mimic learner and Q-network exceeds acceptable thresholds, training diverges and interpretability is lost.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The hyperparameter K controls the trade-off between experience training and active play in XDQN.
- Mechanism: K determines how many recent training steps' state-action pairs are used for mimic learning updates, balancing data recency against training stability.
- Core assumption: Using recent state-action pairs provides more accurate target values while avoiding excessive noise from rapidly changing Q-network parameters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3] "The hyperparameter 𝐾 manages the trade-off between experience training and active play in XDQN"
  - [section 4.4] "we set the memory capacity of the experience replay for the mimic learner, i.e. the hyperparameter 𝐾, equal to the 1/20 of the product of three other hyperparameters"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus papers discuss K hyperparameter in XDQN context
- Break condition: If K is too small, mimic training becomes unstable; if too large, mimic learner learns outdated Q-values.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: XDQN provides both global and local explainability through feature contribution analysis.
- Mechanism: Use Gradient Boosting Regressor's node residues to measure feature importance for individual decisions (local) and aggregate over time for policy understanding (global).
- Core assumption: Feature contributions measured through node residues accurately reflect the model's decision-making process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "we utilize a Gradient Boosting Regressor [36] as the mimic learner, so as to exploit its boosting ability to learn effectively"
  - [section 4.7] "we demonstrate XDQN global explainability by aggregating the importance of features on particular action selections"
  - [section 4.7] "we demonstrate the ability of the mimic learner to provide local explainability"

- Break condition: If feature importance metrics don't correlate with actual decision-making patterns, explanations become misleading.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: State → Online Q-network → Action selection → Environment feedback → Experience replay → Both networks update → Explanations generated

- Critical path: State → Online Q-network → Action selection → Environment feedback → Experience replay → Both networks update → Explanations generated

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Mimic learner complexity vs interpretability: More complex models may improve fidelity but reduce interpretability
  - Update frequency of target parameters: More frequent updates improve responsiveness but may destabilize training
  - K hyperparameter: Balances between recent data recency and training stability

- Failure signatures:
  - High MAE between mimic learner and Q-network predictions
  - Degraded play performance compared to standard DQN
  - Unstable training curves with large variance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the hyperparameter K affect the trade-off between experience training and active play in XDQN, and what is the optimal value for K in different scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the role of K in managing the trade-off between experience training and active play, stating "If K is large, the mimic model learns from state-action pairs that may have been collected through more suboptimal instances of θ; deploying however data-augmented versions of Q-value. On the other hand, if K is small, it learns from the most recent instances of θ; making use of up-to-date Q-values."
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a general discussion on the role of K and does not empirically determine the optimal value for different scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing the performance of XDQN with different values of K across various scenarios would help determine the optimal value.
