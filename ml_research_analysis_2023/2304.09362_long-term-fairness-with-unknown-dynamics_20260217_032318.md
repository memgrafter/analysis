# Long-Term Fairness with Unknown Dynamics

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09362
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09362
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RL algorithm L-UCBFair achieves sublinear bounds on regret and distortion while learning to balance short-term utility with long-term fairness

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a reinforcement learning formulation for long-term fairness, where an agent learns to balance immediate utility with driving a population towards more equitable equilibria. The authors develop L-UCBFair, a model-free RL algorithm with theoretical safety guarantees that adapts least-squares value iteration with Upper Confidence Bounds to handle unknown dynamics. The algorithm achieves sublinear bounds on regret (suboptimality of cumulative loss) and distortion (suboptimality of cumulative disparity) with high probability. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that both L-UCBFair and a deep RL variant (R-TD3) can learn to sacrifice short-term utility to drive populations towards states with higher long-term utility and fairness, outperforming a myopic baseline.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two algorithms for long-term fairness: L-UCBFair (theoretical) and R-TD3 (practical deep RL variant). L-UCBFair assumes a linear MDP structure and uses least-squares value iteration with UCB exploration to learn both reward and disparity functions while maintaining theoretical regret and distortion bounds. R-TD3 uses a Lagrangian relaxation approach with time-dependent regularization to balance short-term and long-term fairness objectives without requiring linearity assumptions. Both algorithms operate on population dynamics modeled via replicator dynamics, where actions are binary classification thresholds that affect group qualification rates over time.

## Key Results
- L-UCBFair achieves sublinear bounds on both regret and distortion with high probability under linear MDP assumptions
- R-TD3 can achieve similar qualitative behavior without restrictive assumptions by using time-dependent regularization
- Both algorithms outperform a myopic greedy baseline that optimizes only short-term utility
- The algorithms can drive populations towards more equitable equilibria by sacrificing short-term utility

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: L-UCBFair learns to drive population towards favorable equilibria by sacrificing short-term utility
- Mechanism: The algorithm uses an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) to encourage exploration of actions that may reduce immediate loss but improve long-term fairness metrics. By maintaining estimates of both reward (Q_r) and disparity (Q_g) functions, the algorithm can balance immediate gains against long-term fairness objectives.
- Core assumption: The system dynamics can be modeled as a linear MDP with unknown transition kernel P that can be learned online
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We prove that this algorithm achieves simultaneous probabilistic bounds on cumulative loss and cumulative violations of fairness"
  - [section 3.1.1] "L-UCBFair inherits from a family of algorithms that treat an episodic Markov decision process"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct comparison to similar UCB-based fairness approaches
- Break condition: If the linear MDP assumption fails or if the feature map φ cannot be learned effectively, the theoretical guarantees break down

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: R-TD3 can achieve similar long-term fairness outcomes without restrictive assumptions
- Mechanism: Uses a Lagrangian relaxation with time-dependent regularization to encourage short-term constraint violations while maintaining long-term fairness objectives. The algorithm optimizes a weighted combination of loss and disparity, with the weight on disparity increasing over time.
- Core assumption: Deep reinforcement learning can approximate the optimal policy for the long-term fairness objective without requiring linearity assumptions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "we utilize 'Twin-Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient' (TD3) with the implementation and default parameters provided by the open-source package 'Stable Baselines 3'"
  - [section 5.2] "R-TD3 achieves similar qualitative behavior (i.e., driving near-universal qualification at the expense of short-term utility)"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct comparison to other Lagrangian-based RL approaches for fairness
- Break condition: If the neural network approximation fails to capture the true value function or if the scheduling of regularization weights is suboptimal

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The adaptive search policy enables handling of continuous action spaces
- Mechanism: Instead of sampling directly from continuous actions, the policy represents a distribution over finitely many regions of the action space. Actions are sampled uniformly from Voronoi regions, allowing the algorithm to explore effectively while maintaining theoretical guarantees.
- Core assumption: The action space can be partitioned into a finite number of regions such that any action is close to some region center
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1.1] "Instead of drawing an action directly from a distribution over continuous values, π represents a distribution over finitely many, finite regions of A"
  - [section 3.1.1] "Theorem 3.5. If the number M of distinct loci or regions partitioning A is sufficiently large, there exists a set of loci I such that ∀a ∈ I_i, i ∈ M, ||a - I_i||_2 ≤ ε_I"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct comparison to other continuous action space handling methods
- Break condition: If M is too small to adequately cover the action space or if ε_I is too large to maintain policy performance

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Linear MDP assumption
  - Why needed here: Enables the use of least-squares value iteration with provable regret bounds
  - Quick check question: How does the linear MDP assumption allow us to represent the transition dynamics as ⟨φ(s,a), μ_h(s')⟩?

- Concept: Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) exploration
  - Why needed here: Provides optimism in the face of uncertainty, encouraging exploration of potentially beneficial but uncertain actions
  - Quick check question: Why does adding the bonus term β√(φ^T Λ^-1 h φ) to the Q-function estimate encourage exploration?

- Concept: Lagrangian relaxation for constrained optimization
  - Why needed here: Transforms the constrained optimization problem into an unconstrained one that can be solved with standard RL algorithms
  - Quick check question: How does the time-dependent regularization λ_τ = τ/H help balance short-term and long-term fairness objectives?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Population distribution parameters (qualification rates) → Policy selection → Binary classification thresholds → Environment transition → New qualification rates → Update estimates → Policy improvement
- Critical path: State → Policy selection → Action → Environment transition → New state → Update estimates → Policy improvement
- Design tradeoffs: Theoretical guarantees (L-UCBFair) vs. practical flexibility (R-TD3), discrete vs. continuous action spaces, exploration vs. exploitation
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on synthetic vs. real data, sensitivity to initial conditions, inability to learn meaningful feature maps
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test L-UCBFair on synthetic data with known dynamics to verify theoretical bounds
  2. Compare L-UCBFair vs. R-TD3 on synthetic data to understand tradeoff between guarantees and performance
  3. Evaluate both algorithms on real-world data to assess practical applicability and identify failure modes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper acknowledges several limitations and open questions:
- The linear MDP assumption may not hold in many real-world fairness scenarios
- The feature map φ is not fully specified, raising questions about appropriate representations for different fairness problems
- The empirical evaluation is limited to relatively simple synthetic and real-world datasets
- The generalizability to complex, high-dimensional fairness problems with many groups and non-linear dynamics remains unclear

## Limitations

- The theoretical guarantees of L-UCBFair rely heavily on the linear MDP assumption, which may not hold in many real-world fairness scenarios
- The R-TD3 variant sacrifices theoretical guarantees for flexibility, with no guarantees on finding optimal balance between short-term utility and long-term fairness
- The empirical evaluation is limited in scope, focusing on relatively simple synthetic and real-world datasets

## Confidence

**High confidence** in the core hypothesis that reinforcement learning can be used to seek desirable long-term fairness outcomes, supported by both theoretical analysis and empirical results across multiple datasets.

**Medium confidence** in the specific mechanisms proposed (L-UCBFair and R-TD3), as the theoretical guarantees are conditional on strong assumptions that may not hold in practice, and the empirical evaluation is limited in scope.

**Low confidence** in the generalizability of the results to complex, high-dimensional fairness problems with many groups and non-linear dynamics.

## Next Checks

1. **Assumption violation analysis**: Systematically evaluate L-UCBFair's performance when the linear MDP assumption is violated, testing on datasets with non-linear dynamics and non-linear feature representations.

2. **Scalability testing**: Extend the empirical evaluation to high-dimensional datasets with many groups and complex feature spaces, comparing the performance of L-UCBFair and R-TD3 on datasets like COMPAS and FairFace.

3. **Ablation study**: Conduct an ablation study on the R-TD3 algorithm, varying the time-dependent regularization schedule and the neural network architecture to understand their impact on long-term fairness outcomes.
