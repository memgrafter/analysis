# Scale-aware neural calibration for wide swath altimetry observations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04497
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04497
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: Neural network with scale-space decomposition achieves 1.4 cm residual error in SWOT altimetry calibration across scales 10-1000 km, outperforming UNet and matching operational methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of calibrating wide-swath altimetry observations from the SWOT mission, which are affected by instrument and geophysical errors. The proposed method uses a neural network architecture that combines a scale-space decomposition adapted to the SWOT swath geometry with a convolutional network. The scale-space decomposition helps to better separate the sea surface height signal from the error components. The neural network is trained in a supervised manner using calibrated nadir altimetry products and the raw SWOT observations. The results show that the proposed method achieves a residual error of approximately 1.4 cm across scales from 10 km to 1000 km, outperforming a standard UNet architecture and reaching performance comparable to state-of-the-art operational methods. The quality of the gridded altimetry product used as input significantly impacts the calibration performance, with better products leading to improved results.

## Method Summary
The method uses a neural network architecture that processes SWOT KaRIN observations alongside nadir-altimeter-derived gridded SSH products. The network applies a 1D Gaussian scale-space decomposition along the along-track direction (20 scales from 8-160 km), followed by batch normalization and residual convolutional blocks with swath-mixer layers that share information between the two sides of the non-contiguous SWOT swath. The model is trained using weighted MSE loss over 250 epochs with cyclical learning rate, and evaluated on held-out NATL60 simulation data using RMSE and gradient RMSE metrics.

## Key Results
- The proposed method achieves a residual error of approximately 1.4 cm across scales from 10 km to 1000 km
- Outperforms a standard UNet architecture by approximately 3% in RMSE
- Reaches performance comparable to state-of-the-art operational calibration methods
- Quality of nadir-altimetry-derived gridded products significantly impacts calibration performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The scale-space decomposition adapted to SWOT swath geometry enables better separation of SSH from error signals by isolating characteristic spatial scales.
- Mechanism: The method applies a one-dimensional Gaussian scale-space decomposition along the along-track direction, creating multiple scale components (20 scales from 8km to 160km) that are processed independently by the neural network. This decomposition exploits the different characteristic scales of SSH (dominant 10-200km) versus instrument errors (dominant large-scale) and geophysical errors.
- Core assumption: SSH and error signals have sufficiently distinct spectral characteristics that can be separated through scale decomposition.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It benefits from calibrated nadir altimetry products and a scale-space decomposition adapted to SWOT swath geometry and the structure of the different processes in play."
  - [section] "This supports both to consider a scale-space decomposition and to investigate a cross-calibration approach with the exploitation of nadir-altimeter-derived altimetry products"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on neural mapping and interpolation but don't specifically address scale-space decomposition for SWOT calibration.
- Break condition: If SSH and error signals have overlapping spectral characteristics at critical scales, or if the scale decomposition doesn't match the actual error characteristics, the separation will fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The swath-mixer layer enables effective information sharing between the two sides of the non-contiguous SWOT swath while preventing unwanted cross-contamination.
- Mechanism: The architecture processes each side of the swath separately through "Split Conv" layers (with padding to maintain dimensions), then uses a "Swath-mix" layer that transposes the across-track dimension as a channel dimension and applies convolution. This allows the network to learn correlations between error patterns on both sides of the swath without mixing SSH information inappropriately.
- Core assumption: Error signals are correlated between the two sides of the swath, but SSH signals should remain separated to avoid introducing artifacts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The swath observed from the KaRIN sensor is not contiguous in the across-track dimension. The observation errors are however clearly correlated between the two sides of the swath."
  - [section] "To exploit these correlations in our architecture, we design a swath-mixer block with two specific layers."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus papers about swath-mixer layers for SWOT data.
- Break condition: If error correlations between swath sides are weaker than assumed, or if the mixing layer introduces unwanted SSH artifacts, calibration performance will degrade.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using calibrated nadir-altimetry-derived gridded products as input provides essential large-scale SSH information that complements the SWOT observations.
- Mechanism: The network takes as input both the raw KaRIN observations and a nadir-altimeter-derived gridded SSH product interpolated onto the SWOT swath. The gridded product provides reliable large-scale SSH information (scales >80km) that is missing or corrupted in the raw KaRIN data due to instrument errors, while the KaRIN data provides high-resolution information at smaller scales.
- Core assumption: Nadir altimetry products provide accurate large-scale SSH information that can be combined with SWOT data to improve overall calibration.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It benefits from calibrated nadir altimetry products"
  - [section] "we consider the resulting interpolated SSH products as auxiliary data of interest to address the calibration of KaRIN observations"
  - [section] "Overall, these four models lead to a significantly lower performance. The largest impact comes from the nadir-altimetry-only gridded product which provides large scale information about the SSH."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss interpolation but don't specifically validate the use of nadir products for SWOT calibration.
- Break condition: If nadir altimetry products have significant errors at the scales where they're most relied upon, or if there are temporal/spatial mismatches between nadir and SWOT data, the calibration will fail.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Scale decomposition (20 scales, 8-160 km) → Batch normalization → Initial convolution → Residual blocks (3x with swath-mixer) → Output convolution → Add to gridded product

- Critical path: Input → Scale decomposition → Batch normalization → Initial convolution → Residual blocks → Output convolution → Add to gridded product

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Scale decomposition vs. learned multi-scale features: The paper uses fixed Gaussian scale decomposition rather than learning it, trading potential expressiveness for stability and interpretability
  - Separate vs. joint processing of swath sides: The architecture processes each side separately then mixes, trading some cross-contamination risk for better exploitation of correlated errors
  - Depth vs. width: 3 residual blocks with 128 channels chosen as a balance between expressiveness and training stability

- Failure signatures:
  - Calibration performance similar to nadir-only interpolation: Indicates the network isn't extracting useful information from KaRIN observations
  - Calibration performance worse than nadir-only interpolation: Suggests the network is introducing artifacts or overfitting
  - Large residual errors at specific scales: May indicate scale decomposition parameters don't match error characteristics
  - Inconsistent performance across swath: May indicate swath-mixer layer issues or spatial bias

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Remove scale decomposition and use raw concatenated inputs - expect significant performance degradation, confirming scale decomposition is crucial
  2. Remove swath-mixer layer and process full swath directly - expect moderate performance drop, confirming the importance of specialized swath handling
  3. Use nadir-only gridded product without KaRIN observations - should perform worse than the full model but better than nadir-only interpolation, confirming the model extracts useful information from KaRIN data

## Limitations
- Relies on simulated SWOT data rather than real observations, which may not capture all real-world error characteristics
- Performance claims based on a specific test domain (NATL60) and may not generalize to other ocean regions with different dynamical regimes
- Method's effectiveness depends critically on the quality of the nadir-altimetry-derived gridded products

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The architectural design principles and scale-space decomposition approach are well-grounded in signal processing theory and oceanographic knowledge of error characteristics
- **Medium confidence**: The quantitative performance claims (1.4 cm RMSE) are based on simulated data and may not directly translate to real SWOT observations
- **Medium confidence**: The ablation studies showing the importance of nadir products and scale decomposition are convincing but limited to the specific test dataset
