# Training trajectories, mini-batch losses and the curious role of the learning rate

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02312
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02312
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Shows that the loss landscape on fixed mini-batches is remarkably convex-like, allowing large steps to reach very low loss values, while the same steps increase loss on held-out data. Demonstrates equivalence between averaging techniques and modified learning rate schedules.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the loss landscape and optimization behavior of deep networks trained with stochastic gradient descent (SGD). The authors propose a simple quadratic model for the loss on a fixed mini-batch and analyze how it behaves differently than the loss on held-out data. They show that for ResNet architectures on ImageNet, a single large step in the direction of a fixed batch can reduce the training loss to values much lower than the average loss of a fully trained model. However, the same step increases the loss on held-out data, suggesting that the loss basins for fixed and unseen batches are fundamentally different.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze the optimization behavior of deep networks trained with SGD by proposing a simple quadratic model for the loss on a fixed mini-batch. They conduct experiments on ResNet architectures trained on ImageNet and CIFAR datasets to validate their model's predictions. The study also establishes connections between various averaging techniques (e.g., SWA, EMA) and specific learning rate schedules, showing that averaging points on the SGD trajectory is equivalent to following a modified learning rate schedule that decays the learning rate over time.

## Key Results
- For ResNet on ImageNet, a single large step can reduce the training loss to values below the average loss of a fully trained model, but increases the held-out loss.
- Averaging points on the SGD trajectory is equivalent to following a modified learning rate schedule that decays the learning rate over time.
- The authors' quadratic model accurately captures the behavior of the loss on fixed mini-batches and provides insights into the relationship between mini-batch gradients and the full batch gradient.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The key insight is that the loss landscape on a fixed mini-batch is remarkably convex-like, allowing large steps to reach very low loss values. However, the same steps increase the loss on held-out data, suggesting that the SGD trajectory does not follow the true gradient of the full batch loss. The authors' simple quadratic model captures this behavior and provides a framework for understanding the relationship between mini-batch gradients and the full batch gradient.

## Foundational Learning
- Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD): An optimization algorithm used to minimize the loss function by updating the model parameters in the direction of the negative gradient. Understanding SGD is crucial for interpreting the optimization behavior and the role of learning rates.
- Loss Landscape: The high-dimensional surface defined by the loss function over the parameter space. Analyzing the properties of the loss landscape, such as convexity and curvature, is essential for understanding the optimization dynamics and the effectiveness of different learning rate schedules.
- Averaging Techniques (SWA, EMA): Methods that improve generalization by averaging model parameters or predictions over the course of training. The paper establishes connections between these techniques and specific learning rate schedules, highlighting the importance of understanding their relationship for optimization.

## Limitations
- The analysis primarily focuses on ResNet architectures on ImageNet and CIFAR datasets, limiting the generalizability of the findings to other architectures and tasks.
- The quadratic model proposed is a simplification of the true loss landscape and may not fully capture the complexities of high-dimensional optimization problems.
- The paper does not extensively explore the impact of different learning rate schedules, batch sizes, and other hyperparameters on the observed phenomena.

## Confidence
- The claim that a single large step in the direction of a fixed batch can reduce the training loss to values much lower than the average loss of a fully trained model: High confidence.

- The claim that the loss landscape on a fixed mini-batch is remarkably convex-like, allowing large steps to reach very low loss values: Medium confidence.
