# Generating Adversarial Samples in Mini-Batches May Be Detrimental To Adversarial Robustness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17720
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17720
- Reference count: 27
- One-line primary result: Generating adversarial samples in large mini-batches can significantly degrade their effectiveness due to mean loss reduction, leading to overestimation of model robustness.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that using large mini-batches for generating adversarial samples can significantly weaken their effectiveness, leading to overestimated model robustness. The degradation occurs because mean loss reduction over mini-batches results in vanishing gradients, producing weaker adversarial perturbations. Experiments with Fast Gradient Method (FGM) and Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) attacks on ImageNetV2 across four neural network architectures show that attack success rates drop notably as batch size increases. For instance, PGD attack success against InceptionV3 drops from 94.54% with batch size 1 to 85.47% with batch size 128. The study proposes using total loss instead of mean loss and explores mixed precision as potential solutions to mitigate this effect.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates adversarial attack effectiveness across different mini-batch sizes using FGM and PGD attacks on ImageNetV2 dataset with pre-trained InceptionV3, Xception, ResNet50, and DenseNet121 models. Experiments test both L2 and L∞ norms while comparing mean loss versus total loss formulations across batch sizes of 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, and 128. The primary metric is adversarial attack success rate (ASR). The research also investigates mixed precision (16-bit vs 32-bit floating point) to understand its impact on adversarial strength degradation.

## Key Results
- Adversarial attack success rates drop significantly as mini-batch size increases, with PGD against InceptionV3 falling from 94.54% (batch size 1) to 85.47% (batch size 128)
- Using total loss instead of mean loss mitigates the adversarial strength degradation caused by larger batch sizes
- Mixed precision (16-bit) can partially compensate for batch size effects but introduces its own trade-offs in adversarial strength

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Mini-batch size affects adversarial sample strength due to mean loss reduction.
- Mechanism: When calculating the mean loss over a mini-batch, the contribution of each individual sample's loss is reduced as batch size increases. This reduction leads to smaller gradient magnitudes, resulting in vanishing gradients and weaker adversarial perturbations.
- Core assumption: The reduction of loss over mini-batch to mean loss is the primary cause of adversarial strength degradation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We demonstrate that an increase in mini-batch size results in a decrease in the efficacy of the samples produced, and we draw connections between these observations and the phenomenon of vanishing gradients."
  - [section]: "The most drastic example of this can be seen when we look at the InceptionV3 model... the rate of successful attacks against InceptionV3 drops from 94.54% when samples are generated individually, to 85.47% when samples are generated within batches of size 128."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in corpus.
- Break condition: If the adversarial attack method does not rely on gradient information or if the loss function is not averaged over the mini-batch.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using total loss instead of mean loss mitigates adversarial strength degradation.
- Mechanism: By summing the losses over the mini-batch instead of averaging, each sample's contribution to the gradient remains constant regardless of batch size. This prevents the vanishing gradient problem and maintains adversarial strength.
- Core assumption: The computational complexity of total loss is equivalent to mean loss, and the switch does not introduce additional overhead.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We formulate loss functions such that adversarial sample strength is not degraded by mini-batch size."
  - [section]: "In contrast, when we use our formulation given within equation 5 which calculates the total loss rather than the mean loss, we can see that the penalty to adversarial strength induced by larger batch sizes is alleviated."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in corpus.
- Break condition: If the adversarial attack method is not sensitive to gradient magnitude or if the computational overhead of total loss becomes significant.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mixed precision can compensate for adversarial strength loss due to batch size.
- Mechanism: Reducing precision (e.g., from 32-bit to 16-bit floating point) can increase gradient magnitudes by reducing numerical precision. This can partially offset the loss of strength due to mean loss reduction in large batches.
- Core assumption: The loss of precision leads to a less exact gradient descent direction, which can compensate for the reduced gradient magnitude due to mean loss reduction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our third set of experiments repeat our experiments using our original loss formulation given in equation 1 while using 16-bit floating point precision instead of 32-bit floating point precision for our tensors."
  - [section]: "As can be observed from our findings, regardless of batch size, a decrease in precision leads to a drop in adversarial strength."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence found in corpus.
- Break condition: If the adversarial attack method is not sensitive to numerical precision or if the precision reduction leads to numerical instability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the degradation of adversarial sample strength in large mini-batches affect adversarial training as significantly as it affects adversarial testing?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses how switching from mean loss to total loss during testing is straightforward, but notes this switch is more complicated during training due to potential need to adjust hyperparameters like step-size and momentum decay.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on adversarial testing and mentions training implications briefly, but does not conduct experiments or provide detailed analysis of the impact on adversarial training specifically.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing adversarial training outcomes (robustness metrics) using mean loss versus total loss formulations across various batch sizes would provide direct evidence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the mini-batch size effect on adversarial sample strength vary across different neural network architectures and attack types beyond those tested?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests four architectures (InceptionV3, Xception, ResNet50, DenseNet121) and two attack methods (FGM, PGD) across L2 and L∞ norms, but acknowledges the need for broader exploration.
- Why unresolved: The tested set of architectures and attack types, while diverse, does not cover the full spectrum of possible combinations in the literature.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of additional architectures (e.g., Vision Transformers, EfficientNets) and attack variants (e.g., AutoAttack, different optimization strategies) across various norms would clarify generalizability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal precision level for balancing computational efficiency and adversarial sample strength when using large mini-batches?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that mixed precision (16-bit) reduces adversarial strength compared to full precision (32-bit), but notes that for large batches, batch-corrected mixed precision can outperform full precision without batch correction.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates mixed precision effects, it does not explore the full range of precision levels or provide guidance on optimizing precision for different batch sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study varying precision levels (e.g., 8-bit, 10-bit, 16-bit, 32-bit) and batch sizes to identify optimal combinations for different architectures and attack methods would provide actionable insights.

## Limitations
- The findings are based on experiments with four pre-trained models on the ImageNetV2 dataset, which may not generalize to all neural network architectures or datasets.
- The study focuses on white-box attacks using cross-entropy loss, and results may not apply to other attack types or loss functions.
- Mixed precision experiments introduce trade-offs that require further investigation to fully understand their impact on adversarial strength and computational efficiency.
