# Understanding Political Polarisation using Language Models: A dataset and method

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00891
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00891
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Longformer model achieves 76.862% classification accuracy on political data and 69.681% on background data for detecting US political affiliation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses political polarization in US politics using language models on a dataset spanning 120 years of Wikipedia data for US Congress members. The authors develop a comprehensive dataset split into background, political, and other sections, then apply classical models (Word2Vec, Doc2Vec) and advanced transformer-based models (Longformer) to classify political affiliation. Using attention mechanisms, they identify high-influence words and visualize nearest neighbors of politicians to quantify polarization. Results indicate significant polarization, with background information also influencing political leanings. The authors propose an interactive website to help voters assess candidate polarization.

## Method Summary
The authors scraped Wikipedia data for US Congress members from the 58th to 117th congress, splitting it into four chronological phases and manually annotating sections as background, political, or other. Data underwent extensive preprocessing including NER cleaning to remove names, organizations, and locations (from political sections only), followed by application of Word2Vec, Doc2Vec, and Longformer models. The Longformer with global attention tokens processed up to 4096 tokens per document, significantly outperforming classical models. Attention scores were analyzed to identify politically sensitive words, and nearest neighbor visualization was used to quantify polarization patterns.

## Key Results
- Longformer achieved classification accuracies of 76.862% on political data and 69.681% on background data
- Background information significantly influences political leaning detection, challenging the hypothesis of independence
- Word "abortion" appeared in top attention scores for Ted Stevens, demonstrating model's ability to identify politically sensitive terms
- Proposed interactive website for voter assessment of candidate polarization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Longformer outperforms classical models due to its ability to capture long-range dependencies across extended document contexts.
- Mechanism: The Longformer uses sliding window attention with global attention tokens, processing up to 4096 tokens compared to 512 in BERT, enabling better representation of nuanced political narratives.
- Core assumption: Longer political biographies contain critical information for party affiliation distributed throughout the document.
- Evidence anchors: Longformer provides classification accuracies of 76.862% (political) and 69.681% (background), significantly higher than Doc2Vec results of 59.52% and 61.846%.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Background information contains detectable signals of political affiliation, indicating personal history influences political leanings.
- Mechanism: The model learns correlations between biographical features and party affiliation during training, using background context as supplementary predictive signals.
- Core assumption: Political polarization extends beyond policy positions to encompass lifestyle, education, and regional factors manifesting in biographical data.
- Evidence anchors: Results indicate significant polarization with background information also influencing political leanings; "abortion" appeared in top 10 percentile words when analyzing attention scores.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Attention mechanisms can identify politically sensitive words and their contextual relationships, revealing polarization patterns.
- Mechanism: The model computes attention weights between tokens, with high attention scores indicating semantically important words that differ systematically between parties.
- Core assumption: Political discourse contains specific vocabulary and semantic patterns that differ between parties and are learnable by attention-based models.
- Evidence anchors: Attention mechanisms identified high-influence words and visualized nearest neighbors of politicians to quantify polarization; global attention scores calculated for last layer to find highest attention score words.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Data collection pipeline (Wikipedia scraping → section classification → NER cleaning → preprocessing) → Model architecture (Longformer with global attention tokens → classification head) → Analysis tools (attention score extraction → nearest neighbor computation → interactive visualization) → Evaluation (accuracy metrics → attention-based interpretability → polarization ratio calculations)

- **Critical path**: Data collection → preprocessing → model training → attention analysis → visualization. The most time-consuming component is data preprocessing due to careful section classification and bias removal.

- **Design tradeoffs**: Longformer vs. standard BERT (better performance vs. higher computational cost), background inclusion vs. exclusion (more predictive power vs. testing independence hypothesis), NER cleaning level (reduced bias vs. potential loss of contextual information)

- **Failure signatures**: Low accuracy on both political and background data (model architecture issues or insufficient training data), high accuracy on political but not background (hypothesis about independence may be correct, or background contains noise), high attention scores on irrelevant words (preprocessing issues or model overfitting)

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Reproduce Doc2Vec baseline results using same data splits and preprocessing pipeline, 2) Train Longformer with only political data to establish upper bound performance, 3) Train Longformer with only background data to test independence hypothesis

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop more robust metrics for quantifying political polarization that go beyond binary classification accuracy?
- Basis in paper: Authors mention "Future work includes expanding metrics for measuring political polarization" and discuss limitations of current classification approaches
- Why unresolved: Current evaluation relies on binary classification accuracy (e.g., 76.862% for political data), which doesn't capture nuanced spectrum of polarization or temporal changes
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of new metrics measuring polarization intensity, temporal trends, and contextual factors, tested across multiple datasets and political systems

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent does background information (education, birthplace, etc.) causally influence political views versus merely correlating with them?
- Basis in paper: Authors explicitly state "our hypothesis is that the political views of a candidate should be based on reason and be independent of factors such as birthplace, alma mater, etc." but find background data significantly influences classification
- Why unresolved: Paper demonstrates correlation between background information and political affiliation but cannot establish causation due to observational nature
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking individuals' political views over time while controlling for background factors, or natural experiments where background factors change independently of political views

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does choice of language model architecture (BERT, Longformer, BigBird) affect detection of political polarization, and what are optimal architectural choices for this task?
- Basis in paper: Authors compare multiple models (BERT, Longformer, BigBird) and find Longformer performs best, but acknowledge this may be due to its ability to process longer sequences
- Why unresolved: Paper only tests limited set of models and doesn't explore architectural variations systematically or consider whether certain architectures might introduce bias
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive ablation studies testing various transformer architectures, sequence lengths, and attention mechanisms, validated across different time periods and political contexts

### Open Question 4
- Question: How has nature and intensity of political polarization evolved over 120-year span of dataset, and what factors drive these changes?
- Basis in paper: Authors divide data into 4 chronological phases and note "to understand if and how the polarization amongst candidates changes" but don't provide detailed temporal analysis
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on overall polarization detection rather than detailed temporal trend analysis or identification of driving factors
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed temporal analysis showing polarization trends over time, correlation with historical events, and statistical modeling to identify key drivers of polarization changes

### Open Question 5
- Question: How do attention mechanisms in transformer models reveal meaningful patterns of political polarization, and what are limitations of current attention-based analysis?
- Basis in paper: Authors use attention mechanisms to identify high-influence words and discuss interesting results but acknowledge limitations in interpreting Longformer's attention
- Why unresolved: Paper demonstrates attention-based analysis but doesn't fully explore what attention patterns reveal about polarization mechanisms or limitations of current methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic analysis of attention patterns across different models and time periods, validation of attention-based insights with human annotation, and development of more interpretable attention mechanisms

## Limitations

- Reliance on Wikipedia biographies as proxy for political positions may not fully capture complexity of political beliefs
- Binary classification (Democrat vs. Republican) oversimplifies multi-dimensional nature of political beliefs and may miss centrist or third-party perspectives
- Attention mechanism interpretation assumes high attention scores directly indicate political relevance, but attention weights can be influenced by syntactic patterns unrelated to political content

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Classification performance improvements of Longformer over classical models are well-supported by reported accuracy metrics (76.862% vs 59.52% for Doc2Vec). The mechanism by which Longformer processes longer contexts through extended attention windows is well-established.

- **Medium confidence**: Claim that background information contains predictive signals for political affiliation is supported by experimental results showing classification on background data alone, but causal interpretation requires additional validation. Assumption that attention scores directly reveal politically sensitive words may conflate correlation with causation.

- **Low confidence**: Broader claims about quantifying political polarization and creating actionable insights for voters through proposed website are not empirically validated within this study. Leap from classification accuracy to understanding polarization mechanisms requires additional theoretical and empirical support.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-validation with independent political datasets**: Validate model's ability to classify political affiliation using congressional voting records or campaign finance data as ground truth, rather than relying solely on Wikipedia biographies.

2. **Ablation study on preprocessing components**: Systematically remove different preprocessing steps (NER cleaning, number removal, location filtering) to quantify their individual contributions to model performance.

3. **Temporal analysis of polarization trends**: Apply trained models across 120-year timeframe to track changes in classification accuracy over time, testing whether observed polarization is recent phenomenon or consistent throughout studied period.
