# Thompson Sampling with Diffusion Generative Prior

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.05182
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05182
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: DiffTS consistently outperforms baselines like UCB and Thompson sampling with Gaussian/GMM priors on various synthetic and real bandit problems

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to Thompson sampling for bandit problems by incorporating diffusion models as generative priors. The core innovation lies in training a diffusion model on bandit task data to learn the underlying task distribution, which is then combined with Thompson sampling to balance prior knowledge with noisy observations. A specialized training procedure handles incomplete and noisy data, enabling the method to learn from imperfect bandit datasets. The proposed DiffTS algorithm demonstrates consistent improvements over traditional approaches across various synthetic and real bandit problems.

## Method Summary
The proposed DiffTS algorithm works by first training a diffusion model on historical bandit task data to learn the underlying task distribution. This learned prior is then combined with Thompson sampling, where the diffusion model generates samples from the learned prior distribution, which are used as initial beliefs in the Thompson sampling framework. As observations are made, the algorithm updates these beliefs by combining the prior with the likelihood of the observed rewards. The method includes a novel training procedure specifically designed to handle incomplete and noisy bandit data, allowing for more robust learning of the task distribution.

## Key Results
- DiffTS consistently outperforms UCB and Thompson sampling with Gaussian/GMM priors across various bandit problems
- Particularly significant improvements observed on complex task distributions
- The method demonstrates better sample efficiency compared to baseline approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by leveraging the expressive power of diffusion models to learn rich, complex priors from bandit data. Unlike simple parametric priors like Gaussian or GMM distributions, diffusion models can capture complex, multi-modal distributions that better represent the true underlying task structure. This allows for more informed initial beliefs in the Thompson sampling framework, leading to faster learning and better performance. The ability to handle incomplete and noisy data during training also enables learning from real-world bandit datasets that often contain imperfections.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Diffusion Model -> Prior Generation -> Thompson Sampling -> Reward Observation -> Belief Update

**Critical Path**: 
1. Train diffusion model on bandit data
2. Generate prior samples using diffusion model
3. Initialize Thompson sampling with learned prior
4. Observe rewards and update beliefs
5. Repeat steps 3-4 for each round

**Design Tradeoffs**:
- Expressiveness vs. computational cost: More complex priors can capture better task distributions but require more computation for sampling
- Prior strength vs. adaptability: Stronger priors can speed up learning but may hinder adaptation to new tasks
- Model complexity vs. data requirements: More complex diffusion models need more data to learn accurate priors

**Failure Signatures**:
- Poor performance on simple tasks where simple priors suffice
- Computational bottlenecks due to expensive diffusion model sampling
- Overfitting to training data if diffusion model is too complex

**First Experiments**:
1. Compare DiffTS against standard Thompson sampling on a simple Gaussian bandit problem
2. Evaluate performance on a synthetic multi-modal bandit task
3. Test scalability by increasing the dimensionality of the action space

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis is limited, with no formal regret bounds or sample complexity guarantees
- Experimental evaluation primarily on synthetic datasets with limited real-world validation
- Computational overhead of training and sampling from diffusion models may be prohibitive in resource-constrained settings

## Confidence
- **High**: The core algorithmic framework combining diffusion models with Thompson sampling is technically sound and well-motivated
- **Medium**: The empirical results showing performance improvements over baselines are likely valid but may not fully generalize to all bandit problem settings
- **Low**: The claimed advantages in terms of sample efficiency and robustness to noisy observations require further validation and theoretical justification
