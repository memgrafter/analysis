# Improving novelty detection with generative adversarial networks on hand gesture data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06696
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06696
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Proposed GAN-based method achieves 95.4% novelty detection accuracy with only 5% loss in classification accuracy on hand gesture data

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting out-of-vocabulary hand gestures in neural network classifiers using a GAN-based framework. The proposed method augments training data with synthetic samples generated by a GAN, using stochastic target vectors to lower prediction scores and facilitate novelty detection. Evaluated on UC2017 SG and UC2018 DualMyo datasets, the approach significantly improves novelty detection accuracy (95.4% or 90.2%) while maintaining high classification accuracy on trained classes, demonstrating that synthetic data generation enables robust detection of untrained gestures.

## Method Summary
The method employs a custom GAN architecture with a generator that creates synthetic hand gesture samples using noise vectors and stochastic target vectors, and a discriminator that classifies samples as real/generated and assigns them to gesture classes. The generator produces samples online during training, augmenting the dataset with realistic variations of known gestures. Stochastic target vectors replace one-hot vectors to lower prediction scores, making threshold-based novelty detection more effective. The discriminator's output includes validity and class probabilities, with a decision threshold applied for final classification. Training uses Adam optimizer with specific learning rates, batch sizes, and loss weights tailored to each dataset.

## Key Results
- GAN-generated samples show significantly better mean L2 distance to real samples compared to random noise
- Novelty detection accuracy reaches 95.4% (UC2017) or 90.2% (UC2018) with only 5% loss in classification accuracy
- Online data augmentation with GANs yields better novelty detection performance than offline augmentation
- Stochastic target vectors significantly improve novelty detection while maintaining high classification accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The GAN's generator augments the training data with realistic samples, improving novelty detection accuracy.
- Mechanism: The generator learns patterns from the real dataset and creates synthetic samples that mimic real gestures, including out-of-vocabulary gestures. These synthetic samples are then used to train the discriminator, making it more robust to novel patterns.
- Core assumption: The generator can produce samples that are statistically similar to real gestures.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Stochastic target vectors lower the average prediction score, facilitating threshold tuning for novelty detection.
- Mechanism: By using stochastic target vectors instead of one-hot vectors, the discriminator learns to produce lower confidence scores for both known and unknown classes. This makes it easier to set a threshold that distinguishes between trained and untrained gestures.
- Core assumption: Lower prediction scores correlate with novelty.
- Evidence anchors:

- Claim: Online data augmentation with GANs yields better novelty detection performance than offline augmentation.
- Mechanism: The generator continuously produces new samples during training, providing a dynamic and diverse set of synthetic data. This ongoing augmentation helps the discriminator adapt to a wider range of patterns, improving its ability to detect novelties.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Generator (noise + target vectors) -> Discriminator (validity + class probabilities) -> Threshold-based classification
- Critical path: 1) Generate synthetic samples using the generator. 2) Train the discriminator on both real and synthetic samples. 3) Evaluate the discriminator's performance on trained and novel classes.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Online vs. offline augmentation: Online augmentation provides continuous diversity but may be more computationally intensive.
  - Stochastic vs. deterministic targets: Stochastic targets lower prediction scores but may reduce classification accuracy.
- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse: The generator produces a single pattern instead of diverse samples.
  - Discriminator overfitting: The discriminator becomes too specialized on real data and fails to generalize to synthetic samples.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the GAN and evaluate the quality of generated samples using the L2 distance metric.
  2. Test the discriminator's performance with and without stochastic target vectors.
  3. Compare the novelty detection accuracy of online vs. offline data augmentation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of noise distribution (beyond Gaussian) impact the quality and diversity of generated samples in the GAN framework for hand gesture data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses Gaussian noise for sampling in the generator but does not explore alternative noise distributions or their effects on sample generation quality.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on Gaussian noise without comparing its effectiveness against other distributions like uniform, Laplacian, or learned noise models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies showing GAN performance metrics (sample quality, diversity, stability) across different noise distributions for the same hand gesture datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of varying the dimensionality of the latent space (l) on the GAN's ability to generate diverse and realistic hand gesture samples?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper sets the latent size to specific values (23 for UC2017, 8 for UC2018) but does not explore how different dimensionalities affect sample quality or novelty detection performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper fixes the latent size for each dataset without investigating the sensitivity of GAN performance to this hyperparameter or its relationship to dataset complexity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying latent dimensionality across a range of values and measuring GAN performance metrics, sample diversity scores, and novelty detection accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed method generalize to more complex hand gesture datasets with larger numbers of classes and higher-dimensional feature spaces?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates the method on two datasets (UC2017 and UC2018) but does not test its scalability to datasets with more classes or higher-dimensional features.
- Why unresolved: The datasets used are relatively small and simple, and the paper does not address potential challenges or performance degradation when scaling up to more complex scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on larger, more diverse hand gesture datasets and analyzing performance metrics, training stability, and novelty detection accuracy as dataset complexity increases.

## Limitations

- The GAN's generalization capability to truly unseen gesture classes outside validation sets remains untested
- Limited ablation studies prevent clear attribution of performance gains to specific architectural choices
- The effectiveness of stochastic targets across different domain shifts is assumed but not empirically validated

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: GAN-generated samples show significantly better distance metrics to real data than random noise (empirically validated)
- **Medium confidence**: The 95.4%/90.2% novelty detection accuracy claims are based on held-out test sets but may not generalize to completely unseen gesture vocabularies
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that online augmentation outperforms offline is supported by controlled experiments but could be dataset-dependent
