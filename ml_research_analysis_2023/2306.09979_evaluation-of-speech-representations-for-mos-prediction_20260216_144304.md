# Evaluation of Speech Representations for MOS prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09979
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09979
- Reference count: 39
- This paper evaluated feature extraction models for predicting speech quality using MOS prediction

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates different speech representation models for predicting Mean Opinion Score (MOS) in speech quality assessment. The study compares supervised learning, self-supervised learning, and speaker verification models using two datasets: VCC2018 and BRSpeechMOS. The proposed model architecture extracts embeddings from various pre-trained models and uses them to predict speech quality metrics. The research demonstrates that Whisper models perform consistently well across different scenarios and datasets, while also highlighting the competitive performance of speaker verification models like SpeakerNet and TitaNet.

## Method Summary
The study employs a model architecture that extracts embeddings from various pre-trained speech representation models, including supervised learning models, self-supervised learning models, and speaker verification models. These embeddings are then used to predict the Mean Opinion Score (MOS) for speech quality assessment. The evaluation is conducted on two datasets: VCC2018, a standard dataset for voice conversion challenges, and BRSpeechMOS, a Brazilian-Portuguese dataset specifically designed for speech quality evaluation. The approach focuses on comparing the effectiveness of different feature extraction models in capturing speech quality-related information.

## Key Results
- Whisper model performs well across both VCC2018 and BRSpeechMOS datasets
- Whisper-Small achieves best linear correlation of 0.6980 on BRSpeechMOS dataset
- Whisper-Large achieves best linear correlation of 0.7274 on VCC2018 dataset
- Speaker verification models show competitive performance with SpeakerNet (0.6963) and TitaNet (0.6933) on their respective datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of the proposed approach stems from leveraging pre-trained speech representation models that have learned rich, contextual features from large-scale training data. These models capture various aspects of speech quality, including intelligibility, naturalness, and speaker characteristics. By using embeddings from multiple model types (supervised, self-supervised, and speaker verification), the approach can capture diverse aspects of speech quality. The consistent performance of Whisper models across different datasets suggests that their training objectives and architecture are particularly suited for capturing quality-relevant features in speech signals.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Speech Signal -> Feature Extraction Models -> Embedding Extraction -> MOS Prediction

Critical Path:
The critical path involves extracting embeddings from pre-trained models and using these embeddings to predict MOS. The quality of embeddings directly impacts prediction accuracy, making the choice of feature extraction model crucial. The model architecture must efficiently process these embeddings and learn the mapping to MOS scores.

Design Tradeoffs:
The study balances between using complex, high-performing models (like large Whisper variants) and more efficient alternatives. Larger models typically provide better performance but require more computational resources. The choice between supervised, self-supervised, and speaker verification models involves tradeoffs between domain specificity and generalization capability.

Failure Signatures:
Poor performance may occur when embeddings fail to capture quality-relevant features, particularly in challenging acoustic conditions or when datasets have different characteristics than the models were trained on. Cross-dataset performance degradation may indicate overfitting to specific dataset characteristics.

## Limitations
