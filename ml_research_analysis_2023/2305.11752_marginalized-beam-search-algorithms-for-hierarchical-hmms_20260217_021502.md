# Marginalized Beam Search Algorithms for Hierarchical HMMs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11752
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11752
- Reference count: 38
- Key outcome: Two new beam search algorithms (GMBS and LFBS) for decoding outer states in HHMMs outperform Viterbi algorithm

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of decoding outer state sequences in Hierarchical Hidden Markov Models (HHMMs) where traditional Viterbi algorithm cannot efficiently marginalize over inner states. The authors propose two beam search-based algorithms - Greedy Marginalized Beam Search (GMBS) and Local Focus Beam Search (LFBS) - that approximate the most likely outer state sequence by strategically managing computational complexity. These algorithms demonstrate higher performance than Viterbi on both simulated and real nanopore base calling data.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces two algorithms for HHMM outer state decoding: GMBS maintains a fixed beam width and tracks the most likely state sequences through expansion, marginalization, and pruning steps, while LFBS maintains a fixed focus-length limiting attention to the last few outer states for computational efficiency. Both algorithms approximate marginalization over inner states through strategic beam management rather than exhaustive computation. The methods are evaluated on an explicit duration HMM using simulation and nanopore base calling data, comparing accuracy metrics against the Viterbi baseline.

## Key Results
- GMBS and LFBS achieve higher outer state decoding accuracy than Viterbi algorithm
- GMBS maintains W beams while LFBS uses M = |S|^K beams with focus length K
- Both algorithms successfully decode homopolymers in nanopore data
- Computational complexity reduced compared to full marginalization approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
GMBS improves outer state decoding by marginalizing over critical edges (indicators F₂) rather than ignoring them or fully marginalizing the entire state sequence. It expands candidate beams into leaf beams representing both extension and continuation sets, then merges redundant leaf beams by summing their scores, implicitly marginalizing over possible inner state alignments.

### Mechanism 2
LFBS trades some marginalization completeness for computational efficiency by limiting beam focus to the last K outer states. It maintains M = |S|^K beams, each encoding the last K outer states, and expands/marginalizes within groups of size |S| + 1 for efficient sorting and pruning.

### Mechanism 3
Both GMBS and LFBS approximate the optimal decoding solution as W or K grows large by performing exhaustive marginalization over an increasingly representative subset of state sequences. As the beam width (W) or focus-length (K) increases, the pruned set approaches the full set of candidate sequences, recovering the exact marginalization.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Hierarchical Hidden Markov Models (HHMMs) extend HMMs by introducing multiple levels of time-aligned states, with outer states aligned to observations and inner states modeling dwell times.
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding the state hierarchy and the surjective mapping from time-aligned states to sequence-aligned outer states is essential to grasp why standard Viterbi fails for outer state decoding.
  - **Quick check question:** In an HHMM with R=2, what are the roles of S1 (outer state) and S2 (inner state) in the decoding problem?

- **Concept:** Beam Search (BS) algorithms maintain a fixed number of candidate state sequences (beams) and expand/prune them iteratively to approximate the most likely sequence.
  - **Why needed here:** GMBS and LFBS are specialized BS algorithms that modify expansion and pruning to handle HHMM marginalization.
  - **Quick check question:** How does the standard BS algorithm differ from GMBS in handling candidate expansion and pruning?

- **Concept:** Marginalization in probabilistic models involves summing over latent variables to compute the probability of observed data.
  - **Why needed here:** Both GMBS and LFBS perform marginalization over inner states and critical edges to approximate the decoding objective.
  - **Quick check question:** In the context of HHMM decoding, what does marginalizing over F₂ (indicators) achieve compared to ignoring them?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Observation sequence X1:T -> HHMM parameters -> GMBS/LFBS algorithm (expansion, marginalization, pruning) -> Decoded outer state sequence ˆS1:L

- **Critical path:**
  1. Initialize beams with prior probabilities
  2. For each time step t: Expand beams into leaf beams, marginalize by merging redundant leaf beams using ID system, prune to keep top-W (GMBS) or top-1 per group (LFBS)
  3. Back-track from highest-scored beam to reconstruct ˆS1:L

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - GMBS: Higher representation of candidate sequences (W beams) vs. expensive O(W|S| log(W|S|)) sorting
  - LFBS: Cheaper O(W|S| log(|S|)) sorting per group vs. limited ability to decode long homopolymers (K constraint)
  - Both: Trade marginal completeness for computational tractability

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Degenerate performance similar to Viterbi: pruning removes beams with important marginalization contributions
  - Missing homopolymers longer than K (LFBS): focus-length too short for dataset
  - High computational cost despite pruning: beam width or focus-length set too large for available hardware

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run GMBS with W=8 and LFBS with K=5 on a short simulated sequence (L≈500) to verify basic functionality and compare to Viterbi
  2. Increase W to 64 (GMBS) or K to 6 (LFBS) and observe accuracy gains and runtime scaling on the same dataset
  3. Test both algorithms on a real nanopore base calling dataset with known reference, focusing on homopolymer decoding accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** How do GMBS and LFBS compare in performance and computational efficiency to other approximate inference methods for HHMMs, such as the marginalized Viterbi algorithm or the max-product algorithm?
- **Open Question 2:** Can GMBS and LFBS be extended to handle higher-order Markov assumptions in HHMMs, and how would this affect their performance and computational complexity?
- **Open Question 3:** How sensitive are GMBS and LFBS to the choice of beam width (W) and focus length (K), and is there an optimal way to select these parameters for a given dataset or application?

## Limitations
- No theoretical guarantees on approximation error bounds for finite W or K
- Limited ablation studies on how beam width/focus length affect accuracy vs. computation
- No comparison to other HHMM-specific decoding methods that might exist in literature

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in performance improvement claims based on experimental results
- **Medium confidence** in claimed computational complexity improvements based on algorithmic analysis
