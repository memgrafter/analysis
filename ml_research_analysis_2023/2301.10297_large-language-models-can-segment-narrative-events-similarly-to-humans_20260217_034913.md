# Large language models can segment narrative events similarly to humans

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10297
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10297
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: GPT-3 segments narratives into events that align closely with human consensus annotations

## Executive Summary
This study presents a zero-shot method using GPT-3 to automatically segment continuous narratives into events, offering a scalable alternative to human annotation. By prompting GPT-3 to identify "ongoing coherent situations" in stories, the model produces event boundaries that closely match human consensus annotations. The method achieves statistically significant alignment with human perception of event structure, demonstrating that large language models can capture shared cognitive representations of narrative events.

## Method Summary
The method involves prompting GPT-3 (text-davinci-002) with sliding window approach to segment stories into events based on narrative coherence. For each story, the model processes text in 2048-token windows with temperature=0, adjusting max_tokens to accommodate story length. Event boundaries are extracted from the model's predicted newline characters, and their log-probabilities are used to create continuous boundary probability signals. These model boundaries are then compared statistically to human consensus annotations using Hamming distance and cross-correlation measures.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 segmentation of "Pieman" story yielded 23 events, closely matching human consensus of 19-20 events
- Model boundaries were statistically closer to human consensus than individual human annotators' boundaries
- Continuous boundary probability from GPT-3 significantly correlated with human button press probability (r=0.308-0.362, p<0.001)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: GPT-3 infers event boundaries by treating them as "ongoing coherent situations" and detecting where coherence breaks down
- **Mechanism**: The prompt defines events as coherent situations and asks the model to segment at those boundaries. GPT-3's next-token prediction identifies where it expects narrative structure changes, signaled by high likelihood of newline character
- **Core assumption**: The model's internal representation of narrative coherence aligns with human perception of event structure
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states model produces boundaries that "closely align with human consensus annotations"; section describes using log-probability of newline character to assess event boundary prediction
- **Break condition**: If model's coherence representation diverges from human perception, or prompt fails to trigger intended behavior

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Model's segmentation approximates "consensus solution" of human annotators better than individual human annotations
- **Mechanism**: Averaging human annotations creates consensus boundary. GPT-3's boundaries, derived from shared model representation, are statistically closer to this consensus than single human annotator boundaries
- **Core assumption**: GPT-3 captures shared underlying event structure that emerges from averaging individual human perceptions
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract and section both state "boundaries identified by GPT-3 are closer to the consensus, on average, than boundaries identified by individual human annotators"
- **Break condition**: If consensus is poorly defined or model's boundaries are systematically biased away from consensus

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Continuous log-probability of newline character correlates with continuous probability of human button presses at event boundaries
- **Mechanism**: Both outputs treated as probabilistic signals: human button presses aggregated into probability over time, model's predicted likelihood of newline mapped to same time axis. High correlation indicates shared sensitivity to event structure
- **Core assumption**: Model's newline prediction reflects genuine event boundary and is temporally aligned with human perception
- **Evidence anchors**: Abstract states "continuous measure of event boundary perception in the model is significantly correlated with the continuous measure of agreement between human raters"; section provides correlation coefficients r=0.308 and r=0.362 with p<0.001
- **Break condition**: If newline prediction driven by factors unrelated to event structure, or mapping between model tokens and human time is inaccurate

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Event segmentation as a cognitive process
  - **Why needed here**: Understanding event segmentation as shared human perceptual process, not just linguistic task, is crucial for interpreting why GPT-3's output might align with human annotations
  - **Quick check question**: What is the difference between an event boundary and a sentence boundary, and why might they not always coincide?

- **Concept**: Large language model prompting and zero-shot learning
  - **Why needed here**: Method relies on GPT-3's ability to perform novel task (event segmentation) without fine-tuning, using only carefully constructed prompt
  - **Quick check question**: What are the risks and benefits of using zero-shot prompting for tasks the model was not explicitly trained on?

- **Concept**: Statistical comparison of event annotations (Hamming distance, cross-correlation)
  - **Why needed here**: Paper's main claims rest on statistical comparisons between model and human segmentations
  - **Quick check question**: Why is Hamming distance an appropriate measure for comparing event boundaries, and what are its limitations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text preprocessing/tokenization -> Prompt construction/GPT-3 API calls -> Parse model output for event boundaries -> Statistical comparison with human annotations -> Continuous boundary probability extraction and correlation analysis
- **Critical path**: Most sensitive steps are prompt construction (must elicit correct behavior), model output parsing (must handle edge cases like truncated output), and statistical comparison (must handle alignment between model tokens and human time)
- **Design tradeoffs**: Sliding window for long stories trades context for coverage; temperature=0 increases reproducibility but may miss model variability; parsing for word-for-word copy trades simplicity for robustness
- **Failure signatures**: Model output diverges from word-for-word copy (summarization), event boundaries are too frequent or too sparse, statistical comparisons are not significant, or continuous probability signals are uncorrelated
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test prompt variations (with/without "long" qualifier) on short story and compare boundary counts and Hamming distances to human consensus
  2. Vary sliding window size and max_tokens to see how context affects output quality and reproducibility
  3. Compare GPT-3's continuous newline log-probability to human button press probability on story with dense annotations, checking for correlation at multiple lags

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does GPT-3's event segmentation performance compare when using audio narratives versus text transcripts?
  - **Basis in paper**: Authors note human participants segmented audio narratives while GPT-3 used text transcripts, suggesting audio may contain prosodic cues absent in text
  - **Why unresolved**: Study only tested GPT-3 on text transcripts, not audio narratives directly
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Testing GPT-3's event segmentation on audio narratives directly and comparing to text-based segmentation

- **Open Question 2**: To what extent does GPT-3's event segmentation reflect genuine understanding of event structure versus pattern matching based on training data?
  - **Basis in paper**: Authors suggest GPT-3 may offer insights into event cognition but acknowledge uncertainty about whether performance stems from genuine understanding or learned statistical patterns
  - **Why unresolved**: Study demonstrates correlation with human annotations but doesn't investigate underlying mechanisms
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Systematic testing of GPT-3's performance on novel narrative structures not present in training data

- **Open Question 3**: Can GPT-3's event segmentation be further optimized to match human performance more closely, and what modifications would achieve this?
  - **Basis in paper**: Authors note "substantial gap" between GPT-3's performance and most consistent human annotators
  - **Why unresolved**: Study used basic zero-shot prompting without fine-tuning or advanced prompting techniques
  - **What evidence would resolve it**: Experimenting with different prompting strategies, fine-tuning approaches, or architectural modifications to enhance event segmentation accuracy

## Limitations
- Method relies on assumption that GPT-3's internal representations of narrative coherence align with human cognitive event boundaries
- Sliding window approach for long stories may fragment context and affect boundary detection quality
- Validation limited to three stories, raising questions about generalizability across diverse narrative genres and structures

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: GPT-3 can segment narratives into events using zero-shot prompting, producing statistically significant alignment with human consensus boundaries
- **Medium confidence**: GPT-3 approximates consensus solution of human annotators better than individual humans
- **Medium confidence**: Continuous probability measure from GPT-3 correlates with human event boundary perception

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-genre validation**: Apply method to narratives from different genres (news articles, technical descriptions, dialogue-heavy scripts) to test generalizability
