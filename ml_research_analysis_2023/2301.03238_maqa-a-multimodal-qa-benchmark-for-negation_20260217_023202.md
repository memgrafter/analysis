# MAQA: A Multimodal QA Benchmark for Negation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03238
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03238
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 12
- Key outcome: Standard fine-tuning of multimodal transformers fails to generalize negation in music QA, with near-chance performance on negated questions; task augmentation with PaLM-generated negated examples significantly improves results, achieving over 80% accuracy on negated questions.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MAQA, a multimodal QA benchmark designed to evaluate negation understanding in music-related audio. The authors find that standard fine-tuning of multimodal transformers (MT5) fails to correctly interpret negation in music questions, scoring only 50% on negated questions despite over 90% accuracy on non-negated ones. They propose task augmentation with negated QA examples as a solution, showing that using PaLM to generate these examples with natural linguistic patterns significantly outperforms template-based approaches. The best results are achieved with MT5-XL using MuLan audio embeddings, reaching over 80% accuracy on negated questions after task augmentation.

## Method Summary
The authors create MAQA by generating QA pairs from AudioSet music labels, sampling hard negatives from sibling nodes in the ontology, and using PaLM to generate negated question variations. They implement a multimodal MT5 architecture with frozen audio encoders (VGGish or MuLan) that project audio embeddings into text token space, fine-tuning on the generated QA pairs with task augmentation. The model is trained with batch size 64 (MT5-base) or 128 (MT5-XL), learning rate 1e-3, for 50,000 steps on Cloud TPU Pods, evaluating accuracy and AUC-ROC on ASBaseEval (non-negated) and ASNegationEval (negated) benchmarks.

## Key Results
- Standard fine-tuning of multimodal MT5 achieves over 90% AUC-ROC on non-negated questions but only 50% on negated questions in high compound divergence settings
- Task augmentation with PaLM-generated negated examples improves negated question accuracy by nearly 40% compared to baseline
- Template-based negation augmentation performs 10% worse than PaLM-based fine-tuning due to increased compound divergence between training and evaluation data
- MT5-XL with MuLan embeddings achieves the best performance, exceeding 80% accuracy on negated questions after task augmentation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PaLM-based task augmentation with negated examples enables compositional generalization for negation understanding in multimodal transformers.
- Mechanism: By prompting PaLM to generate natural language negated QA pairs that combine learned music attributes with negation, the multimodal transformer is exposed to novel compositional concepts during training that weren't present in the original AudioSet labels.
- Core assumption: The PaLM-generated negated examples contain linguistically natural patterns that the transformer can learn from, and these patterns are sufficiently different from template-based negations to provide meaningful generalization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "augmenting the original training task distributions with negated QA examples allow the model to reliably reason with negation"
  - [section 6.3] "The task augmentation proves to be an effective strategy for tackling negation. As shown in Table 4(b), multimodal MT5 fined-tuned with task augmentation improves the baseline on ASNegationEval as shown in Table 4(a) by nearly 40%"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - only 1 related paper directly addresses negation understanding in VLMs
- Break condition: If the PaLM-generated examples don't capture natural linguistic variations or if the compositional divergence remains too high between training and test sets.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multimodal transformers can learn to fuse audio and text modalities for music understanding when fine-tuned on non-negated QA pairs.
- Mechanism: The MT5-based architecture projects audio embeddings into the text token space, allowing the transformer encoder to learn joint representations that capture both music attributes and their corresponding audio features.
- Core assumption: The frozen audio encoder (either VGGish or MuLan) provides sufficiently rich embeddings that, when projected and combined with text, allow the transformer to learn meaningful multimodal representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "with standard fine-tuning approach multimodal transformers are still incapable of correctly interpreting negation irrespective of model size"
  - [section 6.1] "The fine-tuned multimodal MT5 score over 90% AUC-ROC on the ASBaseEval benchmark that features Audio QA tasks on music styles, moods, genres, instruments, etc."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus papers about multimodal fusion for music QA
- Break condition: If the audio embeddings are too coarse or the projection layer doesn't adequately align audio and text spaces.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Increasing compound divergence between training and test sets makes compositional generalization more difficult for multimodal transformers.
- Mechanism: When training and test sets have different compound distributions (different combinations of music attributes and negation patterns), the model must generalize beyond simple memorization to novel compositions.
- Core assumption: The compound divergence metric accurately captures the difficulty of generalization tasks and that models struggle more with high CD settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.2] "The multimodal MT5 fine-tuned on non-negated audio QA pairs (ASBaseTrain) scores only 50% on the ASNegationEval benchmark in this High CD setting"
  - [section 6.4] "The Template-based fine-tuning scores around 10% lower in AUC score compared to PaLM-based fine-tuning. The observed gap can be explained by the increased compound divergence between the training data and the evaluation data"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers don't directly address compound divergence in multimodal settings
- Break condition: If compound divergence doesn't correlate with generalization difficulty or if other factors dominate model performance.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Compositional generalization
  - Why needed here: The benchmark tests whether models can understand novel concepts formed by composing known concepts (like "no vocals" as negation of "vocals")
  - Quick check question: Can a model that understands "guitar" and "bass guitar" also understand "no guitar" as a distinct concept?

- Concept: Multimodal transformer architectures
  - Why needed here: The paper uses a modified MT5 architecture that fuses audio and text inputs for the QA task
  - Quick check question: How does projecting audio embeddings into the text token space enable multimodal fusion?

- Concept: Task augmentation
  - Why needed here: Negated examples are added to the training data to improve negation understanding
  - Quick check question: Why does adding negated examples to training data improve performance on negated test questions?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Audio encoder (VGGish or MuLan) -> Linear projection layer -> MT5 encoder -> Classification head
- Critical path:
  1. Extract audio features using frozen encoder
  2. Project audio features to text embedding space
  3. Concatenate with text token embeddings
  4. Process through MT5 encoder layers
  5. Apply classification head to final representations
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using frozen audio encoders vs. fine-tuning them
  - Template-based vs. PaLM-generated negated examples
  - MT5-Base vs. MT5-XL model sizes
- Failure signatures:
  - High performance on non-negated questions but chance-level on negated questions
  - Performance doesn't improve with larger model sizes
  - Better performance with PaLM-generated vs. template negations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune MT5-Base with VGGish on non-negated examples, evaluate on ASBaseEval
  2. Fine-tune MT5-XL with MuLan on non-negated examples, evaluate on ASNegationEval
  3. Fine-tune MT5-XL with MuLan on negated examples, evaluate on ASNegationEval

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the effectiveness of PaLM-based task augmentation generalize to other domains beyond music, such as natural language understanding or visual question answering?
- Basis in paper: [inferred]
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates the PaLM-based task augmentation approach on the MAQA music audio QA benchmark. It does not explore whether this approach would be effective for other types of multimodal tasks or domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on other multimodal benchmarks like visual question answering datasets or natural language inference tasks to see if PaLM-based task augmentation improves performance on negated questions in those domains.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of multimodal transformers on negated questions scale with model size, and is there a point of diminishing returns?
- Basis in paper: [explicit]
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests model sizes up to MT5-XL (3.7B parameters). It does not explore whether even larger models like PaLM itself (540B parameters) would perform better on negated questions, or if there is a plateau in performance gains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Fine-tuning and evaluating even larger multimodal transformers like PaLM on the MAQA benchmark to see if performance continues to improve with model size.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Are there more efficient ways to generate negated QA examples than using PaLM, which requires significant computational resources?
- Basis in paper: [inferred]
- Why unresolved: The paper proposes using PaLM to generate negated QA examples with natural linguistic variations. However, PaLM is a very large model (540B parameters) that requires substantial computational resources to run. It is unclear if there are more efficient methods to generate high-quality negated examples.
- What evidence would resolve it: Exploring alternative methods for generating negated QA examples, such as using smaller language models, rule-based approaches, or human annotation, and comparing their effectiveness and efficiency to the PaLM-based approach.

## Limitations

- The paper doesn't address potential artifacts in the generated data or whether PaLM introduces biases in the negation patterns
- The effectiveness of task augmentation relies heavily on PaLM, a very large model (540B parameters) that requires substantial computational resources
- The evaluation focuses on a specific music domain with AudioSet labels, making it unclear how well findings generalize to other multimodal domains

## Confidence

**Confidence: Medium** The paper demonstrates that multimodal transformers struggle with negation understanding, but the solution relies heavily on PaLM-generated examples. The effectiveness of task augmentation is shown empirically but the underlying mechanism for why PaLM-generated negations work better than template-based ones isn't fully explained. The compound divergence metric, while intuitive, lacks theoretical grounding for why it predicts generalization difficulty.

**Confidence: Medium** The evaluation focuses on a specific music domain with AudioSet labels. While the benchmark design is sound, it's unclear how well the findings generalize to other multimodal domains or whether negation understanding in music translates to other audio-visual tasks. The reliance on a frozen audio encoder (VGGish or MuLan) also limits the model's ability to learn domain-specific audio representations.

**Confidence: Low** The paper doesn't address potential artifacts in the generated data or whether PaLM introduces biases in the negation patterns. The ablation studies show task augmentation helps, but don't explore alternative augmentation strategies or the minimum number of negated examples needed for effective learning.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-domain validation**: Test the task augmentation approach on a different multimodal domain (e.g., video understanding or image-text QA) to verify whether PaLM-based negation generation generalizes beyond music audio.

2. **Ablation on audio encoders**: Compare performance when fine-tuning vs. freezing audio encoders to determine whether the frozen feature extractor limitation is a bottleneck for negation understanding.

3. **Synthetic vs. natural negations**: Create a controlled experiment comparing PaLM-generated negations against more systematic template variations (e.g., different syntactic structures) to isolate what makes the linguistic patterns effective.
