# Implementing BERT and fine-tuned RobertA to detect AI generated news by ChatGPT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07401
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07401
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Fine-tuned RoBERTa achieved 98% accuracy in detecting AI-generated news, outperforming BERT (87%) and baseline models

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of detecting AI-generated news from ChatGPT on social media platforms. The researchers implemented and fine-tuned BERT and RoBERTa transformer models, evaluating their performance against traditional neural networks including CNN, LSTM, BiLSTM, and CNN-BiLSTM architectures. The fine-tuned RoBERTa model demonstrated superior performance with 98% accuracy, highlighting the effectiveness of transformer-based approaches for rumor detection. The results underscore the importance of task-specific model selection and fine-tuning for combating AI-generated misinformation.

## Method Summary
The researchers fine-tuned pre-trained BERT and RoBERTa models on a dataset containing real and fake news to detect AI-generated content. They compared these transformer models against baseline architectures (CNN, LSTM, BiLSTM, CNN-BiLSTM) using standard classification metrics. The fine-tuning process adapted the models to capture contextual and semantic features specific to AI-generated news patterns, with RoBERTa showing particular effectiveness due to its larger pre-training corpus and different training objectives.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned RoBERTa achieved 98% accuracy in detecting AI-generated news
- BERT model reached 87% accuracy after fine-tuning
- RoBERTa outperformed all baseline models including the best-performing BiLSTM

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Model loading -> Fine-tuning loop -> Evaluation -> Experiment tracking
- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess dataset
  2. Load pre-trained model and tokenizer
  3. Fine-tune on labeled data
  4. Evaluate on validation set
  5. Compare against baselines
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. inference speed
  - Fine-tuning all layers vs. freezing early layers
  - Dataset size vs. risk of overfitting
- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: Train accuracy >> validation accuracy
  - Underfitting: Both accuracies low and similar
  - Class imbalance: Skewed confusion matrix
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train BERT with default hyperparameters on 80/20 split
  2. Fine-tune RoBERTa with reduced learning rate (2e-5)
  3. Compare BiLSTM baseline to transformer models on same data split
