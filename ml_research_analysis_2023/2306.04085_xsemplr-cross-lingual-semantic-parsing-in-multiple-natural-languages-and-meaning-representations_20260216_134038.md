# XSemPLR: Cross-Lingual Semantic Parsing in Multiple Natural Languages and Meaning Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04085
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04085
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Encoder-decoder models (mT5) achieve highest performance, and cross-lingual few-shot training significantly reduces performance gaps

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces XSemPLR, a unified benchmark for cross-lingual semantic parsing covering 22 natural languages and 8 meaning representations across 5 tasks and 164 domains. The authors construct this benchmark by unifying 9 high-quality datasets and conduct comprehensive experiments on 3 groups of multilingual language models across 6 training settings. Results show that encoder-decoder models (mT5) outperform other architectures, multilingual training improves average performance, and cross-lingual few-shot learning effectively reduces the performance gap between monolingual and cross-lingual transfer.

## Method Summary
The authors construct XSemPLR by selecting and unifying 9 high-quality cross-lingual semantic parsing datasets, ensuring consistent train/dev/test splits across languages. They evaluate three groups of multilingual language models: encoder-based models (mBERT, XLM-R), encoder-decoder models (mBART, mT5), and decoder-based models (Codex, BLOOM). The experiments cover 6 settings including monolingual, multilingual, and cross-lingual training with full, few-shot, and zero-shot learning scenarios. Models are evaluated using exact match, execution score, and denotation accuracy metrics across the unified benchmark.

## Key Results
- Encoder-decoder models (mT5) achieve highest performance on monolingual evaluation compared to other model architectures
- Multilingual training can further improve average performance across languages
- Cross-lingual few-shot training significantly reduces the performance gap between monolingual and cross-lingual transfer learning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Encoder-decoder models (mT5) achieve the highest performance on monolingual evaluation compared with other models. These models combine bidirectional context from the encoder with autoregressive generation from the decoder, allowing them to better handle the structured nature of meaning representations. The semantic parsing task benefits from both context understanding and generation capabilities.

### Mechanism 2
Multilingual training can further improve the average performance. Training on multiple languages creates shared representations that capture cross-linguistic patterns, improving generalization. Semantic structures have commonalities across languages that can be exploited through shared parameters.

### Mechanism 3
Cross-lingual few-shot training significantly reduces the performance gap between monolingual and cross-lingual transfer. Few-shot examples in the target language provide crucial adaptation signals that help bridge the cross-lingual gap. Even a small amount of target language data can effectively guide cross-lingual transfer.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dataset selection → Data alignment/unification → Train/dev/test split → Model training (6 settings) → Evaluation and analysis
- Critical path: Dataset construction → Model training under different settings → Evaluation and analysis
- Design tradeoffs: Enc-PTR models offer better handling of rare tokens but are more complex; Enc-Dec models provide end-to-end training but may struggle with long sequences; LLMs offer zero-shot capabilities but perform poorly in few-shot scenarios
- Failure signatures: Poor cross-lingual transfer (large performance gap between monolingual and zero-shot settings); Data leakage (inconsistent splits across languages); Evaluation errors (using wrong metrics for specific datasets)
- First 3 experiments: 1) Reproduce the monolingual mT5 results on a single dataset to verify baseline performance; 2) Test cross-lingual zero-shot transfer from English to one other language to observe the performance gap; 3) Apply cross-lingual few-shot training with 10% target language data to measure gap reduction

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do the performance differences between FunQL and SQL in semantic parsing tasks relate to the complexity of their grammatical structures? The paper speculates that FunQL outperforms SQL due to the more complex grammar of SQL and the structure information provided by FunQL's brackets, but provides no detailed analysis or empirical evidence.

### Open Question 2
What specific aspects of multilingual large language models (e.g., Codex, BLOOM) limit their effectiveness in cross-lingual semantic parsing tasks? The paper identifies these models as inadequate but doesn't delve into the specific limitations or characteristics that lead to this outcome.

### Open Question 3
How does the language family proximity between the source and target languages affect the performance of cross-lingual transfer learning in semantic parsing? While the paper observes that German (closer to English in language family) has smaller performance loss than Chinese, it doesn't provide comprehensive analysis or quantification of this impact.

## Limitations

- Potential data contamination issues in multilingual datasets, particularly for datasets like MTOP that have been split across multiple settings
- Limited evaluation scope covering only 3 model architectures and 6 specific settings, leaving performance of other model types unknown
- Cross-lingual few-shot results based on relatively small sample sizes (10% of target language data) with unclear scalability to larger datasets

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** Encoder-decoder models (specifically mT5) consistently outperform other model architectures across monolingual settings; Multilingual training improves average performance compared to monolingual training; Cross-lingual zero-shot transfer shows significant performance gaps compared to monolingual training

**Medium Confidence:** Cross-lingual few-shot training effectively reduces the performance gap between monolingual and cross-lingual transfer; The XSemPLR benchmark provides comprehensive coverage of cross-lingual semantic parsing tasks; Encoder-based models with pointer networks perform better than standard encoders for certain datasets

**Low Confidence:** Specific performance rankings between individual model variants (e.g., mT5 vs mBART); Generalizability of results to languages and meaning representations not covered in the benchmark; Long-term stability and effectiveness of cross-lingual few-shot learning approaches

## Next Checks

1. **Data Contamination Verification:** Conduct an independent audit of the dataset splits to verify the absence of data leakage, particularly for datasets like MTOP that have been reused across multiple settings.

2. **Robustness Testing:** Evaluate model performance across different few-shot sampling strategies (e.g., random vs. stratified sampling) and varying few-shot proportions (5%, 20%, 50%) to determine the optimal configuration for cross-lingual transfer.

3. **Cross-Representation Generalization:** Test the models on unseen meaning representations (e.g., AMR, UCCA) that are not included in the XSemPLR benchmark to assess the models' ability to generalize beyond the 8 represented forms.
