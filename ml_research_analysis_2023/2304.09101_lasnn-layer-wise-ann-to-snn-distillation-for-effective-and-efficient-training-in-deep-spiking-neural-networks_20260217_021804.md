# LaSNN: Layer-wise ANN-to-SNN Distillation for Effective and Efficient Training in Deep Spiking Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09101
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09101
- Reference count: 35
- Key outcome: Introduces layer-wise ANN-to-SNN distillation framework achieving competitive accuracy with 20x faster inference than converted SNNs.

## Executive Summary
LaSNN addresses the accuracy-latency trade-off in deep Spiking Neural Networks by transferring knowledge from pre-trained ANNs through layer-wise attention-based distillation. The framework uses a three-stage process: train a teacher ANN, convert it to an initial SNN via threshold balancing, and refine the SNN through layer-wise distillation using activation or gradient-based attention schemes. Experimental results on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet demonstrate that LaSNN achieves competitive top-1 accuracy compared to ANNs while significantly reducing inference latency.

## Method Summary
LaSNN employs a novel three-stage approach to train deep SNNs by transferring knowledge from well-trained ANNs. First, a teacher ANN is trained without batch normalization using ReLU activations. Second, this ANN is converted to an initial SNN using threshold balancing normalization, creating a student SNN with IF neurons and shared thresholds. Third, layer-wise distillation refines the SNN by computing attention maps from the teacher ANN's activations or gradients and applying an attention-based loss function during backpropagation. The framework supports both activation-based attention (faster, less memory) and gradient-based attention (potentially richer, more expensive) schemes, with the option to use Poisson spike encoding or direct input currents.

## Key Results
- LaSNN achieves competitive top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet compared to ANNs
- Inference latency is 20x faster than converted SNNs with similar performance
- The framework is flexible and works across different architectures, depths, and input encoding methods
- Activation-based attention distillation is more computationally efficient than gradient-based while maintaining comparable accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Layer-wise attention distillation bridges the information gap between ANNs and SNNs by transferring intermediate feature-level knowledge.
- Mechanism: Instead of converting weights directly, the teacher ANN's intermediate activation patterns are distilled to the student SNN through an attention-based loss. This allows the SNN to learn how to activate neurons in a way that mimics the ANN's spatial attention at each layer.
- Core assumption: Attention maps (spatial or gradient-based) can be meaningfully compared between ANNs and SNNs despite their different signal representations.
- Evidence anchors: The information gap between heterogeneous ANN and SNN is bridged by introducing the attention scheme, the knowledge in an ANN is effectively compressed and then efficiently transferred by utilizing our layer-wise distillation paradigm.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using activation-based attention instead of spike-based knowledge improves training stability and accuracy.
- Mechanism: Activation-based attention distills the spatial importance of activations (squared and averaged across channels) from the teacher ANN to the student SNN, which is more stable than spike-based distillation that accumulates losses over time steps.
- Core assumption: Squared activation magnitudes provide a robust surrogate for spatial importance that the SNN can learn to approximate.
- Evidence anchors: After introducing attention information for the activation tensor of teacher and student models, we have a new attention loss term Lat(Ta,St).

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hybrid initialization + layer-wise refinement yields better accuracy-latency tradeoff than pure conversion.
- Mechanism: First, the student SNN is initialized by converting an intermediate ANN (threshold balancing, no bias). Then, layer-wise distillation refines the SNN parameters to match the teacher ANN's attention patterns. This combines the stability of conversion with the fine-grained learning of distillation.
- Core assumption: Converting an intermediate ANN provides a better starting point than random initialization for subsequent attention-based refinement.
- Evidence anchors: Utilizing both the accuracy advantages of ANNs and the computing efficiency of SNNs, a novel SNN training framework is proposed, namely layer-wise ANN-to-SNN knowledge distillation (LaSNN).

  - Why needed here: The initialization step of LaSNN relies on converting an intermediate ANN to SNN; understanding this process is key to setting up the student SNN correctly.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher ANN (pre-trained) -> Intermediate ANN (smaller) -> Student SNN (LIF neurons) -> Layer-wise attention distillation
- Critical path: ANN training → ANN-to-SNN conversion (init) → Layer-wise attention distillation → Optimized SNN
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Attention type: Activation-based (faster, less memory) vs. gradient-based (potentially richer, more expensive)
  - Time steps: More steps improve accuracy but increase latency and energy cost
  - Teacher size: Larger teacher provides richer attention maps but increases computation
- Failure signatures:
  - Distillation loss plateaus early → attention maps incompatible or learning rate too low
  - SNN accuracy worse than converted baseline → initialization poor or attention loss misaligned
  - GPU OOM → gradient-based attention with long time steps; switch to activation-based or reduce time steps
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a small teacher ANN (e.g., VGG5) on CIFAR-10, convert to SNN, evaluate baseline accuracy with 100 time steps
  2. Apply LaSNN with activation-based attention, same time steps, compare accuracy and training time
  3. Switch to gradient-based attention, monitor GPU memory usage and accuracy; if memory too high, reduce time steps or revert to activation-based

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the gradient-based attention scheme in LaSNN compare to activation-based attention in terms of computational efficiency and accuracy across different network depths and architectures?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that the gradient-based scheme is more resource-intensive and achieves weaker performance compared to the activation-based scheme, but it also mentions that with fewer time steps, the memory requirements of the gradient-based scheme can be reduced to the same level as the activation-based scheme.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides a comparison between the two schemes, it does not explore the trade-offs in detail or investigate the performance of the gradient-based scheme with fewer time steps in various network architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance and computational efficiency of both schemes across different network depths and architectures with varying time steps.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different teacher ANN architectures on the performance of LaSNN, and how does the depth and structure of the teacher network influence the effectiveness of the layer-wise distillation process?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that LaSNN can effectively transfer knowledge from various ANNs to SNNs and is flexible for different architectures/depths, but it does not provide a detailed analysis of how different teacher ANN architectures affect the performance of LaSNN.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the relationship between the teacher ANN architecture and the effectiveness of the layer-wise distillation process in depth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed experiments comparing the performance of LaSNN with different teacher ANN architectures and analyzing the impact of their depth and structure on the distillation process.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of input encoding method (Poisson rate encoding vs. direct input encoding) affect the performance and efficiency of LaSNN in terms of accuracy, latency, and energy consumption?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the impact of input encoding on the average spike rate and mentions that direct input encoding reduces the time steps required to encode the input, but it does not provide a comprehensive comparison of the two methods in terms of accuracy, latency, and energy consumption.
- Why unresolved: While the paper touches on the effects of input encoding, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how the choice of encoding method impacts the overall performance and efficiency of LaSNN.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance, latency, and energy consumption of LaSNN with different input encoding methods across various network architectures and datasets.

## Limitations
- Limited ablation studies on attention map quality and compatibility between ANN activations and SNN spikes
- No explicit wall-clock timing comparisons to validate the claimed 20x faster inference
- Limited exploration of architectural variations beyond standard CNN backbones

- Claims about framework flexibility across architectures: Medium confidence
