# Cheap and Deterministic Inference for Deep State-Space Models of Interacting Dynamical Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01773
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01773

- Primary result: Novel deterministic moment matching approach for deep state-space models of interacting agents, demonstrating improved efficiency and accuracy over Monte Carlo sampling methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for modeling stochastic dynamical systems with interacting agents by combining graph neural networks with deep state-space models. The key innovation is a deterministic moment matching scheme that replaces computationally expensive Monte Carlo sampling during training, enabling efficient and stable inference. The method employs structured approximations to covariance matrices to handle systems with many agents, making it scalable to real-world applications. Experiments on autonomous driving datasets demonstrate superior predictive accuracy and uncertainty quantification compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed method employs graph neural networks within a deep state-space model framework to capture interactions between agents in dynamical systems. A novel deterministic moment matching scheme is introduced for efficient training, avoiding the computational burden of Monte Carlo sampling. To handle systems with many agents, structured approximations to the covariance matrices of Gaussian mixture model components are proposed. This combination enables scalable, accurate modeling of interacting systems while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods in predictive accuracy and uncertainty quantification on autonomous driving datasets
- Demonstrates superior computational efficiency compared to Monte Carlo sampling alternatives
- Shows good generalization to unseen traffic environments
- Enables modeling of systems with many agents through structured covariance approximations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The deterministic moment matching scheme works by directly computing the required moments of the posterior distribution rather than relying on sampling-based approximations. This eliminates the variance inherent in Monte Carlo methods and provides more stable gradients for training. The graph neural network component effectively captures local interaction patterns between agents, while the structured covariance approximations maintain computational tractability even as the number of agents grows. This combination allows the model to learn rich representations of system dynamics while remaining computationally feasible.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Input Observations -> Graph Neural Network -> Latent State Evolution -> Structured Covariance Approximations -> Moment Matching Loss -> Parameter Updates

Critical Path:
The critical computational path involves message passing through the GNN to compute agent interactions, followed by latent state updates and covariance computations. The moment matching loss then provides deterministic gradients for backpropagation through this entire pipeline.

Design Tradeoffs:
The primary tradeoff is between computational efficiency and modeling fidelity. The deterministic approach sacrifices some flexibility compared to sampling-based methods but gains substantial efficiency. The structured covariance approximations further trade off expressive power for scalability. These choices enable practical application to large systems while maintaining reasonable accuracy.

Failure Signatures:
Potential failure modes include poor capture of long-range dependencies due to local GNN aggregation, breakdown of structured covariance approximations in highly correlated systems, and optimization difficulties when moment matching becomes ill-conditioned. The method may also struggle with non-Gaussian noise distributions.
