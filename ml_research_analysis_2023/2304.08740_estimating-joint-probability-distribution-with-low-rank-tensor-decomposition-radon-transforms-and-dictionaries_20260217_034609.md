# Estimating Joint Probability Distribution With Low-Rank Tensor Decomposition, Radon Transforms and Dictionaries

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08740
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08740
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Novel RAD algorithm for joint probability density estimation using Radon transforms and dictionary representations achieves lowest Jensen-Shannon Divergence across various density families

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RAD, a novel algorithm for estimating joint probability densities from data samples by leveraging the structure of low-rank tensor decomposition combined with Radon transforms and dictionary representations. The approach assumes the underlying distribution can be decomposed as a mixture of product densities with few mixture components. By using 1-D marginals instead of 2-D marginals, RAD significantly reduces sample complexity while maintaining estimation accuracy. The method is evaluated on synthetic probability densities and demonstrates superior performance compared to previous dictionary-based approaches and Gaussian Mixture Models across various families of densities including Gaussian, Laplacian, and mixtures of continuous and discrete features.

## Method Summary
The RAD algorithm combines dictionary representations of 1-D densities with random projections to estimate joint distributions from 1-D marginals. The method exploits the assumption that the underlying density can be decomposed as a mixture of product densities with few components. Random projections are used to map high-dimensional data to lower dimensions while preserving essential structure, and dictionaries are employed to efficiently represent the 1-D marginal densities. This combination allows the algorithm to leverage the benefits of low-rank tensor decomposition while achieving improved sample complexity compared to previous dictionary-based methods like JUPAD.

## Key Results
- RAD achieves the lowest Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) in both low and high sample regimes
- Outperforms JUPAD and Gaussian Mixture Models across Gaussian, Laplacian, and mixed continuous-discrete density families
- The use of 1-D marginals instead of 2-D marginals significantly lowers sample complexity requirements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm works by exploiting the low-rank structure of the joint probability density through tensor decomposition. By using Radon transforms (random projections), the method can efficiently compute marginals in lower dimensions, which are then reconstructed using dictionary representations. This approach effectively reduces the curse of dimensionality by focusing on the essential structure of the distribution rather than requiring samples from the full joint space. The mixture of product densities assumption allows for a parsimonious representation that captures the key features of the distribution while remaining computationally tractable.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Data samples -> Random projections -> 1-D marginals -> Dictionary learning -> Joint density reconstruction

**Critical path:** Data acquisition → Random projection → Marginal estimation → Dictionary representation → Tensor decomposition → Joint density reconstruction

**Design tradeoffs:** The algorithm trades off between model complexity and sample efficiency by assuming low-rank structure. This assumption enables reduced sample complexity but may limit applicability to distributions that don't conform to this structure.

**Failure signatures:** Poor performance when the underlying density cannot be well-approximated by a mixture of product densities, or when the number of mixture components is large, violating the low-rank assumption.

## Limitations
- Assumes underlying density can be decomposed as a mixture of product densities with few components, limiting applicability to distributions that don't conform to this structure
- Performance evaluation restricted to synthetic data, leaving uncertainty about real-world applicability
- No computational complexity analysis or runtime comparisons provided

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The mathematical framework combining Radon transforms with dictionary representations is well-established and rigorously presented. The theoretical justification for using 1-D marginals to improve sample complexity is sound.

**Medium Confidence:** Synthetic experiment results are promising but may not fully represent real-world scenarios. The improvement over JUPAD and GMMs is demonstrated but the relative advantage in practical applications remains to be seen.

**Low Confidence:** Generalizability to distributions that don't conform to the low-rank mixture assumption and performance on high-dimensional real-world data remain uncertain.
