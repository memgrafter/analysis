# Taming AI Bots: Controllability of Neural States in Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18449
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18449
- Reference count: 40
- Defines formal framework for analyzing controllability in LLMs through meaning equivalence classes

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a formal framework for analyzing controllability in large language models by defining meaning operationally through equivalence classes of sentences. The authors establish theoretical conditions under which LLMs used as conversational AI bots can be controlled, showing that well-trained models are controllable within the space of meanings. The work connects theoretical controllability to practical concerns about adversarial steering and bot safety, while also proposing the use of LLMs themselves as reward models for reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

## Method Summary
The paper formalizes LLM controllability by defining meaningful sentences as equivalence classes and establishing when well-trained models can reach any meaningful sentence with high probability. It characterizes conditions for attentive bots to be controllable, including necessary and sufficient conditions for such controllability. The framework introduces meaning attribution mechanisms and explores using LLMs as reward models in RLHF to potentially eliminate separate reward and policy models.

## Key Results
- A well-trained LLM is theoretically controllable within the space of meanings
- Well-trained attentive bots satisfy necessary and sufficient conditions for controllability
- Meaning attribution is highly sensitive to prompt choice, with fine-tuned LLMs functioning better than pre-trained ones

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by establishing mathematical conditions under which LLMs can transition between meaningful states in the space of equivalence classes. Controllability emerges from the theoretical properties of well-trained models that can reach any meaningful sentence with high probability. The mechanism relies on meaning attribution to map neural states to semantic equivalence classes, enabling formal analysis of state transitions and control strategies.

Failure signatures: Inconsistent meaning attribution, inability to reach target meanings, prompt sensitivity failures

## Limitations
- Operational definition of "meaning" through equivalence classes may not capture full semantic complexity
- Theoretical assumptions about LLM capabilities lack extensive empirical validation across architectures
- Practical implementation of safety safeguards against adversarial steering not demonstrated

## Confidence

- Practical applicability of framework to real-world bot control: Low confidence
