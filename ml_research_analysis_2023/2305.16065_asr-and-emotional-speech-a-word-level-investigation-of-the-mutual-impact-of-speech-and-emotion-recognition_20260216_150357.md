# ASR and Emotional Speech: A Word-Level Investigation of the Mutual Impact of Speech and Emotion Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16065
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16065
- Reference count: 0
- One-line primary result: Word-level analysis reveals that ASR performance on emotional speech is significantly impacted by Part-of-Speech tags, affective scores, and utterance length, with nouns and short utterances showing particularly high Word Error Rates.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the mutual impact of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) through a comprehensive word-level analysis. Using four ASR models (Kaldi, wav2vec2, Conformer, Whisper) and three emotion corpora (IEMOCAP, MOSI, MELD), the research reveals that ASR performance is significantly affected by word-level characteristics such as Part-of-Speech tags, affective scores, and utterance length. The findings show that nouns and short utterances particularly increase Word Error Rates (WERs), while SER accuracy degrades substantially with higher WER levels, dropping steeply between 15%-25% WER.

## Method Summary
The study employs four ASR models to generate transcripts from three emotion corpora, then analyzes word-level error patterns across Part-of-Speech tags, affective scores (Valence, Arousal, Dominance), and utterance lengths. WER distributions are computed for each model-corpus combination, and a BERT-based SER model is trained on ground truth transcripts and evaluated on progressively corrupted ASR transcripts. Confidence scores from ASR models are analyzed and calibrated to assess their impact on SER performance.

## Key Results
- ASR performance is significantly impacted by Part-of-Speech tags, with nouns showing a 74% increase in Class Error Rate compared to verbs
- Utterance length strongly modulates WER, with utterances ≤10 words showing 59.6% WER versus 25% for utterances ≥30 words
- SER accuracy degrades substantially with higher WER, dropping steeply between 15%-25% WER levels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Word-level Part-of-Speech (PoS) distribution significantly impacts ASR performance on emotional speech.
- Mechanism: Certain PoS classes (e.g., Nouns) are inherently more difficult for ASR to recognize, and emotional speech alters the relative frequency of these classes, thereby degrading overall ASR accuracy.
- Core assumption: The difficulty of recognizing different PoS tags is consistent across emotional and neutral speech.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] The study investigates how ASR performance is affected by word-level characteristics such as Part-of-Speech tags.
  - [section] "Noun saw a 74% increase in CER (33.3 to 58.0) compared to 33% for Verb (26.0 to 34.5)" and "ASR performs better on verbs than expected based on verb frequency."
  - [corpus] Data from IEMOCAP, MOSI, and MELD show varying PoS distributions across emotional states.
- Break condition: If PoS recognition difficulty changes dramatically between emotional states, or if the ASR model learns to compensate for PoS-based error patterns, this mechanism would weaken.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Utterance length modulates ASR error rates, with shorter utterances generally yielding higher Word Error Rates (WERs).
- Mechanism: Shorter utterances provide less context for the ASR model to disambiguate words, leading to increased recognition errors.
- Core assumption: The contextual benefits of longer utterances are consistent across emotional speech.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] Mentions "short utterances particularly increase Word Error Rates (WERs)."
  - [section] "The shorter the utterances, the higher the WER, regardless of corpus" and data showing 59.6% WER for utterances ≤10 words vs. 25% for utterances ≥30 words.
  - [corpus] Consistent patterns across IEMOCAP, MOSI, and MELD corpora.
- Break condition: If emotional speech inherently provides more disambiguating cues in shorter utterances, or if the ASR model is specifically trained to handle short utterances better, this mechanism would break.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Confidence scores from ASR models vary significantly across emotions and require careful calibration for reliable SER.
- Mechanism: Raw confidence scores are influenced by emotion-specific acoustic characteristics and utterance length, necessitating calibration to ensure equitable treatment across emotional states.
- Core assumption: Confidence scores are interpretable across different ASR models and emotional contexts without calibration.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Different ASR models show varying confidence scores that change with emotion and utterance length."
  - [section] "The scores of every emotion increases with utterance length in MELD but barely changes in MOSI" and "Calibration also adjusts the overconfident behavior of end-to-end ASR models."
  - [corpus] Comparison of CONF and KLA models with and without calibration on IEMOCAP.
- Break condition: If confidence calibration introduces systematic biases or if SER systems can effectively use uncalibrated scores, this mechanism would weaken.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different ASR models perform on specific emotion classes, and what are the underlying reasons for these performance differences?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper analyzes ASR performance on different emotions (angry, happy, neutral, sad) and mentions that neutral speech often has higher WER than expected due to the high ratio of short utterances.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies that neutral speech has higher WER than expected, it doesn't provide a detailed analysis of why each ASR model performs differently on specific emotion classes. The paper also doesn't explore the acoustic characteristics of emotional speech that might contribute to these differences.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed comparative analysis of ASR performance on each emotion class, including acoustic feature analysis (e.g., prosody, pitch, energy) and word-level characteristics specific to each emotion.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can confidence scores be effectively utilized in SER systems to improve emotion recognition accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the variation in confidence scores across different ASR models and emotions, and mentions that confidence scores can help identify words of high recognition quality. However, it also notes that the measurement and use of confidence scores is a complicated task.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that confidence scores vary with emotion and ASR model, but it doesn't provide a concrete method for using these scores to improve SER accuracy. The paper also doesn't explore the impact of confidence score calibration on SER performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing SER performance using different confidence score utilization strategies (e.g., weighted attention, word filtering) and the impact of confidence score calibration on SER accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different language models perform on imperfect ASR transcripts in SER tasks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the accuracy drop in SER with increasing WER may be due to the use of a different language model (BERT) compared to previous studies. It suggests exploring how different language models function on imperfect ASR transcripts.
- Why unresolved: The paper only uses BERT as the language model for SER and doesn't compare its performance with other language models or feature extraction methods on imperfect ASR transcripts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing SER performance using different language models (e.g., Bag-of-Words, other transformer-based models) and feature extraction methods on ASR transcripts with varying WERs.

## Limitations
- The study focuses on word-level analysis but doesn't explore phrase-level or semantic context effects on ASR performance
- The analysis is limited to four ASR models and three emotion corpora, which may not capture all variations in emotional speech patterns
- The relationship between confidence score calibration and SER performance improvement needs further exploration

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: The core finding that ASR performance degrades with emotional speech, particularly for nouns and short utterances, is well-supported by the data across multiple corpora and ASR models.
**Medium Confidence**: The relationship between WER and SER accuracy degradation is clearly demonstrated, though the exact threshold where performance drops precipitously may vary with different SER architectures.
**Medium Confidence**: The analysis of confidence score variations across emotions is supported, but the practical implications for real-world SER systems need further exploration.
