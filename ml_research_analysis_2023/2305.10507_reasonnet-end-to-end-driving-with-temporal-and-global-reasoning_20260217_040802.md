# ReasonNet: End-to-End Driving with Temporal and Global Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10507
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10507
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: End-to-end autonomous driving framework incorporating temporal and global reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art performance on CARLA benchmarks with driving score of 71.89 and infraction score of 58.73

## Executive Summary
This paper presents ReasonNet, an end-to-end autonomous driving framework that addresses urban dense traffic scenarios by incorporating temporal and global reasoning capabilities. The method specifically targets the challenges of predicting future scene evolution and handling occluded objects. ReasonNet processes multi-modal sensor data to generate BEV features, waypoints, and occupancy maps, while leveraging temporal reasoning to aggregate information across frames and global reasoning to model interactions among objects and the environment.

The authors introduce a new benchmark called DriveOcclusionSim for comprehensive evaluation of occlusion events, and demonstrate that ReasonNet outperforms all prior methods on multiple CARLA benchmarks, achieving the highest driving score and infraction score on the public CARLA Leaderboard.

## Method Summary
ReasonNet is a two-stage trained end-to-end autonomous driving framework that processes multi-view multi-modal sensor readings (RGB cameras and LiDAR) to generate BEV features, traffic sign features, and waypoints. The framework includes a perception module using ResNet-50 for images, PointPillars for LiDAR, and a transformer-based BEV decoder. A temporal reasoning module processes current and historic features through a memory bank with short-term (Ts=4) and long-term (Tl=2) buffers, using attention mechanisms to compute similarity between current queries and historic keys. A global reasoning module employs a graph attention network to model object-environment and object-object interactions, generating occupancy maps and applying consistency loss to ensure waypoint and occupancy map predictions align.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on CARLA benchmarks with driving score of 71.89 and infraction score of 58.73
- Outperforms all prior methods on the public CARLA Leaderboard
- Demonstrates superior occlusion handling through DriveOcclusionSim benchmark evaluation
- Shows significant improvements in success rate (44.44% vs 26.85%) and route completion (90.63% vs 85.37%) on DOS benchmark compared to LSS

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Temporal reasoning enables high-fidelity future motion forecasting by aggregating information across frames
- Mechanism: The model stores historic feature keys and values in a memory bank, computes similarity between current queries and historic keys, and aggregates values weighted by similarity. This allows reasoning about objects that may be intermittently occluded or temporarily outside the field of view
- Core assumption: Objects' future behavior can be inferred by observing their past behavior and relationships with other objects
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By reasoning on the temporal behavior of objects, our method can effectively process the interactions and relationships among features in different frames"
  - [section 3.2] "Our observation is that, while humans also suffer from similar limitations to autonomous vehicles regarding occluded objects, they are able to reason about these unobservable spaces by exploiting global information of the scene such as road geometry and driving interaction patterns"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Global reasoning improves perception performance and helps detect occluded objects by modeling interactions between objects and the environment
- Mechanism: A graph attention network takes object features (extracted from regions with high object probability) and environment features, models their interactions, and uses the resulting features for traffic sign prediction and occupancy forecasting
- Core assumption: Objects' behavior and the environment provide clues about occluded objects through interaction patterns
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Reasoning about the global information of the scene can also improve overall perception performance and benefit the detection of adverse events, especially the anticipation of potential danger from occluded objects"
  - [section 3.3] "Our insight is that humans perceive their surroundings not only through sensors, but also by exploiting global information on the scene to reason over the unobservable spaces"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The consistency loss ensures that predicted waypoints do not overlap with high-probability obstacle regions in the occupancy map
- Mechanism: The loss term minimizes the average object existence probability at waypoint locations and maximizes the trajectory length, encouraging longer, safer paths that avoid obstacles
- Core assumption: Waypoints and occupancy maps should be consistent, and longer trajectories are more efficient
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "the consistency loss also encourages generating longer waypoint trajectories for efficient driving"
  - [section 3.5] "The consistency loss aims at minimizing the average object existence probability of the cells that cover the predicted waypoints, and maximizing the average l2 length of the waypoint trajectory"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Perception -> Temporal Reasoning -> Global Reasoning -> Control
- Critical path: The perception module generates features that are processed by the temporal reasoning module, which then feeds into the global reasoning module for final predictions and control
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory buffer sizes (Ts and Tl) vs. computational resources and ability to track long-term object behavior
  - Attention mechanism vs. simpler feature concatenation for temporal processing
  - Consistency loss weight vs. other loss terms for balancing different objectives
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on occlusion scenarios when memory buffers are too small
  - Inconsistent predictions between waypoints and occupancy maps when consistency loss is not properly tuned
  - Suboptimal driving behavior when global reasoning is not capturing relevant interactions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate performance with different short-term buffer sizes (Ts) to find the optimal tradeoff between performance and computational cost
  2. Test the impact of removing the consistency loss to quantify its contribution to safe driving behavior
  3. Compare the performance with and without the long-term memory buffer to assess the importance of tracking long-term object behavior

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the ReasonNet's performance scale with longer time horizons for occlusion prediction beyond the current 0.5 second intervals?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions predicting waypoints and occupancy maps for Tf = 4 time steps with 0.5 second intervals, but does not explore longer prediction horizons
- Why unresolved: The paper does not report experiments with extended prediction horizons to test the limits of temporal reasoning capabilities
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing ReasonNet's performance on longer time horizons (e.g., 2-4 seconds) for occlusion prediction and waypoint generation

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the computational overhead of the temporal and global reasoning modules compared to simpler end-to-end driving methods?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper introduces complex temporal and global reasoning modules with memory banks and transformer-based architectures, but does not report inference time or computational requirements
- Why unresolved: No runtime performance metrics or comparisons to simpler baseline methods are provided
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed timing analysis showing latency and computational requirements of ReasonNet versus baseline methods

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the ReasonNet generalize to completely unseen occlusion scenarios not covered in the DOS benchmark?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The DOS benchmark covers specific occlusion scenarios (#1-#4), but the paper does not test on novel occlusion types
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates on predefined occlusion scenarios and does not test generalization to new occlusion patterns
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing ReasonNet on completely novel occlusion scenarios not present in the training data or DOS benchmark

## Limitations

- Memory Buffer Design Uncertainty: The paper provides limited details on optimal configuration of short-term (Ts=4) and long-term (Tl=2) memory buffers
- Occlusion Detection Granularity: The global reasoning module's ability to detect occluded objects relies on interaction patterns, but quantitative analysis of detection accuracy for specific occlusion types is lacking
- Generalization Across Environments: While achieving state-of-the-art performance on CARLA benchmarks, the extent of real-world generalization remains uncertain

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The temporal reasoning mechanism's contribution to motion forecasting and the overall architecture's ability to outperform prior methods on CARLA benchmarks

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of global reasoning for occlusion detection and the consistency loss's role in improving safe driving behavior, based on ablation studies and benchmark results

**Low Confidence**: The model's performance on occlusion scenarios not represented in the DriveOcclusionSim benchmark and its ability to generalize to completely unseen urban environments
