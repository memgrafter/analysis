# Self-building Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01086
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01086
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: SBNN outperforms traditional neural networks on OpenAI control tasks, especially with >80% weight pruning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Self-building Neural Networks (SBNNs), a novel approach that simulates brain synaptogenesis by starting with fully connected networks with zero weights and growing synapses through Hebbian learning. During task execution, connections are activated based on experience, and at a predetermined episode, weak connections are pruned. The resulting network structure adapts to the task, enabling better performance and generalization compared to traditional neural networks, particularly when high pruning rates are applied.

## Method Summary
The SBNN starts with a fully connected network initialized with zero weights. During task execution, Hebbian learning grows synapses based on the ABCD rule (w_ij = w_ij + η(Aa_i + Ba_j + Ca_ia_j + D)), where each connection has its own ABCD parameters. At a predetermined pruning episode (PT), connections below a global magnitude threshold are removed. Topological sort and cycle detection then define the activation order for the pruned network. The entire architecture, including ABCD parameters and pruning timing, is optimized using Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES).

## Key Results
- SBNNs outperform traditional neural networks on OpenAI control tasks (Cart Pole, Mountain Car, Lunar Lander)
- Performance advantage is particularly pronounced when over 80% of weights are pruned
- SBNNs show better generalization to unseen tasks compared to traditional neural networks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SBNN outperforms traditional NNs under high pruning rates (>80%)
- Mechanism: Hebbian learning activates task-relevant connections, then pruning removes weak ones, adapting network structure
- Core assumption: Pruning after learning doesn't degrade performance because important connections are already identified
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states SBNNs adapt better especially when over 80% pruned; section notes smaller performance decay than NNs
- Break condition: Early pruning before learning relevant connections causes significant performance degradation

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SBNNs generalize better to unseen tasks than traditional NNs
- Mechanism: Pruning removes redundant connections, leaving efficient task-specific structure that adapts better to new tasks
- Core assumption: Pruned networks with relevant connections are more adaptable than fully connected networks
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section both state SBNNs adapt better to new tasks when over 80% pruned
- Break condition: Insufficient pruning leaves network too complex to realize generalization benefits

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Network structure adapts to task, creating different structures for different tasks
- Mechanism: Hebbian learning grows synapses based on experience, pruning removes irrelevant ones, resulting in task-specific structure
- Core assumption: Task-specific activation during Hebbian learning leads to task-specific structure after pruning
- Evidence anchors: Abstract notes network builds itself during task execution; section describes capability to compose structure based on experience
- Break condition: Ineffective pruning fails to remove irrelevant connections, preventing optimal task adaptation

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Hebbian learning
  - Why needed here: Enables synapses to grow based on experience, activating frequently used connections for task adaptation
  - Quick check question: How does Hebbian learning differ from traditional supervised learning in neural networks?

- Concept: Synaptogenesis and pruning
  - Why needed here: Synaptogenesis grows synapses while pruning removes weak ones, fundamental to SBNN's adaptive structure
  - Quick check question: What is the role of pruning in the brain, and how is it simulated in the SBNN?

- Concept: Topological sort and cycle detection
  - Why needed here: Defines activation order for pruned networks that may contain cycles
  - Quick check question: Why is it important to define an activation order for the network after pruning?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer -> Hidden layer(s) -> Output layer
- Critical path: 1) Initialize zero weights, 2) Apply Hebbian learning during task, 3) Prune at episode PT, 4) Define activation order via topological sort, 5) Use pruned network
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pruning rate: Higher rates increase efficiency but risk removing important connections
  - Pruning time: Early pruning removes connections before learning; late pruning reduces benefits
  - Hidden nodes: More nodes increase capacity but raise computational cost
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance: Pruning too early or rate too high
  - Task unsolvable: Inappropriate network structure or excessive connection removal
  - Training instability: High Hebbian learning rate or network too complex
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare SBNN with low pruning rate (20%) to traditional NN on Cart Pole
  2. Vary pruning time on Lunar Lander to observe effects
  3. Test SBNN generalization by training on one task, evaluating on another

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does pruning affect long-term memory and generalization in SBNNs compared to traditional NNs?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention of SBNNs adapting better to new tasks with over 80% pruning and performance boosts of 7-13% (Mountain Car) and 6-100% (Lunar Lander)
- Why unresolved: Paper demonstrates pruning improves performance but doesn't investigate long-term memory retention or multi-task adaptation over time
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing SBNN and traditional NN performance on multiple unseen tasks over extended periods, measuring both performance and memory retention

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does SBNN scale to complex tasks with larger input/action spaces like soft robot control?
- Basis in paper: Explicit mention of plans to test SBNN on more complex tasks including soft robot control with larger input/action spaces
- Why unresolved: Current experiments limited to three classical control tasks with small input/action spaces; unclear how SBNN performs on complex tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying SBNN to complex tasks with larger input/action spaces like soft robot control, comparing performance to traditional NNs and state-of-the-art models

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does choice of Hebbian learning rule affect SBNN performance and structure?
- Basis in paper: Uses ABCD model but mentions various Hebbian models exist and each connection has its own ABCD parameters
- Why unresolved: Paper doesn't explore effects of different Hebbian learning rules on performance and structure
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing SBNN performance using different Hebbian learning rules (ABCD, Oja's rule, BCM rule) on various tasks, measuring adaptation and generalization

## Limitations

- Limited experimental methodology details, particularly CMA-ES hyperparameter settings and activation function specifications
- Generalization claims based on single experiment with limited scope, reducing confidence
- Pruning effectiveness unclear across different network architectures and task complexities
- Comparison with traditional NNs doesn't account for potential architectural differences
- Lacks ablation studies to isolate Hebbian learning versus pruning contributions

## Confidence

- High confidence in pruning mechanism's effectiveness when properly timed
- Medium confidence in generalization claims due to limited experimental validation
- Medium confidence in Hebbian learning benefits without detailed hyperparameter analysis
