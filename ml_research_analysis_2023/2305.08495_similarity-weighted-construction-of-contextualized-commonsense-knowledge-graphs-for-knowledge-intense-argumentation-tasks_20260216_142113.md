# Similarity-weighted Construction of Contextualized Commonsense Knowledge Graphs for Knowledge-intense Argumentation Tasks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.08495
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08495
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Unsupervised construction of contextualized commonsense knowledge graphs that improve argumentation task performance while providing interpretable knowledge injection.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method for constructing contextualized commonsense knowledge graphs (CCKGs) to enhance arguments with structured background knowledge. The approach extracts relevant knowledge paths from large knowledge graphs by computing semantic similarity between triplets and textual arguments, then combines multiple weighted shortest paths into a CCKG. The method is evaluated both intrinsically against manually created explanation graphs and extrinsically in a knowledge-intense argumentation task predicting validity and novelty of conclusions.

## Method Summary
The approach constructs CCKGs by first identifying relevant knowledge paths from a large knowledge graph (Wikidata) through semantic similarity computation between triplets and textual arguments. Multiple weighted shortest paths are then combined to form the contextualized graph. The method is unsupervised and aims to provide interpretable, traceable knowledge injection while maintaining competitive task performance. Evaluation includes intrinsic assessment against manually created explanation graphs and extrinsic evaluation in predicting conclusion validity and novelty for argumentation tasks.

## Key Results
- CCKGs achieve strong performance in concept and triplet metrics when compared to manually created explanation graphs
- Manual annotation confirms high recall and precision of implicit commonsense knowledge extraction
- In extrinsic evaluation, CCKGs excel at predicting validity and novelty of conclusions, outperforming strong baselines and rivaling GPT-3 based systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging semantic similarity between argument text and knowledge graph triplets to identify relevant background knowledge paths. By computing weighted shortest paths and combining them, the approach captures multiple relevant knowledge routes that support argumentation. The unsupervised nature allows flexible adaptation to different contexts while maintaining interpretability through explicit knowledge graph structures.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
Knowledge Graph (Wikidata) -> Semantic Similarity Module -> Path Extraction Engine -> Weighted Shortest Path Combiner -> CCKG Construction -> Task Integration

**Critical Path:**
Argument text → Semantic similarity computation → Path identification → Weighted shortest path calculation → CCKG construction → Task-specific application

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Coverage vs precision tradeoff in semantic similarity thresholds
- Knowledge graph size vs computational efficiency
- Path complexity vs interpretability
- Unsupervised approach vs potential for specialized domain adaptation

**Failure Signatures:**
- Overly broad knowledge extraction leading to irrelevant information
- Missed relevant knowledge due to similarity threshold settings
- Path fragmentation resulting in disconnected knowledge structures
- Domain-specific knowledge gaps in the underlying knowledge graph

## Limitations
- Intrinsic evaluation relies on manually created explanation graphs that may not capture real-world argumentation complexity
- Performance depends heavily on the underlying knowledge graph (Wikidata) and semantic similarity computation, introducing potential biases
- Unsupervised approach may struggle with highly specialized or domain-specific knowledge
- Semantic similarity thresholds could lead to either overly broad or overly narrow knowledge extraction

## Confidence

**High confidence** in the methodology for constructing CCKGs and the intrinsic evaluation metrics.
**Medium confidence** in the extrinsic evaluation results and the comparison with GPT-3 based systems.
**Low confidence** in the generalizability of the approach to highly specialized domains and the robustness of the semantic similarity thresholds.
