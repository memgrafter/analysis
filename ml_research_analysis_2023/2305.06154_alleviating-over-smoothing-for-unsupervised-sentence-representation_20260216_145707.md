# Alleviating Over-smoothing for Unsupervised Sentence Representation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06154
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06154
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: SSCL improves BERT and SimCSE performance on STS tasks by 15.68% and 1.65% respectively

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the over-smoothing problem in unsupervised sentence representation learning, where sentence representations from adjacent layers in pre-trained language models become highly similar, reducing model effectiveness. The authors propose Self-Contrastive Learning (SSCL), a simple method that samples negatives from intermediate layers of the PLM to make the final sentence representation more discriminative. SSCL can be easily extended to various state-of-the-art models and serves as a plug-and-play contrastive framework.

## Method Summary
The authors propose Self-Contrastive Learning (SSCL) to address over-smoothing in unsupervised sentence representation learning. The method samples negatives from intermediate layers of pre-trained language models to create contrastive pairs, forcing the model to produce more discriminative final representations. This approach can be easily integrated into existing models like BERT and SimCSE as a plug-and-play contrastive framework.

## Key Results
- SSCL improves performance on 7 STS tasks by 15.68% for BERT and 1.65% for SimCSE
- The method reduces vector dimension requirements while maintaining performance
- SSCL demonstrates improved ability to capture surface, syntactic, and semantic information
- The approach alleviates over-smoothing at both sentence and token levels

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SSCL addresses over-smoothing by creating contrastive learning objectives that force the model to distinguish between representations from different layers. By sampling negatives from intermediate layers, the model learns to maintain meaningful differences between representations rather than allowing them to converge to similar vectors. This preserves information across layers and creates more discriminative sentence embeddings.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks comprehensive theoretical analysis explaining why intermediate layer sampling mitigates over-smoothing
- Claims about accelerated convergence speed lack quantitative evidence or ablation studies
- Performance gains are primarily demonstrated on STS tasks, with limited analysis on more complex downstream applications

## Confidence

- High confidence: SSCL improves performance on STS and Transfer tasks when applied to BERT and SimCSE
- Medium confidence: SSCL reduces vector dimension requirements while maintaining performance
- Medium confidence: SSCL effectively alleviates over-smoothing at both sentence and token levels
- Low confidence: SSCL accelerates convergence speed (insufficient empirical support)
