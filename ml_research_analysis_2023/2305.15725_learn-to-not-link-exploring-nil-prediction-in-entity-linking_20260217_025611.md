# Learn to Not Link: Exploring NIL Prediction in Entity Linking

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15725
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15725
- Reference count: 19
- This paper introduces the NEL dataset to address NIL prediction in entity linking, categorizing NIL mentions into Missing Entity and Non-Entity Phrase.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of NIL prediction in entity linking, where mentions don't correspond to any entity in the knowledge base. The authors introduce the NEL dataset, constructed through careful selection of ambiguous entities as seeds, Wikipedia context mining, and human annotation. They categorize NIL mentions into two distinct patterns: Missing Entity (entities absent from KB) and Non-Entity Phrase (common phrases not viewed as entities). The study demonstrates that both types of NIL mentions are essential for training effective NIL prediction models, and that integrating type information through an entity typing subtask significantly improves performance.

## Method Summary
The paper constructs the NEL dataset by selecting ambiguous entities as seeds, mining mention contexts from Wikipedia, and performing human annotation and entity masking. The entity linking models use bi-encoder and cross-encoder architectures with BERT-large as the encoder base, integrating type information through a typing subtask. The models combine semantic and type similarity scores to identify NIL mentions by applying a similarity threshold. Experiments are conducted on NEL and traditional datasets (AIDA, MSNBC, WNED-WIKI) to evaluate the impact of NIL prediction on overall accuracy.

## Key Results
- NIL prediction accuracy degrades significantly when models are trained on non-NIL data alone (33.57% NIL in NEL dataset)
- Both Missing Entity and Non-Entity Phrase NIL mentions are essential for triggering effective NIL prediction ability
- Integrating entity typing subtasks improves entity linking accuracy by enabling better disambiguation between candidates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Type information improves entity linking accuracy by enabling better disambiguation between candidates.
- Mechanism: Adding a typing subtask to the model allows it to predict entity types from both context and candidate descriptions, creating a separate type similarity score that complements semantic similarity.
- Core assumption: Entity types provide orthogonal information to semantic similarity that helps distinguish between candidates with similar semantic content but different types.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We combine semantic and type similarity as the final similarity score, and identify NIL mentions by setting a similarity threshold."
  - [section 4.2] "We use the focal loss (Lin et al., 2017) on the typing task" and combine it with semantic loss.
  - [corpus] Neighbor papers like "NASTyLinker: NIL-Aware Scalable Transformer-based Entity Linker" also use type information for entity linking.
- Break condition: If the type system is too coarse-grained or if types are not predictive of the correct entity, type similarity may not provide additional benefit beyond semantic similarity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The NIL prediction problem can be decomposed into two distinct patterns: Missing Entity and Non-Entity Phrase.
- Mechanism: By categorizing NIL mentions into these two patterns, the model can learn different features for each case - Missing Entity requires recognizing entities absent from KB, while Non-Entity Phrase requires recognizing common phrases not viewed as entities.
- Core assumption: These two patterns have distinct linguistic and semantic characteristics that can be learned separately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We categorize mentions linking to NIL into Missing Entity and Non-Entity Phrase"
  - [section 2] "We propose to use the widely used bi-encoder and cross-encoder structures as the model backbone, and further integrate type information by adding an entity typing subtask."
  - [corpus] The corpus analysis shows 25 related papers on NIL prediction, suggesting this is a recognized problem area.
- Break condition: If the two patterns are not actually distinct or if they overlap significantly, separating them may not provide benefit and could complicate the learning process.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Both types of NIL mentions in training data are essential for triggering the ability of NIL prediction.
- Mechanism: Training on both Missing Entity and Non-Entity Phrase examples allows the model to learn the full range of features that distinguish NIL mentions from valid entities.
- Core assumption: The model needs exposure to both patterns to develop a complete understanding of when mentions should be linked to NIL.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "results show that both types of NIL mentions in training data have a significant influence on the accuracy of NIL prediction."
  - [section 5.2.2] "We discover that entity linking models may be unaware of the NIL mentions when there is insufficient training data."
  - [section 3.3] "NEL contains a fair number of entries, and is human-annotated to ensure correctness."
- Break condition: If one type of NIL mention is much more common in practice than the other, training on both may not be necessary and could lead to overfitting on the less common type.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (context and mention tokens) -> Encoder (BERT-large or BART-large) -> Typing layer (predicts entity types) -> Similarity scoring (semantic and type similarity) -> Threshold layer (NIL decision) -> Output (predicted entity or NIL)

- Critical path:
  1. Encode context and candidate entities
  2. Predict types for context and entities
  3. Compute semantic and type similarity scores
  4. Combine scores and apply threshold
  5. Output predicted entity or NIL

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Bi-encoder vs Cross-encoder: Bi-encoder is faster but less accurate; cross-encoder is slower but more accurate
  - Number of types: More types provide finer granularity but may introduce noise; fewer types are more robust but less discriminative
  - NIL threshold: Lower threshold increases recall but decreases precision; higher threshold does the opposite

- Failure signatures:
  - High false positive rate on NIL prediction: Threshold may be too low or model not distinguishing well between valid entities and NIL
  - High false negative rate on NIL prediction: Threshold may be too high or model not recognizing valid NIL patterns
  - Poor accuracy on entity disambiguation: Typing system may be inadequate or semantic encoder not capturing relevant features

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train and evaluate on NEL dataset with both bi-encoder and cross-encoder to establish baseline performance
  2. Add entity typing subtask and compare performance with and without type similarity
  3. Train on subsets of NIL data (only Missing Entity, only Non-Entity Phrase, both) to determine impact on NIL prediction accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would integrating entity typing into the pretraining process affect model transferability across datasets?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that integrating the entity typing task into the pretraining process may enhance model transferability and remove dependency on a fixed type system.
- Why unresolved: The paper only discusses using entity typing during fine-tuning with a fixed type system, not during pretraining.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing models with and without entity typing in pretraining, evaluated on multiple datasets with different type distributions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal granularity for the entity typing system to balance disambiguation ability and noise reduction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that a system with too many types would introduce noise to long-tail types, while insufficient types would weaken disambiguation ability.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses different type systems for different datasets but does not explore the optimal granularity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of types and measuring impact on both NIL prediction and overall entity linking accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of NIL prediction models vary with the percentage of NIL data in the training set?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that using only 25% of Non-Entity Phrase examples in training allows the NIL accuracy to recover to a decent level.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests up to 100% NIL data usage and does not explore performance at different NIL percentages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training models with varying percentages of NIL data (e.g., 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) and measuring NIL accuracy on test sets.

## Limitations
- The dataset construction methodology relies on ambiguous entity seeds and human annotation without fully disclosing annotation guidelines or inter-annotator agreement metrics
- The typing system integration lacks detailed specification of the type hierarchy and how type embeddings are learned
- The claim that both NIL patterns are essential for training is based on limited ablation studies that don't explore intermediate scenarios

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core observation that NIL prediction accuracy degrades significantly when models are trained on non-NIL data alone is well-supported by experimental results
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that type information improves entity linking accuracy has strong theoretical grounding but relies on specific implementation details that aren't fully specified
- **Low confidence**: The assertion that both NIL patterns must be present in training data for optimal performance is based on limited ablation studies
