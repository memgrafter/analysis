# Commonsense-Aware Prompting for Controllable Empathetic Dialogue Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01441
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01441
- Reference count: 8

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a framework that enhances empathetic dialogue generation by incorporating commonsense knowledge through prompt verbalization and controlling response generation using a strategy-driven future discriminator. The approach leverages COMET-BART to generate social commonsense knowledge, verbalizes it using natural language templates, and integrates it with dialogue history for pre-trained language models. Additionally, the framework employs a plug-and-play text generation control method (FUDGE) to enforce strategy-specific responses. Experiments on the ESConv dataset demonstrate that both commonsense knowledge incorporation and generation control improve dialogue response quality, with the proposed method achieving better performance than baseline models across multiple automatic evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework consists of three main components: commonsense prompting, dialogue strategy prediction, and FUDGE-controlled response generation. Commonsense prompting involves using COMET-BART to generate social commonsense relations from dialogue history, which are then verbalized using natural language templates and appended to the dialogue context. Dialogue strategy prediction can be implemented either as a separate classifier or jointly trained with the language model. The FUDGE method is used to enforce strategy control by training a future discriminator that predicts strategy categories for each token prefix, allowing the model to steer generation toward strategy-consistent responses. The framework is evaluated on the ESConv dataset, which contains 1,053 multi-turn dialogues with annotated dialogue strategies.

## Key Results
- Incorporating commonsense knowledge through prompt verbalization improves dialogue generation quality compared to baseline models
- Using FUDGE for controllable generation with predicted dialogue strategies enhances response quality
- Joint training of language model with strategy classification objective improves vanilla BART performance
- The proposed method achieves better performance than baseline models across BLEU, ROUGE-L, and BERTScore metrics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Prompt verbalization of commonsense knowledge improves empathetic dialogue generation by making implicit social reasoning explicit to the language model.
- Mechanism: COMET-BART generates commonsense entailments as relation-entailment tuples from dialogue history, which are converted into natural language sentences using templates. These verbalized prompts are concatenated to the dialogue history, providing explicit context about social relationships and emotional reactions.
- Core assumption: Language models can better utilize structured commonsense knowledge when it's presented in natural language form rather than abstract tuples.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "incorporating commonsense knowledge through prompt verbalization"; [section] "To better help the language model to comprehend the commonsense relations obtained from COMET-BART, we verbalize the commonsense tuples with natural language templates"; [corpus] Weak evidence - only mentions related work but no direct comparison of verbalized vs non-verbalized prompts
- Break condition: If verbalization templates are too verbose or introduce noise, they may overwhelm the model's attention mechanism and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Future discriminator (FUDGE) enables controllable dialogue generation by enforcing strategy-specific responses through token-level classification.
- Mechanism: A separate classifier predicts the dialogue strategy for each token prefix in the generated sequence. During decoding, tokens that don't align with the target strategy receive lower probability scores, steering generation toward strategy-consistent responses.
- Core assumption: Strategy consistency at the token level is sufficient to ensure coherent strategy adherence in the final response.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "controlling dialogue generation using a strategy-driven future discriminator"; [section] "We adopt the method of FUDGE (Yang and Klein 2021) to enforce the strategy control"; [corpus] Weak evidence - mentions FUDGE for controllable generation but no specific results for this application
- Break condition: If the strategy classifier has poor accuracy, the future discriminator will consistently steer generation in the wrong direction, producing irrelevant responses.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Joint training with both generation and strategy classification objectives improves baseline BART performance by encouraging strategy-awareness during fine-tuning.
- Mechanism: The model's loss function combines standard language modeling loss with an additional term for strategy prediction accuracy. This multi-task learning approach forces the model to develop representations that capture both semantic content and strategic intent.
- Core assumption: Strategy prediction and dialogue generation are complementary tasks that benefit from shared representations.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "We experimented using a simple dense layer module as the strategy predictor...jointly with a generation objective"; [section] "When fine-tuning the language model with a joint loss of generation and strategy classification, the performance of vanilla BART improved"; [corpus] Weak evidence - mentions multi-task learning but no specific results for this combination
- Break condition: If the strategy classification loss dominates the generation loss, the model may prioritize strategy prediction accuracy over response quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dialogue history → COMET-BART → verbalization templates → encoder → strategy prediction → FUDGE control → decoder → response generation
- Critical path: Dialogue history → COMET-BART → verbalization templates → encoder → strategy prediction → FUDGE control → decoder → response generation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Verbalization verbosity vs. information density: More detailed prompts may help comprehension but could overwhelm the model
  - Strategy predictor accuracy vs. model complexity: More sophisticated predictors may improve control but increase computational cost
  - Fine-tuning vs. plug-and-play: Fine-tuning allows better integration but requires more resources; plug-and-play offers flexibility but may have weaker control
- Failure signatures:
  - Strategy drift: Generated responses gradually deviate from target strategy despite FUDGE control
  - Prompt saturation: Model ignores commonsense prompts due to information overload
  - Classification confusion: Strategy predictor consistently confuses similar strategy categories
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Compare generation quality with and without verbalized commonsense prompts to quantify their contribution
  2. Control strength analysis: Vary the FUDGE temperature parameter to find the optimal balance between creativity and strategy adherence
  3. Classifier comparison: Test different strategy prediction architectures (linear, BERT, LSTM) to identify the best trade-off between accuracy and efficiency

## Limitations

- Experimental validation relies primarily on automatic metrics rather than human evaluation, limiting confidence in practical effectiveness for generating genuinely empathetic responses
- Method's dependence on COMET-BART for commonsense knowledge generation introduces potential failure point if generated relations are irrelevant or low-quality
- Framework's effectiveness demonstrated only on ESConv dataset, limiting generalizability claims across different domains or conversation types

## Confidence

- High confidence: The technical implementation of the framework components (COMET-BART integration, verbalization templates, FUDGE control) follows established methodologies
- Medium confidence: The claim that commonsense knowledge incorporation improves dialogue quality, based on automatic metrics showing improvements over baselines
- Low confidence: The claim that the approach produces genuinely empathetic responses, as this is not directly validated through human evaluation
