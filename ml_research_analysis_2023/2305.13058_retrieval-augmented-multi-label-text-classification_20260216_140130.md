# Retrieval-augmented Multi-label Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13058
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13058
- Reference count: 15
- Improves macro-F1 for infrequent labels in multi-label classification

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of multi-label text classification with highly imbalanced label distributions, particularly focusing on improving performance on infrequent labels. The authors propose a retrieval-augmented approach that leverages similar documents from the training set to enhance classification. By integrating retrieved documents through cross-attention, the model achieves substantial improvements in macro-F1 scores for rare labels, especially in scenarios with limited training data or long documents.

## Method Summary
The method employs a two-phase training approach. First, a vanilla multi-label classifier (transformer encoder with classification heads) is trained, and its document embeddings are cached in a retrieval repository. Second, a retrieval-augmented classifier is trained, which uses cross-attention to integrate the top-K (K=4) most similar documents retrieved from the repository based on cosine similarity. The approach is evaluated on four legal and biomedical datasets using domain-specific language models like PubMed-BERT and LexLM.

## Key Results
- Retrieval augmentation substantially improves macro-F1 scores for infrequent labels
- Largest gains observed for datasets with longer documents (MIMIC and ECtHR)
- Performance benefits diminish as training data increases, with maximum gains at 5K samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The retrieval-augmented classifier leverages nearest-neighbor information for infrequent labels by conditioning predictions on both the input document and similar documents retrieved from the training set. Cross-attention integrates these neighbors into the input representation before classification. Core assumption: retrieved documents share meaningful label overlap with the input. Break condition: if retrieved documents lack label overlap or cross-attention fails to extract useful information.

### Mechanism 2
Retrieval augmentation is particularly effective for long documents and limited training data. For long documents, it provides additional context that may be lost in fixed-length representations. For limited data, it acts as implicit data augmentation. Core assumption: long documents contain complex information benefiting from external context. Break condition: if retrieval repository lacks diversity or cross-attention is improperly tuned for long documents.

### Mechanism 3
Benefits primarily appear in macro-F1 scores rather than micro-F1 scores because macro-F1 equally weights all labels. Retrieval augmentation specifically targets infrequent labels, improving macro-F1 while micro-F1 (dominated by frequent labels) sees limited impact. Core assumption: highly imbalanced label distribution with many infrequent labels. Break condition: if label distribution were balanced or augmentation didn't effectively target infrequent labels.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Vanilla classifier (Phase 1) -> Transformer encoder + classification heads; Retrieval repository -> Cached document representations; Retrieval-augmented classifier (Phase 2) -> Transformer encoder (initialized from vanilla) + classification heads (initialized from vanilla) + cross-attention module; Retrieval mechanism -> Cosine similarity-based retrieval of K nearest neighbors

- **Critical path**: 1) Train vanilla classifier on training data; 2) Cache document representations from vanilla classifier in retrieval repository; 3) Train retrieval-augmented classifier using cached representations and cross-attention; 4) Evaluate on test data

- **Design tradeoffs**: Number of retrieved documents (K) - higher K provides more context but increases computational cost; Cross-attention architecture - more layers/heads capture complex relationships but increase computational cost; Representation of retrieved documents - document representations vs. label representations vs. combination

- **Failure signatures**: Low label overlap between retrieved documents and input document; Cross-attention module failing to extract useful information; Overfitting to retrieval repository with limited training data

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Train vanilla classifier and cache document representations; 2) Experiment with different values of K and cross-attention architectures; 3) Compare performance of retrieval-augmented classifier vs. vanilla classifier on infrequent labels

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several implicit questions arise from the work. The approach's effectiveness in domains beyond legal and biomedical remains unexplored. The interpretability trade-offs introduced by retrieval augmentation are not discussed. Additionally, the paper does not address how the approach handles cases where retrieved documents are not relevant to the input document.

## Limitations

- Effectiveness heavily depends on quality of retrieved documents and their label overlap with input
- Requires computationally expensive training of initial vanilla classifier and caching all document representations
- Cross-attention mechanism adds computational overhead during inference
- Results focused on legal and biomedical domains, generalizability to other domains unclear

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Mechanism 3 (retrieval augmentation improves macro-F1 for infrequent labels) - well-supported by experimental results across multiple datasets
- **Medium confidence**: Mechanism 2 (effectiveness for long documents and limited data) - results show improvements but relationship with document length and data size not fully characterized
- **Medium confidence**: Mechanism 1 (core assumption about label overlap) - plausible given results but not directly verified

## Next Checks

1. **Label Overlap Analysis**: Conduct quantitative analysis of label overlap between input documents and top-K retrieved neighbors, measuring average Jaccard similarity and examining low-overlap failure cases.

2. **Ablation Study on Retrieval Quality**: Systematically vary retrieval mechanism (random neighbors, K-means centroids, learned retriever) to isolate contribution of retrieval quality to performance gains.

3. **Generalization Testing**: Apply retrieval-augmented classifier to datasets from different domains (news classification, sentiment analysis) to assess generalizability beyond legal and biomedical text.
