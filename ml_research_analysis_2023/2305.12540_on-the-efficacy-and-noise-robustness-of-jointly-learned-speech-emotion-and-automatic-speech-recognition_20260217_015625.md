# On the Efficacy and Noise-Robustness of Jointly Learned Speech Emotion and Automatic Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.12540
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12540
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Joint ASR-SER multitask learning improves both ASR WER (10.7%) and SER accuracy (2.3%) in clean scenarios, and outperforms independent models across most noisy conditions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates joint training of ASR and SER using a shared wav2vec2 encoder with skip-connection-based fusion of acoustic and linguistic embeddings. The approach improves both ASR and SER performance in clean conditions and demonstrates greater noise robustness across music and background noise types. While joint models generally outperform independent baselines, they underperform in babble scenarios. The work provides a simple architectural modification and demonstrates benefits in low-resource settings using IEMOCAP with MUSAN noise augmentation.

## Method Summary
The joint ASR-SER architecture uses a pre-trained wav2vec2 encoder (frozen convolutional part) with parallel ASR and SER branches. The ASR branch employs CTC loss while the SER branch fuses acoustic embeddings with BERT-based linguistic embeddings through an MLP with skip connections. A multitask loss function with α=0.1 balances the two objectives. The model is trained on IEMOCAP data augmented with MUSAN noise at SNR levels 5 and 15, evaluated across seven test scenarios using 10-fold cross-validation.

## Key Results
- Joint model improves clean-condition ASR WER by 10.7% and SER accuracy by 2.3% over baselines
- Outperforms independent ASR and SER models across music and background noise conditions
- Underperforms baseline in babble scenarios regardless of training approach or SNR level

- Core assumption: Acoustic embeddings contain emotion-relevant variability that can be captured by a shared encoder without degrading ASR performance.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) loss for ASR
  - Why needed here: Enables training ASR without explicit alignment between input frames and output labels, essential for streaming and end-to-end systems.
  - Quick check question: How does CTC loss handle variable-length speech segments when aligning with fixed-length transcripts?

- Concept: Cross-entropy loss for emotion classification
  - Why needed here: Standard supervised classification objective for mapping fused embeddings to discrete emotion categories.
  - Quick check question: What happens to the gradient magnitude if the model predicts a highly confident wrong class during training?

- Concept: Multitask learning loss balancing
  - Why needed here: The scaling factor α balances contribution of SER and ASR losses to prevent one task from dominating the shared encoder updates.
  - Quick check question: How would you tune α if SER performance improves but ASR WER increases sharply?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Speech input -> wav2vec2 encoder -> acoustic features -> ASR CTC decoder + SER acoustic branch -> BERT embeddings -> fusion MLP with skip connections -> SER classifier

- Critical path:
  1. Input speech → wav2vec2 encoder → acoustic features
  2. Acoustic features → ASR decoder (transcripts) + SER acoustic branch
  3. ASR transcripts → BERT → linguistic embeddings
  4. Acoustic embeddings + linguistic embeddings → fusion MLP → SER classifier

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Joint vs separate models: Joint saves compute and improves SER but can hurt ASR in babble-heavy scenarios.
  - Skip connections vs plain MLP: Skip connections improve stability but add parameters.
  - Noise augmentation level: More augmentation improves robustness but may hurt clean-condition performance.

- Failure signatures:
  - ASR WER degrades sharply on clean data: Likely under-regularization or α too biased toward SER.
  - SER accuracy drops on babble: Joint model may be overfitting to non-speech noise, not speech-like babble.
  - Training instability: Check gradient norms; may need to adjust α or add gradient clipping.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run joint model with α = 0.1 on clean IEMOCAP; verify WER and accuracy improvements over baselines.
  2. Train on noisy data (MUSAN overlay) and evaluate on SNR 15 noise; confirm robustness gain over clean-trained baselines.
  3. Remove skip connections from fusion; compare SER performance to detect their contribution.

## Limitations
- Performance degradation in babble scenarios where baseline outperforms joint model
- Limited noise augmentation using only MUSAN corpus, potentially limiting generalizability
- Skip-connection benefits not isolated through ablation studies

## Confidence
- **High**: WER and accuracy improvements in clean data; noise-robustness gain for non-babble noise types
- **Medium**: Consistency of skip-connection benefits; scaling factor selection (α=0.1)
- **Low**: Rationale for skip-connection design; performance dip on babble; MUSAN-only augmentation
