# VaxxHesitancy: A Dataset for Studying Hesitancy towards COVID-19 Vaccination on Twitter

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06660
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06660
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Dataset of 3,101 tweets with four stance categories; VaxxBERT achieves 73.0% accuracy and 69.3% F1-score

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces VaxxHesitancy, a dataset of 3,101 Twitter posts annotated for stance toward COVID-19 vaccination across four categories: pro-vaxx, anti-vaxx, vaccine-hesitant, and irrelevant. The dataset spans October 2020 to May 2022 and is the first to systematically distinguish vaccine hesitancy from anti-vaccination content. The authors also present VaxxBERT, a domain-specific language model pre-trained on 175 million COVID-19 vaccine-related tweets. VaxxBERT achieves state-of-the-art performance on the stance classification task, outperforming BERT and COVID-BERT baselines.

## Method Summary
The dataset was collected from Twitter using relevant keywords and hashtags related to COVID-19 vaccines. Three annotators manually labeled tweets across four stance categories. VaxxBERT was developed by pre-training BERT on 175 million COVID-19 vaccine-related tweets, then fine-tuning on the VaxxHesitancy dataset. The model architecture follows standard transformer-based approaches with domain-specific pretraining to capture vaccine-related discourse patterns.

## Key Results
- VaxxBERT achieves 73.0% accuracy and 69.3% F1-score on the four-way classification task
- VaxxBERT outperforms BERT (65.2% accuracy) and COVID-BERT (68.1% accuracy) baselines
- Dataset successfully distinguishes vaccine hesitancy from anti-vaxx content, a novel contribution

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of VaxxBERT stems from domain-specific pretraining on a large corpus of COVID-19 vaccine-related tweets, which captures the unique linguistic patterns and terminology of vaccine discourse. By fine-tuning on the carefully annotated VaxxHesitancy dataset, the model learns to distinguish subtle differences between pro-vaxx, anti-vaxx, and vaccine-hesitant stances. The separation of hesitancy as a distinct category allows for more nuanced analysis of public sentiment toward vaccination.

### Design Tradeoffs
The authors chose domain-specific pretraining over larger general models to capture vaccine-specific language patterns. This tradeoff sacrifices general language understanding for specialized domain knowledge. The relatively small annotated dataset (3,101 tweets) limits model generalizability but allows for focused analysis of vaccine hesitancy.

### Failure Signatures
Model may misclassify nuanced or sarcastic tweets expressing complex views. Ambiguous statements that could be interpreted as either hesitant or anti-vaxx may receive incorrect labels. Limited temporal scope may miss evolving discourse patterns beyond May 2022.

## Limitations
- Small dataset size (3,101 tweets) may limit generalizability
- Limited to three annotators, raising potential bias concerns
- English-language Twitter data only, limiting broader applicability
- Temporal scope (Oct 2020-May 2022) may not capture long-term trends
