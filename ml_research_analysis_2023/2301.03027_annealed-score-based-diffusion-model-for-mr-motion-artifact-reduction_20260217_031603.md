# Annealed Score-Based Diffusion Model for MR Motion Artifact Reduction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03027
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03027
- Reference count: 33
- Primary result: Achieved PSNR of 31.40 dB and SSIM of 0.916 for random motion artifacts, outperforming state-of-the-art deep learning methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an annealed score-based diffusion model for MRI motion artifact reduction that operates without requiring paired motion-free training data. The method employs an unconditionally trained score-based diffusion model that iteratively applies forward and reverse diffusion processes with gradually imposed data consistency constraints. By annealing the weight of high-frequency data consistency during the reverse diffusion, the approach successfully preserves detailed image structures while effectively removing motion artifacts. The method was evaluated on both simulated and in vivo motion artifacts, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing deep learning approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed method utilizes an unconditionally trained score-based diffusion model that learns the distribution of motion-free images only. For artifact reduction, the motion-corrupted input undergoes iterative forward and reverse diffusion cycles. During the reverse diffusion process, data consistency is gradually imposed with an annealing schedule that initially preserves low-frequency consistency and progressively corrects high-frequency artifacts. This approach leverages the assumption that motion artifacts primarily affect high-frequency regions of k-space, while low-frequency regions remain largely uncorrupted. The method employs bootstrap subsampling and aggregation to improve robustness, and uses the CCDF algorithm to reduce inference time.

## Key Results
- Achieved PSNR of 31.40 dB and SSIM of 0.916 for random motion artifacts on simulated data
- Achieved PSNR of 37.01 dB and SSIM of 0.940 for respiratory motion artifacts on simulated data
- Clinical evaluation on in vivo data showed highest scores across all assessment criteria: motion artifact reduction (3.63), noise level (3.58), blurring (3.97), vessel clarity (3.71), and overall image quality (3.45)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Annealed data consistency preserves high-frequency details while removing motion artifacts
- Mechanism: Gradually increasing high-frequency data consistency weight during reverse diffusion maintains low-frequency consistency first, then progressively corrects high-frequency artifacts without losing fine details
- Core assumption: Motion artifacts primarily affect high-frequency regions of k-space, while low-frequency regions remain largely uncorrupted
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By annealing the weight of high-frequency data consistency, the method preserves detailed image structures while removing motion artifacts."
  - [section] "Therefore, during the reverse diffusion, the low-frequency region needs to be maintained and only the high-frequency region should be corrected by diffusion sampling."
- Break condition: If motion artifacts are not primarily concentrated in high-frequency k-space regions, this mechanism will fail to preserve details

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Conditional diffusion sampling enables artifact removal without paired training data
- Mechanism: The unconditionally trained score-based diffusion model is applied to motion-corrupted images with data consistency constraints that gradually enforce low-frequency fidelity
- Core assumption: An unconditionally trained score-based diffusion model can learn the data distribution well enough to generate motion-free images when guided by appropriate constraints
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method uses an unconditionally trained score-based diffusion model with only motion-free images"
  - [section] "Because the score-based diffusion models require several steps of reverse diffusion, it takes a long time to generate outputs. Although we utilized the CCDF algorithm to reduce the inference time, our method also requires several seconds"
- Break condition: If the unconditional model fails to learn the true data distribution, the guided sampling will not produce artifact-free results

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Bootstrap subsampling and aggregation rejects k-space outliers to reduce motion artifacts
- Mechanism: Random subsampling of k-space lines removes motion-corrupted data points probabilistically, and aggregation across multiple subsampled reconstructions improves robustness
- Core assumption: Motion artifacts manifest as sparse outliers in k-space that can be removed through probabilistic rejection
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Although our prior method outperforms other simulation-based or unpaired deep learning methods, there exist limitations if the motion artifact does not appear as sparse outliers in k-space."
  - [section] "Rather than using the sparse outlier assumption in Eq. (8), our method is based on a more relaxed assumption that the motion artifacts in MRI mainly occur in the high-frequency region of k-space."
- Break condition: If motion artifacts are not sparse outliers or are distributed across k-space, this mechanism will be ineffective

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Unconditional score-based diffusion model -> Forward diffusion process -> Reverse diffusion with annealed data consistency -> Annealing schedule controller -> K-space sampling operator
- Critical path:
  1. Train score model on motion-free images
  2. Apply forward diffusion to motion-corrupted input
  3. Perform reverse diffusion with annealed data consistency
  4. Repeat forward/reverse cycles M times
  5. Output final artifact-reduced image
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Annealing schedule vs. detail preservation: Too aggressive annealing may lose details; too conservative may not remove artifacts
  - Number of iterations (M) vs. computation time: More iterations improve quality but increase runtime
  - N' (reverse steps) vs. performance: Too few steps may not converge; too many increase computation without benefit
- Failure signatures:
  - Blurry output images: Annealing too aggressive or insufficient detail injection
  - Remaining artifacts: Annealing too conservative or insufficient iterations
  - Excessive noise: Data consistency not properly enforced
  - Long computation time: Inefficient annealing schedule or too many iterations
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary λN′ parameter (0, 0.01, 0.1) to observe effect on detail preservation vs. artifact removal
  2. Test different combinations of N′ and M to find optimal balance of quality vs. computation time
  3. Compare performance on simulated random motion vs. respiratory motion to validate high-frequency assumption

## Limitations
- Computational efficiency is a primary limitation, requiring multiple forward and reverse diffusion cycles that take several seconds per reconstruction
- Performance heavily depends on the unconditional score model's ability to accurately learn the motion-free image distribution
- The high-frequency assumption for motion artifacts may not hold for all artifact types beyond respiratory and random motion

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (Annealing preserves details): Medium - supported by quantitative metrics but qualitative assessment limited to expert scores
- Mechanism 2 (Unconditional training works): High - validated on both simulated and in vivo data with state-of-the-art performance
- Mechanism 3 (Bootstrap subsampling): Low - this mechanism was largely replaced by the high-frequency assumption approach

## Next Checks

3. Evaluate the method's performance on different anatomical regions and scan types to assess generalizability beyond brain MRI.
