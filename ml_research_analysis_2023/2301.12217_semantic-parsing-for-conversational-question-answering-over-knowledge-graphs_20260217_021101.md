# Semantic Parsing for Conversational Question Answering over Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12217
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12217
- Reference count: 27
- This paper introduces SPICE, a dataset for conversational semantic parsing over Wikidata, and presents two semantic parsing approaches that struggle with coreference beyond the previous turn, ellipsis, and generalizing to unseen question intents.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SPICE, a large-scale dataset for conversational semantic parsing over Wikidata consisting of 197K conversations with annotated SPARQL queries. The authors present two semantic parsing approaches: a single decoder with dynamic vocabularies derived from KG subgraphs, and a multi-task model with multiple decoders and an ontology classifier. Experiments show that both models face significant challenges with coreference resolution beyond the previous turn, ellipsis, and generalizing to unseen question intents. The oracle model with gold annotations performs best, highlighting the critical importance of accurate entity and type linking in semantic parsing.

## Method Summary
The paper presents two baseline models for conversational semantic parsing over Wikidata. The first approach (BertSP) uses a BERT encoder fine-tuned for semantic parsing, combined with a Transformer decoder that employs dynamic vocabularies derived from KG subgraphs for each question. The second approach (LasagneSP) is a multi-stage model that first predicts SPARQL query templates, then fills in entity, relation, and type slots using an entity recognition module and ontology classifier. Both models are trained using ADAM optimizer and evaluated on the SPICE dataset using standard semantic parsing metrics including F1-score, accuracy, and exact match.

## Key Results
- Both baseline models struggle with coreference resolution beyond the previous turn, achieving only 22.1% accuracy for coreference spanning 2-3 utterances
- Models show poor performance on ellipsis, with accuracy dropping to 6.5% when the question contains only a relation
- Oracle model with gold annotations significantly outperforms non-oracle models, achieving 50.1% F1 compared to 30.3% for BertSP and 28.8% for LasagneSP
- Both models fail to generalize well to unseen question intents, with performance dropping to 6.8% F1 on non-i.i.d splits

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Dynamic vocabularies derived from KG subgraphs improve semantic parsing by reducing search space and focusing on relevant symbols.
- Mechanism: For each question, the model identifies related KG entities in the conversation context and extracts their one-hop neighborhood. This subgraph becomes the target vocabulary, filtering out irrelevant KG symbols and improving the decoder's focus.
- Core assumption: Relevant KG symbols for a question are contained within the one-hop neighborhood of entities mentioned in the question and context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The first approach (Gu et al., 2021) uses dynamic vocabularies derived from KG subgraphs for each question and a simple sequence-to-sequence architecture to predict complete SPARQL queries."
  - [section] "Following previous work (Gu et al., 2021; Marion et al., 2021), we assume the symbols related toxt are those appearing in sub-graph Gt of knowledge-graphK, Gt ⊆ K."
  - [corpus] Weak: No explicit quantitative comparison of performance with/without dynamic vocabularies is provided.
- Break condition: If important KG symbols are beyond one-hop or not directly linked to mentioned entities, they will be missed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-stage semantic parsing with separate modules for template prediction, entity recognition, and type/relation classification improves accuracy by decomposing the task.
- Mechanism: The Lasagne model first predicts a SPARQL template with placeholders, then uses an entity recognition module to link mentions to KG entities, and finally employs an ontology classifier to fill in type and relation slots.
- Core assumption: Decomposing semantic parsing into specialized subtasks allows each module to focus on its specific strengths.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The other approach (Kacupaj et al., 2021) predicts SPARQL query templates and then fills in entity, relation, and type slots by means of an entity and ontology classifier."
  - [section] "Lasagne employs an encoder-decoder model based on Transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017) to convert a user questionxt in a conversation into a logical form template."
  - [corpus] Weak: The paper does not provide ablation studies isolating the impact of each module.
- Break condition: If the subtasks are too interdependent or the errors compound, the multi-stage approach may be less effective than an end-to-end model.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Training with oracle annotations (gold entities, types, and coreference) provides an upper bound on semantic parsing performance and highlights the importance of accurate grounding.
- Mechanism: By providing the model with perfect entity and type linking, the model can focus solely on generating the correct SPARQL structure without being hindered by grounding errors.
- Core assumption: The difficulty of semantic parsing is separable into generating the correct SPARQL structure and accurately grounding mentions to KG symbols.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The oracle model with access to gold annotations performs best, highlighting the importance of accurate entity and type linking."
  - [section] "BertSPG has access to oracle entities, types, and coreference annotations which allows us to disassociate the complexity of the SPARQL generation task from the problem of grounding and disambiguating entities to KG symbols."
  - [corpus] Weak: No quantitative comparison of oracle vs. non-oracle performance on individual question types is provided.
- Break condition: If generating the correct SPARQL structure is highly dependent on accurate grounding, the oracle model may not provide a true upper bound.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Conversation context, user question, and KG subgraph (for BertSP) or conversation context and user question (for LasagneSP)
  - BertSP Encoder: BERT fine-tuned for semantic parsing
  - BertSP Decoder: Transformer network with dynamic vocabulary derived from KG subgraph
  - LasagneSP Encoder: Transformer encoder
  - LasagneSP Decoder: Transformer decoder for template prediction
  - Entity Recognition Module: LSTM-based BIO sequence labeling and NEL using Elasticsearch
  - Ontology Classifier: Graph Attention Network for type and relation classification
  - Output: Predicted SPARQL query

- Critical path:
  - BertSP: Input → BERT Encoder → Dynamic Vocabulary Extraction → Transformer Decoder → SPARQL Query
  - LasagneSP: Input → Transformer Encoder → Transformer Decoder (Template) → Entity Recognition → Ontology Classifier → SPARQL Query

- Design tradeoffs:
  - BertSP: Simpler architecture but requires truncating large subgraphs; dynamic vocabularies may miss important symbols.
  - LasagneSP: More complex multi-stage approach but allows for specialized modules; limited by the size of the ontology graph.

- Failure signatures:
  - Incorrect entities or relations in the SPARQL query
  - Missing entities or variables in the SPARQL query
  - Incorrect argument order (subject/object positions)
  - Ill-formed SPARQL syntax
  - Inability to handle coreference beyond the previous turn or ellipsis

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate the impact of oracle vs. non-oracle annotations on each model's performance.
  2. Test the models' ability to generalize to unseen question intents by creating query-based splits.
  3. Analyze the models' performance on different linguistic phenomena (coreference, ellipsis, multiple entities) to identify specific weaknesses.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several open questions, particularly regarding how to improve coreference resolution beyond the previous turn, handle ellipsis more effectively, and enable models to generalize to unseen question intents. While the paper demonstrates the challenges these phenomena present, it does not provide definitive solutions, leaving these as important directions for future research.

## Limitations
- The SPICE dataset, while large, is derived from CSQA and may inherit biases from the original data collection process
- Evaluation metrics focus on exact match and F1 scores without considering semantic equivalence of logically different but functionally equivalent queries
- Significant performance gap between oracle and non-oracle models indicates that grounding and disambiguation remain major challenges not fully addressed

## Confidence

**High Confidence Claims:**
- The SPICE dataset successfully provides conversational context with annotated SPARQL queries and answers
- Both baseline models demonstrate the difficulty of conversational semantic parsing, particularly for coreference beyond the previous turn and ellipsis handling
- Oracle model performance confirms that accurate entity and type linking is crucial for semantic parsing success

**Medium Confidence Claims:**
- Dynamic vocabularies from KG subgraphs improve semantic parsing by reducing search space
- Multi-stage semantic parsing with specialized modules offers advantages over single decoder approaches
- The dataset effectively captures various linguistic phenomena present in conversational question answering

**Low Confidence Claims:**
- The specific mechanisms by which dynamic vocabularies improve performance (lacking quantitative ablation studies)
- The optimal architecture for conversational semantic parsing over large KGs (limited model comparison)
- The generalizability of findings to other knowledge graphs beyond Wikidata

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study on Dynamic Vocabulary Size**: Conduct experiments varying the subgraph size used for dynamic vocabulary extraction to determine the optimal balance between coverage and computational efficiency. Test whether expanding beyond one-hop neighborhoods or using different subgraph sampling strategies improves performance.

2. **Semantic Equivalence Evaluation**: Implement a semantic equivalence checker to evaluate whether semantically equivalent but syntactically different SPARQL queries receive appropriate credit. This would provide a more nuanced understanding of model capabilities beyond exact match metrics.

3. **Cross-KG Generalization Test**: Evaluate the baseline models on conversational semantic parsing datasets grounded in different knowledge graphs (e.g., DBpedia, Freebase) to assess the generalizability of the SPICE dataset and baseline approaches beyond Wikidata. This would reveal whether the challenges identified are KG-specific or more general to conversational semantic parsing.
