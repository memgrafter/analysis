# Domain Adaptation of Reinforcement Learning Agents based on Network Service Proximity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01013
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01013
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: Domain adaptation using Cycle-GANs achieves comparable performance to training from scratch but requires 5-6 times fewer samples.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a domain adaptation approach for reinforcement learning agents managing telecom services using Cycle-GANs to build correspondence mappings between state-action pairs across different services or radio environments. The method enables trained policies to adapt to new situations without retraining by learning bidirectional mappings between source and target domains. A heuristic based on lax-bisimulation distance is proposed to select the most suitable source policy for adaptation. Experimental results on a custom telecom emulator show that domain-adapted policies achieve comparable performance to policies trained from scratch but require significantly fewer samples (5-6x improvement).

## Method Summary
The approach uses Cycle-GANs to construct observation alignment (G: S_source → S_target) and action alignment (H: S_source × A_source → A_target) functions, along with their inverses, using adversarial training and cycle-consistency losses to align states, actions, and transition dynamics. A PPO policy is first trained on a source domain, then the Cycle-GAN module is trained using 3-tuples (state, action, next-state) collected from both source and target domains. The lax-bisimulation distance heuristic predicts adaptation success by measuring the similarity of state trajectories under the same action sequences across domains. The adapted policy inherits structure from the source domain, requiring fewer samples to reach target performance thresholds compared to training from scratch.

## Key Results
- Domain-adapted policies achieve comparable performance to policies trained from scratch
- Sample efficiency improvement: ~5-6x fewer samples required (1200 vs 5500 samples to reach 2% deviation)
- Method is particularly effective when source and target domains are proximal, as determined by the heuristic
- Lax-bisimulation distance successfully predicts adaptation success rates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Cycle-GANs enable domain adaptation by learning bidirectional mappings between state-action pairs without requiring paired data or reward signals from the target domain. The adversarial training and cycle-consistency losses align states, actions, and transition dynamics across domains. This works when source and target domains share structural similarities.

### Mechanism 2
The lax-bisimulation distance heuristic predicts adaptation success by measuring similarity of state trajectories under the same action sequences. The heuristic computes deviation between state trajectories in source and target domains, with smaller deviations indicating higher potential for successful adaptation.

### Mechanism 3
Domain adaptation is more sample-efficient than training from scratch because it leverages existing knowledge from a proximal source policy. The adapted policy inherits structure and learned features from the source domain, requiring fewer samples to reach performance thresholds.

  - Why needed here: Enable learning of bidirectional mappings between unpaired state-action pairs across domains without requiring paired data or reward signals
  - Quick check question: Can Cycle-GANs learn correspondences between domains when the state and action spaces have different dimensions or distributions?

  - Why needed here: Provides principled way to measure similarity between MDPs by comparing state trajectories under same action sequences
  - Quick check question: How does lax-bisimulation distance differ from regular bisimulation, and why is it more suitable for this application?

  - Why needed here: Representing state as tuple of current KPI value and normalized deviation from target enables policy to learn to achieve any target value

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Network Emulator -> PPO Trainer -> Domain Adaptation Pipeline -> Cycle-GAN Module -> Adapted Policy
- Critical path: PPO Trainer → Domain Adaptation Pipeline → Cycle-GAN Module → Adapted Policy
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Cycle-GANs allow adaptation without paired data but require careful tuning of adversarial and cycle-consistency losses
  - Heuristic for selecting source policy adds preprocessing step but improves adaptation success rates
  - Service-specific KPIs and control parameters require domain expertise for each new service type
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance indicates source and target domains are not proximal enough
  - High sample complexity suggests learned correspondences are ineffective due to domain dissimilarity
  - Unstable Cycle-GAN training indicates issues with loss weighting
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train policy in URLLC domain and evaluate performance on mIoT and CV domains without adaptation
  2. Adapt URLLC policy to mIoT domain using proposed method, compare sample efficiency with training from scratch
  3. Use heuristic to predict success of adapting URLLC policy to CV domain and verify prediction experimentally

## Limitations
- Method effectiveness diminishes when source and target domains are fundamentally different (e.g., CV vs URLLC)
- Lax-bisimulation heuristic provides imperfect measure of domain proximity, potentially missing hidden structural similarities
- Sample efficiency advantage depends critically on accurate heuristic for selecting appropriate source domains

## Confidence
- High confidence in core Cycle-GAN domain adaptation mechanism (supported by experimental results)
- Medium confidence in lax-bisimulation heuristic's predictive power (validated only within specific telecom emulator)
- Medium confidence in sample efficiency claims (limited by single network emulator environment and three service types)
