# Wasserstein Projection Pursuit of Non-Gaussian Signals
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12693
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12693
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: Proposes Wasserstein projection pursuit method for recovering low-dimensional non-Gaussian signal subspaces from high-dimensional data

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental unsupervised learning problem of recovering a low-dimensional non-Gaussian signal subspace from high-dimensional data. The authors propose a novel projection pursuit approach that identifies orthogonal directions maximizing the 2-Wasserstein distance between empirical data projections and a standard Gaussian distribution. Theoretical analysis shows that under a spiked Gaussian model with a low-dimensional non-Gaussian signal subspace, the method can accurately recover the signal space with performance inversely proportional to the signal-to-noise ratio.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses projection pursuit to sequentially find unit vectors that maximize the empirical Wasserstein distance of projected data to Gaussianity while maintaining orthogonality constraints. The approach involves estimating the 2-Wasserstein distance between the empirical distribution of data projections and a standard Gaussian, then identifying directions that maximize this distance. The method is designed to work in regimes where data dimensionality is comparable to sample size, contrasting with recent literature on infeasibility in high-dimensional regimes.

## Key Results
- Theoretical guarantees show recovered directions have small components in the Gaussian subspace
- Method can estimate signal dimension when signal-to-noise ratio is sufficiently large
- Uniform bounds demonstrate empirical Wasserstein distances approximate true distances across all directions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach leverages the fact that non-Gaussian distributions have distinctive geometric properties that can be captured by Wasserstein distance metrics. By maximizing the Wasserstein distance between projected data and Gaussian distributions, the method identifies directions that best preserve non-Gaussian structure. The orthogonal pursuit ensures that each subsequent direction captures unique non-Gaussian information not already captured by previous directions.

## Foundational Learning
- Wasserstein distance: Metric measuring distance between probability distributions based on optimal transport
  - Why needed: Provides a way to quantify deviation from Gaussianity
  - Quick check: Verify that Wasserstein distance is well-defined for empirical distributions
- Projection pursuit: Iterative method for finding optimal projections of high-dimensional data
  - Why needed: Enables sequential discovery of informative directions
  - Quick check: Confirm orthogonality constraints are properly maintained
- Spiked covariance model: Statistical model with low-rank signal plus noise structure
  - Why needed: Provides theoretical framework for analysis
  - Quick check: Validate model assumptions match data characteristics

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Data -> Wasserstein distance calculation -> Direction optimization -> Orthogonal projection -> Signal subspace estimation

Critical path: Data projection → Wasserstein distance computation → Direction maximization → Orthogonality enforcement → Signal dimension estimation

Design tradeoffs: Computational efficiency vs. accuracy in Wasserstein distance estimation; orthogonality constraints vs. flexibility in direction selection

Failure signatures: Poor performance when signal-to-noise ratio is low; computational bottlenecks in high-dimensional settings; failure to maintain orthogonality in noisy conditions

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees primarily established for spiked covariance model with specific signal structure
- Orthogonal signal direction assumption may not hold in practical scenarios
- Computational complexity of Wasserstein distance calculation could be prohibitive in very high dimensions

## Confidence
- Theoretical recovery guarantees: High
- Practical effectiveness on real data: Medium
- Scalability to very high dimensions: Low
